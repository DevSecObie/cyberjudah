## Introduction

*[[0:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGsquI9jztQ&t=3s)]* The backdrop of today's lesson is Zechariah 8:19, and we are going to expound on that scripture. Often this scripture is read and people ask: do these fasts go back to Leviticus 23, are they found anywhere else in the Bible, and why does it mention these particular fasts over and over like that? Captain Josiah shows from 2 Kings 25 what happened to Judah in the fourth, fifth, seventh and tenth months.

## Scriptures Opened

**[Zechariah 8:19](/bible/zechariah/8#v19)**  *[[0:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGsquI9jztQ&t=25s)]*

> <sup>[19](/bible/zechariah/8#v19)</sup> Thus saith the Lord of hosts; The fast of the fourth month, and the fast of the fifth, and the fast of the seventh, and the fast of the tenth, shall be to the house of Judah joy and gladness, and cheerful feasts; therefore love the truth and peace.

- The fast of the fourth month, the fifth, the seventh and the tenth. Do these go back to Leviticus 23? Why does it mention these particular fasts? That is what we are going to explain.


**[Zechariah 7:5](/bible/zechariah/7#v5)**  *[[1:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGsquI9jztQ&t=64s)]*

> <sup>[5](/bible/zechariah/7#v5)</sup> Speak unto all the people of the land, and to the priests, saying, When ye fasted and mourned in the fifth and seventh month, even those seventy years, did ye at all fast unto me, even to me?

- This connects with Zechariah 8:19. It mentions two right there, the fifth and the seventh month.

- Even those seventy years, did ye at all fast unto me? The Lord is asking a question: when you did that, was it truly a fast for me?


**[Zechariah 1:1](/bible/zechariah/1#v1)**  *[[1:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGsquI9jztQ&t=102s)]*

> <sup>[1](/bible/zechariah/1#v1)</sup> In the eighth month, in the second year of Darius, came the word of the Lord unto Zechariah, the son of Berechiah, the son of Iddo the prophet, saying,

- In the eighth month, in the second year of Darius: the book of Zechariah is during the time of the Persian Mede captivity.

- When you fasted and mourned: notice what it mentioned. We are going to explain it.


**[Jeremiah 25:11-12](/bible/jeremiah/25#v11)**  *[[2:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGsquI9jztQ&t=170s)]*

> <sup>[11](/bible/jeremiah/25#v11)</sup> And this whole land shall be a desolation, and an astonishment; and these nations shall serve the king of Babylon seventy years.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/jeremiah/25#v12)</sup> And it shall come to pass, when seventy years are accomplished, that I will punish the king of Babylon, and that nation, saith the Lord, for their iniquity, and the land of the Chaldeans, and will make it perpetual desolations.

- What are the seventy years talking about? This whole land is Jerusalem; it shall be a desolation, and these nations shall serve the king of Babylon seventy years.

- When seventy years are accomplished I will punish the king of Babylon. How did he punish them? By bringing in the Medo-Persian empire.

- That is what we are reading in the book of Zechariah, after the seventy years. It is mentioning that those fasts happened while we were in Babylon those seventy years.


**[Isaiah 58:3-6](/bible/isaiah/58#v3)**  *[[4:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGsquI9jztQ&t=264s)]*

> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/58#v3)</sup> Wherefore have we fasted, say they, and thou seest not? wherefore have we afflicted our soul, and thou takest no knowledge? Behold, in the day of your fast ye find pleasure, and exact all your labours.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/58#v4)</sup> Behold, ye fast for strife and debate, and to smite with the fist of wickedness: ye shall not fast as ye do this day, to make your voice to be heard on high.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/58#v5)</sup> Is it such a fast that I have chosen? a day for a man to afflict his soul? is it to bow down his head as a bulrush, and to spread sackcloth and ashes under him? wilt thou call this a fast, and an acceptable day to the Lord?
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/58#v6)</sup> Is not this the fast that I have chosen? to loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy burdens, and to let the oppressed go free, and that ye break every yoke?

- Why is the Lord asking, when you fasted, did you fast unto me? Because often, brothers and sisters, you can enter a fast but your spirit is not right; the intent behind the fast is not right.

- Sometimes you go into a fast and you cram yourself with a bunch of food and water, drink and eat everything you can: yeah, I'm preparing for a fast. That ain't the right spirit.

- Wherefore have we fasted, and thou seest not: why have we fasted, Lord, and you don't really see it? In the day of your fast you find pleasure and exact all your labours. Don't fast for wickedness, strife and debate.

- To spread sackcloth and ashes under him: now you are walking around all mopey, I'm fasting, brothers. You think the Lord honors that?

- To loose the bands of wickedness means go out and teach, go out and do the work. That is why, on what we call the Day of Atonement, we go out and teach.

- To let the oppressed go free: free in our minds, our spirits. We go tell our people, you are God's chosen people, come out of Babylon's ways. We have got to make sure we have the right spirit behind the fast.


**[2 Kings 25:1](/bible/2-kings/25#v1)**  *[[7:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGsquI9jztQ&t=478s)]*

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/25#v1)</sup> And it came to pass in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, in the tenth day of the month, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came, he, and all his host, against Jerusalem, and pitched against it; and they built forts against it round about.

- Zechariah 8:19 mentions them in numerical order: the fourth, fifth, seventh and tenth. Watch what happens in 2 Kings 25.

- In the tenth month Nebuchadnezzar came in and besieged the city. That is why it was a fast of the tenth month that is mentioned in Zechariah 8:19.

- Zechariah 7:5 says when you fasted and mourned. This is why: Nebuchadnezzar came in, in the tenth month, and besieged the city.


**[2 Kings 25:3-7](/bible/2-kings/25#v3)**  *[[9:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGsquI9jztQ&t=548s)]*

> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/25#v3)</sup> And on the ninth day of the fourth month the famine prevailed in the city, and there was no bread for the people of the land.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/25#v4)</sup> And the city was broken up, and all the men of war fled by night by the way of the gate between two walls, which is by the king’s garden: (now the Chaldees were against the city round about:) and the king went the way toward the plain.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/25#v5)</sup> And the army of the Chaldees pursued after the king, and overtook him in the plains of Jericho: and all his army were scattered from him.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/25#v6)</sup> So they took the king, and brought him up to the king of Babylon to Riblah; and they gave judgment upon him.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/25#v7)</sup> And they slew the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes, and put out the eyes of Zedekiah, and bound him with fetters of brass, and carried him to Babylon.

- What happened in the fourth month? The city is besieged in the tenth month; then you have got the eleventh, the twelfth, then back to the first, second, third, fourth month. So you have got like six months of the city being besieged, and in the fourth month there was a famine. There was no bread.

- They gave judgment upon Zedekiah, slew his sons before his eyes, put out his eyes, and carried him to Babylon. This all started in the fourth month.

- Zechariah 8:19 mentioned the fast of the fourth month first because it was in numerical order, but in 2 Kings we are going in chronological order: the city was besieged in the tenth month, and now we are at the fourth.


**[2 Kings 25:8-12](/bible/2-kings/25#v8)**  *[[11:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGsquI9jztQ&t=666s)]*

> <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/25#v8)</sup> And in the fifth month, on the seventh day of the month, which is the nineteenth year of king Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, came Nebuzar–adan, captain of the guard, a servant of the king of Babylon, unto Jerusalem:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/25#v9)</sup> And he burnt the house of the Lord, and the king’s house, and all the houses of Jerusalem, and every great man’s house burnt he with fire.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-kings/25#v10)</sup> And all the army of the Chaldees, that were with the captain of the guard, brake down the walls of Jerusalem round about.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/25#v11)</sup> Now the rest of the people that were left in the city, and the fugitives that fell away to the king of Babylon, with the remnant of the multitude, did Nebuzar–adan the captain of the guard carry away.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-kings/25#v12)</sup> But the captain of the guard left of the poor of the land to be vinedressers and husbandmen.

- In the fifth month he burnt the house of the Lord: he burnt the temple, the king's house, and all the houses of Jerusalem.

- That is why we had the fast of the fifth month: Nebuchadnezzar came in and burnt the temple and all the houses in Jerusalem.

- The captain of the guard left the poor of the land to be vinedressers and husbandmen.


**[Obadiah 1:10-13](/bible/obadiah/1#v10)**  *[[12:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGsquI9jztQ&t=761s)]*

> <sup>[10](/bible/obadiah/1#v10)</sup> For thy violence against thy brother Jacob shame shall cover thee, and thou shalt be cut off for ever.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/obadiah/1#v11)</sup> In the day that thou stoodest on the other side, in the day that the strangers carried away captive his forces, and foreigners entered into his gates, and cast lots upon Jerusalem, even thou wast as one of them.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/obadiah/1#v12)</sup> But thou shouldest not have looked on the day of thy brother in the day that he became a stranger; neither shouldest thou have rejoiced over the children of Judah in the day of their destruction; neither shouldest thou have spoken proudly in the day of distress.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/obadiah/1#v13)</sup> Thou shouldest not have entered into the gate of my people in the day of their calamity; yea, thou shouldest not have looked on their affliction in the day of their calamity, nor have laid hands on their substance in the day of their calamity;

- A digression to the book of Obadiah, for the coming in with Babylon. For thy violence against thy brother Jacob, thou shalt be cut off for ever. This didn't happen yet, brothers and sisters. You have got these stupid people out here saying this happened during the Babylonian captivity. No, it did not.

- In the day that the strangers carried away captive his forces: that is what we just read in 2 Kings. When Babylon came in, the so-called white men assisted them. Foreigners entered into his gates: the Babylonians. Even thou wast as one of them: you were as one of them, Esau.

- In the day that he became a stranger: Israelites did become strangers. For you fake Christians, when you read about strangers in the New Testament, it is talking about Israelites.

- Thou shouldest not have entered into the gate of my people in the day of their calamity. Everything Nebuchadnezzar and Nebuzaradan did, Esau was right with them: burn them over here too, destroy this over here too. They were assisting Babylon in conquering and destroying us.


**[2 Kings 25:24-26](/bible/2-kings/25#v24)**  *[[15:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGsquI9jztQ&t=900s)]*

> <sup>[24](/bible/2-kings/25#v24)</sup> And Gedaliah sware to them, and to their men, and said unto them, Fear not to be the servants of the Chaldees: dwell in the land, and serve the king of Babylon; and it shall be well with you.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-kings/25#v25)</sup> But it came to pass in the seventh month, that Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, the son of Elishama, of the seed royal, came, and ten men with him, and smote Gedaliah, that he died, and the Jews and the Chaldees that were with him at Mizpah.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-kings/25#v26)</sup> And all the people, both small and great, and the captains of the armies, arose, and came to Egypt: for they were afraid of the Chaldees.

- Gedaliah told them to serve the king of Babylon and it shall be well with you. Why? Because you cannot circumvent God's prophecies. God says you are going to go into captivity, and it is going to happen.

- In the seventh month Ishmael came, with ten men, and smote Gedaliah, and he died. So this was a fast, a mourning, for us in the seventh month.


**[Zechariah 8:19](/bible/zechariah/8#v19)**  *[[16:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGsquI9jztQ&t=985s)]*

> <sup>[19](/bible/zechariah/8#v19)</sup> Thus saith the Lord of hosts; The fast of the fourth month, and the fast of the fifth, and the fast of the seventh, and the fast of the tenth, shall be to the house of Judah joy and gladness, and cheerful feasts; therefore love the truth and peace.

- We had those months commemorating the events that happened to us in Babylon. But God says, when y'all fast, it is going to be joy and peace.

- The tenth month was when Babylon came in and besieged the city. The fourth month was the famine prevailing. The fifth month was when they came in and burnt all the houses. The seventh was when Gedaliah was killed. That is why there was a fast.
