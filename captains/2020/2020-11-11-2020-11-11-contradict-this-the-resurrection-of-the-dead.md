## Introduction

*[[0:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3A0dHCjcKyg&t=14s)]* Contradict This on the resurrection of the dead: they say Job 7:9 contradicts John 5:28-29. Job is saying a man can't bring himself back from the dead and walk back into his house, but God can. Job himself waited for his change, the change Paul shows at the last trump, and knew that in his flesh he would see God.

## Scriptures Opened

**[Psalms 111:10](/bible/psalms/111#v10)**  *[[0:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3A0dHCjcKyg&t=46s)]*

> <sup>[10](/bible/psalms/111#v10)</sup> The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom: a good understanding have all they that do his commandments: his praise endureth for ever.

- You blacks, Hispanics and Native Americans are the Israelites the Bible speaks of, and if you want to become wise, the fear of the Lord is the beginning of that. The reason people think there are so many contradictions in the Bible is they don't keep God's commandments, so they don't have a good understanding.


**[Job 7:9](/bible/job/7#v9)**  *[[1:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3A0dHCjcKyg&t=83s)]*

> <sup>[9](/bible/job/7#v9)</sup> As the cloud is consumed and vanisheth away: so he that goeth down to the grave shall come up no more.

- As the cloud is consumed and vanisheth away, so he that goeth down to the grave shall come up no more. Read it in its proper context with verse 10: he shall return no more to his house. You can't bring yourself back from the dead, but God can. If you die you ain't waking up and walking into your house. It's no contradiction.

  Precepts:
  - **[Job 7:10](/bible/job/7#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/job/7#v10)</sup> He shall return no more to his house, neither shall his place know him any more.

    He shall return no more to his house, neither shall his place know him any more.


**[John 5:28](/bible/john/5#v28)**  *[[1:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3A0dHCjcKyg&t=100s)]*

> <sup>[28](/bible/john/5#v28)</sup> Marvel not at this: for the hour is coming, in the which all that are in the graves shall hear his voice,

- What Christ said: the hour is coming in which all that are in the graves shall hear his voice and shall come forth. That seems like a contradiction, until you read Job in context.

  Precepts:
  - **[John 5:29](/bible/john/5#v29)**
    > <sup>[29](/bible/john/5#v29)</sup> And shall come forth; they that have done good, unto the resurrection of life; and they that have done evil, unto the resurrection of damnation.

    And shall come forth; they that have done good, unto the resurrection of life; and they that have done evil, unto the resurrection of damnation.


**[Job 14:14](/bible/job/14#v14)**  *[[3:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3A0dHCjcKyg&t=201s)]*

> <sup>[14](/bible/job/14#v14)</sup> If a man die, shall he live again? all the days of my appointed time will I wait, till my change come.

- Job knew men can be regenerated: if a man die, shall he live again? All the days of my appointed time will I wait, till my change come.


**[1 Corinthians 15:51](/bible/1-corinthians/15#v51)**  *[[4:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3A0dHCjcKyg&t=255s)]*

> <sup>[51](/bible/1-corinthians/15#v51)</sup> Behold, I shew you a mystery; We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed,

- This is the change Job was talking about. In the Bible it's called regeneration; people today call it resurrection.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Corinthians 15:52](/bible/1-corinthians/15#v52)**
    > <sup>[52](/bible/1-corinthians/15#v52)</sup> In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed.

    In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed.


**[Job 19:25](/bible/job/19#v25)**  *[[5:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3A0dHCjcKyg&t=351s)]*

> <sup>[25](/bible/job/19#v25)</sup> For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth:

- I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth. Even once he dies, in his flesh he knew he was going to come back and see God. If you didn't have an understanding of the Bible this would make no sense; all you got to do is keep reading.

  Precepts:
  - **[Job 19:26](/bible/job/19#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/job/19#v26)</sup> And though after my skin worms destroy this body, yet in my flesh shall I see God:

    And though after my skin worms destroy this body, yet in my flesh shall I see God:


**[2 Samuel 12:23](/bible/2-samuel/12#v23)**  *[[6:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3A0dHCjcKyg&t=395s)]*

> <sup>[23](/bible/2-samuel/12#v23)</sup> But now he is dead, wherefore should I fast? can I bring him back again? I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me.

- After David's son died he stopped fasting: can I bring him back again? I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me. Only God has the power to raise the dead, Christ or the heavenly Father; you can't raise yourself.
