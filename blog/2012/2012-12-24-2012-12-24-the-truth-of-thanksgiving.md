## Introduction

Shalom, and welcome to another edition of *The Truth Shall Make You Free*. I am your host, Elder Nathanyel; on my right, Deacon Asaph. Today's topic is the truth of Thanksgiving.

**[John 8:32](/bible/john/8#v32)**  *[[0:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfeSju75iMY&t=38s)]*

> <sup>[32](/bible/john/8#v32)</sup> And ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free.

- And ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free. Black man, black woman, Latin man, Latin woman: the truth is that you are the Israelites. We were made slaves in America for breaking God's commandments. If we want deliverance, we must repent as the Israelites and keep the Most High's laws, statutes and commandments.

### The History Behind Thanksgiving

The familiar story says peaceful pilgrims and Indians sat with Squanto over turkey, squash and cranberry sauce. That version hides the conquest, slavery and genocide that surrounded the settlement.

**[Deuteronomy 28:15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)**  *[[10:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfeSju75iMY&t=654s)]*

> <sup>[15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)</sup> But it shall come to pass, if thou wilt not hearken unto the voice of the Lord thy God, to observe to do all his commandments and his statutes which I command thee this day; that all these curses shall come upon thee, and overtake thee:

- These curses came because Israel would not hearken to the Lord or observe his commandments and statutes. The history of the tribes on this land must be measured against the curses rather than against a schoolroom holiday story.

**[Deuteronomy 28:30-33](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v30)**  *[[11:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfeSju75iMY&t=684s)]*

> <sup>[30](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v30)</sup> Thou shalt betroth a wife, and another man shall lie with her: thou shalt build an house, and thou shalt not dwell therein: thou shalt plant a vineyard, and shalt not gather the grapes thereof.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v31)</sup> Thine ox shall be slain before thine eyes, and thou shalt not eat thereof: thine ass shall be violently taken away from before thy face, and shall not be restored to thee: thy sheep shall be given unto thine enemies, and thou shalt have none to rescue them.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v32)</sup> Thy sons and thy daughters shall be given unto another people, and thine eyes shall look, and fail with longing for them all the day long: and there shall be no might in thine hand.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v33)</sup> The fruit of thy land, and all thy labours, shall a nation which thou knowest not eat up; and thou shalt be only oppressed and crushed alway:

- Wives were violated; houses and vineyards were taken; oxen and buffalo were slaughtered before their owners' eyes; sheep were given to enemies; sons and daughters were taken by another people. Historical photographs show thousands of buffalo slain before the tribes.

- Indigenous children and adults were sold as slaves, including from the markets of colonial New York. Their parents had no power to recover them. Squanto could interpret between English settlers and his people because he himself had been enslaved in Europe and later returned.

Reading from an article by Glen Ford, published November 27, 2006, on Thanksgiving and colonial genocide  *[[14:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OfeSju75iMY&t=888s)]*

> Why did America embrace Thanksgiving? Because a majority of the population glories in the fruits, if not the unpleasant details, of genocide and slavery—and feels good about its heritage.

- The comforting story allows people to avoid facing the genocide that produced the country. The pilgrims are made to appear as innocent victims of harsh weather, motivated only by religion and a wholesome desire for a new beginning.

> Captain Hunt, an English slave trader, arrived at Patuxet. It was common practice for explorers to capture Indians, take them to Europe and sell them into slavery for 220 shillings apiece.

- They crossed the Atlantic on slave ships. Enslavement of the tribes in Europe, the West Indies and the colonies is part of the record that is usually left out.

> Most historians believe about seven hundred Pequots were slaughtered at Mystic River. Many prisoners were executed, and surviving women and children were sold into slavery in the West Indies. Massachusetts offered a bounty for every Indian scalp and a larger bounty for prisoners who could be sold.

- Even people called praying Indians—those converted to Christianity who fought beside European troops—were enslaved or killed. Others were invited to negotiate or take refuge at trading posts and then sold onto slave ships.

> The country they claimed as their own was fathered by genocide and mothered by slavery. The children's version cannot produce brotherhood; it prolongs the worship of colonial forefathers and erases their crimes.

- Defending the holiday as merely a day for family and brotherhood still preserves the myth. Brotherhood cannot be built by erasing the people who were burned, enslaved and dispossessed.

### Broken Covenants and Conquest

> <sup>[21](/bible/psalms/55#v21)</sup> The words of his mouth were smoother than butter, but war was in his heart: his words were softer than oil, yet were they drawn swords.

- The dates matter: Europeans reached the mainland long before the 1620–1621 pilgrim story. Shiploads, trading ventures and plans to make the land an extension of Europe were already moving back and forth.

>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-chronicles/28#v11)</sup> Now hear me therefore, and deliver the captives again, which ye have taken captive of your brethren: for the fierce wrath of the Lord is upon you.

- The northern kingdom once carried two hundred thousand of Judah's women, sons and daughters captive to Samaria. The prophet Oded confronted them: God had delivered Judah because of sin, but Israel had slain them in a rage and intended to keep their own brethren as bondmen and bondwomen.

- Oded commanded them to release the captives because the fierce wrath of the Lord was upon them. This history answers the claim that one tribe enslaving another proves either is not Israel. Sin and captivity happened among the tribes before, and the command was repentance and restoration.

- The same divide-and-conquer pattern appeared later when colonial powers pressed some indigenous nations to enslave black people and recruited Buffalo Soldiers against indigenous people. Every tribe must acknowledge its sin rather than using that history to reject its brethren.
