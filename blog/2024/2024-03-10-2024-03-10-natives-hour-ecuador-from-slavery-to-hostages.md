## In The News

- *[[43:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IPN-bITmtlk&t=2606s)]* **Britannica, Ecuador, the colonial period: under the Audiencia of Quito and the Spanish Crown, Spanish culture spread by religious orders and male colonists; in the Sierra, large estates worked by indigenous peons; rough textiles and primitive sweatshops the only industry; on the coast much of the population died of introduced diseases, with a small population of enslaved people, free blacks and mixed ethnicities; in the Oriente the Shuar successfully repelled European invaders, but Jesuits and other missionaries spread Christianity and the Quechua language** — Introduced diseases: don't that sound like what the English did to the Native Americans? They couldn't beat them physically, so they came with Christianity.
- *[[49:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IPN-bITmtlk&t=2983s)]* **Quito claims the first patriot uprising against Spanish rule, in 1809; in 1822 the armies of Simon Bolivar and Antonio Jose de Sucre came from Colombia, and on May 24 Sucre won the decisive battle of Pichincha near Quito** — The officers brought out that Bolivar was a Venezuelan soldier and revolutionary fighting the Spanish dependency in South America, who served as president of Venezuela and was friends with a Haitian revolutionary; the Haitian Revolution sparked those independence movements. One officer was born in Bolivar County, Mississippi, established February 9, 1836 and named for Simon Bolivar.
- *[[1:07:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IPN-bITmtlk&t=4076s)]* **Wikipedia, History of Ecuador: indigenous tribes absorbed into the Inca Empire in the early 15th century, the Incas conquered by the Spanish under Francisco Pizarro, the region under the Viceroyalty of Peru, the Quito Audiencia established in 1563, joined to New Granada in 1720, independent in 1830 after Gran Colombia** — It says the Incas themselves were conquered shortly afterwards, by the Spanish.
- *[[1:10:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IPN-bITmtlk&t=4241s)]* **The encomienda: a Spanish labor system that rewarded conquerors with the labor of conquered non-Christian peoples, in theory with benefits like military protection and education; first established in Spain after the Reconquista of the Moorish territories, then applied on a much larger scale in the Americas and the Spanish East Indies, where conquered peoples were considered vassals of the Spanish monarch** — In theory, that's bull: that's slavery. The French and English imported the southern kingdom; the Spanish kept the northern kingdom on the land and forced them to work it until they died. They used the same method on the Moors, Judah, that they used on Israel, and not on the Chinese, the Japanese or the East Indians: we have to be the same people.
