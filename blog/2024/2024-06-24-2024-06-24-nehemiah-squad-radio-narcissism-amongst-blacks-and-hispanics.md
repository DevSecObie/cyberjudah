## Class Questions

- **Diana, give us the clinical definition of narcissism, because we're all calling each other narcissists every five minutes.** Diana: typically somebody grandiose, with a lack of empathy, not understanding the emotions of others and unable to regulate themselves without dysregulating somebody else. Their homes and workplaces are chaotic, like a tornado of emotions. People don't usually seek help for it on their own, because those are not symptoms they want to change; most of the cases she saw were forced into therapy.
- **Leon, how did you know to go to a doctor and get checked?** Leon: in the military, after retrieving the bodies of sailors who were killed, they had to see a psychiatrist to decompress. He had no feelings about the deaths and thought that was odd; that's where it started.
- **Joy, tell us about the abuse and your recovery.** Joy: it starts small, the constant belittling and devaluing, making you feel you're the problem, even telling you you're bipolar, until he almost took her life and the kids called the police. Recovery was a long journey, and the children are still recovering from his abandonment. He was never diagnosed; she examines the fruit of the relationship, the evidence, not the emotion.
- **What is idealization, devaluation and discard?** Diana: it doesn't start off bad, or the average person would run. It's a ritual of making you comfortable, learning your weaknesses and strengths, picking away at your strengths so you don't have the power to leave, and feeding on you until nothing is left. Once they no longer have control, they lose interest.
- **Leon, are you still in therapy?** Leon: therapy for narcissistic personality disorder is indefinite and he has access to it for life. There are no pills for NPD, only talk therapy, with constructive criticism, homework and reflecting, so a lot of people run from it; he left therapy twice and went back. He was abused as a child, which led to anger, addictions and wanting to hurt people, and he became verbally abusive to his own children.
- **Are black men really more likely to be narcissists?** Diana: black men are overdiagnosed with severe mental illness because of laziness and a lack of awareness of cultural norms; what presents differently in us can be post-traumatic stress or depression.
- **Is it true there's no cure?** Diana: there's no cure for psychopathy, which is different. With narcissism a person can come to understand how they've harmed people and work on impulse control and changing their behavior, usually when the behaviors no longer serve them. Leon: he changed when he found a therapist who told him he didn't have to be the big bad leader in her office.
