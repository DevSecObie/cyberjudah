## Introduction

*[[0:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3ONy4yAFek&t=14s)]* The Revolutionaries of Gad, a short series from IUIC Raleigh, on Crazy Horse: the legendary Lakota Sioux leader and warrior who resisted the United States government's push to put the so-called Native American Indians on reservations, and defeated Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer at the Battle of the Greasy Grass, the Little Bighorn, June 25 and 26, 1876. The Gadites were men of war fit for the battle, and that is exactly what Crazy Horse was.

## Scriptures Opened

**[1 Chronicles 12:8](/bible/1-chronicles/12#v8)**  *[[1:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3ONy4yAFek&t=77s)]*

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-chronicles/12#v8)</sup> And of the Gadites there separated themselves unto David into the hold to the wilderness men of might, and men of war fit for the battle, that could handle shield and buckler, whose faces were like the faces of lions, and were as swift as the roes upon the mountains;

- Men of war fit for the battle: the Gadites were always ready for war, and that's exactly what Crazy Horse was.

- Whose faces were like the faces of lions: they wore war paint, and every paint meant something different. As swift as the roes upon the mountains: a roe is a deer, and deer run up to 30 miles an hour; they were elusive runners and very good at riding horses.

- At the Greasy Grass Crazy Horse led a coalition of the Lakota, the Cheyenne and the Arapaho against the Seventh Cavalry. The battle sparked because the US government encroached on native lands after discovering gold in the Black Hills, the sacred region of the Lakota.


**[1 Chronicles 5:18](/bible/1-chronicles/5#v18)**  *[[3:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3ONy4yAFek&t=194s)]*

> <sup>[18](/bible/1-chronicles/5#v18)</sup> The sons of Reuben, and the Gadites, and half the tribe of Manasseh, of valiant men, men able to bear buckler and sword, and to shoot with bow, and skilful in war, were four and forty thousand seven hundred and threescore, that went out to the war.

- The Gadites, men able to bear buckler and sword, and to shoot with bow, and skilful in war: y'all can't tell me that ain't the Native American Indians today.

- Crazy Horse was a tactical genius with innovative military strategies; he killed over 260 of the government's soldiers and Custer himself, one of the greatest battles in Native American history. He will be remembered for his bravery, resilience and loyalty to the Lakota and to the Native Americans, who are the tribe of Gad.
