Reading from Origins of the American Indians: Acosta changes the pattern  *[[2:51:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4vadD-QENH4&t=10282s)]*

> Europeans made surprisingly little headway before 1589 in their attempts to discover the origins of the Indians. Their attempts consisted of noting certain cultural affinities, dress, speech, manners, religion, between the Americans and some Old World people of antiquity, and then inventing a mode of transporting that people to America. Joseph de Acosta changed that pattern.
>
> Acosta had already expressed his disdain for those who argued that the name Peru derived from Ophir, or Yucatan from Joktan. Furthermore, he said, I am not convinced that the first Indians came to this New World as a result of purposeful navigation, nor do I wish to concede that the ancients had developed the art of navigation whereby men of this day pass from place to place on the ocean. Nor do I find evidence of so notable a voyage in all antiquity.
>
> Since the beasts could not have swum the broad ocean, and since they probably did not come in the ships with men, how did they get to America? Acosta was forced by these considerations to conjecture that the New World we call the Indies is not completely divided and separated from the other world, and that in the undiscovered north or south the two worlds had a land connection.

- Everyone else said Israelites, Carthaginians, Canaanites; he said they weren't smart enough to have ships. Some Israelites today say the same nonsense, that they only had rowboats.

> Acosta contemptuously dismissed the Atlantis origin theory. Acosta was equally disdainful of the supposed Jewish origin of the Indians. True, both Indians and Jews were fearful, submissive, ceremonious and deceitful, but the Hebrews possessed writing and the Indians did not. The Hebrews also loved silver, but the Indians did not. The Hebrews practiced circumcision, but Acosta knew of no Indians who did so. If the Indians were Jewish, they had forgotten their lineage, their law, their ceremonies, their Messiah and their entire Judaism.
>
> Acosta thought that the Indians came to the New World little by little, and that they came by land or across a narrow strait. The first to have come, a few thousand years ago, would have been savage hunters, driven by hunger or loss of homeland.
