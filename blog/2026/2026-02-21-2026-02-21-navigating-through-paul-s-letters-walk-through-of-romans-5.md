## Introduction

*[[5:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OKnkK-FyKyQ&t=331s)]* Navigating through Paul's letters: a walk through Romans 5, the third lesson in a series after Acts 10 and Romans 11, and the Christians' last safe haven. Paul writes to the saints at Rome, Jews who know the law, citing nothing but Moses and the prophets. Romans 4 sets up Abraham, whose faith came before his sacrifice, and the works of the law are temple sacrifice, a debt that ends when the temple falls. Romans 5 then says we are justified by faith and reconciled by Christ's blood: justification, atonement, and reconciliation are one word, at-one-ment, the two kingdoms brought back to God again, which is impossible for nations that were never with him. Isaiah 53 names whose offenses Christ bore, my people; Romans 3 proves both Jews and Gentiles under sin from the Old Testament, where only Israel was under the law; and 2 Esdras 3 gives the origin of sin from Adam through the righteous line that God foreknew and predestinated to be justified and glorified.

- Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin, and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned. Sin and death are synonymous, the wages of sin. Yes, all nations are capable of sinning through Adam; the question is who God held accountable. For until the law sin was in the world, from Adam to Moses, but sin is not imputed, charged, when there is no law: Hebrews 9:22, almost all things are by the law purged with blood, and without shedding of blood is no remission, so sin was only imputed where there was a sacrifice. Hebrews 10:18: where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin, no more animals once Christ died. Romans 4:15: the law worketh wrath, death, and where no law, no sacrifice, is, there is no transgression.

- Nevertheless death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over them that had not sinned after the similitude of Adam's transgression, who is the figure of him that was to come. Adam was a similitude of Christ, but not as the offence: Adam brought the offence and Christ brought the opposite, the free gift. If through the offence of one many be dead, paradise removed and the flood, much more the grace of God by one man, Jesus Christ, hath abounded unto many. They parallel: one brought death upon the chosen nation and one brought life upon it.

- The judgment was by one to condemnation, but the free gift is of many offences unto justification: whose offences? Israel's, Romans 4:25, our offences and our justification; my righteous servant shall justify many. Paul is giving the origin story of sin, how it killed off the chosen people who always came back to the commandments, and why Christ had to come and die for that same chosen line. As by the offence of one judgment came upon all men to condemnation, even so by the righteousness of one the free gift came upon all men unto justification of life; as by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous. Acts 13:38-39: by him all that believe are justified from all things, from which ye could not be justified by the law of Moses, since almost all things were purged with blood and Christ's death purges everything.

- Moreover the law entered, that the offence might abound: the law was introduced so sin could be understood and exposed, thou shalt not covet, thou shalt not commit adultery, to guide a naturally sinful people. But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound: keeping the commandments we realize how much we rise, fall, and stumble and how much more grace we need than under Moses, where animals could not atone for everything. That as sin hath reigned unto death, even so might grace reign through righteousness unto eternal life by Jesus Christ our Lord.

## Class Questions

- **Who is the letter to the Romans addressed to?** The saints at Rome, Romans 1:7, and the saints are the children of Israel, Psalms 148:14: Jews who rest in the law, Romans 2:17 and 7:1, with northern-kingdom Gentiles among them. Other nations are never called saints.
- **What was Paul's source material?** None other things than those which the prophets and Moses did say, Acts 26:22, teaching out of the law and the prophets, Acts 28:23. To understand Paul you must understand the other scriptures Peter mentioned.
- **Why is Abraham our father not by faith?** Our father as pertaining to the flesh, Romans 4:1, means by ancestry. All nations do not descend from Abraham, so Paul cannot be writing to all nations.
- **What came first for Abraham, faith or works?** Faith. In Genesis 15 he believed the promise of an heir and of the land and it was counted for righteousness before he was told to divide the heifer, goat, ram, and birds. The works followed the faith.
- **What are the works, and what is the debt, in Romans 4:2-4?** Temple sacrifice, the offerings that made atonement for Israel, Nehemiah 10:33. Under sacrifice you were indebted to offer an animal or die without mercy, Hebrews 10:28, and once the temple fell that debt condemned you.
- **Whose offenses was Christ delivered for?** Our offenses, Romans 4:25: he was wounded for our transgressions and cut off out of the land of the living for the transgression of my people, Isaiah 53:5-8, cut off but not for himself, Daniel 9:26. In the Lord shall all the seed of Israel be justified, Isaiah 45:25.
- **What do justification, atonement, and reconciliation mean?** The same thing: at-one-ment, being brought back together with God again, as the priests made reconciliation with the goats' blood to make an atonement for all Israel, 2 Chronicles 29:24. Christ's blood replaced the animals.
- **Why does the word reconcile destroy Christianity?** To reconcile is to come together again. Israel was God's people and became his enemy, Isaiah 63:10, and Christ's death brought both kingdoms back to him in one body, Ephesians 2:14-16. The nations were never with God, so they cannot be reconciled to him.
- **Who are the Jews and Gentiles that are all under sin in Romans 3:9?** Judah and Ephraim, both kingdoms proven wicked from Psalms 14, Psalms 10, Isaiah 59, and Psalms 36. Whatever the law saith, it saith to them under the law, Romans 3:19, and no nation by name was ever under the covenant with Israel.
- **What does by the law is the knowledge of sin mean?** To acknowledge a sin, willful or ignorant, you had to bring an animal, Romans 3:20. Sin is not imputed when there is no law, Romans 5:13, because almost all things are purged with blood, Hebrews 9:22.
- **What is the reparation and the breach?** The root of reparations is repair. Thy breach is great like the sea, Lamentations 2:13; thou shalt be called the repairer of the breach, Isaiah 58:12; I will raise up the tabernacle of David and close up the breaches thereof, Amos 9:11, and Israel possesses the remnant of Edom, not the reverse.
- **How is Adam the figure of him that was to come?** Adam is a similitude of Christ, but not as the offence, Romans 5:14-15: by Adam's offence many are dead, and by Christ's righteousness the free gift of many offences unto justification came upon the same chosen line.
- **What does 2 Esdras 3 add to Romans 5?** The origin of sin: Adam's wicked heart passed to all his generations, of whom came all nations, but God dealt only with the righteous line of Noah, Abraham, and Jacob, gave the law to the seed of Jacob without taking away the wicked heart, and destroyed and restored them in a repeated cycle.
- **Whom did God foreknow and predestinate?** Israel: you only have I known of all the families of the earth, Amos 3:2; whom he foreknew he predestinated, called, justified, and glorified, Romans 8:29-30; chosen in him before the foundation of the world unto the adoption, Ephesians 1:4-5; the elect according to the foreknowledge of God, 1 Peter 1:2.
- **Why did the law enter, and where does grace abound?** The law entered that the offence might abound, so sin could be exposed and understood, Romans 5:20. Keeping it we see how much more grace we need than animals could give, and grace reigns through righteousness unto eternal life.
