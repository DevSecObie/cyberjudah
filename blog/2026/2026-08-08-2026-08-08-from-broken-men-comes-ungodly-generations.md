## Introduction

A morning class taught by Captain Noah. It opens with Psalms 51 read in full and prayer for the nation and for vengeance on those who hunt our steps.

The subject is the line running from a broken man to the generation that comes out of him, and the argument is that the Bible already contains the fix. Some of us have been sitting with it and have still not taken the medicine, and are running the trauma back instead. The class works through what shaped us, the imagery, the media, the neglect, and then through what the scriptures require of a parent: instruction, correction, repetition and patience.

## In The News

The clips shown and answered:

- The question of which generation is worse, the yins of the late nineties or the ones now. The answer is that this generation did not invent any of it. They did not create the Crips or the Bloods or MS-13. The weapons upgraded from bats and knives to what they carry now, but our parents said the same things about us in the eighties and the nineties.
- Images and self-esteem, from Tarzan to Superman to a pope commissioning a white Christ in 1505. Free three hundred slaves but do not teach them who they are, control their history and their images, and you will not have to watch them. Whoever controls the mind controls the body.
- How media trains desire. What you see repeatedly becomes normal, what is normal becomes desirable, desire drives spending. And nobody was ever taught to separate their own preferences from a repeated pattern.
- A woman asking why she cannot find one show that portrays our community well. We had Martin, Living Single, Sister Sister, A Different World with black people in college. Now it is dealers and murderers, or reality television built on women fighting each other.
- The man who described his mother teaching him to steal at nine years old, sending him and his brothers into houses and waiting around the corner with the hatchback open outside the shoe store. He got older and worked out that stealing is cowardly, which is why it always involves sneaking and running, and he had to unlearn what the person he trusted most had taught him.
- The mother who realised she was raising hard, independent daughters and letting her sons do whatever, and so was producing strong daughters and weak men. You are raising boys into men you would not want to be with, and girls to end up where you are.
- The material on neglect and the developing brain: a child whose cries go unanswered learns that its needs do not matter, and the inner critic is not abstract, it is neurons rehearsing words once spoken by somebody else. And the point that mattered most for this class: the brain stays plastic. What was written in childhood is powerful and it is not the final draft.
- Gen Z as the first generation in modern history to underperform their parents on nearly every cognitive measure despite more schooling, tracking with the adoption of digital tools in classrooms across eighty countries.

> <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/3#v12)</sup> My son, help thy father in his age, and grieve him not as long as he liveth.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/3#v13)</sup> And if his understanding fail, have patience with him; and despise him not when thou art in thy full strength.

- My son, help thy father in his age, and grieve him not as long as he liveth. And if his understanding fail, have patience with him. Everything you built in a child comes back to you, and the older he gets the more he can carry.

**[Romans 5:3-5](/bible/romans/5#v3)**  *[2:24:18]*

> <sup>[3](/bible/romans/5#v3)</sup> And not only so, but we glory in tribulations also: knowing that tribulation worketh patience;
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/romans/5#v4)</sup> And patience, experience; and experience, hope:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/romans/5#v5)</sup> And hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us.

- We glory in tribulations also, knowing that tribulation worketh patience, and patience experience, and experience hope.

- Patience comes first, and it has no time limit. Parents say they should not have to repeat themselves. Consider how many times the Most High has told us to keep the commandments and live, and how many times we heard it and did not. Then reckon what patience he is carrying with us, and give your children some of the same.

- And repetition is how anything is learned. Not volume. Louder is not learned. Ask instead what structure exists in the house to support it: is it written down, is there a routine, is there accountability, or is the whole thing resting on your voice.

**[Romans 12:1-2](/bible/romans/12#v1)**  *[2:28:37]*

> <sup>[1](/bible/romans/12#v1)</sup> I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/romans/12#v2)</sup> And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.

- Present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.

- And be not conformed to this world, but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind. Everything a man learned in the world about what a man is, he does not actually know, at fifty or at eighty. The renewing is where he becomes one, and the proof of it is when he can teach it to somebody else.

**[Romans 13:1-4](/bible/romans/13#v1)**  *[2:30:31]*

> <sup>[1](/bible/romans/13#v1)</sup> Let every soul be subject unto the higher powers. For there is no power but of God: the powers that be are ordained of God.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/romans/13#v2)</sup> Whosoever therefore resisteth the power, resisteth the ordinance of God: and they that resist shall receive to themselves damnation.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/romans/13#v3)</sup> For rulers are not a terror to good works, but to the evil. Wilt thou then not be afraid of the power? do that which is good, and thou shalt have praise of the same:
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/romans/13#v4)</sup> For he is the minister of God to thee for good. But if thou do that which is evil, be afraid; for he beareth not the sword in vain: for he is the minister of God, a revenger to execute wrath upon him that doeth evil.

- Let every soul be subject unto the higher powers, for there is no power but of God, the powers that be are ordained of God. The father and the mother set in the household are set there by him, and so is the order above that.

- Whosoever therefore resisteth the power, resisteth the ordinance of God.

- For he is the minister of God to thee for good, but if thou do that which is evil, be afraid, for he beareth not the sword in vain. That is the point the whole class ends on. Everyone will be subject to somebody, and it had better be you. If a child is not learning from his parents, the Lord will choose who teaches him, and it will be the sword.

## In Closing

Broken men produce ungodly generations, and the Bible was written down so the chain could be seen and cut. Ezekiel says the son who sees all his father's sins and does not do the same shall not die for the iniquity of his father. That verse is the reason anybody is sitting in these seats.

What was done to us is real. The image was taken, the history was taken, and the media has been training desire for as long as any of us have been alive. But Hosea says the land mourns when there is no knowledge in it, and Psalms asks who will rise up against the evildoers. Complaining about a generation is not an answer to that question.

So the work is at home and it is unglamorous. Instruct rather than tell, and check that the instruction was understood. Correct while they are young and keep the correction righteous. Give no liberty in the youth and do not wink at the small things, and have the conversation before it ever needs to be anything else. Then have patience with the repeating, because repetition is how anybody learns anything, and because that is precisely the patience the Most High has been carrying with us the whole time.
