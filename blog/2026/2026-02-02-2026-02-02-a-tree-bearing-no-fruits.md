## Introduction

*[[7:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6Pzne8A1Nww&t=433s)]* A New Moon night class from a deacon on the seed, the tree and the fruit. Starting from the law against sowing a vineyard with divers seeds, the deacon uses Malcolm X's own explanation of why a movement grows only when the season of oppression makes minds fertile, then shows the same principle in Isaiah 30: affliction is the season in which God sends teachers and rain. The word of God is the seed, the teachers water it, God gives the increase, and every tree that grows from it is expected to bear the fruits of the Spirit. A fig tree with leaves and no fruit gets cut down.

## In The News

- *[[14:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6Pzne8A1Nww&t=888s)]* **Malcolm X, asked why the Nation of Islam grew so fast after thirty-three years on Harlem street corners with nobody listening, answers that a seed stays beneath the soil until the season changes, and that political, social and economic conditions had made Black minds fertile for the seed that had been planted for decades.** — The Nation of Islam is idolatry, but the analogy holds: the seed only springs up in its season, and that season is oppression. The camp has taught in the street for years without yet seeing the abundance, and now Trump's America, the marching Nazis and the detentions are making our people's minds receptive.

- Every good tree bringeth forth good fruit, but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit; a brother who keeps doing evil and does not repent is a corrupt tree. Every tree that bringeth not forth good fruit is hewn down and cast into the fire, which is damnation. Examine yourself, and understand why the deacon keeps his distance from some who bring forth evil fruit unrepented. Not every one that saith Lord, Lord shall enter the kingdom.

- I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman. Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away, and every branch that beareth fruit he purgeth, that it may bring forth more fruit. Pruning a tomato plant, removing dead branches and extra leaves, sends more water and nutrients to the fruit; God prunes you with trials, tribulation and correction because he wants more fruit from a branch that is already bearing.

- As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself except it abide in the vine, no more can ye except ye abide in me. You cannot grow fruit by yourself; Christ has to be in you. If you are not changing, maybe the Lord's spirit is not in you. Without me ye can do nothing: even the growth of IUIC is the Lord working through us, so the glory is his.

- If a man abide not in me, he is cast forth as a branch and is withered, and men gather them and cast them into the fire. Dead branches are firewood. Those who walk out and go back to Christianity complaining about the Sabbath wither because Christ never dwelt in them. If ye abide in me, ask what ye will and it shall be done: herein is my Father glorified, that ye bear much fruit, so shall ye be my disciples.

- How do you know you have the fruit of the Spirit unless you share it? Love God and love your neighbour; the fruit is proved in keeping the unity of the brotherhood through whatever you go through, not in running at the first trouble.


## Class Questions

- **What is the seed, what is the water, and what is the fruit?** The seed is the word of God sown in a person, Isaiah 55:10-11 and Luke 8:11. The water is the teaching that follows, 1 Corinthians 3:6, and the rain in Joel 2 is the spirit of Christ poured out. The fruit is the fruit of the Spirit in Galatians 5:22-23 and 2 Peter 1:5-7, which God, not the planter or the waterer, gives as the increase.
- **Why does the deacon say now is the season to plant?** Isaiah 30:20-21 says that in the bread of adversity and the water of affliction the teachers will not be hidden any more and the people will see them and hear the way. Malcolm X described the same thing: a seed springs up only when conditions change. Trump's America and the rising oppression are making our people's minds fertile.
- **How can too much rain corrupt the seed?** 2 Esdras 8:43 says too much rain corrupts the husbandman's seed, and 1 Timothy 3:6 shows it: a novice lifted up with pride falls into the condemnation of the devil. Too much wisdom too soon puffs a new brother up. That is why Joel 2:23 says the former rain is given moderately, level by level.
- **What happens to a tree in the truth that bears no fruit?** Christ cursed the fig tree with leaves and no fruit and it withered, Matthew 21:19. In Luke 13 the owner orders the three-year fruitless tree cut down, and the dresser wins it one more year of digging and dung, the teaching you are receiving now. John 15:6 says the branch that does not abide is cast forth, withered, and burned.
