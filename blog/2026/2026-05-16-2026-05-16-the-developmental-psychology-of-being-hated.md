## Introduction

Shalom, my brothers and sisters. We are here in Miami, Florida. In this truth, those of us in leadership come across members of our congregation that suffer many different things, and our job is to help them. Our job is to administer the medicine of the Bible to them, to clean them up. None of us come into this truth because we are whole; people that are whole do not need a physician. Going back many years in New York, at the end of The Book of Our Fathers we would put up the stencil that said the Bible has the solution to our people's problems. That is the reason we still use it today. The Bible is the medicine cabinet: all of the ingredients of the healing are in this chest. We as the doctors, teachers, guides, and leaders assess, counsel, and as we hear the situations it is up to us to go inside the medicine cabinet and find the scriptures that deal with the problems, just like a doctor would do. We are dealing with a whole nation of people that are sick, spiritually sick and psychologically sick.

Some subjects get much attention, and there are other subjects that do not get quite so much. This class deals with the ones we do not talk about a lot: the developmental psychology of being hated, and the escapes our people run to, especially alcohol. It is going to be ugly in places, but there is a way out, and we are going to see it in the scriptures.
