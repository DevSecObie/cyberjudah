## Introduction

*[[8:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g3Yh2GSq8E4&t=530s)]* Part two of the Dividing Hatred series, following the class on Cornelius the centurion. A deacon, with Deacon Malachi beside him, works through the stumbling block that Romans 11 has become in Israel: who the Gentiles, the strangers and the wild olive tree really are. The class starts from the command to love your brother, traces how Ephraim became 'not a people' in Hosea and Isaiah, shows the builders appointed to reject the stone so Christ could die for both houses, and ends in Ephesians 2, where the aliens and strangers who were far off are made nigh and the two are made one new man.

You can only graft olive into olive, not a mango into an olive; the branches are the same kind.

## Class Questions

- **Who are 'not a people' and the 'foolish nation' of Deuteronomy 32:21?** The northern kingdom. Isaiah 7:8 says Ephraim would be broken 'that it be not a people,' Hosea 1 names them Lo-ammi, and Sirach 50:26 calls the people of Shechem 'no nation' and 'that foolish people.' Paul quotes the same verse in Romans 10:19 before Romans 11.
- **Who is the wild olive tree graffed in, in Romans 11?** Ephraim. Jeremiah 11:16 calls both houses God's olive tree, and Hosea calls Ephraim wild; nowhere are the nations called olive trees. 'Graffed in again' and 'reconciling' both mean bringing back what was once there, and the chapter concludes 'and so all Israel shall be saved.'
- **Why did the builders have to reject Christ?** 1 Peter 2:8 says they were appointed to stumble. Psalm 118 calls it the Lord's doing: their rejection led to his death, and his death is what reconciled the northern kingdom, far off, with Judah, nigh, as Caiaphas prophesied in John 11:52 and Ephesians 2 records.
- **What does hating your brother have to do with understanding these scriptures?** 1 John 2:11 says he that hateth his brother walks in darkness that has blinded his eyes. The rulers who hated the Samaritans stumbled at the stone, and Israelites today who work to save their oppressors instead of their people stumble at Cornelius and Romans 11 the same way.
