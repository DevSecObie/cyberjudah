## Introduction

*[[4:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CiL1d9RUSfE&t=299s)]* This beginner's class addresses listeners with a Muslim or Nation of Islam background. Captain Yahn traces the connection he sees between earlier movements for Black identity and contemporary Israelite teaching, then turns to family life, outreach, and patient persuasion.

These notes document the class's religious argument in paraphrase. Its claims about modern ancestry, other religions, historical influence, and prophecy are the speakers' interpretations, not independently established conclusions. Broad accusations about ethnic or religious groups are not adopted as facts. Scripture quotations are checked against the site's KJV and Apocrypha; book passages, clips, and songs are summarized rather than reproduced.

The service opens with Psalm 138 and prayer. Captain Hananiah and other guests participate. The introduction compares a new video with an older production from *The Truth Shall Make You Free*, illustrating the congregation's growth in media work. Brief comedy clips lead into a discussion of identity and the lesson's intended audience.

*[[1:06:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CiL1d9RUSfE&t=4000s)]* Captain Yahn describes how study changed his own attitude toward reading. He credits an elder's recommendation of *The Autobiography of Malcolm X* with encouraging further learning.

### Public figures, religious claims, and careful speech

*[[1:48:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CiL1d9RUSfE&t=6499s)]* The class discusses Khalid Abdul Muhammad, Louis Farrakhan, Professor Griff, and Public Enemy. Clips and on-screen summaries are used to describe their language about identity, their public influence, and controversies involving antisemitic, racist, or homophobic remarks.

The speaker argues that some of their ideas came through contact with Israelite teachers and recounts hearing older recordings. These influence claims remain attributed to the class. The notes do not endorse the prejudicial statements or calls for violence discussed in this segment.

*[[1:55:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CiL1d9RUSfE&t=6911s)]* The teacher contrasts speaking in one's own inflammatory language with citing scripture. Accurate study still requires distinguishing an ancient passage from a blanket accusation against a modern religious or ethnic group.

### The work of bringing people in

- The invitation into the streets and lanes becomes a practical model for outreach. The focus is bringing people in, rather than merely being seen teaching.

**[James 5:20](/bible/james/5#v20)**  *[[2:04:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CiL1d9RUSfE&t=7453s)]*

> <sup>[20](/bible/james/5#v20)</sup> Let him know, that he which converteth the sinner from the error of his way shall save a soul from death, and shall hide a multitude of sins.

- Helping someone turn from error is presented as the purpose of the work. Members are encouraged to see ordinary acts of sharing and teaching as consequential.

> <sup>[17](/bible/psalms/73#v17)</sup> Until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end.

- The teacher contrasts outward prosperity with understanding its limits. He applies entering the sanctuary to learning in the congregation.

**[Isaiah 42:22](/bible/isaiah/42#v22)**  *[[2:07:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CiL1d9RUSfE&t=7622s)]*

- The class extends the outreach discussion to people in prison and other difficult circumstances. Restoration, rather than neglect, is the practical theme.

- The class uses the images of a stronghold, bow, and sword to describe its mission. The speaker explicitly identifies the weapon in his application as biblical teaching, not physical weapons.

**[Jeremiah 1:5](/bible/jeremiah/1#v5)**  *[[2:10:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CiL1d9RUSfE&t=7827s)]*

> <sup>[5](/bible/jeremiah/1#v5)</sup> Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations.

- The teacher applies the idea of a calling from the womb to members who began studying at different ages. He argues that arriving later in life need not make a person useless.

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/49#v1)</sup> Listen, O isles, unto me; and hearken, ye people, from far; The Lord hath called me from the womb; from the bowels of my mother hath he made mention of my name.

> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/49#v2)</sup> And he hath made my mouth like a sharp sword; in the shadow of his hand hath he hid me, and made me a polished shaft; in his quiver hath he hid me;

- The sharp sword and hidden arrow are applied to words used in teaching. The class asks listeners to consider how their abilities can serve others.

**[Proverbs 11:30](/bible/proverbs/11#v30)**  *[[2:14:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CiL1d9RUSfE&t=8070s)]*

> <sup>[30](/bible/proverbs/11#v30)</sup> The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life; and he that winneth souls is wise.

- The corrected reference is verse 30, not verse 21. Winning souls is the purpose assigned to the preceding weapon imagery.

- The teacher emphasizes the possibility of helping another person change rather than winning personal recognition.

**[Luke 15:7,10](/bible/luke/15#v7)**  *[[2:15:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CiL1d9RUSfE&t=8126s)]*

> <sup>[7](/bible/luke/15#v7)</sup> I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/luke/15#v10)</sup> Likewise, I say unto you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner that repenteth.

- Joy over repentance reinforces the value of helping even one person. The two verses are read separately in the recording.

**[Proverbs 25:15](/bible/proverbs/25#v15)**  *[[2:17:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CiL1d9RUSfE&t=8239s)]*

> <sup>[15](/bible/proverbs/25#v15)</sup> By long forbearing is a prince persuaded, and a soft tongue breaketh the bone.

- The final practical teaching centers on patience and gentle persuasion. The speakers ask whether a teacher is trying to win an argument or help a person.

- Listen, recognize timing, and do not take a rejected flyer personally. A person may be willing to listen later.

- The speakers caution against chasing someone who wants to leave or overwhelming a listener with more references. Give space, trust the process, and remain open to another conversation.

## In Closing

*[[2:30:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CiL1d9RUSfE&t=9044s)]* The class closes with the thought that faithful work continues to matter even after a person's death. The concluding appeal is for repentance and useful service. Its most concrete teaching on outreach is to listen patiently, avoid coercive argument, and keep the other person's response in view.

### Review and application

1. Which passage is actually read when the captions say Amos 8:1? **Amos 8:11–12, the famine of hearing the word.**
2. Which passage describes responsibilities within the household? **Colossians 3:18–21.**
3. What is the stated purpose of the weapon imagery in the outreach section? **Teaching that helps people change, rather than physical violence.**
4. How does Proverbs 25:15 guide outreach? **Use patience, listen, and persuade without turning the encounter into a contest.**
5. What distinction should be maintained when studying the historical and prophetic sections? **Separate the source text from the teacher's modern identification, recollection, or prediction.**
