## Introduction

A Sabbath class taught by Bishop Nathaniel with Captain Yuri reading, the first Sabbath back from the men's conference in London. It opens with Psalms 138 read in full and prayer for the sick, for the brothers travelling to wake the twelve tribes, and for judgment on the enemies of the nation.

The class begins with a definition. In the Bible the word prison is used two ways. Literally, of a place where people were held: Joseph, Jeremiah, John the Baptist, Paul. Figuratively, of spiritual bondage, oppression or slavery, of being under the dominion of another people, of being trapped by sin, fear, suffering or disobedience.

Everything after that works the second meaning. Deuteronomy 28 says the yoke of iron stays on the neck until the people are destroyed, and the argument is that the literal yoke came off precisely because the mental one was secure. From there the class runs through the psychological record of that destruction, then through the political prisoners nobody talks about, then through Lamentations 3 and Isaiah 14, and finally to the promise: that thou mayest say to the prisoners, Go forth.

The definition set at the top does all the work. Prison in this book is literal and it is figurative, and the figurative one is the one holding our people. Deuteronomy 28 says the yoke of iron stays until the destruction is complete, and Isaiah 14 says of the same power that it opened not the house of his prisoners. The bars came off. The house never opened.

What follows from that is the diagnosis and the instruction. The psychological record read out at the start is not a modern complaint, it is the curse itemised, and every man in the room came in carrying part of it. Lamentations says let us search and try our ways, and that is where anything begins, because the prayer that never gets past the cloud is the one offered without the examining. Then separate, as Nehemiah's people did and as Paul repeats: come out from among them, be ye separate, touch not the unclean thing.

And the promise is that the door is already open. Isaiah 42 says he called us to bring out the prisoners from the prison and them that sit in darkness out of the prison house. Isaiah 49 says, say to the prisoners, Go forth. Zechariah calls us prisoners of hope and sends us to the stronghold. The gorilla in the cage stood at a line that was no longer there, and would not cross it until somebody backed up and called him out. That is the work.

Fear none of those things which thou shalt suffer. Some will be cast into prison, and some will not be delivered out of it. Be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life.
