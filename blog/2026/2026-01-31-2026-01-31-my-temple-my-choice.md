## Introduction

*[[7:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0nTb2YB-Tns&t=476s)]* Captain Gideon teaches that keeping the dietary law means more than avoiding the unclean animals of Leviticus 11. Overeating, constant snacking and a diet built on sugar are sins against the body, and the class names them plainly. Those who call such teaching bullying are the people Isaiah describes, who want smooth things. The class moves from Deuteronomy 30:19, choose life, through the case against pork and bottom feeders, to Sirach 37 and 31 on excess and moderate eating, and ends with exercise, raising children to eat well, and asking God for the spirit of discipline.

## Class Questions

- **Does 1 Timothy 4 make every creature clean to eat?** No. Verse 3 says the meats are for them which believe and know the truth, and the truth is the law (Psalms 119:142). The meat is sanctified by the word, so only what the law calls clean is received with thanksgiving.
- **Is avoiding pork enough to keep the dietary law?** No. Sirach 37 warns against being unsatiable in any dainty thing and says excess of meats brings sickness. Overeating and constant snacking break the law too.
- **Why is rebuking overeating not bullying?** The watchman must warn the wicked (Ezekiel 3:17). Those who want only smooth things are the rebellious people of Isaiah 30, and their breach comes suddenly.
- **What does Scripture say about eating and sleep?** Sirach 31 says sound sleep cometh of moderate eating, and the moderate eater rises early with his wits about him.
- **How do parents pass on bad eating?** As is the mother, so is her daughter (Ezekiel 16:44). Children eat what is in the house, so parents must train them in the right way (Proverbs 22:6).

## In Closing

*[[2:12:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0nTb2YB-Tns&t=7972s)]* The holy spirit of discipline will not stay with a man who keeps sinning. Stop overeating, stop snacking, fast, exercise and take care of your temple, because the nation needs healthy laborers.
