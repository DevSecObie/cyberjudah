## Introduction

Thumbnail video: "Growing up, I thought this was normal. Partying, women, and drugs. This was life. Look where it got me. Ain't nothing working. Seems like can't nobody save me. What am I supposed to do?"

It has been a while since the Bishop has been back in New York, back at the headquarters. Today is going to be an interesting class. It is going to be different: no historical context or anything like that. Today is going to be looking in the mirror at our people, at what they call community. Some of you are going to like it and you are going to change. Some of you are going to get mad. The name of the class is The Negro Community, a Wasteland of Tears.

The question to start with: what is needed for any community to thrive and to survive? Read from the screen, the basics: water supply and wastewater management; energy systems; food systems and local food production; housing and shelter; sanitation and hygiene services; transportation networks; communications and information systems; health care facilities and public health; education, skills development, and community services; public safety, emergency response, and governance. Notice a community does not just have homes; it has hospitals, businesses, and services. Think about the black and Latino community: some of those things we do not have. Then the question was asked, why do other races control what is important for the black community to survive? The answer read from the screen spoke of history and power, structural bias privileging the perspectives of the more powerful groups, and representation. If representation were true in black communities, we would have our own hospitals run by us, our own stores, not other races controlling our food intake and our health services. The black community lacks a large part of what was listed there, and one hundred percent of it is run by others. We do not run anything in the black community. Based on what a community consists of, the black community is a wasteland.

Clips were played from the sit down this week where Captain Mattathias, Captain Galayah, and Captain Zakar sat down with Fred Price Jr. On salvation: the pastor said works and deeds cannot save, only belief. The captains answered that this is the reason the black and brown communities are full of crime: people are taught they will be saved with no works toward Christ and no charity toward their neighbor. And when we say you still have to keep the commandments with the faith in Christ, they think we are talking about animal sacrifice. We are not: Christ took the place of the law of sacrifice; we are talking about the commandments of God, and even the ceremonial laws, the feast days, which are celebrations of the salvation we received in our captivities. The interview shows you the confusion in the black Christian church; they lack the learning to articulate what the scriptures are talking about.

My spirit that is upon thee, and my words which I have put in thy mouth, shall not depart out of thy mouth, nor out of the mouth of thy seed, nor out of the mouth of thy seed's seed, saith the Lord, from henceforth and for ever.

If we do not get rid of the sin, we can never be that proper city the Lord is building where the light shineth. We have to learn how to apply God's commandments; that is how we build that city and grow like that mustard seed into great righteousness.
