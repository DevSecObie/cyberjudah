## Introduction

Shalom, Israel. Most High and Christ bless. Happy Sabbath. I know it is bright and early, but it is time to get it in; we got classes all day. The Captain brings the class from his phone after his laptop with the whole class on it went down: it seems like when I go live, something bad happens. That is not IT's fault; it is Satan, trying to get you off your grip. This is the opening session of the class; the teaching continues in the second session under the same title.

Today's topic: character and integrity, the hidden strength behind wisdom. We just finished Passover, and after Passover things get shaken up; that tree is shaking hard. Coming back from the Marriage is Honorable retreat, with the sit-downs every night around the leadership, one thing stood out: a lot of people lack integrity, and it shows in their character. Your character is the representation of you; that is what you are. Integrity is the action that fed that character. Real wisdom shows up in your character, your discipline, your honesty, your consistency, and how you move when nobody is watching. Wisdom leaves its footprints: you see a brother has wisdom and discernment based off how he moves, how he deals with people, how he deals with his wife and children, and how he takes correction.

A person can know the scriptures, pull them left and right, and still have terrible character: secretly battling pride and lust, a lying spirit, lazy, selfish, a hypocrite, bitterness. You know where the scriptures are, but you do not apply any of it. That shows you lack integrity and character.

Definitions read in class:

- Character fundamentally refers to the distinctive combination of mental, moral, and ethical traits that make an individual unique. In psychology and ethics, it is the aggregate of moral and ethical qualities, such as integrity, courage, and honesty, that dictate how a person thinks, feels, and behaves. Notice the first quality listed is integrity.
- Integrity is the quality of being honest and having strong moral or ethical principles. It involves aligning your actions with your values consistently, regardless of the circumstances or whether anyone is watching. Key aspect: consistency, practicing what you preach; your words and actions match up even when it is difficult or inconvenient.
- The difference: think of character as the sum of your moral habits and values, and integrity as the unwavering consistency of acting in alignment with those values, especially when it is difficult. When you get corrected, when it is not convenient, when your back is against the wall: that is when integrity kicks in.
- Integrity guides your life. Without integrity, emotions guide you; lust guides you if you are not disciplined; pride, anger, and impulse guide you. Integrity keeps you grounded.

If you missed the retreat sit-downs, you missed hundreds of years of wisdom that was right there in your face: how to deal with your marriage, how to deal with your family, how to grow as a man in this truth. Some brothers were there but were not there, because they did not want their character on blast. You have to be around these men to grow; your mind plays tricks on you, and the scripture says woe to him that is alone. God did not design us to be by ourselves; he set up leaders when we came out of Egypt and when Christ walked the earth. The integrity of the laws of the Most High is what separates us from the nations; the Most High has been trying to teach us integrity since creation, and the Christian church is fighting against it right now.

## Scriptures Opened

**[Proverbs 11:1-3](/bible/proverbs/11#v1)**  *[19:21]*

> <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/11#v1)</sup> A false balance is abomination to the Lord: but a just weight is his delight.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/11#v2)</sup> When pride cometh, then cometh shame: but with the lowly is wisdom.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/proverbs/11#v3)</sup> The integrity of the upright shall guide them: but the perverseness of transgressors shall destroy them.

- A false balance is abomination to the Lord: falsifying something is not good character. But a just weight is his delight. When pride cometh, then cometh shame: but with the lowly is wisdom.
- The integrity of the upright shall guide them: the upright is the person keeping the commandments, practicing what they preach. Your integrity is going to guide you into doing the right thing, no matter the circumstances.
- But the perverseness of transgressors shall destroy them: the person that lacks integrity and character is destroyed by that same lack.
- Discussion in class: a prison interview clip was recalled where a man convicted of multiple murders said he had no reason, felt nothing, and did not think he had any feelings of humanity, and that only a tenth of what he had done was ever found. That shows what the scripture says of the enemy: his soul is not upright in him. Many of the nations lack the ethics, empathy, and sympathy the Lord gave us; that is why we had the rule as a nation, because we showed the example of morals and ethics in the world. Without us in power, the world is in shambles.

Interactive question 1, put on the screen *[27:36]*: you are alone online late at night, and temptation hits; nobody from the congregation would know. What shows integrity? A, entertain the temptation privately. B, watch a little and justify it later. C, cut it off immediately and fight temptation. D, convince yourself grace covers everything.

- This is a moment of trial; this is what you call battling. You are fighting your war against yourself: you do not want to push play, but you want to push play, and nobody is there to tell you a thing.
- The answer is C. The questions are easy and the answers are easy; the problem is when it actually comes to fruition in real time. That is when C gets blurred out.
- Correct answer read: the real battlefield is private. A lot of people fall spiritually in secret long before they fall publicly. The representative shows up at the Sabbath; the real you shows up when you are at home, when you are alone, when you are with your family. Your wife knows exactly who you are. You cannot come up here saluting hard like you are over righteous and then go home and verbally, financially, or physically abuse your wife: you lack integrity and you have poor character. And vice versa, sisters: you cannot bow all the way down at the door and then go home and curse your husband out and keep nothing. You lack integrity in that area too.

  Precepts:
  - **[Romans 6:1](/bible/romans/6#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/romans/6#v1)</sup> What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound?

    Answer D is a scary mindset to have. What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? That is the mindset of the Christian church: I found grace, grace covers me, I can do what I want as long as I believe; I do not have to do what Christ says. That is a terrible mindset, and it is exactly what this verse is warning against.

> <sup>[11](/bible/proverbs/20#v11)</sup> Even a child is known by his doings, whether his work be pure, and whether it be right.

- Even a child is known by his doings, whether his work be pure, and whether it be right. Everybody is going to be accounted for their works, period. The works you choose to get into are dictated by the type of integrity and character you have: how you make decisions, how you give counsel, how you deal with your brother and sister.

**[1 Samuel 2:3](/bible/1-samuel/2#v3)**  *[35:21]*

> <sup>[3](/bible/1-samuel/2#v3)</sup> Talk no more so exceeding proudly; let not arrogancy come out of your mouth: for the Lord is a God of knowledge, and by him actions are weighed.

- Talk no more so exceeding proudly; let not arrogancy come out of your mouth: for the Lord is a God of knowledge, and by him actions are weighed.
- Your actions will be weighed. We are going to be judged off our actions, our works, period. You have to prove yourself to the Lord: that you are studying, the works you do in this body, constantly growing, constantly consistent.
- We are the representation of this truth. When they look at the Israelites as a face, who do they look toward? The brothers in the purple and gold, Israel United in Christ. Why? Because our leadership is heavy on integrity, character, structure, and order: traits we were never taught growing up and have to be taught now, around men who have built themselves up.

**[Psalms 15:1-5](/bible/psalms/15#v1)**  *[38:25]*

> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/15#v1)</sup> Lord, who shall abide in thy tabernacle? who shall dwell in thy holy hill?
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/15#v2)</sup> He that walketh uprightly, and worketh righteousness, and speaketh the truth in his heart.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/15#v3)</sup> He that backbiteth not with his tongue, nor doeth evil to his neighbour, nor taketh up a reproach against his neighbour.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/15#v4)</sup> In whose eyes a vile person is contemned; but he honoureth them that fear the Lord. He that sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/15#v5)</sup> He that putteth not out his money to usury, nor taketh reward against the innocent. He that doeth these things shall never be moved.

- Lord, who shall abide in thy tabernacle? Who shall dwell in thy holy hill? He that walketh uprightly, and worketh righteousness: that worketh is an action; you are putting your hand to the plow.
- And speaketh the truth in his heart: that means he is the same all the time; he shows the integrity behind his thoughts.
- He that backbiteth not with his tongue, nor doeth evil to his neighbour: a person working toward righteousness is not going to do evil to his neighbor, because he has great moral character. He honoureth them that fear the Lord; he that sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not. He that doeth these things shall never be moved.
- It comes with consistency, and it also comes with the company you keep. If you are the smartest person in the room, you are in the wrong room. I want to be around men I can learn from, men that will tell me, no, that is not right, that will tell me where I am in error. But a lot of you like yes men and yes women, because that gives you the opportunity for murmuring and backbiting, like the scripture just said. A lot of you are not putting in any action; you lack integrity.

Interactive question 2, put on the screen *[41:57]*: a brother corrects you and you feel embarrassed. What shows wisdom? A, get defensive immediately. B, talk about them afterward. C, examine yourself honestly. D, leave emotionally.

- You got corrected, and you were wrong. A lot of brothers shut down, or explode and cannot control their emotions, because they got corrected.
- The answer is C: examine yourself honestly. Everybody says C when they are looking at the screen, but when it actually happens in real time, C is blurred out; they do not even consider it.
- A, getting defensive: people do not like being told they are wrong, but how can you fix something if you are never corrected? B, talking about them afterward: taking the correction to the face, then going behind his back to minimize it as if the brother was out of the spirit. D, leaving emotionally: you have no emotional strength to deal with correction; that is that 2018 spirit; you cannot take it.
- Correct answer read: strong character can handle correction without collapsing emotionally. Pride hates correction, but wisdom loves instruction.
- At the Marriage is Honorable sit-downs we saw the same thing: really only one sister answered honestly. It was not correct, but she answered honestly, and we can deal with that; we can grow from that. A lot of people were being politically correct, and you could tell they were not answering right at all.
- Couples that went: keep the same energy you built down there. Do not come back and slide into the negative because you got the kids back; that is backtracking and not upholding your integrity.

You cannot keep the commandments with all your heart and have horrible character; it makes no sense.

## In Closing

The opening session lays the foundation: character is what you are, and integrity is the consistent action that builds it. Wisdom is hidden behind both; it shows up in your discipline, your honesty, and how you move when nobody is watching. Keep the commandments, love instruction, stay around men you can learn from, and let your integrity guide you. The class continues in the second session, going deeper into fixing the spirit.
