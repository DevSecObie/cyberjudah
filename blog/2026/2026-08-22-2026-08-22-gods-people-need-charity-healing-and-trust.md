## Introduction

A Sabbath class taught in Sacramento by a visiting bishop, with a second bishop in the building. It is a leadership class before it is anything else, and it is aimed at the whole nation rather than one school.

The opening statement is the whole thesis: the body of Christ cannot grow in unity while hatred, unresolved wounds, suspicion and selfishness remain among God's people. We all come out of the world and we all come in sick. Growth begins on the individual level, then the family, then the body. As long as things stay unresolved, the congregation hits a ceiling.

From there the class works through three things a leader owes the people. Healing, which is the application of God's laws to a sick body starting with the head. Trust, which is built by transparency, work, fairness and accountability, and destroyed by lying, stealing, favouritism and idleness. And charity, which is the bond that holds the whole thing together, without which every gift a man has amounts to noise.

## In The News

Read as a class of its own kind, the material shown and answered was internal rather than from the headlines:

- The frame set at the top: Sacramento has tremendous potential, and the brothers who ran camp that day said the same of the whole stretch between Sacramento, San Francisco and Oakland. The trip was made because the leadership does care, and because things heard on the back end have to go through a process before anything is seen.
- A repeated answer to a repeated complaint. You do not hear from us, but do not think our eyes and ears are not paying attention. When brothers travelled across the country and certain men did not show up, that was the small sign that started the digging, and it came out in the wash.
- The plainest illustration of a corrupt head: congregations where the money that came in went to strip clubs, and one where they made action figures of themselves. Brothers and sisters give up the little they have, and it gets spent on that.
- The line about camps full of unemployed men whose wives take care of them, and the shame of a man laid out in the bed on a woman's wages.
- Bishop Yawasap's anger at the men's conferences, read not as temper but as a man saying we can do better, we need to be better. And what happens at those same conferences when brothers take pictures of themselves and talk to other brothers' wives while accountability and integrity are the subject.
- A story from twenty-odd years ago: told to put a broken item back in the box and return it, the young man answered, so you telling me to be dishonest, you got the devil on you. The correction landed and stayed for two decades.

## In Closing

The summary the bishop gave for a leader who is in error is a sequence: listen, acknowledge, confess, correct, forgive, restore. Rebuild the trust of the people. Pray and intercede for them. Then demonstrate that the behaviour has actually changed. Being a leader does not mean being perfect, and you are going to make mistakes. The worst thing is compounding a mistake with lies, refusing accountability, and still expecting the people to respect and trust you.

Healing is the application of God's laws, and it starts at the head, because when the head is sick the whole body is faint. Trust is built by working, by transparency, by keeping your hands off what belongs to the people, and by judging without respect of persons, and it is destroyed the moment any one of those slips. Charity is the bond over all of it, and without it the tongues, the prophecy and the knowledge all vanish away and leave nothing behind.

Nehemiah is the model for how it begins: he confessed the sin of the people and put himself and his father's house inside the confession. And Isaiah 58 is the model for where it ends, with men called the repairer of the breach and the restorer of paths to dwell in, raising up the foundations of many generations. The closing charge to Sacramento was exactly that. Be the example, show what the power of God can do, and watch how it takes off.
