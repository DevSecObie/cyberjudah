# Genesis 28: The Charge and the Vow at Bethel

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 28]]

## Introduction

This session read [[Genesis 28|Genesis 28]] through 31 straight through and then opened the key points of each chapter. The recording of part one ends with the chapter 28 teaching; the teaching on chapters 29 through 31 in these notes comes from the part two recording of the session.

**[[Genesis 28#^v1|Genesis 28:1-4]]**

![[Genesis 28#^v1]]
![[Genesis 28#^v2]]
![[Genesis 28#^v3]]
![[Genesis 28#^v4]]

- Isaac called Jacob, blessed him, and charged him: Thou shalt not take a wife of the daughters of Canaan; arise, go to Padanaram, to the house of Bethuel, and take a wife of the daughters of Laban.
- The fathers from the beginning, Noah, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, married wives of their own kindred and were blessed in their children. Abraham gave the same charge for Isaac, and Isaac gives it to Jacob; the instruction was kept in the family line from the beginning.
- With the charge came the blessing of Abraham, passed now to Jacob and his seed: to be fruitful, to multiply into a multitude of people, and to inherit the land promised to Abraham.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Tobit 4#^v12|Tobit 4:12]]**
    ![[Tobit 4#^v12]]

    ![[Genesis 24#^v2]]
    ![[Genesis 24#^v3]]
    ![[Genesis 24#^v4]]
    Abraham made his servant swear the same charge for Isaac: no wife of the daughters of the Canaanites, but of his own kindred.

![[Genesis 28#^v5]]

- Jacob was sent to Laban, the son of Bethuel the Syrian, the brother of Rebekah. Rebekah herself was born to Bethuel, son of Milcah, the wife of Nahor, Abraham's brother; the family line was kept in place, generation after generation going back to their own kindred.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 24#^v15|Genesis 24:15]]**
    ![[Genesis 24#^v15]]
    Rebekah was born to Bethuel, son of Milcah, wife of Nahor, Abraham's brother; the marriages stayed within the kindred.

- Esau had already taken Hittite wives that were a grief of mind unto Isaac and Rebekah, and his generations show wives of the daughters of Canaan. He was hardheaded from the beginning; that is the spirit of the wicked, who hate instruction and cast the words of the Most High behind them.

**[[Genesis 28#^v20|Genesis 28:20-22]]**

![[Genesis 28#^v20]]
![[Genesis 28#^v21]]

- Jacob vowed a vow: if God will be with me, and keep me in this way, and give me bread to eat and raiment to put on, so that I come again to my father's house in peace, then shall the Lord be my God; the stone set for a pillar shall be God's house, and of all that thou shalt give me I will surely give the tenth unto thee.
- A vow to the Most High is not to be made lightly. When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it; better not to vow than to vow and not pay. Jacob vowed and rolled with the Most High, and he paid his vow.

# Genesis 29: Bone and Flesh in Haran

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 29]]

**[[Genesis 29#^v1|Genesis 29:1-4]]**

![[Genesis 29#^v1]]
![[Genesis 29#^v2]]
![[Genesis 29#^v3]]
![[Genesis 29#^v4]]

- Jacob went on his journey out of the land of Canaan and came into the land of the people of the east. On the map he goes north out of Canaan and then east to Haran in Padanaram; that is why the men at the well answer him, Of Haran are we.
- This is the same ground the family had already covered. Isaac charged Jacob to go to Padanaram to the house of Bethuel, and generations earlier Terah had brought Abram from Ur of the Chaldees up along the Euphrates, and they came unto Haran and dwelt there.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 28#^v1|Genesis 28:1-2]]**
    ![[Genesis 28#^v1]]
    ![[Genesis 28#^v2]]
    Isaac charged Jacob to arise and go to Padanaram to the house of Bethuel; that is where Jacob was headed.
  - **[[Genesis 11#^v31|Genesis 11:31]]**
    ![[Genesis 11#^v31]]
    Terah took Abram from Ur of the Chaldees to go into Canaan, and they came unto Haran and dwelt there; Jacob's road runs back to the family's old dwelling.

**[[Genesis 29#^v5|Genesis 29:5]]**

![[Genesis 29#^v5]]

- Jacob asks, Know ye Laban the son of Nahor, and the men of Haran know him. Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran, so Laban dwells in that area because the family remained there when Terah stopped at Haran.
- This ties the family line together: Abraham first journeyed from Ur of the Chaldees northwest to Haran, and now Jacob comes up from Canaan northeast to Haran. Because of the way Jacob traveled, he went back home and is dwelling amongst family.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 11#^v26|Genesis 11:26-27]]**
    ![[Genesis 11#^v26]]
    ![[Genesis 11#^v27]]
    Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran; Laban is of Nahor's line, so Jacob has come to his own people.

**[[Genesis 29#^v14|Genesis 29:14-15]]**

![[Genesis 29#^v14]]
![[Genesis 29#^v15]]

- Laban said unto Jacob, Surely thou art my bone and my flesh. In the scriptures that phrase means we are family; Jacob abode with him a month, and Laban, because Jacob was his brother, asked what his wages should be.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Judges 9#^v1|Judges 9:1-2]]**
    ![[Judges 9#^v1]]
    ![[Judges 9#^v2]]
    Abimelech told his mother's brethren, Remember also that I am your bone and your flesh; the phrase declares, we are family.
  - **[[2 Samuel 19#^v12|2 Samuel 19:12]]**
    ![[2 Samuel 19#^v12]]
    Ye are my brethren, ye are my bones and my flesh; further proof that bone and flesh speaks of kindred.

**[[Genesis 29#^v16|Genesis 29:16-18]]**

![[Genesis 29#^v16]]
![[Genesis 29#^v17]]
![[Genesis 29#^v18]]

- Leah was tender eyed, but Rachel was beautiful and well favoured, and Jacob loved Rachel. He had already run to roll the stone from the well's mouth, watered her flock, and kissed her when she came out with the sheep; his eyes were fixed on her from the start.
- Do not be hasty to credit somebody based on their looks. Of course you want somebody you are attracted to, but commending a person for beauty alone gets a lot of people in trouble.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 36#^v22|Ecclesiasticus 36:22]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 36#^v22]]
    A man loveth nothing better than the beauty of a woman; that is what Jacob saw in Rachel.
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 11#^v2|Ecclesiasticus 11:2]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 11#^v2]]
    Do not be hasty to credit somebody for their beauty or their outward appearance.

**[[Genesis 29#^v20|Genesis 29:20]]**

![[Genesis 29#^v20]]

- Jacob served seven years for Rachel, and they seemed unto him but a few days for the love he had to her. That is how fine she was to him: with six and a half years still left, it felt like a couple of days.
- Men gather gold and silver and every goodly thing, then let all those things go and fix their eyes fast on a woman comely in favour and beauty. Many run out of their wits for a woman and become servants for their sakes; Jacob became a servant fourteen years, because he was deceived and served another seven.

  Precepts:
  - **[[1 Esdras 4#^v18|1 Esdras 4:18-22]]**
    ![[1 Esdras 4#^v18]]
    ![[1 Esdras 4#^v19]]
    ![[1 Esdras 4#^v20]]
    ![[1 Esdras 4#^v21]]
    ![[1 Esdras 4#^v22]]
    Men let gold and silver go and fix their eyes fast on a comely woman; by this ye must know that women have dominion over you.
  - **[[1 Esdras 4#^v25|1 Esdras 4:25-26]]**
    ![[1 Esdras 4#^v25]]
    ![[1 Esdras 4#^v26]]
    Many have run out of their wits for women and become servants for their sakes; Jacob served fourteen years for Rachel.

**[[Genesis 29#^v30|Genesis 29:30-31]]**

![[Genesis 29#^v30]]
![[Genesis 29#^v31]]

- Jacob loved Rachel more than Leah and served with Laban yet seven other years. When the Lord saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb; but Rachel was barren. Jacob was showing more favor to Rachel because he was more attracted to Rachel.
- Favour is deceitful and beauty is vain. You are not going to look the same at seventy that you did at twenty-five; our days are threescore years and ten, or by reason of strength fourscore, and outside a handful who retain a semblance of their youth, the looks fade along the way. A woman that feareth the Lord, she shall be praised.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Proverbs 31#^v30|Proverbs 31:30]]**
    ![[Proverbs 31#^v30]]
    Favour is deceitful and beauty is vain, but a woman that feareth the Lord, she shall be praised.
  - **[[Psalms 90#^v10|Psalms 90:10]]**
    ![[Psalms 90#^v10]]
    Our days are threescore years and ten, or by reason of strength fourscore years; the beauty of youth does not last the span.

# Genesis 30: Trouble in the Flesh and a Righteous Hire

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 30]]

**[[Genesis 30#^v1|Genesis 30:1-2]]**

![[Genesis 30#^v1]]
![[Genesis 30#^v2]]

- Rachel saw that she bare Jacob no children, envied her sister, and told Jacob, Give me children, or else I die. Jacob's anger was kindled against her: Am I in God's stead, who hath withheld from thee the fruit of the womb? Jacob was not the problem; he was having no trouble producing children.
- Their arguing is the trouble in the flesh the scriptures speak of. If thou marry, thou hast not sinned, but such shall have trouble in the flesh: it is not all going to be sweet all the time. Rachel's trouble in the flesh was that she envied her sister and could not bear children, and she took it out on Jacob.

  Precepts:
  - **[[1 Corinthians 7#^v28|1 Corinthians 7:28]]**
    ![[1 Corinthians 7#^v28]]
    They that marry have not sinned, but such shall have trouble in the flesh; in marriage there will be some hard times.

**[[Genesis 30#^v14|Genesis 30:14-18]]**

![[Genesis 30#^v14]]
![[Genesis 30#^v15]]
![[Genesis 30#^v16]]
![[Genesis 30#^v17]]
![[Genesis 30#^v18]]

- Reuben found mandrakes in the field in the days of wheat harvest and brought them unto his mother Leah. Rachel asked for them, and Leah answered, Is it a small matter that thou hast taken my husband? and wouldest thou take away my son's mandrakes also? So Rachel granted, He shall lie with thee to night for thy son's mandrakes.
- When Jacob came out of the field in the evening, Leah went out to meet him: Thou must come in unto me, for surely I have hired thee with my son's mandrakes. He lay with her that night, and she bare a son and called him Issachar.
- The mandrake is a flower with several uses; it was used medicinally and as an aphrodisiac, a help for those having problems conceiving. That is why the sisters set such a price on them.

**[[Genesis 30#^v22|Genesis 30:22-24]]**

![[Genesis 30#^v22]]
![[Genesis 30#^v23]]
![[Genesis 30#^v24]]

- God remembered Rachel, hearkened unto her, and opened her womb; she conceived and bare a son and called his name Joseph, saying, God hath taken away my reproach.
- It was not the mandrakes that gave her the child; it is only coincidence that the baby came after she had them. The Most High is the one who showed her mercy and opened up her womb, for he maketh the barren woman to keep house and to be a joyful mother of children.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Psalms 113#^v1|Psalms 113:1-2]]**
    ![[Psalms 113#^v1]]
    ![[Psalms 113#^v2]]
    Praise ye the Lord, O ye servants of the Lord; blessed be the name of the Lord from this time forth and for evermore.
  - **[[Psalms 113#^v9|Psalms 113:9]]**
    ![[Psalms 113#^v9]]
    He maketh the barren woman to keep house and to be a joyful mother of children; the Most High is the one who opened Rachel's womb.

**[[Genesis 30#^v27|Genesis 30:27-30]]**

![[Genesis 30#^v27]]
![[Genesis 30#^v28]]
![[Genesis 30#^v29]]
![[Genesis 30#^v30]]

- Laban asked Jacob to tarry: I have learned by experience that the LORD hath blessed me for thy sake. Laban understood that with Jacob in his household he was increasing, and he offered to appoint Jacob his wages.
- Jacob answered that Laban knew how he had served him and how the cattle fared with him: it was little which thou hadst before I came, and it is now increased unto a multitude; the LORD hath blessed thee since my coming. And now, when shall I provide for mine own house also? A man looks to provide for his own house.

**[[Genesis 30#^v31|Genesis 30:31-36]]**

![[Genesis 30#^v31]]
![[Genesis 30#^v32]]
![[Genesis 30#^v33]]
![[Genesis 30#^v34]]
![[Genesis 30#^v35]]
![[Genesis 30#^v36]]

- Jacob said, Thou shalt not give me any thing. He asked no gift, only terms: he would keep feeding Laban's flock, and the speckled, spotted, and brown cattle among the sheep and goats from that day forward would be his hire.
- That refusal is the spirit our forefathers rolled in. We have to come back to these scriptures and to the understanding of who we are, because our people are lost and have fainted from that spirit; we cannot roll in a handout spirit, and when we repent we have to conduct ourselves as men and provide for our own houses, for the Most High provides.
- So shall my righteousness answer for me in time to come: Jacob set terms that could be checked in the open. Every one that was not speckled and spotted among the goats and brown among the sheep would be counted stolen with him. Judge me on my works; my word is my bond. He said it because he knew they were going to have something bad to say about his increase.
- Laban agreed, then removed that same day the he goats that were ringstraked and spotted, all the speckled and spotted she goats, and the brown among the sheep, and gave them into the hand of his own sons, setting three days' journey between himself and Jacob.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 14#^v21|Genesis 14:21-23]]**
    ![[Genesis 14#^v21]]
    ![[Genesis 14#^v22]]
    ![[Genesis 14#^v23]]
    After the war Abram told the king of Sodom, I will not take from a thread even to a shoelatchet, lest thou shouldest say, I have made Abram rich; the Most High was his provider, and that is the spirit of the forefathers.

# Genesis 31: The Return Home and Laban's Idols

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 31]]

**[[Genesis 31#^v1|Genesis 31:1-2]]**

![[Genesis 31#^v1]]
![[Genesis 31#^v2]]

- Jacob heard the words of Laban's sons, saying, Jacob hath taken away all that was our father's, and of that which was our father's hath he gotten all this glory; and Jacob beheld the countenance of Laban, and behold, it was not toward him as before.
- Remember, Laban had just acknowledged, I have learned by experience that the LORD hath blessed me for thy sake; now he is looking at Jacob sideways because of what his sons said. That is exactly why Jacob said, So shall my righteousness answer for me: he dealt righteously in the open so his dealings would be his answer.

**[[Genesis 31#^v3|Genesis 31:3]]**

![[Genesis 31#^v3]]

- The LORD said unto Jacob, Return unto the land of thy fathers, and to thy kindred; and I will be with thee. The command to go home came with the promise of the Most High's presence.

**[[Genesis 31#^v17|Genesis 31:17-19]]**

![[Genesis 31#^v17]]
![[Genesis 31#^v18]]
![[Genesis 31#^v19]]

- Jacob rose up, set his sons and his wives upon camels, and carried away all his cattle and all the goods he had gotten in Padanaram, to go to Isaac his father in the land of Canaan; he was returning home.
- Laban went to shear his sheep, and Rachel had stolen the images that were her father's. Remember what we read in Proverbs about beauty: yes, she was fine, but she was a thief. She might have been a fine thief, but she was a thief.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Proverbs 31#^v30|Proverbs 31:30]]**
    ![[Proverbs 31#^v30]]
    Favour is deceitful and beauty is vain; Rachel's beauty did not keep her from being a thief.

**[[Genesis 31#^v30|Genesis 31:30-32]]**

![[Genesis 31#^v30]]
![[Genesis 31#^v31]]
![[Genesis 31#^v32]]

- Laban overtook Jacob and asked, Wherefore hast thou stolen my gods? That question tells you what Laban was: Laban was an idolater.
- The scriptures prove it. Your fathers dwelt on the other side of the flood in old time, even Terah, the father of Abraham and the father of Nachor, and they served other gods. Laban was of the house of Nahor, an idolater just like his fathers. That is why the Most High told Abram to get out from his country and from his kindred: he separated Abram because of what he had set up for Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and for us, that we not be idolaters.
- Jacob answered that he went away because he was afraid Laban would take his daughters from him by force, and said, With whomsoever thou findest thy gods, let him not live. For Jacob knew not that Rachel had stolen them.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Joshua 24#^v2|Joshua 24:2]]**
    ![[Joshua 24#^v2]]
    Your fathers dwelt on the other side of the flood in old time, even Terah the father of Abraham and the father of Nachor, and they served other gods; Laban came of that idolatrous house.
  - **[[Genesis 12#^v1|Genesis 12:1]]**
    ![[Genesis 12#^v1]]
    The Most High told Abram, Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred; he separated Abram from the idolaters.
