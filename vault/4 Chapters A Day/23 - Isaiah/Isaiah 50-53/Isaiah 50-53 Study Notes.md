# Isaiah 50: The Servant's Obedience and the Prophecy of Christ Beaten

Read the chapter → [[Isaiah 50]]

This session reads the four chapters [[Isaiah 50|Isaiah 50]] through 53. It moves from the charge that Israel sold themselves for their own iniquities, into the prophecy of the Servant who gave his back to the smiters, through the comfort of Zion and the removal of the cup of trembling, and finally into chapter 53, which the teacher says is the whole account of the sacrifice Christ made, why he made it, what he went through, and how he bore our sins.

**[[Isaiah 50#^v1|Isaiah 50:1-3]]**

![[Isaiah 50#^v1]]
![[Isaiah 50#^v2]]
![[Isaiah 50#^v3]]

- The Most High asks where is the bill of the mother's divorcement, or which creditor he sold them to. The point of the charge is that for their iniquities they sold themselves, and for their transgressions their mother was put away.
- This is not that the people literally went and sold themselves. They brought this upon themselves by their own sins, and that is what put them into their trouble.
- His hand is not shortened that it cannot redeem, and he has power to deliver. At his rebuke he dries up the sea, makes the rivers a wilderness, and clothes the heavens with blackness.

**[[Isaiah 50#^v4|Isaiah 50:4-6]]**

![[Isaiah 50#^v4]]
![[Isaiah 50#^v5]]
![[Isaiah 50#^v6]]

- The Lord God gave the Servant the tongue of the learned, to know how to speak a word in season to him that is weary, and he wakens the ear morning by morning to hear as the learned.
- The Servant was not rebellious and did not turn away back. He gave his back to the smiters and his cheeks to them that plucked off the hair, and he hid not his face from shame and spitting.
- This goes right into the prophecy of Christ, who was getting beat. The gave-my-back-to-the-smiters is the beating and shame he took.

**[[Isaiah 50#^v7|Isaiah 50:7-11]]**

![[Isaiah 50#^v7]]
![[Isaiah 50#^v8]]
![[Isaiah 50#^v9]]
![[Isaiah 50#^v10]]
![[Isaiah 50#^v11]]

- Because the Lord God will help him, the Servant is not confounded; he sets his face like a flint and knows he shall not be ashamed. The one who justifies him is near, so who will contend with him or condemn him.
- His adversaries shall wax old as a garment and the moth shall eat them up. The one who fears the Lord and obeys the voice of his servant, though he walks in darkness and has no light, is told to trust in the name of the Lord and stay upon his God.
- Those who kindle their own fire and compass themselves with sparks, walking in the light of their own fire, shall have this at his hand: they shall lie down in sorrow.

# Isaiah 51: Comfort for Zion and the Cup of Trembling Removed

Read the chapter → [[Isaiah 51]]

**[[Isaiah 51#^v1|Isaiah 51:1-8]]**

![[Isaiah 51#^v2]]

- Those who follow after righteousness and seek the Lord are told to look to the rock they were hewn from and to Abraham their father and Sarah, for he called Abraham alone and blessed him and increased him.
- The Lord shall comfort Zion and all her waste places, making her wilderness like Eden and her desert like a garden of the Lord, with joy, gladness, thanksgiving, and the voice of melody found in her.
- The heavens shall vanish away like smoke and the earth wax old like a garment, and they that dwell therein shall die, but his salvation shall be forever and his righteousness shall not be abolished. Those who know righteousness are told not to fear the reproach of men, for the moth and the worm shall eat their revilers.

- The arm of the Lord is called to awake and put on strength as in the ancient days, the arm that cut Rahab and wounded the dragon and dried up the sea, making a way for the ransomed to pass over.
- The redeemed of the Lord shall return and come with singing to Zion, with everlasting joy upon their heads; they shall obtain gladness and joy, and sorrow and mourning shall flee away.
- He is the one that comforts them, and asks why they should be afraid of a man that shall die and forget the Lord their maker who stretched forth the heavens and laid the foundations of the earth. He has put his words in their mouth and covered them in the shadow of his hand, and says unto Zion, Thou art my people.

- Jerusalem is called to awake and stand up, having drunk at the hand of the Lord the cup of his fury and the dregs of the cup of trembling. There was none among her sons to guide her or take her by the hand.
- Desolation, destruction, famine, and the sword came upon her, and her sons fainted and lay at the head of the streets, full of the fury of the Lord.
- The Lord who pleads the cause of his people declares he has taken the cup of trembling out of her hand; she shall no more drink it. All the curses and punishments that came upon us we drank every bit of, and now that cup is put into the hand of them that afflicted us and made us bow down so they could go over. So be patient, Israel, be patient.

# Isaiah 52: Awake, Zion, Redeemed Without Money

Read the chapter → [[Isaiah 52]]

**[[Isaiah 52#^v1|Isaiah 52:1-6]]**

![[Isaiah 52#^v2]]

- Zion is called to awake and put on strength, and Jerusalem the holy city to put on her beautiful garments, for henceforth the uncircumcised and the unclean shall no more come into her. She is told to shake off the dust, arise, and loose the bands of her neck.
- They sold themselves for nothing and shall be redeemed without money. His people went down into Egypt to sojourn, and the Assyrian oppressed them without cause.
- Because those that rule over them make them howl and his name is blasphemed continually every day, his people shall therefore know his name, and know in that day that he is the one who speaks.

- Beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that brings good tidings, that publishes peace and salvation and says to Zion, Thy God reigneth. The watchmen shall lift up the voice and sing together when they see eye to eye and the Lord brings again Zion.
- The waste places of Jerusalem are told to break forth into joy, for the Lord has comforted his people and redeemed Jerusalem, making bare his holy arm in the eyes of all the nations, so all the ends of the earth shall see the salvation of our God.
- They are told to depart and go out from thence, touching no unclean thing and being clean that bear the vessels of the Lord; they shall not go out in haste or by flight, for the Lord will go before them and be their rearward.

- The Servant shall deal prudently and be exalted, extolled, and made very high. Many were astonished at him because his visage was marred more than any man and his form more than the sons of men.
- So shall he sprinkle many nations, and kings shall shut their mouths at him, for what had not been told them they shall see, and what they had not heard they shall consider.

# Isaiah 53: The Suffering Servant Bears Our Sins

Read the chapter → [[Isaiah 53]]

**[[Isaiah 53#^v1|Isaiah 53:1-6]]**

![[Isaiah 53#^v1]]
![[Isaiah 53#^v2]]
![[Isaiah 53#^v3]]
![[Isaiah 53#^v4]]
![[Isaiah 53#^v5]]
![[Isaiah 53#^v6]]

- Who has believed the report, and to whom is the arm of the Lord revealed. He grows up before him as a tender plant and a root out of dry ground, with no form nor comeliness and no beauty that we should desire him.
- He is despised and rejected of men, a man of sorrows and acquainted with grief; we hid our faces from him and esteemed him not, though he bore our griefs and carried our sorrows.
- He was wounded for our transgressions and bruised for our iniquities; the chastisement of our peace was upon him, and with his stripes we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray, turned everyone to his own way, and the Lord laid on him the iniquity of us all.

**[[Isaiah 53#^v7|Isaiah 53:7-12]]**

![[Isaiah 53#^v7]]

- He was oppressed and afflicted, yet opened not his mouth; he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter and as a sheep dumb before her shearers. He was taken from prison and from judgment and cut off from the land of the living for the transgression of the people.
- He made his grave with the wicked and with the rich in his death, though he had done no violence and no deceit was in his mouth. It pleased the Lord to bruise him and make his soul an offering for sin, and by his knowledge shall his righteous servant justify many, for he shall bear their iniquities.
- This whole chapter is the sacrifice that Christ made, why he made it, what he went through, and the fact that he bore our sins. He poured out his soul unto death, was numbered with the transgressors, bore the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors.
