# Isaiah 34: The Lord's Indignation Upon All Nations and the Slaughter of Edom

Read the chapter → [[Isaiah 34]]

This session reads [[Isaiah 34|Isaiah 34]] through 37 in the four chapters a day study. The first two chapters, 34 and 35, are prophecies: chapter 34 declares the Most High's indignation and fury upon all nations and His great slaughter in the land of Idumea, the land of Edom, while chapter 35 is a prophecy of Christ's return and of Israel's return to Zion. Chapters 36 and 37 turn to the history of King Hezekiah and the Assyrian king Sennacherib, whose servant Rabshakeh came up to blaspheme the living God and threaten Jerusalem. The whole reading shows that no nation can stand against Israel unless the Most High Himself has ordained that nation to overthrow His people, and He made it clear that Jerusalem was not going to be touched.

**[[Isaiah 34#^v1|Isaiah 34:1-4]]**

![[Isaiah 34#^v1]]
![[Isaiah 34#^v2]]
![[Isaiah 34#^v3]]
![[Isaiah 34#^v4]]

- The Most High calls the nations to come near and hear, and the earth and all that is in it to give ear, for His indignation is upon all nations and His fury upon all their armies. He has utterly destroyed them and delivered them to the slaughter.
- The slain are cast out and the stink of their carcasses rises up, and the mountains are melted with their blood. This is the language of the Most High's vengeance poured out on the wicked.
- The hosts of heaven are dissolved and the heavens rolled together as a scroll, and all their host falls down as a leaf falls off the vine and as a falling fig from the fig tree. The judgment reaches from the ground to the sky.

**[[Isaiah 34#^v5|Isaiah 34:5-8]]**

![[Isaiah 34#^v5]]
![[Isaiah 34#^v6]]
![[Isaiah 34#^v7]]
![[Isaiah 34#^v8]]

- His sword is bathed in heaven and comes down upon Idumea, upon the people of His curse, to judgment. Idumea is the land of Edom, the wicked, marked out here for the day of the Most High's vengeance.
- The sword of the Most High is filled with blood and made fat with the blood of lambs and goats and rams, for He has a sacrifice in Bozrah and a great slaughter in the land of Idumea. The animals named picture the slaughter of the people.
- It is the day of the Most High's vengeance and the year of recompense for the controversy of Zion. The judgment on Edom is the Most High settling the account for what was done to His people.

**[[Isaiah 34#^v9|Isaiah 34:9-10]]**

![[Isaiah 34#^v9]]
![[Isaiah 34#^v10]]

- The streams of Edom are turned into pitch and the dust into brimstone, and the land becomes burning pitch. This is the same imagery of fire and brimstone used against the wicked.
- The burning is not quenched night or day, and the smoke goes up forever from generation to generation. The land lies waste so that none passes through it forever and ever, a permanent desolation.

**[[Isaiah 34#^v11|Isaiah 34:11-13]]**

![[Isaiah 34#^v11]]
![[Isaiah 34#^v12]]
![[Isaiah 34#^v13]]

- The cormorant and the bittern possess the land, and the owl and the raven dwell in it. When the Most High is finished, only the unclean birds and beasts inhabit the place.
- He stretches out upon it the line of confusion and the stones of emptiness. The nobles are called to a kingdom that is not there, and all her princes come to nothing.
- Thorns come up in her palaces, and nettles and brambles in her fortresses, and it becomes a habitation of dragons and a court for owls. What was once a proud place is left to ruin and wild things.

**[[Isaiah 34#^v14|Isaiah 34:14-17]]**

![[Isaiah 34#^v14]]
![[Isaiah 34#^v15]]
![[Isaiah 34#^v16]]
![[Isaiah 34#^v17]]

- The wild beasts of the desert meet with the wild beasts of the island, and the satyr cries to his fellow, and the shrieking owl rests there and finds a place of rest. The desolation is total and lasting.
- Seek out of the book of the Most High and read: none of these shall fail, none shall want her mate, for His mouth has commanded it and His spirit has gathered them. What He has spoken over Edom will surely come to pass.
- He has cast the lot for them and divided the land to them by line, and they shall possess it forever and dwell in it from generation to generation. The judgment is fixed and permanent.

# Isaiah 35: The Ransomed of the Lord Return to Zion

Read the chapter → [[Isaiah 35]]

This chapter is a prophecy of Christ's return and of Israel's return. The wilderness and the desert become glad and blossom, the weak and fearful are strengthened, the Most High comes with vengeance to save, and a highway called the way of holiness is opened for the redeemed alone to walk on.

**[[Isaiah 35#^v1|Isaiah 35:1-2]]**

![[Isaiah 35#^v1]]
![[Isaiah 35#^v2]]

- The wilderness and the solitary place are glad, and the desert rejoices and blossoms as the rose. The barren places become fruitful when the Most High moves.
- The glory of Lebanon and the excellency of Carmel and Sharon are given to it, and they shall see the glory of the Most High. The prophecy points to the restoration that comes with His return.

**[[Isaiah 35#^v3|Isaiah 35:3-4]]**

![[Isaiah 35#^v3]]
![[Isaiah 35#^v4]]

- Strengthen the weak hands and confirm the feeble knees, and say to those of a fearful heart, Be strong, fear not. The message to the people is one of encouragement in the day of trouble.
- Behold, your God will come with vengeance, even the Most High with a recompense; He will come and save you. His coming is both vengeance on the wicked and salvation for His people.

**[[Isaiah 35#^v5|Isaiah 35:5-7]]**

![[Isaiah 35#^v5]]
![[Isaiah 35#^v6]]
![[Isaiah 35#^v7]]

- Then the eyes of the blind are opened and the ears of the deaf unstopped, the lame man leaps as a hart, and the tongue of the dumb sings. The Most High's coming brings healing and restoration.
- In the wilderness waters break out and streams in the desert, the parched ground becomes a pool, and the thirsty land springs of water. The dry places are made alive again.

**[[Isaiah 35#^v8|Isaiah 35:8-10]]**

![[Isaiah 35#^v8]]
![[Isaiah 35#^v9]]
![[Isaiah 35#^v10]]

- A highway is there called the way of holiness, and the unclean shall not pass over it. Wayfaring means a traveling man, and though fools travel through many places, they shall not err on this way, because the unclean cannot walk it.
- No lion or ravenous beast goes up on it; it is not found there, but the redeemed walk there. The wayfaring man, the fool, is somebody outside of these people, and only the redeemed are those who can walk on this way.
- The ransomed of the Most High return and come to Zion with songs and everlasting joy upon their heads; they obtain joy and gladness, and sorrow and sighing flee away. This is the return of the people to Zion when the Most High saves them.

# Isaiah 36: Rabshakeh's Blasphemy Against Jerusalem

Read the chapter → [[Isaiah 36]]

This chapter begins the history of King Hezekiah. In the fourteenth year of his reign, Sennacherib king of Assyria came up against the defended cities of Judah and took them, then sent his servant Rabshakeh with a great army to Jerusalem to threaten Hezekiah and mock trust in the Most High.

**[[Isaiah 36#^v1|Isaiah 36:1-3]]**

![[Isaiah 36#^v1]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v2]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v3]]

- In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah, Sennacherib king of Assyria came up against all the defended cities of Judah and took them. Assyria was the rod the Most High used to press Judah.
- The king of Assyria sent Rabshakeh from Lachish to Jerusalem with a great army, and he stood by the conduit of the upper pool. Eliakim, Shebna the scribe, and Joah the recorder came out to meet him.

**[[Isaiah 36#^v4|Isaiah 36:4-10]]**

![[Isaiah 36#^v4]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v5]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v6]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v7]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v8]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v9]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v10]]

- Rabshakeh challenges Hezekiah's confidence, asking in whom he trusts, and calls his words but vain words. He mocks any counsel or strength for war.
- He warns against trusting in the staff of the broken reed of Egypt, which pierces the hand of the man who leans on it. Trusting Egypt instead of the Most High is the constant temptation set before Judah.
- He twists Hezekiah's removal of the high places, claiming it offended the Most High, and boasts that even Assyria was sent by the Lord to destroy the land. He speaks as though the Most High is on his side.

**[[Isaiah 36#^v11|Isaiah 36:11-12]]**

![[Isaiah 36#^v11]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v12]]

- Eliakim and Shebna and Joah ask Rabshakeh to speak in the Syrian language, which they understand, and not in the Jews' language in the ears of the people on the wall. They do not want the people to hear the threats.
- Rabshakeh refuses, saying his master sent him to the men on the wall, who are the ones set to eat their own dung and drink their own piss with them. He deliberately speaks to frighten the common people.

**[[Isaiah 36#^v13|Isaiah 36:13-20]]**

![[Isaiah 36#^v13]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v14]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v15]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v16]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v17]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v18]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v19]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v20]]

- Rabshakeh stands and cries with a loud voice in the Jews' language, telling the people not to let Hezekiah deceive them, for he cannot deliver them. He works to break the people's trust in their king.
- He tells them not to let Hezekiah make them trust in the Most High to deliver the city, but to make an agreement with him and come out, promising each man his own vine and fig tree until he takes them to a land like their own. It is a bribe dressed up as mercy.
- He lists the gods of Hamath, Arphad, and Sepharvaim, and asks whether any of them delivered their land or Samaria out of his hand, so that the Most High should deliver Jerusalem. He counts the living God as no better than the idols of the nations.

**[[Isaiah 36#^v21|Isaiah 36:21-22]]**

![[Isaiah 36#^v21]]
![[Isaiah 36#^v22]]

- The people hold their peace and answer him not a word, for Hezekiah's commandment was to answer him not. They do not argue with the blasphemer.
- Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah come to Hezekiah with their clothes torn and tell him the words of Rabshakeh. They bring the blasphemy before the king in mourning.

# Isaiah 37: Hezekiah's Prayer and the Overthrow of the Assyrians

Read the chapter → [[Isaiah 37]]

Hezekiah rends his clothes, goes into the house of the Most High, and sends to Isaiah the prophet. Isaiah answers with the word that Sennacherib will hear a rumor, return to his own land, and fall by the sword there. Hezekiah prays, the Most High defends Jerusalem, and the angel of the Most High smites the Assyrian camp. This history shows that no nation can stand against Israel unless the Most High has ordained it, and He made clear that Jerusalem would not be touched.

**[[Isaiah 37#^v1|Isaiah 37:1-4]]**

![[Isaiah 37#^v1]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v2]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v3]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v4]]

- When Hezekiah hears the words, he rends his clothes, covers himself with sackcloth, and goes into the house of the Most High. He turns to the Most High rather than to Egypt or to fear.
- He sends Eliakim, Shebna, and the elders of the priests, covered with sackcloth, to Isaiah the prophet. He calls it a day of trouble, rebuke, and blasphemy.
- He asks that Isaiah pray for the remnant that is left, hoping the Most High will hear the words of Rabshakeh, who was sent to reproach the living God. Hezekiah seeks the prophet's intercession.

**[[Isaiah 37#^v5|Isaiah 37:5-7]]**

![[Isaiah 37#^v5]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v6]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v7]]

- Isaiah answers the servants: tell your master, Be not afraid of the words with which the servants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed Me. The Most High answers the blasphemy directly.
- Behold, I will send a blast upon him, and he shall hear a rumor and return to his own land, and I will cause him to fall by the sword in his own land. This is the prophecy that Sennacherib would be killed at home.
- This is exactly what happened: Sennacherib was killed in his own land by his own sons, showing the word of the Most High through Isaiah came to pass. No nation can stand against Israel when the Most High has spoken against it.

**[[Isaiah 37#^v8|Isaiah 37:8-13]]**

![[Isaiah 37#^v8]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v9]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v10]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v11]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v12]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v13]]

- Rabshakeh returns and finds the king of Assyria warring against Libnah, for he had departed from Lachish. Word comes of Tirhakah king of Ethiopia coming out to make war.
- Sennacherib sends messengers again to Hezekiah, telling him not to let the God in whom he trusts deceive him that Jerusalem will not be given into his hand. He renews the same threat and mockery.
- He points to all the lands and kings that Assyria destroyed, Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and the rest, asking whether they were delivered. He counts Jerusalem as one more city to fall.

**[[Isaiah 37#^v14|Isaiah 37:14-20]]**

![[Isaiah 37#^v14]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v15]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v16]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v17]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v18]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v19]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v20]]

- Hezekiah receives the letter, reads it, goes up to the house of the Most High, and spreads it before Him. He lays the whole threat out before the Most High in prayer.
- He calls on the Lord of hosts, God of Israel, who dwells between the cherubims, as the God alone of all the kingdoms of the earth, who made heaven and earth. He asks Him to incline His ear and hear the words of Sennacherib that reproach the living God.
- He acknowledges that Assyria has laid waste the nations and cast their gods into the fire, but those were no gods, only the work of men's hands, wood and stone. He asks the Most High to save them, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that He alone is the Lord.

**[[Isaiah 37#^v21|Isaiah 37:21-29]]**

![[Isaiah 37#^v21]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v22]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v23]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v24]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v25]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v26]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v27]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v28]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v29]]

- Isaiah sends to Hezekiah that the Most High has heard his prayer against Sennacherib. The virgin daughter of Zion has despised and laughed him to scorn.
- Sennacherib reproached and blasphemed the Holy One of Israel, boasting by his chariots that he climbed the mountains and cut down the cedars and dried up the rivers. His pride is set against the Most High Himself.
- The Most High declares that He formed and brought it to pass of ancient times, that Sennacherib should lay waste the defended cities, and that the inhabitants were weak because the Most High allowed it. Because of his rage against the Most High, He will put His hook in his nose and His bridle in his lips and turn him back the way he came.

**[[Isaiah 37#^v30|Isaiah 37:30-32]]**

![[Isaiah 37#^v30]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v31]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v32]]

- The sign to Hezekiah is that this year they eat what grows of itself, the second year what springs of the same, and the third year they sow, reap, plant vineyards, and eat the fruit. The Most High promises restoration and provision.
- The remnant that escapes of the house of Judah takes root downward and bears fruit upward, for out of Jerusalem goes forth a remnant. The zeal of the Lord of hosts performs this.

**[[Isaiah 37#^v33|Isaiah 37:33-35]]**

![[Isaiah 37#^v33]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v34]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v35]]

- Concerning the king of Assyria, the Most High says he shall not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with shields, nor cast a bank against it. Jerusalem is not going to be touched.
- By the way he came, by the same he shall return, and shall not come into the city. The Most High will defend the city to save it for His own sake and for His servant David's sake.

**[[Isaiah 37#^v36|Isaiah 37:36-38]]**

![[Isaiah 37#^v36]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v37]]
![[Isaiah 37#^v38]]

- The angel of the Most High goes forth and smites the camp of the Assyrians, a hundred and eighty-five thousand, and in the morning they are all dead corpses. One angel destroys the whole army, proving no nation stands against Israel when the Most High defends it.
- Sennacherib departs and returns and dwells at Nineveh, and as he worships in the house of Nisroch his god, his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer smite him with the sword and escape into Armenia. The prophecy of [[Isaiah 37#^v7|Isaiah 37:7]] is fulfilled: he falls by the sword in his own land, killed by his own children.
- Esarhaddon his son reigns in his stead. The Most High's word stands from beginning to end.
