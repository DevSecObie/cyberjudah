# 2 Kings 3: Three Kings and the Water in the Ditches

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 3]]

This session covers [[2 Kings 3|2 Kings 3]] through 6, following the acts and ministry of Elisha after he received a double portion of the spirit of Elijah. It moves through the war of the three kings against Moab, the miracles among the sons of the prophets and the Shunammite woman, the cleansing of Naaman the Syrian and the judgment on Gehazi, the floating axe head, the blinding of the Syrian host, and the famine and siege of Samaria. These chapters show the hand of the Most High working through his prophet, a witness to Israel of how great a people they were when the Lord was with them, even while they remained a rebellious nation.

- Nevertheless he cleaves to the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin, and departs not from them. The house of Ahab had reared up images and led the kingdom into idolatry.

- King Jehoram goes out of Samaria, numbers all Israel, and sends to Jehoshaphat king of Judah, telling him Moab has rebelled and asking whether he will go up with him to battle. Jehoshaphat answers, I will go up; I am as thou art, my people as thy people, and my horses as thy horses.

- The king of Israel, the king of Judah, and the king of Edom fetch a compass of seven days' journey, and there is no water for the host or for the cattle that follow them. The king of Israel says the Lord has called these three kings together to deliver them into the hand of Moab.
- Jehoshaphat asks whether there is not a prophet of the Lord that they may inquire of the Lord by him. One of the king of Israel's servants answers that Elisha the son of Shaphat is there, who poured water on the hands of Elijah.
- Jehoshaphat says the word of the Lord is with him, so the three kings go down to Elisha.

- Elisha says, as the Lord of hosts liveth before whom I stand, that were it not that he regarded the presence of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, he would not look toward the king of Israel nor see him. He addresses the matter only for the sake of the righteous king of Judah.

- Though they will see neither wind nor rain, the valley shall be filled with water that they and their cattle and beasts may drink. This is but a light thing in the sight of the Lord.
- The Lord will also deliver the Moabites into their hand, and they shall smite every fenced city and every choice city, fell every good tree, stop all the wells of water, and mar every good piece of land with stones.

- In the morning, when the meat offering is offered, water comes by the way of Edom and the country is filled with water. When the Moabites hear the kings have come up to fight, they gather all who can put on armor and stand in the border.
- Rising early, the Moabites see the sun shining on the water on the other side red as blood, and conclude the kings are slain and have smitten one another. They cry, Moab to the spoil, and run toward the camp expecting to gather the plunder of dead men.
- When they come to the camp of Israel, the Israelites rise up and smite the Moabites so that they flee, and Israel goes forward smiting them even into their own country, beating down cities, casting stones on every good piece of land, stopping the wells, and felling the good trees, leaving only the stones of Kir-haraseth, which the slingers went about and smote.
- This victory is a testament to how great a people Israel was when the hand of the Lord was with them. When Israel was in its right mind and its prayers went forth, the Lord fought for them, which is part of why the heathen nations, and Moab in particular, have hated Israel to this day.

- There is great indignation against Israel, and they depart from him and return to their own land.

# 2 Kings 4: The Widow's Oil and the Shunammite's Son

- A certain woman of the wives of the sons of the prophets cries to Elisha that her husband is dead, that her husband feared the Lord, and that the creditor has come to take her two sons to be bondmen. Because the husband died in debt, the creditor comes to take the sons to work off what is owed.
- Elisha asks what she has in the house, and she says nothing but a pot of oil. He tells her to borrow empty vessels from all her neighbors, not a few, then shut the door upon herself and her sons and pour oil into all the vessels.
- She pours out, and the oil continues until every vessel is full, and only then does the oil stay. Elisha tells her to sell the oil, pay her debt, and live with her children on the rest. The empty vessels she borrowed from her people, the children of Israel, were filled through the spirit of the Lord.

She perceives that he is a holy man of God and tells her husband to make a little chamber on the wall with a bed, a table, a stool, and a candlestick, so he may turn in whenever he comes.
- On a day when he rests in the chamber, Elisha wants to bless her and sends Gehazi to ask what may be done for her, whether she would be spoken for to the king or the captain of the host. She answers that she dwells among her own people.
- Gehazi tells Elisha the woman has no child and her husband is old. Elisha tells her that about this season, according to the time of life, she shall embrace a son. Like our mother Sarah, she does not believe it and tells the man of God not to lie to his handmaid, yet she conceives and bears a son at that season.

- Elisha tells them to bring meal, casts it into the pot, and says pour out for the people that they may eat, and there is no harm in the pot. He cures the poisoned food so the men may eat safely.

# 2 Kings 5: Naaman Cleansed and Gehazi Struck

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 5]]

**[[2 Kings 5#^v1|2 Kings 5:1-4]]**
![[2 Kings 5#^v1]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v2]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v3]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v4]]
- Naaman, captain of the host of the king of Syria, is a great man with his master and honorable, because by him the Lord had given deliverance to Syria. He is a mighty man of valor, but he is a leper.
- The Syrians had gone out by companies and brought away captive out of the land of Israel a little maid, and she waits on Naaman's wife. This maid is an Israelite sister serving in a foreign house.
- She tells her mistress that if only her lord were with the prophet in Samaria, he would recover him of his leprosy. Naaman goes and tells his lord what the maid of Israel said.

**[[2 Kings 5#^v5|2 Kings 5:5-7]]**
![[2 Kings 5#^v5]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v6]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v7]]
- The king of Syria sends Naaman with a letter to the king of Israel, and Naaman takes ten talents of silver, six thousand pieces of gold, and ten changes of raiment. The letter asks the king of Israel to recover Naaman of his leprosy.
- When the king of Israel reads the letter, he rends his clothes and asks, am I God, to kill and to make alive, that this man sends to me to recover a man of his leprosy. He thinks the king of Syria is seeking a quarrel against him, knowing he has no such power.

**[[2 Kings 5#^v8|2 Kings 5:8-10]]**
![[2 Kings 5#^v8]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v9]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v10]]
- When Elisha the man of God hears that the king of Israel has rent his clothes, he sends to the king asking why, and says let the man come to him, and he shall know that there is a prophet in Israel.
- Naaman comes with his horses and chariot and stands at the door of Elisha's house. Elisha sends a messenger out to him, saying go and wash in Jordan seven times, and thy flesh shall come again to thee, and thou shalt be clean.

**[[2 Kings 5#^v11|2 Kings 5:11-14]]**
![[2 Kings 5#^v11]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v12]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v13]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v14]]
- Naaman is wroth and goes away, saying he thought the prophet would surely come out to him, stand, call on the name of the Lord his God, strike his hand over the place, and recover the leper. He complains in his pride that this was not the process he expected.
- He asks whether Abana and Pharpar, rivers of Damascus, are not better than all the waters of Israel, and turns away in a rage, thinking of a physical cleansing rather than a spiritual one. His mind is carnal, not thinking as the prophet thinks.
- His servants come near and reason with him, that if the prophet had bidden him do some great thing he would have done it; how much rather then when he says only wash and be clean. Taking heed, Naaman goes down and dips himself seven times in Jordan according to the saying of the man of God, and his flesh comes again like the flesh of a little child, and he is clean.

**[[2 Kings 5#^v15|2 Kings 5:15-19]]**
![[2 Kings 5#^v15]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v16]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v17]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v18]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v19]]
- Naaman returns to the man of God with all his company and stands before him, saying, behold, now I know that there is no God in all the earth but in Israel. He asks Elisha to take a blessing of his servant.
- Elisha says, as the Lord liveth before whom I stand, I will receive none, and though Naaman urges him, he refuses. Naaman asks that he be given two mules' burden of earth, for from now on he will offer neither burnt offering nor sacrifice to other gods but to the Lord.
- He asks that the Lord pardon him in one thing, that when his master goes into the house of Rimmon to worship and leans on his hand, and he bows there, the Lord pardon his servant. Elisha says, go in peace, and Naaman departs a little way.

**[[2 Kings 5#^v20|2 Kings 5:20-24]]**
![[2 Kings 5#^v20]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v21]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v22]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v23]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v24]]
- Gehazi, the servant of Elisha, reasons that his master has spared Naaman the Syrian by not receiving what he brought, and swears, as the Lord liveth, he will run after him and take somewhat of him. When Naaman sees him running, he lights down from the chariot to meet him and asks whether all is well.
- Gehazi lies, saying his master has sent him, that two young men of the sons of the prophets have come from mount Ephraim, and asks for a talent of silver and two changes of garments. Naaman urges him to take two talents, binds them in two bags with two changes of garments, and lays them on two of his servants to carry before Gehazi.
- When Gehazi comes to the tower, he takes the goods from their hand, bestows them in the house, and sends the men away, hiding what he took like a thief.

**[[2 Kings 5#^v25|2 Kings 5:25-27]]**
![[2 Kings 5#^v25]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v26]]
![[2 Kings 5#^v27]]
- Gehazi goes in and stands before his master, and Elisha asks where he has come from, and Gehazi says his servant went nowhere. Elisha says, went not my heart with thee when the man turned from his chariot to meet thee; is it a time to receive money, garments, oliveyards, vineyards, sheep, oxen, menservants, and maidservants.
- Because Gehazi took in a time that was not the time to receive, the leprosy of Naaman shall cleave to him and to his seed forever, and he goes out from Elisha's presence a leper as white as snow. This shows how Israel thought then and how the nation still thinks now, hasty to grasp things by unjust means.

    There is a time to get, and a time to lose, which is what Elisha explained to Gehazi, that there was a time to receive the gifts and this was not it.

# 2 Kings 6: The Floating Axe Head and the Blinded Host

- The sons of the prophets tell Elisha the place where they dwell with him is too small, and ask to go to Jordan, take every man a beam, and make a place to dwell. Elisha agrees to go with them.
- As they fell wood by Jordan, one man's axe head falls into the water, and he cries out to Elisha, alas master, for it was borrowed. The axe was not his own.
- Elisha asks where it fell, the man shows him the place, and Elisha cuts down a stick and casts it in, and the iron swims. Though iron sinks by nature, the Lord makes it float, and Elisha tells the man to take it up, and he reaches out his hand and takes it.

- The king of Syria wars against Israel and takes counsel with his servants, choosing where to set his camp. But the man of God sends to the king of Israel warning him to beware of passing a certain place, because the Syrians have come down there.
- The king of Israel sends to the place Elisha warned him of and saves himself, not once nor twice, so the heart of the king of Syria is sore troubled. He calls his servants and asks which of them is for the king of Israel.
- One servant answers that none of them is, but Elisha the prophet in Israel tells the king of Israel the very words the king of Syria speaks in his bedchamber.

- When the servant of the man of God rises early and sees the host with horses and chariots surrounding the city, he says, alas my master, how shall we do. Elisha answers, fear not, for they that be with us are more than they that be with them.
- Elisha prays that the Lord open the young man's eyes, and the Lord opens them so he sees the mountain full of horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha. When Israel goes out to war, there is more with them than with their enemies, for the angels of the Lord are with them.

- In Samaria, Elisha prays that the Lord open their eyes, and they see they are in the midst of the city. The king of Israel asks whether he shall smite them, calling Elisha my father.
- Elisha answers that he shall not smite those he did not take captive by his own sword and bow, but shall set bread and water before them to eat and drink and send them back to their master. A great feast is prepared, and after they eat and drink they are sent away, and the bands of Syria come no more into the land of Israel.

As the king of Israel passes by upon the wall, a woman cries to him for help.
- The king answers, if the Lord does not help thee, from where shall I help thee, out of the barnfloor or the winepress. He asks what ails her.
- The woman tells how another woman said, give thy son that we may eat him today, and we will eat my son tomorrow, so they boiled and ate her son, but the next day the other woman hid her son. When the king hears this, he rends his clothes, and the people see he has sackcloth upon his flesh, and he swears that the head of Elisha shall not stand on him this day.

- While Elisha yet talks with them, the messenger comes down and says, behold, this evil is of the Lord; what should I wait for the Lord any longer. The king and his messenger blame the Lord for the evil and lose patience for his deliverance.
