# 2 Kings 11: The Hidden King and the Covenant to Be the Lord's People

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 11]]

This session covers [[2 Kings 11|2 Kings 11]] through 14. It follows the throne of Judah through a season of crisis and reform: Athaliah destroys the royal seed, but the child Joash is hidden and later crowned, and Jehoiada the priest makes a covenant that the people should again be the Lord's people. From there the reading moves through Joash repairing the house of the Most High, the days of Jehoahaz and Jehoash in Israel, the death of Elisha, and the reign of Amaziah and Jeroboam II. The thread throughout is a call to repent, come out of idolatry, and be instructed in the commandments.

**[[2 Kings 11#^v1|2 Kings 11:1-3]]**

![[2 Kings 11#^v1]]
![[2 Kings 11#^v2]]
![[2 Kings 11#^v3]]

- When Athaliah the mother of Ahaziah saw her son was dead, she moved to destroy all the royal seed so she could seize the throne.
- Jehosheba, sister of Ahaziah, took the infant Joash and stole him away from among the king's sons who were being slain, hiding him in the house of the Most High for six years while Athaliah reigned.
- The rightful king was preserved and concealed the entire time the usurper sat on the throne.

- The guard was to compass the king round about, every man with his weapon in his hand, and to stay with him as he went out and came in.

- Jehoiada gave the captains King David's spears and shields that were in the temple, and the guard stood every man with his weapons in hand around the king.
- Then he brought forth the king's son, put the crown upon him, gave him the testimony, anointed him, and the people clapped their hands and said, God save the king.
- The weapons they carried then were physical; today the weapon of Israel is the scriptures.

- She was not to be slain in the house of the Most High, so they took her out by the way the horses came into the king's house and slew her there.

- Jehoiada made a covenant between the Most High, the king, and the people, that they should be the Lord's people.
- All the people went to the house of Baal, broke down its altars and images, and slew Mattan the priest of Baal before the altars.
- Joash was brought to the throne, the people rejoiced, the city was quiet, and he was seven years old when he began to reign.
- This same covenant is what happens today: as a person wakes up, repents, keeps the commandments, and comes out of idolatry, they again become the Lord's people.

# 2 Kings 12: Repairing the House and Dealing Faithfully

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 12]]

**[[2 Kings 12#^v1|2 Kings 12:1-3]]**

![[2 Kings 12#^v1]]
![[2 Kings 12#^v2]]
![[2 Kings 12#^v3]]

- Jehoash began to reign in the seventh year of Jehu and reigned forty years in Jerusalem.
- He did that which was right in the sight of the Most High all the days that Jehoiada the priest instructed him.
- No one comes to the truth alone; the Most High set up a structure of instruction, and a person must be taught the word rather than claiming private revelation.
  - **[[Romans 10#^v13|Romans 10:13-15]]**
    ![[Romans 10#^v13]]
    ![[Romans 10#^v14]]
    ![[Romans 10#^v15]]
    None can call on the Lord unless they believe, none believe unless they hear, and none hear without a preacher who is sent to guide and instruct.

**[[2 Kings 12#^v4|2 Kings 12:4-8]]**

![[2 Kings 12#^v4]]
![[2 Kings 12#^v5]]
![[2 Kings 12#^v6]]
![[2 Kings 12#^v7]]
![[2 Kings 12#^v8]]

- Jehoash ordered that all the dedicated money brought into the house be gathered by the priests to repair the breaches of the house.
- But by the twenty-third year of his reign the priests still had not repaired the breaches, though he had come to power around age seven.
- Jehoash called Jehoiada and the priests, asked why the house was not repaired, and directed that the money no longer be received by them but delivered directly for the repairs.

**[[2 Kings 12#^v9|2 Kings 12:9-16]]**

![[2 Kings 12#^v9]]
![[2 Kings 12#^v10]]
![[2 Kings 12#^v11]]

![[2 Kings 12#^v14]]
![[2 Kings 12#^v15]]
![[2 Kings 12#^v16]]

- Jehoiada took a chest, bored a hole in the lid, and set it beside the altar, and the priests put in all the money brought to the house.
- When there was much money, the king's scribe and the high priest counted it and gave it into the hands of the workmen who oversaw the repair.
- The money went to carpenters, builders, masons, and for timber and stone, and none was diverted to make vessels; the workmen dealt faithfully so no reckoning was needed.
- This is why records must be kept, especially where money is concerned; all things touching the body must be put in writing.
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 42#^v7|Ecclesiasticus 42:7]]**  Deliver all things in number and weight; and put all in writing that thou givest out, or receivest in.

- Jehoash took the hallowed things dedicated by the former kings, and the gold from the treasures, and sent them to Hazael, who then went away.
- His own servants made a conspiracy and slew Joash in the house of Millo, and Amaziah his son reigned in his place.

- Jehoahaz besought the Most High, who heard him and gave Israel a saviour so they escaped the hand of the Syrians, yet they did not depart from the sins of the house of Jeroboam.

- Then Elisha told him to smite the ground, and the king smote only three times and stopped; Elisha was angry, saying he should have smitten five or six times to consume Syria, but now would smite Syria only three times.

# 2 Kings 14: Amaziah, the Imperfect Heart, and Jeroboam II

- Amaziah the son of Joash began to reign over Judah at twenty-five and reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem.
- He did that which was right in the sight of the Most High, yet not like David his father, meaning he did not serve with a perfect heart.
- When the kingdom was confirmed, he slew the servants who had killed his father, but did not slay their children, according to the law of Moses that every man is put to death for his own sin.
  - **[[2 Chronicles 25#^v1|2 Chronicles 25:1-2]]**
    ![[2 Chronicles 25#^v1]]
    ![[2 Chronicles 25#^v2]]
    Amaziah did right in the sight of the Lord, but not with a perfect heart, explaining why he was not counted like David.
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 49#^v4|Ecclesiasticus 49:4]]**  For all except David and Ezekias and Josias were defective: for they forsook the law of the most High, even the kings of Juda failed.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 24#^v16|Deuteronomy 24:16]]**

- Jehoash broke down the wall of Jerusalem, took the gold, silver, and vessels from the house of the Most High and the king's house, took hostages, and returned to Samaria.
