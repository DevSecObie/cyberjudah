# 2 Kings 15: The Kings of a Divided House

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 15]]

This session covers [[2 Kings 15|2 Kings 15]] through 18. It follows the fast turnover of kings in Israel, marked by conspiracy and murder, while Judah's kings walk in mixed obedience. It moves through the rise of Assyria against the land, the idolatry of Ahaz, the carrying away of Israel into captivity for breaking the covenant, and the reforms of Hezekiah as Assyria begins to posture against Judah. The thread throughout is that the Most High keeps His word: He blesses those who keep His commandments and delivers into the hand of spoilers those who forsake them.

# 2 Kings 16: Ahaz and the Altar of Damascus

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 16]]

**[[2 Kings 16#^v1|2 Kings 16:1-4]]**
![[2 Kings 16#^v1]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v2]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v3]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v4]]
- Ahaz son of Jotham begins to reign over Judah and does not that which is right, but walks in the way of the kings of Israel.
- He makes his son to pass through the fire after the abominations of the heathen whom the Most High cast out before Israel.
- He sacrifices and burns incense in the high places, on the hills, and under every green tree, the very idolatry the law forbade.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 12#^v2|Deuteronomy 12:2-4]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 12#^v2]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 12#^v3]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 12#^v4]]
    The nations' places of worship on the mountains, hills, and under every green tree were to be utterly destroyed, not imitated.

**[[2 Kings 16#^v5|2 Kings 16:5-9]]**
![[2 Kings 16#^v5]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v6]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v7]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v8]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v9]]
- Rezin king of Syria and Pekah king of Israel come up to war against Jerusalem and besiege Ahaz but cannot overcome him.
- Syria recovers Elath and drives the Jews out, and Ahaz is left weakened.
- Rather than trust the Most High, Ahaz sends to Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria saying, I am thy servant and thy son, come up and save me.
- This is much like the nation today looking to Esau as though he were a savior instead of turning to the Most High.
- Ahaz strips the silver and gold from the house of the Most High and the king's treasures and sends it as a present to Assyria, who then takes Damascus and slays Rezin.

**[[2 Kings 16#^v10|2 Kings 16:10-16]]**
![[2 Kings 16#^v10]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v11]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v12]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v13]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v14]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v15]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v16]]
- Ahaz goes to Damascus to meet the king of Assyria and sees an altar there, then sends its pattern and workmanship to Urijah the priest.
- Urijah builds the altar according to the pattern before Ahaz returns, and the king offers his burnt offering, meat offering, drink offering, and peace offering upon it.
- The true altar was made after the pattern of heavenly things given through Moses, but Ahaz copies a heathen altar he admired in Damascus.
- Ahaz moves the brazen altar and commands Urijah to make the new altar the place of all the offerings, keeping the brazen altar for himself to inquire by.
  - **[[Hebrews 8#^v5|Hebrews 8:5]]**
    ![[Hebrews 8#^v5]]
    Moses was warned to make all things according to the pattern shown in the mount, not after the fashion of the nations.

**[[2 Kings 16#^v17|2 Kings 16:17-20]]**
![[2 Kings 16#^v17]]
![[2 Kings 16#^v18]]

![[2 Kings 16#^v20]]
- Ahaz cuts off the borders of the bases, removes the laver, and takes down the brazen sea from off the oxen, dismantling the vessels of the temple.
- He turns the covert for the sabbath and the king's entry away from the house of the Most High for the sake of the king of Assyria.
- Ahaz sleeps with his fathers and Hezekiah his son reigns in his stead.

# 2 Kings 17: The Carrying Away of Israel

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 17]]

**[[2 Kings 17#^v1|2 Kings 17:1-6]]**
![[2 Kings 17#^v1]]
![[2 Kings 17#^v2]]
![[2 Kings 17#^v3]]
![[2 Kings 17#^v4]]
![[2 Kings 17#^v5]]
![[2 Kings 17#^v6]]
- Hoshea son of Elah reigns nine years over Israel in Samaria and does evil, though not as the kings before him.
- Shalmaneser king of Assyria comes up, and Hoshea becomes his servant and pays tribute.
- The king of Assyria finds conspiracy in Hoshea because he sent messengers to So king of Egypt and stopped his yearly tribute, so he shuts him up in prison.
- Assyria besieges Samaria three years, then in the ninth year of Hoshea takes it and carries Israel away to Assyria, placing them in Halah, Habor, and the cities of the Medes.

**[[2 Kings 17#^v7|2 Kings 17:7-12]]**
![[2 Kings 17#^v7]]

- This captivity comes because the children of Israel sinned against the Most High who brought them out of Egypt and feared other gods.
- They walked in the statutes of the heathen, built high places in all their cities, and set up images and groves on every high hill and under every green tree.
- They wrought wicked things to provoke the Most High and served idols, though He had said plainly, ye shall not do this thing.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 12#^v2|Deuteronomy 12:2-3]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 12#^v2]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 12#^v3]]
    The law had already commanded Israel to destroy such high places, not build them.

- The Most High testified against Israel and Judah by all the prophets and seers, calling them to turn from evil and keep His commandments.
- They would not hear but hardened their necks like their fathers who did not believe in the Most High.
- To believe in the Most High is to take heed to the commandments, so those who claim belief while breaking the commandments do not truly believe.
- They rejected His statutes and covenant, followed vanity, made molten images and two calves, worshipped the host of heaven, and served Baal.
- They caused their sons and daughters to pass through the fire, used divinations and enchantments, and sold themselves to do evil.
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 32#^v24|Ecclesiasticus 32:24]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 32#^v24]]
    He that believeth in the Most High taketh heed to the commandments, tying true belief to obedience.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 4#^v16|Deuteronomy 4:16-19]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 4#^v16]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 4#^v17]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 4#^v18]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 4#^v19]]
    Israel was warned not to corrupt themselves by worshipping the host of heaven.

**[[2 Kings 17#^v18|2 Kings 17:18-23]]**
![[2 Kings 17#^v18]]

![[2 Kings 17#^v21]]

- The Most High is very angry and removes Israel out of His sight, leaving only the tribe of Judah.
- Judah also fails to keep the commandments and walks in the statutes that Israel made.
- The Most High rejects all the seed of Israel, afflicts them, and delivers them into the hand of spoilers until He casts them out of His sight.
- This rejection is why the seed of Israel are later called heathens and Gentiles in the New Testament, which resolves much of the struggle over Jews and Gentiles there.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 32#^v21|Deuteronomy 32:21]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 32#^v21]]
    The Most High moved Israel to jealousy and anger, foretelling their rejection among the nations.

- Because they do not fear the Most High, He sends lions among them that slay some of them.
- One of the carried-away priests is brought back to Bethel to teach the settlers how they should fear the Most High.

- The men of Babylon made Succoth-benoth, Cuth made Nergal, Hamath made Ashima, the Avites made Nibhaz and Tartak, and the Sepharvites burnt their children to Adrammelech and Anammelech.
- They feared the Most High and also served their own gods after the manner of the nations they came from.
- The Most High made His covenant and charged the children of Jacob whom He named Israel, showing the covenant belongs to Israel and not to all nations.

- They were to observe His statutes, ordinances, and commandments forevermore, and He would deliver them out of the hand of all their enemies.
- No nation and no treaty can redeem Israel from their condition, only turning to the Most High can.
- These settler nations feared the Most High yet served their graven images to this day, and as a nation Israel does the same, serving the gods of the nations.

# 2 Kings 18: Hezekiah and the Assyrian Threat

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 18]]

**[[2 Kings 18#^v1|2 Kings 18:1-4]]**
![[2 Kings 18#^v1]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v2]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v3]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v4]]
- Hezekiah son of Ahaz begins to reign over Judah in the third year of Hoshea, the last king of Israel before the captivity.
- He does that which is right according to all that David his father did, removing the high places, breaking the images, and cutting down the groves.
- He breaks in pieces the brazen serpent Moses made in the wilderness, which had stood from that time and was now being burned incense to, and he calls it Nehushtan.

**[[2 Kings 18#^v5|2 Kings 18:5-8]]**
![[2 Kings 18#^v5]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v6]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v7]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v8]]
- Hezekiah trusts in the Most High so that none among the kings of Judah, before or after, was like him.
- He clave to the Most High, kept the commandments, and the Most High was with him so that he prospered.
- Prospering here does not mean a mansion or luxury, but the blessing that comes from keeping and doing the commandments.
- He rebels against the king of Assyria and smites the Philistines even unto Gaza.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 29#^v9|Deuteronomy 29:9]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 29#^v9]]
    Keep the words of the covenant and do them, that ye may prosper in all that ye do.

**[[2 Kings 18#^v9|2 Kings 18:9-12]]**
![[2 Kings 18#^v9]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v10]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v11]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v12]]
- In Hezekiah's fourth year Shalmaneser besieges Samaria, and after three years, in Hezekiah's sixth year, Samaria is taken.
- Israel was not removed all at once but carried away in stages into Assyria.
- The reason given again is that they obeyed not the voice of the Most High but transgressed His covenant and all Moses commanded.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v15|Deuteronomy 28:15]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v15]]
    The captivity comes because Israel would not hearken to the voice of the Most High.

**[[2 Kings 18#^v13|2 Kings 18:13-16]]**
![[2 Kings 18#^v13]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v14]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v15]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v16]]
- Sennacherib king of Assyria comes up against the fenced cities of Judah and takes them.
- Hezekiah sends to Lakish acknowledging offense and agrees to bear whatever tribute is laid on him.
- Assyria appoints three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold, so Hezekiah gives all the silver of the house of the Most High and even cuts the gold from the temple doors.

**[[2 Kings 18#^v17|2 Kings 18:17-25]]**
![[2 Kings 18#^v17]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v18]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v19]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v20]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v21]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v22]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v23]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v24]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v25]]
- The king of Assyria sends Tartan, Rabsaris, and Rabshakeh with a great host to Jerusalem, and they stand by the conduit of the upper pool.
- Rabshakeh challenges Hezekiah's confidence, mocking his trust and calling Egypt a bruised reed that pierces the hand of any who lean on it.
- He argues that because Hezekiah removed the high places, even Judah's own worship is now confined to one altar, twisting the reform into a weakness.
- He boasts that Assyria came up against the land by the word of the Most High Himself, claiming divine backing to destroy it.

**[[2 Kings 18#^v26|2 Kings 18:26-37]]**
![[2 Kings 18#^v26]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v27]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v28]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v29]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v30]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v31]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v32]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v33]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v34]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v35]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v36]]
![[2 Kings 18#^v37]]
- Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah ask Rabshakeh to speak in the Syrian language so the people on the wall will not understand, but he refuses.
- Rabshakeh cries aloud in the Jews' language, warning the people not to let Hezekiah deceive them or make them trust in the Most High.
- He promises the people their own vines, fig trees, and waters if they surrender, then a land like their own, seeking to turn them from their king.
- He asks whether the gods of Hamath, Arpad, Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivah delivered their lands, claiming no god can deliver Jerusalem out of his hand.
- The people hold their peace and answer not a word, for Hezekiah had commanded silence.
- Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah come to Hezekiah with their clothes rent and tell him the words of Rabshakeh.
