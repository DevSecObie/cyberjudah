# 2 Kings 19: The Most High Defends Jerusalem

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 19]]

This session covers [[2 Kings 19|2 Kings 19]] through 22. It opens with Hezekiah turning to the Most High when Assyria blasphemes the living God, and the prophet Isaiah returning the word that Sennacherib will fall by the sword in his own land. It shows the Most High schooling the proud king of Assyria, reminding him that his power to lay cities waste was given by the Most High for His own purposes. It moves through the healing of Hezekiah and his folly with the envoys of Babylon, the deep wickedness of Manasseh and Amon, and the reforms of Josiah who found the book of the law. The thread throughout is that the Most High keeps His word: He hears the prayer of the humble, defends His city for His own sake and for David's sake, and brings righteous judgment upon those who blaspheme and forsake Him. The prophets Isaiah, Micah, and Hosea speak the same thing in this same time period, because they were all sent to declare what would come upon the nation.

- When Hezekiah hears the reproach of Assyria he rends his clothes, covers himself with sackcloth, and goes into the house of the Most High, then sends his servants to Isaiah the prophet.
- He calls it a day of trouble, rebuke, and blasphemy, and asks Isaiah to lift up prayer for the remnant that is left.
- This follows chapter 18, where Rabshakeh the messenger of Sennacherib told Judah their God was no different from the gods of the nations Assyria had already destroyed.
- Isaiah, Micah, and Hosea prophesied in this same period, so much of what is read in one is found in the others.
- Isaiah returns the word of the Most High: fear not the blasphemy, for the Most High will send a blast upon Sennacherib, and he shall hear a rumor, return to his own land, and fall by the sword there.

- The letter mocks Judah's trust, listing the nations and kings Assyria destroyed, saying their gods could not deliver them, so Jerusalem will not be delivered either.

- Hezekiah receives the letter, reads it, goes up to the house of the Most High, and spreads it before Him.
- He prays acknowledging the Most High as God alone of all the kingdoms of the earth, maker of heaven and earth, and asks Him to bow down His ear and hear the words that reproached the living God.
- He confesses that the gods Assyria cast into the fire were no gods but the work of men's hands, wood and stone, and asks the Most High to save them so all the nations may know He alone is the Lord God.
- The prayer of Hezekiah is that the Most High show His power and prove His people are the chosen and that He is the one true living God. This is a righteous prayer, the same praying for the downfall of enemies and for righteous judgment to come upon them.

- The Most High asks whom Sennacherib has reproached and blasphemed, for he has lifted up his eyes on high against the Holy One of Israel. Israel is laughing at the enemy, for it is already written that they lose.
- Assyria boasted with the multitude of his chariots to cut down cedars and dry up rivers, the same as a nation today boasting of its army, military, and political system.
- The Most High answers that He did it and formed it of ancient times; He gave Assyria the very ability to lay fenced cities waste as ruinous heaps for His own purpose.
- Because their rage and tumult came up to His ears, the Most High will put His hook in Sennacherib's nose and His bridle in his lips, and turn him back the way he came, which joins with the blast promised in verse 7.

- The Most High will defend the city to save it for His own sake and for His servant David's sake; what He did to Samaria He will not do to Jerusalem.

# 2 Kings 20: Hezekiah's Healing and His Folly

- Hezekiah falls sick unto death, and Isaiah brings the word to set his house in order, for he shall die and not live.
- Hezekiah turns his face to the wall and prays, asking the Most High to remember how he walked before Him in truth and with a perfect heart, and he weeps sore.
- Before Isaiah leaves the middle court the word returns: the Most High has heard his prayer and seen his tears, will heal him, add fifteen years, and defend the city for His own sake and for David's sake.
- Isaiah applies a lump of figs to the boil and Hezekiah recovers, showing that remedies and herbs go together with prayer, for healing is in the hand of the Most High.
  - **[[Wisdom of Solomon 16#^v12|Wisdom of Solomon 16:12]]**
    ![[Wisdom of Solomon 16#^v12]]
    It is neither herb nor plaster that heals, but the word of the Most High, in whose hand is our healing.

- Hezekiah asks for a sign that the Most High will heal him and that he shall go up to the house of the Most High on the third day.
- Isaiah offers the shadow going forward ten degrees or back ten degrees, and Hezekiah answers that going forward is a light thing, but let it return backward ten degrees.
- Isaiah cries to the Most High and He brings the shadow ten degrees backward on the dial of Ahaz, reversing the natural course of the sun as proof of His power.

- Berodach-baladan son of Baladan king of Babylon sends letters and a present to Hezekiah, having heard he had been sick; this is the introduction of Babylon, still only a province under Assyria at this time.
- Hezekiah hearkens to them and shows them all his precious things, his silver, gold, spices, ointment, and armor, holding nothing back, trusting his enemies because of the gift.
- Isaiah asks what the men saw and where they came from, then declares that all in the house shall be carried into Babylon, and Hezekiah's own sons shall be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.
- Hezekiah answers that the word of the Most High is good, content that peace and truth be in his days. This same account is given in [[Isaiah 39|Isaiah 39]].

- Bringing water into the city is vision and planning like no other; this is nation building.
- Hezekiah sleeps with his fathers, and Manasseh his son reigns in his stead.

# 2 Kings 21: The Wickedness of Manasseh and Amon

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 21]]

**[[2 Kings 21#^v1|2 Kings 21:1-9]]**

![[2 Kings 21#^v2]]
![[2 Kings 21#^v3]]

![[2 Kings 21#^v8]]

- Manasseh begins to reign at twelve years old and reigns fifty-five years, doing evil after the abominations of the heathen the Most High cast out before Israel.
- He rebuilds the high places Hezekiah destroyed, rears altars for Baal, makes a grove as Ahab did, and worships the host of heaven, even building altars in the house of the Most High where He set His name; this is a wickedness with zero fear of the Most High.
- The people did not serve idols for nothing but for the self-gratification in it, the same as men today keep pagan feasts for the gifts, the food, and the reveling, not for what the day claims to be.
- He makes his son pass through the fire, observes times, uses enchantments, and deals with familiar spirits and wizards, provoking the Most High to anger, and seduces Judah to do more evil than the nations the Most High destroyed.
- Repentance is always possible; our people are repenting, not repented, so no man should judge before the time who is or is not of the two-thirds. Continue in the work of the Most High and let Christ judge in the end.

- He will forsake the remnant of His inheritance and deliver them into the hand of their enemies to be a prey and a spoil, because they have provoked Him to anger since the day their fathers came out of Egypt. The Most High has borne with this wickedness for hundreds of years.

- Manasseh sheds innocent blood very much, filling Jerusalem from one end to the other, beside the sin with which he made Judah to sin.
- His remaining acts are written in the chronicles of the kings of Judah, and the prayer of Manasseh is recorded in the Apocrypha, prayed while he was held captive in Babylon.
- Chronicles shows the captains of the king of Assyria took Manasseh among the thorns and carried him with fetters to Babylon, then a province under Assyria; there he repented and prayed.
- The history shows a power struggle beginning, where Assyrian kings ruled over Babylon, and later Babylonian kings ruled over Assyria; Manasseh sleeps with his fathers and Amon his son reigns.
  - **[[2 Chronicles 33#^v11|2 Chronicles 33:11]]**

    Nebuchadnezzar, a Babylonian, reigns over the Assyrians in Nineveh, showing Babylon rising to take all that Assyria held.

- He forsakes the Most High God of his fathers and walks not in His way.
- His own servants conspire against him and slay him in his house; the people of the land slay the conspirators and make Josiah his son king in his stead.

# 2 Kings 22: Josiah and the Book of the Law

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 22]]

**[[2 Kings 22#^v1|2 Kings 22:1-7]]**
![[2 Kings 22#^v1]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v2]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v3]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v4]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v5]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v6]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v7]]
- Josiah begins to reign at eight years old and reigns thirty-one years, doing that which is right in the sight of the Most High and walking in all the way of David, turning neither to the right nor the left.
- In his eighteenth year he sends Shaphan the scribe to Hilkiah the priest to gather the silver brought to the house of the Most High and deliver it to the workmen for repairs.
- The money is given to carpenters, builders, and masons and for timber and hewn stone, and no reckoning is made with them because they dealt faithfully.
- Josiah rebuilds and reestablishes what Manasseh and Amon destroyed, and he makes sure the workmen are paid, for by the law the workman is worthy of his hire.

**[[2 Kings 22#^v8|2 Kings 22:8-13]]**
![[2 Kings 22#^v8]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v9]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v10]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v11]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v12]]

- Hilkiah the high priest finds the book of the law in the house of the Most High and gives it to Shaphan, who reads it. Manasseh and Amon were so wicked the very book of the law had been lost.
- Shaphan reports to the king about the money and the workmen, then tells him Hilkiah delivered him a book, and reads it before the king.
- When the king hears the words of the book of the law he rends his clothes, for the king was meant to read the law before all the people, which the wicked kings had not done.
- Josiah commands Hilkiah and his servants to inquire of the Most High for him and the people, for great is the wrath kindled against them because their fathers did not hearken to this book.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v15|Deuteronomy 28:15]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v15]]
    If they will not hearken to do all the commandments, all these curses shall come upon them, the wrath Josiah heard when the book was read.

**[[2 Kings 22#^v14|2 Kings 22:14-20]]**
![[2 Kings 22#^v14]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v15]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v16]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v17]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v18]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v19]]
![[2 Kings 22#^v20]]
- Hilkiah and the king's men go to Huldah the prophetess, who dwells in Jerusalem in the college; there were women among the people who were prophetesses, known by their works.
- Huldah returns the word of the Most High: He will bring evil upon this place and its inhabitants, all the words of the book, because they forsook Him and burned incense to other gods, and His wrath shall not be quenched.
- But to Josiah, because his heart was tender and he humbled himself and wept before the Most High, the word is that he shall be gathered to his fathers and to his grave in peace, and his eyes shall not see the evil to come.
- Because Josiah was righteous, repaired the temple, found the book, and began to keep the commandments again, he found mercy; yet the word spoken against Israel for its sin would still come to pass.
