# 2 Kings 23: Josiah Reads the Law and Cleanses the Land

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 23]]

This session covers [[2 Kings 23|2 Kings 23]] through 25. It follows Josiah as he hears the book of the law that was found in the house of the Most High, rends his clothes in the fear of the Lord, and leads the whole nation in a covenant to keep the commandments. He tears down the idols, altars, and high places that his fathers had set up, keeps a great Passover, and turns to the Lord with all his heart. Yet the wrath kindled by Manasseh's sins is not turned away: Josiah is slain at Megiddo, his sons do evil, and the reading moves through the fall of Judah, the burning of the temple, and the carrying away of the people to Babylon, closing with a note of mercy shown to Jehoiachin. The thread throughout is repentance: seeing in the book of the law why the nation is in its condition and turning back to keep what is written.

- Josiah gathered all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem, went up to the house of the Most High with the priests, the prophets, and all the people both small and great, and read in their ears all the words of the book of the covenant that was found there.
- Our forefathers had the Bible; the king read it openly, then stood by a pillar and made a covenant before the Lord to walk after him and keep his commandments, testimonies, and statutes with all their heart and soul.
- All the people stood to the covenant to perform the words written in the book.

- The king commanded Hilkiah and the priests to bring out of the temple all the vessels made for Baal, for the grove, and for all the host of heaven, and he burned them in the fields of Kidron and carried their ashes to Bethel.
- He put down the idolatrous priests whom the kings of Judah had ordained to burn incense in the high places and around Jerusalem, and those that burnt incense to Baal, to the sun, the moon, the planets, and all the host of heaven.
- When Josiah read the law the fear of the Lord came upon him and he saw why the nation was under judgment, just as a person is cut when they first hear the truth and realize what they have been doing wrong.

    ![[Deuteronomy 7#^v25]]

    ![[Deuteronomy 4#^v19]]
    Israel was not to lift up their eyes and be driven to worship the sun, moon, and stars, which the Lord divided to the other nations.

- He brought the grove out of the house of the Lord to the brook Kidron, burned it, stamped it to powder, and cast the powder on the graves of the children of the people.
- He broke down the houses of the sodomites that were by the house of the Lord, where the women wove hangings for the grove; there should be no sodomite among the sons of Israel, yet they had grown so bold as to do this wickedness right beside the temple.
- He brought all the priests out of the cities of Judah and defiled the high places where they had burnt incense, and though the priests of the high places did not come up to the altar, they still ate of the unleavened bread among their brethren.

- Josiah defiled Topheth in the valley of the children of Hinnom so that no man might make his son or daughter pass through the fire to Molech.
- Topheth was the place where the people sacrificed their children, and it had to be defiled because Israel would not stop the evil on its own.

- He took away the horses the kings of Judah had given to the sun and burnt the chariots of the sun with fire.
- He beat down the altars on the roof of Ahaz's chamber and the altars Manasseh had made in the two courts of the house of the Lord, and cast their dust into the brook Kidron.
- He defiled the high places Solomon had built for Ashtoreth, Chemosh, and Milcom, the abominations of the Zidonians, Moabites, and Ammonites, and broke in pieces the images, cut down the groves, and filled their places with the bones of men.
- This is repentance in action: cleaning house, getting rid of every idol and evil that causes a people to go off from the Lord and to fall under punishment.

- Josiah also destroyed the altar and high place at Bethel that Jeroboam had made when he set up the golden calves, burning it and stamping it small to powder.
- Turning himself, he spied the sepulchres in the mount, took out the bones, and burnt them upon the altar to pollute it, according to the word the man of God had proclaimed.
- When told which sepulchre belonged to the man of God who prophesied these things, he said to let him alone and let no man move his bones.

    ![[1 Kings 13#^v2]]

![[2 Kings 23#^v19]]
![[2 Kings 23#^v20]]

- Josiah also took away all the houses of the high places in the cities of Samaria, which the kings of Israel had made to provoke the Lord to anger, and did to them as he had done at Bethel.
- The northern kingdom had already been carried into captivity by Assyria, so most of the people were gone, yet the idolatrous houses remained.
- He slew all the priests of the high places upon their altars, burnt men's bones upon them, and returned to Jerusalem.

**[[2 Kings 23#^v21|2 Kings 23:21-23]]**

![[2 Kings 23#^v21]]
![[2 Kings 23#^v22]]
![[2 Kings 23#^v23]]

- The king commanded all the people to keep the Passover to the Lord their God as it is written in the book of this covenant.
- Such a Passover had not been held from the days of the judges nor in all the days of the kings of Israel and Judah, but in the eighteenth year of Josiah this Passover was held in Jerusalem.
- Reading the book of the law brought the nation back to keeping the appointed feasts in order, just as the feast is kept in order today.
  - **[[1 Esdras 1#^v1|1 Esdras 1:1]]**
    ![[1 Esdras 1#^v1]]
    Josiah held the Passover on the fourteenth day of the first month.
  - **[[1 Esdras 1#^v9|1 Esdras 1:9]]**
    ![[1 Esdras 1#^v9]]
    The magnitude of the offering showed how great and well ordered this Passover was.

- Josiah also put away the workers with familiar spirits, the wizards, the images, the idols, and all the abominations spied in Judah and Jerusalem, that he might perform the words of the law found by Hilkiah.
- There was no king before or after him that turned to the Lord with all his heart, soul, and might according to all the law of Moses; he put things in order like a true leader.
- Nevertheless the Lord did not turn from the fierceness of his great wrath because of the provocations of Manasseh, and said he would remove Judah out of his sight as he had removed Israel and cast off Jerusalem.

- Josiah got involved in a matter that did not concern him and lost his life in it, a warning not to enter into quarrels that are not one's own.
- His servants carried him dead in a chariot from Megiddo to Jerusalem and buried him, and the people took Jehoahaz his son and anointed him king in his father's place.

# 2 Kings 24: Judah Carried Away for the Sins of Manasseh

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 24]]

**[[2 Kings 24#^v1|2 Kings 24:1-4]]**

![[2 Kings 24#^v1]]
![[2 Kings 24#^v2]]
![[2 Kings 24#^v3]]
![[2 Kings 24#^v4]]

- In Jehoiakim's days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up, and Jehoiakim became his servant three years, then turned and rebelled against him.
- The Lord sent bands of the Chaldeans, Syrians, Moabites, and Ammonites against Judah to destroy it according to the word he spake by his prophets.
- This came upon Judah at the commandment of the Lord to remove them out of his sight for the sins of Manasseh and the innocent blood he shed, which the Lord would not pardon.
  - **[[Jeremiah 4#^v6|Jeremiah 4:6]]**
    ![[Jeremiah 4#^v6]]
    The Lord warned he would bring evil from the north, a great destruction, which is the army of Babylon that came against Judah.

![[2 Kings 24#^v5]]
![[2 Kings 24#^v6]]
![[2 Kings 24#^v7]]

- Jehoiakim slept with his fathers and Jehoiachin his son reigned in his place.
- The king of Egypt came no more out of his land, for the king of Babylon had taken all that pertained to Egypt from the river of Egypt to the river Euphrates.
- Babylon took over as had been prophesied throughout the Bible, in Daniel and in Jeremiah, that Nebuchadnezzar would ransack Jerusalem and the surrounding lands.

- The servants of Nebuchadnezzar came up against Jerusalem and besieged it, and the king himself came against the city while it was besieged.
- Jehoiachin went out to the king of Babylon with his mother, servants, princes, and officers, and the king of Babylon took him in the eighth year of his reign.

- He carried away all Jerusalem, all the princes, all the mighty men of valour, ten thousand captives, and all the craftsmen and smiths, leaving only the poorest of the people of the land.
- He carried Jehoiachin, the king's mother, wives, and officers to Babylon, along with the men of might and craftsmen and smiths, wanting them to build for him what they had built in their own land, as conquering nations always used Israel's skill and industry.

- Through the anger of the Lord it came to pass in Jerusalem and Judah, until he had cast them out of his presence, that Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.

# 2 Kings 25: The Fall of Jerusalem and the Burning of the Temple

Read the chapter → [[2 Kings 25]]

**[[2 Kings 25#^v1|2 Kings 25:1-3]]**

![[2 Kings 25#^v1]]
![[2 Kings 25#^v2]]
![[2 Kings 25#^v3]]

- In the ninth year of Zedekiah's reign Nebuchadnezzar came with all his host against Jerusalem, pitched against it, and built forts round about it.
- The city was besieged until the eleventh year of Zedekiah, and the famine prevailed in the city.
- Because the city was besieged no food, water, or goods could get in, so there was no bread for the people of the land.

**[[2 Kings 25#^v4|2 Kings 25:4-7]]**

![[2 Kings 25#^v4]]
![[2 Kings 25#^v5]]
![[2 Kings 25#^v6]]
![[2 Kings 25#^v7]]

- The city was broken up and the men of war fled by night by the way of the gate between two walls by the king's garden, while the Chaldeans were round about the city.
- The army pursued the king, overtook him in the plains of Jericho, and his army was scattered from him, and they took him to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where judgment was given upon him.
- They slew Zedekiah's sons before his eyes, then put out his eyes, bound him with fetters of brass, and carried him to Babylon, just as Jeremiah had warned him.

![[2 Kings 25#^v8]]

- In the fifth month of the nineteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard came to Jerusalem.
- He burnt the house of the Lord, the king's house, and every great man's house, and the army broke down the walls of Jerusalem round about.
- He carried away the rest of the people and the fugitives, but left the poor of the land to be vinedressers and husbandmen, since the poor could not fend for themselves.

- The Chaldeans broke in pieces the pillars of brass, the bases, and the brasen sea that were in the house of the Lord, and carried the brass to Babylon because it was valuable.
- They took away the pots, shovels, snuffers, spoons, and vessels of brass, and the firepans and bowls of gold and silver the captain of the guard took away.
- The two pillars, one sea, and the bases Solomon had made were of brass without weight, the one pillar eighteen cubits high with a brasen chapiter, wreathen work, and pomegranates round about.

- The captain of the guard took Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the second priest, and the three keepers of the door.
- He took an officer over the men of war, five men in the king's presence, the principal scribe of the host, and threescore men of the people found in the city.
- Nebuzaradan brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah, and the king smote and slew them there; so Judah was carried away out of their land.

- Over the people left in the land Nebuchadnezzar made Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, the son of Shaphan, governor.
- Gedaliah counselled the captains and their men not to fear to serve the Chaldeans but to dwell in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it would be well with them.
- But Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, of the royal seed, came with ten men and smote Gedaliah and the Jews and Chaldeans with him, and the people fled to Egypt for fear of the Chaldeans, killing a man who gave wise counsel.

**[[2 Kings 25#^v27|2 Kings 25:27-30]]**

- In the thirty seventh year of the captivity of Jehoiachin, Evilmerodach king of Babylon lifted up his head out of prison and spake kindly to him.
- He set his throne above the thrones of the other kings in Babylon, changed his prison garments, and let him eat bread continually before him all the days of his life.
- Jehoiachin was given a continual daily allowance for the rest of his life, a mercy shown at the very end of the account.
