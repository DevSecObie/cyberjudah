# 2 Samuel 21: The Famine, the Gibeonites, and the Sons of the Giant

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 21]]

## Introduction

This session finishes the book of 2 Samuel, chapters 21 through 24. A three year famine comes upon Israel because Saul broke his oath and slew the Gibeonites, so David makes atonement and Israel learns to pay what it vows unto the Most High. The Philistines war again, and David, now aged, is delivered by his mighty men while the sons of the giant are slain. David then sings his great song of deliverance, praising the Most High as his rock and giving all his strength and might back to the God who trained his hands to war. His last words set the standard for a just ruler. Finally David numbers the people, the Most High sends a pestilence, and David buys the threshing floor of Araunah and builds an altar so the plague is stayed. The running thread is trust in the Most High and not in man, keeping the ways of the Lord, and giving him all the glory for the strength that is his and not our own.

The Gibeonites were not of the children of Israel but of the remnant of the Amorites, and Israel had sworn unto them, yet Saul sought to slay them in his zeal.
- David asked the Gibeonites what he should do and wherewith he should make the atonement. The blessing was not truly coming from the Gibeonites who are of Ammon; it was coming from the Most High, who was teaching Israel a lesson through this famine. The Gibeonites would take no silver nor gold of Saul nor of his house, but asked for seven of Saul's sons to be hanged unto the Lord in Gibeah of Saul.
- The lesson the Most High is teaching Israel here is to pay what you vow. Let your yea be yea and your nay be nay; Saul went back on his word after he had given his yea to the Amorites, and the whole house paid for it.

# 2 Samuel 22: The Lord Is My Rock and My Fortress

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 22]]

## Introduction

This chapter is David's song of deliverance, spoken in the day the Most High delivered him out of the hand of all his enemies and out of the hand of Saul. David praises the Most High as his rock, his fortress, his shield and the horn of his salvation, and he confesses that all his strength, his war, and his victories came from God and not from himself. The thread is trust in the Lord, keeping his ways, and giving the glory of every deliverance back to the Most High.

# 2 Samuel 23: The Last Words of David and His Mighty Men

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 23]]

## Introduction

This chapter gives the last words of David, the sweet psalmist of Israel, through whom the spirit of the Lord spoke. He sets the standard for a just ruler: he that ruleth over men must be just, ruling in the fear of God. Then the record of David's mighty men is given, the men of valour who fought next to him, showing the power the Most High put upon Israel. The thread is just rule in the fear of the Most High and the strength he gives his people when they walk with him.

# 2 Samuel 24: David Numbers Israel and Buys the Threshing Floor

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 24]]

## Introduction

This last chapter records the numbering of Israel. The anger of the Most High was kindled against Israel, so he moved David to number the people, a task that belonged to the Levites and not the king. When David's heart smote him he confessed his sin, and the Most High offered him three judgments through the prophet Gad; David chose to fall into the hand of the Lord, and a pestilence killed seventy thousand men. When the angel stretched out his hand over Jerusalem, the Most High said, It is enough. David then bought the threshing floor of Araunah, refusing to offer that which cost him nothing, built an altar, and the plague was stayed. The thread is that all judgment is in the hand of the Most High, and true worship costs us something.

- The Lord sent the pestilence upon Israel from the morning to the time appointed, and there died seventy thousand men from Dan to Beersheba. When the angel stretched out his hand upon Jerusalem to destroy it, the Lord repented him of the evil and said to the angel, It is enough, stay now thine hand; and the angel was by the threshing place of Araunah the Jebusite.
- David saw the angel that smote the people, something we cannot see today, for the Most High has blinded our eyes to it and this walk is by faith and the keeping of the commandments. David spoke unto the Lord, Lo, I have sinned and done wickedly, but these sheep, what have they done; let thine hand be against me and against my father's house.
