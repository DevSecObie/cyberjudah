# 2 Samuel 13: Amnon Forces Tamar and Absalom Slays Amnon

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 13]]

## Introduction

This session covers 2 Samuel 13-16. It is the working out of the judgment the Most High pronounced in the previous chapter, that the sword would never depart from David's house because of what he did with Bathsheba and Uriah. In four chapters David's daughter Tamar is raped by her brother Amnon, one son Absalom murders that brother, Absalom later steals the hearts of Israel and drives his own father out of the kingdom, and a man of the house of Saul curses David as he flees. None of this would have happened if David had not done what he did, but he did do it, and this is the punishment. These are people who know they are Israel, so coming into the truth does not mean everything is sugar cookies and cakes; issues occur in Israel, and how we deal with them is why the Most High gave us charity, [[Matthew 18|Matthew 18]], and the example of Christ.

- Jonadab was sneaky. We all know somebody like this, that grimy dude who knows exactly how to get over on somebody, and that is what Jonadab was.

# 2 Samuel 14: Joab Brings Absalom Home

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 14]]

## Introduction

Chapter 14 turns on David's longing for Absalom. Joab, perceiving the king's heart, sends a wise woman of Tekoah to speak a parable that traps David into recalling his own banished son. David discerns Joab's hand in it, yet still grants the request and brings Absalom back to Jerusalem, though he refuses to see his face for two full years. The wisdom never departed from David even after his fall; he was still anointed of the Most High, still king over all Israel, and still a very wise man. Absalom's beauty and his weight of hair are noted, and his persistence in setting Joab's field on fire finally gets him back into the king's presence.

# 2 Samuel 15: Absalom Steals the Hearts of Israel

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 15]]

## Introduction

Chapter 15 shows Absalom's conspiracy come to a head. He prepares chariots and horsemen, stands by the gate, and steals the hearts of the men of Israel with friendish flattery, telling every man his cause is good but there is no man deputed of the king to hear him. Under the pretense of paying a vow in Hebron he has himself proclaimed king, and the conspiracy grows so strong that David flees Jerusalem with his household and his loyal men. David sends the ark back to the city in faith, and sends his friend Hushai back to defeat the counsel of Ahithophel. David treated this as a game of chess, not checkers, strategizing even as he wept, and much of the book of Psalms was written while David was on the run like this.

# 2 Samuel 16: Ziba, Shimei's Cursing, and Absalom Defiles the Concubines

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 16]]

## Introduction

Chapter 16 continues David's flight. Ziba meets him with provisions and slanders his master Mephibosheth to gain his inheritance, and Shimei of the house of Saul comes out cursing David and casting stones, blaming him for the blood of Saul's house. David refuses to let Abishai kill Shimei, submitting to the possibility that the Most High had bidden him curse, and trusting the Most High to requite him good. Meanwhile Hushai reaches Absalom in Jerusalem, and Ahithophel counsels Absalom to go in unto his father's concubines in the sight of all Israel, which fulfills the exact word the Most High spoke in chapter 12. None of this was coincidence; it was prophesied to happen because of what David did, and you cannot make this stuff up.

The counsel of Ahithophel was as if a man had inquired at the oracle of God.
- Ahithophel counselled Absalom to lie with the ten concubines David left, in the sight of all Israel, on the roof of the house. This is the exact fulfillment of what the Most High spoke in chapter 12, that David's wives would be taken and one would lie with them in the sight of the sun, and that what David did secretly would be done before all Israel. It was not coincidence or irony; it was prophesied to happen, and none of it would have happened if David had not done what he did. The Most High keeps mercy and forgives, but by no means clears the guilty, so even when we come in and repent, we still must remember the fear of the Most High, for there is but one step between us and death.
