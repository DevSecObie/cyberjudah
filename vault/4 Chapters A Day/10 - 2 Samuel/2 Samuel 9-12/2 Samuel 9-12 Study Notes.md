# 2 Samuel 9: David Shows Kindness for Jonathan's Sake

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 9]]

## Introduction

This session covers 2 Samuel 9-12. It is a short day, but it carries a heavy weight: David's love for his people, his war against the children of Ammon and the Syrians, and then his great fall with Bathsheba and the murder of Uriah, followed by the judgment the Most High brought upon his house. Chapter 9 shows David seeking out the house of Saul so that he may show kindness for Jonathan's sake, restoring all of Saul's land to Jonathan's lame son Mephibosheth and seating him at the king's table continually. This is the same love and care Israel needs to bring back: when we lose people in Israel, we take care of their family as if they were there themselves. That is why the Most High called David a man after his own heart; David loved his people so much that even though things did not go well between him and Saul, he still had enough love for Jonathan to take care of his seed.

**[[2 Samuel 9#^v1|2 Samuel 9:1]]**

![[2 Samuel 9#^v1]]

- David asked whether there was yet any left of the house of Saul that he might show him kindness for Jonathan's sake. Remember that Saul's son Jonathan and David were always very close.
- That closeness goes back to the beef between Saul and David in the book of Samuel; Jonathan was always there for David, that was David's homeboy, they were really close.

- The king sent and fetched him out of Lodebar. Because of the respect and love David had for Jonathan, David was going to show kindness to Jonathan's son, even though the son could not walk.

- To show his love for Jonathan, David gave everything that Saul owned to Jonathan's son.

- Mephibosheth bowed himself and asked what David saw in such a dead dog as he was. David then called Ziba and gave unto his master's son all that pertained to Saul, and charged Ziba, his sons, and his servants to till the land and bring in the fruits, that Mephibosheth might have food, though he himself would eat at the king's table always.
- Ziba had fifteen sons and twenty servants. This shows the level of riches Israel had as a nation: Saul's servant had servants of his own, that is how wealthy this man was, and all of them were set to till the land and take care of the house for Mephibosheth, all out of the respect David had for Jonathan.

- Mephibosheth had a young son named Micha, and all that dwelt in the house of Ziba were servants unto Mephibosheth, who dwelt in Jerusalem and did eat continually at the king's table, and was lame on both his feet.
- This is what we need to bring back into Israel: this same love, care, and admiration for the people we lose. It is unfortunate, but we do lose people in Israel, and we need to take care of their family as if they were there themselves. That is why the Most High said David is a man after his own heart; David loved his people, grounding Jonathan's family and establishing his seed in Israel.

# 2 Samuel 10: The Children of Ammon and the Syrians Smitten

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 10]]

## Introduction

Chapter 10 turns to war. David seeks to show kindness to Hanun the son of Nahash, king of Ammon, as his father had shown kindness to David, but the princes of Ammon poison the king's mind, humiliate David's servants, and hire the Syrians against Israel. Joab and Abishai divide the host and put the enemy to flight, and when the Syrians regroup David gathers all Israel and smites them so thoroughly that the Syrians fear to help Ammon anymore. When Israel keeps the commandments and the Most High is with them, they do not lose.

![[2 Samuel 10#^v1]]

- The king of the children of Ammon died and Hanun his son reigned in his stead. David said he would show kindness unto Hanun as his father Nahash had shown kindness unto David, and sent his servants to comfort him for his father.
- Remember that the children of Ammon had been warring with Israel; they got smashed and put under tribute back in the days of Saul.

- Through one generation to another they still had this beef with Israel; they kept it in their mind that these people were enemies, not meant to be at peace with them. So they put it in the king's mind that David was really coming to spy the land out and overthrow them.

When it was told David, he sent to meet them because the men were greatly ashamed, telling them to tarry at Jericho until their beards be grown, then return.
- One of the first things we learn coming into this truth is that a man has to have a beard and men have to pull up their pants. The beard is a badge of manly dignity, a law given by the Most High, and it is a shame for a man to shave it. These men were ashamed to come into the city with half a beard and their garments cut so their behind was showing; David did not even want them in the city until their beards grew back. That is what Israel has to change as a people; today men walk around with their behind showing as a fashion statement, but these men were greatly ashamed.

- Once these men saw David did not like what happened, they knew David was a man of war and Israel a people of war, so they sought help. But David had already subdued these same nations; they simply tried to get the men who had always been beefing with Israel to come against Israel yet again.

# 2 Samuel 11: David, Bathsheba, and the Murder of Uriah

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 11]]

## Introduction

Chapter 11 is the great fall. While the kings went forth to battle, David tarried at Jerusalem, saw Bathsheba washing herself, sent for her, and lay with her, and she conceived. David then schemed to cover it, calling Uriah home so it would appear the child was his; when the loyal Uriah refused to go down to his house, David sent him back to the front with a letter ordering his death. Bathsheba mourned, then David took her to wife, but the thing that David had done displeased the Most High. People who want to justify wrongdoing always run to David and Bathsheba, but David was a man just like the rest of us, and this was his mess up; it does not make the deed right, and we will see what the judgment was.

In the evening he walked upon the roof, saw a woman washing herself who was very beautiful, inquired and learned she was Bathsheba the wife of Uriah the Hittite, sent and took her, and lay with her; and she conceived and told David, I am with child.
- Masons and people in these fraternities always run here to justify wrongdoing, saying David slept with Bathsheba and the Most High still forgave him. But you are not King David; David was a man like the rest of us, and he messed up, and this is the mess up. About ninety percent of them go right back to David and Bathsheba every time they want to justify some wrongdoing.

But Uriah slept at the door of the king's house with the servants of his lord and went not down to his house. When David heard it, he asked Uriah why he came not from his journey and went not down.
- David's plan, which nobody was privy to, was that if Uriah went down and slept with his wife, they could say the child was his. A lot of us do that today: many people raise children that are not theirs, and just because David did it does not make it right.

As David lived, he would not do this thing. David kept him another day and made him drunk, but that evening Uriah still went out to lie on his bed with the servants of his lord and went not down to his house.
- Uriah had a sense of loyalty that was probably killing David all the more, because David could not deceive this man into laying with his wife. Even drunk, when a man might just go home to sleep it off, Uriah still slept at David's house and would not go down.

This goes back to Deuteronomy 27: cursed is he that smiteth his neighbour secretly, because nobody knew what David was doing, and cursed is he that taketh reward to slay an innocent person. The reward was that woman; he slew an innocent man who had done nothing.

- Other men of Israel died too, not just Uriah, so with David's sin other men died as well. Joab knew what the king really wanted, so he made sure to name Uriah as dead. David's reply was that it is what it is, the mission was accomplished, you win some and lose some; being content with murder is a wicked thing to do.

When David called for her she went, she did not refuse because she was married, and it was consensual, not rape. She knew she was pregnant, so this was deceitful on both sides.

# 2 Samuel 12: Nathan's Parable and the Judgment on David's House

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 12]]

## Introduction

Chapter 12 brings the reckoning. The Most High sends Nathan, who tells David the parable of the rich man who took the poor man's one little ewe lamb; David's anger is kindled against the man, and Nathan answers, Thou art the man. The Most High recounts all He gave David and pronounces judgment: the sword shall never depart from his house, evil shall be raised up out of his own house, and the child shall die. David repents with godly sorrow, the child dies, and afterward Solomon is born, whom the Most High loved. This shows that David was judged; he did not get off scot-free, but because he had godly sorrow he was not put to death, and through that Solomon, and ultimately Christ, came.

- The Most High sent Nathan unto David, who told him of two men in one city, one rich and one poor. The rich man had many flocks and herds, but the poor man had nothing save one little ewe lamb he had bought and nourished up, which was unto him as a daughter. When a traveler came, the rich man spared his own flock and took the poor man's lamb. David's anger was greatly kindled, and he said the man that had done this shall surely die and restore the lamb fourfold, because he had no pity.
- Nathan came to David with a parable. When David judged that the man deserved to die for taking the poor man's one lamb, he was unknowingly pronouncing sentence on himself.

**[[2 Samuel 12#^v7|2 Samuel 12:7-9]]**

![[2 Samuel 12#^v7]]

- Nathan said unto David, Thou art the man. Thus saith the Most High: I anointed thee king over Israel, delivered thee out of the hand of Saul, gave thee thy master's house and wives, and the house of Israel and of Judah, and would have given thee more. Wherefore hast thou despised the commandment to do evil, killing Uriah the Hittite with the sword and taking his wife, slaying him with the sword of the children of Ammon?
- The Most High said if David would have just asked, He would have given him anything, for He had already given him the kingdom, Saul's house, and protection from Saul. The commandment David despised was what was read in [[Deuteronomy 27|Deuteronomy 27]]. There is a term called suicide by cop; David committed murder by the hand of another nation, because he set the whole thing up.

Behold, I will raise up evil against thee out of thine own house, and I will take thy wives before thine eyes and give them unto thy neighbour, who shall lie with them in the sight of the sun. Thou didst it secretly, but I will do this thing before all Israel and before the sun.
- As the four chapters go on, and beyond, you are going to see the fruition of what just happened worked out in David's own house.

- The judgment of David was that child being put to death. This goes back to Exodus 34: the Most High keeps mercy and forgives iniquity, but will by no means clear the guilty, visiting the iniquities of the fathers upon the children. David got pardoned, but he did not get cleared; the punishment was still coming, one part the men of Israel dying in battle, the other his son having to die and his house having nothing but trouble.

When asked why, he said that while the child lived he fasted and wept, for who could tell whether God would be gracious; but now the child was dead, he could not bring him back, he would go to him but the child would not return to him.
- David did all the fasting and prayer before the child died because he knew it was of the Most High that this would happen; no amount of prayer would bring the child back. David was content, saying he did not want it to happen but had no choice in the matter, for the Most High had said it would be so.

The backup master plan behind everything was Christ, who still had to be born through David's lineage; David was still loved and still forgiven, which shows repentance, and that even the mighty among us can fall and yet rise back up.

- David won against the children of Ammon once again. What we gather from these four chapters is that David was called, he was king, a very mighty man, but he slipped and fell; he had godly sorrow, and in that godly sorrow he was not put to death. If he had been, there would be no Solomon, and through that no Christ. The only one perfect in this world was Christ, in whom there was no iniquity; but the men before us, when they failed, had godly sorrow, repented, and kept the commandments of the Most High even after they fell, rising back up again.
