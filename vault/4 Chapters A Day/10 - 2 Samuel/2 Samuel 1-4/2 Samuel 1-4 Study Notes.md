# 2 Samuel 1: David Laments Saul and Jonathan

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 1]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[2 Samuel 1|2 Samuel 1]] through 4, the opening of the second book of Samuel. It moves from the death of Saul and Jonathan to David's coming into the kingdom. The Amalekite who claimed to have slain the Lord's anointed is put to death; David laments Saul and Jonathan in the spirit; David is anointed king over Judah while Ishbosheth is set up over the rest of Israel; and the long war between the two houses runs its course, with David waxing stronger and the house of Saul weaker. Running through it all is one thread: the Most High has His own way of bringing His purpose to pass, and men who take vengeance and shed blood by their own hand, out of order and not ordained of the Lord, answer for it.

- David asked him how the matter went, and he answered that the people were fled from the battle, many had fallen and were dead, and Saul and Jonathan his son were dead also.

- The man said he was an Amalekite, and that Saul asked him to stand upon him and slay him because anguish was come upon him; so he slew him, being sure he could not live, and took the crown from his head and the bracelet from his arm and brought them to David.
- The Amalekites are the seed of Esau, of Eliphaz his son. Their lineage is written in Genesis, and it is a thing to know when reading of Israel's enemies.

- David took hold on his clothes and rent them, and all the men with him did likewise, and they mourned and wept and fasted until evening for Saul, for Jonathan, and for the house of Israel that were fallen by the sword.
- The renting of garments was the sign of mourning, but the Most High looks for a rent heart and not rent garments only; true repentance is inward, not just an outward show.
- David asked the man how he was not afraid to stretch forth his hand to destroy the Lord's anointed. Even a man about to die was not to be touched; the judgment of the Lord was to be let play out. David then commanded one of his young men to fall upon the Amalekite, and he died, for his own mouth had testified against him.

- David lamented over Saul and over Jonathan, and bade them teach the children of Judah the use of the bow, which is written in the book of Jasher. The book of Jasher is not in the canon because the Most High did not will us to have it; He put in the book exactly what He wanted His people to read in the last days.
- This walk in the last days is not by might nor by power but by the Spirit of the Lord. The captivity is not overthrown by rising up with the bow or the gun; this is spiritual war and not physical. There is nothing wrong with knowing how to defend yourself, but the redemption of Israel does not come by force of arms.
- David's lament is a prophecy given in the spirit, almost a eulogy: Saul and Jonathan were lovely and pleasant in their lives, and in their death they were not divided; they were swifter than eagles and stronger than lions. Before the devil jumped on Saul he was good, and the two of them were good together.
- Israel's forefathers died only when the Most High said it was time; that is the only way any of them died. David mourned Jonathan, saying his love to him was wonderful, passing the love of women.

- David inquired of the Lord whether he should go up into any of the cities of Judah, and the Lord said, Go up, unto Hebron. David did not move to take the kingdom until he asked whether it was time to be king.
- David went up with his two wives, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess and Abigail the widow of Nabal the Carmelite, and with every man and his household, and they dwelt in the cities of Hebron.
- The men of Judah came and there anointed David king over the house of Judah. He was raised up because the Most High had anointed him to be the next king over Israel.

- Abner and the servants of Ishbosheth met Joab and the servants of David at the pool of Gibeon, sitting on opposite sides. Abner said, Let the young men arise and play, meaning let them compete and battle one another; the Lord is a man of war, and such contests were done in that day.
- Twelve of Benjamin and twelve of David's servants caught each his fellow by the head and thrust the sword in his fellow's side, so they fell down together, and that place was called Helkathhazzurim. Abner and the men of Israel were beaten before the servants of David.
- Asahel, one of the three sons of Zeruiah, was as light of foot as a wild roe, meaning he was fast, and he pursued Abner and would not turn aside. Abner warned him again and again to turn off and take another man's armor, not wanting to have to kill him and face Joab his brother.
- When Asahel refused to turn aside, Abner smote him with the hinder end of the spear under the fifth rib, and he died there. A strong thrust is needed to drive a spear through a man, and Abner was strong. Those who came to the place stood still; the lesson is to take heed to the warning and not keep testing a man.

- Abner made himself strong for the house of Saul. When Ishbosheth charged him over Rizpah, Saul's concubine, Abner was very wroth, saying he had shown kindness to the house of Saul and not delivered them to David, and now was charged with a fault concerning this woman.
- Abner swore to do to himself and more, to translate the kingdom from the house of Saul and set up the throne of David over Israel and Judah, from Dan even to Beersheba. Ishbosheth could not answer him a word, because he feared him. Abner was through with Ishbosheth and turned to go with David and the Lord.

- Michal was taken from her husband Phaltiel, who went weeping behind her until Abner told him to return. David was taking back what was his as the king before the Lord.
- Abner spoke with the elders of Israel and with Benjamin, reminding them they had sought David in times past, and that the Lord had said by the hand of David He would save Israel out of the hand of the Philistines and all their enemies. - Joab, without David's knowledge, sent messengers after Abner and brought him back to Hebron, took him aside in the gate to speak quietly, and smote him there under the fifth rib, that he died, for the blood of Asahel his brother.
- It was payback; Joab's blood was burning for revenge. Abner was unwise to return to that place, knowing what Joab would do, but it was of the Lord.

He commanded Joab and all the people to rend their clothes and gird with sackcloth and mourn before Abner, and the king himself followed the coffin.
- David wept at the grave and lamented over Abner in the spirit, another eulogy and prophecy: Abner died as a fool dieth, his hands not bound nor his feet in fetters, but he fell as a man falleth before wicked men. All the people wept again and took notice, and David swore not to taste bread till the sun went down, and it pleased the people; whatsoever the king did pleased them, for David was in the right spirit and the Lord was dealing with the nation.
- David said he was weak that day though anointed king, and that the sons of Zeruiah were too hard for him, ruthless men he could not bring under; but the Lord would reward the doer of evil according to his wickedness. That payback came later through Solomon concerning Joab.

# 2 Samuel 4: The Slaying of Ishbosheth

Read the chapter → [[2 Samuel 4]]

**[[2 Samuel 4#^v1|2 Samuel 4:1-3]]**

![[2 Samuel 4#^v1]]
![[2 Samuel 4#^v2]]
![[2 Samuel 4#^v3]]

- When Saul's son Ishbosheth heard that Abner was dead in Hebron, his hands were feeble and all the Israelites were troubled.
- Ishbosheth had two captains of bands, Baanah and Rechab, sons of Rimmon the Beerothite of the children of Benjamin, for Beeroth also was reckoned to Benjamin; and the Beerothites had fled to Gittaim.

**[[2 Samuel 4#^v4|2 Samuel 4:4]]**

![[2 Samuel 4#^v4]]

- Jonathan's son Mephibosheth was lame of his feet. When the tidings came of Saul and Jonathan out of Jezreel, he was five years old, and his nurse took him up and fled, and in her haste he fell and became lame.
- She fled because a new king taking over would customarily kill all the descendants of the former king to make sure none could overthrow him. That is why the nurse ran with the child.

- The sons of Rimmon came about the heat of the day to the house of Ishbosheth, who lay on a bed at noon in the hottest part of the day, and came into the midst of the house as though they would fetch wheat, and smote him under the fifth rib.
- Rechab and Baanah escaped, having smitten and slain him on his bed; and they beheaded him and took his head and got away through the plain all night, and brought the head to David at Hebron, saying, Behold the head of Ishbosheth thine enemy, and claiming the Lord had avenged the king this day of Saul and his seed.
- They did too much; they went overboard. Israel being out of order took the matter upon themselves. David had no quarrel with Saul's sons; it was Saul who sought his life.

**[[2 Samuel 4#^v9|2 Samuel 4:9-12]]**

![[2 Samuel 4#^v9]]

- David answered Rechab and Baanah that as the Lord liveth, who had redeemed his soul out of all adversity, the man who told him Saul was dead, thinking to bring good tidings, he had taken hold of and slain in Ziklag; that was the reward he gave for such tidings.
- How much more, he said, when wicked men had slain a righteous person in his own house upon his bed, should he not require his blood of their hand and take them away from the earth. The man was at rest, asleep, not even a man-to-man contest for the kingdom; it was not how the Most High brings His purpose to pass.
- David commanded his young men, and they slew Rechab and Baanah, cut off their hands and feet, and hanged them up over the pool in Hebron, but took the head of Ishbosheth and buried it in the sepulchre of Abner. The Most High has His own way of doing things; His people are not to put the cart before the horse.
- This is why David is called a man after the Most High's own heart. He did not accept wickedness done in his name or let the murderers slide; he called them out and they received judgment for taking a man's life not ordained of the Lord nor commanded by the king. Let all things be done decently and in order.
