# 1 Esdras 9: The Strange Wives Put Away and the Law Read to the People

Read the chapter → [[1 Esdras 9]]

## Introduction

This session reads 1 Esdras 9 through 2 Esdras 3; these notes cover 1 Esdras 9, which opens the reading. It is a reading session, so the chapters are read straight through and the notes follow the text. The chapter closes the book of 1 Esdras: the assembly called at Jerusalem over the strange marriages, the list of those who had taken strange wives and put them away, and then Esdras standing on the pulpit of wood and reading the law from morning until midday, with the Levites making the people understand it.

- Esdras, rising from the court of the temple, went to the chamber of Joanan the son of Eliasib, and remained there, and did eat no meat nor drink water, mourning for the great iniquities of the multitude.
- There was a proclamation in all Jewry and Jerusalem to all them that were of the captivity, that they should be gathered together at Jerusalem.
- In three days were all they of the tribe of Judah and Benjamin gathered together at Jerusalem, the twentieth day of the ninth month; and all the multitude sat trembling in the broad court of the temple because of the present foul weather.
- Esdras arose up and said unto them, Ye have transgressed the law in marrying strange wives, thereby to increase the sins of Israel. Now by confessing give glory unto the Lord God of our fathers, and do his will, and separate yourselves from the heathen of the land, and from the strange women.

- Esdras the priest chose unto him the principal men of their families, all by name; and in the first day of the tenth month they sat together to examine the matter, and their cause was brought to an end in the first day of the first month.

- The priests and the Levites, and they that were of Israel, dwelt in Jerusalem and in the country, in the first day of the seventh month.
- The whole multitude came together with one accord into the broad place of the holy porch toward the east, and spake unto Esdras the priest and reader, that he would bring the law of Moses, that was given of the Lord God of Israel.
- Esdras the chief priest brought the law unto the whole multitude, from man to woman, and to all the priests, to hear the law in the first day of the seventh month, and he read in the broad court before the holy porch from morning unto midday.
- Esdras stood upon a pulpit of wood, which was made for that purpose, and there stood up by him the men appointed on his right hand and on his left.
- When he opened the law, they stood all straight up. So Esdras blessed the Lord God most High, the God of hosts, Almighty; and all the people answered, Amen: and lifting up their hands they fell to the ground, and worshipped the Lord.

- The Levites taught the law of the Lord, making them withal to understand it.
- Then spake Attharates unto Esdras the chief priest and reader, and to the Levites that taught the multitude, even to all, saying, This day is holy unto the Lord; for they all wept when they heard the law.
- Go then, and eat the fat, and drink the sweet, and send part to them that have nothing; for this day is holy unto the Lord: and be not sorrowful; for the Lord will bring you to honour.
- They went their way, every one to eat and drink, and make merry, and to give part to them that had nothing, and to make great cheer; because they understood the words wherein they were instructed, and for the which they had been assembled.
