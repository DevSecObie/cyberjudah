# 1 Esdras 1: The Passover of Josiah and the Fall of Jerusalem

Read the chapter → [[1 Esdras 1]]

## Introduction

This session reads 1 Esdras 1 through 4. It is a reading session, so the chapters are read straight through and the notes follow the text. The book opens with the passover Josiah kept in Jerusalem, the like of which had not been kept since the days of Samuel, and runs down through the kings after him to the burning of the house and the seventy years. Then comes the proclamation of Cyrus, the letters that stopped the building, and the contest of the three young men of the guard before Darius, which Zerubbabel wins by naming the truth.

- He spake unto the Levites, the holy ministers of Israel, that they should hallow themselves unto the Lord, and set the holy ark in the house that Solomon had built: Ye shall no more bear the ark upon your shoulders.

- Josiah gave the people thirty thousand lambs and kids, and three thousand calves, out of the king's allowance; the governors of the temple and the captains over thousands gave in like manner to the priests and the Levites.

- The governors of the people and of the priests did many things against the laws, and passed all the pollutions of all nations, and defiled the temple of the Lord.
- The God of their fathers sent by his messenger to call them back, because he spared them and his tabernacle; but they had his messengers in derision, and made a sport of his prophets.
- He commanded the kings of the Chaldees to come up against them, who slew their young men with the sword, even within the compass of their holy temple, and took all the holy vessels away into Babylon.
- They burnt the house of the Lord, brake down the walls of Jerusalem, and set fire upon her towers; and the people not slain with the sword were carried unto Babylon, until the land had enjoyed her sabbaths, the whole time of her desolation, till the full term of seventy years.

- In the first year of Cyrus king of the Persians, that the word of the Lord might be accomplished which he had promised by the mouth of Jeremy, the Lord raised up the spirit of Cyrus.
- He made proclamation through all his kingdom: The Lord of Israel, the most high Lord, hath made me king of the whole world, and commanded me to build him an house at Jerusalem.
- If therefore there be any of you that are of his people, let him go up to Jerusalem, and build the house of the Lord of Israel; and let their neighbours help them with gold, and with silver, with gifts, with horses, and with cattle.

- Darius made a great feast unto all his subjects, and unto all the princes of Media and Persia, and to all the governors and captains and lieutenants under him, from India unto Ethiopia, of an hundred twenty and seven provinces.
- Three young men of the guard that kept the king's body spake one to another: Let every one of us speak a sentence; he whose sentence shall seem wiser than the others, to him shall king Darius give great gifts.
- He shall be clothed in purple, and drink in gold, and sleep upon gold, and shall sit next to Darius because of his wisdom, and shall be called Darius his cousin.
- Every one wrote his sentence, sealed it, and laid it under king Darius his pillow.

- When they are in their cups they forget their love both to friends and brethren, and a little after draw out swords; and when they are from the wine, they remember not what they have done.

- Then he began to speak of the truth: Great is the earth, high is the heaven, swift is the sun in his course; is not he great that maketh these things? therefore great is the truth, and stronger than all things.
- All the earth calleth upon the truth, and the heaven blesseth it: all works shake and tremble at it, and with it is no unrighteous thing.
- Wine is wicked, the king is wicked, women are wicked, all the children of men are wicked; and in their unrighteousness they shall perish.
- As for the truth, it endureth, and is always strong; it liveth and conquereth for evermore. With her there is no accepting of persons or rewards, but she doeth the things that are just.
- She is the strength, kingdom, power, and majesty, of all ages. Blessed be the God of truth. And all the people then shouted, and said, Great is truth, and mighty above all things.
