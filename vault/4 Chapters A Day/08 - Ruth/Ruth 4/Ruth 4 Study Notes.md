# Ruth 4: Boaz Redeems the Inheritance

Read the chapter → [[Ruth 4]]

## Introduction

This session covers [[Ruth 4|Ruth 4]] together with 1 Samuel 1-3; these notes cover [[Ruth 4|Ruth 4]], the end of the book of Ruth, before the class moves into the history of Israel in first Samuel. The chapter closes the book: Boaz goes up to the gate and conducts the laws of the land before witnesses, the law of inheritance is traced back to [[Numbers 36|Numbers 36]] and the law of the husband's brother back to [[Deuteronomy 25|Deuteronomy 25]], the nearer kinsman refuses to redeem, Boaz buys the field and takes Ruth to raise up the name of the dead, Obed is born and Naomi becomes nurse unto him, and the generations of Pharez run down to David. Nothing too major happens in [[Ruth 4|Ruth 4]] beyond the laws of the land and the law of loving thy neighbour as thyself; that is what Boaz was exercising, and that is why he is mentioned in the scriptures.

**[[Ruth 4#^v1|Ruth 4:1-2]]**

![[Ruth 4#^v1]]
![[Ruth 4#^v2]]

- Boaz went up to the gate, called the kinsman of whom he had spoken to turn aside and sit down, and took ten men of the elders of the city to sit with them.
- What he is doing is calling witnesses. He is about to make a purchase, so he calls witnesses, that it can be put in the books for the future that this is what was done.

**[[Ruth 4#^v3|Ruth 4:3-4]]**

![[Ruth 4#^v3]]
![[Ruth 4#^v4]]

- Boaz laid the matter before the kinsman: Naomi, that is come again out of the country of Moab, selleth a parcel of land which was our brother Elimelech's. If thou wilt redeem it, redeem it; but if not, tell me, for there is none to redeem it beside thee, and I am after thee. And the kinsman said, I will redeem it.
- What is this going back to? The law of the land, the law of inheritance when it comes to land, read back in the book of Numbers. The husband died and the two sons died, and the only person left is the wife with the land; so she instinctively went to Boaz, and Boaz is now conducting the laws of the land of who it should be passed down to, going down the list of inheritance.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Numbers 36#^v1|Numbers 36:1-4]]**
    ![[Numbers 36#^v1]]
    ![[Numbers 36#^v2]]
    ![[Numbers 36#^v3]]
    ![[Numbers 36#^v4]]
    The chief fathers of Gilead brought the case of the daughters of Zelophehad: if the daughters married into another tribe, their inheritance would be taken from the inheritance of the fathers and put to the tribe wherein they were received, and fixed there at the jubile.
  - **[[Numbers 36#^v5|Numbers 36:5-9]]**
    ![[Numbers 36#^v5]]
    ![[Numbers 36#^v6]]
    ![[Numbers 36#^v7]]
    ![[Numbers 36#^v8]]
    ![[Numbers 36#^v9]]
    The answer: let them marry to whom they think best, only to the family of the tribe of their father, so that no inheritance of the children of Israel remove from tribe to tribe.

- Reading between the lines of verses two and three of Numbers 36: you are able to marry outside your particular tribe. You can be Judah and marry somebody from Manasseh; you can be Manasseh and marry somebody from Ephraim. Marrying outside the tribe was fine, and that is why they had this complaint; it just has to be within the twelve tribes of Israel, inside your nation, not outside the nation with the heathen.

**[[Ruth 4#^v5|Ruth 4:5]]**

![[Ruth 4#^v5]]

- What day thou buyest the field of the hand of Naomi, thou must buy it also of Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of the dead, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance. Whoever took the land had to take the dead brother's wife with it; Ruth the Moabitess came along as a parcel of the land, to keep the name of Mahlon alive.
- What law is that going back to? That law goes back to [[Deuteronomy 25|Deuteronomy 25]], the duty of the husband's brother: the widow of the dead does not marry without unto a stranger, and the firstborn she bears succeeds in the name of the dead brother, that his name be not put out of Israel. This law was taught in full when it was read ahead at [[Ruth 1#^v6|Ruth 1:6-9]] in these notes: the firstborn carries the weight of the name, and the shoe was loosed from him that would not build up his brother's house.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 25#^v5|Deuteronomy 25:5-6]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v5]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v6]]
    The wife of the dead shall not marry without unto a stranger; her husband's brother shall take her to wife, and the firstborn shall succeed in the name of his brother which is dead, that his name be not put out of Israel.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 25#^v7|Deuteronomy 25:7-10]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v7]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v8]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v9]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v10]]
    If the man refused, the widow loosed his shoe and spat in his face before the elders, because he would not build up his brother's house.
  - **[[Genesis 49#^v3|Genesis 49:3]]**
    ![[Genesis 49#^v3]]
    The firstborn is the might and the beginning of strength, the excellency of dignity and power; you want somebody to carry on your name.
  - **[[1 Chronicles 5#^v1|1 Chronicles 5:1]]**
    ![[1 Chronicles 5#^v1]]
    Reuben was the firstborn, but because he defiled his father's bed his birthright was given unto the sons of Joseph; the birthright shows why the firstborn and the inheritance mattered so much in Israel.

- Notice the bottom of verse 5: to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance. This law applies to inheritance; it went into the law of land being passed down. It does not apply today: your husband dying does not mean his brother now has to go in unto you and raise up children, because that duty was due to the law of inheritance, and Israel has no land inheritance in this captivity. It is wicked to pull that scripture today to try to get with your brother's widow.

**[[Ruth 4#^v6|Ruth 4:6]]**

![[Ruth 4#^v6]]

- The kinsman said, I cannot redeem it for myself, lest I mar mine own inheritance: redeem thou my right to thyself, for I cannot redeem it. In other words, I already have kids in the way; I cannot do it, I am not doing it.

**[[Ruth 4#^v7|Ruth 4:7-8]]**

![[Ruth 4#^v7]]
![[Ruth 4#^v8]]

- Now this was the manner in former time in Israel concerning redeeming and concerning changing: for to confirm all things, a man plucked off his shoe and gave it to his neighbour, and this was a testimony in Israel. So the kinsman said unto Boaz, Buy it for thee, and he drew off his shoe.
- You can read about that shoe back in [[Deuteronomy 25|Deuteronomy 25]].

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 25#^v9|Deuteronomy 25:9-10]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v9]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v10]]
    The loosed shoe was the testimony in Israel against him that would not build up his brother's house; his name was called in Israel, The house of him that hath his shoe loosed.

**[[Ruth 4#^v9|Ruth 4:9-10]]**

![[Ruth 4#^v9]]
![[Ruth 4#^v10]]

- Boaz said unto the elders and unto all the people, Ye are witnesses this day that I have bought all that was Elimelech's, and all that was Chilion's and Mahlon's, of the hand of Naomi; moreover Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of Mahlon, have I purchased to be my wife, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance, that the name of the dead be not cut off from among his brethren, and from the gate of his place.
- What law is Boaz exercising in this? Love thy neighbour as thyself. That is what Boaz was putting into place and applying, and that is why he is mentioned in the scriptures.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Leviticus 19#^v18|Leviticus 19:18]]**
    ![[Leviticus 19#^v18]]
    Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: the law Boaz was exercising toward his dead kinsman's house.

**[[Ruth 4#^v11|Ruth 4:11-12]]**

![[Ruth 4#^v11]]
![[Ruth 4#^v12]]

- All the people that were in the gate and the elders said, We are witnesses. They blessed the marriage: the Lord make the woman that is come into thine house like Rachel and like Leah, which two did build the house of Israel; and let thy house be like the house of Pharez, whom Tamar bare unto Judah, of the seed which the Lord shall give thee of this young woman.
- In that blessing they are prophesying about David to come.

**[[Ruth 4#^v13|Ruth 4:13-15]]**

![[Ruth 4#^v13]]
![[Ruth 4#^v14]]
![[Ruth 4#^v15]]

- So Boaz took Ruth, and she was his wife; and when he went in unto her, the Lord gave her conception, and she bare a son.
- The women said unto Naomi, Blessed be the Lord, which hath not left thee this day without a kinsman, that his name may be famous in Israel; he shall be unto thee a restorer of thy life, and a nourisher of thine old age: for thy daughter in law, which loveth thee, which is better to thee than seven sons, hath born him.

**[[Ruth 4#^v16|Ruth 4:16-17]]**

![[Ruth 4#^v16]]
![[Ruth 4#^v17]]

- Naomi took the child, and laid it in her bosom, and became nurse unto it; and the women her neighbours gave it a name, saying, There is a son born to Naomi; and they called his name Obed: he is the father of Jesse, the father of David.
- Why did Naomi take that child from Ruth the Moabitess? Because it is not the custom of Israel to allow a child to be raised up under the hand of a heathen. Although Ruth was used to keep Mahlon's name alive in Israel, and although she was brought in, she was a vessel; she did not have the same standing of rights as an Israelite woman, and she had no hand in raising the child. Naomi took it and raised it as her own, so that it could learn the ways of Israel.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Nehemiah 13#^v23|Nehemiah 13:23-24]]**
    ![[Nehemiah 13#^v23]]
    ![[Nehemiah 13#^v24]]
    Nehemiah saw Jews that had married wives of Ashdod, of Ammon, and of Moab, and Ruth was of Moab; their children spake half in the speech of Ashdod, and could not speak in the Jews' language.

- A child's first teacher is the mother: she is the one who nurses him, he spends all his time with her, and he takes on her ways. So what Naomi did in the spirit, she did immediately after that child was born: she took it and raised it as her own. Notice the neighbours said, There is a son born to Naomi; Naomi did not bear that son, Ruth did, but she took it as her own.

**[[Ruth 4#^v18|Ruth 4:18-22]]**

![[Ruth 4#^v18]]
![[Ruth 4#^v19]]
![[Ruth 4#^v20]]
![[Ruth 4#^v21]]
![[Ruth 4#^v22]]

- Now these are the generations of Pharez: Pharez is of Judah, so this is the lineage of Judah, how they came about right down to the king. Pharez begat Hezron, and Hezron begat Ram, and Ram begat Amminadab, and Amminadab begat Nahshon, and Nahshon begat Salmon, and Salmon begat Boaz, and Boaz begat Obed, and Obed begat Jesse, and Jesse begat David.
- We read that lineage in Matthew chapter 1: how David came about, how Solomon came about, ultimately down to Christ.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Matthew 1#^v5|Matthew 1:5-6]]**
    ![[Matthew 1#^v5]]
    ![[Matthew 1#^v6]]
    Salmon begat Booz of Rachab, and Booz begat Obed of Ruth, and Obed begat Jesse, and Jesse begat David the king; the same lineage runs on down to Christ.
