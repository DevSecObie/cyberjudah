# 2 Chronicles 25: Amaziah Serves the Most High With a Divided Heart

Read the chapter → [[2 Chronicles 25]]

## Introduction

This session covers 2 Chronicles 25-28, four kings of Judah in a row: Amaziah, Uzziah, Jotham, and Ahaz. The thread that runs through all four is that the king in Judah was held under very strict orders. He was to write the law, meditate in it, and take counsel from the prophets and the priests, because the king did not know everything on his own. As long as these men sought the Most High they prospered, and the moment they became lifted up, took bad counsel, or ran to other nations and their gods, judgment came. In these chapters the northern kingdom of Israel and the southern kingdom of Judah are treated as two separate nations at war, brother against brother, which is the same division we still see among the people today. Prosperity comes from the Most High, and no nation, no army, and no idol can deliver a man from the curses of God when he departs from the commandments.

- Amaziah began to reign at twenty and five years old and reigned twenty and nine years in Jerusalem. He did that which was right in the sight of the Most High, but not with a perfect heart. As soon as the kingdom was established he slew the servants that had killed his father the king.
- He slew the murderers but he did not slay their children, because it is written in the law of Moses that the fathers shall not die for the children, nor the children for the fathers, but every man shall die for his own sin. This is a foundational law: a man answers for his own sin, not for another man's.

- Amaziah gathered Judah and Benjamin, numbered them from twenty years old and upward, and found three hundred thousand choice men able to go to war. He also hired a hundred thousand mighty men out of Israel for a hundred talents of silver.
- A man of the Most High came and told him not to let the army of Israel go with him, for the Most High was not with Israel, with all the children of Ephraim. Because of the sin of Ephraim the Most High had cast them off and was not dealing with them, so their help would only bring defeat.
- When Amaziah asked what to do about the hundred talents he had already paid, the prophet told him the Most High is able to give him much more than that. Amaziah obeyed, separated the army of Ephraim to go home, and they went home in great anger.

- Amaziah strengthened himself, led his people to the valley of salt, and smote ten thousand of the children of Seir, and cast down another ten thousand from the top of the rock. But he brought back the gods of the children of Seir, set them up to be his own gods, bowed to them, and burned incense to them.
- This is the divided heart in action. The Most High gave him the victory, and he responded by grabbing the idols of the people he had just defeated. So the anger of the Most High was kindled against him.
- The Most High sent a prophet who asked why he sought after gods that could not even deliver their own people out of his hand. This is why counsel is important. The prophet was trying to counsel Amaziah to destroy the idols, and instead Amaziah threatened him and told him to forbear, be quiet. The prophet forbore, but said the Most High had determined to destroy him because he had done this and had not hearkened to counsel.

- Amaziah took advice and sent to Joash king of Israel saying, Come, let us see one another in the face. Joash answered with a parable of the thistle and the cedar, telling him his heart lifted him up to boast, and to abide at home rather than meddle to his hurt.
- Amaziah would not hear, for it came of the Most High, that he might deliver them into the hand of their enemies because they had sought the gods of Edom. Judah was put to the worse before Israel, and they fled every man to his tent.
- Joash took Amaziah, came to Jerusalem, and broke down the wall of Jerusalem four hundred cubits. He took all the gold and silver and the vessels found in the house of the Most High, and the treasures of the king's house, and hostages, and returned to Samaria. The two kingdoms had real animosity, calling each other another nation, brother stealing the vessels out of his own brother's temple.

- All the people of Judah took Uzziah, who was sixteen years old, and made him king in place of his father Amaziah. He reigned fifty and two years in Jerusalem and did that which was right in the sight of the Most High.
- He sought the Most High in the days of Zechariah, who had understanding in the visions of God, and as long as he sought the Most High, God made him to prosper. Prosperity comes from the Most High, and it lasts only as long as a man keeps seeking Him.

- Uzziah went to war against the Philistines, broke down walls, and built cities. God helped him against the Philistines, the Arabians, and the Mehunims, and his name spread abroad, for he was marvellously helped till he was strong.
- He built towers in Jerusalem and in the desert, dug many wells, and loved husbandry, keeping much cattle and vine dressers. He had a trained host of fighting men, three hundred thousand and seven thousand five hundred that made war with mighty power, and he prepared for them shields, spears, helmets, and engines of war invented by cunning men to shoot arrows and great stones.
- All of this came because he was helped by the Most High. The strength, the army, the wealth, and even the ingenuity of his engineers were a gift.

- But when he was strong, his heart was lifted up to his destruction. He transgressed against the Most High and went into the temple to burn incense on the altar of incense, which was not his office.
- Azariah the priest and fourscore priests went in after him, withstood him, and told him it did not pertain to him to burn incense but to the priests, the sons of Aaron who are consecrated, and told him to go out of the sanctuary for he had trespassed. Uzziah was wroth, and while he was wroth with a censer in his hand, leprosy rose up in his forehead before the priests.
- The lesson to all of us is to stay in the spirit, stay humble, and never forget who put you in the position you are in. When a man becomes lifted up he loses his humility and forgets who set him where he is. Uzziah was a leper to the day of his death and dwelt in a separate house, cut off from the house of the Most High.

- The rest of the acts of Uzziah, first and last, Isaiah the prophet wrote. Uzziah slept with his fathers and they buried him in the field of burial that belonged to the kings, for they said, He is a leper. Jotham his son reigned in his stead.
- When Uzziah stayed with the Most High he prospered, and when he departed and did his own thing, going contrary to the laws of God, the Most High made an example of him. He is a warning against thinking of yourself as more than what you are, for a man who thinks he is something when he is nothing deceives himself.

- Jotham was twenty and five years old when he began to reign and reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did that which was right in the sight of the Most High, according to all that his father Uzziah did, only he did not enter into the temple of the Most High. He would not go into the temple, and the people still did corruptly.
- He built the high gate of the house of the Most High and built much on the wall of Ophel, and cities in the mountains, and castles and towers. He fought against the Ammonites and prevailed, and they paid him a hundred talents of silver and ten thousand measures of wheat and ten thousand of barley, both that year and the second and the third.
- Jotham became mighty because he prepared his ways before the Most High. It is a short chapter, but the point is plain: the Most High was with our forefathers when they followed the commandments, and Jotham ordered his ways before God.

- Ahaz was twenty years old when he began to reign and reigned sixteen years, but he did not do that which was right in the sight of the Most High like his father David. He walked in the ways of the kings of Israel and made molten images for Baalim.
- He burned incense in the valley of the son of Hinnom and burned his children in the fire, after the abominations of the heathen that the Most High cast out before the children of Israel. This was witchcraft and child sacrifice, the practice called Tophet, in the high places under every green tree.

- The men named by name took the captives, clothed all that were naked among them, shod them, gave them to eat and drink, anointed them, carried the feeble on asses, and brought them to Jericho, the city of palm trees, to their brethren, then returned to Samaria. Even in the middle of the division, these men feared the Most High and would not treat their brethren as slaves.

- At that time Ahaz sent to the kings of Assyria to help him, for the Edomites had come and smitten Judah and carried away captives, and the Philistines had invaded the cities of the low country and the south of Judah and taken them. The Most High brought Judah low because of Ahaz, for he made Judah naked and transgressed sore against the Most High.
- Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria came to him and distressed him but strengthened him not. Ahaz took a portion out of the house of the Most High, the king's house, and the princes, and gave it to the king of Assyria, but it did not help him. Running to another nation for deliverance from the curses of God never works, then or now.

- In the time of his distress Ahaz trespassed yet more against the Most High. He sacrificed to the gods of Damascus that smote him, saying, Because the gods of the kings of Syria help them, I will sacrifice to them that they may help me. But they were the ruin of him and of all Israel.
- Ahaz gathered up the vessels of the house of God, cut them in pieces, shut up the doors of the house of the Most High, and made altars in every corner of Jerusalem. This is the pattern of running to a nation that cannot save, the same spirit that trusts in the systems of the world instead of the Most High.
- Ahaz slept with his fathers and they buried him in the city, in Jerusalem, but not in the sepulchres of the kings of Israel, and Hezekiah his son reigned in his stead. The Most High is the only one who can bring the people out of their condition, then and now.
