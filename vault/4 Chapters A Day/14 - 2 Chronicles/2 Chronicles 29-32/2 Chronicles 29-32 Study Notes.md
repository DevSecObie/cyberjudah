# 2 Chronicles 29: Hezekiah Cleanses the House of the Lord

Read the chapter → [[2 Chronicles 29]]

## Introduction

This session covers the reign of Hezekiah king of Judah, one of the great reformers among the children of Israel. Where his fathers had turned their backs on the Most High, Hezekiah reopens the temple, sanctifies the Levites, keeps the Passover, restores the tithe, and stands against the king of Assyria. The thread running through all four chapters is a man who did that which was right in the sight of the Lord and set the service of the house of God back in order, doing everything with all his heart so that he prospered. Read these chapters as the pattern for how a people returns to the Most High after captivity and forsaking.

- He names the sin plainly: the fathers had trespassed, forsaken the Most High, turned their backs on his habitation, shut the doors, put out the lamps, and stopped burning incense and offering burnt offerings.

- The Levites are named and sanctify themselves, cleansing the house of the Lord and carrying every unclean thing out to the brook Kidron over sixteen days.
- They report to the king that they have cleansed all the house of the Lord, the altar of burnt offering, the showbread table, and even the vessels that king Ahaz had cast away in his transgression.

- Hezekiah rises early, gathers the rulers, and brings seven bullocks, seven rams, seven lambs, and seven he goats for a sin offering for the kingdom, the sanctuary, and Judah.
- The priests kill the animals and sprinkle the blood on the altar, making reconciliation with the blood to make an atonement for all Israel.
- Under the law, atonement required animal sacrifice, but the reconciliation the Most High provides now is through the death of his son. You cannot be reconciled to someone you were never joined to, so the reconciliation is for the house of Israel his people.

- Now that the temple is cleansed and the priests sanctified, Hezekiah sends letters to all Israel and Judah, even to Ephraim and Manasseh, calling them to keep the Passover at Jerusalem.
- The king and congregation agree to keep it in the second month, because the priests had not sanctified themselves sufficiently and the people had not yet gathered in the first month.
- They make a proclamation from Beersheba to Dan to come keep the Passover, for they had not done it for a long time in the way it was written.

- If they turn again, their captive brethren and children will find compassion and return to the land, for the Lord is gracious and merciful and will not turn his face away from those who return to him.

# 2 Chronicles 31: The Tithe Restored

Read the chapter → [[2 Chronicles 31]]

**[[2 Chronicles 31#^v1|2 Chronicles 31:1]]**

![[2 Chronicles 31#^v1]]

- When the feast is finished, all Israel present goes out to the cities and breaks the images, cuts down the groves, and throws down the high places and altars throughout Judah, Benjamin, Ephraim, and Manasseh.
- Then every man returns to his own possession and city, the reformation now reaching beyond the temple into the whole land.
- This obeyed the law directly: rather than intermarry with the nations, Israel was to destroy their altars and burn their graven images.

# 2 Chronicles 32: Assyria Defeated and Hezekiah's End

- After these acts of faithfulness Sennacherib king of Assyria enters Judah and encamps against the fenced cities, thinking to win them for himself.
- Hezekiah takes counsel to stop the waters of the fountains outside the city, repairs the broken wall, raises towers, builds another wall, and makes darts and shields in abundance. Our people are resourceful and skilled, doing the work themselves.
- He sets captains over the people and comforts them: be strong and courageous, for there be more with us than with him; with him is an arm of flesh, but with us is the Lord our God to help us and fight our battles.

  - **[[2 Kings 20#^v20|2 Kings 20:20]]**
    ![[2 Kings 20#^v20]]
    Hezekiah made a pool and a conduit and brought water into the city, the water system he now used against the siege.
  - **[[Jeremiah 17#^v5|Jeremiah 17:5]]**
    ![[Jeremiah 17#^v5]]
    Cursed be the man that trusteth in man and maketh flesh his arm, the opposite of Hezekiah's trust in the Most High.

- Sennacherib sends servants to Jerusalem asking on what the people trust that they abide in the siege, and claims Hezekiah is persuading them to die by famine and thirst.
- He mocks that Hezekiah took away the high places and altars and commanded worship at one altar, twisting obedience to the commandment into a weakness.
- He argues that no god of any nation delivered its people from his hand, so neither will the God of Hezekiah. This is the same seed the world plants to make Israel weary and turn a people off one accord.

  - **[[Colossians 2#^v8|Colossians 2:8]]**
    ![[Colossians 2#^v8]]
    Beware lest any man spoil you through vain deceit, for the enemy still seeks to deceive Israel out of the truth.

- Sennacherib's servants speak yet more against the Lord God and against his servant Hezekiah, and he writes letters to rail on the Lord God of Israel.
- They cry with a loud voice in the Jews' speech to the people on the wall to frighten and trouble them so they might take the city.
- They speak against the God of Jerusalem as against the gods of the peoples of the earth, which were only the work of men's hands. The one true God is the God of Israel, not an idol like the false gods of the nations.

- Hezekiah the king and Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amos, pray and cry to heaven over this railing.
- The Lord sends an angel that cuts off all the mighty men of valour, leaders, and captains in the camp of Assyria, so Sennacherib returns with shame to his own land, and his own sons slay him with the sword in the house of his god.
- Thus the Lord saves Hezekiah and Jerusalem and guides them on every side, and many bring gifts to the Lord and presents to Hezekiah, so he is magnified in the sight of all nations, a picture of the gifts the nations will bring back to Israel in the kingdom.

- Then Hezekiah humbles himself for the pride of his heart, he and the people of Jerusalem, so that the wrath of the Lord does not come upon them in his days.

- When the ambassadors of Babylon come to inquire of the wonder, God leaves him to try him and know his heart. Hezekiah then sleeps with his fathers, is buried in honour among the sons of David, and Manasseh his son reigns in his stead.
