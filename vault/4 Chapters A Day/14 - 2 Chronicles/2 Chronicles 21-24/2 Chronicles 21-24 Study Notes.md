# 2 Chronicles 21: Jehoram's Wicked Reign

Read the chapter → [[2 Chronicles 21]]

These four chapters cover a dark stretch in the kingdom of Judah. Jehoshaphat was one of the righteous kings the Most High raised up, a man who did that which was right and was given a long reign of peace. As often happens in the history of Israel, right after a good king comes one who tears everything down. Jehoram takes the throne and turns to the ways of the wicked kings, and the account runs through his son Ahaziah, the usurping queen Athaliah, and the boy king Joash. Second Chronicles 21 correlates with Second Kings 8:17 and following; read the two accounts together, for this is the same history told once again, the way the four gospels retell one story.

**[[2 Chronicles 21#^v1|2 Chronicles 21:1-4]]**
![[2 Chronicles 21#^v1]]
![[2 Chronicles 21#^v2]]
![[2 Chronicles 21#^v3]]
![[2 Chronicles 21#^v4]]
- Jehoshaphat slept with his fathers and was buried in the city of David, and Jehoram his son reigned in his stead.
- Jehoshaphat had given his sons great gifts of silver, gold, and precious things with fenced cities, but he gave the kingdom to Jehoram because he was the firstborn.
- As soon as Jehoram was risen up and strengthened himself, he slew all his brethren with the sword, along with divers of the princes of Israel.
- This is a pattern to watch: as soon as some among Israel get power, they turn and hurt everyone around them; the moment he got the throne he killed his own brothers.

**[[2 Chronicles 21#^v5|2 Chronicles 21:5-7]]**
![[2 Chronicles 21#^v5]]
![[2 Chronicles 21#^v6]]
![[2 Chronicles 21#^v7]]
- Jehoram was thirty and two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem.
- He walked in the way of the kings of Israel, as did the house of Ahab, meaning he was wicked; Israel's kings were all wicked, and he had the daughter of Ahab to wife.
- Yet the Most High would not destroy the house of David, because of the covenant He had made with David, promising to give a light to him and to his sons forever.
- The Most High keeps His promises. Just as He did not break His word to David, He did not break His promise to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob; those promises were made to the Israelites and stand to this day, and we have plenty of proof of it.

**[[2 Chronicles 21#^v8|2 Chronicles 21:8-11]]**
![[2 Chronicles 21#^v8]]
![[2 Chronicles 21#^v9]]

- In Jehoram's days Edom revolted from under the dominion of Judah and made themselves a king, and Libnah revolted at the same time because he had forsaken the Most High.
- When we obey the Most High the nations are subject to us; when we come out from under the commandments, that is when the other nations rise up and revolt against us and keep us in sin.
- Jehoram made high places in the mountains of Judah and caused the inhabitants of Jerusalem to commit fornication and compelled Judah thereto.
- Dealing with other gods is spiritual fornication, but physical fornication is built into these false worships; the pagan holidays passed down to our people, whatever the season, are built on fornication, and that is what draws the people to follow them.
  - **[[Genesis 27#^v40|Genesis 27:40]]**
    ![[Genesis 27#^v40]]
    Isaac's blessing to Esau, that he would serve his brother but break his yoke off his neck when he had the dominion, fulfilled here when Edom revolted.

- There came a writing to Jehoram from Elijah the prophet, telling him he had not walked in the ways of Jehoshaphat his father nor in the ways of Asa king of Judah, but in the way of the kings of Israel.
- Elijah charged him with making Judah and Jerusalem go a whoring like the whoredoms of the house of Ahab, and with slaying his brethren who were better than himself.
- The Most High declared He would smite his people, his children, his wives, and his goods with a great plague.
- His own body would be struck: he would have great sickness by disease of his bowels, until his bowels fell out by reason of the sickness day by day. This is what it looks like to mess with the Most High.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v61|Deuteronomy 28:61]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v61]]
    Every sickness not written in the book of the law the Most High would bring upon a disobedient people until they be destroyed.
  - **[[1 Corinthians 11#^v30|1 Corinthians 11:30]]**
    ![[1 Corinthians 11#^v30]]
    Because of disobedience many are weak and sickly, and many sleep, showing that sickness follows coming out from under the commandments.

- The Most High stirred up against Jehoram the spirit of the Philistines and the Arabians that were near the Ethiopians, and they came up into Judah and carried away all his substance, his sons, and his wives, leaving only Jehoahaz his youngest.
- Because of his disobedience his whole family was carried into captivity by the Philistines, the Arabs, and the Ethiopians, the same peoples who later held our people in the trans-Saharan slave trade.
- After all this the Most High smote him in his bowels with an incurable disease, and at the end of two years his bowels fell out and he died of sore diseases.
- He reigned eight years and departed without being desired; many were glad to see him go. They made no burning for him and buried him in the city of David but not in the sepulchres of the kings.
- A good name is better than precious ointment; a man cannot rule wickedly and shame the people and then expect them to stand by him when disease comes. It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the Most High.

# 2 Chronicles 22: Ahaziah and the Rise of Athaliah

Read the chapter → [[2 Chronicles 22]]

**[[2 Chronicles 22#^v1|2 Chronicles 22:1-4]]**
![[2 Chronicles 22#^v1]]
![[2 Chronicles 22#^v2]]
![[2 Chronicles 22#^v3]]
![[2 Chronicles 22#^v4]]
- The inhabitants of Jerusalem made Ahaziah, Jehoram's youngest son, king in his stead, for the band of Arabians had slain all the elder sons.
- Ahaziah was forty and two years old when he began to reign and reigned one year in Jerusalem, and his mother's name was Athaliah the daughter of Omri.
- He walked in the ways of the house of Ahab, for his mother was his counsellor to do wickedly, and the house of Ahab were his counsellors after his father died, to his destruction.
- This is a warning against being ruled by the wrong counsel: instead of going to the priests and the judges, he went to his mother, and she counselled him into ruin. A woman with a grudge can rule a kingdom through her son, and that is what Athaliah did.

**[[2 Chronicles 22#^v5|2 Chronicles 22:5-9]]**
![[2 Chronicles 22#^v5]]
![[2 Chronicles 22#^v6]]
![[2 Chronicles 22#^v7]]
![[2 Chronicles 22#^v8]]
![[2 Chronicles 22#^v9]]
- Ahaziah walked after their counsel and went with Jehoram son of Ahab to war against Hazael king of Syria at Ramoth-gilead, where Jehoram was wounded.
- He went down to see the wounded Jehoram at Jezreel, and the destruction of Ahaziah was of the Most High, for by going he came into the path of Jehu, whom the Most High had anointed to cut off the house of Ahab.
- That same Jehu who killed the seventy sons of Ahab found the princes of Judah and the sons of Ahaziah's brethren and slew them, then sought and caught Ahaziah, who was hid in Samaria.
- They buried Ahaziah for the sake of Jehoshaphat his forefather who sought the Most High with all his heart; but the house of Ahaziah had no power to keep the kingdom, so the wicked king was cut off by the same hand the Most High anointed to avenge Israel.

**[[2 Chronicles 22#^v10|2 Chronicles 22:10-12]]**
![[2 Chronicles 22#^v10]]
![[2 Chronicles 22#^v11]]
![[2 Chronicles 22#^v12]]
- When Athaliah saw that her son was dead, she arose and destroyed all the seed royal of the house of Judah, killing the king's sons.
- But Jehoshabeath the daughter of king Jehoram, wife of Jehoiada the priest and sister of Ahaziah, took Joash the son of Ahaziah and stole him from among the king's sons that were slain, hiding him and his nurse in a bedchamber.
- She hid the child in the house of God six years while Athaliah reigned over the land, showing that Athaliah was ruling in the position of the son she had lost.
- Athaliah did not carry out this slaughter alone; she had weak men helping her run over everything, which is how she seized and held the throne.

# 2 Chronicles 23: Jehoiada Anoints Joash and Athaliah Is Slain

Read the chapter → [[2 Chronicles 23]]

**[[2 Chronicles 23#^v1|2 Chronicles 23:1-7]]**
![[2 Chronicles 23#^v1]]
![[2 Chronicles 23#^v2]]
![[2 Chronicles 23#^v3]]
![[2 Chronicles 23#^v4]]

![[2 Chronicles 23#^v6]]
![[2 Chronicles 23#^v7]]
- In the seventh year Jehoiada strengthened himself and took the captains of hundreds into covenant with him, and they gathered the Levites and the chief of the fathers out of all the cities of Judah to Jerusalem.
- The congregation made a covenant with the king in the house of God, and Jehoiada declared that the king's son should reign, as the Most High had said of the sons of David.
- He set his plan: a third part of the priests and Levites to keep the doors, a third at the king's house, and a third at the gate of the foundation, with all the people in the courts.
- None but the priests and ministering Levites were to enter the house of the Most High, and the Levites were to compass the king round about, every man with his weapon, and put to death any who forced their way in.

**[[2 Chronicles 23#^v8|2 Chronicles 23:8-11]]**
![[2 Chronicles 23#^v8]]
![[2 Chronicles 23#^v9]]
![[2 Chronicles 23#^v10]]
![[2 Chronicles 23#^v11]]
- The Levites and all Judah did according to all Jehoiada commanded, each man keeping his company, for Jehoiada did not dismiss the courses of the Levites but kept them at their work with security added for the king.
- Jehoiada delivered to the captains the spears and shields that had been king David's, kept in the house of God, and set every man with his weapon from one side of the temple to the other, round about the king.
- They brought out the king's son, put the crown upon him, gave him the testimony, and made him king, and Jehoiada and his sons anointed him and said, God save the king.
- This was done by the proper custom: a priest anoints the king, as Samuel the priest anointed Saul and then David; so Jehoiada the priest anointed Joash.

- When Athaliah heard the noise of the people running and praising the king, she came into the house of the Most High and saw the king standing at his pillar with the princes, trumpets, and singers rejoicing.
- She rent her clothes and cried, Treason, treason, but her cry came too late, for the covenant was already made and the true king was crowned.
- Jehoiada commanded the captains to have her forth from the ranges and to slay any that followed her, but not to slay her in the house of the Most High.
- They laid hands on her, brought her to the entering of the horse gate by the king's house, and slew her there, ending the reign of the woman who had killed the seed royal.

![[2 Chronicles 23#^v16]]

- Jehoiada made a covenant between himself, all the people, and the king, that they should be the Most High's people, and the people went to the house of Baal, broke it down, brake its altars and images, and slew Mattan the priest of Baal.
- Jehoiada appointed the officers of the house of the Most High by the hand of the priests and Levites whom David had distributed, to offer burnt offerings with rejoicing and singing as ordained by David.
- He set the porters at the gates so that none unclean in anything should enter, then brought the king down and set him upon the throne of the kingdom, and the people rejoiced and the city was quiet.
- Before a king is set in his position he is under tutors and governors; young Joash was king in rank, yet he was still a learner under Jehoiada, for even kings had to be taught. Athaliah had no place ruling Israel; being the king's mother did not make her the ruler.

# 2 Chronicles 24: Joash Repairs the House and Then Falls

Read the chapter → [[2 Chronicles 24]]

**[[2 Chronicles 24#^v1|2 Chronicles 24:1-3]]**
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v1]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v2]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v3]]
- Joash was seven years old when he began to reign and reigned forty years in Jerusalem, and his mother's name was Zibiah of Beersheba.
- Joash did that which was right in the sight of the Most High all the days of Jehoiada the priest, because that is what he was taught.
- He had seen the wicked men of Israel try to kill him, so he followed the man who showed him the ropes; Jehoiada taught him how to be a righteous king and keep the commandments.
- A king had a job to do and could not simply sit back; he had a kingdom to watch over, and he had the priests there to help him do it.

**[[2 Chronicles 24#^v4|2 Chronicles 24:4-7]]**
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v4]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v5]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v6]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v7]]
- Joash was minded to repair the house of the Most High and gathered the priests and Levites, telling them to go to the cities of Judah and gather money of all Israel to repair the house from year to year, and to hasten the matter.
- The Levites did not hasten it, so the king called Jehoiada the chief and asked why he had not required of the Levites the collection appointed by Moses for the tabernacle of witness.
- Money was needed because Israel did not let the workmen labor for nothing; when men did the jobs they were paid, for the workman is worthy of his hire, and we dealt righteously with our people.
- The sons of Athaliah, that wicked woman, had broken up the house of God and bestowed all its dedicated things upon Baalim, which is why so much repair was needed.

**[[2 Chronicles 24#^v8|2 Chronicles 24:8-14]]**
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v8]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v9]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v10]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v11]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v12]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v13]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v14]]
- At the king's commandment they made a chest and set it at the gate of the house of the Most High, and made a proclamation to bring in the collection Moses laid upon Israel in the wilderness, and the princes and people rejoiced and cast in until they made an end.
- Day by day the king's scribe and the high priest's officer emptied the chest and gathered money in abundance, and the king and Jehoiada gave it to those who did the work, hiring masons, carpenters, and workers of iron and brass.
- Money was a defense here because it paid the skilled brothers who got the temple work done; we must deal righteously with our people today, treating a brother's business as a place to help the family and the body.
- Not everyone was given the same task; masons, carpenters, and metalworkers each had their charge, and every man had to listen to a manager, for business requires order and submission to authority.
- The workmen perfected the work and set the house of God in its state, then brought the rest of the money to make vessels of gold and silver to minister and offer with, and they offered burnt offerings continually all the days of Jehoiada.
  - **[[Joshua 1#^v8|Joshua 1:8]]**
    ![[Joshua 1#^v8]]
    When a man keeps and meditates on the law and does it, he makes his way prosperous and has good success, which is why the willing work on the temple succeeded.

**[[2 Chronicles 24#^v15|2 Chronicles 24:15-16]]**
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v15]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v16]]
- Jehoiada waxed old and was full of days and died, being a hundred and thirty years old at his death.
- They buried him in the city of David among the kings, because he had done good in Israel both toward the Most High and toward his house.
- That a priest was buried among the kings shows how much respect and diligence this man had earned; his faithfulness set him alongside the rulers of the land.

**[[2 Chronicles 24#^v17|2 Chronicles 24:17-19]]**
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v17]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v18]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v19]]
- After the death of Jehoiada the princes of Judah came and made obeisance to the king, and the king hearkened to them.
- They left the house of the Most High and served groves and idols, and wrath came upon Judah and Jerusalem for their trespass.
- The Most High sent prophets to bring them again unto Him, and they testified against them, but the people would not give ear.
- As soon as Jehoiada died, Israel went off again, because the king was weak and started listening to those who served other gods; this teaches the lesson that lust drives Israel to idolatry, and that the Most High will always send prophets to warn them.

**[[2 Chronicles 24#^v20|2 Chronicles 24:20-22]]**
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v20]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v21]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v22]]
- The spirit of God came upon Zechariah the son of Jehoiada the priest, who stood above the people and asked why they transgressed the commandments so that they could not prosper, telling them that because they had forsaken the Most High, He had also forsaken them.
- They conspired against Zechariah and stoned him with stones at the commandment of the king, in the court of the house of the Most High.
- When the prophet came to correct the king, the king had him killed for telling him the truth; this is the same spirit our people show when a laborer brings the medicine and is met with violence.
- As Zechariah died he said, The Most High look upon it and require it. He prayed for vengeance on his murderer, and this is scripture; it is right to pray for the downfall of enemies who do much wickedness, even while we pray for the recovery and repentance of our own people.
  - **[[Malachi 4#^v6|Malachi 4:6]]**
    ![[Malachi 4#^v6]]
    The Most High sends the prophet to turn the hearts of the fathers and children, lest He come and smite the earth with a curse; our people are destroyed for lack of knowledge.
  - **[[2 Chronicles 15#^v2|2 Chronicles 15:2]]**
    ![[2 Chronicles 15#^v2]]
    If we seek the Most High He will be found, but if we forsake Him He will forsake us, the very charge Zechariah brought against Judah.

**[[2 Chronicles 24#^v23|2 Chronicles 24:23-27]]**
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v23]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v24]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v25]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v26]]
![[2 Chronicles 24#^v27]]
- At the end of the year the host of Syria came up against Joash, destroyed the princes of the people, and sent the spoil to the king of Damascus.
- The Syrians came with a small company, yet the Most High delivered a very great host of Judah into their hand, because Judah had forsaken the Most High God of their fathers; so judgment was executed on Joash for killing Zechariah.
- They left Joash in great diseases, and his own servants conspired against him for the blood of the sons of Jehoiada and slew him on his bed, and he was buried in the city of David but not in the sepulchres of the kings.
- Joash began by doing right under Jehoiada's guidance, repairing the house and clearing out the idols, but none of that was remembered in the end because of the wickedness he turned to after being warned.
- Amaziah his son reigned in his stead; the fuller record of Joash is written in the story of the book of the kings.
  - **[[Ezekiel 3#^v20|Ezekiel 3:20]]**
    ![[Ezekiel 3#^v20]]
    When a righteous man turns from his righteousness and commits iniquity, his righteousness shall not be remembered, and he shall die in his sin, exactly as it went for Joash.
