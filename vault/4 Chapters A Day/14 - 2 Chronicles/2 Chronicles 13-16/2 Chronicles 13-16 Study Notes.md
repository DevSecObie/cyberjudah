# 2 Chronicles 13: Abijah Stands on the Covenant

Read the chapter → [[2 Chronicles 13]]

This session covers [[2 Chronicles 13|2 Chronicles 13]] through 16, which links directly to the account in [[1 Kings 15|1 Kings 15]]. Reading the Kings and the Chronicles together fills in the detail: what Kings gives briefly, the Chronicles often expands. These chapters follow the civil war between the southern kingdom of Judah and the northern kingdom of Israel, the reforms of Asa, and the lesson of trusting the Most High rather than leaning on men. The precepts here keep the two books linked and open up the understanding.

**[[2 Chronicles 13#^v1|2 Chronicles 13:1-3]]**
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v1]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v2]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v3]]
- Abijah begins to reign over Judah in the eighteenth year of Jeroboam the first. War breaks out between Abijah and Jeroboam.
- Abijah sets the battle with 400,000 chosen men; Jeroboam answers with 800,000. Judah is outnumbered two to one, yet Abijah stands on the covenant of the Most High.

**[[2 Chronicles 13#^v4|2 Chronicles 13:4-6]]**
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v4]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v5]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v6]]
- Standing on mount Zemaraim in mount Ephraim, Abijah declares that the Most High gave the kingdom to David forever by a covenant of salt.
- He names Jeroboam as the servant of Solomon who rose up and rebelled against his lord. To understand that rebellion, the account goes back to [[1 Kings 11|1 Kings 11]].
  - **[[1 Kings 11#^v26|1 Kings 11:26-31]]**
    ![[1 Kings 11#^v26]]
    ![[1 Kings 11#^v27]]
    ![[1 Kings 11#^v28]]
    ![[1 Kings 11#^v29]]
    ![[1 Kings 11#^v30]]
    ![[1 Kings 11#^v31]]
    Jeroboam, Solomon's industrious servant, lifts his hand against the king, and the prophet Ahijah tears his garment in twelve pieces and gives him ten tribes.

**[[2 Chronicles 13#^v7|2 Chronicles 13:7]]**
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v7]]
- Vain men, the children of Belial, gathered to Jeroboam and strengthened themselves against Rehoboam when he was young and tenderhearted and could not withstand them.
- Young and tenderhearted does not mean a small child. Rehoboam was forty and one years old when he began to reign; he simply knew nothing of war, for the land had rest in Solomon's days.
  - **[[2 Chronicles 12#^v13|2 Chronicles 12:13]]**
    ![[2 Chronicles 12#^v13]]
    Rehoboam was one and forty years old when he began to reign in Jerusalem.

**[[2 Chronicles 13#^v8|2 Chronicles 13:8-9]]**
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v8]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v9]]
- Abijah warns the great multitude that trusts in the golden calves Jeroboam made and in the priests he ordained after the manner of the nations.
- Jeroboam cast out the priests of the Most High, the sons of Aaron and the Levites, and made priests of any man who came to consecrate himself, priests of them that are no gods.
  - **[[2 Chronicles 11#^v14|2 Chronicles 11:14]]**
    ![[2 Chronicles 11#^v14]]
    The Levites left their suburbs and came to Judah, for Jeroboam cast them off from the priest's office and ordained his own priests for the high places and the devils.

**[[2 Chronicles 13#^v10|2 Chronicles 13:10-11]]**
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v10]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v11]]
- Judah answers that the Most High is their God and they have not forsaken him; the sons of Aaron minister and the Levites wait upon their business.
- They burn the daily burnt sacrifices morning and evening, set the shewbread in order, and keep the charge of the Most High that Jeroboam forsook.
  - **[[Numbers 3#^v5|Numbers 3:5-10]]**
    ![[Numbers 3#^v5]]
    ![[Numbers 3#^v6]]
    ![[Numbers 3#^v7]]
    ![[Numbers 3#^v8]]
    ![[Numbers 3#^v9]]
    ![[Numbers 3#^v10]]
    The tribe of Levi is given to Aaron and his sons to wait on the priest's office; the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death.
  - **[[Numbers 28#^v4|Numbers 28:4]]**
    ![[Numbers 28#^v4]]
    The one lamb offered in the morning and the other at evening set the pattern of the daily sacrifice.

**[[2 Chronicles 13#^v12|2 Chronicles 13:12-16]]**
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v12]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v13]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v14]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v15]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v16]]
- The Most High himself is Judah's captain, with his priests sounding the trumpets of alarm. Abijah warns Israel not to fight against the God of their fathers.
- Jeroboam sets an ambush behind Judah, so the battle is before and behind. Judah cries unto the Most High, the priests sound the trumpets, the men shout, and the Most High smites Jeroboam and all Israel.

**[[2 Chronicles 13#^v17|2 Chronicles 13:17-20]]**
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v17]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v18]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v19]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v20]]
- Five hundred thousand chosen men of Israel fall in this civil war, northern kingdom against southern kingdom. Judah prevails because they relied upon the Most High God of their fathers.
- Abijah pursues Jeroboam and takes cities from him. Jeroboam recovers no strength; the Most High strikes him and he dies.

**[[2 Chronicles 13#^v21|2 Chronicles 13:21-22]]**
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v21]]
![[2 Chronicles 13#^v22]]
- Abijah waxes mighty, marries fourteen wives, and begets twenty and two sons and sixteen daughters. His acts are written in the story of the prophet Iddo.
- The great sin the account underlines is Jeroboam casting off the Levites, a law the Most High was very specific about.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 14#^v27|Deuteronomy 14:27]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 14#^v27]]
    Thou shalt not forsake the Levite within thy gates, for he has no part nor inheritance with thee.

# 2 Chronicles 14: Asa Seeks the Most High and Finds Rest

Read the chapter → [[2 Chronicles 14]]

**[[2 Chronicles 14#^v1|2 Chronicles 14:1-5]]**
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v1]]
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v2]]
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v3]]
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v4]]
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v5]]
- Abijah sleeps with his fathers and Asa his son reigns; the land is quiet ten years. Asa does that which is good and right, taking away the altars of the strange gods, the high places, the images, and the groves.
- Asa commands Judah to seek the Most High and to do the law and the commandment. The pattern of Rehoboam warns what happens when a strong king forsakes that law.
  - **[[2 Chronicles 11#^v17|2 Chronicles 11:17]]**
    ![[2 Chronicles 11#^v17]]
    For three years they walked in the way of David and Solomon and strengthened the kingdom of Judah.
  - **[[2 Chronicles 12#^v1|2 Chronicles 12:1]]**
    ![[2 Chronicles 12#^v1]]
    When Rehoboam had established the kingdom and strengthened himself, he forsook the law of the Lord, and all Israel with him.

**[[2 Chronicles 14#^v6|2 Chronicles 14:6-8]]**
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v6]]
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v7]]
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v8]]
- Asa builds fenced cities because the land has rest and no war, for the Most High has given him rest. Rest is not idleness; while the land is before them they build walls, towers, gates, and bars.
- Asa has an army of 300,000 of Judah and 280,000 of Benjamin, all mighty men of valor.
  - **[[Numbers 35#^v4|Numbers 35:4]]**
    ![[Numbers 35#^v4]]
    The suburbs of the Levites' cities reach from the wall outward a thousand cubits round about, for the cities have walls.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v52|Deuteronomy 28:52]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v52]]
    A curse on the seed is the enemy besieging the gates until the high and fenced walls come down wherein they trusted.
  - **[[Ecclesiastes 10#^v18|Ecclesiastes 10:18]]**
    ![[Ecclesiastes 10#^v18]]
    By much slothfulness the building decayeth, so the rest from war was spent building up, not idling.

**[[2 Chronicles 14#^v9|2 Chronicles 14:9-13]]**
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v9]]
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v10]]
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v11]]
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v12]]
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v13]]
- Zerah the Ethiopian, a Cushite, comes out with a host of a thousand thousand and three hundred chariots. Asa cries unto the Most High that it is nothing for him to help with many or with them that have no power.
- The Most High smites the Ethiopians before Asa; they flee and are destroyed, and Judah carries away very much spoil. Faith in the Most High, not the numbers of the enemy, wins the battle.
  - **[[2 Kings 6#^v15|2 Kings 6:15-18]]**
    ![[2 Kings 6#^v15]]
    ![[2 Kings 6#^v16]]
    ![[2 Kings 6#^v17]]
    ![[2 Kings 6#^v18]]
    Elisha's servant sees the mountain full of horses and chariots of fire, for they that be with us are more than they that be with them.

**[[2 Chronicles 14#^v14|2 Chronicles 14:14-15]]**
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v14]]
![[2 Chronicles 14#^v15]]
- The fear of the Most High comes on all the cities round about Gerar, and Judah spoils them, for there was exceeding much spoil in them.
- They smite the tents of cattle and carry away sheep and camels in abundance, then return to Jerusalem.

# 2 Chronicles 15: The Covenant to Seek the Most High

Read the chapter → [[2 Chronicles 15]]

**[[2 Chronicles 15#^v1|2 Chronicles 15:1-7]]**
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v1]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v2]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v3]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v4]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v5]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v6]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v7]]
- The Spirit of God comes on Azariah the son of Oded, who meets Asa and says the Most High is with them while they are with him; if they seek him he will be found, but if they forsake him he will forsake them.
- Israel had long been without the true God, a teaching priest, and law, but when in their trouble they turned to the Most High, he was found of them. Azariah charges Asa to be strong, for his work shall be rewarded.

**[[2 Chronicles 15#^v8|2 Chronicles 15:8]]**
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v8]]
- When Asa hears these words and the prophecy of Oded, he takes courage, puts away the abominable idols out of Judah, Benjamin, and the cities he took from mount Ephraim, and renews the altar of the Most High.
- Hearing the word gives courage still, for we teach our people who they are, that the Israelites and the Christ are black. Faith comes by hearing.
  - **[[Romans 10#^v17|Romans 10:17]]**
    ![[Romans 10#^v17]]
    So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.

**[[2 Chronicles 15#^v9|2 Chronicles 15:9-15]]**
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v9]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v10]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v11]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v12]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v13]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v14]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v15]]
- Asa gathers Judah, Benjamin, and the strangers out of Ephraim, Manasseh, and Simeon, who fell to him in abundance when they saw the Most High was with him. These strangers are Israelites whose hearts turned back.
- In the fifteenth year of Asa they enter a covenant to seek the Most High with all their heart and soul, that whosoever would not seek him should be put to death. They swear with a loud voice, and the Most High gives them rest round about.

**[[2 Chronicles 15#^v16|2 Chronicles 15:16-19]]**
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v16]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v17]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v18]]
![[2 Chronicles 15#^v19]]
- Asa removes Maachah his mother from being queen because she made an idol in a grove; he cuts it down, stamps it, and burns it at the brook Kidron.
- Though the high places are not all taken away, the heart of Asa is perfect all his days. He brings the dedicated silver, gold, and vessels into the house of God, and there is no more war until the thirty and fifth year of his reign.

# 2 Chronicles 16: Asa Leans on Syria Instead of the Most High

Read the chapter → [[2 Chronicles 16]]

**[[2 Chronicles 16#^v1|2 Chronicles 16:1]]**
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v1]]
- In the six and thirtieth year of Asa, Baasha king of Israel comes up against Judah and builds Ramah to keep any from going out or coming in to Asa.
- Baasha is trying to stop the northern kingdom from coming down to Judah, because tribes out of Israel had been turning their hearts back to the Most High and joining Asa.

**[[2 Chronicles 16#^v2|2 Chronicles 16:2-6]]**
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v2]]
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v3]]
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v4]]
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v5]]
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v6]]
- Instead of crying to the Most High as he did against Zerah, Asa takes silver and gold out of the house of the Lord and the king's house and hires Benhadad king of Syria to break his league with Baasha.
- Benhadad smites the cities of Israel, so Baasha leaves off building Ramah. Asa carries away the stones and timber of Ramah and builds Geba and Mizpah with them.

**[[2 Chronicles 16#^v7|2 Chronicles 16:7-10]]**
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v7]]
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v8]]
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v9]]
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v10]]
- Hanani the seer comes to Asa and rebukes him for relying on the king of Syria and not on the Most High, so the host of Syria has escaped his hand. Had he relied on the Most High, he would have delivered them as he did the Ethiopians.
- The eyes of the Most High run to and fro throughout the whole earth to show himself strong for them whose heart is perfect. Asa is wroth, puts the seer in prison, and oppresses some of the people.

**[[2 Chronicles 16#^v11|2 Chronicles 16:11-14]]**
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v11]]
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v12]]
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v13]]
![[2 Chronicles 16#^v14]]
- In the thirty and ninth year of his reign Asa is diseased in his feet, exceeding great, yet he seeks not to the Most High but to the physicians. The rebuke is not against physicians but against seeking them in place of the Most High, from whom healing cometh.
- Asa dies in the one and fortieth year of his reign and is buried in his own sepulchre in the city of David, on a bed of sweet odours and spices, with a very great burning.
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 38#^v1|Ecclesiasticus 38:1-2]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 38#^v1]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 38#^v2]]
    Honour the physician, but healing itself comes from the Most High.
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 38#^v9|Ecclesiasticus 38:9-10]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 38#^v9]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 38#^v10]]
    In sickness pray to the Most High and leave off from sin, then seek the physician.
