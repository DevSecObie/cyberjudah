# 2 Chronicles 1: Solomon Asks for Wisdom

Read the chapter → [[2 Chronicles 1]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[2 Chronicles 1|2 Chronicles 1]] through 4. It is a part of the Bible where the history repeats what was written earlier, in the books of Samuel and Kings, sometimes with a different part of the account and sometimes in greater detail. To line up what is read here, go back and read First Kings chapter 3 through chapter 7, which cover the same events concerning Solomon and the building of the house of the Lord. These chapters move from Solomon being strengthened in his kingdom and asking the Most High for wisdom, to his league with Huram king of Tyre, to the beginning of the temple, and to the vessels of brass and gold that Huram made for the house of God. We rehearse the righteous acts of the Most High in the land of our captivity, referencing precept upon precept so that the questions we write down while reading are answered.

- In that night the Most High appeared unto Solomon and said, Ask what I shall give thee. This same account is told in First Kings chapter 3, where the Lord appeared to Solomon in a dream by night; reading it there gives more detail of what was said.

- Solomon asked for wisdom and knowledge that he might go out and come in before the people, for who could judge so great a people. When you read between Kings and Chronicles and line up the history, you get the full detail of what went on with Solomon and David.

- The offerings Solomon named, morning and evening, on the sabbaths, on the new moons, and on the solemn feasts, are ordained forever to Israel. This is why we keep them in the land of our captivity, rehearsing the righteous acts of the Most High according to the law given through Moses in the book of Numbers.

- Huram the king of Tyre answered Solomon in writing, blessing the Lord God of Israel that made heaven and earth, who had given David a wise son endued with prudence and understanding to build a house for the Lord and a house for his kingdom.
- Huram sent a cunning man named Huram, skilful to work in gold, silver, brass, iron, stone, timber, purple, blue, fine linen, and crimson, and to grave any manner of graving. Here it says he was the son of a woman of the daughters of Dan.
- The same history in First Kings 7 says this workman was a widow's son of the tribe of Naphtali. There is no contradiction: his mother was of the tribe of Naphtali, but she was of the city of Dan, which lay within the land of Naphtali. This is cleared up by using the Bible dictionary and the maps, and by chasing the references.
- The children of Dan had gone up and taken the city of Laish, also called Leshem, smiting it with the edge of the sword and renaming it Dan after their father. Israel had put the nations to tribute rather than driving them out as the Lord commanded, but the city itself sat in the borders of Naphtali, so a woman there could be spoken of both ways.
- Huram promised to cut wood out of Lebanon as much as Solomon needed and bring it in floats by sea to Joppa, from where it would be carried up to Jerusalem. These floats are like the large cargo ships and barges seen today upon the rivers and the seas.

- Solomon began to build the house of the Lord at Jerusalem in mount Moriah, where the Lord appeared unto David his father, in the place David had prepared in the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite. This threshingfloor was read of a few days ago in First Chronicles 21.
- He began to build in the second day of the second month, in the fourth year of his reign. There is a mix here of First Kings 6 and 7, but Kings goes into much more detail on the building.
- These are the things wherein Solomon was instructed for the building of the house of God: the length was threescore cubits, the breadth twenty cubits, and the porch in front was twenty cubits long according to the breadth of the house, and the height was a hundred and twenty. He overlaid it within with pure gold.

- The greater house Solomon cieled with fir tree, overlaid with fine gold, and set thereon palm trees and chains. He garnished the house with precious stones for beauty, and the gold was gold of Parvaim. He overlaid the house, the beams, the posts, the walls, and the doors with gold, even real gold through and through, not the counterfeit metal of today.
- He graved cherubims on the walls. Our unlearned Christian brothers and sisters, and some in other doctrines, say a man cannot have images at all, but that is not what the Bible says. The command in [[Exodus 20|Exodus 20]] forbids making a graven image to bow down to and serve, not the mere carving of a figure.
- The curse in [[Deuteronomy 27|Deuteronomy 27]] falls on the man that maketh a graven or molten image and putteth it in a secret place. A wall in a man's house is not a secret place, for it is seen plainly when a man enters the home; it is not hidden.
- [[Ezekiel 8|Ezekiel 8]] shows what a secret place is: the Lord had the prophet dig through a wall into a hidden chamber where the ancients of Israel practiced idolatry in the dark, saying the Lord seeth us not. That is the abomination, worshipping images in secret, not the goodly images made openly for the house of the Lord.
- Solomon was not bowing down to the cherubims and images he made. [[Hosea 10|Hosea 10]] speaks of goodly images, and these goodly images are the very ones read of here in the building of the house of the Lord.
- He made the most holy house twenty cubits long and twenty broad, overlaid with fine gold to the amount of six hundred talents, and the weight of the nails was fifty shekels of gold. He overlaid the upper chambers also with gold.

The wings of the cherubims were twenty cubits long altogether, each wing five cubits, one wing reaching to the wall and the other joining to the wing of the other cherub, and they stood on their feet with their faces inward.
- He made the vail of blue, purple, crimson, and fine linen, and wrought cherubims thereon. Even the vail bore the figures of the cherubims, made openly for the service of the Most High.

- Solomon made an altar of brass twenty cubits long, twenty broad, and ten cubits high. He also made a molten sea, ten cubits from brim to brim, round in compass, five cubits high, and a line of thirty cubits compassed it about.
- Under the brim was the similitude of oxen compassing it about, ten in a cubit, cast in two rows when the sea was cast. It stood upon twelve oxen, three looking toward the north, three toward the west, three toward the south, and three toward the east, with the sea set above them and all their hinder parts inward.
- The thickness of the sea was a handbreadth, and the brim was like the brim of a cup, with flowers of lilies; it received and held three thousand baths. He made ten lavers and put five on the right hand and five on the left, to wash in them the things offered for the burnt offerings, but the sea was for the priests to wash in.
