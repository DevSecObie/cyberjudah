# 1 Chronicles 10: The Death of Saul

Read the chapter → [[1 Chronicles 10]]

Chronicles is written from the standpoint of the southern kingdom, whose registry was kept in Jerusalem. After the name chapters, First Chronicles becomes a recap of First Samuel, retelling the life, troubles, and rise of David. The name chapters are not tedious filler; they were written for a reason. When Israel returned from captivity a registry was kept so that it could be known who was a priest, what tribe a man came from, and where he could be, because each tribe had its own alignment and lane. Read everything and do not skip. This session covers the death of Saul, the anointing of David, his mighty men, the gathering of the tribes, and the moving of the ark. First Chronicles 10 parallels First Samuel 31 directly.

- Saul died, his three sons, and all his house together, and when Israel saw it they forsook their cities and fled, and the Philistines came and dwelt in them.

# 1 Chronicles 11: David Anointed and His Mighty Men

Read the chapter → [[1 Chronicles 11]]

First Chronicles 11 parallels Second Samuel chapter 5. The chapter records all Israel gathering to make David king, the taking of Jerusalem, and the roll of David's mighty men. As they were mighty in the physical war, we are to be that much more mighty scripturally and spiritually.

- The Most High had said to David, "Thou shalt feed my people Israel, and thou shalt be ruler over my people Israel." The phrase "my people Israel" is repeated, and the Bible does not change.
- The elders came to Hebron, David made a covenant with them before the Most High, and they anointed him king according to the word of the Most High by Samuel.

# 1 Chronicles 12: The Tribes Gather to David

Read the chapter → [[1 Chronicles 12]]

First Chronicles 12 records the mighty men who came to David at Ziklag and at Hebron, tribe by tribe, to turn the kingdom to him. When the Most High is working with you, He sends the help. The theme is the unity of the brethren: if we unify, there is nothing we cannot do.

- These came to David at Ziklag while he kept himself close because of Saul. They were mighty helpers of the war, armed with bows, able to use both the right hand and the left in hurling stones and shooting arrows.
- They were of Saul's own brethren of Benjamin. Benjamin always had the reputation of being ambidextrous, able to hurl a stone with either hand and hit a hair's breadth.

- The Gadites separated themselves to David into the hold in the wilderness, men of might and men of war fit for battle, able to handle shield and buckler, whose faces were like the faces of lions and were as swift as roes upon the mountains.
- Their captains ranged from the least being over a hundred to the greatest over a thousand. These went over Jordan in the first month when it had overflowed all its banks and put to flight all who were in the valleys.
- Gad stood on David's side. Historically Gad fought against Esau and helped Judah escape the plantations, especially in New Orleans, which is why many Creoles descend from a mixture of Judah, Benjamin, Levi, and Gad.

- The spirit came upon Amasai, chief of the captains, who said, "Thine are we, David, and on thy side, thou son of Jesse: peace be unto thee, for thy God helpeth thee." David received them and made them captains.

# 1 Chronicles 13: Bringing Up the Ark

Read the chapter → [[1 Chronicles 13]]

David consulted the leaders and gathered all Israel to bring the ark of the Most High to himself so the priests could inquire of Him, for it had been neglected in the days of Saul. The chapter also records the death of Uzza, a warning that the Most High does not play when His word is broken.

- David consulted with the captains of thousands and hundreds and every leader, and proposed to all the congregation that they send abroad to their brethren everywhere, with the priests and Levites, to gather to them.
- His purpose was to bring again the ark of the Most High, for they had not inquired at it in the days of Saul. All the congregation agreed, for the thing was right in the eyes of all the people.

- They brought up the ark of the Most High, who dwelleth between the cherubims, carrying it in a new cart out of the house of Abinadab.
- David and all Israel played before the Most High with all their might, with singing, harps, psalteries, timbrels, cymbals, and trumpets, as they moved the ark to where David was so the priests could inquire.
- Do not let anyone claim the ark is in Ethiopia. The prophet Jeremiah hid the tabernacle, the ark, and the altar of incense in a cave, and it will not be found until the Most High gathers His people again.
