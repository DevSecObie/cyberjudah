# 1 Chronicles 2: The Sons of Israel and the Line of Judah

Read the chapter → [[1 Chronicles 2]]

The reading continues in 1 Chronicles, the book of our records. Chronicles is our history, the record of our forefathers set down by name. This chapter names the twelve sons of Israel and then follows the line of Judah down through Perez, on through Hezron, Caleb, and Jerahmeel, and toward the house of David. The reading is heavy with names, and the teaching held that every name here has significance and belongs to our forefathers. Where a stretch of names was read without added exposition, the main entries below follow the verse order and hold the record as it stands. The framing set at the start is that it is an honor and a privilege to be known as the sons of God, for we are the descendants of these men, and we are told to inquire of the former age and prepare ourselves for the search of our fathers.

- These are the same sons of Jacob that came into Egypt, and we carry their name, their lineage, and their descent from the twelve tribes of Israel.
- The nations call us a minority and divide us by many labels so they can deal wisely with us, but we are not a minority, and the Most High promised to multiply our seed like the stars of heaven and the sand of the sea.

# 1 Chronicles 3: The Sons of David and the Kings of Judah

Read the chapter → [[1 Chronicles 3]]

This chapter narrows the record of Judah to the house of David: the sons born to him in Hebron, the sons born to him in Jerusalem, and then the kings that came from Solomon down through the captivity and the sons of Jeconiah after it. This is the line of our kings, kept by name.

![[1 Chronicles 3#^v1]]

![[1 Chronicles 3#^v3]]

- The sons born to David in Hebron are named, from Amnon the firstborn to the sixth, with their mothers.
- Amnon was the son who defiled his sister Tamar, and Absalom, named here among the younger sons, later avenged her and paid for it with his own life.
- David reigned in Hebron seven years and six months before he reigned in Jerusalem.

- Many of these names carry meaning; among them Shecaniah was found to mean one intimate with God and Elioenai to mean I look to God.

# 1 Chronicles 4: The Families of Judah and Simeon

Read the chapter → [[1 Chronicles 4]]

This chapter gathers more of the families of Judah, including the prayer of Jabez, and then turns to the sons of Simeon, their dwellings, and their conquests. The teaching drew out that the blessing rested heavily on Judah, who multiplied above his brethren, and that the tribe of Simeon took land that had belonged to the sons of Ham.

- Jabez called on the God of Israel and asked that He would bless him indeed, enlarge his coast, keep His hand with him, and keep him from evil, and the Most High granted him what he requested.

# 1 Chronicles 5: The Tribes East of Jordan and Their Captivity

Read the chapter → [[1 Chronicles 5]]

This chapter records the eastern tribes: Reuben, Gad, and the half tribe of Manasseh. It sets down why Reuben lost the birthright, names the families and their dwellings, tells of their victory over the Hagarites because they trusted in the Most High, and closes with their captivity because they went a-whoring after the gods of the land.

- Reuben was the firstborn of Israel, but because he defiled his father's bed his birthright was given to the sons of Joseph, and the genealogy is not reckoned after the birthright.
- Judah prevailed above his brethren, and of him came the chief ruler, though the birthright was Joseph's, much as Esau came out first but did not keep the place of the heir.
- The sons and dwellings of Reuben are named down to Beerah, whom Tilgath-pilneser king of Assyria carried away captive.

- They made war with the Hagarites and were helped against them because they cried unto the Most High in the battle and put their trust in Him, and He was entreated of them.
- They took away much cattle and a hundred thousand captives, for the war was of the Most High; this is the curse of [[Deuteronomy 28|Deuteronomy 28]] falling on the enemy instead of on us.

- Because of this the Most High stirred up the spirit of Pul, that is Tilgath-pilneser king of Assyria, who carried Reuben, Gad, and the half tribe of Manasseh into captivity unto this day.
