# 1 Chronicles 22: David Prepares to Build the House of the Lord

Read the chapter → [[1 Chronicles 22]]

## Introduction

This session covers 1 Chronicles chapters 22 through 25. It is a brief but important reading, heavy with names, offices, and order. David, now old and full of days, cannot build the temple himself because he has shed much blood in war, so he prepares the materials in abundance and charges his son Solomon to build the house unto the name of the Most High. David then sets the Levites in order, dividing them for the work of the house, numbers the priests into their courses by lot, and appoints the singers and musicians for the service of the sanctuary. The running thread is order and organization: keeping the commandments to prosper, and everyone in Israel knowing his place and his service before the Most High.

- David prepared iron in abundance for the nails and for the joinings, brass in abundance without weight, and cedar trees in abundance. The Zidonians and they of Tyre brought much cedar wood to David.

Because of all the wars and fighting David had done, the Most High did not allow him to build the temple.
- The Lord promised David a son who would be a man of rest, to whom he would give rest from all his enemies round about, whose name would be Solomon, and in whose days he would give peace and quietness unto Israel. This son would build the house for the Most High's name, and the Most High would establish the throne of his kingdom over Israel forever.

This is what David was there to do: he subdued all the nations by the power of the Most High.
- He charged them to set their heart and soul to seek the Lord their God, to arise and build the sanctuary of the Lord God, and to bring the ark of the covenant and the holy vessels of God into the house that was to be built. Earlier David had moved the ark from place to place, and that is when he desired to build a house for it before the Lord.

# 1 Chronicles 23: The Numbering and Ordering of the Levites

Read the chapter → [[1 Chronicles 23]]

## Introduction

David, old and full of days, makes Solomon king over Israel and gathers the princes, the priests, and the Levites. The Levites are numbered and divided for the different tasks of the house of the Lord. This chapter shows the order and organization of the Levites, the genealogies of their families, and the duties assigned to them for the service of the sanctuary.

- When David was old and full of days, he made Solomon his son king over Israel and gathered together all the princes of Israel with the priests and the Levites. The Levites were numbered from the age of thirty years and upward, and their number man by man was thirty and eight thousand.
- Of these, twenty and four thousand were to set forward the work of the house of the Lord, six thousand were officers and judges, four thousand were porters, and four thousand praised the Lord with the instruments David made. The Levites were divided up for these different tasks.

- David divided the Levites into courses among the sons of Levi, namely Gershon, Kohath, and Merari, and named their families and their sons in order down the line. He names the father and then names the children, and then names that one's children, going down the line, which is like an inheritance each of them received.
- Concerning Moses the man of God, his sons were named of the tribe of Levi. Aaron was separated that he should sanctify the most holy things, he and his sons forever, to burn incense before the Lord, to minister unto him, and to bless in his name forever. Aaron was set apart specifically for that priestly part, along with all the other Levites who had their different jobs.

David said the Lord God of Israel had given rest unto his people that they might dwell in Jerusalem forever, so the Levites would no more carry the tabernacle nor any vessels of it.
- Their office was to wait on the sons of Aaron for the service of the house of the Lord, in the courts and the chambers, in the purifying of all holy things, and the work of the shewbread and the fine flour and the offerings. They were to stand every morning to thank and praise the Lord, and likewise at evening, and to offer all burnt sacrifices unto the Lord on the sabbaths, the new moons, and the set feasts, and to keep the charge of the tabernacle and the holy place. This shows the order and what each one's job was in the building and service of the temple.

# 1 Chronicles 24: The Divisions and Courses of the Priests

Read the chapter → [[1 Chronicles 24]]

## Introduction

This chapter sets in order the divisions of the sons of Aaron, the priests, dividing them into their courses by lot for their service in the house of the Lord. It shows the ranking and order of the priests and, at the end, the casting of lots of the rest of the Levites over against their brethren.

- There were more chief men found of the sons of Eleazar, sixteen of the house of their fathers, and eight among the sons of Ithamar. They were divided by lot, one sort with another, and Shemaiah the scribe wrote them before the king and the princes, taking record for each.

# 1 Chronicles 25: The Singers and Musicians of the Sanctuary

Read the chapter → [[1 Chronicles 25]]

## Introduction

This chapter appoints the singers and musicians for the service of the house of the Lord, the sons of Asaph, Heman, and Jeduthun, who prophesied with harps, psalteries, and cymbals. It teaches that music is a form of prophecy, and it shows the same order and organization seen throughout these chapters, the musicians divided by lot into twenty and four courses.

**[[1 Chronicles 25#^v1|1 Chronicles 25:1-4]]**

![[1 Chronicles 25#^v1]]

- David and the captains of the host separated to the service the sons of Asaph, Heman, and Jeduthun, who should prophesy with harps, psalteries, and cymbals. Music is a form of prophecy. Miriam, when she came out, prophesied with the women, leading them in prophecy which was with music.
- The sons of Asaph prophesied according to the order of the king, the sons of Jeduthun prophesied with a harp to give thanks and to praise the Lord, and the sons of Heman were given by God to lift up the horn, for God gave Heman fourteen sons and three daughters.

- The number of them with their brethren that were instructed in the songs of the Lord, all that were cunning, was two hundred fourscore and eight. They cast lots, ward against ward, as well the small as the great, the teacher as the scholar.

- This closes the four chapters. They cover the organization of the Levites, the priests, the singers, and the materials for the temple. It is a brief and simple reading, but it shows how the Most High set every office and every family in its proper order and service before him.
