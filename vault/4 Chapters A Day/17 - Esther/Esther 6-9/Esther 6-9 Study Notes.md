# Esther 6: The King Honors Mordecai

Read the chapter → [[Esther 6]]

This session moves through [[Esther 6|Esther 6]] to 9. The recap sets the stage: Vashti was replaced, Esther the Benjamite, raised by her uncle Mordecai, was chosen queen and her name changed from Hadassah. Mordecai had saved the king from a plot against his life. Haman the Agagite, an Edomite, hated Mordecai because Mordecai would not bow to him, for the men of Israel do not bow down. Haman got in the king's ear and had a decree published throughout the kingdom that all the Jews be put to death. Chapter 6 opens with the turning of the tide: what Haman meant for evil, the Most High turned to good. Just as the other nations kept chronicles of their history, so did Israel, and this Bible is the record of that history kept through every captivity.

**[[Esther 6#^v1|Esther 6:1-3]]**

![[Esther 6#^v1]]
![[Esther 6#^v2]]
![[Esther 6#^v3]]

- That night the king could not sleep, and he called for the book of records of the chronicles to be read before him. This is how the Most High works, moving even a king's sleeplessness to set His purpose in motion.
- It was found written that Mordecai had told of Bigthana and Teresh, two chamberlains who sought to lay hand on the king, and yet nothing had been done to honor him for it.
- The king asked what honor had been done to Mordecai, and his servants answered that there was nothing done for him.

**[[Esther 6#^v4|Esther 6:4-5]]**

![[Esther 6#^v4]]
![[Esther 6#^v5]]

- The king asked who was in the court, and Haman had just come into the outer court to speak to the king about hanging Mordecai on the gallows he had prepared.
- In the previous chapter Haman's wife had told him to make a gallows fifty cubits high and hang Mordecai on it, so Haman was coming to carry out that plan at the very moment the king had learned Mordecai saved his life.
- The king's servant said Haman stood in the court, and the king called for him to come in.

**[[Esther 6#^v6|Esther 6:6-9]]**

![[Esther 6#^v6]]
![[Esther 6#^v7]]
![[Esther 6#^v8]]
![[Esther 6#^v9]]

- The king asked Haman what should be done to the man the king delights to honor, and Haman, being so full of himself, assumed the king must mean him, for who in the kingdom was better than he.
- Haman laid out a full plan of royal apparel the king wears, the horse the king rides, and the crown set upon the head, all delivered by a noble prince to array the man and parade him through the city.
- Because of his pride Haman designed the highest honor imaginable, thinking he himself would receive it.

**[[Esther 6#^v10|Esther 6:10-11]]**

![[Esther 6#^v10]]
![[Esther 6#^v11]]

- The king told Haman to make haste and do exactly as he said to Mordecai the Jew who sat at the king's gate, letting nothing fail of all he had spoken.
- Haman then took the apparel and the horse, arrayed Mordecai, brought him on horseback through the street, and proclaimed before him. What Haman planned for himself was given to the man he hated. This is how the Most High works, and it should put a smile on your face.

**[[Esther 6#^v12|Esther 6:12-14]]**

![[Esther 6#^v12]]
![[Esther 6#^v13]]
![[Esther 6#^v14]]

- Mordecai returned to the king's gate, but Haman hurried to his house mourning with his head covered, and told his wife Zeresh and his friends all that had happened.
- His wise men and his wife told him that if Mordecai was of the seed of the Jews, before whom he had begun to fall, he would not prevail but would surely fall before him.
- While they yet talked, the king's chamberlains came to hasten Haman to the banquet Esther had prepared, the banquet she set up after hearing of the decree against her people.

# Esther 7: Haman Hanged on His Own Gallows

Read the chapter → [[Esther 7]]

**[[Esther 7#^v1|Esther 7:1-5]]**

![[Esther 7#^v1]]
![[Esther 7#^v2]]
![[Esther 7#^v3]]
![[Esther 7#^v4]]
![[Esther 7#^v5]]

- At the banquet of wine the king again asked Esther her petition, promising it would be granted even to the half of the kingdom.
- Esther pleaded for her life and the life of her people, for they had been sold to be destroyed, slain, and to perish, and had it been only bondage she would have held her tongue.
- The king demanded to know who he was that dared presume in his heart to do such a thing.

**[[Esther 7#^v6|Esther 7:6]]**

![[Esther 7#^v6]]

- Esther named the adversary and enemy as this wicked Haman, and Haman was afraid before the king and the queen.
- Sitting at the table, Haman could hardly eat, for he had just arrayed Mordecai and just heard his own household warn him he would fall before the Jews, and now he was exposed at the dinner table.

**[[Esther 7#^v7|Esther 7:7-8]]**

![[Esther 7#^v7]]
![[Esther 7#^v8]]

- The king rose in his wrath and went into the palace garden, and Haman stood up to make request for his life to Esther, seeing that evil was determined against him.
- When the king returned, Haman had fallen upon the bed where Esther was, and the king took it as an assault on the queen in his own house.
- As the word went out of the king's mouth, they covered Haman's face.

**[[Esther 7#^v9|Esther 7:9-10]]**

![[Esther 7#^v9]]
![[Esther 7#^v10]]

- Harbonah, a chamberlain, told the king of the gallows fifty cubits high that Haman had made for Mordecai, who had spoken good for the king, standing at Haman's house.
- The king commanded Haman be hanged on it, and they hanged Haman on the gallows he had prepared for Mordecai, and the king's wrath was pacified. This is the Most High, a man of war, and this is how He moves.

# Esther 8: The Decree Reversed for the Jews

Read the chapter → [[Esther 8]]

**[[Esther 8#^v1|Esther 8:1-2]]**

![[Esther 8#^v1]]
![[Esther 8#^v2]]

- That day the king gave the house of Haman, the Jews' enemy, to Esther, and Mordecai came before the king, for Esther had told what he was to her.
- The king took off the ring he had taken from Haman and gave it to Mordecai, and Esther set Mordecai over the house of Haman. Everything Haman had was given to Mordecai.

**[[Esther 8#^v3|Esther 8:3-6]]**

![[Esther 8#^v3]]
![[Esther 8#^v4]]
![[Esther 8#^v5]]
![[Esther 8#^v6]]

- Esther fell down at the king's feet and besought him with tears to put away the mischief of Haman the Agagite and the device he had devised against the Jews.
- The king held out the golden scepter, and Esther rose and asked that letters be written to reverse the letters Haman devised to destroy the Jews in all the provinces.
- She asked how she could endure to see the evil that would come upon her people and the destruction of her kindred.

**[[Esther 8#^v7|Esther 8:7-8]]**

![[Esther 8#^v7]]
![[Esther 8#^v8]]

- The king told Esther and Mordecai that he had given Esther the house of Haman and had hanged Haman for laying hand upon the Jews.
- He granted them to write for the Jews in the king's name and seal it with the king's ring, for a writing sealed with the king's ring could no man reverse.
- Esther and Mordecai were given the king's authority, so the reputation and word of the king stood behind whatever they wrote.

**[[Esther 8#^v9|Esther 8:9-10]]**

![[Esther 8#^v9]]
![[Esther 8#^v10]]

- The king's scribes were called in the third month, Sivan, on the twenty-third day, and it was written to the Jews, lieutenants, deputies, and rulers of the provinces from India to Ethiopia, a hundred twenty and seven provinces, each in its own writing and language.
- This is the span of the Persian empire, and Israel was scattered all through it, from India to Ethiopia, not concentrated in one place but spread out during the captivity, which shows we have history in these lands.

  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v64|Deuteronomy 28:64]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v64]]
    The Most High foretold Israel would be scattered among all people from one end of the earth unto the other.

**[[Esther 8#^v11|Esther 8:11-14]]**

![[Esther 8#^v11]]
![[Esther 8#^v12]]
![[Esther 8#^v13]]
![[Esther 8#^v14]]

- The king granted the Jews in every city to gather, stand for their life, and destroy any power that would assault them, both little ones and women, and to take the spoil.
- Everyone who had listened to Haman's proclamation to kill the Jews was now subject to the Jews' revenge upon their enemies.
- The decree was to be ready upon the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, Adar, the day the Jews would avenge themselves, for nothing pacifies the Most High but vengeance on the enemies of His people.
- The posts rode out hastened on mules, camels, and dromedaries by the king's commandment.

**[[Esther 8#^v15|Esther 8:15-17]]**

![[Esther 8#^v15]]
![[Esther 8#^v16]]
![[Esther 8#^v17]]

- Mordecai went out from the king in royal apparel of blue and white, a great crown of gold, and a garment of fine linen and purple, and the city of Shushan rejoiced and was glad.
- The Jews had light, gladness, joy, and honor, and in every province and city where the decree came they had a feast and a good day.
- Many of the people of the land became Jews, for the fear of the Jews fell upon them, a picture of the future when the fear of Israel will be on all nations and they will serve the Most High.

# Esther 9: Purim and the Victory of the Jews

Read the chapter → [[Esther 9]]

**[[Esther 9#^v1|Esther 9:1-4]]**

![[Esther 9#^v1]]
![[Esther 9#^v2]]
![[Esther 9#^v3]]
![[Esther 9#^v4]]

- In the twelfth month Adar, on the thirteenth day, when the enemies of the Jews hoped to have power over them, it was turned to the contrary and the Jews had rule over those that hated them.
- The Jews gathered in their cities, and no man could withstand them, for the fear of them fell upon all people, and the rulers, lieutenants, deputies, and officers helped the Jews because the fear of Mordecai fell upon them.
- Mordecai was great in the king's house and his fame went throughout all the provinces, and this man waxed greater and greater. Though Esther was queen, she was still subject to Mordecai, the man of Israel who wrote the edict.

**[[Esther 9#^v5|Esther 9:5-10]]**

![[Esther 9#^v5]]
![[Esther 9#^v6]]
![[Esther 9#^v7]]
![[Esther 9#^v8]]
![[Esther 9#^v9]]
![[Esther 9#^v10]]

- The Jews smote all their enemies with the sword, slaughter, and destruction, and did what they would to those that hated them.
- In Shushan the palace they slew and destroyed five hundred men, and they slew the ten sons of Haman the son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews.
- On the spoil they laid not their hands.

**[[Esther 9#^v11|Esther 9:11-15]]**

![[Esther 9#^v11]]
![[Esther 9#^v12]]
![[Esther 9#^v13]]
![[Esther 9#^v14]]
![[Esther 9#^v15]]

- The number slain in Shushan was brought before the king, who told Esther the Jews had slain five hundred men and the ten sons of Haman, and asked what more she desired.
- Esther asked that the Jews in Shushan be granted the next day also according to the decree, and that Haman's ten sons be hanged on the gallows.
- The king commanded it done, and the Jews in Shushan gathered on the fourteenth day and slew three hundred more, but on the spoil they laid not their hands.

**[[Esther 9#^v16|Esther 9:16-19]]**

![[Esther 9#^v16]]
![[Esther 9#^v17]]
![[Esther 9#^v18]]
![[Esther 9#^v19]]

- The other Jews in the king's provinces gathered, stood for their lives, had rest from their enemies, and slew of their foes seventy and five thousand, laying not their hands on the prey.
- This was on the thirteenth day of Adar, and on the fourteenth they rested and made a day of feasting and gladness.
- The Jews of Shushan rested on the fifteenth day, so the Jews of the unwalled towns made the fourteenth a day of gladness, feasting, and sending portions one to another. This is where the gift giving of Purim comes from.

**[[Esther 9#^v20|Esther 9:20-22]]**

![[Esther 9#^v20]]
![[Esther 9#^v21]]
![[Esther 9#^v22]]

- Mordecai wrote these things and sent letters to all the Jews near and far to establish that they keep the fourteenth and fifteenth days of Adar yearly.
- These were the days the Jews rested from their enemies, the month turned from sorrow to joy and from mourning to a good day.
- They were to make them days of feasting, joy, sending portions one to another, and gifts to the poor. This is where the feast of Purim comes from, a feast that commemorates the destruction of Israel's enemies.

**[[Esther 9#^v23|Esther 9:23-25]]**

![[Esther 9#^v23]]
![[Esther 9#^v24]]
![[Esther 9#^v25]]

- The Jews undertook to keep what Mordecai wrote, because Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite had devised against the Jews to destroy them and had cast Pur, that is the lot, to consume them.
- Pur was the Persian custom of casting lots, much like the lot cast for Jonah to see whose fault the storm was.
- When Esther came before the king, he commanded by letters that Haman's wicked device return upon his own head, and that he and his sons be hanged on the gallows.

  - **[[Jonah 1#^v7|Jonah 1:7]]**
    ![[Jonah 1#^v7]]
    The mariners cast lots to know for whose cause the evil was upon them, and the lot fell upon Jonah.

**[[Esther 9#^v26|Esther 9:26-28]]**

![[Esther 9#^v26]]
![[Esther 9#^v27]]
![[Esther 9#^v28]]

- These days were called Purim after the name Pur, and the Jews ordained and took upon themselves and their seed to keep these two days every year.
- The days were to be remembered and kept throughout every generation, family, province, and city.
- These days of Purim should not fail from among the Jews nor the memorial of them perish from their seed, which shows Israel still celebrates Purim today.

**[[Esther 9#^v29|Esther 9:29-32]]**

![[Esther 9#^v29]]
![[Esther 9#^v30]]
![[Esther 9#^v31]]
![[Esther 9#^v32]]

- Esther the queen, the daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew wrote with all authority to confirm this second letter of Purim.
- Letters were sent to all the Jews in the hundred twenty and seven provinces with words of peace and truth, to confirm the days of Purim in their appointed times.
- The decree of Esther confirmed these matters of Purim, and it was written in the book, which is Israel's own book of history.
