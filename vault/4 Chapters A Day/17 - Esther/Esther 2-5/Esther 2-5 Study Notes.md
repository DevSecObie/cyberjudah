# Esther 2: Esther Made Queen and Mordecai Uncovers the Plot

Read the chapter → [[Esther 2]]

## Introduction

This session covers the book of Esther chapters two through five, picking up after chapter one where Vashti had a spirit on her, thought she could disrespect the king, and lost her royal estate. Chapter two brings in a fair young virgin named Hadassah, also called Esther, the uncle's daughter of Mordecai a Benjamite carried away from Jerusalem in the captivity. She is racially ambiguous, a daughter of Israel who does not look like what people expect, which is why the Most High teaches us not to judge after the sight of the eyes but by whether a spirit bears witness with the Bible. Esther is set as queen instead of Vashti, and Mordecai uncovers a plot against the king. Chapters three through five bring in Haman the Agagite, a descendant of the Amalekites Saul failed to utterly destroy, whose hatred of Mordecai becomes a decree to destroy all the Jews, and Esther is moved to stand for her people for such a time as this.

# Esther 3: Haman the Agagite and the Decree to Destroy the Jews

Read the chapter → [[Esther 3]]

![[Esther 3#^v2]]

- After these things the king promoted Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite and set his seat above all the princes, and all the king's servants in the king's gate bowed and reverenced him, for the king had so commanded, but Mordecai bowed not nor did him reverence.
- The servants asked daily why Mordecai transgressed the king's commandment, and he hearkened not, but told them he was a Jew, and they told Haman to see whether Mordecai's matter would stand. We do not bow, the same as the three Hebrew children did not bow to the image in Babylon, for we pledge allegiance to none but the Most High.

    Saul spared Agag king of the Amalekites when he was commanded to utterly destroy them, so the Agagite line lived on and Haman descends from Amalek, which explains his deep disdain for Israel.

    ![[Additions to Esther 13#^v13]]

    Mordecai's own prayer shows he refused to bow not from contempt but so as not to set the glory of man above the glory of the Most High.

![[Esther 3#^v6]]

- When Haman saw that Mordecai bowed not nor did him reverence, he was full of wrath, and thought scorn to lay hands on Mordecai alone once they had shown him the people of Mordecai.
- Haman sought to destroy all the Jews throughout the whole kingdom of Ahasuerus, the people of Mordecai, not one man only. Because Mordecai would not bow, Edom's hatred sought a death sentence for the entire nation, seeking to erase us out of history.

- Haman told the king there was a certain people scattered abroad whose laws were diverse from all people and who kept not the king's laws, and it was not for the king's profit to suffer them, offering ten thousand talents of silver to have them destroyed. The king took his ring and gave it to Haman the Jews' enemy, granting him the silver and the people to do as he pleased. This is putting a hit out on the entire nation of Israel.

# Esther 5: Esther Finds Favour and Haman Builds the Gallows

Read the chapter → [[Esther 5]]

**[[Esther 5#^v1|Esther 5:1-2]]**

![[Esther 5#^v1]]
![[Esther 5#^v2]]

- On the third day Esther put on her royal apparel and stood in the inner court of the king's house over against the king's house, and the king sat on his royal throne over against the gate.
- When the king saw Esther the queen standing in the court, she obtained favour in his sight, and he held out to her the golden sceptre in his hand, and Esther drew near and touched the top of the sceptre. She was not called by name, yet as soon as he saw her he received her, for a daughter of Israel finds favour in the eyes of a man.

**[[Esther 5#^v3|Esther 5:3-5]]**

![[Esther 5#^v3]]
![[Esther 5#^v4]]
![[Esther 5#^v5]]

- The king asked what would Esther the queen and what was her request, offering even to the half of the kingdom, and Esther asked that the king and Haman come that day to a banquet she had prepared.
- The king said cause Haman to make haste that he may do as Esther said, so the king and Haman came to the banquet Esther had prepared. When a woman he loves prepares a table for him, the king bends to hear her every request.

**[[Esther 5#^v6|Esther 5:6-8]]**

![[Esther 5#^v6]]
![[Esther 5#^v7]]
![[Esther 5#^v8]]

- At the banquet of wine the king again asked Esther's petition and request, to the half of the kingdom, and Esther answered that if she had found favour and it pleased the king, let the king and Haman come again to the banquet she would prepare on the morrow, and she would do as the king had said.
- The book of Esther shows both sides, the good side of a woman doing what she should and the wicked side of what she should not, so it puts a check on the spirit that rises up in women to rebel.

**[[Esther 5#^v9|Esther 5:9-14]]**

![[Esther 5#^v9]]
![[Esther 5#^v10]]
![[Esther 5#^v11]]
![[Esther 5#^v12]]
![[Esther 5#^v13]]
![[Esther 5#^v14]]

- Haman went forth that day joyful and glad of heart, but when he saw Mordecai in the king's gate who stood not up nor moved for him, he was full of indignation, yet refrained himself and went home and called for his friends and Zeresh his wife.
- Haman told them of the glory of his riches, his many children, and all the ways the king had advanced him above the princes, and that Esther had let no man come to the banquet with the king but himself, and he was bidden again on the morrow. Yet all this availed him nothing so long as he saw Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king's gate.
- Then Zeresh his wife and all his friends counselled that a gallows fifty cubits high be made, and on the morrow he should speak to the king that Mordecai be hanged thereon, and then go merrily to the banquet, and the thing pleased Haman and he caused the gallows to be made. It was the wife's idea, for the enemy's women are in cahoots with the work against Israel.
