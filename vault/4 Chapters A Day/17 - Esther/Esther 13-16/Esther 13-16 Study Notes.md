# Esther 13: The Decree of Destruction and the Prayer of Mardocheus

Read the chapter → [[Additions to Esther 13]]

## Introduction

This session finishes the book of Esther by going through the Apocrypha, the four additions known as "The Rest of the Book of Esther," chapters 13 through 16. It picks up where the canonical account left off: Haman has been promoted, Esther has been made queen, and Haman has laid a plot against the Jews because Mardocheus would not bow to him. Chapter 13 gives the copy of the first letter, the decree Haman drafted in the king's name to destroy all the Jews in one day, and then the prayer of Mardocheus. Chapter 14 is Esther's prayer. Chapter 15 shows Esther going before the king. Chapter 16 gives the second letter, the reversal, which calls Israel the children of the most high God. As we read, remember this is not just the story of Esther and Mardocheus and Haman; it is the story of our people, a day of sorrow turned into joy, and it is history to lean on when we go through our own affliction in captivity.

**[[Additions to Esther 13#^v1|Esther 13:1-3]]**

![[Additions to Esther 13#^v1]]
![[Additions to Esther 13#^v2]]
![[Additions to Esther 13#^v3]]

- The copy of the first letter opens with the great king Artaxerxes writing to the princes and governors under him, from India unto Ethiopia, over an hundred and seven and twenty provinces. This is the same king called Ahasuerus in the canonical book.
- The king presents himself as one who does not lift himself up in presumption of his authority but carries himself with equity and mildness, purposing to settle his subjects in a quiet life and make the kingdom peaceable. The words sound noble, but the letter itself was written by Haman's scribes; the king gave Haman his seal to send it.
- Aman, that is Haman, is named as the one who excelled in wisdom and held the second place in the kingdom. The whole letter is Haman speaking through the king's authority.

- The letter paints Israel as a malicious people scattered among all nations, with laws contrary to all nations, continually despising the commandments of kings. This is the very picture Haman painted so the uniting of the kingdoms could not go forward.
- The charge is the same slander repeated: that this people alone is continually in opposition unto all men, differing in the strange manner of their laws. Haman wants them seen as a mischievous, malicious nation.
- The command goes out that all the Jews, with their wives and children, be utterly destroyed by the sword without mercy and pity on the fourteenth day of the twelfth month Adar. This is an order for the destruction of the Israelites, young and old together.
- This sounds exactly like the curses laid out for our people in [[Deuteronomy 28|Deuteronomy 28]], where the enemy shows no favor toward the young or the old. Both the ancient and the present generation were marked for the grave in one day.

- He confesses the Most High as maker of heaven and earth and all the wondrous things under the heaven, Lord of all things, whom no man can resist. This is the ground of his prayer: the God who made all things can deliver his people.

- Mardocheus tells the Most High that his refusal to bow to proud Aman was neither in contempt nor pride nor desire of glory. He could have kissed the soles of Haman's feet for the salvation of Israel, but he would not prefer the glory of man above the glory of God, nor worship any but the Most High.
- He asks the Lord to spare his people, whose enemies desire to destroy the inheritance that has been the Lord's from the beginning, the portion delivered out of Egypt. He pleads that the Most High turn their sorrow into joy and not destroy the mouths of them that praise him.
- All Israel in like manner cried most earnestly unto the Lord because their death was before their eyes. Mardocheus rent his clothes and cried through the city with a loud and bitter cry, as the canonical account records.

# Esther 14: The Prayer of Esther

Read the chapter → [[Additions to Esther 14]]

- Queen Esther, in fear of death, resorted unto the Lord. She laid away her glorious apparel and put on the garments of anguish and mourning, covering her head with ashes instead of precious ointments and humbling her body greatly.
- She prayed to the Lord God of Israel, confessing him as her only King and her only helper, for her danger was in her own hand. She was the one who had to go before the king.
- From her youth she had heard in the tribe of her family how the Most High took Israel from among all people and their fathers for a perpetual inheritance, and that he performed whatsoever he promised them. She stands on the covenant history she was taught.

- Esther confesses the true reason for the captivity: our people sinned before the Most High and were given into the hands of their enemies because they worshipped other gods. She says plainly, O Lord, thou art righteous. This is the same confession our people must make about why we went into captivity and under these curses.
- She says the enemy is not satisfied that Israel is in bitter captivity; they have joined hands with their idols to abolish what the Most High ordained, to destroy his inheritance, and to stop the mouth of them that praise him. This is the same thing today: they are not satisfied with us serving in captivity, they want to destroy our people.
- The heathen open their mouths to set forth the praises of idols and to magnify a fleshly king for ever. The Persians, Assyrians, and Egyptians all magnified themselves when they were in power.

**[[Additions to Esther 14#^v11|Esther 14:11-14]]**

- Esther asks that the Lord give not his sceptre to them that be nothing, and that he turn the device of the enemy upon himself, making an example of the one who began this against us, meaning Haman. The evil he devised she prays would fall upon his own head.
- She asks the Most High to make himself known in the time of affliction and to give her boldness before the king, whom she calls the lion. She has to stand before the king to make her request, so she asks for eloquent speech and that the king's heart be turned to hate the one who fights against them.
- She pleads to be delivered by the Lord's hand, calling herself desolate with no other helper but the Most High. Her boldness comes from prayer, not from her own position.

![[Additions to Esther 14#^v15]]

- Esther tells the Most High that she hates the glory of the unrighteous and abhors the bed of the uncircumcised and of all the heathen. She hated the high estate she carried and wore the crown as a menstruous rag, not wearing it when she was private by herself.
- Unlike our people who love the seats of government they are given and do not fight for their people, Esther despised the position and fought for her people. When she saw evil coming upon Israel, she went to the Lord.
- She testifies that she had not eaten at Aman's table nor greatly esteemed the king's feast nor drunk the wine of the drink offerings, and had known no joy since she was brought there except in the Lord God of Abraham. She asks the mighty God above all to hear the forlorn and deliver her out of her fear.

- Upon the third day, when she had ended her prayer, Esther laid away her mourning garments and put on her glorious apparel. The prayer came first; the appearance before the king came after.
- Being gloriously adorned, she first called upon God, who is the beholder and saviour of all things, and then took two maids with her. She leaned upon one, carrying herself daintily, while the other bore up her train.

- Esther was ruddy through the perfection of her beauty, her countenance cheerful and amiable, but her heart was in anguish for fear. The only time one could come before the king was when called, and she was coming without going through the proper order.
- She passed through all the doors and stood before the king, who sat upon his royal throne clothed with robes of majesty glittering with gold and precious stones, and he was very dreadful. When he lifted up his countenance and looked fiercely upon her, the queen fainted and bowed herself upon the head of her maid.
- Then God changed the spirit of the king into mildness. He leaped from his throne, took her in his arms until she came to herself, and comforted her with loving words. The Most High turned the king's heart.

- Not only did the Most High change the spirit of the king when Esther came in, but he had changed it altogether. The king who was so easily persuaded by Haman at first was now moved by the Most High to be persuaded by Esther.

# Esther 16: The Second Letter and the Reversal

![[Additions to Esther 16#^v2]]

- This is the copy of the second letter, again in the name of the great king Artaxerxes to the same provinces from India unto Ethiopia. But this letter was written by Esther with the king's seal, and it runs completely contrary to the first letter that went out under Haman.
- The letter warns that many, the more they are honored by gracious princes, wax the more proud, and endeavor to hurt not only the king's subjects but also those that do them good. This is a description of Haman himself.
- Such men, lifted up with the glorious words of lewd persons, think to escape the justice of God that seeth all things and hateth evil. Fair speech from those put in trust has caused many rulers to be partakers of innocent blood, beguiling the innocency and goodness of princes.

- The king says these things may be seen not so much by ancient histories as by what has been wickedly done of late through those unworthily placed in authority. He resolves to take care for the time to come that the kingdom may be quiet and peaceable, changing his purposes and judging with more equal proceeding.
- Aman is exposed as a Macedonian, the son of Amadatha, a stranger from the Persian blood, received as a stranger yet raised to be called the king's father and honored as the next person unto the king. He was not a Persian, but was set as number two in the kingdom.
- Not bearing his great dignity, Haman went about to deprive the king of his kingdom and life, seeking by cunning deceit the destruction of Mardocheus, who saved the king's life, and of blameless Esther, partaker of the kingdom, together with their whole nation. He thought to translate the kingdom of the Persians to the Macedonians.

**[[Additions to Esther 16#^v15|Esther 16:15-20]]**

![[Additions to Esther 16#^v15]]
![[Additions to Esther 16#^v16]]

- This letter finds that the Jews whom Haman delivered to utter destruction are no evildoers but live by most just laws. This is the direct opposite of the first letter, which called them a people whose laws were contrary and in opposition to all nations.
- The king declares that the Jews are children of the most high and most mighty living God, who ordered the kingdom both unto the king and his progenitors. This is the plain testimony of scripture concerning who Israel is.
- Therefore the first letters sent by Haman are not to be executed, for Haman himself is hanged at the gates of Susa with all his family, the Most High rendering vengeance according to his deserts. The copy of this second letter is to be published everywhere so the Jews may live after their own laws and, on the thirteenth day of Adar, be avenged on those who set upon them.

- Almighty God turned to joy the very day the chosen people should have perished. This is the day that became the feast the Jews keep, a day of sorrow turned into joy.
- The king commands that this day be kept an high day among their solemn feasts with all feasting, that there may be safety both now and hereafter, but to those who conspire against Israel a memorial of destruction. Every city and country that will not do according to these things is to be destroyed without mercy.
- This is the same lesson for our people today. We are going through days of sorrow, but joy is coming as long as we stay in the commandments and trust in the Lord. Search out the former age and the fathers, for this is the story of our people, and it is history to lean on in our own affliction.
