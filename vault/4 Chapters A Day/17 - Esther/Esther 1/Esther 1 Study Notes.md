# Esther 1: Vashti Refuses the King and the Law of Order in the House

Read the chapter → [[Esther 1]]

## Introduction

This chapter opens the book of Esther in the days of Ahasuerus, the Persian king who reigned from India even unto Ethiopia over a hundred and twenty seven provinces. It sets the stage for the whole book with a feast, a queen who refuses the king's command, and a decree sent into every province. The thread the teacher draws out is the biblical order of the house: the Most High set the man to bear rule in his own house, and the earth's societies keep reversing that order, putting the woman over the man and even the children over the parents. Vashti's refusal becomes the occasion for a law written among the Persians and Medes that every man should bear rule in his own house. Read this canonical chapter as the record of a kingdom's excess and a decree that, by the providence of the Most High, opens the door for Esther.

- Vashti the queen also made a feast for the women in the royal house belonging to the king. On the seventh day, when the heart of the king was merry with wine, he commanded his seven chamberlains to bring Vashti before him with the crown royal to show the people and princes her beauty, for she was fair to look on.
- But the queen Vashti refused to come at the king's commandment by his chamberlains, and the king was very wroth and his anger burned in him. She disrespected the king before everyone, and a judgment was now set to come.

    ![[Jeremiah 31#^v22]]
    The Most High created a new thing in the earth, a woman shall compass a man; Vashti compassing the king is the very thing the wise men move to stop before it spreads.

**[[Esther 1#^v13|Esther 1:13-15]]**

- The king turned to the wise men who knew the times, for so was the king's manner toward all that knew law and judgment, and the seven princes of Persia and Media who saw the king's face and sat first in the kingdom were next to him.
- He asked what should be done unto the queen Vashti according to law, because she had not performed the commandment of the king by the chamberlains. A judgment had to be rendered because she disrespected him in front of everybody.

**[[Esther 1#^v16|Esther 1:16-18]]**

![[Esther 1#^v16]]
![[Esther 1#^v17]]
![[Esther 1#^v18]]

- Memucan answered before the king and the princes that Vashti had not done wrong to the king only, but also to all the princes and all the people in all the provinces of the king.
- The deed of the queen would come abroad unto all women, so that they would despise their husbands in their eyes, saying the king commanded Vashti and she came not; likewise the ladies of Persia and Media would say the same that day, and there would arise too much contempt and wrath.

**[[Esther 1#^v19|Esther 1:19-20]]**

![[Esther 1#^v19]]
![[Esther 1#^v20]]

- Memucan advised the king to let a royal commandment go from him, written among the laws of the Persians and Medes that it be not altered, that Vashti come no more before the king, and that he give her royal estate unto another better than she.
- When the king's decree was published throughout all his empire, for it was great, all the wives would give their husbands honour, both to great and small. Vashti loses her estate so that the order of the house would be upheld everywhere.

**[[Esther 1#^v21|Esther 1:21-22]]**

![[Esther 1#^v21]]
![[Esther 1#^v22]]

- The saying pleased the king and the princes, and the king did according to the word of Memucan, sending letters into all his provinces, to every province and people after their language, that every man should bear rule in his own house.
- This order, that every man should bear rule in his own house, is set up in the Bible for the man, and it is the very order the Most High established that the societies of the earth keep reversing.

  - **[[Titus 1#^v5|Titus 1:5]]**
    ![[Titus 1#^v5]]
    Things left wanting are to be set in order, which is the work of restoring the house to its right order that this chapter puts into a decree.
  - **[[Isaiah 3#^v12|Isaiah 3:12]]**
    ![[Isaiah 3#^v12]]
    Where the order is out of course, children become the people's oppressors and women rule over them, the reversal of the house the Most High warns against.
