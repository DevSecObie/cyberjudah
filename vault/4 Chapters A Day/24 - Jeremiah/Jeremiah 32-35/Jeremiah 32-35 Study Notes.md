# Jeremiah 32: Buying the Field of Redemption

Read the chapter → [[Jeremiah 32]]

This session covers Jeremiah chapters 32 through 35 during the Babylonian captivity of the Israelites, a history also found in 2 Kings, 2 Chronicles, and the prophets Daniel and Ezekiel. While Jerusalem is besieged and Jeremiah is shut up in prison, the Most High commands him to buy a field as a sign that houses, fields, and vineyards shall be possessed again in the land after the seventy years. The reading moves through the prophecy of full restoration, the everlasting covenant and the branch of righteousness raised up to David, the broken covenant over the liberty of the servants, and the obedience of the Rechabites held up as a rebuke to Judah.

- The word came to Jeremiah in the tenth year of Zedekiah king of Judah, the eighteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar, when the king of Babylon's army besieged Jerusalem and Jeremiah was shut up in the court of the prison.
- Zedekiah had imprisoned him for prophesying that the Most High would give the city into the hand of the king of Babylon; Zedekiah himself would not escape but would be led to Babylon, speak with him mouth to mouth, and behold his eyes.
- This was the word of the Most High that came through Jeremiah to the king, that though they fight against the Chaldeans they should not prosper.

- Hanameel his uncle's son came to Jeremiah in the court of the prison to sell him the field at Anathoth, for the right of redemption and inheritance was Jeremiah's; then he knew it was the word of the Most High.
- He bought the field, weighed him seventeen shekels of silver, subscribed the evidence, sealed it, and took witnesses, taking both the sealed evidence and the open according to the law and custom, and delivered it to Baruch the son of Neriah.
- Anathoth was in the country of Benjamin because the priests were given cities and suburbs to dwell in.

- The Most High declares that houses, fields, and vineyards shall be possessed again in this land, because the prophecy was that the captivity would last seventy years and then the people would return.

    The whole land would be a desolation and these nations serve the king of Babylon seventy years before He punished Babylon; after Babylon we passed to the Persians and Medes, and Cyrus the Persian let us return.

- After the Babylonian and Persian captivities the northern kingdom came to the western hemisphere, so these things, us all being set back in the land and in full agreement, have yet to take place.

# Jeremiah 33: The Branch of Righteousness and the Covenant of Day and Night

Read the chapter → [[Jeremiah 33]]

**[[Jeremiah 33#^v1|Jeremiah 33:1-5]]**

![[Jeremiah 33#^v1]]
![[Jeremiah 33#^v2]]
![[Jeremiah 33#^v3]]

- The word came to Jeremiah a second time while still shut up in the prison: the Most High who made and formed the earth to establish it invites him to call, promising to answer and show him great and mighty things he knows not.
- The houses thrown down by the mount and the sword would be filled with the dead bodies of men He slew in His anger, for He hid His face from the city for all their wickedness.
  - **[[Hosea 5#^v15|Hosea 5:15]]**
    ![[Hosea 5#^v15]]
    He returns to His place until they acknowledge their offense and seek His face, and in their affliction they will seek Him early.
  - **[[1 Kings 8#^v47|1 Kings 8:47]]**
    ![[1 Kings 8#^v47]]
    We would have to acknowledge our offenses before the Most High, and in the affliction of the other nations we return to Him.

- The Most High will bring health and cure and reveal the abundance of peace and truth, which has not yet been fulfilled because there is still much violence in Israel and against us.
- He will cause the captivity of Judah and Israel to return and build them as at the first, cleansing them from all iniquity and pardoning all their transgressions against Him.
- It shall be to Him a name of joy, a praise, and an honor before all nations, who will fear and tremble at the goodness He procures, for the nations know this is true and are struck when they see us carry ourselves as sons and daughters of the covenant.
  - **[[Amos 9#^v11|Amos 9:11]]**
    ![[Amos 9#^v11]]
    In that day He will raise up the fallen tabernacle of David, close up the breaches, and build it as in the days of old.

    ![[Acts 15#^v15]]
    ![[Acts 15#^v16]]
    Even the New Testament points to future prophecy that He will return and build again the ruins of the tabernacle of David.

- She shall be called The Most High our righteousness, and David shall never want a man to sit upon the throne of the house of Israel.
- Neither shall the priests the Levites want a man to offer burnt offerings, kindle meat offerings, and do sacrifice continually.

# Jeremiah 34: Liberty Proclaimed and Then Revoked

Read the chapter → [[Jeremiah 34]]

**[[Jeremiah 34#^v1|Jeremiah 34:1-7]]**

![[Jeremiah 34#^v1]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v2]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v3]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v4]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v5]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v6]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v7]]

- The word came when Nebuchadnezzar and all his army fought against Jerusalem: Jeremiah is to tell Zedekiah that the Most High will give the city into Babylon's hand to be burned with fire, and Zedekiah shall not escape.
- His eyes shall behold the king of Babylon's eyes and he shall speak with him mouth to mouth and go to Babylon, yet he shall not die by the sword but die in peace, with the burnings and lamentations of the former kings.
- Jeremiah spoke these words while Babylon's army fought against Jerusalem, Lachish, and Azekah, which alone remained of the defensed cities of Judah.

**[[Jeremiah 34#^v8|Jeremiah 34:8-11]]**

![[Jeremiah 34#^v8]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v9]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v10]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v11]]

- Zedekiah had made a covenant with all the people at Jerusalem to proclaim liberty, that every man let his Hebrew manservant and maidservant go free and none serve himself of his Jewish brother.
- The princes and people obeyed and let them go, but afterward they turned and brought the servants and handmaids back into subjection.
  - **[[Leviticus 25#^v9|Leviticus 25:9-10]]**
    ![[Leviticus 25#^v9]]
    ![[Leviticus 25#^v10]]
    The trumpet of the jubilee sounds on the day of atonement to hallow the fiftieth year and proclaim liberty, returning every man to his possession and to his family.

**[[Jeremiah 34#^v12|Jeremiah 34:12-16]]**

![[Jeremiah 34#^v12]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v13]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v14]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v15]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v16]]

- The Most High reminds them He made a covenant with their fathers when He brought them out of Egypt, out of the house of bondage, that at the end of seven years every man should let his Hebrew brother go free.
- The fathers hearkened not, and though this generation had turned and done right in proclaiming liberty, making a covenant before Him in the house called by His name, they turned and polluted His name.
- They caused every man to bring his servant and handmaid back into subjection to be servants and handmaids again.
  - **[[Exodus 21#^v2|Exodus 21:2]]**
    ![[Exodus 21#^v2]]
    The commandment was that a Hebrew servant serve six years and in the seventh go out free.
  - **[[Psalms 138#^v2|Psalms 138:2]]**
    ![[Psalms 138#^v2]]
    Polluting His name was not mispronouncing a name but breaking His commandments, for He magnified His word above His name.

**[[Jeremiah 34#^v17|Jeremiah 34:17-18]]**

![[Jeremiah 34#^v17]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v18]]

- Because they would not proclaim liberty to their brothers, the Most High proclaims a liberty to the sword, the pestilence, and the famine, and will make them to be removed into all the kingdoms of the earth, meaning we would be scattered.
- He will give the men who transgressed His covenant, who cut the calf in twain and passed between the parts thereof, into the hand of their enemies.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 4#^v27|Deuteronomy 4:27]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 4#^v27]]
    He warned we would be scattered among the nations, left few in number.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v64|Deuteronomy 28:64]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v64]]
    He would scatter us among all people from one end of the earth to the other.

**[[Jeremiah 34#^v19|Jeremiah 34:19-22]]**

![[Jeremiah 34#^v19]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v20]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v21]]
![[Jeremiah 34#^v22]]

- The princes, eunuchs, priests, and all the people who passed between the parts of the calf He will give into the hand of their enemies, and their dead bodies shall be meat for the fowls of heaven and the beasts of the earth.
- Zedekiah and his princes He will give into the hand of Babylon's army, which He will cause to return, take, and burn the city, and make the cities of Judah a desolation without inhabitant.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v26|Deuteronomy 28:26]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v26]]
    Our carcasses would be meat unto the fowls of the air and the beasts of the earth.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 32#^v26|Deuteronomy 32:26]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 32#^v26]]
    Being scattered ties into loving your neighbor as yourself; going into the Passover season we must purge out the old leaven of malice and hatred and get our spirits right.

# Jeremiah 35: The Obedience of the Rechabites

- In the days of Jehoiakim the Most High sent Jeremiah to the house of the Rechabites to bring them into the temple chambers and set wine before them to drink.
- Jaazaniah and the whole house of the Rechabites refused, saying Jonadab the son of Rechab their father commanded them to drink no wine, build no house, sow no seed, and plant no vineyard, but dwell in tents all their days that they might live long in the land.
- They had obeyed the voice of Jonadab in all things, dwelling in tents, and only came to Jerusalem for fear of the army of the Chaldeans and the Syrians.

- The Most High tells Jeremiah to point Judah to the Rechabites: the sons of Jonadab kept their father's command to drink no wine to this day, but this people would not hearken to Him though He rose early and spoke.
- He sent all His servants the prophets calling every man to return from his evil way, amend his doings, and go not after other gods, promising they would dwell in the land, yet they would not incline their ear.
- Because the Rechabites obeyed the commandment of their father, Jonadab shall not want a man to stand before Him for ever; but because Judah would not hear or answer, He will bring upon them all the evil He pronounced.
