# Jeremiah 44: The Judgment on the Jews Who Fled to Egypt

Read the chapter → [[Jeremiah 44]]

This session covers Jeremiah chapters 44 through 47, coming to the end of the book of Jeremiah in the Babylonian captivity. The Most High tells the remnant that fled to Egypt that the very curses of [[Deuteronomy 28|Deuteronomy 28]] will pursue and overtake them there, because they went right back to idolatry, burning incense to the queen of heaven. The women worshipped without their men and the men had no rule over their houses, the same disorder we see in Babylon the great today. Then come the words of comfort to Baruch the scribe, whose writings are preserved in the Apocrypha, and the prophecies against the gentile nations, Egypt and the Philistines, that had a hand in spoiling Israel. This is the book we are the people of, and the Most High raised up His prophets, the Israelites, rising early to warn us not to do the abominable things He hates.

- The word came to Jeremiah concerning all the Jews dwelling in the land of Egypt and in the country of Pathros; the Most High reminded them of all the evil He had brought on Jerusalem and the cities of Judah, now a desolation where no man dwelt, because of the wickedness they committed provoking Him to anger.
- They went to burn incense and serve other gods that neither they nor their fathers knew, yet the Most High sent all His servants the prophets, rising early and sending them, pleading with them not to do this abominable thing that He hates.
- The same work goes on today; we fill up the street corners all over the earth bringing forth the glorious gospel of the one true living God and Christ, the prophets He raised up to give warning.

- They hearkened not nor inclined their ear to turn from their wickedness and burn no incense to other gods, so the Most High's fury and anger was poured forth and kindled in the cities of Judah and the streets of Jerusalem until they were wasted and desolate.
- He asks why they commit this great evil against their own souls, cutting off man and woman, child and suckling out of Judah, leaving none to remain.
- They provoke Him to wrath with the works of their hands, burning incense to other gods in the land of Egypt where they had gone to dwell, cutting themselves off to become a curse and a reproach among all the nations of the earth.

- The Most High asks whether they have forgotten the wickedness of their fathers, the kings of Judah, their wives, and their own wickedness committed in the land of Judah and the streets of Jerusalem; they were not humbled even to that day, nor did they fear or walk in His law and statutes.
- Therefore He will set His face against them for evil, to cut off all Judah, and take the remnant that set their faces to go into Egypt to sojourn there; they shall all be consumed and fall by the sword and the famine, from the least to the greatest.
- They shall become an execration, an astonishment, a curse, and a reproach; only a small number escaping the sword shall return, for none shall return to Judah though they have a desire to dwell there.

- All the men who knew their wives had burned incense to other gods, and all the women, a great multitude in Egypt and Pathros, answered Jeremiah defiantly.
- These men did not rule their houses well and had no authority over their households; their wives were in the midst of idolatry, the same disorder we see in this place spiritually called Egypt, in Babylon the great today.
- They said they would not hearken to the word spoken in the name of the Most High but would perform their vows to burn incense to the queen of heaven and pour out drink offerings, claiming they had plenty of victuals and saw no evil until they left off, and then wanted all things and were consumed by the sword and famine.
- The women declared they made their cakes to worship her and poured out drink offerings without their men; the times women gather together without their men, such as baby showers with cakes and drinks and partying, are burning incense to other gods to keep in mind.

- Jeremiah answered all the people, men and women, that the incense they burned in the cities of Judah and the streets of Jerusalem, they and their fathers, kings, and princes, the Most High remembered and it came into His mind.
- The Most High could no longer bear the evil of their doings and the abominations they committed, therefore their land became a desolation, an astonishment, and a curse without an inhabitant as at that day.
- This evil happened to them because they burned incense and sinned against the Most High and did not obey His voice nor walk in His law, statutes, and testimonies.

- Jeremiah told all the people and all the women that they and their wives had both spoken with their mouths and fulfilled with their hands their vows to burn incense to the queen of heaven, and would surely accomplish them.
- Therefore the Most High swore by His great name that His name shall no more be named in the mouth of any man of Judah in all Egypt, saying "The Lord GOD liveth"; He will watch over them for evil and not for good until they are consumed by the sword and famine.
- Only a small number escaping the sword shall return out of Egypt into Judah, and all the remnant that went to Egypt shall know whose words shall stand, His or theirs.

- The Most High gives a sign that He will punish them in that place so they may know His words shall surely stand against them for evil; He will give Pharaoh-hophra king of Egypt into the hand of his enemies as He gave Zedekiah king of Judah into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar.
- Evil does not come merely by chance; the Most High brings that evil upon us when we turn from Him and follow our own mind, sinning against the one true living God in Christ.

    If a trumpet is blown in the city the people are afraid, and there is no evil in the city that the Most High has not done, so we cannot attribute everything bad to the devil.

# Jeremiah 45: The Word of Comfort to Baruch the Scribe

- This is the word Jeremiah spoke to Baruch the son of Neriah when he had written these words in a book at the mouth of Jeremiah, in the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah king of Judah.
- The Most High addresses him directly, "unto thee O Baruch," and the words of the scribe Baruch are preserved in the Apocrypha, so get yourself a 1611 King James Version Bible with the Apocrypha.
- Baruch had said woe is me, for the Most High added grief to his sorrow and he fainted in his sighing and found no rest; the Most High answers that what He built He will break down and what He planted He will pluck up, even this whole land.
- Baruch is told not to seek great things for himself, for the Most High will bring evil upon all flesh, but his life He will give him for a prey in all places wherever he goes.

    ![[Baruch 2#^v31]]

    This is the understanding the christian church lacks; our forefathers learned through the prophets that in the land of our captivity we would remember ourselves, and one way we think upon His name is rehearsing His righteous acts at the Passover.

    ![[Hosea 5#^v15]]
    In our affliction we will seek Him early, so in the land of our captivity He gives us a heart and ears to hear.

- The word of the Most High came to Jeremiah against the gentiles, against Egypt and the army of Pharaoh-necho king of Egypt by the river Euphrates at Carchemish, which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon smote in the fourth year of Jehoiakim.
- The Egyptians are gentiles and heathens; they are called to order the buckler and shield, draw near to battle, harness the horses, furnish the spears, and put on the brigandines.
- Yet the Most High has seen them dismayed and turned back, their mighty ones beaten down and fleeing without looking back, for fear was round about; the swift shall not flee away nor the mighty man escape, stumbling and falling toward the north by the river Euphrates.

- Egypt rises up like a flood whose waters are moved like the rivers, boasting he will go up and cover the earth and destroy the city and its inhabitants, calling forth his horses, chariots, and mighty men, the Ethiopians, Libyans, and Lydians that handle the shield and bend the bow.
- But this is the day of the Lord GOD of hosts, a day of vengeance to avenge Him of His adversaries; the sword shall devour and be made drunk with their blood, for the Most High has a sacrifice in the north country by the river Euphrates.
- The virgin daughter of Egypt is told to go up into Gilead and take balm, but in vain shall she use many medicines, for she shall not be cured; the nations have heard of her shame and her cry filled the land as her mighty men stumbled and fell together.

- The Most High spoke to Jeremiah how Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon should come and smite the land of Egypt; the word is to be declared in Migdol, Noph, and Tahpanhes, calling them to stand fast and prepare, for the sword shall devour round about them.
- Their valiant men are swept away and stand not because the Most High drove them; they fall one upon another and say let us go again to our own people and the land of our nativity from the oppressing sword.
- Pharaoh king of Egypt is but a noise who has passed the time appointed; as surely as Tabor is among the mountains and Carmel by the sea, so shall the conqueror come, and the daughter dwelling in Egypt must furnish herself to go into captivity, for Noph shall be waste and desolate without inhabitant.

- Egypt is like a very fair heifer, but destruction comes out of the north; even her hired men in the midst of her are like fatted bullocks, turned back and fled away together, not standing because the day of their calamity and the time of their visitation was upon them.
- The voice shall go like a serpent as they march with an army and come against her with axes like hewers of wood to cut down her forest, though it cannot be searched, because they are more than the grasshoppers and are innumerable.
- The Most High will punish the multitude of No, Pharaoh, and Egypt with their gods and their kings, delivering them into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar and his servants; afterward it shall be inhabited as in the days of old.

- The Most High turns to comfort His own, telling Jacob His servant not to fear nor be dismayed, for He will save him from afar off and his seed from the land of their captivity, and Jacob shall return and be in rest and at ease with none to make him afraid.
- He will make a full end of all the nations where He has driven us, but He will not make a full end of us; He will correct us in measure and will not leave us wholly unpunished, so there is redemption for us but still a reminder of punishment because of our sins.

# Jeremiah 47: The Prophecy Against the Philistines

Read the chapter → [[Jeremiah 47]]

**[[Jeremiah 47#^v1|Jeremiah 47:1-7]]**

![[Jeremiah 47#^v1]]
![[Jeremiah 47#^v2]]
![[Jeremiah 47#^v3]]
![[Jeremiah 47#^v4]]
![[Jeremiah 47#^v5]]
![[Jeremiah 47#^v6]]
![[Jeremiah 47#^v7]]

- The word of the Most High came to Jeremiah against the Philistines before Pharaoh smote Gaza; waters rise up out of the north and become an overflowing flood that shall overflow the land, the city, and all that dwell therein.
- Men shall cry and all the inhabitants howl at the noise of the stamping hooves of the strong horses, the rushing of the chariots, and the rumbling of the wheels; the fathers shall not look back toward their children for feebleness of hands, because of the day that comes to spoil all the Philistines and cut off from Tyrus and Zidon every helper.
- The Most High will spoil the Philistines, the remnant of the country of Caphtor; baldness has come upon Gaza and Ashkelon is cut off with the remnant of their valley.
- He cries out how long the sword of the Most High will not be quiet, then tells it to put itself into its scabbard and rest, yet how can it be still when the Most High has given it a charge against Ashkelon and the seashore, where He has appointed it.
- As we go into chapters 48 and 49 we read more prophecies of the destruction of the other nations, for there has not been any nation that did not have a hand in spoiling us.
  - **[[Zechariah 2#^v8|Zechariah 2:8]]**
    ![[Zechariah 2#^v8]]
    He that touches us, Jacob the children of Israel, touches the apple of the Most High's eye.
