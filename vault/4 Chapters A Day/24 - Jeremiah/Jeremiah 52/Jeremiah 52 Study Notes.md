# Jeremiah 52: The Fall of Jerusalem and the Puppet King Zedekiah

Read the chapter → [[Jeremiah 52]]

This session covers Jeremiah chapter 52, which is essentially the same account as Second Kings 25, the fall of Jerusalem to Babylon. It is heavy history: the puppet king Zedekiah rebels, the city is besieged and starved, his sons are slain before his eyes and his own eyes are put out, the house of the Most High is burned, and the temple treasures are carried off in what amounts to a billion dollar heist. Every stroke of it is the generational curses of [[Deuteronomy 28|Deuteronomy 28]] and [[Leviticus 26|Leviticus 26]] coming to pass, warnings given hundreds of years before through Moses and repeated through Jeremiah, that a fierce nation from far would besiege us, starve us until we ate our own children, put our mighty men to death, and carry the remnant into captivity to serve strangers. This is the book we are the people of.

**[[Jeremiah 52#^v1|Jeremiah 52:1]]**

![[Jeremiah 52#^v1]]

- Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he began to reign and reigned eleven years in Jerusalem, and his mother was Hamutal the daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.
- Zedekiah was not of the royal lineage per se, and Zedekiah was not really his name; to see how he became king we go back to when Babylon took the real king away.
  - **[[2 Kings 24#^v15|2 Kings 24:15-17]]**
    ![[2 Kings 24#^v15]]
    ![[2 Kings 24#^v16]]
    ![[2 Kings 24#^v17]]
    Nebuchadnezzar carried the real king Jehoiachin to Babylon and made his father's brother Mattaniah king in his stead, changing his name to Zedekiah, a puppet king set up by the oppressor.

![[Jeremiah 52#^v3]]

- He did that which was evil in the eyes of the Most High as Jehoiakim had done, and through the anger of the Most High against Jerusalem and Judah, Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.
- The Most High had told through Jeremiah that the people would have to serve Babylon seventy years, but Zedekiah decided to rebel against it instead of submitting to the prophecy.
  - **[[Jeremiah 25#^v11|Jeremiah 25:11-12]]**
    ![[Jeremiah 25#^v11]]
    ![[Jeremiah 25#^v12]]
    The whole land would be a desolation and these nations would serve the king of Babylon seventy years, and only then would the Most High punish Babylon for their iniquity.

- In the ninth year of his reign Nebuchadnezzar and all his army came against Jerusalem, pitched against it, and built forts round about, and the city was besieged until the eleventh year of Zedekiah.
- By the fourth month the famine was so sore in the city that there was no bread for the people of the land.
- This should sound familiar; the same siege and famine came on us again under the Romans in 70 A.D. These are generational curses that fall on multiple generations when we break the Most High's commandments.

- They brought Zedekiah to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where he gave judgment upon him, and the king of Babylon slew Zedekiah's sons before his eyes and slew all the princes of Judah.
- Then they put out Zedekiah's eyes, so the last thing he would ever see was his sons put to death in his face, and they bound him in chains, carried him to Babylon, and kept him in prison till the day of his death; the Most High allowed these heathen to bring hell into Jerusalem.

- In the fifth month Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard came to Jerusalem and burned the house of the Most High, the king's house, and all the houses of the great men with fire.
- The Chaldeans birthed this destruction, but Edom, the so-called white man, took part in it; Esau loves burning things, which is ironic since he is destined to burn forever, wasted away by the same fire he loved to waste these nations with.
- Edom were Babylon's personal cheerleaders, calling for Jerusalem and the temple to be razed to the foundation; the daughter of Babylon that Edom cheered on became the mother of harlots, Babylon the great, which is to be destroyed.

    ![[1 Esdras 4#^v45]]

- The Chaldeans broke the pillars of brass, the bases, and the brazen sea in the house of the Most High and carried all the brass to Babylon, along with the caldrons, shovels, snuffers, bowls, spoons, and every vessel of brass, gold, and silver.

- The captain of the guard took Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the second priest, the three keepers of the door, the eunuch over the men of war, seven of the king's counselors, the principal scribe, and threescore men of the people found in the city.
- He brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where the king smote them and put them to death, and thus Judah was carried away captive out of his own land.
- Babylon made sure to put our mighty men to death, the same tactic the oppressor always used in slavery and segregation, killing the mightiest men to destroy the people's hope so they would no longer rise up against the enemy.
