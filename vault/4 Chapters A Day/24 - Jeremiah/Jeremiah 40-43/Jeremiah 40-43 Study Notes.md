# Jeremiah 40: Jeremiah Set Free and Gedaliah Made Governor

Read the chapter → [[Jeremiah 40]]

This session covers Jeremiah chapters 40 through 43, the aftermath of Jerusalem's fall to Babylon. The Most High's prophet Jeremiah is loosed from his chains, Gedaliah is set over the poor left in the land, and the same lesson runs through every chapter: the heathen nations themselves knew Israel was carried into captivity because of sin, breaking the Most High's laws. That is how His people are overcome. When the remnant asks whether to flee to Egypt, the Most High tells them plainly to stay in the land and He will build them, but they dissemble in their hearts, refuse the counsel, and run to Egypt to their own destruction. This is the book we are the people of, and these are the same people we are today.

![[Jeremiah 40#^v1]]

- The word came to Jeremiah after Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard let him go from Ramah, where he had been bound in chains among all the captives of Jerusalem and Judah being carried away to Babylon; Jeremiah was let go when Jerusalem was seized.
- The captain, a heathen, told Jeremiah that the Most High had pronounced this evil upon the place because the people sinned against Him and would not obey His voice, so this thing had come upon them.
- The nations always knew why we went into captivity; it is sin, the breaking of the Most High's laws, that becomes our weakness and how we are overcome.

    ![[Judith 5#^v17]]

- The captain loosed Jeremiah from the chains on his hands and gave him an ultimatum, either to come with him to Babylon where he would be looked after well, or to remain in the land as he pleased.
- He directed Jeremiah to Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, whom the king of Babylon had made governor over the cities of Judah, gave him food and a reward, and let him go; Gedaliah was one of Jeremiah's good friends.
- Jeremiah went to Gedaliah at Mizpah and dwelt with him among the people left in the land.

- Gedaliah swore to them not to fear serving the Chaldeans but to dwell in the land, gather wine and summer fruits and oil, and serve the king of Babylon, and it would be well with them; the war was over and they were already conquered.
- All the Jews scattered in Moab, Ammon, Edom, and the other countries heard of the remnant left under Gedaliah and returned to the land of Judah, gathering wine and summer fruits in abundance.

- Johanan spoke secretly to Gedaliah, offering to kill Ishmael himself so that Gedaliah would not be slain and the gathered remnant scattered.
- Gedaliah forbade Johanan to do it, saying he spoke falsely of Ishmael, and refused to believe the plot.

# Jeremiah 41: Ishmael Murders Gedaliah and Johanan Recovers the Remnant

Read the chapter → [[Jeremiah 41]]

**[[Jeremiah 41#^v1|Jeremiah 41:1-3]]**

![[Jeremiah 41#^v1]]
![[Jeremiah 41#^v2]]
![[Jeremiah 41#^v3]]

- In the seventh month Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, of the royal seed and a prince of the king, came with ten men to Gedaliah at Mizpah, and they ate bread together at a banquet held in Ishmael's honor.
- Ishmael and his ten men rose up and smote Gedaliah the son of Ahikam with the sword and slew him, the governor the king of Babylon had made over the land.
- Ishmael also slew all the Jews with Gedaliah at Mizpah and the Chaldeans and men of war that were found there.

- The second day after the murder, before anyone knew of it, eighty men came from Shechem, Shiloh, and Samaria with their beards shaven, their clothes rent, and their bodies cut, bringing offerings and incense to the house of the Most High.
- Ishmael went out from Mizpah weeping as he met them, playing a part, and invited them in to Gedaliah; when they came into the midst of the city he and his men slew them and cast them into a pit.
- Ten of the eighty were spared when they told Ishmael they had hidden treasures of wheat, barley, oil, and honey in the field.
- The pit Ishmael filled with the slain was the one Asa the king had made for fear of Baasha king of Israel during the war between them.

- All the people Ishmael had taken captive were glad to see Johanan, turned about, and returned to him, but Ishmael escaped from Johanan with eight men and went to the Ammonites.

- They set out to seek refuge in Egypt because they were afraid of the Chaldeans, since Ishmael had slain Gedaliah whom the king of Babylon had made governor; with Gedaliah dead they felt they had no reason to stay.

# Jeremiah 42: The Remnant Seeks Counsel and the Most High Says Stay

**[[Jeremiah 42#^v1|Jeremiah 42:1-6]]**

![[Jeremiah 42#^v1]]

- All the captains, Johanan, Jezaniah, and all the people from the least to the greatest came to Jeremiah the prophet before going into Egypt and begged him to pray to the Most High for them, that they might know the way to walk and what to do.
- Jeremiah agreed to pray and promised to declare everything the Most High answered, keeping nothing back.
- They swore the Most High would be a true and faithful witness against them if they did not obey His voice, whether good or evil, so that it might be well with them.

![[Jeremiah 42#^v7]]

- After ten days the word of the Most High came to Jeremiah, and he called Johanan and all the people to hear the answer.
- The Most High told them that if they would still abide in the land He would build them and not pull them down, plant them and not pluck them up, for He repented of the evil He had done to them; He does not desire evil against His people, He only wants His laws kept.
- He told them not to be afraid of the king of Babylon of whom they were afraid, for He was with them to save them, deliver them from his hand, and show them mercies so they could return to their own land.

- If instead they refused to dwell in the land and said they would go into Egypt to see no war, hear no trumpet, and hunger for no bread, then the sword they feared would overtake them there and the famine would follow close after them, and there they would die.
- All the men who set their faces to go into Egypt would die by the sword, the famine, and the pestilence, and none would remain or escape the evil He would bring; the Most High set before them life or death and told them to choose life.
- As His anger was poured out on the inhabitants of Jerusalem, so His fury would be poured out on them when they entered Egypt, and they would become an execration, an astonishment, a curse, and a reproach, and see the land no more.

- The Most High plainly told the remnant of Judah to go not into Egypt, and Jeremiah warned them He had admonished them that day.
- They had dissembled in their hearts when they sent him to pray, giving a false appearance and concealing that they did not truly mean to obey; some people speak words they do not believe.
- Because they had not obeyed His voice, they would certainly die by the sword, the famine, and the pestilence in the very place they desired to go; we are these same people, quick to say we will not do what the Most High commands, though now that we understand who we are in these last days many of us want to keep His laws.

# Jeremiah 43: The Proud Men Reject the Word and Flee to Egypt

Read the chapter → [[Jeremiah 43]]

**[[Jeremiah 43#^v1|Jeremiah 43:1-4]]**

![[Jeremiah 43#^v1]]
![[Jeremiah 43#^v2]]
![[Jeremiah 43#^v3]]
![[Jeremiah 43#^v4]]

- When Jeremiah finished speaking all the words of the Most High, Azariah the son of Hoshaiah, Johanan the son of Kareah, and all the proud men told him he spoke falsely and called him a lying prophet; pride is destruction according to the scriptures.
- They accused Baruch the son of Neriah of setting Jeremiah against them to deliver them into the hand of the Chaldeans, to be put to death or carried captive to Babylon.
- Many people ask for counsel while already having their own minds made up; when the counsel is not what they want to hear they go looking for someone who will tell them what they want, and so these men obeyed not the voice of the Most High to dwell in Judah.

**[[Jeremiah 43#^v5|Jeremiah 43:5-7]]**

![[Jeremiah 43#^v5]]
![[Jeremiah 43#^v6]]
![[Jeremiah 43#^v7]]

- Johanan and all the captains took the whole remnant of Judah, the men, women, children, and the king's daughters that Nebuzaradan had left with Gedaliah, along with Jeremiah the prophet and Baruch.
- They came into the land of Egypt as far as Tahpanhes because they obeyed not the voice of the Most High.

**[[Jeremiah 43#^v8|Jeremiah 43:8-13]]**

![[Jeremiah 43#^v8]]
![[Jeremiah 43#^v9]]
![[Jeremiah 43#^v10]]
![[Jeremiah 43#^v11]]
![[Jeremiah 43#^v12]]
![[Jeremiah 43#^v13]]

- The word of the Most High came to Jeremiah in Tahpanhes to take great stones and hide them in the clay of the brick kiln at the entry of Pharaoh's house, in the sight of the men of Judah.
- The Most High declared He would send for Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, His servant, set his throne upon those hidden stones, spread his royal pavilion over them, and smite the land of Egypt.
- When Nebuchadnezzar came he would deliver such as were for death to death, such as were for captivity to captivity, and such as were for the sword to the sword; in war each person already has an appointed end that the Most High has set.
- He would kindle a fire in the houses of the gods of Egypt, array himself with the land of Egypt as a shepherd puts on his garment so that none escape, break the images of Bethshemesh, and burn the houses of the gods of the Egyptians with fire.
