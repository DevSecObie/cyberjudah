# Jeremiah 20: The Prophet's Fire Shut Up in His Bones

Read the chapter → [[Jeremiah 20]]

This session covers Jeremiah chapters 20 through 23. Jeremiah is persecuted for bringing forth the prophecies the Most High gave him concerning the sins of Israel and the punishment coming from Babylon. He grows so weary of the mocking and the stocks that he resolves to stop speaking, but the word burns in him like a fire he cannot hold. From there the reading moves through the way of life and the way of death set before Zedekiah, the woe pronounced on the unrighteous kings of Judah down to the curse on Coniah, and finally the promise of the Righteous Branch set against the lying prophets who speak visions out of their own hearts.

- Pashur the priest, chief governor in the house of the Most High, hears Jeremiah prophesy, smites him, and puts him in the stocks in the high gate of Benjamin.
- When he is let out the next day, Jeremiah tells him the Most High has renamed him Magor-missabib, a terror to himself and all his friends, for they shall fall by the sword of the king of Babylon.
- All Judah, all the strength and treasures of the city, and Pashur's whole household will be carried captive to Babylon, and Pashur will die there and be buried there with all the friends to whom he prophesied lies.

**[[Jeremiah 20#^v7|Jeremiah 20:7-9]]**

![[Jeremiah 20#^v7]]
![[Jeremiah 20#^v8]]
![[Jeremiah 20#^v9]]

- Jeremiah is going through it so bad, mocked and reproached daily, that he says he will not make mention of the Most High nor speak any more in His name.
- Just like us when we go out to camp, if somebody does not speak evil of us every time we do something big, there is a hater or somebody trying to demonize us.
- But the word was in his heart like a burning fire shut up in his bones; he was weary with holding it in and could not stay, he had to blast them, he could not hold it.

![[Jeremiah 20#^v10]]

![[Jeremiah 20#^v12]]
![[Jeremiah 20#^v13]]

- Jeremiah hears the defaming of many and his familiar friends watching for his stumbling, hoping to be enticed against him and take their revenge.
- The Most High is with him as a mighty terrible one, so his persecutors will stumble, not prevail, and be greatly ashamed in an everlasting confusion that will never be forgotten.
- He commits his cause to the Most High who tries the righteous and sees the reins and the heart, and sings praise because He delivers the soul of the poor from the hand of evildoers.

- Crushed by the persecution, Jeremiah curses the day he was born and the man who brought his father the news of a man child, wishing that man to be like the cities the Most High overthrew.
- He asks why he came forth out of the womb only to see labor and sorrow and to have his days consumed with shame.

# Jeremiah 21: The Way of Life and the Way of Death

Read the chapter → [[Jeremiah 21]]

**[[Jeremiah 21#^v1|Jeremiah 21:1-7]]**

![[Jeremiah 21#^v1]]
![[Jeremiah 21#^v2]]
![[Jeremiah 21#^v3]]
![[Jeremiah 21#^v4]]
![[Jeremiah 21#^v5]]
![[Jeremiah 21#^v6]]
![[Jeremiah 21#^v7]]

- King Zedekiah sends Pashur and Zephaniah the priest to ask Jeremiah to inquire of the Most High, hoping He will deal with them according to His wondrous works while Nebuchadnezzar makes war on them.
- The answer is that the Most High will turn back their own weapons and Himself fight against them with an outstretched hand, in anger, fury, and great wrath, smiting man and beast with a great pestilence.
- Afterward He will deliver Zedekiah, his servants, and the people into Nebuchadnezzar's hand, to be smitten with the sword without pity or mercy.

**[[Jeremiah 21#^v8|Jeremiah 21:8-10]]**

![[Jeremiah 21#^v8]]
![[Jeremiah 21#^v9]]
![[Jeremiah 21#^v10]]

- The Most High sets before the people the way of life and the way of death: he that stays in the city dies by the sword, famine, and pestilence, but he that goes out and falls to the Chaldeans shall live.
- His face is set against this city for evil and not for good, and it shall be given into the king of Babylon's hand to be burned with fire.

**[[Jeremiah 21#^v11|Jeremiah 21:11-14]]**

![[Jeremiah 21#^v11]]
![[Jeremiah 21#^v12]]
![[Jeremiah 21#^v13]]
![[Jeremiah 21#^v14]]

- To the house of David the Most High commands they execute judgment in the morning and deliver the spoiled out of the oppressor's hand, lest His fury go out like an unquenchable fire.
- He stands against the inhabitant of the valley and rock of the plain who think none can come down against them, and will punish them according to the fruit of their doings.

- Jeremiah is sent down to the king of Judah's house to command the king to execute judgment and righteousness, deliver the oppressed, and do no violence to the stranger, the fatherless, or the widow.
- If they obey, kings will keep entering the gates riding in chariots and on horses, but if they will not hear, the Most High swears by Himself that this house shall become a desolation.
- Many nations will pass by and ask why He did this to so great a city, and the answer is that they forsook His covenant and worshipped other gods.

- The people are told not to weep for the dead but to weep sore for him that goes away, Shallum the son of Josiah who reigned in his father's place.
- Shallum went forth out of his place and shall not return, but shall die in the land where they led him captive and see his native country no more.

- His father ate and drank yet did judgment and justice for the poor and needy, and it was well with him, but Jehoiakim's eyes and heart are set only on covetousness, innocent blood, oppression, and violence.
- Therefore no one will lament him; he shall be buried with the burial of an ass, drawn and cast forth beyond the gates of Jerusalem.

# Jeremiah 23: The Righteous Branch and the Lying Prophets

Read the chapter → [[Jeremiah 23]]

**[[Jeremiah 23#^v1|Jeremiah 23:1-4]]**

![[Jeremiah 23#^v1]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v2]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v3]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v4]]

- Woe is pronounced on the pastors that destroy and scatter the sheep of the Most High's pasture and have not visited them, so He will visit upon them the evil of their doings.
- He will gather the remnant of His flock out of the countries where He drove them, bring them again to their folds, and set up shepherds who will feed them so they fear no more and none are lacking.

**[[Jeremiah 23#^v5|Jeremiah 23:5-8]]**

![[Jeremiah 23#^v5]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v6]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v7]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v8]]

- The Most High promises to raise unto David a righteous Branch, a King who shall reign, prosper, and execute judgment and justice, in whose days Judah shall be saved and Israel dwell safely.
- His name is called THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS; this is plainly referring to Christ.
- The day comes when they will no longer swear by the Most High who brought Israel out of Egypt, but by Him who led the seed of the house of Israel out of the north country and all the countries where He had driven them, back to their own land.

**[[Jeremiah 23#^v9|Jeremiah 23:9-15]]**

![[Jeremiah 23#^v9]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v10]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v11]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v12]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v13]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v14]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v15]]

- Jeremiah's heart is broken and his bones shake like a drunken man because of the prophets and the words of the Most High's holiness, for the land is full of adulterers and mourns under swearing.
- Both prophet and priest are profane, and the Most High has found their wickedness even in His house, so their way shall be as slippery ways in the darkness where they are driven and fall.
- The prophets of Samaria prophesied in Baal and caused Israel to err, and the prophets of Jerusalem commit adultery, walk in lies, and strengthen the hands of evildoers so that none return from wickedness, becoming as Sodom and Gomorrah before Him.

**[[Jeremiah 23#^v16|Jeremiah 23:16-22]]**

![[Jeremiah 23#^v16]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v17]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v18]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v19]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v20]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v21]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v22]]

- The people are told not to hearken to the prophets who make them vain and speak a vision of their own heart, not out of the mouth of the Most High, telling those who despise Him that they shall have peace and no evil shall come.
- This is like the pastor who says, let me tell you what God told me to tell you, when it is a lie.
- None of these prophets has stood in the counsel of the Most High; had they stood there and caused the people to hear His words, they would have turned them from their evil way, but instead the whirlwind of His anger will fall grievously upon the head of the wicked in the latter days.

- The Most High asks whether He is a God only near and not far off, when He fills heaven and earth and no one can hide in secret places where He cannot see him.
- He has heard the prophets who prophesy lies in His name saying, I have dreamed, thinking to cause His people to forget His name by their dreams as their fathers forgot it for Baal.
- Let the prophet with a dream tell a dream, but let him that has His word speak it faithfully, for His word is like a fire and like a hammer that breaks the rock in pieces; He is against those who steal His words and use their own tongues to lie on the Most High.

**[[Jeremiah 23#^v33|Jeremiah 23:33-40]]**

![[Jeremiah 23#^v33]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v34]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v35]]
![[Jeremiah 23#^v36]]

- When the people, prophet, or priest ask what is the burden of the Most High, Jeremiah is to answer, What burden? for He will forsake them, and every man who claims the burden of the Most High He will punish in his house.
- Instead of saying the burden of the Most High, they are to ask one another what the Most High has answered and spoken, for they have perverted the words of the living God.
- Because they keep saying the burden of the Most High after being told not to, He will utterly forget them, forsake them, and cast them out of His presence with an everlasting reproach and perpetual shame that shall not be forgotten.
