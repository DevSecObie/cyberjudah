# 1 Kings 13: The Man of God and the Two Prophets

Read the chapter → [[1 Kings 13]]

This session picks up from the reading of Solomon's decline. Against the counsel of his father David, of Moses, and of the prophets, Solomon entangled himself with foreign nations and their women and turned his heart to serve their gods. Because of this the kingdom was rent: ten tribes followed Jeroboam, while Judah and Benjamin stayed with Rehoboam. Chapters 13 through 16 trace the pattern of kings who inherit their fathers' sins, the judgments the Most High pronounces through His prophets, and the slow but certain fulfillment of His word. The chapters open at Bethel, where Jeroboam still burns incense on the altar he devised in his own heart.

- A man of God comes out of Judah by the word of the Most High and cries against Jeroboam's altar in Bethel while the king stands by it burning incense.
- He prophesies that a child named Josiah, of the house of David, will one day burn the false priests of the high places on that very altar and burn men's bones upon it.
- He gives a sign: the altar will be rent and its ashes poured out. When Jeroboam stretches out his hand to have him seized, that hand dries up and cannot be pulled back, and the altar is rent as the sign declared.

- Jeroboam begs the man of God to entreat the Most High so his hand can be restored, and it is healed and becomes as before.
- The king offers reward and a meal, but the man of God refuses even half the house, because the Most High charged him to eat no bread, drink no water, and return by another way.
- He obeys and goes home by a different route than he came, honoring the exact terms of his commission out of Judah.

- An old prophet in Bethel hears from his sons all that the man of God did, and rides after him, finding him resting under an oak.
- The old prophet invites him home to eat, and the man of God repeats the charge he received, that he may not eat, drink, or return the same way.
- The old prophet claims an angel spoke to him by the word of the Most High telling him to bring the man back, but the scripture plainly states he lied. The man of God, giving reverence to the older prophet, goes back and eats.
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 4#^v22|Ecclesiasticus 4:22]]**  Accept no person against thy soul, and let not the reverence of any man cause thee to fall.

- After he departs, a lion meets him on the way and slays him, and his carcass is cast in the road with the ass and the lion standing by it, neither eaten nor torn.
- The Most High used the deceiver to prove whether the man would keep the charge, and disobedience under that dispensation carried the penalty of death.

- This thing becomes sin to the house of Jeroboam, enough to cut it off and destroy it from the face of the earth.

# 1 Kings 14: Judgment on Jeroboam and the Reign of Rehoboam

Read the chapter → [[1 Kings 14]]

**[[1 Kings 14#^v1|1 Kings 14:1-6]]**

- When Jeroboam's son Abijah falls sick, the king tells his wife to disguise herself and go to Ahijah the prophet in Shiloh, carrying loaves, cakes, and honey to inquire about the child.
- Ahijah is old and blind, but the Most High tells him beforehand that Jeroboam's wife is coming feigning to be another woman.
- The moment he hears her feet at the door, Ahijah calls her by name and says he is sent to her with heavy tidings.

- The message declares that though the Most High exalted Jeroboam from among the people and rent the kingdom from the house of David to give it to him, he did not walk as David who kept the commandments.
- Instead he made other gods and molten images and cast the Most High behind his back, doing evil above all before him.
- Therefore the Most High will bring evil on his house, cut off every male, and sweep away the remnant as a man sweeps away dung until it is gone.
  - **[[1 Kings 14#^v9|1 Kings 14:9]]**
    ![[1 Kings 14#^v9]]

- The Most High will raise up a king to cut off the house of Jeroboam and will scatter Israel beyond the river because they made their groves and provoked Him to anger.
- As soon as she reaches the threshold of her house, the child dies, exactly as spoken by the hand of Ahijah, and all Israel mourns.

- In Judah, Rehoboam, forty-one at his accession, reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, and Judah did evil and provoked the Most High to jealousy above all their fathers.
- They built high places, images, and groves on every hill, and there were also sodomites in the land, doing according to the abominations of the nations cast out before them.

- In Rehoboam's fifth year, Shishak king of Egypt comes against Jerusalem and takes the treasures of the house of the Most High and the king's house, including the gold shields Solomon made.
- Rehoboam replaces the gold shields with brass, marking Israel's fall from gold to brass, from the riches of Solomon's day to loss.
- There is continual war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam, and when Rehoboam dies, Abijam his son reigns in his place.

# 1 Kings 15: Abijam, Asa, Nadab, and Baasha

Read the chapter → [[1 Kings 15]]

**[[1 Kings 15#^v1|1 Kings 15:1-5]]**
![[1 Kings 15#^v1]]
![[1 Kings 15#^v2]]
![[1 Kings 15#^v3]]
![[1 Kings 15#^v4]]

- Abijam reigns three years in Jerusalem and walks in all the sins of his father, his heart not perfect with the Most High as David's was.
- The children did what they saw their fathers do, not merely what was said, which is why the Most High teaches His people to be different.
- Yet for David's sake the Most High gives him a lamp in Jerusalem, because David did right all his days save only in the matter of Uriah the Hittite, a wrong from which David repented.

- Asa takes the sodomites out of the land, removes the idols his fathers made, and even removes his own mother Maachah from being queen because she made an idol in a grove, which he destroys and burns at the brook Kidron.
- Though the high places are not removed, Asa's heart is perfect with the Most High all his days, and he brings dedicated silver, gold, and vessels into the house of the Most High.

- Asa uses the stones and timber of Ramah to build Geba and Mizpah. In his old age he is diseased in his feet, then sleeps with his fathers, and Jehoshaphat his son reigns in his place.

# 1 Kings 16: Baasha to Ahab and the Rise of Baal Worship

- The word of the Most High comes to Jehu son of Hanani against Baasha, because though he was exalted out of the dust and made prince over Israel, he walked in the way of Jeroboam and made Israel sin.
- Therefore the Most High will make his house like the house of Jeroboam, and those who die in the city the dogs will eat and those in the field the fowls will eat.
- The same judgment falls because the same sin is repeated, just as descendants keep the pattern of their fathers though they saw the ruin it brought.

- Elah son of Baasha reigns two years, and his servant Zimri, captain of half his chariots, conspires against him while he is drunk in a steward's house and kills him.
- Zimri, on taking the throne, slays all the house of Baasha, leaving not one male, according to the word spoken by Jehu against Baasha.
- All of it comes for the sins of Baasha and Elah, who provoked the Most High to anger with their vanities.

- Zimri reigns only seven days in Tirzah. When the people encamped against Gibbethon hear he has slain the king, they make Omri, captain of the host, king over Israel.
- Omri and Israel go up and besiege Tirzah, and when Zimri sees the city taken, he burns the king's house over himself and dies.
- His death comes for his sins in walking in the way of Jeroboam, and his treason is recorded, treason being punishable by death in that time.

- Israel is divided: half follow Tibni son of Ginath and half follow Omri, but Omri's followers prevail, Tibni dies, and Omri reigns.
- Omri buys the hill Samaria from Shemer for two talents of silver, builds the city, and names it after Shemer.
- Omri does worse than all before him, walking in the way of Jeroboam, then sleeps with his fathers, and Ahab his son reigns in his place.

**[[1 Kings 16#^v29|1 Kings 16:29-34]]**
![[1 Kings 16#^v29]]

- Ahab son of Omri reigns twenty-two years in Samaria and does evil above all that were before him.
- As if walking in Jeroboam's sins were a light thing, he takes Jezebel daughter of Ethbaal king of the Zidonians to wife and serves and worships Baal, rearing an altar and a grove, provoking the Most High more than all the kings before him. This mirrors Solomon, who followed Ashtoreth the goddess of the Zidonians, and the marrying outside the people that the law forbids because it turns the heart to other gods.
- In his days Hiel of Bethel rebuilds Jericho, laying its foundation in his firstborn Abiram and setting up its gates in his youngest Segub, according to the word spoken by Joshua.
  - **[[1 Kings 11#^v4|1 Kings 11:4-5]]**
    ![[1 Kings 11#^v4]]

    Solomon's wives turned his heart after Ashtoreth the goddess of the Zidonians.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 7#^v3|Deuteronomy 7:3]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 7#^v3]]
    Israel is not to marry outside the people, lest their hearts be turned to other gods.
  - **[[Joshua 6#^v26|Joshua 6:26]]**
    ![[Joshua 6#^v26]]
    Joshua's curse: whoever rebuilds Jericho lays its foundation in his firstborn and sets its gates in his youngest son.
