# 1 Kings 5: Solomon Makes a League with Hiram to Build the House

Read the chapter → [[1 Kings 5]]

This session moves through 1 Kings five through eight, the preparation and building of the temple of the Most High. Chapter five shows Solomon reaching out to Hiram king of Tyre for cedar out of Lebanon, a league made possible because David his father had already made a name and a friendship there. Chapter six lays out the building of the house itself, its dimensions, chambers, and gold overlay. Chapter seven turns to Solomon's own house and the brass work of the temple, the pillars, the molten sea, and the bases. Chapter eight brings the ark into the most holy place and records Solomon's long prayer of dedication before the whole congregation of Israel. The teaching stays close to the reading and draws in Ecclesiasticus, Chronicles, Leviticus, Hebrews, and Zechariah to open up what is being built and why.

**[[1 Kings 5#^v1|1 Kings 5:1]]**
![[1 Kings 5#^v1]]
- Hiram king of Tyre sends his servants to Solomon because he had heard that Solomon was anointed king in the room of his father. Hiram was ever a lover of David, so the friendship carries over to the son.
- Hiram was king of Tyre, a Zidonian by his father and connected by lineage to the tribe of Naphtali on his mother's side, which is drawn out later in the reading.

- Solomon asks that cedar trees be hewn out of Lebanon, for the Zidonians had a gift of skill in timber above any other nation, and the best cedar came from Lebanon. Lebanon lay to the northwest on the coast, where the best trees grew, and Solomon knew his own people had none who could hew timber like the Zidonians.

# 1 Kings 6: Building the House of the Lord

Read the chapter → [[1 Kings 6]]

- In the four hundred and eightieth year after Israel came out of Egypt, in the fourth year of Solomon's reign, in the month Zif, he begins to build the house of the Lord. That span reaches back through the time of the judges and the reign of Saul and David, a long stretch in which Israel had no settled kingly rule until this point.
- The house is threescore cubits long, twenty broad, and thirty high, with a porch of twenty cubits before it, and windows of narrow lights, the kind of angled skylights that bring light in from above.

- The house was built of stone made ready before it was brought, so that no hammer nor axe nor any tool of iron was heard while it was in building. Everything was hewn and fitted beforehand and set together on site, and the whole was covered with beams and boards of cedar.

The inside was pure gold everywhere, fit for the King, though Solomon himself confessed that the heaven of heavens cannot contain the Most High, yet this is what was made for him to dwell in while on the earth.

- The priests and Levites bring up the ark, the tabernacle of the congregation, and all the holy vessels, while Solomon and all the congregation sacrifice sheep and oxen that could not be numbered for multitude.

- The king and all Israel offer sacrifices before the Lord, Solomon offering a peace offering of twenty-two thousand oxen and a hundred and twenty thousand sheep, so the king and all the people dedicate the house of the Lord. He hallows the middle of the court to offer there because the brazen altar was too little to receive the multitude of offerings.
- Solomon holds a feast, and all Israel with him from the entering in of Hamath to the river of Egypt, before the Lord seven days and seven days, even fourteen days. On the eighth day he sends the people away, and they bless the king and go to their tents joyful and glad of heart for all the goodness the Lord had done for David and for Israel his people.
