# 1 Kings 21: Naboth's Vineyard and the Judgment on Ahab

Read the chapter → [[1 Kings 21]]

## Introduction

This session moves through the account of Naboth's vineyard. King Ahab covets a vineyard he cannot lawfully take, his wife Jezebel plots murder to seize it, and the Most High sends Elijah to pronounce judgment on the house of Ahab. The chapter shows covetousness turning into extortion and false witness, and it shows that even a righteous judgment can be delayed when a wicked man humbles himself.

**[[1 Kings 21#^v1|1 Kings 21:1-3]]**
![[1 Kings 21#^v1]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v2]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v3]]
- Naboth had a vineyard next to Ahab's palace in Jezreel, and Ahab asked to trade or buy it, offering a better vineyard or its worth in money.
- Naboth refused because the land was the inheritance of his fathers. He could not simply hand off an inheritance that was given to him.

  - **[[Leviticus 25#^v23|Leviticus 25:23]]**
    ![[Leviticus 25#^v23]]
    The land was not to be sold forever, for it belonged to the Most High and was held as a family inheritance.

**[[1 Kings 21#^v4|1 Kings 21:4]]**
![[1 Kings 21#^v4]]
- Ahab went home heavy and displeased, laid down on his bed, turned away his face, and would eat no bread.
- He threw a fit and sulked because he could not get his way, angry that a subject would not surrender his inheritance.

**[[1 Kings 21#^v5|1 Kings 21:5-7]]**
![[1 Kings 21#^v5]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v6]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v7]]
- Jezebel came to Ahab and asked why his spirit was so sad that he would not eat, and he told her Naboth's refusal.
- Jezebel mocked his weakness, telling him he was king and should let his heart be merry, and she promised to get the vineyard of Naboth for him.

**[[1 Kings 21#^v8|1 Kings 21:8-10]]**
![[1 Kings 21#^v8]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v9]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v10]]
- Jezebel wrote letters in Ahab's name and sealed them with his seal, sending them to the elders and nobles of Naboth's city.
- The seal was a signet, an official mark of the king's authority, so the letters carried royal weight as if Ahab himself had written them.
- She commanded them to proclaim a fast, set Naboth on high, and set two sons of Belial before him to bear false witness that he blasphemed God and the king, then stone him. This was evil counsel and a murderous plot.

  - **[[Daniel 6#^v17|Daniel 6:17]]**
    ![[Daniel 6#^v17]]
    A king sealed the den with his own signet so the decree could not be changed, showing the binding authority of a royal seal.
  - **[[Exodus 20#^v16|Exodus 20:16]]**
    ![[Exodus 20#^v16]]
    The commandment forbids bearing false witness, which is exactly what the sons of Belial were hired to do.

**[[1 Kings 21#^v11|1 Kings 21:11-14]]**
![[1 Kings 21#^v11]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v12]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v13]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v14]]
- The elders and nobles obeyed Jezebel's letters, proclaimed a fast, and set Naboth on high among the people.
- Two children of Belial, children of the devil, sat before Naboth and falsely witnessed that he had blasphemed God and the king.
- They carried Naboth out and stoned him to death, then sent word to Jezebel that he was dead.

**[[1 Kings 21#^v15|1 Kings 21:15-16]]**
![[1 Kings 21#^v15]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v16]]
- When Jezebel heard Naboth was dead, she told Ahab to arise and take possession of the vineyard he had been refused.
- Ahab rose up and went down to take the vineyard. This is extortion, seizing what another has by violence and force.
- Ahab shares the guilt. He sat by and let Jezebel commit the crime because he coveted the field, then turned his face from the evil and went to claim the reward.

  - **[[Ezekiel 22#^v12|Ezekiel 22:12-13]]**
    ![[Ezekiel 22#^v12]]
    ![[Ezekiel 22#^v13]]
    The Most High condemns those who greedily gain of their neighbors by extortion and shed blood, smiting his hand against dishonest gain.

**[[1 Kings 21#^v17|1 Kings 21:17-19]]**
![[1 Kings 21#^v17]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v18]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v19]]
- The word of the Lord came to Elijah the Tishbite to go down and meet Ahab in the vineyard of Naboth.
- Elijah was to declare, Thus saith the Lord, hast thou killed and also taken possession, and in the place where dogs licked the blood of Naboth, dogs would lick Ahab's own blood.

**[[1 Kings 21#^v20|1 Kings 21:20-22]]**
![[1 Kings 21#^v20]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v21]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v22]]
- Ahab called Elijah his enemy, and Elijah answered that he had found him because Ahab had sold himself to work evil in the sight of the Lord.
- The Most High would bring evil on Ahab, cut off every male of his house, and make his house like the house of Jeroboam and the house of Baasha because of the provocation by which he made Israel to sin.
- The phrase him that pisseth against the wall refers to the males, whom the Most High said he would cut off entirely.

  - **[[1 Kings 14#^v10|1 Kings 14:10]]**
    ![[1 Kings 14#^v10]]
    The same judgment was spoken over Jeroboam, cutting off every male and taking away the remnant of his house like dung.
  - **[[1 Kings 16#^v11|1 Kings 16:11]]**
    ![[1 Kings 16#^v11]]
    When Zimri reigned he slew all the house of Baasha, leaving not one male among his kinsfolk or friends.

**[[1 Kings 21#^v23|1 Kings 21:23-24]]**
![[1 Kings 21#^v23]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v24]]
- The Most High declared that the dogs would eat Jezebel by the wall of Jezreel.
- Any of Ahab's house dying in the city the dogs would eat, and any dying in the field the fowls of the air would eat, matching the curses spoken in the law.

  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v26|Deuteronomy 28:26]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v26]]
    A curse of disobedience is that the carcass becomes meat for the fowls of the air and the beasts of the earth, with none to fray them away.

**[[1 Kings 21#^v25|1 Kings 21:25-26]]**
![[1 Kings 21#^v25]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v26]]
- There was none like Ahab, who sold himself to work wickedness in the sight of the Lord, stirred up by Jezebel his wife.
- He did very abominably in following idols, as did the Amorites whom the Most High cast out before the children of Israel.

**[[1 Kings 21#^v27|1 Kings 21:27-29]]**
![[1 Kings 21#^v27]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v28]]
![[1 Kings 21#^v29]]
- When Ahab heard these words he rent his clothes, put on sackcloth, fasted, and went softly, humbling himself before the Most High.
- Because Ahab humbled himself, the Most High told Elijah he would not bring the evil in Ahab's own days but would bring it upon his house in his son's days.
- The judgment was not cancelled but delayed and transferred to the next generation, showing that genuine humbling before the Most High moves his hand.
