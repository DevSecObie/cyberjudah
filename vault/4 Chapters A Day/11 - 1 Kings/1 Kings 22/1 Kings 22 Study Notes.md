# 1 Kings 22: A Lying Spirit and the Fall of Ahab

Read the chapter → [[1 Kings 22]]

This session covers [[1 Kings 22|1 Kings 22]], the last chapter of Ahab's reign. Ahab and Jehoshaphat join to take Ramoth-gilead, and four hundred prophets speak with one mouth to send the king up to battle. The teaching follows how Jehoshaphat presses for a true prophet of the Most High, how Micaiah reveals the lying spirit the Most High put in the mouth of the prophets, and how the judgment already pronounced on Ahab is fulfilled when the dogs lick up his blood. The chapter closes with the reign of Jehoshaphat over Judah and the beginning of Ahaziah over Israel.

- After three years without war between Syria and Israel, Jehoshaphat king of Judah comes down to the king of Israel. Ahab reminds his servants that Ramoth in Gilead belongs to Israel, yet they sit still and do not take it out of the hand of the king of Syria.
- Ahab asks Jehoshaphat to go with him to battle, and Jehoshaphat answers that he is as Ahab is, his people and horses as Ahab's, but first asks that they inquire at the word of the Lord. Ahab gathers about four hundred prophets, who all say with one mouth, go up, for the Lord shall deliver it into the hand of the king.
- This is the setting for a division that ran between the northern kingdom and the southern kingdom. After the split, the northern kingdom fell into idolatry and forsook the Levites, so a prophet of the Most High would want nothing to do with their manner of worship.

- Jehoshaphat is not moved by four hundred prophets speaking the same thing, and asks whether there is not yet a prophet of the Lord that they may inquire of him. He wants to hear from a true prophet of the Most High, not from prophets who serve the king.
- Ahab answers that there is one man, Micaiah the son of Imlah, but he hates him because Micaiah does not prophesy good concerning him, but evil. Jehoshaphat rebukes him, saying, let not the king say so.

- The king of Israel calls an officer to hasten Micaiah, while Ahab and Jehoshaphat sit each on his throne in their robes at the entrance of the gate of Samaria, and all the prophets prophesy before them.
- Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah makes horns of iron and says, thus saith the Lord, with these shalt thou push the Syrians until thou have consumed them. Here again a man claims to speak for the Most High while the whole company presses Ahab to go up and prosper.

- The messenger tells Micaiah that all the prophets declare good to the king with one mouth, and urges him to speak likewise. Micaiah answers that whatever the Lord saith unto him, that will he speak, and at first he echoes the others, prompting the king to adjure him to tell nothing but the truth.
- Micaiah then reveals what he saw, all Israel scattered on the hills as sheep having no shepherd, and the Lord saying these have no master. He saw the Lord on his throne with all the host of heaven, and a spirit that offered to be a lying spirit in the mouth of all Ahab's prophets to persuade him to fall at Ramoth-gilead.
- The Most High put a lying spirit in the mouth of these prophets and spoke evil concerning Ahab. The deceiver as well as the deceived are under the hand of the Most High, who uses the lie to bring the judgment he has already declared.

- Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah smites Micaiah on the cheek and asks which way the spirit of the Lord went from him to speak to Micaiah, defending his own false prophecy. The king of Israel orders Micaiah taken back and put in prison, fed with bread and water of affliction until Ahab returns in peace.
- Micaiah answers that if Ahab returns at all in peace, the Lord has not spoken by him, and calls all the people to hearken. His word stands as the test of who truly spoke for the Most High.

- The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat go up to Ramoth-gilead. Ahab disguises himself and tells Jehoshaphat to wear his robes, hoping to escape the word spoken against him, while the king of Syria commands his captains to fight only against the king of Israel.
- The captains pursue Jehoshaphat until they perceive he is not the king of Israel and turn back. A certain man draws a bow at a venture and smites Ahab between the joints of the harness, and the king is held up in his chariot until he dies at even, the blood running into the chariot.
- When they wash the chariot in the pool of Samaria, the dogs lick up his blood according to the word of the Lord. The judgment pronounced earlier against Ahab and his house begins to be fulfilled exactly as spoken.

- Jehoshaphat the son of Asa begins to reign over Judah in the fourth year of Ahab, at thirty-five years old, and reigns twenty-five years in Jerusalem. He walks in the ways of Asa his father, doing right in the eyes of the Lord, though the high places are not taken away and the people still burn incense there.
- Jehoshaphat makes peace with the king of Israel and puts the remnant of the sodomites out of the land. There is then no king in Edom but a deputy, because Edom had been subdued and had not yet revolted.

- Ahaziah the son of Ahab begins to reign over Israel in Samaria in the seventeenth year of Jehoshaphat and reigns two years. He does evil in the sight of the Lord and walks in the way of his father and his mother and in the way of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin.
- He serves Baal and worships him and provokes the Lord God of Israel to anger, according to all that his father had done. The sin of the house of Ahab continues into the next generation.
