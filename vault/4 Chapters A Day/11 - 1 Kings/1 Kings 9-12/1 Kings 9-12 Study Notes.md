# 1 Kings 9: The Most High Appears to Solomon a Second Time

Read the chapter → [[1 Kings 9]]

## Introduction

This session covers 1 Kings 9-12. Solomon has finished the house of the Most High and his own house, and the Most High appears to him a second time to renew the promise and to warn him plainly that if Israel turns to other gods they will be cut off from the land and become a proverb and a byword among all people. From there the reading moves through the greatness of Solomon's kingdom, his gold, his navy, and the visit of the queen of Sheba, and then into his fall, for Solomon loved many strange women and they turned his heart after their gods. Because of that one sin the kingdom is torn, ten tribes given to Jeroboam and two kept for the house of David for David's sake. The reading closes with Rehoboam rejecting the counsel of the elders and Jeroboam setting up golden calves, so that Israel is divided to this day. Solomon built the first temple in Jerusalem, and Solomon was a black man, which the scripture itself declares.

- Everything in the kingdom was in order, the servants in uniform, the cupbearers in place; this is why order matters in the body today, so that those who come in are astonished and the worldly spirit of disorder leaves them, seeing the discipline and love for the people.

- Solomon knew how to entertain the people, providing for singing and joyfulness in the house of the Most High, for he had vision for the house and wanted Israel to enjoy the feasts and serve with gladness of heart.
- Solomon gave the queen of Sheba all her desire, whatever she asked. # 1 Kings 12: The Kingdom Divided Under Rehoboam and Jeroboam

- The heavy yoke was the labor of the building; Solomon did not enslave his own people as he did the other nations, but they did have to put in that work. Rehoboam told them to depart three days and return, which was wisdom, for it is right to take time and get counsel before giving an answer.

- Rehoboam consulted the old men that stood before Solomon his father, who advised that if he would be a servant to the people and speak good words, they would be his servants forever, but he forsook their counsel and consulted the young men he grew up with.
- Solomon was the wisest man on earth and still had counselors, yet Rehoboam forsook the counselors of the wisest man for his peers. The problem is getting counsel from those on your own level rather than from elders.
- The young men told him to say his little finger would be thicker than his father's loins, and that where his father chastised them with whips he would chastise them with scorpions, which was disrespectful.

- When Israel heard Jeroboam had returned, they called him to the congregation and made him king over Israel; none followed the house of David but the tribe of Judah only. That is how Jeroboam got the ten tribes.
