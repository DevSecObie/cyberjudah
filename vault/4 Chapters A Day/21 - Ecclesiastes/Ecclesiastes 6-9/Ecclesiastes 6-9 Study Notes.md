# Ecclesiastes 6: The Vanity of Wealth Without the Power to Enjoy It

Read the chapter → [[Ecclesiastes 6]]

This session of "four chapters a day" moves through [[Ecclesiastes 6|Ecclesiastes 6]] to 9, written by Solomon. The teaching held this to be a very heavy reading, with a lot of understanding on death, on life, and on much else, and it warned that Ecclesiastes is one of those books that raises questions not all of which can be answered. The framing set at the start contrasted the world's wicked holidays that Esau set up, like Valentine's Day and Christmas, with the Lord's feast days that the righteous are now coming into the truth to keep and enjoy. Chapter 6 opens on an evil common among men: a man given riches, wealth, and honor by the Most High, yet not given the power to enjoy them, so that a stranger eats what he laid up.

**[[Ecclesiastes 6#^v1|Ecclesiastes 6:1-2]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 6#^v1]]
![[Ecclesiastes 6#^v2]]

- There is an evil common among men: a man to whom the Most High has given riches, wealth, and honor so that he wants nothing his soul desires, yet is not given the power to eat of it, and a stranger eats it instead.
- This is called vanity and an evil disease, for the wealth profits the man nothing when he has no power to enjoy the labor of his own hands.

**[[Ecclesiastes 6#^v3|Ecclesiastes 6:3-6]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 6#^v3]]
![[Ecclesiastes 6#^v4]]
![[Ecclesiastes 6#^v5]]
![[Ecclesiastes 6#^v6]]

- Though a man beget a hundred children and live many years, if his soul is not filled with good and he has no burial, an untimely birth is better than he.
- The untimely birth comes in with vanity and departs in darkness, having seen nothing, yet it has more rest than the man who lived and saw no good.
- Though a man live a thousand years twice told and yet see no good, all go to one place, and length of days without the enjoyment of them is empty.

**[[Ecclesiastes 6#^v7|Ecclesiastes 6:7-9]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 6#^v7]]
![[Ecclesiastes 6#^v8]]
![[Ecclesiastes 6#^v9]]

- All the labor of man is for his mouth, and yet the appetite is never filled, so the wise man in this has no advantage over the fool.
- Better is the sight of the eyes, the good a man actually has before him, than the wandering of the desire that is never satisfied; this too is vanity and vexation of spirit.

**[[Ecclesiastes 6#^v10|Ecclesiastes 6:10-12]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 6#^v10]]
![[Ecclesiastes 6#^v11]]
![[Ecclesiastes 6#^v12]]

- That which has been is already named and known to be man, and man may not contend with the Most High, who is mightier than he.
- The more a man multiplies words and things, the more he increases vanity, and no man knows what is truly good for him in the few days of his vain life, nor what shall be after him under the sun.

# Ecclesiastes 7: A Good Name and the House of Mourning

Read the chapter → [[Ecclesiastes 7]]

Chapter 7 turns to the sober wisdom of the righteous: a good name, the house of mourning, patience, and the fear of the Most High. Solomon sets sorrow above laughter and the rebuke of the wise above the song of fools, and he tells us plainly that he proved all these things by wisdom, seeking out both the wisdom and the wickedness of folly.

**[[Ecclesiastes 7#^v1|Ecclesiastes 7:1-4]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v1]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v2]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v3]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v4]]

- A good name is better than precious ointment, and the day of death better than the day of one's birth, for the day of death is the end that the living should lay to heart.
- It is better to go to the house of mourning than to the house of feasting, for that is the end of all men, and sorrow is better than laughter because the sadness of the countenance makes the heart better.
- The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning, but the heart of fools is in the house of mirth.

**[[Ecclesiastes 7#^v5|Ecclesiastes 7:5-7]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v5]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v6]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v7]]

- It is better to hear the rebuke of the wise than to hear the song of fools, for the laughter of the fool is like the crackling of thorns under a pot, quick and empty.
- Oppression makes a wise man mad, and a gift destroys the heart, warning that even the wise can be turned by pressure and by bribes.

**[[Ecclesiastes 7#^v8|Ecclesiastes 7:8-10]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v8]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v9]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v10]]

- Better is the end of a thing than its beginning, and the patient in spirit is better than the proud in spirit.
- Be not hasty in thy spirit to be angry, for anger rests in the bosom of fools, and do not ask why the former days were better than these, for that is not asked wisely.

**[[Ecclesiastes 7#^v11|Ecclesiastes 7:11-14]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v11]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v12]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v13]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v14]]

- Wisdom is good with an inheritance and profits those who see the sun, for wisdom is a defense and money is a defense, but the excellency of knowledge is that wisdom gives life to those who have it.
- Consider the work of the Most High, for no man can make straight what He has made crooked; in the day of prosperity be joyful, but in the day of adversity consider that He has set the one against the other.

**[[Ecclesiastes 7#^v15|Ecclesiastes 7:15-18]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v15]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v16]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v17]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v18]]

- Solomon has seen in his vain days a just man perishing in his righteousness and a wicked man prolonging his life in his wickedness.
- Be not righteous over much nor over wise, and be not over much wicked nor foolish; he that fears the Most High shall come forth of them all.

**[[Ecclesiastes 7#^v19|Ecclesiastes 7:19-22]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v19]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v20]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v21]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v22]]

- Wisdom strengthens the wise more than ten mighty men in the city, yet there is not a just man upon the earth that does good and sins not.
- Verse 20 is one of those verses the Christians love to pull out, the same as the New Testament saying we all fall short and Proverbs saying a just man falls seven times; it does not excuse sin but shows that no man is sinless.
- Take no heed to all the words that are spoken, lest thou hear thy servant curse thee, for thine own heart knows that thou hast likewise cursed others.
  - **[[Proverbs 24#^v16|Proverbs 24:16]]**
    ![[Proverbs 24#^v16]]
    A just man falls seven times and rises up again, so a fall does not undo the righteous man.
  - **[[Romans 3#^v23|Romans 3:23]]**
    ![[Romans 3#^v23]]
    All have sinned and come short of the glory of the Most High, the same point Solomon makes here.

**[[Ecclesiastes 7#^v23|Ecclesiastes 7:23-25]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v23]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v24]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v25]]

- Solomon proved all this by wisdom and said he would be wise, but it was far from him; that which is far off and exceeding deep, who can find it out.
- He applied his heart to know and search out wisdom and the reason of things, and also to know the wickedness of folly and the foolishness of madness, seeking out both the wicked and the wise.

**[[Ecclesiastes 7#^v26|Ecclesiastes 7:26-29]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v26]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v27]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v28]]
![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v29]]

- Solomon finds more bitter than death the woman whose heart is snares and nets and whose hands are as bands; whoso pleases the Most High escapes her, but the sinner is taken by her.
- Counting one by one, he found one man among a thousand, but not one woman among all those, and concludes that the Most High made man upright, but they have sought out many inventions.

# Ecclesiastes 8: Keep the King's Commandment

Read the chapter → [[Ecclesiastes 8]]

Chapter 8 counsels obedience to the king, and the teaching made clear that the king spoken of here is the Most High, so keeping the king's commandment means keeping the Lord's commandment. It moves on to the wise man's discernment of time and judgment, and to the difference between the end of the righteous and the wicked.

**[[Ecclesiastes 8#^v1|Ecclesiastes 8:1-4]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v1]]
![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v2]]
![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v3]]
![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v4]]

- A man's wisdom makes his face to shine, and the boldness of his face is changed; the counsel is to keep the king's commandment in regard of the oath of the Most High.
- The king spoken of here is the Most High Himself, for verse 3 says He does whatsoever pleases Him; therefore keeping the king's commandment is keeping the Lord's commandment, and where the word of the king is, there is power.

**[[Ecclesiastes 8#^v5|Ecclesiastes 8:5-8]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v5]]
![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v6]]
![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v7]]
![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v8]]

- Whoso keeps the commandment shall feel no evil thing, and a wise man's heart discerns both time and judgment, for to every purpose there is a time and judgment.
- No man has power over the spirit to retain the spirit, nor power in the day of death; there is no discharge in that war, and wickedness will not deliver those given to it.

**[[Ecclesiastes 8#^v9|Ecclesiastes 8:9-11]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v9]]
![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v10]]
![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v11]]

- Solomon applied his heart to every work done under the sun and saw one man rule over another to his own hurt.
- He saw the wicked buried and then forgotten in the place where they had done their evil, and because sentence against an evil work is not executed speedily, the hearts of the sons of men are fully set to do evil.

**[[Ecclesiastes 8#^v12|Ecclesiastes 8:12-13]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v12]]
![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v13]]

- Though a sinner do evil a hundred times and his days be prolonged, Solomon knows it shall be well with those that fear the Most High.
- It shall not be well with the wicked, and he shall not prolong his days, which are as a shadow, because he fears not before the Most High; the meaning is that the wicked do not inherit the kingdom and do not have the everlasting life the righteous receive.

**[[Ecclesiastes 8#^v14|Ecclesiastes 8:14-17]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v14]]
![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v15]]
![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v16]]
![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v17]]

- There is a vanity done upon the earth, that just men are treated according to the work of the wicked and wicked men according to the work of the righteous.
- Solomon commends mirth, for a man has no better thing under the sun than to eat, drink, and be merry in his labor; yet a man cannot find out the work of the Most High done under the sun, and though he labor to seek it out, he shall not find it.

# Ecclesiastes 9: One Event to All, and Wisdom Better Than Strength

Read the chapter → [[Ecclesiastes 9]]

Chapter 9 declares that the righteous and the wise and their works are in the hand of the Most High, and that one event, death, comes alike to all. Solomon counsels the living to enjoy their labor and to stay in righteousness, and he closes on the theme that wisdom is better than strength, though the poor man's wisdom is despised.

**[[Ecclesiastes 9#^v1|Ecclesiastes 9:1-3]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v1]]
![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v2]]
![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v3]]

- The righteous and the wise and their works are in the hand of the Most High, and no man knows love or hatred by all that is before him.
- All things come alike to all, one event to the righteous and the wicked, the clean and the unclean; this is an evil, that one event comes to all, and the heart of the sons of men is full of evil while they live.

**[[Ecclesiastes 9#^v4|Ecclesiastes 9:4-6]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v4]]
![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v5]]
![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v6]]

- To him that is joined to the living there is hope, for a living dog is better than a dead lion.
- The living know that they shall die, but the dead know not anything, and their love, hatred, and envy have perished, and they have no more portion in anything done under the sun.

**[[Ecclesiastes 9#^v7|Ecclesiastes 9:7-10]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v7]]
![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v8]]
![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v9]]
![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v10]]

- Eat thy bread with joy and drink thy wine with a merry heart, for the Most High now accepts thy works.
- Let thy garments be always white, which means stay in righteousness, and let thy head lack no ointment, the ointment being the understanding.
- Live joyfully with the wife thou lovest all the days of thy vain life, and whatsoever thy hand finds to do, do it with thy might, for there is no work nor device nor knowledge nor wisdom in the grave.

**[[Ecclesiastes 9#^v11|Ecclesiastes 9:11-12]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v11]]
![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v12]]

- The race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, nor bread to the wise, nor riches to men of understanding, but time and chance happen to them all.
- Time and chance here means death; man knows not his time, and like fishes taken in a net and birds caught in a snare, the sons of men are snared in an evil time when it falls suddenly upon them.

**[[Ecclesiastes 9#^v13|Ecclesiastes 9:13-16]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v13]]
![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v14]]
![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v15]]
![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v16]]

- Solomon saw a wisdom that seemed great to him: a little city with few men, against which a great king came and built great bulwarks, a bulwark being a defensive wall or fortification.
- A poor wise man in the city delivered it by his wisdom, yet no man remembered that same poor man; so wisdom is better than strength, even though the poor man's wisdom is despised and his words are not heard.

**[[Ecclesiastes 9#^v17|Ecclesiastes 9:17-18]]**

![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v17]]
![[Ecclesiastes 9#^v18]]

- The words of wise men are heard in quiet more than the cry of him that rules among fools.
- Wisdom is better than weapons of war, but one sinner destroys much good, closing the reading on the worth of wisdom and the harm a single sinner can do.
