# Proverbs 29: The Righteous in Authority and the Rod of Reproof

Read the chapter → [[Proverbs 29]]

This session is a "four chapters a day" feast-day reading covering [[Proverbs 29|Proverbs 29]] through [[Ecclesiastes 1|Ecclesiastes 1]]. The teacher reads through each chapter and marks it as a good chapter full of "good bits," urging everyone to write down their scriptures and any questions as they read. He notes plainly that these are wisdom chapters to take note of, and that the point of the reading is to keep the commandments and retain what you study a little at a time.

**[[Proverbs 29#^v1|Proverbs 29:1]]**

![[Proverbs 29#^v1]]

- The man who is often corrected and stiffens his neck against it will be destroyed suddenly and without remedy. Hardening yourself against reproof is a fatal path.

**[[Proverbs 29#^v2|Proverbs 29:2]]**

![[Proverbs 29#^v2]]

- When the righteous hold authority the people rejoice, but when the wicked rule the people mourn. Leadership sets the mood and welfare of the whole people.

**[[Proverbs 29#^v3|Proverbs 29:3-6]]**

![[Proverbs 29#^v3]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v4]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v5]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v6]]

- The one who loves wisdom gladdens his father, while the man who keeps company with harlots wastes his substance.
- A king establishes the land by judgment, but the ruler who takes bribes tears it down. The flatterer sets a net for his neighbor's feet.

**[[Proverbs 29#^v7|Proverbs 29:7-8]]**

![[Proverbs 29#^v7]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v8]]

- The righteous man cares about the cause of the poor; the wicked will not even trouble himself to know it.
- Scornful men throw a city into a snare, but wise men turn away wrath.

**[[Proverbs 29#^v9|Proverbs 29:9-11]]**

![[Proverbs 29#^v9]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v10]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v11]]

- Contending with a fool brings no rest whether he rages or laughs. The bloodthirsty hate the upright man.
- A fool pours out his whole mind, but a wise man holds it back and keeps it in until afterwards.

**[[Proverbs 29#^v12|Proverbs 29:12-14]]**

![[Proverbs 29#^v12]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v13]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v14]]

- If a ruler listens to lies, all his servants become wicked. The tone of the leader corrupts or steadies those under him.
- The king who faithfully judges the poor will have his throne established forever.

**[[Proverbs 29#^v15|Proverbs 29:15-17]]**

![[Proverbs 29#^v15]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v16]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v17]]

- The rod and reproof give wisdom, but a child left to himself brings his mother to shame. Correct your son and he will give you rest and delight your soul.

**[[Proverbs 29#^v18|Proverbs 29:18]]**

![[Proverbs 29#^v18]]

- Where there is no vision the people perish, but he that keeps the law is blessed. Without direction and the law a people fall apart.

**[[Proverbs 29#^v19|Proverbs 29:19-23]]**

![[Proverbs 29#^v19]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v20]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v21]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v22]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v23]]

- A servant is not corrected by words alone; the hasty man in his words has less hope than a fool.
- An angry man stirs up strife and a furious man abounds in transgression. A man's pride brings him low, but the humble in spirit hold honor.

**[[Proverbs 29#^v24|Proverbs 29:24-27]]**

![[Proverbs 29#^v24]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v25]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v26]]
![[Proverbs 29#^v27]]

- The partner of a thief hates his own soul. The fear of man brings a snare, but whoever trusts in the Most High is safe.
- A man's judgment comes from the Most High, and the unjust and the upright are an abomination to one another.

# Proverbs 30: Agur's Prophecy and the Purity of God's Word

Read the chapter → [[Proverbs 30]]

**[[Proverbs 30#^v1|Proverbs 30:1-3]]**

![[Proverbs 30#^v1]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v2]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v3]]

- These are the words of Agur, who confesses himself more brutish than any man, having neither learned wisdom nor the knowledge of the holy. Wisdom begins with admitting how little one knows on his own.

**[[Proverbs 30#^v4|Proverbs 30:4]]**

![[Proverbs 30#^v4]]

- Who has gone up into heaven or gathered the wind in his fist? The questions point to the Most High and to His Son, whose name the reader is challenged to tell.

**[[Proverbs 30#^v5|Proverbs 30:5-6]]**

![[Proverbs 30#^v5]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v6]]

- Every word of the Most High is pure, and He is a shield to those who trust in Him. Add nothing to His words, lest He reprove you and you be found a liar.

**[[Proverbs 30#^v7|Proverbs 30:7-9]]**

![[Proverbs 30#^v7]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v8]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v9]]

- Agur asks for two things before he dies: to have vanity and lies removed, and to be given neither poverty nor riches but food convenient for him.
- Too much wealth tempts a man to deny the Most High; poverty tempts him to steal and profane the name of his God.

**[[Proverbs 30#^v10|Proverbs 30:10-14]]**

![[Proverbs 30#^v10]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v11]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v12]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v13]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v14]]

- Do not accuse a servant to his master. There is a generation that curses its father and does not bless its mother, and one pure in its own eyes yet not washed from its filthiness.
- A generation with lofty eyes devours the poor from off the earth and the needy from among men.

**[[Proverbs 30#^v15|Proverbs 30:15-17]]**

![[Proverbs 30#^v15]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v16]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v17]]

- The grave, the barren womb, the dry earth, and the fire never say "enough." The eye that mocks its father and despises its mother will be picked out by the ravens and eaten by young eagles.

**[[Proverbs 30#^v18|Proverbs 30:18-20]]**

![[Proverbs 30#^v18]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v19]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v20]]

- Four things are too wonderful to trace: the eagle in the air, the serpent on the rock, the ship in the sea, and the way of a man with a maid.
- The adulterous woman eats, wipes her mouth, and claims she has done no wickedness.

**[[Proverbs 30#^v21|Proverbs 30:21-23]]**

![[Proverbs 30#^v21]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v22]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v23]]

- Four things disquiet the earth: a servant when he reigns, a fool filled with meat, an odious woman when she is married, and a handmaid who displaces her mistress.

**[[Proverbs 30#^v24|Proverbs 30:24-28]]**

![[Proverbs 30#^v24]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v25]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v26]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v27]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v28]]

- Four small creatures are exceeding wise: the ants who store their meat in summer, the feeble conies who house in the rocks, the kingless locusts who march in bands, and the spider found in kings' palaces.

**[[Proverbs 30#^v29|Proverbs 30:29-33]]**

![[Proverbs 30#^v29]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v30]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v31]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v32]]
![[Proverbs 30#^v33]]

- Four things are comely in their going: the lion, the greyhound, the he-goat, and the king against whom none rise up.
- If you have acted foolishly or thought evil, lay your hand on your mouth; for as churning brings butter and wringing the nose brings blood, so forcing wrath brings forth strife.

# Proverbs 31: The Mother's Charge and the Virtuous Woman

Read the chapter → [[Proverbs 31]]

The teacher closes this chapter by calling it a good one for both sisters and brothers to take from: brothers can figure out the type of wife they need to seek and raise toward, and sisters can figure out how they ought to be.

**[[Proverbs 31#^v1|Proverbs 31:1-3]]**

![[Proverbs 31#^v1]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v2]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v3]]

- These are the words of King Lemuel, the prophecy his mother taught him. She warns her son not to give his strength to women nor his ways to that which destroys kings.

**[[Proverbs 31#^v4|Proverbs 31:4-7]]**

![[Proverbs 31#^v4]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v5]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v6]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v7]]

- It is not for kings to drink wine or strong drink, lest they forget the law and pervert the judgment of the afflicted.
- Strong drink is for those ready to perish and heavy of heart, that they may forget their poverty and misery.

**[[Proverbs 31#^v8|Proverbs 31:8-9]]**

![[Proverbs 31#^v8]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v9]]

- Open your mouth for the dumb and for all who are appointed to destruction. Judge righteously and plead the cause of the poor and needy.

**[[Proverbs 31#^v10|Proverbs 31:10-12]]**

![[Proverbs 31#^v10]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v11]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v12]]

- Who can find a virtuous woman? Her price is far above rubies. Her husband trusts her safely and she does him good, not evil, all her days.

**[[Proverbs 31#^v13|Proverbs 31:13-16]]**

![[Proverbs 31#^v13]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v14]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v15]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v16]]

- She works willingly with her hands, brings her food from afar like merchant ships, rises while it is still night to feed her household, and considers a field to buy and plant a vineyard.

**[[Proverbs 31#^v17|Proverbs 31:17-20]]**

![[Proverbs 31#^v17]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v18]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v19]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v20]]

- She girds her loins with strength, sees that her merchandise is good, keeps her candle burning by night, and reaches out her hands to the poor and needy.

**[[Proverbs 31#^v21|Proverbs 31:21-24]]**

![[Proverbs 31#^v21]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v22]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v23]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v24]]

- She does not fear the snow, for her household is clothed in scarlet. Her clothing is silk and purple, her husband is known in the gates, and she sells the fine linen and girdles she makes.

**[[Proverbs 31#^v25|Proverbs 31:25-27]]**

![[Proverbs 31#^v25]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v26]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v27]]

- Strength and honor are her clothing and she rejoices in time to come. She opens her mouth with wisdom, her tongue carries the law of kindness, and she does not eat the bread of idleness.

**[[Proverbs 31#^v28|Proverbs 31:28-31]]**

![[Proverbs 31#^v28]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v29]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v30]]
![[Proverbs 31#^v31]]

- Her children rise up and call her blessed, and her husband praises her above all. Favor is deceitful and beauty is vain, but a woman who fears the Most High shall be praised and given the fruit of her own hands.
