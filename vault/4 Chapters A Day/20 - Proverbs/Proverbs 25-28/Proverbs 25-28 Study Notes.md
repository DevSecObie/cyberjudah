# Proverbs 25: The Proverbs of Solomon Copied by Hezekiah's Men

Read the chapter → [[Proverbs 25]]

This feast-day "four chapters a day" session covers [[Proverbs 25|Proverbs 25]] through 28. The teacher notes plainly that Esau is the devil, and that our enemy does not want the black, Hispanic, and Israelite man to have anything, all the more reason to keep the commandments, hold the faith of Christ, and pray for the destruction of our enemies. He begins by reversing to the opening verse to show that this chapter is a separate collection: these are proverbs of Solomon which the men of King Hezekiah of Judah copied out. Those men were studying Solomon's writings; they understood that things written before their time were written for our learning and did not treat them as done away. He assigns homework to go back into the prophets, Chronicles, and Kings and read about King Hezekiah. This is a wisdom book, learned a little at a time and not mastered in a week.

**[[Proverbs 25#^v1|Proverbs 25:1]]**

![[Proverbs 25#^v1]]

- These are also proverbs of Solomon copied out by the men of Hezekiah king of Judah. Hezekiah and his men studied Solomon's writings because they understood that what was written before their time was written for our learning, to seek out the ways of our fathers and find the right paths.
- Go back and read about King Hezekiah in the prophets, Chronicles, and Kings as a homework assignment.

# Proverbs 26: Honor Is Not Fitting for a Fool

Read the chapter → [[Proverbs 26]]

This chapter drives home how to deal with fools, sluggards, meddlers, and deceitful flatterers. The teacher walks through each figure Solomon draws, pulling precepts from Ecclesiasticus, Second Peter, Luke, and the law to show the same wisdom taught by the apostles and forefathers, who themselves quoted Solomon.

- As snow in summer and rain in harvest, so honor is not fitting for a fool. As the bird wandering and the swallow flying, the causeless curse shall not come.
- A whip for the horse, a bridle for the ass, and a rod for the fool's back. Ecclesiasticus says the same: correction and work are for a servant, and that correction is the rod for the fool's back.

# Proverbs 27: Boast Not of Tomorrow

Read the chapter → [[Proverbs 27]]

This chapter opens with the warning not to boast of tomorrow, since none of us know how much time we have, and moves through friendship, open rebuke, contentment, and diligence with what the Most High has given. The teacher ties it to James on the uncertainty of life, to the fixed line the Most High sets on a man's days, and to the crown that has not endured for Israel from one generation to the next.

**[[Proverbs 27#^v1|Proverbs 27:1-2]]**

![[Proverbs 27#^v1]]
![[Proverbs 27#^v2]]

- Do not boast of tomorrow, for you do not know what a day may bring forth. James says the same: you do not know what will be tomorrow, for your life is a vapor; you ought to say, if the Most High wills, we will do this or that.
- None of us knows how much time we have. The Most High draws the line: Moses spoke unadvisedly at the waters of strife and was told he would not enter the promised land, and though he died in full strength at 120, the Most High set the end of his days.
- Let another man praise you, and not your own mouth; a stranger, and not your own lips. Do not be boastful.

# Proverbs 28: The Righteous Are Bold as a Lion

Read the chapter → [[Proverbs 28]]

This closing chapter contrasts the wicked and the righteous, the lawkeeper and the lawforsaker, the honest poor and the man of unjust gain. The teacher shows that boldness comes from being with Christ and speaking the words of the Most High, that keeping the commandments prolongs the throne of Israel, and that mercy comes to the one who confesses and forsakes his sins.

**[[Proverbs 28#^v1|Proverbs 28:1-2]]**

![[Proverbs 28#^v1]]
![[Proverbs 28#^v2]]

- The wicked flee when no man pursues, but the righteous are bold as a lion. Peter and John were perceived as unlearned men, yet their boldness made men take knowledge that they had been with Christ, who taught as one having authority. We can be bold this same way by using the words of the Most High, which no one can gainsay.
- For the transgression of a land many are its princes, but by a man of understanding and knowledge its state shall be prolonged. That understanding and knowledge is the Most High's commandments; David told Solomon that keeping the commandments keeps a man on the throne of Israel.

- He that turns away his ear from hearing the law, even his prayer shall be an abomination, as [[John 9|John 9]] says the Most High does not hear sinners.
  - **[[John 9#^v31|John 9:31]]**

    The Most High hears not sinners, but the worshipper that does his will.

- Whoever causes the righteous to go astray in an evil way shall fall into his own pit, but the upright shall have good things. The rich man is wise in his own conceit, but the poor that has understanding searches him out.
- He that covers his sins shall not prosper, but whoever confesses and forsakes them shall have mercy. The Most High has mercy on those that forsake their sins; that is why we must repent, Israel. Happy is the man that fears always, but he that hardens his heart falls into mischief.

**[[Proverbs 28#^v15|Proverbs 28:15-18]]**

![[Proverbs 28#^v15]]
![[Proverbs 28#^v16]]
![[Proverbs 28#^v17]]
![[Proverbs 28#^v18]]

- As a roaring lion and a raging bear, so is a wicked ruler over the people. The prince that lacks understanding is a great oppressor, but he that hates covetousness shall prolong his days.
- A man that does violence to the blood of any person shall flee to the pit; let no man stay him. Whoever walks uprightly shall be saved, but he that is perverse in his ways shall fall at once. Our people cannot say "I'm saved," for he that endures to the end shall be saved; [[Ezekiel 18|Ezekiel 18]] shows that if your last act is evil, the Most High will not remember the good you did before.

**[[Proverbs 28#^v19|Proverbs 28:19-22]]**

![[Proverbs 28#^v19]]
![[Proverbs 28#^v20]]
![[Proverbs 28#^v21]]
![[Proverbs 28#^v22]]

- He that tills his land shall have plenty of bread, but he that follows vain persons shall have poverty enough. Tilling the land is hard work, and the Most High says hate not laborious work; chasing vain persons means always being on some get-rich-quick scheme that does not work.
- A faithful man shall abound with blessings, but he that makes haste to be rich shall not be innocent. To have respect of persons is not good, for a piece of bread that man will transgress. He that hastens to be rich has an evil eye and does not consider that poverty will come; if you chase quick riches you care nothing for your victims.

- He that rebukes a man shall afterward find more favor than he that flatters with the tongue.
- Whoever robs his father or mother and says it is no transgression is a companion of a destroyer. Think of the times, even as grown people, some went into a mother's purse or stole from a father; we must repent from these things, Israel.
- He that is of a proud heart stirs up strife, but he that puts his trust in the Most High shall be made fat.

**[[Proverbs 28#^v26|Proverbs 28:26-28]]**

- He that trusts in his own heart is a fool, but whoever walks wisely shall be delivered.
- He that gives to the poor shall not lack, but he that hides his eyes shall have many a curse. Ecclesiasticus and the law say the same: do not turn your eye from the needy, and open your hand wide to your poor brother, for if he curses you in the bitterness of his soul his prayer is heard. [[Ecclesiasticus 12|Sirach 12]] says if you do good, know to whom you do it, and the law commands us to help our brothers who keep the commandments.
- When the wicked rise, men hide themselves, but when they perish the righteous increase.
