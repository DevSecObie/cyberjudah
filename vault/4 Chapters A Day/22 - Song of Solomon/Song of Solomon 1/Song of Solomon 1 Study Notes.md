# Song of Solomon 1: The Song of Songs, Which Is Solomon's

Read the chapter → [[Song of Solomon 1]]

This session closes with [[Song of Solomon 1|Song of Solomon 1]], read straight through as a single parable. The teacher stresses that this is a heavy book, not one to dig through casually in a four-chapters-a-day reading. In the questions afterward he settles the authorship, explaining that Solomon wrote it, and defines the plant names used for the perfumes and fragrances in the chapter.

**[[Song of Solomon 1#^v1|Song of Solomon 1:1-4]]**

![[Song of Solomon 1#^v1]]
![[Song of Solomon 1#^v2]]
![[Song of Solomon 1#^v3]]
![[Song of Solomon 1#^v4]]

- The book opens, "The song of songs, which is Solomon's." Solomon is the author, not a woman, though that is the doctrine some try to push. The plain words of verse one say these songs belong to Solomon.
- Solomon was a poet. The Most High gave him wisdom and largeness of heart, and he spoke three thousand proverbs and his songs were a thousand and five, which shows plainly who wrote this book.
- The bride longs for the king's love, calling it better than wine; the savor of his good ointments makes his name as ointment poured forth, and therefore the virgins love him. Drawn into the king's chambers, they are glad and rejoice in him.

**[[Song of Solomon 1#^v5|Song of Solomon 1:5-6]]**

![[Song of Solomon 1#^v5]]
![[Song of Solomon 1#^v6]]

- The bride says, "I am black, but comely, O ye daughters of Jerusalem," likening herself to the tents of Kedar and the curtains of Solomon.
- She says, look not upon me because I am black, because the sun has looked upon me; her mother's children were angry with her and made her keeper of the vineyards, but her own vineyard she has not kept.

**[[Song of Solomon 1#^v7|Song of Solomon 1:7-8]]**

![[Song of Solomon 1#^v7]]
![[Song of Solomon 1#^v8]]

- She asks the one her soul loves where he feeds his flock and where he makes it rest at noon, so she need not wander as one who turns aside by the flocks of his companions.
- The answer: if she does not know, then O fairest among women, go by the footsteps of the flock and feed her kids beside the shepherds' tents.

**[[Song of Solomon 1#^v9|Song of Solomon 1:9-11]]**

![[Song of Solomon 1#^v9]]

- He compares his love to a company of horses in Pharaoh's chariots, and praises her cheeks comely with rows of jewels and her neck with chains of gold.
- They will make her borders of gold with studs of silver.

**[[Song of Solomon 1#^v12|Song of Solomon 1:12-14]]**

![[Song of Solomon 1#^v12]]
![[Song of Solomon 1#^v13]]
![[Song of Solomon 1#^v14]]

- While the king sits at his table, her spikenard sends forth its smell; her wellbeloved is a bundle of myrrh that shall lie all night between her breasts, and a cluster of camphire in the vineyards of Engedi.
- Spikenard is a flower or plant used to make oils and perfumes; camphire is a fragrant henna flower. The people back then used no artificial smells but took everything straight from the earth, and those are the best smells.
