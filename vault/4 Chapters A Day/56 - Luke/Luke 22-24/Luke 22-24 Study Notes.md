# Luke 22: The Passover, the Betrayal, and the Denial

Read the chapter → [[Luke 22]]

## Introduction

This session covers Luke 22 through 24 and John 1. These notes cover the three chapters in Luke: the last passover, the betrayal in the garden, the trial and the crucifixion, and the resurrection with the walk to Emmaus. The reading keeps returning to two things, who the covenant belongs to and what the prophets had already written concerning him.

**Luke 22:1-2**

![[Luke 22#^v1]]
![[Luke 22#^v2]]

- The feast of unleavened bread drew nigh, which is called the Passover. The feast of unleavened bread and the passover are spoken of here as one and the same season.

**Luke 22:3-6**

![[Luke 22#^v3]]
![[Luke 22#^v4]]

- The chief priests and captains were glad, and covenanted to give him money. Men are not in their right mind when they can be glad over a thing like that.

![[Luke 22#^v23]]

![[Luke 22#^v26]]

- As a man is raised in knowledge and understanding he is made more of a servant, not less. I am among you as he that serveth, and he was the greatest of them.

**Luke 22:28-30**

![[Luke 22#^v28]]
![[Luke 22#^v29]]
![[Luke 22#^v30]]

**Luke 22:31-32**

![[Luke 22#^v31]]
![[Luke 22#^v32]]

- Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat; but I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not; and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.
- Peter walked with him and was not yet converted. What converts a man is named in the psalm, and what he strengthens his brethren with is the same thing.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Psalms 19#^v7|Psalms 19:7]]**
    ![[Psalms 19#^v7]]
    The law of the Lord is perfect, converting the soul.
  - **[[1 Corinthians 15#^v56|1 Corinthians 15:56]]**
    ![[1 Corinthians 15#^v56]]
    The sting of death is sin; and the strength of sin is the law.

![[Luke 22#^v34]]

- Lord, I am ready to go with thee, both into prison, and to death. He is told the cock shall not crow this day, before he shall thrice deny that he knows him.

# Luke 23: The Trial, the Crucifixion, and the Burial

Read the chapter → [[Luke 23]]

**Luke 23:1-5**

![[Luke 23#^v1]]
![[Luke 23#^v2]]
![[Luke 23#^v3]]
![[Luke 23#^v4]]
![[Luke 23#^v5]]

- They accuse him of perverting the nation and forbidding to give tribute to Caesar, and Pilate says, I find no fault in this man. Matthew's account gives the reason he could see it: he knew that for envy they had delivered him.

**Luke 23:6-12**

![[Luke 23#^v6]]
![[Luke 23#^v7]]
![[Luke 23#^v8]]
![[Luke 23#^v9]]
![[Luke 23#^v10]]
![[Luke 23#^v11]]
![[Luke 23#^v12]]

- Herod was exceeding glad, hoping to have seen some miracle done by him, and questioned him in many words, but he answered him nothing.
- He had nothing to say to that house. Its head had sought his life as a child, and this one had beheaded John.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Luke 13#^v31|Luke 13:31-32]]**
    ![[Luke 13#^v31]]
    ![[Luke 13#^v32]]
    Go ye, and tell that fox, Behold, I cast out devils, and I do cures to day and to morrow.

**Luke 23:13-25**

![[Luke 23#^v13]]
![[Luke 23#^v14]]
![[Luke 23#^v15]]
![[Luke 23#^v16]]
![[Luke 23#^v17]]
![[Luke 23#^v18]]
![[Luke 23#^v19]]
![[Luke 23#^v20]]
![[Luke 23#^v21]]
![[Luke 23#^v22]]
![[Luke 23#^v23]]
![[Luke 23#^v24]]
![[Luke 23#^v25]]

- Pilate says a third time, Why, what evil hath he done? I have found no cause of death in him. He knew the man was innocent and gave sentence anyway.
- Barabbas, cast into prison for sedition and murder, is released, and Jesus is delivered to their will.

**Luke 23:26-31**

![[Luke 23#^v26]]
![[Luke 23#^v27]]
![[Luke 23#^v28]]
![[Luke 23#^v29]]
![[Luke 23#^v30]]
![[Luke 23#^v31]]

- Simon a Cyrenian is laid hold upon and made to bear the cross after him.
- Daughters of Jerusalem, weep not for me, but weep for yourselves, and for your children. If they do these things in a green tree, what shall be done in the dry?

**Luke 23:32-38**

![[Luke 23#^v32]]
![[Luke 23#^v33]]
![[Luke 23#^v34]]
![[Luke 23#^v35]]
![[Luke 23#^v36]]
![[Luke 23#^v37]]
![[Luke 23#^v38]]

- Two malefactors, criminals, are led with him to be put to death, and they crucified him at the place called Calvary.
- Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. The superscription written over him in Greek, Latin and Hebrew reads, This is the King of the Jews.

**Luke 23:39-43**

![[Luke 23#^v39]]
![[Luke 23#^v40]]
![[Luke 23#^v41]]
![[Luke 23#^v42]]
![[Luke 23#^v43]]

- One malefactor railed on him; the other rebuked him, took the blame of his own deeds, and said, Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom.
- That man repented at the last hour, and was answered, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.

**Luke 23:44-46**

![[Luke 23#^v44]]
![[Luke 23#^v45]]
![[Luke 23#^v46]]

- There was a darkness over all the earth until the ninth hour, and the sun was darkened, and the veil of the temple was rent in the midst.
- The rending of the veil is a sign. What stood between the holy place and the holiest was opened, and the sacrificial service under the first testament was coming to its end.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Hebrews 9#^v14|Hebrews 9:14-15]]**
    ![[Hebrews 9#^v14]]
    ![[Hebrews 9#^v15]]
    He is the mediator of the new testament, that by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions that were under the first testament.

**Luke 23:47-49**

![[Luke 23#^v47]]
![[Luke 23#^v48]]
![[Luke 23#^v49]]

- The centurion glorified God, saying, Certainly this was a righteous man, and all the people that came together to that sight smote their breasts and returned.

**Luke 23:50-56**

![[Luke 23#^v50]]
![[Luke 23#^v51]]
![[Luke 23#^v52]]
![[Luke 23#^v53]]
![[Luke 23#^v54]]
![[Luke 23#^v55]]
![[Luke 23#^v56]]

- Joseph of Arimathaea, a good man and a just, had not consented to their counsel and deed, and begged the body and laid it in a sepulchre hewn in stone.
- He was buried the same day, which is the custom written in the law. There is no waiting days over a body.
- The women returned, and prepared spices and ointments; and rested the sabbath day according to the commandment. That is written after the death, in the new testament.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 21#^v22|Deuteronomy 21:22-23]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 21#^v22]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 21#^v23]]
    His body shall not remain all night upon the tree, but thou shalt in any wise bury him that day.

# Luke 24: The Resurrection and the Opening of the Scriptures

Read the chapter → [[Luke 24]]

**Luke 24:1-8**

![[Luke 24#^v1]]
![[Luke 24#^v2]]
![[Luke 24#^v3]]
![[Luke 24#^v4]]
![[Luke 24#^v5]]
![[Luke 24#^v6]]
![[Luke 24#^v7]]
![[Luke 24#^v8]]

- Upon the first day of the week, very early in the morning, they came unto the sepulchre. The first day of the week is the day after the sabbath, not the sabbath itself.
- Why seek ye the living among the dead? He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee.

**Luke 24:9-12**

![[Luke 24#^v9]]
![[Luke 24#^v10]]
![[Luke 24#^v11]]
![[Luke 24#^v12]]

- Their words seemed to them as idle tales, and they believed them not. Peter ran to the sepulchre, saw the linen clothes laid by themselves, and departed wondering.

**Luke 24:13-16**

![[Luke 24#^v13]]
![[Luke 24#^v14]]
![[Luke 24#^v15]]
![[Luke 24#^v16]]

- Jesus himself drew near and went with them, but their eyes were holden that they should not know him.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Mark 16#^v12|Mark 16:12]]**
    ![[Mark 16#^v12]]
    After that he appeared in another form unto two of them, as they walked, and went into the country.

**Luke 24:17-24**

![[Luke 24#^v17]]
![[Luke 24#^v18]]
![[Luke 24#^v19]]
![[Luke 24#^v20]]
![[Luke 24#^v21]]
![[Luke 24#^v22]]
![[Luke 24#^v23]]
![[Luke 24#^v24]]

- Cleopas answers him as a stranger, and rehearses the whole thing: we trusted that it had been he which should have redeemed Israel.
- They walked and talked with him and did not know who he was, which is the plainest picture of what the epistle warns of.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Hebrews 13#^v1|Hebrews 13:1-2]]**
    ![[Hebrews 13#^v1]]
    ![[Hebrews 13#^v2]]
    Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares.

**Luke 24:25-27**

![[Luke 24#^v25]]
![[Luke 24#^v26]]
![[Luke 24#^v27]]

- O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken.
- Beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself. What he opened to them was the writings already given.

**Luke 24:28-32**

![[Luke 24#^v28]]
![[Luke 24#^v29]]
![[Luke 24#^v30]]
![[Luke 24#^v31]]
![[Luke 24#^v32]]

- Their eyes were opened, and they knew him; and he vanished out of their sight.
- Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures?

**Luke 24:33-43**

![[Luke 24#^v33]]
![[Luke 24#^v34]]
![[Luke 24#^v35]]
![[Luke 24#^v36]]
![[Luke 24#^v37]]
![[Luke 24#^v38]]
![[Luke 24#^v39]]
![[Luke 24#^v40]]
![[Luke 24#^v41]]
![[Luke 24#^v42]]
![[Luke 24#^v43]]

- He stood in the midst of them and said, Peace be unto you, and they were terrified, supposing they had seen a spirit.
- Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have.
- They gave him a piece of a broiled fish, and of an honeycomb, and he took it, and did eat before them.

**Luke 24:44-49**

![[Luke 24#^v44]]
![[Luke 24#^v45]]
![[Luke 24#^v46]]
![[Luke 24#^v47]]
![[Luke 24#^v48]]
![[Luke 24#^v49]]

- All things must be fulfilled, which were written in the law of Moses, and in the prophets, and in the psalms, concerning me. He has not yet come back, so those writings are not finished.
- Then opened he their understanding, that they might understand the scriptures.
- That repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem. It goes among all nations because the people it is for were carried away into all nations.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Luke 21#^v24|Luke 21:24]]**
    ![[Luke 21#^v24]]
    They shall fall by the edge of the sword, and shall be led away captive into all nations.

**Luke 24:50-53**

![[Luke 24#^v50]]
![[Luke 24#^v51]]
![[Luke 24#^v52]]
![[Luke 24#^v53]]

- He led them out as far as to Bethany, lifted up his hands, and blessed them; and while he blessed them, he was parted from them, and carried up into heaven.
