# Nehemiah 11: Repopulating the Holy City

Read the chapter → [[Nehemiah 11]]

## Introduction

This session covers the last three chapters of Nehemiah and then opens the book of Esther. [[Nehemiah 11|Nehemiah 11]] and 12 are largely registers of names, the record of who came back and settled Jerusalem, who served as priests and Levites, and how they dedicated the wall with thanksgiving and singing. These are our ancestors, and this is the book of records; a man's name has to be found written in the book to have a part in the inheritance. [[Nehemiah 13|Nehemiah 13]] turns to reform, cutting off the mixed multitude, guarding the Sabbath from buying and selling, and putting away strange wives that had turned the people's hearts and their children's speech away from the law. The thread through it all is order restored according to the covenant given by Moses. Read these chapters as the record of a people applying their laws again once they came back into their land.

    ![[Isaiah 3#^v16]]

# Nehemiah 13: Cleansing the People, the Sabbath, and the Strange Wives

![[Nehemiah 13#^v1]]
![[Nehemiah 13#^v2]]
![[Nehemiah 13#^v3]]

- On that day they read in the books of Moses and found written that the Ammonite and the Moabite should not come into the congregation of God for ever, because they met not Israel with bread and water but hired Balaam against them to curse them, and the Most High turned the curse into a blessing.
- When the people heard the law, they separated from Israel all the mixed multitude. The reading of the law produced immediate obedience, cutting off what the law said to cut off.

    ![[Numbers 22#^v5]]

    ![[Deuteronomy 23#^v3]]

- Nehemiah saw men in Judah treading wine presses on the Sabbath, bringing in sheaves, lading asses, and bringing wine, grapes, and figs into Jerusalem on the Sabbath day, and men of Tyre selling fish and all manner of ware on the Sabbath.
- He contended with the nobles of Judah and asked what evil thing this was to profane the Sabbath day, reminding them that their fathers did the same and the Most High brought this evil upon the city, and now they brought more wrath upon Israel. Buying and selling on the Sabbath is breaking the commandment.

    ![[Exodus 20#^v8]]
    ![[Exodus 20#^v9]]
    ![[Exodus 20#^v10]]
    The commandment to remember the Sabbath day to keep it holy and do no work, which the Sabbath commerce directly transgressed.

    ![[Jeremiah 17#^v21]]
    ![[Jeremiah 17#^v22]]
    The Most High commanded to bear no burden on the Sabbath day nor bring it in by the gates of Jerusalem, the very burdens Nehemiah shut the gates against.

**[[Nehemiah 13#^v19|Nehemiah 13:19-22]]**

![[Nehemiah 13#^v19]]
![[Nehemiah 13#^v20]]
![[Nehemiah 13#^v21]]
![[Nehemiah 13#^v22]]

- When the gates of Jerusalem began to be dark before the Sabbath, Nehemiah commanded them shut and set his servants at the gates so no burden would be brought in on the Sabbath, and the merchants lodged outside Jerusalem once or twice.
- He testified against them and warned that if they came again he would lay hands on them, and from that time they came no more on the Sabbath. This laying on of hands is not a blessing but a serious warning, and the merchants knew he was not joking. He then commanded the Levites to cleanse themselves and keep the gates to sanctify the Sabbath day.

- Nehemiah saw Jews that had married wives of Ashdod, Ammon, and Moab, and their children spake half in the speech of Ashdod and could not speak the Jews' language, but according to the language of each people.
- Because the fathers were not truly in the house, the children learned their mother's tongue and customs instead of Hebrew and the law. Nehemiah contended with them, cursed them, smote certain of them, plucked off their hair, and made them swear not to give or take daughters in marriage with those nations, reminding them that Solomon himself, though beloved of his God, was caused to sin by outlandish women.

- One of the sons of Joiada, son of Eliashib the high priest, was son in law to Sanballat the Horonite, so Nehemiah chased him away because he had defiled the priesthood and the covenant of the priesthood and of the Levites.
- Thus Nehemiah cleansed the people from all strangers, appointed the wards of the priests and Levites every one in his business, and provided for the wood offering and the firstfruits, closing the book with the prayer, Remember me, O my God, for good. This is the book of records, and to see it as history rather than religion is to understand who these people are.
