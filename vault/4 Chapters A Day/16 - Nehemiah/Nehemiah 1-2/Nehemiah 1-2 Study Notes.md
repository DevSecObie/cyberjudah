# Nehemiah 1: Nehemiah's Prayer for Jerusalem

Read the chapter → [[Nehemiah 1]]

## Introduction

This session moves from Ezra into [[Nehemiah 1|Nehemiah 1]] and 2. Nehemiah comes about twelve years after Ezra, so there is again a time gap between the two men. Nehemiah is the Persian king's cupbearer, and when he hears that the remnant in Judah is in affliction and the wall of Jerusalem is broken down, he weeps, fasts, and prays, confessing the sins of Israel and pleading the promise of gathering. The Most High moves the king to send him to rebuild, with letters and an armed escort, and the heathen rulers are grieved that a man has come to seek the welfare of the children of Israel. The thread is that Nehemiah, like Ezra, gives all the glory to the good hand of the Most High upon him, never to his own wisdom, and is not ashamed of the work he came to do.

**[[Nehemiah 1#^v1|Nehemiah 1:1-3]]**

![[Nehemiah 1#^v1]]
![[Nehemiah 1#^v2]]
![[Nehemiah 1#^v3]]

- These are the words of Nehemiah the son of Hachaliah. In the month Chisleu, in the twentieth year, as he was in the palace, Hanani one of his brethren comes with certain men of Judah, and Nehemiah asks concerning the Jews that had escaped the captivity and concerning Jerusalem.
- They tell him the remnant left of the captivity in the province are in great affliction and reproach, the wall of Jerusalem is broken down, and its gates are burned with fire.

**[[Nehemiah 1#^v4|Nehemiah 1:4-6]]**

![[Nehemiah 1#^v4]]
![[Nehemiah 1#^v5]]
![[Nehemiah 1#^v6]]

- When Nehemiah hears these words he sits down, weeps, mourns certain days, and fasts and prays before the God of heaven.
- He beseeches the Lord God of heaven, the great and terrible God that keeps covenant and mercy for them that love him and keep his commandments, that his ear would be attentive and his eyes open.
- He asks that the Most High hear the prayer he prays day and night for the children of Israel, and he confesses the sins of the children of Israel which they have sinned, both himself and his father's house.

**[[Nehemiah 1#^v7|Nehemiah 1:7-9]]**

![[Nehemiah 1#^v7]]
![[Nehemiah 1#^v8]]
![[Nehemiah 1#^v9]]

- He confesses they have dealt very corruptly and not kept the commandments, statutes, or judgments the Most High commanded his servant Moses.
- He pleads the word the Most High commanded Moses, that if they transgress he will scatter them abroad among the nations, but if they turn and keep his commandments and do them, though they were cast out to the uttermost part of heaven, he will gather them from thence and bring them to the place he has chosen to set his name.
- This is Nehemiah quoting [[Deuteronomy 28|Deuteronomy 28]], holding the Most High to what Moses had already taught in the wilderness, both the scattering and the gathering.

  - **[[Deuteronomy 30#^v1|Deuteronomy 30:1-4]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 30#^v1]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 30#^v2]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 30#^v3]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 30#^v4]]
    When the blessing and the curse are come upon them and they return to the Most High among the nations, he promises to gather them from all the peoples where he scattered them, even from the outmost parts of heaven, the very promise Nehemiah pleads.

**[[Nehemiah 1#^v10|Nehemiah 1:10-11]]**

![[Nehemiah 1#^v10]]
![[Nehemiah 1#^v11]]

- These are the servants and the people whom the Most High has redeemed by his great power and strong hand, and Nehemiah asks that the Lord's ear be attentive to the prayer of his servant and of the servants who desire to fear his name.
- He asks the Most High to prosper his servant this day and grant him mercy in the sight of this man, for he was the king's cupbearer, whose job was to serve the wine and keep a joyous countenance before the king.

# Nehemiah 2: The King Sends Nehemiah to Rebuild

Read the chapter → [[Nehemiah 2]]

**[[Nehemiah 2#^v1|Nehemiah 2:1-2]]**

![[Nehemiah 2#^v1]]
![[Nehemiah 2#^v2]]

- In the month Nisan in the twentieth year of Artaxerxes the king, wine is before him and Nehemiah takes up the wine and gives it to the king. This is Artaxerxes the first, the king that came after Ahasuerus of the book of Esther.
- Nehemiah had not before been sad in the king's presence, for a cupbearer must be of a cheerful countenance, so the king asks why his countenance is sad seeing he is not sick, saying this is nothing but sorrow of heart, and Nehemiah is very sore afraid.

**[[Nehemiah 2#^v3|Nehemiah 2:3-4]]**

![[Nehemiah 2#^v3]]
![[Nehemiah 2#^v4]]

- Nehemiah answers, Let the king live for ever, why should not his countenance be sad when the city, the place of his fathers' sepulchres, lies waste and its gates are consumed with fire.
- The king asks what request he makes, and Nehemiah prays to the God of heaven before he answers, seeking the Most High even in that moment before the king.

**[[Nehemiah 2#^v5|Nehemiah 2:5-6]]**

![[Nehemiah 2#^v5]]
![[Nehemiah 2#^v6]]

- Nehemiah asks that if it please the king and if his servant have found favour, the king would send him to Judah, to the city of his fathers' sepulchres, that he may build it.
- The king, the queen also sitting by him, asks how long his journey would be and when he would return, so Nehemiah sets him a time, and it pleased the king to send him.

**[[Nehemiah 2#^v7|Nehemiah 2:7-8]]**

![[Nehemiah 2#^v7]]
![[Nehemiah 2#^v8]]

- Nehemiah asks for letters to the governors beyond the river to convey him until he comes into Judah, and a letter to Asaph the keeper of the king's forest to give him timber for the beams of the gates, the wall, and the house he would enter.
- The king grants him all this according to the good hand of his God upon him. Nehemiah had already prayed, and the Most High heard him and put it upon the king's heart to allow him to go.

  - **[[Jeremiah 15#^v11|Jeremiah 15:11]]**
    ![[Jeremiah 15#^v11]]
    The Most High says he will cause the enemy to entreat his remnant well in the time of evil; it is not that the heathen love Israel, but that the Lord moves a king or a friend to deal well with them.
  - **[[1 Esdras 8#^v25|1 Esdras 8:25]]**
    ![[1 Esdras 8#^v25]]
    Ezra blessed the Most High for putting it into the heart of the king, showing the good the heathen ruler does is the Lord moving him, not his own love for Israel.

**[[Nehemiah 2#^v9|Nehemiah 2:9-10]]**

![[Nehemiah 2#^v9]]
![[Nehemiah 2#^v10]]

- Nehemiah comes to the governors beyond the river and gives them the king's letters, and the king had sent captains of the army and horsemen with him, an armed escort to see him safe.
- When Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, hear of it, it grieves them exceedingly that a man has come to seek the welfare of the children of Israel, the same opposition seen when the nation tried to rebuild before.

  - **[[Ezra 4#^v4|Ezra 4:4-5]]**
    ![[Ezra 4#^v4]]
    ![[Ezra 4#^v5]]
    The people of the land weakened the hands of Judah, troubled them in building, and hired counsellors against them to frustrate their purpose, the same hindering spirit against the rebuilding.
  - **[[Ezra 4#^v12|Ezra 4:12-13]]**
    ![[Ezra 4#^v12]]
    ![[Ezra 4#^v13]]
    The enemies wrote to the king that if the rebellious city were built and the walls set up, the Jews would no more pay toll, tribute, and custom, and so damage the revenue of kings, the same mindset that fears Israel's rebuilding.

**[[Nehemiah 2#^v11|Nehemiah 2:11-16]]**

![[Nehemiah 2#^v11]]
![[Nehemiah 2#^v12]]
![[Nehemiah 2#^v13]]
![[Nehemiah 2#^v14]]
![[Nehemiah 2#^v15]]
![[Nehemiah 2#^v16]]

- Nehemiah comes to Jerusalem and is there three days, then arises in the night with a few men, telling no man what his God had put in his heart to do, for saying too much could ruin the plan.
- He goes out by night through the gate of the valley, past the dragon well to the dung port, and views the walls of Jerusalem broken down and the gates consumed with fire, then goes on to the gate of the fountain and the king's pool.
- The rulers do not know where he went or what he did, neither had he yet told the Jews, priests, nobles, rulers, or the rest that did the work.

**[[Nehemiah 2#^v17|Nehemiah 2:17-18]]**

![[Nehemiah 2#^v17]]
![[Nehemiah 2#^v18]]

- Nehemiah then tells them, Ye see the distress that we are in, how Jerusalem lies waste and its gates are burned; come and let us build up the wall of Jerusalem that we be no more a reproach.
- He tells them of the good hand of his God upon him and the king's words, and they say, Let us rise up and build, and strengthen their hands for this good work.
- What is admirable in Nehemiah, as in Ezra, is that he never says the work was of his own knowledge or wisdom, but that the Lord's hand was upon him, and he puts that same vision on the men around him so they strengthen their hands to help.

**[[Nehemiah 2#^v19|Nehemiah 2:19-20]]**

![[Nehemiah 2#^v19]]
![[Nehemiah 2#^v20]]

- When Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite, and Geshem the Arabian hear it, they laugh them to scorn, despise them, and ask what this thing is, and whether they will rebel against the king.
- Nehemiah answers that the God of heaven will prosper them, and his servants will arise and build, but the mockers have no portion nor right nor memorial in Jerusalem. There was no shame in what he came to do.
