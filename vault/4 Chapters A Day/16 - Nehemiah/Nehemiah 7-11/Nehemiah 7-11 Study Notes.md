# Nehemiah 7: The Register of Those Who Came Up First

Read the chapter → [[Nehemiah 7]]

This session covers [[Nehemiah 7|Nehemiah 7]] through 11, the setting of Jerusalem back in order after the wall was finished. The chapter opens with a long register of the families, priests, Levites, singers, porters, and Nethinims who first came up out of captivity, the same account recorded in [[Ezra 2|Ezra 2]]. From there the reading moves to Ezra opening the book of the law before all the people at the water gate, the keeping of the Feast of Booths, the Levites recounting the whole history of the Most High's mercy toward a stubborn people, and the sure covenant the princes, Levites, and priests sealed to keep the law. The opening precepts mark up how the Most High deals with a people He gathers, corrects, and restores.

- This register matches the one recorded in [[Ezra 2|Ezra 2]]. Every name means something, and Israel kept a careful record of exactly who returned to the land.

# Nehemiah 8: Ezra Reads the Law at the Water Gate

Read the chapter → [[Nehemiah 8]]

**[[Nehemiah 8#^v1|Nehemiah 8:1-3]]**
![[Nehemiah 8#^v1]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v2]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v3]]
- All the people gather as one man into the street before the water gate and ask Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses. He reads before the congregation of men, women, and all that could understand, from morning until midday.
- The Most High does not favor camps and closed rooms. Ezra stood in the open street to teach, men and women together, and the people were attentive to the words of the law.

**[[Nehemiah 8#^v4|Nehemiah 8:4-8]]**
![[Nehemiah 8#^v4]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v5]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v6]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v7]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v8]]
- Ezra stands upon a pulpit of wood with brothers on his right hand and brothers on his left hand, and opens the book in the sight of all the people, blessing the Most High, to whom the people answer, Amen, Amen.
- The Levites cause the people to understand the law, reading distinctly and giving the sense so the people understood the reading. Teaching is meant to bring understanding, not merely to be recited.

**[[Nehemiah 8#^v9|Nehemiah 8:9-12]]**
![[Nehemiah 8#^v9]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v10]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v11]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v12]]
- Nehemiah, Ezra, and the Levites tell the people the day is holy and they should not mourn or weep, for the people wept when they heard the words of the law.
- They are told to eat the fat, drink the sweet, and send portions to those for whom nothing is prepared, for the joy of the Most High is their strength. The people go their way to make great mirth because they understood the words that were declared.

**[[Nehemiah 8#^v13|Nehemiah 8:13-18]]**
![[Nehemiah 8#^v13]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v14]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v15]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v16]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v17]]
![[Nehemiah 8#^v18]]
- On the second day the chief of the fathers, the priests, and the Levites gather to Ezra to understand the words of the law, and they find written that Israel should dwell in booths in the feast of the seventh month.
  - **[[Leviticus 23#^v42|Leviticus 23:42-43]]**
    ![[Leviticus 23#^v42]]
    ![[Leviticus 23#^v43]]
    Israel is commanded to dwell in booths seven days so their generations may know the Most High made them dwell in booths when He brought them out of Egypt.
- The people go out, fetch branches, and make themselves booths, keeping the feast as it had not been kept since the days of Joshua. Ezra reads in the book of the law day by day, and they keep the solemn assembly.

# Nehemiah 9: The Levites Recount the Mercy of the Most High

Read the chapter → [[Nehemiah 9]]

**[[Nehemiah 9#^v1|Nehemiah 9:1-3]]**
![[Nehemiah 9#^v1]]
![[Nehemiah 9#^v2]]
![[Nehemiah 9#^v3]]
- On the twenty and fourth day the children of Israel assemble with fasting, sackcloth, and earth upon them, and the seed of Israel separate themselves from all strangers, standing to confess their sins and the iniquities of their fathers.
- They read in the book of the law a fourth part of the day and another fourth part they confess and worship the Most High, keeping the separation the law required.

**[[Nehemiah 9#^v4|Nehemiah 9:4-6]]**
![[Nehemiah 9#^v4]]
![[Nehemiah 9#^v5]]
![[Nehemiah 9#^v6]]
- The Levites stand up and cry with a loud voice, blessing the glorious name of the Most High who is exalted above all blessing and praise.
- They confess that the Most High alone made heaven, the earth, the seas, and all that is in them, and the host of heaven worships Him.

- The Levites recount how the Most High chose Abram, brought him out of Ur, made covenant with him, saw the affliction in Egypt, divided the sea, and led Israel by the pillar of cloud and fire.
- He came down upon mount Sinai and spake from heaven, giving right judgments, true laws, good statutes, and commandments, and made known the holy sabbath. Moses was not on the mount only for the ten commandments but received the statutes, the laws, and the precepts, and was so in tune with the Most High that he knew what Jacob prophesied over his sons in the last days.
  - **[[Genesis 49#^v1|Genesis 49:1]]**
    ![[Genesis 49#^v1]]
    Jacob gathers his sons to tell them what shall befall them in the last days, which Moses later recorded as a prophet who could show the beginning and the end.

- The Levites confess that the fathers dealt proudly, hardened their necks, and made a molten calf, saying, This is thy god that brought thee up out of Egypt, working great provocations.
- Yet the Most High in His manifold mercies forsook them not in the wilderness, the pillar of cloud and fire departing not, giving His good spirit, manna, and water, so that they lacked nothing forty years.

- When they cried in their trouble He heard them from heaven and delivered them many times according to His mercies, yet He did not utterly consume them, for He is a gracious and merciful God. This same stiff-necked resisting is what Stephen charged the fathers with in Acts.

- They are servants in the land the Most High gave their fathers, its increase going to the kings set over them for their sins, so they make a sure covenant, write it, and their princes, Levites, and priests seal unto it.

- Those that sealed the covenant are named, beginning with Nehemiah the Tirshatha, then the priests, the Levites, and the chief of the people.
- The sealing binds the leaders first, so the covenant to keep the law is entered by name and by house.

**[[Nehemiah 10#^v28|Nehemiah 10:28-29]]**

- The rest of the people, the priests, Levites, porters, singers, Nethinims, and all who had separated themselves from the peoples of the lands unto the law of the Most High, join with their nobles.
- They enter into a curse and an oath to walk in the law of the Most High given by Moses and to observe and do all His commandments, judgments, and statutes.

**[[Nehemiah 10#^v30|Nehemiah 10:30-31]]**
![[Nehemiah 10#^v30]]

- They swear that they will not give their daughters to the people of the land nor take their daughters for their sons, keeping the seed separate from mixed marriages.
- They will not buy on the sabbath or the holy day and will leave the seventh year and the exaction of every debt.

![[Nehemiah 10#^v32]]

- They make ordinances to charge themselves yearly for the service of the house of the Most High: the wood offering, the firstfruits of their ground and trees, the firstborn of their sons and cattle, and the tithes to the Levites.
- The tithes of the ground go to the Levites, for the Levite was the one ordained to receive them. A pastor over a church today is most likely not a Levite, so the pattern of who receives the tithe was set by the law, not by the office men give themselves.
