# Psalm 31: In Thee O Lord Do I Put My Trust

Read the chapter → [[Psalms 31]]

This portion of the "4 Chapters a Day" session moves through [[Psalms 31|Psalms 31]] through 34. The book of Psalms is Hebrew poetry that expresses the spirit and emotions of the true believers of the Most High and His son Christ, comprised of the trust our forefathers had, the prayers they sent up, the deep desire and delight they had in the Most High, the prayer for vengeance against our enemies, and the protection for the nation of Israel. When we go through adversity in this truth, we should revert back to the book of Psalms, because there is something in it that directly relates to whatever we are going through. Before the reading, the session frames prayer itself: Daniel kneeled on his knees three times a day and prayed and gave thanks before his God, and we as the nation of Israel coming back into our heritage have to strive to put prayer into our daily plan, banging on the doors of the Most High and aggravating Him with our prayers so we can get out of this captivity, praying for the right things such as vengeance and destruction of this place rather than vain things.

    Daniel prayed and gave thanks on his knees three times a day even after the writing was signed, showing the prayer habit we must strive for.

- In thee O Lord do I put my trust, let me never be ashamed, deliver me in thy righteousness. This is a psalm of David dealing with his commitment to the Most High.
- If we are doing the righteousness of the Most High, which is keeping His commandments, we will not be put to shame, and that is why David prays this thing.
