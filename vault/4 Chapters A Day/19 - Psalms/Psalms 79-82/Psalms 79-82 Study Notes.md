# Psalm 79: The Heathen Have Defiled Thy Inheritance

Read the chapter → [[Psalms 79]]

This session reads through [[Psalms 79|Psalms 79]] through 82, four short chapters a day for the nation. The teacher notes these are quick chapters, none longer than sixteen or so verses, and that today is mostly reading with a few precepts opened at the end from questions. He frames where we are in the whole reading: the class started in Genesis chapter one verse one a few months back, and now at [[Psalms 79|Psalm 79]] we are about halfway through the Bible. [[Psalms 79|Psalm 79]] is a cry over the heathen coming into the inheritance, defiling the temple, and laying Jerusalem on heaps, and a plea for the Most High to render sevenfold to the neighbors that reproached Him.

**[[Psalms 79#^v1|Psalms 79:1-4]]**

![[Psalms 79#^v1]]
![[Psalms 79#^v2]]
![[Psalms 79#^v3]]
![[Psalms 79#^v4]]

- The heathen have come into the inheritance of the Most High, defiled His holy temple, and laid Jerusalem on heaps. The dead bodies of His servants have been given as meat to the fowls of heaven and the flesh of His saints to the beasts of the earth, their blood shed like water round about Jerusalem with none to bury them.
- This is not a one-time thing. It happened before David a few times, when Babylon besieged us first, again during the time of the Greeks who destroyed our land, and again in 70 A.D. when we were pushed out of the land. During the Greeks not all of us were pushed out and taken captive in other nations like with Babylon and 70 A.D.
- Even during the time of kings, our forefather King Zedekiah was taken into captivity, he and his sons and daughters. A remnant was taken captive in Babylon, which is why Daniel was born there, and they destroyed the temple and the city.
- We are become a reproach to our neighbors, a scorn and a derision to them that are round about us. The neighbors in this context are the enemies, the other nations, the heathen. You know this because verse one already established that the heathen came in and defiled the temple, so the neighbors reproaching us are those same nations.

**[[Psalms 79#^v5|Psalms 79:5-7]]**

![[Psalms 79#^v5]]

- The prayer asks how long the Most High will be angry, and whether His jealousy shall burn like fire forever.
- It asks Him to pour out His wrath upon the heathen that have not known Him and upon the kingdoms that have not called upon His name, for they have devoured Jacob and laid waste his dwelling place.
- The heathen having "not known thee" does not mean there are heathen who do know the Lord. To know God is to keep His commandments, and the commandments were only given to the israelites. The other nations do not truly know God, His ways, or His judgments; we do.

**[[Psalms 79#^v8|Psalms 79:8-10]]**

![[Psalms 79#^v8]]
![[Psalms 79#^v9]]
![[Psalms 79#^v10]]

- The plea is that the Most High remember not against us former iniquities, that His tender mercies speedily prevent us, for we are brought very low.
- It asks Him to help us, O God of our salvation, for the glory of His name, to deliver us and purge away our sins for His name's sake.
- It asks why the heathen should say, Where is their God, and that He be known among the heathen in our sight by avenging the blood of His servants which is shed.

**[[Psalms 79#^v11|Psalms 79:11-13]]**

![[Psalms 79#^v11]]
![[Psalms 79#^v12]]
![[Psalms 79#^v13]]

- Let the sighing of the prisoner come before Him, and according to the greatness of His power let Him preserve those that are appointed to die.
- Render unto our neighbors sevenfold into their bosom the reproach wherewith they have reproached the Lord. The neighbors here in verse twelve, like verse four, are the other nations. You know this because verse thirteen turns to the redemption of the people.
- So we His people and sheep of His pasture will give Him thanks forever and show forth His praise to all generations. This goes into when the Most High redeems us from the hands of our enemies; we will give thanks forever when we get into the kingdom.

# Psalm 80: The Vine Brought Out of Egypt

Read the chapter → [[Psalms 80]]

[[Psalms 80|Psalm 80]] cries out to the Shepherd of Israel to turn us again and cause His face to shine that we may be saved. The heavy point in this chapter is the vine: the Most High brought a vine out of Egypt, cast out the heathen, and planted it. That vine is Israel. We are that vine that the Lord brought out of Egypt.

**[[Psalms 80#^v1|Psalms 80:1-3]]**

![[Psalms 80#^v1]]
![[Psalms 80#^v2]]
![[Psalms 80#^v3]]

- The prayer calls on the Shepherd of Israel that leads Joseph like a flock and dwells between the cherubims to shine forth before Ephraim, Benjamin, and Manasseh.
- It asks Him to stir up His strength and come and save us, to turn us again and cause His face to shine that we shall be saved.

**[[Psalms 80#^v4|Psalms 80:4-7]]**

![[Psalms 80#^v4]]
![[Psalms 80#^v5]]
![[Psalms 80#^v6]]
![[Psalms 80#^v7]]

- It asks the Lord God of hosts how long He will be angry against the prayer of His people. He has fed them with the bread of tears and given them tears to drink in great measure.
- The bread of tears goes into affliction, being afflicted because of our oppression. Isaiah speaks of the Lord giving us the bread of adversity and the water of affliction.
- He has made us a strife unto our neighbors, and our enemies laugh among themselves. Again the refrain: turn us again, O God of hosts, and cause Thy face to shine, and we shall be saved.
- Causing His face to shine goes into God having mercy on us, loving us, giving us strength, being on our behalf and fighting for us. It is like when Moses came down from the mountain and his face shone.
  - **[[Isaiah 30#^v20|Isaiah 30:20]]**
    ![[Isaiah 30#^v20]]
    The Lord gives the bread of adversity and the water of affliction.

**[[Psalms 80#^v8|Psalms 80:8-11]]**

![[Psalms 80#^v8]]
![[Psalms 80#^v9]]
![[Psalms 80#^v10]]
![[Psalms 80#^v11]]

- The Most High brought a vine out of Egypt, cast out the heathen, and planted it. This is the israelites; we are that vine the Lord brought out of Egypt. He cast out the canaanites and planted us in Jerusalem and the land of Israel.
- He prepared room before it and caused it to take deep root, and it filled the land. This is how we grew as a nation.
- The hills were covered with the shadow of it, the boughs were like the goodly cedars, and she sent out her boughs unto the sea and her branches unto the river.

**[[Psalms 80#^v12|Psalms 80:12-15]]**

![[Psalms 80#^v12]]
![[Psalms 80#^v13]]
![[Psalms 80#^v14]]
![[Psalms 80#^v15]]

- Why then has He broken down her hedges, so that all they which pass by the way do pluck her? The "her" is Israel, that same vine brought out of Egypt.
- The boar out of the wood wastes it, and the wild beast of the field devours it.
- The plea is that the God of hosts return, look down from heaven, behold, and visit this vine and the vineyard which His right hand has planted, and the branch that He made strong for Himself.

**[[Psalms 80#^v16|Psalms 80:16-19]]**

![[Psalms 80#^v16]]
![[Psalms 80#^v17]]
![[Psalms 80#^v18]]
![[Psalms 80#^v19]]

- It is burned with fire and cut down; they perish at the rebuke of His countenance. His countenance is the face of God. When God came down in Exodus twenty, the people were in fear at His presence, not seeing His literal face but in fear because He is an omnipotent power.
- Let His hand be upon the man of His right hand, upon the son of man whom He made strong for Himself; so will we not go back from Him. Quicken us and we will call upon Thy name.
- The closing refrain once more: turn us again, O Lord God of hosts, cause Thy face to shine, and we shall be saved. Causing His face to shine is Him taking us out of captivity and blessing us, the opposite of when He set His face against us.

# Psalm 81: Open Thy Mouth Wide and I Will Fill It

Read the chapter → [[Psalms 81]]

[[Psalms 81|Psalm 81]] calls the nation to sing aloud unto God our strength, remembering that this was a statute for Israel and a law of the God of Jacob. The Most High recounts how He relieved our shoulder from the burden in Egypt, and then grieves that His people would not hearken to His voice. The chapter turns on the promise: open thy mouth wide and I will fill it, and the finest of the wheat and honey out of the rock He would have satisfied us with.

**[[Psalms 81#^v1|Psalms 81:1-4]]**

![[Psalms 81#^v1]]
![[Psalms 81#^v2]]
![[Psalms 81#^v3]]
![[Psalms 81#^v4]]

- Sing aloud unto God our strength; make a joyful noise unto the God of Jacob. Take a psalm, bring the timbrel, the pleasant harp with the psaltery.
- Blow up the trumpet in the new moon, in the time appointed, on our solemn feast day. This was a statute for Israel and a law of the God of Jacob.

**[[Psalms 81#^v5|Psalms 81:5-7]]**

![[Psalms 81#^v5]]
![[Psalms 81#^v6]]
![[Psalms 81#^v7]]

- This He ordained in Joseph for a testimony when he went out through the land of Egypt, where he heard a language he understood not.
- The Most High removed his shoulder from the burden and his hands were delivered from the pots. He answered in trouble, in the secret place of thunder, and proved him at the waters of Meribah.

**[[Psalms 81#^v8|Psalms 81:8-10]]**

![[Psalms 81#^v8]]

![[Psalms 81#^v10]]

- Hear, O My people, and I will testify unto thee, O Israel, if thou wilt hearken unto Me: there shall no strange god be in thee, neither shalt thou worship any strange god.
- I am the Lord thy God which brought thee out of the land of Egypt; open thy mouth wide and I will fill it.

**[[Psalms 81#^v11|Psalms 81:11-13]]**

![[Psalms 81#^v11]]

- But My people would not hearken to My voice, and Israel would none of Me. So I gave them up unto their own hearts' lust, and they walked in their own counsels.
- The grief of it: Oh that My people had hearkened unto Me and Israel had walked in My ways.

**[[Psalms 81#^v14|Psalms 81:14-16]]**

![[Psalms 81#^v14]]
![[Psalms 81#^v15]]
![[Psalms 81#^v16]]

- Then He should soon have subdued their enemies and turned His hand against their adversaries. The haters of the Lord should have submitted themselves unto Him, but their time should have endured forever.
- He would have fed them with the finest of the wheat and with honey out of the rock He would have satisfied them. The honey out of the rock goes into salvation and the future rest we were meant to have coming out of Egypt into Canaan, the land that became Israel.

# Psalm 82: God Standeth in the Congregation of the Mighty

Read the chapter → [[Psalms 82]]

[[Psalms 82|Psalm 82]] is a short, straight-to-the-point chapter. God stands in the congregation of the mighty and judges among the gods, rebuking unjust judgment and calling for justice to the poor and fatherless. He declares "ye are gods, children of the most High," yet they shall die like men, and closes with the call for God to arise and judge the earth, for He shall inherit all nations.

**[[Psalms 82#^v1|Psalms 82:1-4]]**

![[Psalms 82#^v1]]
![[Psalms 82#^v2]]
![[Psalms 82#^v3]]
![[Psalms 82#^v4]]

- God stands in the congregation of the mighty and judges among the gods. He asks how long they will judge unjustly and accept the persons of the wicked.
- The charge is to defend the poor and fatherless, do justice to the afflicted and needy, and deliver the poor and needy out of the hand of the wicked.

**[[Psalms 82#^v5|Psalms 82:5-7]]**

![[Psalms 82#^v5]]
![[Psalms 82#^v6]]
![[Psalms 82#^v7]]

- They know not, neither will they understand; they walk on in darkness, and all the foundations of the earth are out of course.
- He has said, Ye are gods, and all of you are children of the most High; but ye shall die like men and fall like one of the princes.

**[[Psalms 82#^v8|Psalms 82:8]]**

![[Psalms 82#^v8]]

- Arise, O God, judge the earth, for Thou shalt inherit all nations. This goes into the israelites, and Christ first and foremost, receiving all nations as our inheritance and ruling over all nations.
  - **[[Psalms 2#^v8|Psalms 2:8]]**
    ![[Psalms 2#^v8]]
    Ask of Me and I shall give thee the heathen for thine inheritance and the uttermost parts of the earth.
