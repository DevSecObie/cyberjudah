# 2 Esdras 1: Shew My People Their Sinful Deeds

Read the chapter → [[2 Esdras 1]]

## Introduction

This session reads 1 Esdras 9 through 2 Esdras 3; these notes cover 2 Esdras 1 through 3, which close the reading. It is a reading session, so the chapters are read straight through and the notes follow the text. The book opens with Esdras sent to shew the people their sinful deeds and to recount everything the Most High did for them from Egypt forward, moves to the mother that bare them and the great people seen upon mount Sion with crowns, and ends with Esdras troubled upon his bed in Babylon, asking why the nation that holds Zion should be counted better than Zion.

- In the thirtieth year after the ruin of the city I was in Babylon, and lay troubled upon my bed, and my thoughts came up over my heart.
- I saw the desolation of Sion, and the wealth of them that dwelt at Babylon; and my spirit was sore moved, so that I began to speak words full of fear to the most High.
- Thou gavest a body unto Adam without soul, which was the workmanship of thine hands, and didst breathe into him the breath of life, and he was made living before thee.
- Unto him thou gavest commandment to love thy way, which he transgressed, and immediately thou appointedst death in him and in his generations.
- In process of time thou broughtest the flood upon those that dwelt in the world, and destroyedst them; nevertheless one of them thou leftest, namely Noah with his household, of whom came all righteous men.

- Thou didst choose thee a man from among them, whose name was Abraham: him thou lovedst, and made an everlasting covenant with him, promising him that thou wouldest never forsake his seed.
- Unto him thou gavest Isaac, and unto Isaac thou gavest Jacob and Esau: as for Jacob, thou didst choose him to thee, and put by Esau.
- When thou leddest his seed out of Egypt, thou broughtest them up to the mount Sinai, and bowing the heavens thou didst set fast the earth, and madest the depths to tremble.
- Thy glory went through four gates, of fire, and of earthquake, and of wind, and of cold; that thou mightest give the law unto the seed of Jacob, and diligence unto the generation of Israel.

- For the first Adam bearing a wicked heart transgressed, and was overcome; and so be all they that are born of him.

- Thou raisedst up a servant, called David, whom thou commandedst to build a city unto thy name, and to offer incense and oblations unto thee therein.
- When this was done many years, then they that inhabited the city forsook thee, and in all things did even as Adam and all his generations had done; and so thou gavest the city over into the hands of thine enemies.

- Are their deeds any better that inhabit Babylon, that they should therefore have the dominion over Sion?
- When I came thither, and had seen impieties without number, then my soul saw many evildoers in this thirtieth year, so that my heart failed me.
- I have seen how thou sufferest them sinning, and hast spared the wicked doers, and hast destroyed thy people, and hast preserved thine enemies.
- Are they then of Babylon better than they of Sion? or is there any other people that knoweth thee beside Israel? or what generation hath so believed thy covenants as Jacob?
- For I have gone here and there through the heathen, and I see that they flow in wealth, and think not upon thy commandments.
- Weigh thou therefore our wickedness now in the balance, and theirs also that dwell in the world; and so shall thy name no where be found but in Israel.
- Thou shalt find that Israel by name hath kept thy precepts, but not the heathen.
