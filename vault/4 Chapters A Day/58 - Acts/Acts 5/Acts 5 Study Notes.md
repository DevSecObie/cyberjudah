# Acts 5: All Things Common, and We Ought to Obey God Rather Than Men

Read the chapter → [[Acts 5]]

This is Chapter 5 of the Man Up Mondays series on the Acts of the Apostles. The teaching frames the whole book as the key that opens the understanding of Paul's letters to Galatia, Philippi, Corinth, Rome, and Thessalonica, because those letters were written to Israelites scattered through the Greek provinces under the captivity of Alexander and his generals. The people were hellenized: they took on the Greek language, dress, and gods, the same way our people were carried through the transatlantic slave trade into America and assimilated to its language, religion, and holidays. History repeats, so the counsel is to read Acts and the letters not with new eyes but as if you were there, seeing the same struggles under different slave masters. [[Acts 5|Acts 5]] continues straight from [[Acts 4|Acts 4]], where none among the believers lacked because the church was the center of the community.

**[[Acts 5#^v1|Acts 5:1-2]]**

![[Acts 5#^v1]]
![[Acts 5#^v2]]

- Ananias, with Sapphira his wife, sold a possession and kept back part of the price, his wife being privy to it, and brought only a portion and laid it at the apostles' feet.
- This continues from Acts 4: none among the believers lacked, because those who possessed lands or houses sold them, laid the money at the apostles' feet, and distribution was made to every man as he had need.
- The church was the epicenter of the community, where counsel was given and economic and community problems were resolved, the way the black Wall Street towns like Rosewood and Tulsa once kept the dollar circulating among our own people for months before it left.
- A community is a people with all things in common: common government, common heritage, common interest, and common culture, and the word community itself comes from the root "common." So-called black people today are not yet a community, divided by religion, politics, and color, until they come into the fullness of Christ.
- Money is only a tool to further the people and the ministry; use wisdom and give only as much as you have, so you do not sell everything and end up begging the congregation.

- Gamaliel warns the council to take heed what they do: before these days Theudas rose up boasting himself to be somebody, and about four hundred joined him, but he was slain and his followers scattered to nothing; after him Judas of Galilee drew people in the days of the taxing and also perished, and his followers were dispersed.
- The record of Josephus tells the same of Theudas, a magician who persuaded a great part of the people that he was a prophet who could divide the river Jordan, until horsemen fell on them, killed many, and beheaded him; examine such men and see what their work comes to.
- Therefore refrain from these men and let them alone: if this counsel or work be of men it will come to nought, but if it be of the Most High it cannot be overthrown, lest they be found to fight against God.
- Examine our works the same way: if the teaching reforms the brothers off the streets and restores our people's identity worldwide, it is of the Most High; the more they persecute, lock up, and kill, the faster prophecy is filled toward the number to be sealed, so every move is checkmate.
