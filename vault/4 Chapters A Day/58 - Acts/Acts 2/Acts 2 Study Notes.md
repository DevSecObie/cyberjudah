# Acts 2: Pentecost and the Scattered House of Israel

Read the chapter → [[Acts 2]]

This session of the Man Up Mondays series works through the whole of Acts chapter 2, the day of Pentecost, the pouring out of the Holy Ghost, and Peter's first sermon. Studying the book of Acts is what makes the writings of Paul and most of the New Testament open up, because the acts of the apostles are literally the acts they carried out on this earth to spread the gospel, and we are living those same acts in these last days. We are defenders of the gospel and defenders of the faith, so no apologist, riserist, or pastor should ever be able to confound us. The whole chapter is built to prove two things: that the law was still in full effect after Christ died, and that the tongues of Pentecost were the languages of the nations where the house of Israel had been scattered, one race of people dispersed to every country on the globe.

**[[Acts 2#^v1|Acts 2:1]]**
![[Acts 2#^v1]]
- The day of Pentecost being kept confounds the claim that after Christ died the law was done away with. If the ceremonial law was nailed to the cross, why did the disciples keep gathering for Pentecost, and why did Christ never once say to stop keeping Passover, the feasts, or the Sabbath.
- Christ was born around the feasts, killed through the Passover, went up at the feast of Tabernacles, and read in the synagogue every Sabbath as his custom. The law remained in effect after his death, and the disciples continued keeping the feast days written in [[Leviticus 23|Leviticus 23]].
- Pentecost comes from penta, meaning fifty, the fiftieth day counted from the Sabbath after Passover. It is also called the Feast of Weeks and the first fruits, and Israel kept it through every captivity, Assyrian, Babylonian, and Greek.

The glossolalia established in the churches in the late 1800s and 1900s is a pagan spirit giving utterance, the spirit of Satan, not the Holy Ghost, because you cannot learn or teach the knowledge of the Most High through babble no one can interpret.

The cloven tongues were the many languages of the lands where Israel was dispersed, and they spoke the word of the Most High in them like fire.

**[[Acts 2#^v5|Acts 2:5]]**

- Devout men, men who kept the laws, were dwelling at Jerusalem out of every nation under heaven, because Israel had been scattered into all the kingdoms of the earth. Only one race of people was prophesied to be dispersed to every country, and that is the reason men from every nation were present.
- The dictionaries define diaspora as the dispersion of the Jewish people beyond Israel, and the word itself originated in the Septuagint at [[Deuteronomy 28#^v25|Deuteronomy 28:25]]. There is no African diaspora that exists apart from this; the so-called African diaspora is the Israelite diaspora, because only Israel was scattered to all the earth by biblical prophecy.
- The historical record in A Tribute to the Negro says the Jews, though their features assimilated to the nations among whom they were scattered, descended from one stock, were forbidden to intermarry, and yet according to the divine prediction were dispersed into every country, marked with the colors of all: brown and swarthy in Turkey and Portugal, black in Spain, Syria, Chaldea, Arabia, Egypt, and at the Congo in Africa. Swarthy means dark and black, tawny means copper or brown, and olive is not "all colors," for a man of all colors would be a diseased spectacle, not a real man; Christ was black and brown.

- When Israel was liberated from Babylon by Cyrus, not everyone returned to Jerusalem; many migrated east and became the black Jews of Cochin, Hindustan, and India, who kept the sacred writings given before the captivity. The Second Commonwealth records this Babylonian Jewry coming into being through the Assyrian conquest of Israel in 719 BCE and the Babylonian conquest of Judea in 586 BCE.
- Today these same people still dwell in those lands under different names: Afro-Iranians in Iran, the Sheedis in Pakistan, black Jews in Afghanistan, and the Dalits and Cochin Jews in India.
