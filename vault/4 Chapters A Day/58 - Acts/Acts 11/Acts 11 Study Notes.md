# Acts 11: The Gentiles Are Scattered Israel

Read the chapter → [[Acts 11]]

This session of the Man Up Mondays series continues the Acts of the Apostles, teaching Acts chapter 11 verse by verse. Chapter 11 is a continuation of chapter 10, where Peter went to Cornelius and the "Gentiles" received the word of God. So the whole question of this chapter is: who are the Gentiles, and who was Cornelius? The class walks the history from Moses through the Assyrian, Babylonian, Persian, Greek, and Roman captivities to show that the "Gentiles" grafted in are the scattered, hidden outcasts of the house of Israel, the same nation the Most High swallowed up among the nations and is now cleansing by the word of Christ.

**[[Acts 11#^v1|Acts 11:1]]**
![[Acts 11#^v1]]
- The apostles and brethren in Judea heard that the Gentiles had also received the word of God, and now Peter is rehearsing what happened in [[Acts 10|Acts 10]]. The "Gentiles" is a reference to Cornelius, who was not Italian by nation; he was an Israelite who served as a general over a hundred men in the Roman army, just as today there are black generals and black soldiers in the American army, and America is an extension of ancient Rome.

    ![[Acts 10#^v29]]
    ![[Acts 10#^v30]]
    ![[Acts 10#^v31]]
    ![[Acts 10#^v32]]
    ![[Acts 10#^v33]]
    Peter rehearses Cornelius fasting, praying at the ninth hour, the man in bright clothing, and being sent to Joppa for Simon Peter.
- Peter says "of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons," but that does not mean the Most High has no respect for his chosen nation. He is a respecter of persons concerning his chosen race, and what Peter means is that God shows no partiality in judgment, whether a man is poor or mighty.
  - **[[Acts 10#^v34|Acts 10:34]]**
    ![[Acts 10#^v34]]
    Peter opens his mouth and says of a truth God is no respecter of persons.
  - **[[Exodus 2#^v25|Exodus 2:25]]**
    ![[Exodus 2#^v25]]
    The Most High looked upon the children of Israel and had respect unto them, so he does respect his chosen nation.
  - **[[Leviticus 19#^v15|Leviticus 19:15]]**
    ![[Leviticus 19#^v15]]
    Do no unrighteousness in judgment; respect not the poor nor honour the mighty, but judge thy neighbour in righteousness.
- "But in every nation he that feareth him and worketh righteousness is accepted with him." The "every nation" is not the Chinese, Japanese, East Indian, or Caucasian; it is every nation into which the Israelites were scattered for disobedience.
  - **[[Acts 2#^v5|Acts 2:5]]**
    ![[Acts 2#^v5]]
    There were dwelling at Jerusalem Jews, devout men, out of every nation under heaven, because Israel was scattered out of every nation.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 4#^v27|Deuteronomy 4:27]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 4#^v27]]
    The Most High would scatter Israel among the nations and leave them few in number among the heathen.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v64|Deuteronomy 28:64]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v64]]
    Scattered from one end of the earth to the other, serving other gods of wood and stone, part of the curse for disobedience.
  - **[[James 1#^v1|James 1:1]]**
    ![[James 1#^v1]]
    James writes to the twelve tribes scattered abroad, the same scattered nation Peter and Paul addressed.
- To fear him and work righteousness means to keep the commandments; "devout" means to keep the law of the Most High.
  - **[[Psalms 119#^v142|Psalms 119:142]]**
    ![[Psalms 119#^v142]]
    His righteousness is everlasting and his law is the truth, so to walk in truth is to keep his commandments.
  - **[[Acts 22#^v12|Acts 22:12]]**
    ![[Acts 22#^v12]]
    Ananias, a devout man according to the law, having a good report of all the Jews, showing "devout" means law-keeping.
- Cornelius specifically was of the northern kingdom of Israel; the word was sent unto the children of Israel, and Cornelius was a just man giving alms and keeping the commandments even while scattered.
  - **[[Acts 10#^v36|Acts 10:36]]**
    ![[Acts 10#^v36]]
    The word which God sent unto the children of Israel, preaching peace by Jesus Christ, marking Cornelius an Israelite.
  - **[[Tobit 1#^v1|Tobit 1:1-6]]**
    ![[Tobit 1#^v1]]
    ![[Tobit 1#^v2]]
    ![[Tobit 1#^v3]]
    ![[Tobit 1#^v4]]
    ![[Tobit 1#^v5]]
    ![[Tobit 1#^v6]]
    Tobit, of the tribe of Nephthali in the northern kingdom, was carried captive to Nineveh yet still walked in truth and went up to Jerusalem, a remnant keeping the law like Cornelius.
  - **[[Matthew 4#^v15|Matthew 4:15]]**
    ![[Matthew 4#^v15]]
    Zabulon and Nephthali, Galilee of the Gentiles, because the northern kingdom that went into idolatry was called Gentiles.
- When Israel was scattered they mingled among the heathen and became Gentiles themselves, taking on the nations' names, customs, idols, and nationalities, just as black Americans today assimilate, calling themselves African-American, Haitian-American, or Latin American, pledging the flag, and eating swine flesh. The scholars confirm it: the Zondervan compact Bible dictionary says "Gentiles" usually means a non-Israelite people but sometimes means Israelites, and the Banners of Israel shows that Paul had to conceal the identity of scattered Israel, calling the hidden ones Gentiles, heathen, and uncircumcision.

    ![[Psalms 106#^v35]]
    ![[Psalms 106#^v36]]
    They were mingled among the heathen, learned their works, and served their idols, which were a snare unto them.

![[Acts 11#^v2]]
![[Acts 11#^v3]]
- When Peter came up to Jerusalem, they of the circumcision contended with him, saying he went in to men uncircumcised and did eat with them. The complaint was about Peter dealing with the Gentiles, meaning the northern kingdom, and there had been contention between the northern and southern kingdom ever since the split under Solomon.
- To understand circumcision and uncircumcision you have to go to the origin: circumcision was the token, the sign, the seal of the covenant the Most High made with Abraham, a visible confirmation carried through all the generations.
  - **[[Genesis 17#^v10|Genesis 17:10-11]]**
    ![[Genesis 17#^v10]]
    ![[Genesis 17#^v11]]
    Every man child was to be circumcised, and it was a token of the covenant between the Most High and Abraham's seed.
  - **[[Romans 4#^v11|Romans 4:11]]**
    ![[Romans 4#^v11]]
    Abraham received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had while yet uncircumcised.
  - **[[Exodus 4#^v24|Exodus 4:24-26]]**
    ![[Exodus 4#^v24]]
    ![[Exodus 4#^v25]]
    ![[Exodus 4#^v26]]
    The Most High sought to kill Moses until Zipporah circumcised her son, because Moses represented God yet had not kept the seal of the covenant.
  - **[[Genesis 17#^v14|Genesis 17:14]]**
    ![[Genesis 17#^v14]]
    The uncircumcised man child whose flesh is not circumcised shall be cut off; he hath broken my covenant.

    ![[Leviticus 12#^v1]]
    ![[Leviticus 12#^v2]]
    ![[Leviticus 12#^v3]]
    On the eighth day the flesh of a man child's foreskin shall be circumcised, made a law in Israel confirming Abraham's seal.
- The split into circumcision and uncircumcision came in history under the Greeks. Antiochus decreed that all should be one people and leave their own laws, and many Israelites consented, sacrificing to idols, profaning the Sabbath, leaving their children uncircumcised, and forgetting the law.
  - **[[1 Maccabees 1#^v41|1 Maccabees 1:41-43]]**
    ![[1 Maccabees 1#^v41]]
    ![[1 Maccabees 1#^v42]]
    ![[1 Maccabees 1#^v43]]
    Antiochus commanded one people and one law, and many Israelites consented, sacrificing to idols and profaning the Sabbath.
  - **[[1 Maccabees 1#^v48|1 Maccabees 1:48-49]]**
    ![[1 Maccabees 1#^v48]]
    ![[1 Maccabees 1#^v49]]
    They were made to leave their children uncircumcised so that they would forget the law and change all the ordinances.
  - **[[1 Maccabees 1#^v59|1 Maccabees 1:59-61]]**
    ![[1 Maccabees 1#^v59]]
    ![[1 Maccabees 1#^v60]]
    ![[1 Maccabees 1#^v61]]
    The Greeks understood the power of the covenant and put to death the women and infants who kept circumcision.
- Mattathias and the Hasmonean dynasty, the house of Simeon, along with the Assideans who were later called Pharisees, went about circumcising valiantly and restoring the law.
  - **[[1 Maccabees 2#^v1|1 Maccabees 2:1]]**
    ![[1 Maccabees 2#^v1]]
    Mattathias, son of John, son of Simeon, from whom came the Hasmonean dynasty, meaning the house of Simeon.
  - **[[1 Maccabees 2#^v42|1 Maccabees 2:42-46]]**
    ![[1 Maccabees 2#^v42]]
    ![[1 Maccabees 2#^v43]]
    ![[1 Maccabees 2#^v44]]
    ![[1 Maccabees 2#^v45]]
    ![[1 Maccabees 2#^v46]]
    The Assideans, mighty men devoted to the law, joined Mattathias and circumcised the uncircumcised valiantly. The Jewish Encyclopedia notes the Assideans, or Hasidim, were pious men devoted to the law from whom the Pharisees are held to have developed.
- So the circumcision was primarily the southern kingdom, Judah, raised in the law, and the Pharisees who pushed circumcision above everything, even above faith and the commandments; the uncircumcision was the northern kingdom and the scattered Israelites.
  - **[[Romans 3#^v1|Romans 3:1-2]]**
    ![[Romans 3#^v1]]
    ![[Romans 3#^v2]]
    What advantage hath the Jew, or profit of circumcision? Much every way, chiefly because to them were committed the oracles of God.
  - **[[Philippians 3#^v5|Philippians 3:5]]**
    ![[Philippians 3#^v5]]
    Paul was circumcised the eighth day, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Pharisee, because the Pharisees upheld circumcision.
- Paul reminds the Israelites in Ephesus that in time past they were Gentiles in the flesh, called the uncircumcision, because under the Greeks it was unlawful to profess yourself a Jew, so Israel had to call themselves Greeks or Gentiles.
  - **[[Ephesians 2#^v11|Ephesians 2:11]]**
    ![[Ephesians 2#^v11]]
    Remember that ye being in time past Gentiles in the flesh, called uncircumcision by that which is called the circumcision.
  - **[[2 Maccabees 6#^v6|2 Maccabees 6:6]]**
    ![[2 Maccabees 6#^v6]]
    It was not lawful to keep the Sabbath or the feasts or to profess to be a Jew, so Israel took on Gentile titles.

    ![[2 Maccabees 6#^v9]]
    Whoever would not conform to the manners of the Gentiles was put to death, forcing Israel into Gentile customs.

- Peter rehearses the vision from the beginning: he was in Joppa praying, and in a trance saw a vessel descend like a great sheet. That vessel is Israel, swallowed up and consumed among the Gentiles, made a vessel of no honour by learning their works and serving their idols.
  - **[[Hosea 8#^v8|Hosea 8:8]]**
    ![[Hosea 8#^v8]]
    Israel is swallowed up and shall be among the Gentiles as a vessel wherein is no pleasure.
- The sheet was let down from heaven by four corners, meaning Israel was cast down from a position of rulership to the earth into servitude, to be gathered again from the four corners as the outcasts of Israel.
  - **[[Lamentations 2#^v1|Lamentations 2:1]]**
    ![[Lamentations 2#^v1]]
    The Most High cast down from heaven to the earth the beauty of Israel, cast down from rulership.
  - **[[Isaiah 11#^v10|Isaiah 11:10-12]]**
    ![[Isaiah 11#^v10]]
    ![[Isaiah 11#^v11]]
    ![[Isaiah 11#^v12]]
    The root of Jesse whom the Gentiles seek, and the Most High recovers the remnant and assembles the outcasts of Israel from the four corners of the earth.
- Peter then saw four-footed beasts, wild beasts, creeping things, and fowls, which represent men, the unclean men of scattered Israel who took up idolatry.
  - **[[Ecclesiastes 3#^v18|Ecclesiastes 3:18]]**
    ![[Ecclesiastes 3#^v18]]
    Men are shown themselves to be beasts, so the beasts in the vision are a representation of men.

- The voice said, "Arise, Peter; slay and eat," but Peter refused because nothing common or unclean had ever entered his mouth. The voice answered, "What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common," because it was never about food but about people.
  - **[[Acts 10#^v28|Acts 10:28]]**
    ![[Acts 10#^v28]]
    It is unlawful for a Jew to keep company with one of another nation, but God showed Peter not to call any man common or unclean.
- The "one of another nation" points back to the split kingdom, and the Most High promised to make the two nations one again under one king, cleansed from their idols.
  - **[[Ezekiel 37#^v22|Ezekiel 37:22-23]]**
    ![[Ezekiel 37#^v22]]
    ![[Ezekiel 37#^v23]]
    He will make them one nation under one king, no more two kingdoms, cleansed from their idols to be his people.
- To "slay and eat" means to teach and cleanse the scattered Israelites through the word of God.
  - **[[John 15#^v3|John 15:3]]**
    ![[John 15#^v3]]
    Now ye are clean through the word which I have spoken unto you, cleansing the scattered nation by the word.
- The vision was done three times, symbolizing Israel being revived and raised up the third day.
  - **[[Hosea 6#^v1|Hosea 6:1-2]]**
    ![[Hosea 6#^v1]]
    ![[Hosea 6#^v2]]
    After two days he will revive us, and in the third day he will raise us up, a foreshadow of Israel restored.
  - **[[John 2#^v19|John 2:19]]**
    ![[John 2#^v19]]
    Destroy this temple and in three days I will raise it up, Christ's resurrection foreshadowing Israel's revival.
- All was drawn up again into heaven, meaning Israel restored again to its position of rulership.
  - **[[Acts 1#^v6|Acts 1:6]]**
    ![[Acts 1#^v6]]
    The disciples ask whether the Most High will at this time restore again the kingdom to Israel.
  - **[[Revelation 11#^v12|Revelation 11:12]]**
    ![[Revelation 11#^v12]]
    A great voice from heaven says "Come up hither," and they ascend up to heaven in a cloud, Israel raised up again.

Six brethren went with him into the man's house, and Cornelius told how an angel had stood in his house and directed him to send to Joppa for Simon Peter, who would tell him words whereby he and all his house would be saved.

- As Peter began to speak, the Holy Ghost fell on them as it did on the apostles at the beginning, and Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how that John baptized with water but they would be baptized with the Holy Ghost. Since the Most High gave these Israelites the same gift he gave the apostles who believed on Christ, Peter asks what he was that he could withstand God.

**[[Acts 11#^v18|Acts 11:18]]**
![[Acts 11#^v18]]
- When they heard this they held their peace and glorified God, saying the Most High had also to the Gentiles granted repentance unto life. These Gentiles are the Israelites who took on Gentile customs, whether the northern kingdom broken off before Assyria when Pekah the son of Remaliah conspired against Judah, or Judah who became Gentiles under the Greeks.
  - **[[Acts 5#^v30|Acts 5:30-31]]**
    ![[Acts 5#^v30]]
    ![[Acts 5#^v31]]
    The God of our fathers raised up Jesus and exalted him to be a Prince and a Saviour, to give repentance to Israel, so the repentance goes to Israel.

**[[Acts 11#^v19|Acts 11:19-20]]**
![[Acts 11#^v19]]
![[Acts 11#^v20]]
- Those scattered by the persecution over Stephen traveled as far as Phenice, Cyprus, and Antioch, preaching the word to none but the Jews only, going where the Israelites were scattered. Phenice was the coastal region north of Israel, modern Lebanon; Cyprus is an island in the eastern Mediterranean; and Antioch was a major Roman city in ancient Syria, now Antakya, Turkey.
- The reason they preached to the Jews only is the prophecy that the Most High saves the tents of Judah first.
  - **[[Zechariah 12#^v7|Zechariah 12:7]]**
    ![[Zechariah 12#^v7]]
    The Lord shall save the tents of Judah first, that the house of David not magnify itself against Judah.
- Some of them, men of Cyprus and Cyrene, came to Antioch and spake to the Grecians, preaching the Lord Jesus. Those Grecians, or Hellenists, were Greek-speaking Jews. The Holman Christian Standard Bible calls them Hellenists, and the Zondervan compact Bible dictionary defines a Hellenist as a Jew who made Greek his tongue and adopted Greek ideas and practices.

- The hand of the Lord was with them and a great number believed and turned to the Lord. The church at Jerusalem sent Barnabas, a good man full of the Holy Ghost and faith, who exhorted them to cleave to the Lord, then went to Tarsus to seek Saul and brought him to Antioch, where for a whole year they taught much people.
- The disciples were called Christians first in Antioch, and those disciples were Jews. Christians were not all nations or races as men think today; the Greek word Christianos comes from Christos, the anointed one, so a Christian is a kinsman of the Messiah and a follower of Christ, an anointed one.
  - **[[Acts 26#^v28|Acts 26:28]]**

  - **[[Acts 9#^v2|Acts 9:2]]**
    ![[Acts 9#^v2]]
    Saul sought letters to bind any of "this way," whether men or women, and bring them to Jerusalem.
  - **[[Acts 22#^v4|Acts 22:4]]**
    ![[Acts 22#^v4]]
    Paul confesses he persecuted "this way" unto the death, binding men and women into prisons.
  - **[[John 14#^v6|John 14:6]]**
    ![[John 14#^v6]]
    Jesus says, "I am the way, the truth, and the life," which is why the followers were called this way.

- In those days prophets came from Jerusalem to Antioch, and one named Agabus signified by the Spirit that there would be a great dearth throughout all the world, which came to pass in the days of Claudius Caesar. This famine is historically documented; Elliott's commentary notes the frequent famines of Claudius's reign, and Josephus records a famine affecting Judea and Syria under the procurator Cuspius Fadus around AD 45, when Queen Helena of Adiabene sent supplies of corn and figs, again giving validity to the Bible as an accurate history book.
- The disciples, every man according to his ability, determined to send relief to the brethren in Judea, which they did, sending it to the elders by the hand of Barnabas and Saul. That is the end of [[Acts 11|Acts 11]].
