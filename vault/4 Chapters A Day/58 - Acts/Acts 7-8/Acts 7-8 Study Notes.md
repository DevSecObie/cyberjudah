# Acts 7: Stephen Rehearses the History and the Stiffnecked Resist the Holy Ghost

Read the chapter → [[Acts 7]]

## Introduction

This session continues the Man Up Mondays series through the Acts of the Apostles, covering chapters 7 and 8 in one sitting. Chapter 7 is Stephen standing before the council of the Pharisees and Sadducees, rehearsing the whole history of the fathers off the top of his head, from Abraham in Mesopotamia down to Moses and the prophets, to bring the audience up to Christ, the prophet Moses said the Most High would raise up. It ends with Stephen charging them as stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, seeing Christ standing on the right hand of God, and being stoned while Saul consents to his death. Chapter 8 opens with the great persecution that scatters the disciples abroad, and follows Philip into Samaria and down to the Ethiopian eunuch, teaching heavily on the two baptisms, the name of Jesus in place of animal sacrifice, and the remnant of Israel scattered among the nations. The thread of the whole class is the spirit: the Holy Ghost is first and foremost the laws, and the same persecution and the same work that happened back then is what comes on the scene again today.

That is why the scripture says judge no one before their time; a man as evil as Saul was doing back there became Paul the apostle.
- At that time there was a great persecution against the church at Jerusalem, and they were all scattered abroad throughout Judea and Samaria, except the apostles. Notice that unlike the other epistles, the book of Acts never closes with Amen, showing there is a continuation: the apostles come back on the scene and redo the Acts all over again, so the things that happened back then are the things that happen today.
- There will be a great persecution today. This gospel is a witness against the whole world. When you pray thy kingdom come, thy will be done, you are saying the Most High's will overpowers America's will, Russia's, China's, every nation racing for world domination. To preach a black Christ in the streets is to speak against the system that was designed to keep our people debased, a Christ with many crowns upon his head who takes the crowns of all the nations. They are not going to let a people they despise rise up and preach a gospel that overthrows the world superpowers and do nothing; it is going to be a great persecution. Gird your mind, study, pray, and apply, because wisdom and knowledge will be the stability of our time.

    Christ said, Now ye are clean through the word which I have spoken unto you; his baptism cleanses you consciously through his word, the laws and testimony.
  - **[[Isaiah 8#^v20|Isaiah 8:20]]**
    ![[Isaiah 8#^v20]]
    To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, there is no light in them; the word Christ cleanses us with is the laws and testimony.
  - **[[1 Peter 3#^v21|1 Peter 3:21]]**
    ![[1 Peter 3#^v21]]
    Baptism doth also now save us, not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience; Christ's baptism is based on the conscience, not on washing the body.
  - **[[Isaiah 1#^v16|Isaiah 1:16]]**
    ![[Isaiah 1#^v16]]
    Wash you, make you clean, put away the evil of your doings from before mine eyes, and cease to do evil; that is the putting away of evil, not the washing of the flesh.
  - **[[Jeremiah 2#^v22|Jeremiah 2:22]]**
    ![[Jeremiah 2#^v22]]
    Though thou wash thee with nitre and take thee much soap, thine iniquity is marked before the Most High; you cannot scrub your conscience clean, only Christ can.

**[[Acts 8#^v18|Acts 8:18-19]]**

![[Acts 8#^v18]]
![[Acts 8#^v19]]

- When Simon saw that through laying on of the apostles' hands the Holy Ghost was given, he offered them money, saying, Give me also this power, that on whomsoever I lay hands he may receive the Holy Ghost. Simon had the spirits of covetousness, envy, preeminence, and vainglory; he had held status as the great power of God, and when he lost it those spirits rose up in him and he wanted to buy back what he had, thinking, I have the money, I will pay you for what you have.

**[[Acts 8#^v20|Acts 8:20-23]]**

![[Acts 8#^v20]]
![[Acts 8#^v21]]
![[Acts 8#^v22]]
![[Acts 8#^v23]]

- Peter said, Thy money perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of the Most High may be purchased with money; thou hast neither part nor lot in this matter, for thy heart is not right. Repent therefore of this thy wickedness and pray, for I perceive thou art in the gall of bitterness and in the bond of iniquity.
- The gall of bitterness is resentment and bitterness of spirit. Simon became jealous because the signs the Most High worked through the apostles overshadowed what the people had known him for. That thing did not come overnight; he fed envy, preeminence, vainglory, and covetousness day by day until the roots of bitterness took hold and bound him, and he became a prisoner of his own mind, trapped and sinning.
- This is why, when people come into the truth, some who had status, fame, or money as celebrities, pastors, or deacons have to come up as a member and clean themselves. Some get jealous, hold on to the envy, and try to bring different doctrines to sway members away, the same spirit of bitterness that moved the Pharisees to kill Christ because the disciples came from nowhere, spoke more eloquently, and went to the scriptures better without the status.

**[[Acts 8#^v26|Acts 8:26-27]]**

![[Acts 8#^v26]]
![[Acts 8#^v27]]

- The angel of the Lord told Philip, Arise, and go toward the south unto the way that goeth down from Jerusalem unto Gaza, which is desert. And he arose and went, and behold, a man of Ethiopia, an eunuch of great authority under Candace queen of the Ethiopians, who had charge of all her treasure and had come to Jerusalem to worship.
- People say this proves Philip went to the heathen Ethiopians, but this eunuch was a Jew, part of the remnant of Israel scattered in Ethiopia, in Kush, during the Babylonian and Persian captivity. That is why Christians confuse the Ethiopian eunuch; they do not understand Bible prophecy or Bible history. Candace was a real title of the queens of the kingdom of Kush who ruled from the city of Meroe, and the queen of that time was likely Amanitore, around the same period as the famine in Acts chapter 11.

**[[Acts 8#^v28|Acts 8:28-31]]**

![[Acts 8#^v28]]
![[Acts 8#^v29]]
![[Acts 8#^v30]]
![[Acts 8#^v31]]

- The eunuch was returning, sitting in his chariot, reading Esaias the prophet, and the Spirit said to Philip, Go near and join thyself to this chariot. Philip ran and heard him read Isaiah and asked, Understandest thou what thou readest? The eunuch answered, How can I, except some man should guide me, and desired Philip to come up and sit with him.
- Notice the humility of the brother. Rather than making his own assumptions, he admitted he did not understand. Some brothers and sisters lack that humility, claiming, I always knew I was Israel, or, the Most High spoke to me while I was in my sins, rather than saying, This is the first time I heard this, teach me. You had to be taught this Bible to know this Bible; the Most High is not just going to hand it to you out of nowhere. Expressing that you do not know keeps you in a low state and away from the spirit of preeminence and vainglory.

**[[Acts 8#^v32|Acts 8:32-35]]**

![[Acts 8#^v32]]
![[Acts 8#^v33]]
![[Acts 8#^v34]]
![[Acts 8#^v35]]

- The place of the scripture he read was Isaiah: He was led as a sheep to the slaughter, and like a lamb dumb before his shearer, so opened he not his mouth; in his humiliation his judgment was taken away, and who shall declare his generation, for his life is taken from the earth.
- The eunuch asked Philip, Of whom speaketh the prophet this, of himself or of some other man? He did not know, so rather than assume or give his own interpretation, he asked. That is how you should handle what you do not know: write it down and ask, rather than making an assumption and teaching others, which is how heresies come into the body. Then Philip opened his mouth, began at the same scripture, and preached unto him Jesus, because Philip had the Holy Ghost, wisdom, and understanding, having learned under the feet of the apostles.

![[Acts 8#^v36]]
![[Acts 8#^v37]]
![[Acts 8#^v38]]

- As they went on their way they came to a certain water, and the eunuch said, See, here is water, what doth hinder me to be baptized? Philip said, If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest. He answered, I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God, and he commanded the chariot to stand still, and they went down both into the water, and Philip baptized him.

Philip baptized the eunuch in water because, like Ezra and others, some of the disciples came from John the Baptist's teaching and held to his water baptism; John understood his own place and told his disciples to follow Christ, who must increase while he decreased.
