# Acts 10: Cornelius and the Northern Kingdom Grafted In

Read the chapter → [[Acts 10]]

This session teaches Acts chapter 10 verse by verse across two classes, and it takes the history further than usual on purpose, so there is no confusion. The chapter is built around Cornelius, the centurion of the Italian band, and the vision given to Peter. Read carelessly, the chapter looks like salvation being thrown open to every nation and permission to eat unclean food. Taught precept upon precept, it is about the northern kingdom. The whole class runs on the split: after Rehoboam and Jeroboam, Israel divided into the southern kingdom (Judah, Benjamin, Levi, the Jews) and the northern kingdom (Ephraim and the rest), and the north went off into idolatry until the Most High broke them, took away their nationality, and scattered them among the heathen so that they became "not a people," the "Gentiles." Cornelius, his vision, and the outpouring of the Holy Ghost are the beginning of the Most High gathering that broken northern kingdom back into the fold through Christ, so that the two sticks become one nation again.

**[[Acts 10#^v1|Acts 10:1]]**
![[Acts 10#^v1]]
- There was a certain man in Caesarea called Cornelius, a centurion of the Italian band. When our people read "Italian band" they automatically picture a Caucasian, but Caesarea Maritima sat on a seacoast in the land of Israel, and Cornelius was an Israelite officer in the Roman army, not a man of another nation.
- Jews of the diaspora were fully enmeshed in Roman culture, took Greek and Latin names, and built careers in the Roman military, so a black man, an Israelite, serving under the Italian band is nothing strange. It is the same as so-called black people and Latinos serving in the American military today, from the Buffalo Soldiers to a general like Colin Powell.
- Because the Israelites were scattered through every province and took the names of their captivities, scholars cannot tell a Roman Jewish soldier from a Roman by his name alone, and the New Testament was written in Greek because Israel spoke Greek under the Greeks. Correcting a misheard reference to [[Luke 3|Luke 3]], the centurions of scripture were often Israelite servants in the army.

- Cornelius rehearses the events: four days ago at the ninth hour he was praying and fasting when a man stood before him in bright clothing, an angel, and told him his prayer was heard and his alms had in remembrance in the sight of the Most High. This shows our alms and prayers are regarded and counted as a memorial before the Most High.
- Because of his good works, his alms, his being a just and devout man who feared the Most High and prayed often, the Most High counted him worthy that through him the whole northern kingdom could receive the salvation already promised through Abraham. Cornelius says they are all present before the Most High to hear all things commanded of God.

- Peter opens his mouth and says the Most High is no respecter of persons, but in every nation he that feareth him and worketh righteousness is accepted, and the word was sent unto the children of Israel. "No respecter of persons" does not mean nations are equal, because the Most High plainly had respect unto Israel and chose Israel above all people; it means he does not respect persons in judgment concerning his law, so whether a man sins outside the law or inside it he is judged.
  - **[[Exodus 2#^v25|Exodus 2:25]]**
    ![[Exodus 2#^v25]]
    The Most High looked upon the children of Israel and had respect unto them, so he does respect Israel by nation.
  - **[[Proverbs 24#^v23|Proverbs 24:23]]**
    ![[Proverbs 24#^v23]]
    It is not good to have respect of persons in judgment, which is the kind of respect being denied.

    Tribulation and wrath upon every soul that doeth evil, of the Jew first and also of the Gentile, for there is no respect of persons with the Most High.
- It says "the Jew first, and also the Gentile" because prophecy set Judah, Benjamin, and Levi to rise up first, and the "Gentiles" are the dispersed northern tribes scattered among the heathen, left few in number. This is the diaspora, the same scattering some now call the African diaspora.
  - **[[Zechariah 12#^v7|Zechariah 12:7]]**

  - **[[Deuteronomy 4#^v27|Deuteronomy 4:27]]**

    The Most High will scatter Israel among the nations and leave them few in number among the heathen.
- Once scattered, they were mingled among the nations, learned their works, served their idols, and by decree were forbidden to keep the Sabbath or the feasts or even to call themselves a Jew, forced to conform to the manners of the Gentiles on pain of death. This is the same butchering endured in slavery, where it became unlawful to hold any Israelite name, custom, or nationality.

They sat in the shadow of death because Ephraim was broken and taken into Assyria.

- Peter rehearses the ministry of Christ, published throughout Judea from Galilee, how the Most High anointed Jesus of Nazareth, who went about doing good and healing all oppressed of the devil, whom they slew and hanged on a tree, and whom the Most High raised the third day and shewed openly to chosen witnesses. To him all the prophets witness that through his name whosoever believeth receiveth remission of sins.
- The "whosoever" is whosoever of Israel, of Zion, because Paul opens the same subject praying for Israel to be saved, and the stone laid in Zion is Christ, so those who believe on him shall not be ashamed.
  - **[[Romans 10#^v1|Romans 10:1]]**
