# Acts 1: The Promise of the Father and the Kingdom Restored to Israel

Read the chapter → [[Acts 1]]

This is the opening of the Man Up Monday series through the Acts of the Apostles, which will lead afterward into the letters of Paul. Many brothers and sisters are approached by Christians who cannot make sense of Paul's letters, and the reason is that those letters are built upon the writings of Acts. Studying Acts gives the clarity needed to answer the Christians and to understand Paul. This session covers [[Acts 1#^v1|Acts 1:1]] through 1:14: Luke's account written to Theophilus, the forty days Christ was seen after his passion, the commandment to wait in Jerusalem for the promise of the Holy Ghost, the ascension by chariot, and the eleven gathered with the women and Mary in the upper room.

**[[Acts 1#^v1|Acts 1:1]]**
![[Acts 1#^v1]]
- Luke wrote both the Gospel of Luke and the book of Acts. The "former treatise" is the Gospel of Luke, which was also addressed to Theophilus, so the two books are two letters from the same hand.
- The scriptures we have are a collection of the apostles' teaching passed down to the young men under them, the seventy, and the other disciples. Luke was not one of the twelve, though it is speculated he was of the seventy, and he was there from the beginning. Just as the bishops and elders pass down history to us today so that we can speak on it though we were not there, that knowledge was passed down to Luke.
- Theophilus' name means love of God. He was an Israelite, not a gentile, and Luke addresses him as "most excellent," a title of nobility or government office, the same way Paul addressed "most noble Festus."

**[[Acts 1#^v2|Acts 1:2]]**
![[Acts 1#^v2]]
- Luke gave a full historical account of everything Christ did, from his birth to the day he was taken up, and records that Christ, through the Holy Ghost, gave commandments to the apostles he had chosen.
- The end of Luke segues straight into Acts. The commandment was that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.
- "All nations" does not mean the Chinese or the Japanese. Prophetically it means the Israelites scattered among all nations: into Assyria (which is why Jonah preached to Nineveh, where the northern kingdom was exiled), then Babylon, Persia, Media, Greece, and Rome, then into Africa and out through the transatlantic and Indian Ocean slave trades. That prophecy did not happen only once; it happened all through history.

    Thus it behoved Christ to suffer and rise the third day, and that repentance and remission of sins should be preached among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.

**[[Acts 1#^v4|Acts 1:4]]**
![[Acts 1#^v4]]
- The commandment was to not depart from Jerusalem but to wait for the promise of the Father, which is the Holy Ghost, given only after Christ was glorified.
- Christ could teach only so much in three years because a man can bear only so much. The Comforter would open their understanding in increments after his death, guiding them into all truth and bringing his words back to their remembrance.
- The Holy Ghost comes in an order that begins with the law: turn at his reproof and keep the commandments, and then he pours out his spirit and opens the understanding. Only the Most High can give the wisdom to interpret this book, which is the most purchased yet least understood book on the earth.

**[[Acts 1#^v5|Acts 1:5]]**
![[Acts 1#^v5]]
- John truly baptized with water, but they would be baptized with the Holy Ghost not many days hence. There is a distinction between what John taught and what Christ brought.
- Baptism comes from the word baptizo, out of the purification laws. John was of the tribe of Levi, his father Zacharias a priest and his mother Elizabeth of the daughters of Aaron, so his water baptism was always symbolic, a preparation leading up to Christ.
- John said he must decrease and Christ must increase, and that happens in increments. Two of John's own disciples heard Christ and followed him, which is why in Acts the disciples were still baptizing with water even though Christ himself baptized not with water but with the Holy Ghost. When you believe on Christ as the scripture says, rivers of living water flow out of your belly, meaning wisdom and understanding flow out of the mind that consumes and filters information; but that Holy Ghost was not yet given because Jesus was not yet glorified.
  - **[[Matthew 3#^v11|Matthew 3:11]]**
    ![[Matthew 3#^v11]]
    John baptized with water unto repentance, but he that came after him would baptize with the Holy Ghost and with fire.
  - **[[Luke 1#^v5|Luke 1:5]]**
    ![[Luke 1#^v5]]

**[[Acts 1#^v6|Acts 1:6]]**
![[Acts 1#^v6]]
- Even after all of this they asked whether he would at this time restore again the kingdom to Israel, which shows the Holy Ghost was not yet fully given. It is given in measures, and every man is dealt a measure of faith that he has to grow in; in Christ there was no measure, but the disciples knew only so much at that time.
- They asked because they thought Rome was the last captivity, and John the Baptist thought the same when he sent to ask whether Christ was the one or they should look for another. They knew Daniel's beasts up to Rome because their fathers had lived through them, but they did not yet understand the little horn, Babylon the great and America, or the seven seals that only the Lion of the tribe of Judah could open. That fuller understanding was given later to John, to Peter, and now in these last days.
- The falling away had to come first and the man of sin be revealed; he could not be openly named under Rome, where you were jailed for sedition, so Paul wrote in allegory. He would be revealed in a land like America, where the freedom to speak allows the man of sin to be identified plainly.
- "Restore" means to bring back and make new. The kingdom of heaven is Jerusalem and it always belonged to Israel, not to all nations; it is not pie in the sky when you die.

**[[Acts 1#^v7|Acts 1:7]]**
![[Acts 1#^v7]]
- Christ said it is not for them to know the times or the seasons, which the Father hath put in his own power. Even after his resurrection Christ did not know the time, which disproves the trinity and the idea that Christ is the Father, showing the Son is separate from the Father.
- You come to understand the times and seasons as you live through the prophecies and see prophecy become history.

**[[Acts 1#^v8|Acts 1:8]]**
![[Acts 1#^v8]]
- They would receive power after the Holy Ghost came upon them, and be witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judaea, in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.
- Christians misunderstand this power as merely speaking in tongues, but the Bible defines the power of the Holy Ghost by the signs that follow: casting out devils, speaking with new tongues, taking up serpents, drinking any deadly thing unharmed, and laying hands on the sick to recover. Peter was sought to heal by his shadow and Paul by his handkerchief.

    ![[Mark 16#^v18]]

    ![[Acts 2#^v4]]
    They were all filled with the Holy Ghost and began to speak with other tongues as the Spirit gave them utterance; one instance of that power coming to pass.

**[[Acts 1#^v9|Acts 1:9]]**
![[Acts 1#^v9]]
- While they beheld, Christ was taken up and a cloud received him out of their sight. He was taken up in the middle of his speaking after forty days; that was the exit.
- The Most High maketh the clouds his chariot, so this was not merely a cloud but a chariot, an instrument of the angels, the same as the moon being an instrument of the armies above. Christ was received up by a chariot, not carried by little white angels in the clouds as the Christian movies depict.
- The kind of chariot that masks itself in a cloud was seen in 2011 over the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem, a glowing orb that descended and then shot off at about four thousand miles an hour over one of the most secure no-fly zones in the world, witnessed by thousands and reported internationally.
  - **[[Psalms 104#^v3|Psalms 104:3]]**
    ![[Psalms 104#^v3]]

**[[Acts 1#^v14|Acts 1:14]]**
![[Acts 1#^v14]]
- They all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and his brethren.
- This was not only the men; the women also had to learn. The men always understood that the women learn alongside them, so a camp that keeps the women out of the congregation is wrong. Our people came into slavery together and will come out of captivity together.
- "His brethren" are Christ's own brothers. During his ministry they did not believe in him and told him to go show himself openly in Judaea, but after his death and the miracles they came to believe. James the Just, the Lord's brother, became an apostle and was later killed.
