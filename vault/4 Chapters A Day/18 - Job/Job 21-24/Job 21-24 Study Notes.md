# Job 21: Why the Wicked Prosper

Read the chapter → [[Job 21]]

This session covers [[Job 21|Job 21]] through 24 on the feast of dedication. Job answers his friends by pressing the hard question they keep dodging: if calamity always falls on the wicked, then why do the wicked live, grow old, and grow mighty in power? Job walks through the visible prosperity of the wicked, the twofold reckoning of the wicked of Israel and the wicked of Esau, and the certainty that the wicked are reserved to the day of destruction. Eliphaz answers back in [[Job 22|Job 22]] with accusations and a call to acquaint yourself with the Most High, Job replies in [[Job 23|Job 23]] that he would come even to the seat of God and come forth as gold, and [[Job 24|Job 24]] lays out the crimes of the oppressor who removes the landmarks and gathers all nations to himself.

- Job tells his friends to hear his speech and let this be their consolation. Suffer me that I may speak, and after I have spoken, mock on. Let Job speak, then see if you have anything to say, or mock him.
- Job asks a direct question: is my complaint to man? If it were so, why should not my spirit be troubled? His complaint is not against men, so his trouble is justified.
- Mark me and be astonished, and lay your hand upon your mouth. When Job remembers, he is afraid, and trembling takes hold on his flesh.

![[Job 21#^v7]]

- Wherefore do the wicked live, become old, yea, are mighty in power? This verse opens the whole thread on how the wicked do prosper.

    ![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v11]]

  - **[[Ecclesiastes 8#^v12|Ecclesiastes 8:12]]**
    ![[Ecclesiastes 8#^v12]]
    Though a sinner do evil a hundred times and his days be prolonged, yet it shall be well with them that fear God.

**[[Job 21#^v8|Job 21:8-10]]**

![[Job 21#^v8]]

- Their seed is established in their sight with them, and their offspring before their eyes. Their houses are safe from fear, neither is the rod of God upon them. This is why they think the way they do, because no rod falls on them.
- This is going twofold, dealing with the wicked of Israel and the wicked of Esau. This passage is the pride of Esau.
- Their bulls gender and fail not, their cow calveth and casteth not her calf. They are even prosperous in their animals.
  - **[[Psalms 49#^v11|Psalm 49:11]]**
    ![[Psalms 49#^v11]]
    Their inward thought is that their houses shall continue forever, and they call their lands after their own name.
  - **[[Psalms 49#^v12|Psalm 49:12]]**
    ![[Psalms 49#^v12]]
    Nevertheless man being in honor abideth not, so the honor does not last.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v31|Deuteronomy 28:31]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v31]]
    When Israel is wicked, curses fall as a nation: the ox slain before your eyes, the ass taken away, the seed given to the enemy with none to rescue.

**[[Job 21#^v11|Job 21:11-13]]**

![[Job 21#^v11]]
![[Job 21#^v12]]

- They send forth their little ones like a flock, and their children dance. They take the timbrel and harp and rejoice at the sound of the organ. This is the wicked holiday, Christmas: they make merry, make songs, and send gifts.
- They spend their days in wealth, and in a moment go down to the grave. The wealth is real, but the end comes suddenly.
  - **[[Revelation 11#^v10|Revelation 11:10]]**
    ![[Revelation 11#^v10]]
    They that dwell on the earth rejoice, make merry, and send gifts one to another, mocking the two prophets, the northern and southern kingdom of Israel.

    ![[Zechariah 2#^v6]]
    Ho, ho, come forth and flee from the land of the north. Santa Claus mocks you by quoting this while holding you in captivity; they hide the truth in your face to see how dumbed down we are.

![[Job 21#^v14]]
![[Job 21#^v15]]
![[Job 21#^v16]]

- Therefore they say unto God, depart from us, for we desire not the knowledge of thy ways. This is why the fool has said in his heart there is no God, and why they call themselves atheists.
- What is the Almighty that we should serve him, and what profit shall we have if we pray unto him? Lo, their good is not in their hand. The counsel of the wicked is far from Job.
- The scripture says they were not born right; the soul that is lifted up is not right in them.

- God layeth up his iniquity for his children; he rewardeth him, and he shall know it. His eyes shall see his destruction, and he shall drink of the wrath of the Almighty. They are going to drink from the wrath of God.
- For what pleasure hath he in his house after him, when the number of his months is cut off in the midst? The prosperity means nothing once his time is cut short.

- Who shall declare his way to his face, and who shall repay him what he hath done? He is brought to the grave and remains in the tomb, and every man draws after him as there are innumerable before him.
- All souls belong to God; he can bring you back in regeneration to judge you, just as we were brought forth into captivity over here on slave ships for breaking the commandments. That is living proof God controls the souls.
- Job says their counsel comforts him in vain, seeing in their answers there remaineth falsehood. When you read the whole book, God lets them know that Job was right.

# Job 22: Acquaint Now Thyself With Him

Read the chapter → [[Job 22]]

- Then Eliphaz the Temanite answers: can a man be profitable unto God, as he that is wise is profitable to himself? Is it any pleasure to the Almighty that you are righteous, or gain that you make your ways perfect?
- Will he reprove you for fear of you? Will he enter with you into judgment? Is not your wickedness great and your iniquity infinite? Eliphaz throws Job's righteousness back as filthy rags, as if keeping the commandments profits nothing.
- But Christ told us if we keep the commandments and follow him, we will rule on thrones with him, so there is falsehood in Eliphaz's answer.
  - **[[Matthew 12#^v28|Matthew 12:28]]**
    ![[Matthew 12#^v28]]
    If the Most High cast out devils by the Spirit of God, then the kingdom of God is come unto you.
  - **[[1 Corinthians 6#^v1|1 Corinthians 6:1-2]]**
    ![[1 Corinthians 6#^v1]]
    ![[1 Corinthians 6#^v2]]
    Dare any of you go to law before the unjust and not the saints? Do you not know that the saints shall judge the world?
  - **[[Matthew 19#^v27|Matthew 19:27-28]]**
    ![[Matthew 19#^v27]]
    ![[Matthew 19#^v28]]
    Those who followed Christ in the regeneration shall sit upon twelve thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel.
  - **[[Ezekiel 18#^v21|Ezekiel 18:21-22]]**
    ![[Ezekiel 18#^v21]]
    ![[Ezekiel 18#^v22]]
    If the wicked turn from all his sins and keep the statutes, he shall surely live, and his transgressions shall not be mentioned unto him.
  - **[[Romans 15#^v4|Romans 15:4]]**
    ![[Romans 15#^v4]]
    Whatsoever was written aforetime was written for our learning, that through patience and comfort of the scriptures we might have hope, even when men call us a hate group.

- Eliphaz accuses Job: you have taken a pledge from your brother for naught and stripped the naked of their clothing, withheld water from the weary and bread from the hungry, sent widows away empty, and broken the arms of the fatherless.
- Therefore snares are round about you, and sudden fear troubleth you. Eliphaz claims Job's suffering proves Job was sinning, but the Lord was only trying him.
- The truth is the opposite: when Job had his riches he dealt righteously, helped people, taught people, and was looked up to like an elder in the city.

    ![[Jonah 3#^v7]]

  - **[[Jonah 3#^v9|Jonah 3:9]]**
    ![[Jonah 3#^v9]]

    ![[Job 31#^v3]]
    ![[Job 31#^v4]]
    Is not destruction to the wicked? Doth not he see my ways and count all my steps?

    ![[Job 31#^v9]]
    ![[Job 31#^v10]]
    If Job's heart was deceived by a woman or he laid wait at his neighbor's door, let his wife grind unto another; Job shows he dealt righteously.

# Job 23: I Shall Come Forth As Gold

Read the chapter → [[Job 23]]

**[[Job 23#^v1|Job 23:1-4]]**

![[Job 23#^v1]]
![[Job 23#^v2]]
![[Job 23#^v3]]
![[Job 23#^v4]]

- Then Job answers: even today is my complaint bitter, my stroke is heavier than my groaning. He does have a complaint, and the stroke on him is heavier than the sound of his groaning.
- Oh that I knew where I might find him, that I might come even to his seat. I would order my cause before him and fill my mouth with arguments. Job wants to bring his case straight to God's seat.
  - **[[Hebrews 4#^v16|Hebrews 4:16]]**
    ![[Hebrews 4#^v16]]
    Let us come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy and find grace to help in time of need; we go straight to Christ our high priest.

**[[Job 23#^v5|Job 23:5-9]]**

![[Job 23#^v5]]
![[Job 23#^v6]]
![[Job 23#^v7]]
![[Job 23#^v8]]
![[Job 23#^v9]]

- I would know the words he would answer me and understand what he would say. Will he plead against me with his great power? No, but he would put strength in me. Job comes to the Lord pointing to the very promises in the word and asking God to fulfill them in him.
- The Most High has many names for different reasons, called on when you build up your prayer life: Jehovah Nissi when he won against Amalek, Jehovah Tsidkenu when you need him to be your righteousness.
- There the righteous might dispute with him, and Job would be delivered forever from his judge. Job goes forward but God is not there, backward but cannot perceive him, on the left and right he cannot behold him. Like Jacob wrestling with God, we war against principalities to come out victorious.

**[[Job 23#^v10|Job 23:10-12]]**

![[Job 23#^v10]]
![[Job 23#^v11]]
![[Job 23#^v12]]

- But he knoweth the way that I take; when he hath tried me, I shall come forth as gold. Job's foot has held his steps, he has kept God's way and not declined. Regardless of what he is going through, Job keeps the commandments.
- Neither has Job gone back from the commandment of his lips; he has esteemed the words of his mouth more than his necessary food. Let us see if Job was moving in the spirit of Christ when he said that.
  - **[[John 4#^v32|John 4:32-34]]**
    ![[John 4#^v32]]
    ![[John 4#^v33]]
    ![[John 4#^v34]]
    Christ said, I have meat to eat that you know not of; my meat is to do the will of him that sent me and to finish his work.

**[[Job 23#^v13|Job 23:13-17]]**

![[Job 23#^v13]]
![[Job 23#^v14]]
![[Job 23#^v15]]
![[Job 23#^v16]]
![[Job 23#^v17]]

- But he is in one mind, and who can turn him? What his soul desireth, even that he doeth. In another scripture he says, who can prevent me? No one can stop God from doing what he wants.
- He performeth the thing that is appointed for me, and many such things are with him. Therefore Job is troubled at his presence; when he considers it, he is afraid of him.
- God maketh his heart soft and the Almighty troubleth him, because he was not cut off before the darkness, neither has he covered the darkness from Job's face.

# Job 24: The Crimes of the Oppressor

    ![[Habakkuk 2#^v4]]

    ![[Habakkuk 2#^v9]]
    Woe to him that coveteth an evil covetousness to his house.
  - **[[Micah 2#^v2|Micah 2:2]]**
    ![[Micah 2#^v2]]
    They covet fields and take them by violence, and houses and take them away; they oppress a man, his house, his heritage, changing your name.

![[Job 24#^v9]]

- They which make oil within their walls and tread their winepresses still suffer thirst. This is the curse where you plant vineyards and do not eat from them, left neither corn nor wine.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v32|Deuteronomy 28:32]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v32]]

    ![[Lamentations 1#^v16]]
    Mine eyes run down with water, because the comforter that should relieve my soul is far from me.

![[Job 24#^v12]]
![[Job 24#^v13]]
![[Job 24#^v14]]

- Men groan from out of the city and the soul of the wounded crieth out, yet God layeth not folly to them. The comforter has left, as read in Lamentations.
- They are of those that rebel against the light; they know not the ways thereof nor abide in the paths thereof. The murderer rising with the light killeth the poor and needy, and in the night is as a thief. He will kill you in broad daylight.
  - **[[Zechariah 11#^v5|Zechariah 11:5]]**
    ![[Zechariah 11#^v5]]

    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v67]]
    In the morning you shall say, would God it were even, and at evening, would God it were morning; you cannot tell if day or night is worse.

![[Job 24#^v15]]

![[Job 24#^v17]]

- They scope you out in the daytime, marking your house, then come back at nighttime to hit you. For the morning is to them as the shadow of death; if one knows them, they are in the terrors of the shadow of death.
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 23#^v17|Ecclesiasticus 23:17-19]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 23#^v17]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 23#^v18]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 23#^v19]]

- The womb shall forget him, the worm shall feed sweetly on him; he shall be no more remembered, and wickedness shall be broken as a tree. He evil entreateth the barren that beareth not and doeth not good to the widow.

    ![[Isaiah 66#^v24]]

- He draweth also the mighty with his power; he riseth up and no man is sure of life. This is the curse of no assurance of life, failing eyes, and sorrow of mind.
- Though it be given him to be in safety, his eyes are upon their ways. They are exalted for a little while, but are gone and brought low, taken out of the way and cut off as the tops of the ears of corn.
- And if it be not so now, who will make Job a liar and make his speech nothing worth? The prophecy will come true because Job is speaking as the mouthpiece of God.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v66|Deuteronomy 28:66]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v66]]
    Thy life shall hang in doubt before thee, and thou shalt fear day and night, and have none assurance of thy life.
  - **[[Romans 3#^v3|Romans 3:3-4]]**
    ![[Romans 3#^v3]]
    ![[Romans 3#^v4]]
    Shall the unbelief of some make the faith of God without effect? God forbid; let God be true, but every man a liar, that Job be justified in his sayings.
