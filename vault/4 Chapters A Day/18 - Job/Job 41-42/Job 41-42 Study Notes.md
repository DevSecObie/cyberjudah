# Job 41: Leviathan, the King Over All the Children of Pride

Read the chapter → [[Job 41]]

This session of "4 Chapters a Day" covers [[Job 41|Job 41]] through [[Psalms 2|Psalm 2]]. [[Job 41|Job 41]] is the Most High's description of Leviathan, the beast He set to rule the sea, and [[Job 42|Job 42]] closes the book with Job's repentance, the rebuke of his three friends, and the turning of his captivity when the Most High blessed his latter end more than his beginning. The teacher ties Job's trial to our own walk: we endure to the end as Job did, and the Most High will turn the captivity of the Israelites and repay them far more than double what they have now.

**[[Job 41#^v1|Job 41:1-2]]**

![[Job 41#^v1]]
![[Job 41#^v2]]

- The Most High is speaking about Leviathan, one of the beasts He created. He made two great beasts, Behemoth to rule the earth and Leviathan to rule the waters, and Leviathan is the one that deals in the waters.
- He asks whether you can draw out Leviathan with a hook or his tongue with a cord, or put a hook into his nose or bore his jaw with a thorn, comparing this massive creature to fishing for a small fish. Leviathan is a massive, massive creature.

    The previous chapter opens Behemoth, the beast set to rule the earth, before chapter 41 turns to Leviathan of the sea.

- The Most High asks whether Leviathan will make many supplications, speak soft words, make a covenant, or be taken for a servant forever. This beast is not something a man can tame, play with like a bird, or bind for his maidens.
- No one can fill his skin with barbed irons or his head with fish spears. Lay a hand upon him, remember the battle, and do no more, for the hope of taking him is in vain and a man is cast down even at the sight of him.
- None is so fierce that he dare stir Leviathan up. Then the Most High turns it: who can stand before Him, who has ever given to Him first that He should repay, for whatsoever is under the whole heaven is His.

- His teeth are terrible round about, and his scales are his pride, shut up together as with a close seal. They are so near one to another that no air can come between them, joined and stuck together so they cannot be sundered. These are tight scales.

- By his sneezings a light shines, and his eyes are like the eyelids of the morning. Out of his mouth go burning lamps and sparks of fire leap out. Out of his nostrils goes smoke as out of a seething pot or cauldron, his breath kindles coals, and a flame goes out of his mouth.
- This is where the world gets its fire-breathing dragons and creatures like Godzilla. People treat these as mythological, but the Most High is describing a real beast He created. They did not just make these up, they found them in the scriptures, even as the Apocrypha describes the strange beasts in Egypt when it was dark. No man will just run into a Leviathan or a Behemoth, but the scripture is describing this particular beast.
- In his neck remains his strength, and sorrow is turned into joy before him. The flakes of his flesh are joined together, firm in themselves and unmovable, and his heart is as firm as a stone, as hard as a piece of the nether millstone.

- When Leviathan raises himself up, the mighty are afraid. This is a beast that would terrify the mightiest of men, and no man is going to come up and fight him, because the Most High made him to rule the sea, strong and great above all the creatures in his domain.
- The sword, the spear, the dart, and the habergeon cannot hold against him. He esteems iron as straw and brass as rotten wood, so weapons of iron and brass do nothing. The arrow cannot make him flee, sling stones turn into stubble, darts are counted as stubble, and he laughs at the shaking of a spear.
- He makes the deep to boil like a pot and the sea like a pot of ointment, and makes a path to shine after him. Upon earth there is not his like, a creature made without fear, and he is a king over all the children of pride. It sounds so far-fetched that it is hard even to imagine, and the scriptures say the kingdom will be a place we cannot even imagine, so we cannot imagine what these beasts look like either.

# Job 42: The Lord Turned the Captivity of Job

Read the chapter → [[Job 42]]

**[[Job 42#^v1|Job 42:1-6]]**

![[Job 42#^v1]]
![[Job 42#^v2]]
![[Job 42#^v3]]
![[Job 42#^v4]]
![[Job 42#^v5]]
![[Job 42#^v6]]

- Job answers the Most High and confesses that He can do everything and that no thought can be withholden from Him. This is a little thing in the scriptures to take heed to: the Most High hears our words and our thoughts, so the hatred or the private spirit a man carries cannot be hidden from Him. He knows the inward parts of men.
- Job says he uttered what he understood not, things too wonderful for him which he knew not. He had heard of the Most High by the hearing of the ear, but now his eye sees Him, and therefore he abhors himself and repents in dust and ashes.
- Dust and ashes refer to the custom our forefathers had of putting ashes on the head as a sign of sorrow and repentance.
  - **[[Matthew 12#^v37|Matthew 12:37]]**
    ![[Matthew 12#^v37]]
    We are judged by our words, for the Most High hears all our words and even our thoughts, so nothing can be withholden from Him.

**[[Job 42#^v7|Job 42:7-9]]**

![[Job 42#^v7]]
![[Job 42#^v8]]
![[Job 42#^v9]]

- After the Most High had spoken these words, He tells Eliphaz the Temanite that His wrath is kindled against him and his two friends, because they have not spoken of Him the thing that is right, as His servant Job has. Throughout the book Job's three friends dealt wrongly with him after his children were killed and everything was taken, so they did not deal righteously as their brother Job did.
- He commands them to take seven bullocks and seven rams and go to Job and offer a burnt offering for themselves, and Job will pray for them, for him will He accept, lest He deal with them after their folly. The sacrifice for their sin had to be brought to Job, the righteous one in the situation.
- This is how the priesthood worked, all the way from Leviticus and the five books throughout history: we brought the sacrifice to the Levites and priests and they did it on our behalf, and if a man did not bring his sacrifice, the Most High would deal with him and what he did would not be forgiven. Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite went and did as the Most High commanded, and He accepted Job.
  - **[[Job 1#^v1|Job 1:1]]**
    ![[Job 1#^v1]]
    Job was a perfect man, which is why the Most High held him up as the one who spoke rightly while his friends did not.

**[[Job 42#^v10|Job 42:10-11]]**

![[Job 42#^v10]]
![[Job 42#^v11]]

- The Most High turned the captivity of Job when he prayed for his friends, and gave him twice as much as he had before. It was not the prayer alone that availed, but the prayer of a righteous man, so the Most High deals with those that are dealing in righteousness.
- Then all his brethren, sisters, and former acquaintances came and ate bread with him, bemoaned him, and comforted him over all the evil the Most High had brought upon him, and every man gave him a piece of money and an earring of gold. After his captivity was turned his friends returned as well.
  - **[[James 5#^v16|James 5:16]]**
    ![[James 5#^v16]]
    Confess your faults one to another and pray one for another, for the effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much, and it was Job's righteous prayer that spared his friends.

- The Most High blessed the latter end of Job more than his beginning: fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, a thousand yoke of oxen, and a thousand she asses, along with seven sons and three daughters. He named the daughters Jemima, Kezia, and Keren-happuch, and in all the land were no women found so fair, and their father gave them inheritance among their brethren.
- Job lived a hundred and forty years after this and saw his sons and his sons' sons, even four generations, and died being old and full of days. He received much more than he had in the beginning, all because he stayed faithful to the Most High through the whole trial.
- The purpose of the trial was to see whether Job would fix himself and stay faithful or curse the Most High. The same thing happens to us: we endure to the end as Job did, and at some point the Most High will turn the captivity of the Israelites who kept the commandments and repay them for all their righteousness, far more than double the houses, families, cars, and jobs we have now, giving us much more in the kingdom.

    Satan comes before the Most High from going to and fro in the earth, and the whole point of the trial was to see whether Job would curse the Most High to His face once the hedge was removed.
  - **[[Revelation 14#^v12|Revelation 14:12]]**
    ![[Revelation 14#^v12]]
    Here is the patience and the faith of the saints, for we endure to the end just as Job endured his trial.
