# Job 17: My Days Are Extinct, the Graves Are Ready for Me

Read the chapter → [[Job 17]]

This session of "4 Chapters a Day" moves through [[Job 17|Job 17]] to 20, and it was read straight through with only a few points of exposition, because the class fell on the closing of the feast and the new moon and many were already traveling to the school feast. The spirit was put on the bishop and the leaders to have the congregation read four chapters a day and read the Bible in a year, and we started in Genesis and are now up to Job. In these chapters Job continues to answer his friends: his breath is corrupt and his days are extinct, Bildad the Shuhite paints the ruin of the wicked, Job cries that God has overthrown him and yet declares that his Redeemer liveth, and Zophar the Naamathite says the triumphing of the wicked is short. The teacher held these chapters lightly, told the class he was not going through precepts today and to post any questions to the captains, and pointed to the places where the reading is going into things that are happening right now.

**[[Job 17#^v1|Job 17:1-2]]**

![[Job 17#^v1]]
![[Job 17#^v2]]

- Job says his breath is corrupt, his days are extinct, and the graves are ready for him.
- He is surrounded by mockers, and his eye continues in their provocation, so his complaint is that even now the men around him do not comfort him but scorn him.

**[[Job 17#^v6|Job 17:6-9]]**

![[Job 17#^v6]]
![[Job 17#^v7]]
![[Job 17#^v8]]
![[Job 17#^v9]]

- Job has been made a byword of the people, a man in whose face others spit, and by reason of sorrow all his members are as a shadow.
- Even so, upright men shall be astonished at this, and the innocent shall stir up himself against the hypocrite.
- The righteous shall hold on his way, and he that hath clean hands shall be stronger and stronger, so the trial does not turn the righteous back but presses him forward.

**[[Job 17#^v11|Job 17:11-16]]**

![[Job 17#^v11]]
![[Job 17#^v12]]
![[Job 17#^v13]]
![[Job 17#^v14]]
![[Job 17#^v15]]
![[Job 17#^v16]]

- Job's purposes are broken off and the thoughts of his heart are undone, so that he speaks as a man with no future left in this life.
- He has made his bed in the darkness, called corruption his father and the worm his mother and sister, and asks where his hope now is.
- His hope goes down with him to the bars of the pit, when their rest together is in the dust, which is Job speaking as one who looks only to the grave.

# Job 18: The Light of the Wicked Shall Be Put Out

Read the chapter → [[Job 18]]

**[[Job 18#^v1|Job 18:1-4]]**

![[Job 18#^v1]]
![[Job 18#^v2]]
![[Job 18#^v3]]
![[Job 18#^v4]]

- Bildad the Shuhite answers and asks Job how long it will be before he makes an end of words, telling him to mark, and afterward they will speak.
- He is offended that Job counts them as beasts and reputes them vile, and says Job tears himself in his anger, as if the earth should be forsaken for his sake.

**[[Job 18#^v5|Job 18:5-11]]**

![[Job 18#^v5]]

- Bildad lays out the doctrine that the light of the wicked shall be put out and the spark of his fire shall not shine.
- The wicked man is cast into a net by his own feet, the gin takes him by the heel, and the robber prevails against him, so his own steps bring him down.
- Terrors make him afraid on every side and drive him to his feet, which is the picture of a man overtaken by the ruin he set for himself.

- His confidence is rooted out of his tabernacle and he is brought to the king of terrors, his remembrance perishes from the earth, and he has no name in the street.
- He is driven from light into darkness and chased out of the world, with neither son nor nephew nor any remaining in his dwellings, and this is the place of him that knoweth not God.

# Job 19: I Know That My Redeemer Liveth

Read the chapter → [[Job 19]]

**[[Job 19#^v1|Job 19:1-6]]**

![[Job 19#^v1]]
![[Job 19#^v2]]
![[Job 19#^v3]]
![[Job 19#^v4]]
![[Job 19#^v5]]

- Job answers and asks how long they will vex his soul and break him in pieces with words, saying they have reproached him ten times and are not ashamed.
- He grants that if indeed he has erred, his error remains with himself, but tells them that God has overthrown him and compassed him with his net.

- Job cries out of wrong and is not heard, and there is no judgment, for God has fenced up his way and set darkness in his paths.
- God has stripped him of his glory, taken the crown from his head, destroyed him on every side, and removed his hope like a tree that is pulled up.
- God's troops come together and raise up their way against him and camp round about his tabernacle, so Job feels himself besieged by the hand of the Most High.

- Job's brethren are put far from him, his acquaintance are estranged, his kinsfolk have failed, and his familiar friends have forgotten him.
- He is an alien in the sight of his own household, his servant gives him no answer, and his breath is strange to his wife.
- Even young children despise him and those he loved are turned against him, so he pleads, Have pity upon me, O ye my friends, for the hand of God hath touched me.

**[[Job 19#^v23|Job 19:23-27]]**

![[Job 19#^v23]]
![[Job 19#^v24]]
![[Job 19#^v25]]

- Job wishes his words were written and printed in a book, graven with an iron pen and lead in the rock forever, so that this testimony would stand.
- He declares that he knows his Redeemer liveth and shall stand at the latter day upon the earth, and that though worms destroy his body, yet in his flesh shall he see God.
- Verses 25 through 27 are going into the regeneration; when you go back and read them, this is Job looking past the grave to seeing God for himself in a resurrected body.
  - **[[Matthew 19#^v28|Matthew 19:28]]**
    ![[Matthew 19#^v28]]

![[Job 19#^v28]]
![[Job 19#^v29]]

- Job warns his friends that if they say the root of the matter is found in him and keep persecuting him, they should be afraid of the sword themselves.
- The wrath brings the punishments of the sword, that they may know there is a judgment, so Job turns their own accusation back on them.

# Job 20: The Triumphing of the Wicked Is Short

Read the chapter → [[Job 20]]

The teacher said he was not going through precepts on this chapter, and that if there were questions the class should tune in or post them to the captains. He still marked the places in the chapter where the reading touches what is happening right now.

**[[Job 20#^v1|Job 20:1-5]]**

![[Job 20#^v1]]
![[Job 20#^v2]]
![[Job 20#^v3]]
![[Job 20#^v4]]
![[Job 20#^v5]]

- Zophar the Naamathite answers, saying his thoughts cause him to answer and for this he makes haste, having heard the check of his reproach.
- He asks whether Job does not know that since man was placed upon earth the triumphing of the wicked is short and the joy of the hypocrite is but for a moment.

**[[Job 20#^v6|Job 20:6-11]]**

![[Job 20#^v6]]
![[Job 20#^v7]]
![[Job 20#^v8]]
![[Job 20#^v9]]
![[Job 20#^v10]]
![[Job 20#^v11]]

- Though the wicked man's excellency mounts up to the heavens and his head reaches to the clouds, yet he shall perish forever like his own dung, and they that saw him shall say, Where is he.
- He flies away as a dream and is chased away as a vision of the night, and the eye that saw him shall see him no more.
- His bones are full of the sin of his youth, which shall lie down with him in the dust.
- Verses 5 and 11 are going on right now: the truth is springing out of the earth and this Bible is revealing him, so the short triumph of the wicked and the buried sin of his youth are being uncovered in this age.
  - **[[Psalms 85#^v11|Psalm 85:11]]**
    ![[Psalms 85#^v11]]
    Truth shall spring out of the earth, and righteousness shall look down from heaven, which is the uncovering happening now.

- Though wickedness is sweet in his mouth and he hides it under his tongue, yet his meat in his bowels is turned, it is the gall of asps within him.
- He has swallowed down riches, but God shall cast them out of his belly and he shall vomit them up again.
- He shall suck the poison of asps and the viper's tongue shall slay him, and he shall not see the rivers, the floods, the brooks of honey and butter.
- Because he has oppressed and forsaken the poor and violently taken away a house which he built not, that which he labored for he shall restore and shall not rejoice therein.

- The wicked man shall not feel quietness in his belly and shall not save of that which he desired, for there shall none of his meat be left.
- In the fullness of his sufficiency he shall be in straits, every hand of the wicked shall come upon him, and God shall cast the fury of his wrath upon him while he is eating.
- He flees from the iron weapon, but the bow of steel strikes him through, and the glittering sword comes out of his gall, with terrors upon him.
- The heaven reveals his iniquity and the earth rises up against him; this is the portion of a wicked man from God, and the heritage appointed unto him by God.
