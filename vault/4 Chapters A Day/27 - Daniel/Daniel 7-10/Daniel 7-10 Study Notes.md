# Daniel 7: The Four Beasts

Read the chapter → [[Daniel 7]]

This session covers [[Daniel 7|Daniel 7]] through 10. These four chapters go into the same subject from different angles: the vision of the four beasts, which are four kingdoms of men. Daniel sees them first as beasts coming up out of the sea, then again as a ram and a goat, and he is given the seventy weeks and a final vision by the river. The kingdoms are Babylon, Persia and the Medes, Greece, and Rome, with Rome's extension reaching into Spain, France, Great Britain, and America. Daniel is given to understand these dark sayings because he was greatly beloved of the Most High.

**[[Daniel 7#^v1|Daniel 7:1]]**
![[Daniel 7#^v1]]
- In the first year of Belshazzar king of Babylon, Daniel had a dream and visions of his head upon his bed, and he wrote the dream and told the sum of the matters.
- This dream is the same subject Daniel returns to in chapter 8 and again in the later chapters. He tells the vision more than once.

**[[Daniel 7#^v2|Daniel 7:2-4]]**
![[Daniel 7#^v2]]
![[Daniel 7#^v3]]
![[Daniel 7#^v4]]
- Daniel saw the four winds of heaven strive upon the great sea, and four great beasts came up from the sea, diverse one from another.
- These beasts are not animals; they are men. When the Most High speaks of beasts here, he is dealing with four kingdoms of men.
  - **[[Ecclesiastes 3#^v18|Ecclesiastes 3:18]]**
    ![[Ecclesiastes 3#^v18]]
    Concerning the estate of the sons of men, the Most High might manifest them that they themselves are beasts.
- The first beast was like a lion with eagle's wings, lifted up and made to stand upon its feet as a man. This first kingdom is Babylon.

**[[Daniel 7#^v5|Daniel 7:5]]**
![[Daniel 7#^v5]]
- The second beast was like a bear, raised up on one side, with three ribs in its mouth, told to arise and devour much flesh.
- This second beast is the Persians and the Medes. It raised itself higher on one side because one of those two kingdoms was stronger than the other.

**[[Daniel 7#^v6|Daniel 7:6]]**
![[Daniel 7#^v6]]
- The third beast was like a leopard with four wings of a fowl and four heads, and dominion was given to it.
- This third beast is the Greeks. The details and breakdowns are covered in the classes on these chapters.

**[[Daniel 7#^v7|Daniel 7:7]]**
![[Daniel 7#^v7]]
- The fourth beast was dreadful and terrible and strong exceedingly, with great iron teeth, devouring and breaking in pieces and stamping the residue with its feet, diverse from all the beasts before it.
- This fourth beast is the Romans. Out of Rome comes the extension: Rome, then Spain, France, Great Britain, and then America.

**[[Daniel 7#^v8|Daniel 7:8]]**
![[Daniel 7#^v8]]
- Daniel considered the horns, and behold, a little horn came up among them, before whom three of the first horns were plucked up by the roots, and in this horn were eyes like the eyes of a man and a mouth speaking great things.
- This little horn is the same horn that makes war with the saints later in the chapter.

**[[Daniel 7#^v9|Daniel 7:9-14]]**
![[Daniel 7#^v9]]
![[Daniel 7#^v10]]
![[Daniel 7#^v11]]
![[Daniel 7#^v12]]
![[Daniel 7#^v13]]
![[Daniel 7#^v14]]
- Daniel saw the thrones cast down and the Ancient of days sit, his garment white as snow and the hair of his head like pure wool, his throne like the fiery flame, a fiery stream issuing before him, thousand thousands ministering unto him, the judgment set and the books opened.
- The fourth beast is slain and its body given to the burning flame, while the rest of the beasts have their dominion taken away but their lives prolonged for a season.
- One like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven to the Ancient of days, and there was given him dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages should serve him, an everlasting dominion that shall not pass away.
- His coming with the clouds is his coming with his chariots, the same coming [[Matthew 24|Matthew 24]] speaks of.
  - **[[Matthew 24#^v30|Matthew 24:30]]**
    ![[Matthew 24#^v30]]
    The Son of man comes in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory.

**[[Daniel 7#^v15|Daniel 7:15-16]]**
![[Daniel 7#^v15]]
![[Daniel 7#^v16]]
- Daniel was grieved in his spirit and the visions of his head troubled him, so he came near unto one that stood by and asked the truth of all this, and was made to know the interpretation.
- The interpretation of the vision described in verses 1 through 14 now begins.

**[[Daniel 7#^v17|Daniel 7:17-18]]**
![[Daniel 7#^v17]]
![[Daniel 7#^v18]]
- The four great beasts are four kings that shall arise out of the earth.
- But the saints of the Most High shall take the kingdom and possess it forever, even for ever and ever.

**[[Daniel 7#^v19|Daniel 7:19-22]]**
![[Daniel 7#^v19]]
![[Daniel 7#^v20]]
![[Daniel 7#^v21]]
![[Daniel 7#^v22]]
- Daniel wanted the truth of the fourth beast, diverse from all the others, exceeding dreadful, with teeth of iron and nails of brass, and of the ten horns and the little horn with eyes and a mouth speaking great things.
- The ten horns are the ten common markets, as the bishop's class lays out. The little horn made war with the saints and prevailed against them.
- This continues until the Ancient of days came and judgment was given to the saints of the Most High, and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom.
  - **[[2 Esdras 12#^v11|2 Esdras 12:11-12]]**
    ![[2 Esdras 12#^v11]]
    ![[2 Esdras 12#^v12]]
    Second Esdras opens up that fourth beast, the eagle kingdom, which is an evil, tying it directly back to Daniel's vision.

**[[Daniel 7#^v23|Daniel 7:23-28]]**
![[Daniel 7#^v23]]
![[Daniel 7#^v24]]
![[Daniel 7#^v25]]
![[Daniel 7#^v26]]
![[Daniel 7#^v27]]
![[Daniel 7#^v28]]
- The fourth beast is the fourth kingdom upon the earth, diverse from all kingdoms, devouring the whole earth, treading it down and breaking it in pieces. The ten horns are ten kings, and another rises after them, subduing three.
- This last horn speaks great words against the Most High, wears out the saints, and thinks to change times and laws, and they are given into his hand until a time and times and the dividing of times.
- Then the judgment sits, his dominion is taken away and destroyed unto the end, and the kingdom and dominion under the whole heaven is given to the people of the saints of the Most High, whose kingdom is everlasting.
- Daniel kept the matter in his heart, his cogitations much troubling him and his countenance changed.

# Daniel 8: The Ram and the Goat

Read the chapter → [[Daniel 8]]

**[[Daniel 8#^v1|Daniel 8:1-2]]**
![[Daniel 8#^v1]]
![[Daniel 8#^v2]]
- In the third year of Belshazzar's reign, a vision appeared to Daniel after the first, and he saw himself at Shushan in the palace in the province of Elam, by the river of Ulai.
- This is the same vision as chapter 7, but now the kingdoms are described with different animals.

**[[Daniel 8#^v3|Daniel 8:3-4]]**
![[Daniel 8#^v3]]
![[Daniel 8#^v4]]
- Daniel saw a ram with two horns, one higher than the other, the higher coming up last, pushing westward, northward, and southward so that no beast could stand before him.
- This ram is the Persians and the Medes again. Before they were a bear; now they are a ram, with the two horns being the two kingdoms.

**[[Daniel 8#^v5|Daniel 8:5-8]]**
![[Daniel 8#^v5]]
![[Daniel 8#^v6]]
![[Daniel 8#^v7]]
![[Daniel 8#^v8]]
- A he goat came from the west over the face of the whole earth without touching the ground, with a notable horn between his eyes, and ran unto the ram in the fury of his power.
- This goat is Greece, Alexander the Greek. Moved with hatred, he smote the ram, broke his two horns, cast him down, and stamped upon him, and none could deliver the ram.
- When the goat was strong, the great horn was broken, and four notable horns came up toward the four winds of heaven.

**[[Daniel 8#^v9|Daniel 8:9-12]]**
![[Daniel 8#^v9]]
![[Daniel 8#^v10]]
![[Daniel 8#^v11]]
![[Daniel 8#^v12]]
- Out of one of them came a little horn which waxed exceeding great toward the south, the east, and the pleasant land, magnifying itself even to the host of heaven and casting down some of the host and the stars.
- It magnified itself against the prince of the host, took away the daily sacrifice, cast down the place of the sanctuary, and cast down the truth to the ground, practicing and prospering.

**[[Daniel 8#^v13|Daniel 8:13-14]]**
![[Daniel 8#^v13]]
![[Daniel 8#^v14]]
- A saint asked how long the vision concerning the daily sacrifice and the transgression of desolation should last, and the answer came: unto two thousand and three hundred days, then shall the sanctuary be cleansed.
- The cleansing of the sanctuary goes into Hanukkah, the feast of dedication. That history is recorded in First Maccabees.
  - **[[1 Maccabees 4#^v52|1 Maccabees 4:52-54]]**
    ![[1 Maccabees 4#^v52]]
    ![[1 Maccabees 4#^v53]]
    ![[1 Maccabees 4#^v54]]
    The sanctuary is dedicated again on the very day the heathen had profaned it, the dedication Daniel's cleansing points to.

**[[Daniel 8#^v15|Daniel 8:15-19]]**
![[Daniel 8#^v15]]
![[Daniel 8#^v16]]
![[Daniel 8#^v17]]
![[Daniel 8#^v18]]
![[Daniel 8#^v19]]
- As Daniel sought the meaning, one with the appearance of a man stood before him, and a voice called Gabriel to make him understand the vision.
- Gabriel came near, and Daniel was afraid and fell on his face, but was told to understand, for at the time of the end shall be the vision. Gabriel set him upright to know what shall be in the last end of the indignation.

**[[Daniel 8#^v20|Daniel 8:20-22]]**
![[Daniel 8#^v20]]
![[Daniel 8#^v21]]
![[Daniel 8#^v22]]
- The ram with two horns are the kings of Media and Persia. The rough goat is the king of Grecia, and the great horn between his eyes is the first king, Alexander the Greek.
- The great horn being broken and four standing up in its place are four kingdoms that stand up out of the nation but not in his power. These four were Alexander's four generals.

**[[Daniel 8#^v23|Daniel 8:23-25]]**
![[Daniel 8#^v23]]
![[Daniel 8#^v24]]
![[Daniel 8#^v25]]
- In the latter time of their kingdom, when the transgressors are come to the full, a king of fierce countenance understanding dark sentences shall stand up, mighty but not by his own power, destroying wonderfully and prospering, and destroying the mighty and the holy people.
- Through his policy he causes craft to prosper in his hand, magnifies himself, and by peace destroys many, standing up against the Prince of princes, but he shall be broken without hand.
- The nation that stands against the Prince of princes is the United States, Babylon the Great. This is how we know Greece, Rome, and America are an extension of the same beast.

**[[Daniel 8#^v26|Daniel 8:26-27]]**
![[Daniel 8#^v26]]
![[Daniel 8#^v27]]
- The vision of the evening and the morning is true, but Daniel was to shut it up, for it is for many days.
- Daniel fainted and was sick certain days, then rose and did the king's business, astonished at the vision, but none understood it. The Most High gives his prophets to understand dark sayings and visions.

# Daniel 9: The Seventy Weeks

Read the chapter → [[Daniel 9]]

**[[Daniel 9#^v1|Daniel 9:1-2]]**
![[Daniel 9#^v1]]
![[Daniel 9#^v2]]
- In the first year of Darius the son of Ahasuerus, of the seed of the Medes, made king over the realm of the Chaldeans, Daniel understood by the books the seventy years of the desolations of Jerusalem spoken to Jeremiah.
- This is the same Darius dealt with when the Persians and Medes were shown in chapters 7 and 8.

**[[Daniel 9#^v3|Daniel 9:3-6]]**
![[Daniel 9#^v3]]
![[Daniel 9#^v4]]
![[Daniel 9#^v5]]
![[Daniel 9#^v6]]
- Daniel set his face unto the Most High to seek him by prayer and supplications, with fasting, sackcloth, and ashes, confessing that the people had sinned, committed iniquity, done wickedly, and rebelled, departing from his precepts and judgments.
- They had not hearkened unto the prophets who spake in his name to their kings, princes, fathers, and all the people of the land.

**[[Daniel 9#^v7|Daniel 9:7-10]]**
![[Daniel 9#^v7]]
![[Daniel 9#^v8]]
![[Daniel 9#^v9]]
![[Daniel 9#^v10]]
- Righteousness belongs to the Most High, but confusion of faces to the people of Judah, the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and all Israel, near and far off, driven through all the countries because of their trespasses.
- Our people were scattered everywhere because of our transgressions, exactly as the law warned.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v64|Deuteronomy 28:64]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v64]]
    The Most High shall scatter thee among all people, from the one end of the earth even unto the other.
- To the Most High belong mercies and forgiveness, though the people had rebelled and had not obeyed his voice to walk in his laws set before them by the prophets.

**[[Daniel 9#^v11|Daniel 9:11-14]]**
![[Daniel 9#^v11]]
![[Daniel 9#^v12]]
![[Daniel 9#^v13]]
![[Daniel 9#^v14]]
- All Israel transgressed the law by departing, so the curse was poured upon them and the oath written in the law of Moses, because they had sinned against him.
- The oath that was written is [[Deuteronomy 28|Deuteronomy 28]], verses 1 through 68.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v15|Deuteronomy 28:15]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 28#^v15]]
    If thou wilt not hearken unto the voice of the Most High to do his commandments, all these curses shall come upon thee and overtake thee.
- He confirmed his words by bringing upon them a great evil, for under the whole heaven none was done as was done upon Jerusalem, yet even so they had not made their prayer to turn from their iniquities. The Most High is righteous in all his works.

**[[Daniel 9#^v15|Daniel 9:15-19]]**
![[Daniel 9#^v15]]
![[Daniel 9#^v16]]
![[Daniel 9#^v17]]
![[Daniel 9#^v18]]
![[Daniel 9#^v19]]
- Daniel appealed to the Most High who brought his people out of Egypt with a mighty hand, confessing they had sinned and done wickedly, and asking that his anger and fury be turned away from his city Jerusalem, his holy mountain.
- He pleaded for the Most High to hear, forgive, hearken, and do, not for their righteousness but for his great mercies, since the city and the people are called by his name.

**[[Daniel 9#^v20|Daniel 9:20-23]]**
![[Daniel 9#^v20]]
![[Daniel 9#^v21]]
![[Daniel 9#^v22]]
![[Daniel 9#^v23]]
- While Daniel was speaking, praying, and confessing his sin and the sin of his people Israel, the man Gabriel, whom he had seen in the vision, was caused to fly swiftly and touched him about the time of the evening oblation.
- Gabriel came to give him skill and understanding, for at the beginning of his supplications the commandment came forth, and Daniel was greatly beloved.

**[[Daniel 9#^v24|Daniel 9:24-27]]**
![[Daniel 9#^v24]]
![[Daniel 9#^v25]]
![[Daniel 9#^v26]]
![[Daniel 9#^v27]]
- Seventy weeks are determined upon the people and the holy city to finish the transgression, make an end of sins, make reconciliation for iniquity, bring in everlasting righteousness, seal up the vision, and anoint the most Holy.
- From the going forth of the commandment to restore and build Jerusalem unto Messiah the Prince shall be seven weeks and threescore and two weeks, the street built again even in troublous times.
- After threescore and two weeks Messiah is cut off, but not for himself, and the people of the prince that shall come destroy the city and the sanctuary. He confirms the covenant with many for one week, and in the midst of the week causes the sacrifice and oblation to cease.
- Daniel was given the four beasts first, then this further vision of the seventy weeks by Gabriel in the midst of his prayer and supplications. The bishop's class on the seventy weeks opens this up in full.

# Daniel 10: A Man Clothed in Linen

Read the chapter → [[Daniel 10]]

**[[Daniel 10#^v1|Daniel 10:1-3]]**
![[Daniel 10#^v1]]
![[Daniel 10#^v2]]
![[Daniel 10#^v3]]
- In the third year of Cyrus king of Persia, a thing was revealed unto Daniel, whose name was called Belteshazzar, and the thing was true but the time appointed was long, and he understood the vision.
- Daniel was mourning three full weeks, eating no pleasant bread, no flesh or wine, and did not anoint himself until the three weeks were fulfilled.

**[[Daniel 10#^v4|Daniel 10:4-9]]**
![[Daniel 10#^v4]]
![[Daniel 10#^v5]]
![[Daniel 10#^v6]]
![[Daniel 10#^v7]]
![[Daniel 10#^v8]]
![[Daniel 10#^v9]]
- By the side of the great river Hiddekel, Daniel lifted his eyes and saw a certain man clothed in linen, his loins girded with fine gold of Uphaz, his body like the beryl, his face as lightning, his eyes as lamps of fire, his arms and feet like polished brass, and his voice like the voice of a multitude.
- Daniel alone saw the vision; the men with him did not see it but a great quaking fell on them and they fled. Daniel was left alone, his strength turned to corruption, and he fell into a deep sleep on his face toward the ground.

**[[Daniel 10#^v10|Daniel 10:10-14]]**
![[Daniel 10#^v10]]
![[Daniel 10#^v11]]
![[Daniel 10#^v12]]
![[Daniel 10#^v13]]
![[Daniel 10#^v14]]
- A hand touched Daniel and set him upon his knees and palms, and he was told he is a man greatly beloved, to understand the words spoken and stand upright, for he was now sent to him.
- The one speaking told Daniel that from the first day he set his heart to understand and to chasten himself before the Most High, his words were heard, and he came for his words. The Most High heard Daniel's supplications and prayer because he was beloved, and gave him these visions for the same reason. It is the same with Israel, the beloved of the Most High.
  - **[[1 Kings 8#^v33|1 Kings 8:33-34]]**
    ![[1 Kings 8#^v33]]
    ![[1 Kings 8#^v34]]
    When Israel is smitten because they sinned, if they turn again and confess and make supplication, the Most High will hear and forgive.
- The prince of the kingdom of Persia withstood him twenty-one days, until Michael, one of the chief princes, came to help him. He came to make Daniel understand what shall befall his people in the latter days, for the vision is for many days.
- Michael and Gabriel contending with the prince of Persia and the prince of Grecia shows that there are angels set over cities and even governments. The Most High has his angels everywhere, as with the angels that came and dealt with Sodom and Gomorrah.

**[[Daniel 10#^v15|Daniel 10:15-19]]**
![[Daniel 10#^v15]]
![[Daniel 10#^v16]]
![[Daniel 10#^v17]]
![[Daniel 10#^v18]]
![[Daniel 10#^v19]]
- Daniel set his face toward the ground and became dumb, until one like the similitude of the sons of men touched his lips, and he opened his mouth and spoke of his sorrows and lack of strength.
- One like the appearance of a man touched him again and strengthened him, telling him not to fear, for he is greatly beloved, and Daniel was strengthened to hear his lord speak.

**[[Daniel 10#^v20|Daniel 10:20-21]]**
![[Daniel 10#^v20]]
![[Daniel 10#^v21]]
- The messenger told Daniel he would return to fight with the prince of Persia, and when he is gone forth, the prince of Grecia shall come.
- He would show Daniel that which is noted in the scripture of truth, and none holds with him in these things but Michael, Israel's prince. Even the close of chapter 10 validates chapters 7 and 8, letting it be known that Greece came and overthrew the Persians and the Medes.
