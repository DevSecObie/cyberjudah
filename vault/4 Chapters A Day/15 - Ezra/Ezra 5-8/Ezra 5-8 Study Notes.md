# Ezra 5: The Prophets Rise Up To Build

Read the chapter → [[Ezra 5]]

## Introduction

This session moves through Ezra chapters five through eight. It opens with the work on the house of the Most High being hindered by adversaries, then follows the decrees of Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes that funded and protected the rebuilding, the dedication of the house and the keeping of the passover in Jerusalem, and finally Ezra the ready scribe preparing his heart to seek the law and leading a company back from Babylon. As a why-we-study preface, the word and the judgments of the Most High are the heritage of Israel and no other nation.

  - **[[Psalms 147#^v19|Psalms 147:19-20]]**
    ![[Psalms 147#^v19]]
    ![[Psalms 147#^v20]]
    The word, statutes, and judgments are given to Jacob alone and belong to Israel as their heritage.

**[[Ezra 5#^v1|Ezra 5:1-2]]**

![[Ezra 5#^v1]]
![[Ezra 5#^v2]]

- After the work had been hindered for roughly two years, the prophets Haggai and Zechariah stirred up the leaders, and Zerubbabel and Jeshua rose again to build the house of God.
- The prophets came together to accomplish a common mission. The work of the house does not get done unless the prophets and the people gather and labor together.

  - **[[Zephaniah 2#^v1|Zephaniah 2:1-3]]**
    ![[Zephaniah 2#^v1]]
    ![[Zephaniah 2#^v2]]
    ![[Zephaniah 2#^v3]]
    The nation is called to gather itself together and seek the Most High before the day of his anger.
  - **[[Genesis 49#^v1|Genesis 49:1-2]]**
    ![[Genesis 49#^v1]]
    ![[Genesis 49#^v2]]
    Jacob calls his sons to gather together so they may hear what shall befall them in the last days.

**[[Ezra 5#^v3|Ezra 5:3-5]]**

![[Ezra 5#^v3]]
![[Ezra 5#^v4]]
![[Ezra 5#^v5]]

- Tatnai the governor and his companions challenged the elders, demanding who commanded them to build the house and the wall.
- The eye of their God was upon the elders of the Jews, so the adversaries could not force the work to stop while a letter went to Darius.

**[[Ezra 5#^v11|Ezra 5:11-13]]**

![[Ezra 5#^v11]]
![[Ezra 5#^v12]]
![[Ezra 5#^v13]]

- The elders answered plainly that they are servants of the God of heaven and earth rebuilding a house a great king of Israel had built long before.
- They confessed that the fathers provoked the God of heaven, who delivered them into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar and carried them to Babylon. This captivity is read in Second Kings, where Assyria took Israel and later Babylon took Judah.

  - **[[2 Kings 17#^v6|2 Kings 17:6-7]]**
    ![[2 Kings 17#^v6]]
    ![[2 Kings 17#^v7]]
    Assyria carried Israel away captive because they had sinned against the Most High their God.

**[[Ezra 5#^v14|Ezra 5:14-17]]**

![[Ezra 5#^v14]]
![[Ezra 5#^v15]]
![[Ezra 5#^v16]]
![[Ezra 5#^v17]]

- Cyrus is called king of Babylon because he conquered Babylon and took possession of all it held, though he reigned out of Persia. He restored the gold and silver vessels Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple.
- The letter asks Darius to search the king's treasure house to confirm that Cyrus made a decree to build the house of God at Jerusalem. The heathen always keep records on the children of Israel, so the matter had to be proven from the rolls.

# Ezra 6: The Decree Confirmed And The House Dedicated

Read the chapter → [[Ezra 6]]

**[[Ezra 6#^v1|Ezra 6:1-5]]**

![[Ezra 6#^v1]]
![[Ezra 6#^v2]]
![[Ezra 6#^v3]]
![[Ezra 6#^v4]]
![[Ezra 6#^v5]]

- Darius made search, and in the palace at Achmetha in the province of the Medes the roll was found recording the decree of Cyrus.
- The decree ordered the house rebuilt with strong foundations and set dimensions, the expenses given out of the king's house, and the gold and silver vessels restored to their place. Cyrus helped fund the rebuilding of the house of Israel.

**[[Ezra 6#^v6|Ezra 6:6-8]]**

![[Ezra 6#^v6]]
![[Ezra 6#^v7]]
![[Ezra 6#^v8]]

- Darius commanded Tatnai and his companions to leave the work alone and let the elders of the Jews build.
- He decreed that the cost be paid from the king's tribute beyond the river so the builders would not be hindered. Once a Persian decree was made it was upheld rather than vetoed. Even in Esther a decree against the Jews was not revoked but answered by a stronger decree in their favor.

  - **[[Esther 8#^v8|Esther 8:8]]**
    ![[Esther 8#^v8]]
    A writing sealed with the king's ring may not be reversed, so a second decree is made to deliver the Jews.

**[[Ezra 6#^v9|Ezra 6:9-12]]**

![[Ezra 6#^v9]]
![[Ezra 6#^v10]]
![[Ezra 6#^v11]]
![[Ezra 6#^v12]]

- The king ordered daily provision of bullocks, rams, lambs, wheat, salt, wine, and oil so the priests could offer sacrifices without fail, and that they might pray for the life of the king and his sons.
- Whoever altered the decree was to be hanged on timber pulled from his own house, and his house made a dunghill. The God who caused his name to dwell there was called on to destroy any king or people who tried to destroy this house.

**[[Ezra 6#^v14|Ezra 6:14-18]]**

![[Ezra 6#^v14]]
![[Ezra 6#^v15]]
![[Ezra 6#^v16]]
![[Ezra 6#^v17]]
![[Ezra 6#^v18]]

- The elders prospered through the prophesying of Haggai and Zechariah and finished the house by the commandment of the Most High and of the Persian kings.
- The house was finished, and the children of the captivity kept the dedication with joy, offering a hundred bullocks, two hundred rams, four hundred lambs, and twelve he goats for a sin offering according to the number of the tribes of Israel. This was a great praise, and the Levites' hands were full doing the service.
- The priests were set in their divisions and the Levites in their courses as it is written in the book of Moses, every man having his ordered task.

  - **[[Numbers 3#^v5|Numbers 3:5-9]]**
    ![[Numbers 3#^v5]]
    ![[Numbers 3#^v6]]
    ![[Numbers 3#^v7]]
    ![[Numbers 3#^v8]]
    ![[Numbers 3#^v9]]
    The tribe of Levi is given to Aaron and his sons to keep the charge and do the service of the tabernacle.

**[[Ezra 6#^v19|Ezra 6:19-22]]**

![[Ezra 6#^v19]]
![[Ezra 6#^v20]]
![[Ezra 6#^v21]]
![[Ezra 6#^v22]]

- The children of the captivity kept the passover on the fourteenth day of the first month, for the priests and Levites were purified together and killed the passover for all the captivity.
- The passover can be kept in captivity with joyfulness and gladness of heart. Going back to the past shows how Israel is meant to operate, and these had the kingdom mindset to see the vision and finish the work.

# Ezra 7: Ezra The Ready Scribe Prepares His Heart

Read the chapter → [[Ezra 7]]

**[[Ezra 7#^v1|Ezra 7:1-6]]**

![[Ezra 7#^v1]]
![[Ezra 7#^v2]]
![[Ezra 7#^v3]]
![[Ezra 7#^v4]]
![[Ezra 7#^v5]]
![[Ezra 7#^v6]]

- Ezra's genealogy is traced back through Zadok and Phinehas to Eleazar the son of Aaron the chief priest, marking him a priest of the true line.
- Ezra went up from Babylon a ready scribe in the law of Moses, and the king granted all his requests because the hand of the Most High was upon him.

**[[Ezra 7#^v7|Ezra 7:7-10]]**

![[Ezra 7#^v7]]
![[Ezra 7#^v8]]
![[Ezra 7#^v9]]
![[Ezra 7#^v10]]

- Some of the children of Israel, priests, Levites, singers, porters, and Nethinims went up to Jerusalem in the seventh year of Artaxerxes, and Ezra came in the fifth month by the good hand of his God.
- Ezra prepared his heart to seek the law of the Most High, to do it, and to teach statutes and judgments in Israel. Preparing the heart means setting the mind and spirit in order, examining oneself, and putting away the weak nature of sin before teaching others.

  - **[[2 Esdras 14#^v13|2 Esdras 14:13-14]]**
    ![[2 Esdras 14#^v13]]
    ![[2 Esdras 14#^v14]]
    Preparing the heart is setting the house in order and casting off mortal thoughts and the weak nature.
  - **[[1 Esdras 8#^v7|1 Esdras 8:7]]**
    ![[1 Esdras 8#^v7]]
    Ezra's great skill in the word came because he prayed and fasted to understand, and the Most High did great works through him.
  - **[[James 1#^v5|James 1:5]]**
    ![[James 1#^v5]]
    Whoever lacks wisdom may ask of God, who gives to all men liberally, and it shall be given.

**[[Ezra 7#^v11|Ezra 7:11-20]]**

![[Ezra 7#^v11]]
![[Ezra 7#^v12]]
![[Ezra 7#^v13]]
![[Ezra 7#^v14]]
![[Ezra 7#^v15]]
![[Ezra 7#^v16]]
![[Ezra 7#^v17]]
![[Ezra 7#^v18]]
![[Ezra 7#^v19]]
![[Ezra 7#^v20]]

- Artaxerxes' letter names Ezra a scribe of the law of the God of heaven and grants that any of Israel who willingly desire may go up to Jerusalem with him.
- The king and his counselors freely offered silver and gold for the house of God, for sacrifices, and for whatever else was needful, drawn even from the king's treasure house. This was favor, letting the captivity return with the treasures of Babylon, as the nations will one day bring back all Israel's substance.

  - **[[Isaiah 60#^v5|Isaiah 60:5-6]]**
    ![[Isaiah 60#^v5]]
    ![[Isaiah 60#^v6]]
    The abundance of the nations and their wealth shall be brought and turned unto restored Israel.

**[[Ezra 7#^v21|Ezra 7:21-24]]**

![[Ezra 7#^v21]]
![[Ezra 7#^v22]]
![[Ezra 7#^v23]]
![[Ezra 7#^v24]]

- Artaxerxes decreed to the treasurers beyond the river to supply Ezra speedily with silver, wheat, wine, oil, and salt, so that no wrath would come against the king and his sons.
- The priests, Levites, singers, porters, and Nethinims were freed from toll, tribute, and custom. This is using the favor of those set over you without abusing it, being granted the freedom the world around still had to pay.

**[[Ezra 7#^v25|Ezra 7:25-28]]**

![[Ezra 7#^v25]]
![[Ezra 7#^v26]]
![[Ezra 7#^v27]]
![[Ezra 7#^v28]]

- Ezra was told, after the wisdom of his God in his hand, to set magistrates and judges to judge the people and to teach those who did not know the laws of the Most High.
- The king decreed judgment on any who would not do the law of God and the law of the king. Ezra then blessed the Most High who put it in the king's heart to beautify the house, and was strengthened as the hand of his God was upon him. A heathen king supported the work of the prophets even though he was not an Israelite.

- The chief of the fathers and their genealogy are recorded, listing the heads of families who went up with Ezra from Babylon and the number of males with each.
- Every name is set down deliberately. These were actual people who put their work into the building of the house, and names mean something, which is why Israel desires their names written in the book.

**[[Ezra 8#^v15|Ezra 8:15-20]]**

![[Ezra 8#^v15]]

![[Ezra 8#^v18]]

- Ezra gathered the company at the river Ahava, abode three days, and viewing the people found none of the sons of Levi, so he sent men of understanding to Iddo at Casiphia to bring ministers for the house.
- By the good hand of the Most High they brought back men of understanding of the sons of Levi and a large number of Nethinims appointed for the service, all expressed by name. Ezra knew which men among him had understanding and called on them.

**[[Ezra 8#^v21|Ezra 8:21-22]]**

![[Ezra 8#^v21]]
![[Ezra 8#^v22]]

- Ezra proclaimed a fast at the river Ahava that they might afflict their souls before the Most High and seek of him a right way for themselves, their little ones, and their substance. To afflict the soul is to fast, taking neither food nor water, and one may fast and pray for wisdom and a right path.
- Ezra was ashamed to require of the king a band of soldiers and horsemen to help against the enemy in the way, having testified that the hand of God is upon all who seek him for good.

  - **[[Isaiah 58#^v5|Isaiah 58:5]]**
    ![[Isaiah 58#^v5]]
    The fast the Most High chooses is a day to afflict the soul before him.
  - **[[James 1#^v5|James 1:5]]**
    ![[James 1#^v5]]
    Lacking wisdom, one may ask of God, who gives liberally, and it shall be given.

The recording ended at [[Ezra 8#^v22|Ezra 8:22]]. The remainder of the chapter is included here in full King James text so the chapter is complete for reading and study.
