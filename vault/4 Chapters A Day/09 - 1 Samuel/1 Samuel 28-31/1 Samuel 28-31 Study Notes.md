# 1 Samuel 28: Saul and the Woman with the Familiar Spirit

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 28]]

## Introduction

This session covers 1 Samuel chapters 28 through 31, the closing chapters of the book and the end of the history of Saul. The recording rejoins the class at [[1 Samuel 28#^v15|1 Samuel 28:15]], the opening of the chapter having been read before a connection break. Saul, cut off from the Most High for his disobedience, seeks out a woman with a familiar spirit and hears his own death prophesied; the Philistine lords send David home rather than trust an enemy in their ranks; David recovers all that the Amalekites carried away from Ziklag; and Saul and his sons fall on mount Gilboa exactly as Samuel spoke. With chapter 31 the history of Saul comes to an end, and the second book of Samuel takes up the rise and the history of King David.

**[[1 Samuel 28#^v15|1 Samuel 28:15]]**

![[1 Samuel 28#^v15]]

- What Saul was doing when he went to the woman with the familiar spirit is called necromancy. Look that word up: it is when you go to somebody that conjures up spirits.
- Saul turned to this because the Lord was departed from him and answered him no more, neither by prophets nor by dreams, so he had Samuel conjured up to tell him what to do.

**[[1 Samuel 28#^v16|1 Samuel 28:16-18]]**

![[1 Samuel 28#^v16]]
![[1 Samuel 28#^v17]]
![[1 Samuel 28#^v18]]

- Samuel asks Saul why he inquires of him, seeing the Lord is departed from him and is become his enemy. The Lord did to Saul exactly as he spake by Samuel: he rent the kingdom out of Saul's hand and gave it to his neighbour, even to David.
- The reason is stated plainly: because Saul obeyed not the voice of the Lord, nor executed his fierce wrath upon Amalek. The Most High gave a law concerning Amalek, and Saul did not carry it out; that history was read earlier in this book.

- The woman reminded Saul that she had put her life in her hand and obeyed his voice, and asked him to let her set a morsel of bread before him that he might have strength for his way. He refused at first, but his servants together with the woman compelled him; she killed the fat calf, baked unleavened bread, and they did eat and went away that night.
- The chapter in sum: Saul inquired of the Lord and was not answered, so he turned to a woman with a familiar spirit and conjured up Samuel. Samuel told him that because of his disobedience the Lord was not dealing with him anymore, and prophesied that tomorrow Saul and his sons would be with him.

# 1 Samuel 29: Never Trust Thine Enemy

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 29]]

**[[1 Samuel 29#^v1|1 Samuel 29:1-5]]**

![[1 Samuel 29#^v1]]
![[1 Samuel 29#^v2]]
![[1 Samuel 29#^v3]]
![[1 Samuel 29#^v4]]
![[1 Samuel 29#^v5]]

- The Philistines gathered all their armies to Aphek, and Israel pitched by a fountain in Jezreel. David and his men passed on in the rearward with Achish, and the princes of the Philistines demanded, What do these Hebrews here?
- Achish vouched for David: he has been with me these days, or these years, and I have found no fault in him since he fell unto me. But the princes were wroth and said, Make this fellow return to his place; let him not go down with us to the battle, lest in the battle he be an adversary to us. For wherewith should he reconcile himself unto his master? should it not be with the heads of these men?
- The princes remembered the song: is not this David of whom they sang one to another in dances, saying, Saul slew his thousands, and David his ten thousands?

**[[1 Samuel 29#^v6|1 Samuel 29:6-11]]**

![[1 Samuel 29#^v6]]
![[1 Samuel 29#^v7]]
![[1 Samuel 29#^v8]]
![[1 Samuel 29#^v9]]
![[1 Samuel 29#^v10]]
![[1 Samuel 29#^v11]]

- Achish told David, As the Lord liveth, thou hast been upright, and thy going out and thy coming in with me in the host is good in my sight; I have not found evil in thee. Nevertheless the lords favour thee not: return, and go in peace. David protested, What have I done, that I may not go fight against the enemies of my lord the king? But Achish answered that though David was good in his sight as an angel of God, the princes had said he shall not go up with us to the battle. So David and his men rose up early and returned into the land of the Philistines, and the Philistines went up to Jezreel.
- One of the things to take from this reading is that they have more sense than we do. The lords of the Philistines told Achish, No, he cannot go to battle with us, because we will always be looking over our shoulders wondering if he is going to change his mind and go back unto his people.
- This is the wisdom we have to apply today. There is always an outsider who dresses like us, talks like us, and gets himself accepted among our people; yet even the Philistines knew: he might be good to you, he might be serving you, but do not trust your enemy.

# 1 Samuel 30: David Recovers All at Ziklag

- While David and his men were away with Achish, the Amalekites invaded the south and Ziklag, smote Ziklag and burned it with fire, and carried the women captive; they slew not any, great or small, but carried them away. David and his men came to the city on the third day, found it burned, and their wives and sons and daughters taken captive; they lifted up their voice and wept until they had no more power to weep.
- David's two wives were taken, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess and Abigail the wife of Nabal the Carmelite. David was greatly distressed, for the people spake of stoning him, because the soul of all the people was grieved, every man for his sons and his daughters; but David encouraged himself in the Lord his God.
- Notice how this connects to yesterday's reading in chapter 27. Achish had given David Ziklag, and David and his men had gone up and invaded the Amalekites and the other old inhabitants of the land, leaving neither man nor woman alive. Now the Amalekites came back and smote Ziklag the same way, taking David's wives and the families of his men while they were gone up with Achish.

- They found an Egyptian in the field and brought him to David; they gave him bread, water, a piece of a cake of figs, and two clusters of raisins, and when he had eaten his spirit came again to him, for he had eaten no bread nor drunk any water three days and three nights.
- A sidebar worth saying: this man was faint because he had gone without food three days and three nights. Some of our people are like that after fasting one day on the last Friday of the month; we have got to check our spirits.
- He was a young man of Egypt, servant to an Amalekite, left behind because he fell sick three days before; his company had invaded the south of the Cherethites, the coast of Judah, and the south of Caleb, and burned Ziklag with fire. David asked, Canst thou bring me down to this company? - When David came back to the two hundred men at the brook Besor, all the wicked men and men of Belial among those that went with David said, Because they went not with us, we will not give them ought of the spoil that we have recovered, save every man his wife and his children. Notice these were David's own men, yet the scripture calls them wicked men and men of Belial; David called that thinking of the devil, saying they had the devil on them for even thinking like that.
- That is the same spirit a lot of our people have today: I went down there, my hands got me this, you have got to get your own. David said, Ye shall not do so, my brethren, with that which the Lord hath given us, who hath preserved us and delivered the company that came against us into our hand.
- The ruling: as his part is that goeth down to the battle, so shall his part be that tarrieth by the stuff; they shall part alike. Those that went down into the battle split the spoil with those that stayed behind, and from that day forward David made it a statute and an ordinance for Israel unto this day.

# 1 Samuel 31: The Death of Saul

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 31]]

**[[1 Samuel 31#^v1|1 Samuel 31:1-3]]**

![[1 Samuel 31#^v1]]
![[1 Samuel 31#^v2]]
![[1 Samuel 31#^v3]]

- Now the Philistines fought against Israel, and the men of Israel fled from before the Philistines and fell down slain in mount Gilboa. The Philistines followed hard upon Saul and upon his sons, and slew Jonathan, and Abinadab, and Malchishua, Saul's sons.
- The battle went sore against Saul, and the archers hit him, and he was sore wounded of the archers.

**[[1 Samuel 31#^v4|1 Samuel 31:4-6]]**

![[1 Samuel 31#^v4]]
![[1 Samuel 31#^v5]]
![[1 Samuel 31#^v6]]

- Saul said unto his armourbearer, Draw thy sword, and thrust me through therewith; lest these uncircumcised come and thrust me through, and abuse me. What Saul was fearful of is what you see in war, how men will abuse their captives, killing them and abusing their bodies. He was already wounded and could not get away, so instead of being taken captive he told his armourbearer to kill him.
- His armourbearer would not, for he was sore afraid; therefore Saul took a sword and fell upon it, and when his armourbearer saw that Saul was dead, he fell likewise upon his sword and died with him. So Saul died, and his three sons, and his armourbearer, and all his men, that same day together.
- Remember what we read in chapter 28: when Saul went and conjured up the spirit of Samuel, Samuel said to him, To morrow shalt thou and thy sons be with me. He prophesied unto Saul what was going to happen, and now we read the fulfillment of that prophecy.

- They put his armour in the house of Ashtaroth, and they fastened his body to the wall of Bethshan.

- When the inhabitants of Jabeshgilead heard of that which the Philistines had done to Saul, all the valiant men arose and went all night, and took the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons from the wall of Bethshan, and came to Jabesh and burnt them there. They took their bones and buried them under a tree at Jabesh, and fasted seven days.
- So in chapter 31 we read of Saul and his sons and his armourbearer dying at the hands of the Philistines. This concludes the history of Saul coming to an end; the second book of Samuel takes up the rise and much of the history concerning King David.
