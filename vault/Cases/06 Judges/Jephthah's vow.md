# Jephthah's vow
**A rash vow, and its performance on his daughter** · Temporal judgment · Judges · [[Cases index]]

## Summary
Before battle with Ammon, Jephthah vowed that whatever came out of his doors to meet him on his return would be the Lord's and offered as a burnt offering. His only child came out with timbrels. He did with her according to his vow.

## Offense
The vow itself was the sin: sworn thoughtlessly with the lips, as Leviticus describes, binding himself to a thing God never asked and, on the plain reading, forbade. Ecclesiastes: suffer not thy mouth to cause thy flesh to sin. The text records that she bewailed her virginity and knew no man; readers have long divided over whether the sacrifice was literal or a life devoted, and the text does not settle it.

## Judgment
He lost his only child and his line ended with him. The daughters of Israel kept a yearly lament for her. Jephthah is nonetheless counted among the faithful in Hebrews 11, which is the record's own tension.
