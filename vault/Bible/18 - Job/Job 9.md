# Job 9

1 Then Job answered and said, ^v1

2 I know it is so of a truth: but how should man be just with God? ^v2

3 If he will contend with him, he cannot answer him one of a thousand. ^v3

4 He is wise in heart, and mighty in strength: who hath hardened himself against him, and hath prospered? ^v4

5 Which removeth the mountains, and they know not: which overturneth them in his anger. ^v5

6 Which shaketh the earth out of her place, and the pillars thereof tremble. ^v6

7 Which commandeth the sun, and it riseth not; and sealeth up the stars. ^v7

8 Which alone spreadeth out the heavens, and treadeth upon the waves of the sea. ^v8

9 Which maketh Arcturus, Orion, and Pleiades, and the chambers of the south. ^v9

10 Which doeth great things past finding out; yea, and wonders without number. ^v10

11 Lo, he goeth by me, and I see him not: he passeth on also, but I perceive him not. ^v11

12 Behold, he taketh away, who can hinder him? who will say unto him, What doest thou? ^v12

13 If God will not withdraw his anger, the proud helpers do stoop under him. ^v13

14 How much less shall I answer him, and choose out my words to reason with him? ^v14

15 Whom, though I were righteous, yet would I not answer, but I would make supplication to my judge. ^v15

16 If I had called, and he had answered me; yet would I not believe that he had hearkened unto my voice. ^v16

17 For he breaketh me with a tempest, and multiplieth my wounds without cause. ^v17

18 He will not suffer me to take my breath, but filleth me with bitterness. ^v18

19 If I speak of strength, lo, he is strong: and if of judgment, who shall set me a time to plead? ^v19

20 If I justify myself, mine own mouth shall condemn me: if I say, I am perfect, it shall also prove me perverse. ^v20

21 Though I were perfect, yet would I not know my soul: I would despise my life. ^v21

22 This is one thing, therefore I said it, He destroyeth the perfect and the wicked. ^v22

23 If the scourge slay suddenly, he will laugh at the trial of the innocent. ^v23

24 The earth is given into the hand of the wicked: he covereth the faces of the judges thereof; if not, where, and who is he? ^v24

25 Now my days are swifter than a post: they flee away, they see no good. ^v25

26 They are passed away as the swift ships: as the eagle that hasteth to the prey. ^v26

27 If I say, I will forget my complaint, I will leave off my heaviness, and comfort myself: ^v27

28 I am afraid of all my sorrows, I know that thou wilt not hold me innocent. ^v28

29 If I be wicked, why then labour I in vain? ^v29

30 If I wash myself with snow water, and make my hands never so clean; ^v30

31 Yet shalt thou plunge me in the ditch, and mine own clothes shall abhor me. ^v31

32 For he is not a man, as I am, that I should answer him, and we should come together in judgment. ^v32

33 Neither is there any daysman betwixt us, that might lay his hand upon us both. ^v33

34 Let him take his rod away from me, and let not his fear terrify me: ^v34

35 Then would I speak, and not fear him; but it is not so with me. ^v35
