# Peter at Antioch
**Dissembling: withdrawing from the Gentiles for fear of them of the circumcision** · Reprieve · Apostolic · [[Cases index]]

## Summary
At Antioch Peter had eaten with the Gentiles until certain came from James; then he withdrew and separated himself, fearing them which were of the circumcision, and the other Jews and even Barnabas were carried away with his dissimulation. Paul withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed.

## Offense
Hypocrisy in a pillar of the church, contradicting the vision God had given him at Joppa and the judgment he himself had argued at Jerusalem. Paul's charge: they walked not uprightly according to the truth of the gospel, and by the example compelled the Gentiles to live as do the Jews.

## Judgment
A public rebuke before them all, and no more; the epistle records the correction and leaves the reconciliation to be inferred. Peter later commends Paul's letters as scripture. The case stands as the law's own rule for reproof: a trespass taken openly to the transgressor.
