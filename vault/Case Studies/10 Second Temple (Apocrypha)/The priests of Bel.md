# The priests of Bel
**Deceiving the king and the people with an idol that seemed to eat** · Put to death · Second Temple (Apocrypha) · [[Cases index]]

## Summary
The Babylonians spent twelve measures of fine flour, forty sheep, and six vessels of wine daily on Bel, and the king asked Daniel why he would not worship a living god who ate so much. Daniel laughed. Ashes strewn on the temple floor showed the footprints of the seventy priests, their wives and children, who came in by a secret door under the table every night.

## Offense
Priests feeding themselves on offerings made to a lump of clay and brass, and threatening Daniel with death to protect the trade. The Epistle of Jeremiah describes the same racket: as for the things sacrificed unto them, their priests sell and abuse.

## Judgment
Therefore the king slew them, and delivered Bel into Daniel's power, who destroyed him and his temple. When Daniel then destroyed the dragon, the Babylonians forced the king to throw him to the lions; he came out unharmed, and those that were the cause of his destruction were cast into the den and devoured in a moment.
