# Exodus 18: Jethro's Counsel and Righteous Leadership

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 18]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Exodus 18|Exodus 18]] through 21: Jethro's counsel to Moses and the structure of righteous leadership, the covenant at Mount Sinai, the Ten Commandments, and the judgments set before Israel. Each chapter is read through, then the highlighted portions are opened with precepts.

![[Exodus 18#^v1]]
![[Exodus 18#^v2]]

![[Exodus 18#^v6]]

- Jethro the priest of Midian, Moses' father in law, heard all that God had done for Moses and for Israel, and came to Moses in the wilderness with Zipporah and her two sons, Gershom and Eliezer.
- Jethro is the same man called Reuel in Exodus 2: the priest of Midian whose seven daughters Moses helped at the well, and who gave Moses Zipporah his daughter. Reuel and Jethro are one person.
- [[Exodus 2|Exodus 2]] also holds one of the color scriptures: the daughters told their father an Egyptian delivered them out of the hand of the shepherds. Moses, an Israelite, was taken for an Egyptian, because Israel looked like the Egyptians.
- Jethro was not an Israelite; he and his kindred were not of the tribes.

- Other non-Israelites came to that same knowledge. All the gods of the nations are idols, but the Lord made the heavens; give unto the Lord the glory due unto his name.
- Naaman the Syrian, healed of his leprosy in Jordan, confessed that there is no God in all the earth but in Israel. His faith was so strong he vowed to offer neither burnt offering nor sacrifice unto other gods, and asked pardon for even bowing in the house of Rimmon with his master.
- Nebuchadnezzar came to praise and extol and honour the King of heaven, for those that walk in pride he is able to abase. The heathen understand that the Most High is greater than all other gods; it is our people that want a god that does not look like them.

- Able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness, were set as rulers of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens. This is the structure of righteous leadership the Most High established: captains and officers over the tribes, offices never heard in the church.
- The judges were charged to hear the causes between brethren and judge righteously, without respect of persons, not afraid of the face of man, for the judgment is God's. The hard causes went up to Moses, as the hard matters today go to the high-ranking officers and ultimately the bishop, while the smaller matters are judged by the lower-ranking officers.
- Judgment did not pass away with the Old Testament. He that is spiritual judgeth all things, and the spiritual man is the man that keeps the commandments. The saints shall judge the world, so brethren should judge the smallest matters among themselves instead of going to law before the unjust; only matters of life and death under today's system go outside.

# Exodus 19: The Covenant at Mount Sinai

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 19]]

**[[Exodus 19#^v1|Exodus 19:1-2]]**

- In the third month after going forth out of Egypt, Israel departed from Rephidim, came into the wilderness of Sinai, and camped before the mount. The journeys are recorded in Numbers 33: Israel departed from Rameses on the fifteenth day of the first month, on the morrow after the Passover, and later pitched in the wilderness of Sinai.
- Israel went forth out of Egypt with their armies. The tribes are armies, for the Lord is a man of war; Israel has always been at war and is at war today, a spiritual war.
- Moses wrote their goings out by the commandment of the Lord. The Lord gave the word, and great was the company of those that published it; so the claim that another people wrote the scriptures falls, for Moses wrote what God commanded him to write.

- Bounds were set round about the mount: whosoever touched it, whether beast or man, was to be stoned or shot through, and no hand was to touch it.
- The Most High is serious about that charge. When the oxen shook the cart, Uzzah put forth his hand to the ark of God and took hold of it, and the anger of the Lord was kindled and he smote him there, for the stranger that came nigh the priests' office was to be put to death. Every family of the priesthood had its own responsibility in the tabernacle service, and none could transgress against another's charge; what the world later called unions came from that order.
- When the trumpet sounded long they were to come up to the mount. The trumpets were made for the calling of the assembly and for the journeys of the camps, and trumpets still gather the congregation at the new moons and at the start of classes.
- That trumpet in the mount was not blown by Moses, for Moses was down among the people sanctifying them; the trumpet came from heaven.

# Exodus 20: The Ten Commandments

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 20]]

**[[Exodus 20#^v4|Exodus 20:4-5]]**

![[Exodus 20#^v4]]
![[Exodus 20#^v5]]

- An image in itself is not the sin, except for that white image of the beast; making images to bow down to them and serve them is the transgression, for the Lord thy God is a jealous God.
- Visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation: Israel is yet this day in captivity, scattered for a reproach and a curse, according to all the iniquities of the fathers which departed from the Lord. Right now Israel is paying for the sins of the fathers; that is why the people are scattered to the four corners of the earth.

- And shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love me, and keep my commandments. Judith recounted how the Most High tried the fathers for the examination of their hearts: he tried Abraham and Isaac, and Jacob in Mesopotamia of Syria keeping the sheep of Laban, serving twenty years with his wages changed and given Leah when he looked for Rachel.
- In Judith's day there was no tribe, family, people, nor city that worshipped gods made with hands, as had been aforetime; for that cause she trusted the Lord would not despise the nation. Mercy rests on them that know none other God, not Allah, not Buddha, not a white Jesus.

# Exodus 21: The Judgments Set Before Israel

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 21]]

**[[Exodus 21#^v2|Exodus 21:2-6]]**

![[Exodus 21#^v2]]
![[Exodus 21#^v3]]
![[Exodus 21#^v4]]
![[Exodus 21#^v5]]
![[Exodus 21#^v6]]

- If thou buy an Hebrew servant, six years he shall serve, and in the seventh he shall go out free for nothing. If he came in by himself he goes out by himself; if he were married when he came in, his wife goes out with him.
- Deuteronomy repeats the same judgment for a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman sold unto thee. [[Nehemiah 5|Nehemiah 5]] shows how brethren came into servitude: in the dearth some mortgaged lands, vineyards, and houses to buy corn, some borrowed money for the king's tribute, and some brought their sons and daughters into bondage. The brother that waxes poor is not to be compelled to serve as a bondservant, but as an hired servant, and not ruled over harshly.
- If the master gave him a wife and she bare him sons or daughters, the wife and her children are the master's, and he goes out by himself; he did not bring them in. His two choices are to go out alone, or to plainly say, I love my master, my wife, and my children, have his ear bored through with an aul at the door post, and serve his master for ever.
- Zedekiah's covenant in Jeremiah confirms the law in the prophets: every man was to let his Hebrew manservant and maidservant go free at the appointed release.

**[[Exodus 21#^v7|Exodus 21:7-11]]**

![[Exodus 21#^v7]]
![[Exodus 21#^v8]]
![[Exodus 21#^v9]]
![[Exodus 21#^v10]]
![[Exodus 21#^v11]]

- If a man sell his daughter to be a maidservant, she shall not go out as the menservants do. The reason is that her master betrothed her to himself, or unto his son; she is a wife, not a six-year servant, so the release of the menservants does not apply to her.
- If she please not her master who hath betrothed her to himself, he must let her be redeemed; he has no power to sell her unto a strange nation, seeing he hath dealt deceitfully with her. The deceit is that he betrothed her to himself and then would not deal with her, keeping her to himself when she could have been another man's wife.
- If he take him another wife, her food, her raiment, and her duty of marriage shall he not diminish; the duty of marriage is the marriage bed. If he lay with her, she belongs to him and he cannot send her away; and if he do not these three unto her, then shall she go out free without money. He must take care of her.

**[[Exodus 21#^v13|Exodus 21:13-14]]**

![[Exodus 21#^v13]]
![[Exodus 21#^v14]]

- If a man lie not in wait, but God deliver him into his hand, a place was appointed whither he shall flee. That is the case of the slayer that kills his neighbour ignorantly, as when a man hews wood with his neighbour and the axe head slips from the helve and lights upon him that he die: he flees to the appointed city and lives, lest the avenger of blood overtake him while his heart is hot, for he was not worthy of death, having hated him not in time past.
- But if a man come presumptuously upon his neighbour to slay him with guile, thou shalt take him from mine altar, that he may die. Men would run and grab the horns of the altar, but wilful murder found no refuge there.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 19#^v4|Deuteronomy 19:4-6]]**

    ![[Deuteronomy 19#^v5]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 19#^v6]]
    The slayer that kills his neighbour ignorantly, as when the axe head slips from the helve, flees to the city of refuge and lives, for he was not worthy of death.

**[[Exodus 21#^v18|Exodus 21:18-19]]**

![[Exodus 21#^v18]]
![[Exodus 21#^v19]]

- If men strive and one smite another so that he keep his bed, he that smote him shall pay for the loss of his time and shall cause him to be thoroughly healed. That is restitution: paying for the time the man was down and could not work and provide for his family. That is what the court system calls a civil suit today.

**[[Exodus 21#^v20|Exodus 21:20-21]]**

![[Exodus 21#^v20]]
![[Exodus 21#^v21]]

- If a man smite his servant with a rod and he die under his hand, he shall surely be punished; but if he continue a day or two, he shall not be punished, for he is his money.
- The wisdom writings teach not to be ashamed of much correction of children, nor of making the side of an evil servant to bleed. An evil servant that will not get right could be corrected physically, so long as he was not killed.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 42#^v1|Ecclesiasticus 42:1, 5]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 42#^v1]]
    Be not ashamed of much correction of children, nor to make the side of an evil servant to bleed.

**[[Exodus 21#^v22|Exodus 21:22-25]]**

![[Exodus 21#^v22]]
![[Exodus 21#^v23]]
![[Exodus 21#^v24]]
![[Exodus 21#^v25]]

- If men strive and hurt a woman with child so that her fruit depart from her, this judgment covers the miscarriage: if no mischief follow, he is punished as the woman's husband lays upon him and the judges determine; and if any mischief follow, then life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, wound for wound.

**[[Exodus 21#^v28|Exodus 21:28-29]]**

![[Exodus 21#^v28]]
![[Exodus 21#^v29]]

- If an ox gore a man or a woman that they die, the ox is stoned and his flesh not eaten, and the owner is quit. But if the ox were wont to push with his horns in time past, and it hath been testified to his owner, and he hath not kept him in, and it kill a man or a woman, the ox is stoned and his owner also put to death.
- Most do not have oxen today, but the same judgment reaches the yard dogs, the pit bulls, German shepherds, and rottweilers. If the dog has bitten before and it was reported to you, and it gets out again through a snapped chain or a breach in the fence and mauls or kills somebody, the scripture holds the owner responsible unto death; that is why animal control writes the ticket and warns you to keep your dogs in.
- The law is slack right now, as Habakkuk says, so many punishments worthy of death in the scriptures, like rape or this judgment, come out today as a fine or a slap on the hand.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Habakkuk 1#^v4|Habakkuk 1:4]]**
    ![[Habakkuk 1#^v4]]
    The law is slacked, and judgment doth never go forth; that is why death-worthy offences draw light penalties today.
