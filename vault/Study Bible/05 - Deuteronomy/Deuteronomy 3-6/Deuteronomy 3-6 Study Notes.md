# Deuteronomy 3: The Battle Is the Lord's

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 3]]

## Introduction

This session covered Deuteronomy 3-6. As Israel nears the end of the first five books, the thread of each is worth holding: Genesis is the beginning, the book of your genes and genealogy; Exodus is the grand exit from slavery in Egypt; Leviticus carries the levitical laws given through the Levites; Numbers is the numbering of the families and armies of Israel; and Deuteronomy is a reiteration, a repetition of the laws of the Most High and of everything that happened in the previous books. Chapters 1 and 2 retold how the Most High helped Moses by appointing leaders, so that one man would not carry the whole burden of a stiffnecked and rebellious people alone, and gave Israel instruction on which wars to take on, sparing Edom and the children of Moab. Chapter 3 opens with the war against Og king of Bashan.

**[[Deuteronomy 3#^v1|Deuteronomy 3:1-3]]**

![[Deuteronomy 3#^v1]]
![[Deuteronomy 3#^v2]]
![[Deuteronomy 3#^v3]]

- Bashan is about to be conquered, and Og the king of Bashan came out with all his people to battle at Edrei. The Most High said fear him not, for I will deliver him and all his people and his land into thy hand, as was done to Sihon king of the Amorites at Heshbon.
- Always remember, the battle is the Lord's; this fight is spiritual. Israel does not need weapons of mass destruction: the Bible is the weapon, and when Israel keeps the commandments the Most High fights for his people.
- The Lord our God delivered into our hands also the king of Bashan: they were out there doing physical war, but they gave the praise, the glory, and the victory to the Most High. They did not boast in their own swords; that is how you sanctify the Most High.
- The same order stands today: when you wake up you thank the Most High, and you thank him for the food even though you cooked it, because he is the one who allowed you to have it. You always put the Most High first.

# Deuteronomy 5: The Ten Commandments Reiterated

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 5]]

**[[Deuteronomy 5#^v1|Deuteronomy 5:1-3]]**

![[Deuteronomy 5#^v1]]
![[Deuteronomy 5#^v2]]
![[Deuteronomy 5#^v3]]

- Hear, O Israel, the statutes and judgments which I speak in your ears this day, that ye may learn them, and keep, and do them. Doing them is the key thing: it is not verbal, actions carry the weight, and the actions are the commandments.
- The Lord made not this covenant with our fathers, but with us, even us, who are all of us here alive this day.

**[[Deuteronomy 5#^v4|Deuteronomy 5:4-6]]**

![[Deuteronomy 5#^v4]]

- I am the Lord thy God, which brought thee out of the land of Egypt, from the house of bondage: Egypt means slavery. This is a good scripture for the brothers who go out to prophesy, because the whole exodus claim can be shown from [[Deuteronomy 5|Deuteronomy 5]] without ever leaving the book.

**[[Deuteronomy 5#^v7|Deuteronomy 5:7-10]]**

- Thou shalt have none other gods before me, and thou shalt not make thee any graven image: the ten commandments are being reiterated and Israel reminded, because the graven images they knew came from Egypt.
- The scriptures are twofold: the commandment still pertains today, against the false images of Christ, the virgin mary statues, the cobblestone at Mecca, buddha, and the gods of the east.
- The Most High is a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate him, and shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love him and keep his commandments.

  - **[[Job 11#^v6|Job 11:6]]**

**[[Deuteronomy 5#^v11|Deuteronomy 5:11]]**

Solomon shows how the name is taken in vain: lest I be poor, and steal, and take the name of my God in vain. Breaking the commandments, stealing, lying, bearing false witness, committing adultery, is how the name is taken in vain.
- His name is called The Word of God, and the word of God is the book of the commandments; so to profane the commandments is to profane the name.
- Solomon's prayer is the humble spirit: give me neither poverty nor riches, feed me with food convenient for me. Full riches make a man deny the Most High and forget the congregation and the work, and money has pulled people out of this walk; poverty drives a man to steal. The chief things for life are simple, water, bread, clothing, and an house to cover shame, and the Most High blessed Solomon with riches because riches were not his god.

# Deuteronomy 6: Teach Them Diligently unto Thy Children

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 6]]

**[[Deuteronomy 6#^v1|Deuteronomy 6:1-3]]**

![[Deuteronomy 6#^v1]]
![[Deuteronomy 6#^v2]]
![[Deuteronomy 6#^v3]]

- These are the commandments, the statutes, and the judgments, commanded to be taught, that thou mightest fear the Lord thy God: the fear always comes up first, thou, and thy son, and thy son's son, all the days of thy life.
- Hear therefore, O Israel, and observe to do it, that ye may increase mightily. Increase and power are the desire of the people, and keeping the commandments is the one thing our people have not tried; assimilation, the gods of the nations, and politics, liberal or conservative, have all been tried and none of it has worked.
- The land that floweth with milk and honey is the true riches: cattle, vegetation, agriculture, land to cultivate, the gift that keeps on giving.

**[[Deuteronomy 6#^v4|Deuteronomy 6:4-7]]**

![[Deuteronomy 6#^v4]]
![[Deuteronomy 6#^v5]]
![[Deuteronomy 6#^v6]]
![[Deuteronomy 6#^v7]]

- Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God is one Lord: and thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.
- Thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children: diligently means you do not miss a beat. The children are not to be taught the dance challenges, halloween, and the satanic birthdays, and remember that you teach by your actions, so you fix yourself first.
- Talk of them when thou sittest in thine house: go through the scriptures at home, the histories of Samson, Joseph, Solomon, and David. When thou walkest by the way: when you run into each other, ask, are you keeping the laws, what four chapters did you read today?
- When thou liest down, and when thou risest up: the bedtime stories are biblical, the ten plagues of Egypt rather than the fairy tales, prayer before they sleep and when they rise, and even the coloring books painting the images brown, bringing it back to its true course.

**[[Deuteronomy 6#^v8|Deuteronomy 6:8-9]]**

![[Deuteronomy 6#^v8]]
![[Deuteronomy 6#^v9]]

- Thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes: bind them in your mind. The commandments are the light, the torch that guides you and leads you in the correct path.
- And thou shalt write them upon the posts of thy house, and on thy gates.

![[Deuteronomy 6#^v10]]

- When the Lord brings thee into great and goodly cities which thou buildedst not, houses full of all good things which thou filledst not, wells digged which thou diggedst not, and thou shalt have eaten and be full, then beware lest thou forget the Lord which brought thee forth out of the house of bondage.
- Thou shalt fear the Lord thy God, and serve him, and shalt swear by his name; ye shall not go after the gods of the people round about you, for the Lord thy God is a jealous God, lest his anger be kindled and destroy thee from off the face of the earth.
- Ye shall not tempt the Lord your God, as ye tempted him in Massah.

**[[Deuteronomy 6#^v17|Deuteronomy 6:17-19]]**

![[Deuteronomy 6#^v17]]
![[Deuteronomy 6#^v18]]
![[Deuteronomy 6#^v19]]

- Ye shall diligently keep the commandments of the Lord your God, and his testimonies, and his statutes. Testimony is one of the trigger words that confuses our people: the laws are what Moses gave, and the testimonies are the history of the forefathers and foremothers, what they went through and how it was handled according to the laws.
- Everything correlates: when king David had Uriah killed, that goes back to thou shalt not kill, thou shalt not covet, and bearing false witness; he was dealing with much sin in that one matter. That is how you go precept upon precept, line upon line, to the law and to the testimony.
- A personal testimony that cannot come back to the Bible is not biblical; if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light, no truth, in them.
- Thou shalt do that which is right and good in the sight of the Lord: not by personal standards of what feels good in the heart, but by the standard set before us, for the scripture says plainly that the law is good. The reward is to go in and possess the good land, the real wealth, not the bags and rings and sneakers, which are vanity.

  - **[[Isaiah 8#^v20|Isaiah 8:20]]**

- When thy son asketh thee in time to come, saying, what mean the testimonies, and the statutes, and the judgments? The children will ask why David did what he did, why Samson, why Miriam was judged, why Moses was barred; the answer starts with the history.
- Then thou shalt say unto thy son, we were Pharaoh's bondmen in Egypt, and the Lord brought us out with a mighty hand, and shewed signs and wonders upon Egypt and upon Pharaoh; he brought us out that he might bring us in, to give us the land which he sware unto our fathers. The Most High always keeps his covenant; he is not a man, that he should lie.
- The Lord commanded us to do all these statutes, to fear the Lord our God, for our good always, that he might preserve us alive: the fear again stands first, and the commandments are for Israel's good and preservation, obligated and not optional, with punishment for breaking and reward for keeping.
- And it shall be our righteousness, if we observe to do all these commandments: keeping the commandments is what makes you righteous, and righteousness is kept at all times, the fringes worn seven days a week and not on the sabbath only.
- Words like testimony, righteousness, grace, and faith must be defined out of the scripture itself: the Bible is a dictionary within itself. Egypt means slavery, righteousness means keeping the laws, and going by the Bible's own definitions destroys the private interpretations of the religions.
