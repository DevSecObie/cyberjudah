# Deuteronomy 27: Write the Law upon Stones

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 27]]

## Introduction

This session covers [[Deuteronomy 27|Deuteronomy 27]] through 30, the heart of the covenant. Chapter 27 commands Israel to write the law very plainly upon great stones and sets half the tribes on mount Gerizim to bless and half on mount Ebal to curse, with the people answering Amen to every curse. Chapter 28 lays out the blessings for obedience and the curses for disobedience, the curses that identify who the true Israelites are to this day. Chapter 29 is the covenant made in the land of Moab, made not only with those who stood there that day but with us who were not there. Chapter 30 is the goodness of the Most High: the way back out of the curses, to call them to mind in captivity, return, keep the commandments, and choose life.

- On the day Israel passed over Jordan into the land, they were to set up great stones, plaster them with plaster, and write upon them all the words of this law.
- Here is the difference between the truth and Islam and every other religion. The stone is not to be worshipped or thought to be a god; you set it up and put the laws on it so you can remember the laws.

# Deuteronomy 30: Call the Curses to Mind and Choose Life

- With all that being said, here is the goodness of the Most High: he said, I am going to punish you, but he gave us the solution to get up out of the curses.
- When all these things are come upon thee, the blessing and the curse, thou shalt call them to mind among all the nations whither the Lord thy God hath driven thee. Baruch tells you what calling them to mind is: in the land of their captivities they shall remember themselves.
- We are remembering ourselves now: we know we got here on ships, we know we did not speak the white man's language when he came to the west coast of Africa, we know we rise when the alarm clock goes off and work for food, water, and drink, and we know Christianity is a slave religion forced on our ancestors. - And shalt return unto the Lord thy God, and shalt obey his voice according to all that I command thee, thou and thy children, with all thine heart and with all thy soul. The stipulation of returning is keeping the commandments; you cannot just say I accept the Lord in my heart. That does not work. And your children must be in order under your household too; they cannot fake it either.
