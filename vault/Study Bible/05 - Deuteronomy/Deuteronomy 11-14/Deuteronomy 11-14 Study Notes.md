# Deuteronomy 11: Remember His Acts, Keep His Commandments

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 11]]

## Introduction

This session was a reading of the four chapters with basic precepts supplied along the way to help the understanding, not a full class; questions were submitted to the captains. Chapter 11 recounts the great acts of the Most High that Israel's own eyes saw and sets before Israel a blessing and a curse. Chapter 12 commands the destruction of the idolatrous places and fixes worship at the place where the Lord puts his name. Chapter 13 gives the judgment on prophets, family, and cities that entice Israel to other gods. Chapter 14 declares Israel a peculiar people, holy unto the Lord, and lays out the law of clean and unclean foods and the tithe.

# Deuteronomy 12: The Place Where the Lord Puts His Name

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 12]]

**[[Deuteronomy 12#^v1|Deuteronomy 12:1]]**

![[Deuteronomy 12#^v1]]

- These are the statutes and judgments which ye shall observe to do in the land: the Lord gave the fathers a land other nations were living in, and Israel went to war with those nations to take it.
- The whole book of Deuteronomy is the Lord speaking unto his people Israel; he showed his statutes and his judgments to no other nation.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Psalms 147#^v19|Psalms 147:19-20]]**
    ![[Psalms 147#^v19]]

**[[Deuteronomy 12#^v2|Deuteronomy 12:2]]**

![[Deuteronomy 12#^v2]]

- Utterly destroy all the places wherein the nations served their gods, upon the high mountains, upon the hills, and under every green tree.
- Even after being warned, Israel was still idolatrous: the children of Israel secretly built high places in all their cities, set up images and groves in every high hill and under every green tree, and burnt incense as did the heathen, the very thing the Lord said ye shall not do.
- Because Israel served the gods of the nations, the Most High gave those nations the rule over Israel; that is how they became the oppressor, and the word says envy not the oppressor and choose none of his ways. Unlearned and lost brothers and sisters today are still serving other gods, whatever the religion calls itself.

# Deuteronomy 13: Enticers to Other Gods Put Away

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 13]]

**[[Deuteronomy 13#^v5|Deuteronomy 13:5]]**

![[Deuteronomy 13#^v5]]

- The prophet or dreamer of dreams who speaks to turn you away from the Lord your God was put to death under the kingdom. Today Israel is in the land of its oppressors and cannot exact that judgment, and no kind of violence is advocated; the deceived and the deceiver are his, and the judgment belongs to the Most High.
- What is done today: mark them which cause divisions and offences contrary to the doctrine which ye have learned, and avoid them. The doctrine learned is the law, for I give you good doctrine, forsake ye not my law.
- Going contrary to the laws of marriage, of the sabbath, of raising children righteously, or of going to thy brother alone when there is an ought between you, and secretly enticing others to follow, is how one turns people away from the Lord; those men and those women have to be put out from among the body.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Romans 16#^v17|Romans 16:17]]**
    ![[Romans 16#^v17]]
    Mark them which cause divisions and offences contrary to the doctrine which ye have learned, and avoid them.
  - **[[Proverbs 4#^v2|Proverbs 4:2]]**
    ![[Proverbs 4#^v2]]
    For I give you good doctrine, forsake ye not my law; the doctrine is the law.
  - **[[Job 12#^v16|Job 12:16]]**
    ![[Job 12#^v16]]
    The deceived and the deceiver are his; the judgment is the Most High's, not ours.

![[Deuteronomy 13#^v6]]

- If thy brother, thy son, thy daughter, the wife of thy bosom, or thy friend which is as thine own soul entice thee secretly to serve other gods: these are the people closest to you, the ones that can have influence on you.
- Thou shalt not consent, nor hearken, nor pity, nor spare, neither shalt thou conceal him, meaning hide him; when wickedness is concealed, the whole nation is put in jeopardy.
- Trust in a person is no cover for your soul: when someone you love and trust has the devil on them, they will lead you astray. Nobody can make you do anything, but they can influence you; do not give anyone that credit and let them cause you to lose your crown.
- This is why [[Leviticus 5#^v1|Leviticus 5:1]] is opened at the start of class: if you know of anything, you are supposed to utter it. Many grew up on snitches get stitches, but in repentance we have to understand that concealing sin is part of why Israel is in the condition it is in, and we have to teach righteousness. It is that simple.

# Deuteronomy 14: A Peculiar People and the Clean Foods

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 14]]

**[[Deuteronomy 14#^v1|Deuteronomy 14:1]]**

![[Deuteronomy 14#^v1]]

- Ye are the children of the Lord your God: ye shall not cut yourselves, nor make any baldness between your eyes for the dead. These customs came from the other nations, like the young folks today cutting designs into their eyebrows; we are not supposed to be doing that.
- Cutting the flesh was a form of self-torture in mourning; the Most High requires none of that of us, only to be righteous and keep his commandments.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Jeremiah 41#^v5|Jeremiah 41:5]]**
    ![[Jeremiah 41#^v5]]
    Fourscore men came with their beards shaven, their clothes rent, and having cut themselves; an example of the cutting practice the law forbids.

**[[Deuteronomy 14#^v2|Deuteronomy 14:2]]**

![[Deuteronomy 14#^v2]]

- Thou art an holy people unto the Lord thy God, chosen to be a peculiar people unto himself above all the nations upon the earth.
- Peter writes to that same peculiar people, a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation: his letter is addressed to the strangers scattered throughout Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia.
- Who were those strangers? Jews, devout men, out of every nation under heaven, dwelling in the very regions Peter lists, Cappadocia, Pontus, and Asia; that is one way to prove who the strangers of the New Testament are.
- Israel became strangers in the day they were displaced out of their homeland, as Obadiah says of the day of Jacob's captivity. The peculiar people given the peculiar laws of God is Israel, and that is what is taught to our people today, raising them back up into the understanding of who they are and that we must repent in order to get the kingdom.

  Precepts:
  - **[[1 Peter 2#^v9|1 Peter 2:9]]**
    ![[1 Peter 2#^v9]]
    Ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; the same title as Deuteronomy 14:2.
  - **[[1 Peter 1#^v1|1 Peter 1:1]]**
    ![[1 Peter 1#^v1]]
    Peter writes to the strangers scattered throughout Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia.
  - **[[Acts 2#^v5|Acts 2:5]]**
    ![[Acts 2#^v5]]
    There were dwelling at Jerusalem Jews, devout men, out of every nation under heaven.
  - **[[Acts 2#^v9|Acts 2:9]]**
    ![[Acts 2#^v9]]
    The dwellers in Mesopotamia, Judaea, Cappadocia, Pontus, and Asia; the same regions where Peter's strangers were scattered.
  - **[[Obadiah 1#^v12|Obadiah 1:12]]**
    ![[Obadiah 1#^v12]]
    The day that he became a stranger: Israel became strangers when they were displaced out of their homeland.

**[[Deuteronomy 14#^v3|Deuteronomy 14:3]]**

![[Deuteronomy 14#^v3]]

- Thou shalt not eat any abominable thing: this rules the whole chapter. The whatsoever thy soul lusteth after of chapter 12 sits inside these guidelines of clean foods; you cannot eat anything you want.
- Some of our people, still unlearned, think that as long as they pray over it they can eat it; that is not true, it is a misunderstanding carried out of the New Testament. The law says thou shalt not eat any abominable thing.
- The chapter then lists the clean beasts, the beasts that part the hoof and chew the cud, the fish with fins and scales, and the clean fowls; the rest of chapter 14 is clear cut on the laws of the clean and the unclean foods.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 12#^v15|Deuteronomy 12:15]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 12#^v15]]
    Whatsoever thy soul lusteth after, as of the roebuck and as of the hart: the lusting of the soul is bounded by the clean beasts of this chapter.

**[[Deuteronomy 14#^v22|Deuteronomy 14:22-23]]**

![[Deuteronomy 14#^v22]]
![[Deuteronomy 14#^v23]]

- Thou shalt truly tithe all the increase of thy seed that the field bringeth forth year by year, and eat before the Lord the tithe of thy corn, of thy wine, and of thine oil, that thou mayest learn to fear the Lord thy God always.
- The corn, wine, and oil are the same increase promised in chapter 11 for hearkening to the commandments; this all goes together. If Israel did not keep the commandments, he would shut up the heaven, there would be no rain, the land would be chapt, and famine would teach the fear of the Lord.
- Today Israel is disconnected from that fear because the oppressor nourishes us; the food comes from the grocery store. That understanding must be brought back to our people, and that is why the laws are read four chapters a day, from Genesis all the way to Revelation, to gain the understanding that we are the Israelites.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 11#^v13|Deuteronomy 11:13-14]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 11#^v13]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 11#^v14]]
    Hearken diligently and he gives the rain in due season, that thou mayest gather thy corn, thy wine, and thine oil; the increase that is tithed is the increase he gives.
