# Deuteronomy 15: The Year of Release and the Hebrew Servant

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 15]]

## Introduction

This session read [[Deuteronomy 15|Deuteronomy 15]] through 18 with brief exposition. Chapter 15 covers the seventh year of release for the poor, the law of lending and giving to the poor brother, the Hebrew servant who must go out free and well furnished in the seventh year, and the firstling males of the herd and flock sanctified unto the Most High. Chapter 16 covers the feast of the passover, the feast of weeks, and the feast of tabernacles, the three times in the year all males appear before the Most High, judges and just judgment, and the groves and images that are forbidden. Chapter 17 covers the sound sacrifice, the judgment of idolaters, hard controversies determined by the priests and judges, and the election and duty of a king. Chapter 18 covers the priests' and Levites' inheritance and their due, the abominations of the nations to be avoided, the Prophet like unto Moses who must be heard, and the presumptuous prophets who are to die.

- At the end of every seven years thou shalt make a release: every creditor that lendeth ought unto his neighbour shall release it, and shall not exact it of his neighbour or of his brother, because it is called the Lord's release.
- Of a foreigner thou mayest exact it again, but that which is thine with thy brother thine hand shall release. The seven year release stands on the sabbath of the land already given in [[Leviticus 25|Leviticus 25]].

  - **[[Leviticus 25#^v2|Leviticus 25:2-4]]**
    ![[Leviticus 25#^v2]]
    ![[Leviticus 25#^v3]]

![[Deuteronomy 15#^v5]]

- If thou carefully hearken unto the voice of the Most High to observe all these commandments, thou shalt lend unto many nations but thou shalt not borrow, and thou shalt reign over many nations but they shall not reign over thee.
- This is the blessing that was to come upon us. Since we broke the commandments of the Most High, the other nations lend to us and we borrow from them; we go to them for loans and everything else.

  - **[[Deuteronomy 28#^v12|Deuteronomy 28:12]]**

![[Deuteronomy 15#^v7]]
![[Deuteronomy 15#^v8]]

- If there be among you a poor man of one of thy brethren, thou shalt not harden thine heart nor shut thine hand from thy poor brother.
- It is a commandment to us to help our brothers: open thine hand wide unto him and lend him sufficient for his need, in that which he wanteth.

**[[Deuteronomy 15#^v9|Deuteronomy 15:9-11]]**

![[Deuteronomy 15#^v9]]

- Beware that there be not a thought in thy wicked heart saying, The seventh year, the year of release, is at hand, and thine eye be evil against thy poor brother and thou givest him nought; if he cry unto the Lord against thee, it is sin unto thee.
- Give him surely, and let not thine heart be grieved when thou givest, for because of this thing the Most High shall bless thee in all thy works.
- Notice that Esau had this Bible, these commandments and these laws, yet worked us to death for over four hundred years, and when they released us we did not go out with anything. They had these laws written before them and did not do them.
- The poor shall never cease out of the land; Christ said the same, that the poor are always going to be among us. That is why no campaign can fight poverty out of existence: Christ said it will always be among us.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Matthew 26#^v11|Matthew 26:11]]**
    ![[Matthew 26#^v11]]
    Ye have the poor always with you; poverty does not cease, so the commandment is to open thine hand to thy poor brother.

- If thy brother, an Hebrew man or an Hebrew woman, be sold unto thee and serve thee six years, then in the seventh year thou shalt let him go free, and thou shalt not let him go away empty.
- When people say slavery was taking place in Africa among us, it was not slavery like we suffered: it was people among us that fell poor and sold themselves to their brothers, the very case this law and [[Leviticus 25|Leviticus 25]] govern.
- Thou shalt furnish him liberally out of thy flock, out of thy floor, and out of thy winepress; he must leave blessed, with some of the flock, the corn, and the wine. He cannot go out poor the way he came in; that shows you the Most High is a good God.
- The reason given is memory: remember that thou wast a bondman in the land of Egypt, and the Lord thy God redeemed thee.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Leviticus 25#^v39|Leviticus 25:39]]**
    ![[Leviticus 25#^v39]]
    If thy brother that dwelleth by thee be waxen poor and be sold unto thee; the Hebrew sold himself to his brother when he fell poor.

- If the servant say, I will not go away from thee, because he loveth thee and thine house and is well with thee, then thou shalt take an aul and thrust it through his ear unto the door, and he shall be thy servant for ever; likewise unto thy maidservant.
- It shall not seem hard unto thee when thou sendest him away free, for he hath been worth a double hired servant to thee in serving thee six years.
- A double hired servant: reading this makes you think about slavery and how many years they had us. We were double hired servants unto them, and we still serve them even to this day.

- All the firstling males of thy herd and of thy flock thou shalt sanctify unto the Most High: thou shalt do no work with the firstling of thy bullock, nor shear the firstling of thy sheep.
- The firstlings were to do no work at all; they were to be sacrificed to the Most High, and thou shalt eat it before the Lord thy God year by year in the place which the Lord shall choose.
- If there be any blemish therein, as if it be lame or blind, thou shalt not sacrifice it unto the Lord thy God; thou shalt eat it within thy gates, the unclean and the clean person alike, as the roebuck and as the hart.
- Only thou shalt not eat the blood thereof; thou shalt pour it upon the ground as water.

# Deuteronomy 16: The Three Feasts and Just Judgment

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 16]]

**[[Deuteronomy 16#^v1|Deuteronomy 16:1-8]]**

![[Deuteronomy 16#^v1]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v2]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v3]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v4]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v5]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v6]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v7]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v8]]

- Observe the month of Abib and keep the passover unto the Most High, for in the month of Abib the Lord thy God brought thee forth out of Egypt by night.
- Thou shalt eat no leavened bread with it; seven days shalt thou eat unleavened bread, even the bread of affliction, because thou camest forth out of the land of Egypt in haste, that thou mayest remember the day all the days of thy life.
- Nothing of the flesh sacrificed the first day at even, the lamb, may remain all night until the morning. The passover may not be sacrificed within any of thy gates, but only at the place which the Lord thy God shall choose to place his name there, at even, at the going down of the sun.
- Six days thou shalt eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day is a solemn assembly and no work: the first day of the passover is a sabbath, and the seventh day of the passover is a sabbath.

**[[Deuteronomy 16#^v9|Deuteronomy 16:9-12]]**

![[Deuteronomy 16#^v9]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v10]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v11]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v12]]

- Seven weeks shalt thou number unto thee: begin to number the seven weeks from such time as thou beginnest to put the sickle to the corn, and keep the feast of weeks unto the Most High with a tribute of a freewill offering according as the Lord thy God hath blessed thee.
- Another name for the feast of weeks is the feast of firstfruits: you count seven weeks and then a day, fifty days, which is Pentecost.
- Rejoice before the Most High, thou and thy son and thy daughter, thy manservant and thy maidservant, the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless, and the widow; and remember that thou wast a bondman in Egypt.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Leviticus 23#^v15|Leviticus 23:15]]**
    ![[Leviticus 23#^v15]]
    Count from the morrow after the sabbath, from the day the wave sheaf is brought; seven sabbaths shall be complete; the counting law behind the feast of weeks.

**[[Deuteronomy 16#^v13|Deuteronomy 16:13-15]]**

![[Deuteronomy 16#^v13]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v14]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v15]]

- Thou shalt observe the feast of tabernacles seven days, after that thou hast gathered in thy corn and thy wine.
- Rejoice in thy feast, thou and thy son and thy daughter, thy manservant and thy maidservant, the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless, and the widow within thy gates.
- Seven days shalt thou keep a solemn feast in the place which the Lord shall choose, because the Most High shall bless thee in all thine increase and in all the works of thine hands; therefore thou shalt surely rejoice.

**[[Deuteronomy 16#^v16|Deuteronomy 16:16-17]]**

![[Deuteronomy 16#^v16]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v17]]

- Three times in a year shall all thy males appear before the Most High in the place which he shall choose: in the feast of unleavened bread, in the feast of weeks, and in the feast of tabernacles.
- They shall not appear before the Lord empty, meaning they have to come with their sacrifices: every man shall give as he is able, according to the blessing of the Lord thy God which he hath given thee.

**[[Deuteronomy 16#^v18|Deuteronomy 16:18-20]]**

![[Deuteronomy 16#^v18]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v19]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v20]]

- Judges and officers shalt thou make thee in all thy gates, throughout thy tribes, and they shall judge the people with just judgment.
- There is nothing new under the sun: when you see judges and officers among us today, know that we are following the things that are written within this book.
- Thou shalt not wrest judgment, thou shalt not respect persons, neither take a gift, for a gift doth blind the eyes of the wise and pervert the words of the righteous. That which is altogether just shalt thou follow, that thou mayest live and inherit the land.

**[[Deuteronomy 16#^v21|Deuteronomy 16:21-22]]**

![[Deuteronomy 16#^v21]]
![[Deuteronomy 16#^v22]]

- Thou shalt not plant thee a grove of any trees near unto the altar of the Lord thy God; yet these pastors set up Christmas trees inside the church, directly against this law.
- Neither shalt thou set thee up any image, which the Lord thy God hateth.

# Deuteronomy 17: Idolatry Judged and the Duty of a King

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 17]]

**[[Deuteronomy 17#^v1|Deuteronomy 17:1]]**

![[Deuteronomy 17#^v1]]

- Thou shalt not sacrifice unto the Most High any bullock or sheep wherein is blemish, or any evilfavouredness, for that is an abomination unto the Lord thy God.
- Back then you could be put to death for sacrificing a bullock or sheep with a blemish, because doing an abominable thing carried death.

**[[Deuteronomy 17#^v2|Deuteronomy 17:2-5]]**

![[Deuteronomy 17#^v2]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v3]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v4]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v5]]

- If there be found among you man or woman that hath wrought wickedness, transgressed the covenant, and gone and served other gods, the sun or moon or any of the host of heaven, which the Most High commanded not, the matter had to be established first.
- A lot of people do not know how to apply [[Leviticus 5#^v1|Leviticus 5:1]]; the precept for [[Leviticus 5#^v1|Leviticus 5:1]] is [[Deuteronomy 17#^v4|Deuteronomy 17:4]]. Whether it be told thee or thou hast heard of it, thou shalt enquire diligently, and behold, if it be true and the thing certain that such abomination is wrought in Israel, then judgment proceeds.
- Make sure you are certain of a thing before you bring somebody to judgment; only after diligent enquiry was the man or woman brought to the gates and stoned with stones till they die.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Leviticus 5#^v1|Leviticus 5:1]]**
    ![[Leviticus 5#^v1]]
    If a soul hear the voice of swearing, and is a witness, and do not utter it, he shall bear his iniquity; the witness law is applied through the diligent enquiry of Deuteronomy 17:4.

**[[Deuteronomy 17#^v6|Deuteronomy 17:6-7]]**

![[Deuteronomy 17#^v6]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v7]]

- At the mouth of two witnesses or three witnesses shall he that is worthy of death be put to death; at the mouth of one witness he shall not be put to death.
- The hands of the witnesses shall be first upon him to put him to death, and afterward the hands of all the people. Wait till we go back to this: the ones who witnessed against you were the first to lay hands on you, so a false witness could not hide behind the crowd.
- So thou shalt put the evil away from among you.

**[[Deuteronomy 17#^v8|Deuteronomy 17:8-13]]**

![[Deuteronomy 17#^v8]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v9]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v10]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v11]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v12]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v13]]

- If there arise a matter too hard for thee in judgment, between blood and blood, between plea and plea, and between stroke and stroke, matters of controversy within thy gates, then thou shalt get thee up into the place which the Lord shall choose.
- Come unto the priests the Levites and unto the judge that shall be in those days, and they shall shew thee the sentence of judgment; thou shalt do according to all that they inform thee, according to the sentence of the law which they shall teach thee, declining neither to the right hand nor to the left.
- The man that will do presumptuously and will not hearken unto the priest that standeth to minister before the Most High, or unto the judge, even that man shall die; and all the people shall hear, and fear, and do no more presumptuously.

**[[Deuteronomy 17#^v14|Deuteronomy 17:14-17]]**

![[Deuteronomy 17#^v14]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v15]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v16]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v17]]

- When thou art come unto the land and shalt say, I will set a king over me like as all the nations that are about me, thou shalt in any wise set him king whom the Lord thy God shall choose: one from among thy brethren; thou mayest not set a stranger over thee, which is not thy brother.
- These are the laws for a king that is to rule over Israel. He shall not multiply horses to himself, nor cause the people to return to Egypt to multiply horses, for the Lord hath said, Ye shall henceforth return no more that way.
- Neither shall he multiply wives to himself, that his heart turn not away. You hear a lot of these dudes today saying king this and king that, and many of the same men pushing for multiple wives; the law of the king says do not multiply wives to thyself.
- Neither shall he greatly multiply to himself silver and gold.

**[[Deuteronomy 17#^v18|Deuteronomy 17:18-20]]**

![[Deuteronomy 17#^v18]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v19]]
![[Deuteronomy 17#^v20]]

- When he sitteth upon the throne of his kingdom, he shall write him a copy of this law in a book out of that which is before the priests the Levites.
- It shall be with him, and he shall read therein all the days of his life, that he may learn to fear the Most High and keep all the words of this law and these statutes to do them.
- The purpose: that his heart be not lifted up above his brethren, that he turn not aside from the commandment to the right hand or to the left, and that he prolong his days in his kingdom, he and his children, in the midst of Israel.

# Deuteronomy 18: The Priests' Portion and the Prophet Like Unto Moses

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 18]]

**[[Deuteronomy 18#^v1|Deuteronomy 18:1-2]]**

![[Deuteronomy 18#^v1]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v2]]

- The priests the Levites, and all the tribe of Levi, shall have no part nor inheritance with Israel; they shall eat the offerings of the Lord made by fire, and his inheritance.
- Therefore shall they have no inheritance among their brethren: the Lord is their inheritance, as he hath said unto them.

**[[Deuteronomy 18#^v3|Deuteronomy 18:3-5]]**

![[Deuteronomy 18#^v3]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v4]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v5]]

- This shall be the priest's due from the people, from them that offer a sacrifice, whether ox or sheep: they shall give unto the priest the shoulder, and the two cheeks, and the maw.
- The firstfruit also of thy corn, of thy wine, and of thine oil, and the first of the fleece of thy sheep, shalt thou give him.
- For the Most High hath chosen him out of all thy tribes to stand to minister in the name of the Lord, him and his sons for ever.

**[[Deuteronomy 18#^v6|Deuteronomy 18:6-8]]**

![[Deuteronomy 18#^v6]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v7]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v8]]

- If a Levite come from any of thy gates out of all Israel, and come with all the desire of his mind unto the place which the Lord shall choose, then he shall minister in the name of the Lord his God, as all his brethren the Levites do which stand there before the Lord.
- They shall have like portions to eat, beside that which cometh of the sale of his patrimony.

**[[Deuteronomy 18#^v9|Deuteronomy 18:9-14]]**

![[Deuteronomy 18#^v9]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v10]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v11]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v12]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v13]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v14]]

- When thou art come into the land, thou shalt not learn to do after the abominations of those nations: there shall not be found among you any one that maketh his son or his daughter to pass through the fire, or that useth divination, or an observer of times, or an enchanter, or a witch, or a charmer, or a consulter with familiar spirits, or a wizard, or a necromancer.
- These things actually existed and they are all real: wizards, necromancers, divination, observers of times, people that consult with familiar spirits. If they were not real, they would not be in the Bible with the Most High telling us not to do them.
- All that do these things are an abomination unto the Lord, and because of these abominations the Most High driveth those nations out from before thee.
- Thou shalt be perfect with the Lord thy God, meaning upright; the nations hearken unto observers of times and unto diviners, but as for thee, the Most High hath not suffered thee so to do.

**[[Deuteronomy 18#^v15|Deuteronomy 18:15-19]]**

![[Deuteronomy 18#^v15]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v16]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v17]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v18]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v19]]

- The Lord thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken. This is talking about Christ, Yeshua.
- This answers what Israel desired of the Most High in Horeb in the day of the assembly, saying, Let me not hear again the voice of the Lord my God, neither let me see this great fire any more, that I die not; and the Lord said, They have well spoken that which they have spoken.
- I will put my words in his mouth, and he shall speak unto them all that I shall command him; and whosoever will not hearken unto my words which he shall speak in my name, I will require it of him. If we do not follow Christ and do what Christ says, that judgment falls on us.

**[[Deuteronomy 18#^v20|Deuteronomy 18:20-22]]**

![[Deuteronomy 18#^v20]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v21]]
![[Deuteronomy 18#^v22]]

- The prophet which shall presume to speak a word in the name of the Most High which he hath not commanded him to speak, or that shall speak in the name of other gods, even that prophet shall die.
- By this law a lot of these pastors stand condemned: they say things and claim Christ said them, when the Most High never commanded Christ to say it and Christ is not telling them to say it.
- How shall we know the word which the Lord hath not spoken? When a prophet speaketh in the name of the Lord, if the thing follow not, nor come to pass, that is the thing which the Lord hath not spoken; the prophet hath spoken it presumptuously, and thou shalt not be afraid of him.
