# Genesis 40: Do Not Interpretations Belong to God

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 40]]

## Introduction

This is a reading session: [[Genesis 40|Genesis 40]] through 43 were read straight through, with exposition gathered at the verses below. The reading covers the butler and the baker's dreams in the prison, Pharaoh's two dreams and Joseph's exaltation over Egypt, the first journey of Joseph's brethren to buy corn, and Benjamin brought down at the second journey. Questions were taken at the end of the reading; none produced affirmed teaching beyond what is entered under the verses.

**[[Genesis 40#^v8|Genesis 40:8]]**

![[Genesis 40#^v8]]

- We have dreamed a dream, and there is no interpreter of it. Joseph answered on another level: do not interpretations belong to God? Interpretations do belong to God.
- Daniel stood in the same understanding in Babylon; the king confessed that Daniel's God is a God of gods and a revealer of secrets. Joseph already understood that these things belong to the Most High.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Daniel 2#^v47|Daniel 2:47]]**
    ![[Daniel 2#^v47]]
    Your God is a God of gods and a revealer of secrets; the same God that gave Joseph interpretations gave them to Daniel.

**[[Genesis 40#^v9|Genesis 40:9-13]]**

![[Genesis 40#^v9]]
![[Genesis 40#^v10]]
![[Genesis 40#^v11]]
![[Genesis 40#^v12]]
![[Genesis 40#^v13]]

- The chief butler's dream: a vine with three branches that budded and brought forth ripe grapes, pressed into Pharaoh's cup. Joseph interpreted it at once: the three branches are three days, and within three days Pharaoh would restore the butler to his butlership.

**[[Genesis 40#^v16|Genesis 40:16-19]]**

![[Genesis 40#^v16]]
![[Genesis 40#^v17]]
![[Genesis 40#^v18]]
![[Genesis 40#^v19]]

- The chief baker's three white baskets were also three days, but his end was hanging on a tree with the birds eating his flesh from off him.
- Both men saw variations of three, and in both dreams the three stood for three days. The dream is not always what is seen: the vision of dreams is the resemblance of one thing to another, even as the likeness of a face to a face. You may see yourself steal a piece of bread in a dream, and the meaning is that somebody will steal something from you in the days ahead; one thing resembles another.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 34#^v3|Ecclesiasticus 34:3]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 34#^v3]]
    The vision of dreams is the resemblance of one thing to another; the threes in both dreams stood for three days.

**[[Genesis 40#^v20|Genesis 40:20-23]]**

![[Genesis 40#^v20]]
![[Genesis 40#^v21]]

![[Genesis 40#^v23]]

- On the third day, Pharaoh's birthday, it happened just as Joseph laid it out: the chief butler was restored unto his butlership again, and the chief baker was hanged.
- Birthdays are heathen customs, and wicked things take place at them: at this birthday feast a man was put to death before everybody, and on Herod's birthday the daughter of Herodias danced and John the Baptist was beheaded.
- Yet did not the chief butler remember Joseph, but forgat him.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Matthew 14#^v6|Matthew 14:6-8]]**

    On Herod's birthday the dancing pleased him, and the head of John the Baptist was demanded in a charger; another wicked occurrence on a birthday.

# Genesis 41: Pharaoh's Dreams and Joseph's Rise Over Egypt

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 41]]

**[[Genesis 41#^v1|Genesis 41:1-8]]**

![[Genesis 41#^v1]]
![[Genesis 41#^v2]]
![[Genesis 41#^v3]]

![[Genesis 41#^v7]]
![[Genesis 41#^v8]]

- After two full years Pharaoh dreamed: seven well favoured kine eaten up by seven ill favoured ones, and seven good ears devoured by seven thin ears blasted with the east wind. He saw different variations of the number seven, as the butler and the baker saw variations of three; the dream is a resemblance of one thing to another.
- His spirit was troubled, and none of the magicians and wise men of Egypt could interpret the dreams unto Pharaoh.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 34#^v3|Ecclesiasticus 34:3]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 34#^v3]]
    The vision of dreams is the resemblance of one thing to another; the sevens in Pharaoh's dreams resembled seven years.

**[[Genesis 41#^v15|Genesis 41:15-16]]**

![[Genesis 41#^v15]]
![[Genesis 41#^v16]]

- Pharaoh told Joseph he had heard he could understand a dream to interpret it, and Joseph gave all the praise to the Most High: it is not in me; God shall give Pharaoh an answer of peace.
- The Most High reveals his secrets to his servants, and the secret of the Lord is with them that fear him. That is why the interpretation was with Joseph and not with the magicians of Egypt.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Amos 3#^v7|Amos 3:7]]**

- The dream was doubled unto Pharaoh twice because the thing is established by God, and God will surely bring it to pass.
- Joseph did not stop at the interpretation; he gave the counsel: take up the fifth part of the land in the seven plenteous years and store it up, so Egypt could ration food through the years of famine. That shows what was on Joseph: he was wise, understanding, and diligent.
- A prudent man foresees the evil coming and makes preparation so he is not consumed in it; the simple pass on and are punished. There is treasure and oil in the dwelling of the wise, but a foolish man spends it up; spending irrationally is a spirit of folly.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Proverbs 22#^v3|Proverbs 22:3]]**
    ![[Proverbs 22#^v3]]

  - **[[Proverbs 21#^v20|Proverbs 21:20]]**
    ![[Proverbs 21#^v20]]
    There is treasure to be desired in the dwelling of the wise, but a foolish man spendeth it up.

![[Genesis 41#^v38]]
![[Genesis 41#^v39]]

- Can we find such a one as this is, a man in whom the Spirit of God is? Israel has always been a wise and understanding people: Joseph in Egypt, Daniel in ancient Babylon, up until this day, a people responsible for many inventions and cunning arts.
- The children of Israel brought to Babylon were skilful in all wisdom, cunning in knowledge, understanding science, and able to stand in the king's palace to give counsel; and they were taught the tongue of the Chaldeans, for we have the capability to understand multiple languages as well.

  Precepts:

    ![[Daniel 1#^v3]]
    ![[Daniel 1#^v4]]
    Children of Israel skilful in all wisdom and understanding science stood in the king's palace; the same spirit that was in Joseph.

# Genesis 42: Joseph's Brethren Bow Down in Egypt

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 42]]

**[[Genesis 42#^v1|Genesis 42:1-3]]**

![[Genesis 42#^v1]]
![[Genesis 42#^v2]]
![[Genesis 42#^v3]]

- Jacob saw that there was corn in Egypt and said unto his sons, why do ye look one upon another? Get you down thither, and buy for us from thence, that we may live, and not die.
- This is a repeating thing in our history: when famine came, the fathers moved toward Egypt for sustenance and nourishment. There was a famine in Abram's days and he went down into Egypt, and another famine in Isaac's days, when the Lord told him not to go down.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 12#^v9|Genesis 12:9-10]]**
    ![[Genesis 12#^v9]]
    ![[Genesis 12#^v10]]
    There was a famine in the land, and Abram went down into Egypt to sojourn there, for the famine was grievous.
  - **[[Genesis 26#^v1|Genesis 26:1-2]]**
    ![[Genesis 26#^v1]]
    ![[Genesis 26#^v2]]
    In the famine of Isaac's days the Lord appeared unto him and said, go not down into Egypt; the pattern of famine and Egypt runs through the fathers.

**[[Genesis 42#^v4|Genesis 42:4]]**

![[Genesis 42#^v4]]

- Jacob sent not Benjamin with his brethren; lest peradventure means perhaps mischief befall him. Benjamin was the youngest boy, and he was Rachel's son: Rachel died in hard labour bearing him, calling him Benoni as her soul was departing, but his father called him Benjamin.
- Jacob loved Rachel more than Leah, and Rachel was barren for years; she brought forth only Joseph and Benjamin. With Joseph presumed dead, Benjamin was the last son Jacob had of his beloved wife, so he would not let him go down.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 35#^v16|Genesis 35:16-19]]**
    ![[Genesis 35#^v16]]
    ![[Genesis 35#^v17]]
    ![[Genesis 35#^v18]]
    ![[Genesis 35#^v19]]
    Rachel travailed in hard labour and died bearing Benjamin, and was buried in the way to Ephrath, which is Bethlehem.
  - **[[Genesis 29#^v30|Genesis 29:30-31]]**
    ![[Genesis 29#^v30]]
    ![[Genesis 29#^v31]]
    Jacob loved Rachel more than Leah, and Rachel was barren; Joseph and Benjamin were the only sons she brought forth.

**[[Genesis 42#^v7|Genesis 42:7-9]]**

![[Genesis 42#^v7]]
![[Genesis 42#^v8]]
![[Genesis 42#^v9]]

- Joseph knew his brethren, but made himself strange unto them and spake roughly unto them; and they knew him not. Time had passed, and they supposed he was dead or a slave; they never expected their brother to be set as second in command and ruler over Egypt.
- They also knew him not because he blended in among the Egyptians: Israel is black as night, just like the ancient Egyptians. When Jacob died, the Canaanites saw the mourning company and took them all for Egyptians; all twelve tribes looked like the people of Egypt.
- And Joseph remembered the dreams which he dreamed of them. The sheaves that made obeisance to his sheaf were now his brethren bowing down themselves before him with their faces to the earth.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 50#^v10|Genesis 50:10-11]]**
    ![[Genesis 50#^v10]]
    ![[Genesis 50#^v11]]
    The Canaanites saw the mourning and said, this is a grievous mourning to the Egyptians; the twelve tribes were mistaken for Egyptians.

**[[Genesis 42#^v15|Genesis 42:15-16]]**

![[Genesis 42#^v15]]
![[Genesis 42#^v16]]

- Hereby ye shall be proved: ye shall not go forth hence, except your youngest brother come hither. Joseph pressed for Benjamin because that was his own mother's son, his baby brother, whom he wanted to see.

**[[Genesis 42#^v21|Genesis 42:21-24]]**

![[Genesis 42#^v21]]
![[Genesis 42#^v22]]
![[Genesis 42#^v23]]
![[Genesis 42#^v24]]

- We are verily guilty concerning our brother, in that we saw the anguish of his soul when he besought us, and we would not hear. Their conscience was activated; they knew this distress had come upon them for what they did to their brother Joseph, not knowing Joseph stood before them.
- Reuben answered them, spake I not unto you, saying, do not sin against the child? Reuben was the one who would not have Joseph slain, but said to sell him not and lay no hand on him.
- They knew not that Joseph understood them, for he spake unto them by an interpreter. He spoke to them in the tongue of the Egyptians and heard every word they said in their own tongue; we have the capacity to understand many languages.
- He turned himself about from them and wept, for he heard everything they said; then he returned, and took from them Simeon, and bound him before their eyes.

**[[Genesis 42#^v35|Genesis 42:35-38]]**

![[Genesis 42#^v35]]
![[Genesis 42#^v36]]
![[Genesis 42#^v37]]
![[Genesis 42#^v38]]

- Every man's bundle of money was in his sack, and they were afraid; and Jacob their father said, me have ye bereaved of my children: Joseph is not, and Simeon is not, and ye will take Benjamin away.
- My son shall not go down with you; his brother is dead, and he is left alone. If mischief befell Benjamin by the way, Jacob's gray hairs would go down with sorrow to the grave: he would die of a broken heart, for Benjamin was the last son he had of Rachel.

# Genesis 43: Benjamin Brought Down to Egypt

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 43]]

**[[Genesis 43#^v8|Genesis 43:8-10]]**

![[Genesis 43#^v8]]
![[Genesis 43#^v9]]
![[Genesis 43#^v10]]

- Judah stood surety for the lad: of my hand shalt thou require him; if I bring him not unto thee, and set him before thee, then let me bear the blame for ever.
- Except we had lingered, surely now we had returned this second time. Judah reasoned with his father: had Benjamin been sent at the first, they would have been down to Egypt and back already with food.

**[[Genesis 43#^v11|Genesis 43:11-14]]**

![[Genesis 43#^v11]]
![[Genesis 43#^v12]]
![[Genesis 43#^v13]]
![[Genesis 43#^v14]]

- Israel yielded: carry down the man a present of the best fruits in the land, a little balm, a little honey, spices, and myrrh, nuts, and almonds.
- Take double money in your hand, and carry again the money that was brought back in your sacks; peradventure it was an oversight. Jacob would not keep what was not his: return it and be guiltless.
- And God Almighty give you mercy before the man. Jacob committed the outcome to the Most High: if I be bereaved of my children, I am bereaved.

**[[Genesis 43#^v29|Genesis 43:29-31]]**

![[Genesis 43#^v29]]
![[Genesis 43#^v30]]
![[Genesis 43#^v31]]

- Joseph lifted up his eyes and saw his brother Benjamin, his mother's son, and said, God be gracious unto thee, my son. His bowels did yearn upon his brother, and he entered into his chamber and wept there; then he washed his face, and went out, and refrained himself.

**[[Genesis 43#^v32|Genesis 43:32]]**

![[Genesis 43#^v32]]

- The Egyptians might not eat bread with the Hebrews, for that is an abomination unto the Egyptians. The same thing showed up back in the 1900s in America: colored only and whites only, no seat at the same table; they fashioned themselves after ancient Egypt.
- Esau patterns himself after the former kingdoms. He learns how to govern and oppress by studying the kingdoms that were before him: ancient Babylon, ancient Greece, ancient Rome, ancient Egypt. They search out iniquities and accomplish a diligent search; nothing they do is accidental, everything follows the blueprint of the kingdoms that came before them.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Psalms 64#^v5|Psalm 64:5-6]]**
    ![[Psalms 64#^v5]]
    ![[Psalms 64#^v6]]
    They search out iniquities; they accomplish a diligent search; the oppressor studies the former kingdoms and follows their blueprint.

**[[Genesis 43#^v33|Genesis 43:33-34]]**

![[Genesis 43#^v33]]
![[Genesis 43#^v34]]

- They sat before him, the firstborn according to his birthright and the youngest according to his youth, and the men marvelled one at another.
- Benjamin's mess was five times so much as any of theirs. Joseph gave all of them provision, but he made sure his baby brother was well taken care of: Benjamin was his mother's son and had nothing to do with him being sold. And they drank, and were merry with him.
