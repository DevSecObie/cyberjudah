# Genesis 36: The Generations of Esau, Who Is Edom

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 36]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Genesis 36|Genesis 36]] through 39: the generations of Esau, Joseph the dreamer sold into Egypt, Judah and Tamar, and Joseph in Potiphar's house.

**[[Genesis 36#^v1|Genesis 36:1]]**

![[Genesis 36#^v1]]

- Now these are the generations of Esau, who is Edom. Esau is Edom, and this teaching identifies Edom with the people called the white man today.
- Esau came out of the womb red, all over like an hairy garment; his blood could be seen through his skin for lack of melanin, and his name Esau goes into wasted away. Jacob was not described, because Jacob looked like everyone from Adam down to his time; the man of melanin needed no description.
- Esau was called Edom, which means red, because he sold his birthright for that same red pottage.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 25#^v24|Genesis 25:24-26]]**
    ![[Genesis 25#^v24]]
    ![[Genesis 25#^v25]]
    ![[Genesis 25#^v26]]
    The first came out red, all over like an hairy garment; the twins were two nations, polar opposites.
  - **[[Genesis 25#^v30|Genesis 25:30]]**
    ![[Genesis 25#^v30]]
    Feed me with that same red pottage; therefore was his name called Edom, which is red.

**[[Genesis 36#^v2|Genesis 36:2-5]]**

![[Genesis 36#^v2]]
![[Genesis 36#^v3]]
![[Genesis 36#^v4]]
![[Genesis 36#^v5]]

- Esau took his wives of the daughters of Canaan and of Ishmael. He was the first of the Edomites; there were no women of his nation yet, so his lineage began through those wives. Genesis is Bereshith, the beginning of nations.

**[[Genesis 36#^v6|Genesis 36:6-8]]**

![[Genesis 36#^v6]]
![[Genesis 36#^v7]]
![[Genesis 36#^v8]]

- Esau went from the face of his brother because their riches were more than that they might dwell together, the same as with Abram and Lot in [[Genesis 13|Genesis 13]]. Esau was rich at this time, out of the house of Isaac and Abraham.
- Thus dwelt Esau in mount Seir. Mount Seir is the clefts of the rock; the vision of Obadiah speaks to Edom as thou that dwellest in the clefts of the rock, whose pride hath deceived him.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Obadiah 1#^v3|Obadiah 1:3]]**
    ![[Obadiah 1#^v3]]
    The pride of thine heart hath deceived thee, thou that dwellest in the clefts of the rock; that is Edom in mount Seir.

**[[Genesis 36#^v9|Genesis 36:9-11]]**

![[Genesis 36#^v9]]
![[Genesis 36#^v10]]
![[Genesis 36#^v11]]

- Esau is the father of the Edomites in mount Seir, and his grandson Teman, son of Eliphaz, is identified in this teaching with Germany; the reference work Edom and Germany lays that connection out.
- The prophecy over Teman stands: the wise men shall be destroyed out of Edom and understanding out of the mount of Esau, and the mighty men of Teman shall be dismayed. Much of the world's science and weapons technology has come out of Germany, and the word says that wisdom will be destroyed.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Obadiah 1#^v8|Obadiah 1:8-9]]**
    ![[Obadiah 1#^v8]]
    ![[Obadiah 1#^v9]]
    The wise men of Edom and the mighty men of Teman shall be destroyed, that every one of the mount of Esau may be cut off.

**[[Genesis 36#^v12|Genesis 36:12]]**

![[Genesis 36#^v12]]

- Timna was concubine to Eliphaz, and she bare Amalek. Amalek came of a concubine, a lesser wife, which is why the word bastard is studied here: in the scriptures it means a child of incest or one born outside the proper marriage.
- The word says a bastard shall dwell in Ashdod, and Ashdod is the area of modern day Tel Aviv; this teaching places the line of Amalek dwelling there today.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Zechariah 9#^v6|Zechariah 9:6]]**
    ![[Zechariah 9#^v6]]
    And a bastard shall dwell in Ashdod; the prophecy names the place where that seed sits.

- These were the dukes of the sons of Esau: dukes are the chiefs of their tribes, the leaders of the families of Edom.

- The Lord destroyed the Horims from before the children of Esau, and they succeeded them and dwelt in their stead, as Deuteronomy records.

# Genesis 37: Joseph the Dreamer

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 37]]

**[[Genesis 37#^v1|Genesis 37:1-2]]**

![[Genesis 37#^v1]]
![[Genesis 37#^v2]]

- Joseph brought unto his father the evil report of his brethren. That was law, not tale bearing: if a soul hear swearing and be a witness and do not utter it, he shall bear his iniquity. The law was already around, and Joseph kept it.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Leviticus 5#^v1|Leviticus 5:1]]**
    ![[Leviticus 5#^v1]]
    A witness that does not utter what he has seen bears his own iniquity; Joseph uttered the evil report according to that law.

**[[Genesis 37#^v3|Genesis 37:3-4]]**

![[Genesis 37#^v3]]
![[Genesis 37#^v4]]

- Israel loved Joseph more than all his children, the son of his old age, and made him a coat of many colours; and his brethren hated him and could not speak peaceably unto him.
- That is real hatred among brothers, and the same spirit is on Israel today, where a brother cannot even receive a shalom in peace.

**[[Genesis 37#^v5|Genesis 37:5-8]]**

![[Genesis 37#^v5]]
![[Genesis 37#^v6]]
![[Genesis 37#^v7]]
![[Genesis 37#^v8]]

- Joseph dreamed, and the sheaves of his brethren made obeisance to his sheaf. The Lord speaks by the prophets in visions and similitudes; the vision of dreams is the resemblance of one thing to another, as the likeness of a face to a face.
- His brethren understood the dream, that he should reign over them, and hated him yet the more for his dreams and for his words.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Hosea 12#^v10|Hosea 12:10]]**
    ![[Hosea 12#^v10]]
    The Most High multiplies visions and uses similitudes by the ministry of the prophets.
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 34#^v3|Ecclesiasticus 34:3]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 34#^v3]]
    The vision of dreams is the resemblance of one thing to another; the sheaf stood for Joseph and the sheaves for his brethren.

**[[Genesis 37#^v9|Genesis 37:9-10]]**

![[Genesis 37#^v9]]
![[Genesis 37#^v10]]

- In the second dream the sun, the moon, and the eleven stars made obeisance to Joseph. Jacob interpreted it at once: shall I and thy mother and thy brethren indeed come to bow down ourselves to thee?
- The same figures mark Israel: the woman clothed with the sun with a crown of twelve stars is the daughter of Zion, the nation of Israel, and the twelve stars are the tribes.
- The dream came true. In the famine the whole house of Jacob came down to Joseph in Egypt, and he placed them in the best of the land and nourished them with bread; they bowed to the one the Most High had set over them.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Revelation 12#^v1|Revelation 12:1]]**
    ![[Revelation 12#^v1]]
    The woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet and a crown of twelve stars, figures Israel and her tribes.
  - **[[Jeremiah 6#^v2|Jeremiah 6:2]]**
    ![[Jeremiah 6#^v2]]
    The daughter of Zion is likened to a comely and delicate woman; the woman of the vision is Israel.
  - **[[Genesis 47#^v11|Genesis 47:11-12]]**
    ![[Genesis 47#^v11]]
    ![[Genesis 47#^v12]]
    Joseph placed his father and his brethren in the best of the land and nourished them; the dream was fulfilled.

**[[Genesis 37#^v11|Genesis 37:11]]**

![[Genesis 37#^v11]]

- His brethren envied him, but his father observed the saying. Envy consumes a man, and such a man shall have no fellowship with wisdom; when envy takes over, wisdom leaves.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Wisdom of Solomon 6#^v23|Wisdom of Solomon 6:23]]**
    ![[Wisdom of Solomon 6#^v23]]
    Consuming envy has no fellowship with wisdom; the brothers' envy put wisdom away from them.

**[[Genesis 37#^v18|Genesis 37:18-20]]**

![[Genesis 37#^v18]]
![[Genesis 37#^v19]]
![[Genesis 37#^v20]]

- When they saw him afar off, they conspired against him to slay him: Behold, this dreamer cometh. That is the same spirit Cain had, and the same spirit Esau had.
- Whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer. Inner hatred, given room, plots death; that is why the law says thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart.

  Precepts:
  - **[[1 John 3#^v15|1 John 3:15]]**
    ![[1 John 3#^v15]]
    Whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer, and no murderer hath eternal life abiding in him.

**[[Genesis 37#^v21|Genesis 37:21-28]]**

![[Genesis 37#^v21]]
![[Genesis 37#^v22]]
![[Genesis 37#^v23]]
![[Genesis 37#^v24]]
![[Genesis 37#^v25]]
![[Genesis 37#^v26]]
![[Genesis 37#^v27]]
![[Genesis 37#^v28]]

- Reuben delivered him out of their hands: cast him into this pit, but lay no hand upon him; he meant to return him to his father.
- Judah said, What profit is it if we slay our brother and conceal his blood? They sold Joseph to the Ishmaelites, the people known today as the Arab man, for twenty pieces of silver, and they brought him into Egypt.

**[[Genesis 37#^v29|Genesis 37:29-36]]**

![[Genesis 37#^v29]]
![[Genesis 37#^v30]]
![[Genesis 37#^v31]]
![[Genesis 37#^v32]]
![[Genesis 37#^v33]]
![[Genesis 37#^v34]]
![[Genesis 37#^v35]]
![[Genesis 37#^v36]]

- The brothers dipped the coat in the blood of a kid and let their father believe an evil beast had devoured Joseph. They dealt falsely and lied, against the law: ye shall not steal, neither deal falsely, neither lie one to another.
- Jacob could not see the lie because the thing was of the Lord. As Joseph told them later, ye thought evil against me, but God meant it unto good, to save much people alive; had Joseph never gone down to Egypt, the whole house of Israel would have died in the famine.
- The Midianites sold him in Egypt to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh and captain of the guard.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Leviticus 19#^v11|Leviticus 19:11]]**
    ![[Leviticus 19#^v11]]
    Neither deal falsely, neither lie one to another; the brothers broke that law before their father.
  - **[[Genesis 50#^v20|Genesis 50:20]]**
    ![[Genesis 50#^v20]]
    Ye thought evil against me, but God meant it unto good, to save much people alive.

# Genesis 38: Judah and Tamar

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 38]]

**[[Genesis 38#^v1|Genesis 38:1-7]]**

![[Genesis 38#^v1]]
![[Genesis 38#^v2]]
![[Genesis 38#^v3]]
![[Genesis 38#^v4]]
![[Genesis 38#^v5]]
![[Genesis 38#^v6]]
![[Genesis 38#^v7]]

- Judah took the daughter of Shuah and had Er, Onan, and Shelah; and Er, Judah's firstborn, was wicked in the sight of the Lord, and the Lord slew him.

**[[Genesis 38#^v8|Genesis 38:8-10]]**

![[Genesis 38#^v8]]
![[Genesis 38#^v9]]
![[Genesis 38#^v10]]

- Onan was told to go in unto his brother's wife and raise up seed to his brother, and he refused, spilling it on the ground, and the Lord slew him also.
- The sin was refusing to raise up seed to his brother, not the act itself; do not build a doctrine on the wrong point. What happened here later became law: the husband's brother raises up the name of the dead, and he that refuses has his shoe loosed and his face spat in, and his house carries the reproach.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 25#^v5|Deuteronomy 25:5-9]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v5]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v6]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v7]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v8]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v9]]
    The brother's duty to raise up a name in Israel became law, and the refuser bore public reproach; Onan refused that duty.

**[[Genesis 38#^v11|Genesis 38:11-19]]**

![[Genesis 38#^v11]]
![[Genesis 38#^v12]]
![[Genesis 38#^v13]]
![[Genesis 38#^v14]]
![[Genesis 38#^v15]]
![[Genesis 38#^v16]]
![[Genesis 38#^v17]]
![[Genesis 38#^v18]]
![[Genesis 38#^v19]]

- Judah promised Tamar to Shelah and did not perform it. When she saw Shelah was grown and she was not given to him, she put off her widow's garments, covered herself with a vail, and sat where Judah would pass.
- Judah thought her to be an harlot because of the attire; the scriptures show a harlot was always known by her dress, as the woman with the attire of an harlot and subtil of heart.
- She took his signet, bracelets, and staff as a pledge, and conceived by him.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Proverbs 7#^v10|Proverbs 7:10]]**
    ![[Proverbs 7#^v10]]
    There met him a woman with the attire of an harlot, and subtil of heart; the attire spoke before she did.

**[[Genesis 38#^v24|Genesis 38:24-26]]**

![[Genesis 38#^v24]]
![[Genesis 38#^v25]]
![[Genesis 38#^v26]]

- When Judah heard Tamar was with child by whoredom he said, Bring her forth, and let her be burnt. A woman promised in the covenant that plays the harlot and brings an heir by another disobeys the law of the Most High, and her reproach is not blotted out; that is why the judgment was so heavy.
- Then she produced the signet, bracelets, and staff, and Judah acknowledged them: She hath been more righteous than I, because that I gave her not to Shelah my son. And he knew her again no more.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 23#^v22|Ecclesiasticus 23:22-26]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 23#^v22]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 23#^v23]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 23#^v24]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 23#^v25]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 23#^v26]]
    The wife that brings in an heir by another disobeys the law of the Most High and leaves her memory to be cursed; that standard is why Judah called for judgment.

**[[Genesis 38#^v27|Genesis 38:27-30]]**

![[Genesis 38#^v27]]
![[Genesis 38#^v28]]
![[Genesis 38#^v29]]
![[Genesis 38#^v30]]

- Twins were in her womb: the midwife bound a scarlet thread on the hand of the first, but his brother broke forth first and was called Pharez, and after him came Zarah with the scarlet thread.
- Judah's line went down into Egypt in the end, and Judah died there with his brethren; and Joseph died, and all his brethren, and all that generation.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Exodus 1#^v6|Exodus 1:6]]**
    ![[Exodus 1#^v6]]
    And Joseph died, and all his brethren, and all that generation; Judah ended his days in Egypt.

# Genesis 39: Joseph in Potiphar's House

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 39]]

**[[Genesis 39#^v1|Genesis 39:1-6]]**

![[Genesis 39#^v1]]
![[Genesis 39#^v2]]
![[Genesis 39#^v3]]
![[Genesis 39#^v4]]
![[Genesis 39#^v5]]
![[Genesis 39#^v6]]

- The Lord was with Joseph in his captivity, and his master saw that the Lord made all that he did to prosper; Potiphar made him overseer over his house, and the Lord blessed the Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake.
- The Most High deals with His people even in captivity and gives them compassion before them who carried them captive; the favor on Joseph is the pattern of that prayer.

  Precepts:
  - **[[1 Kings 8#^v50|1 Kings 8:50]]**
    ![[1 Kings 8#^v50]]
    Give them compassion before them who carried them captives; the Lord gave Joseph that compassion in Egypt.

**[[Genesis 39#^v7|Genesis 39:7-9]]**

![[Genesis 39#^v7]]
![[Genesis 39#^v8]]
![[Genesis 39#^v9]]

- His master's wife cast her eyes upon Joseph and said, Lie with me; and he refused: how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?
- Adultery was known as sin from the beginning, long before Sinai. Abraham kept the commandments, statutes, and laws; and Abimelech rebuked Isaac because one of the people might have lain with his wife and brought guiltiness upon them. The nations themselves knew adultery brought guilt.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 26#^v5|Genesis 26:5]]**
    ![[Genesis 26#^v5]]
    Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws; the laws are nothing new.
  - **[[Genesis 26#^v10|Genesis 26:10]]**
    ![[Genesis 26#^v10]]
    Abimelech knew lightly lying with another man's wife would have brought guiltiness upon them; adultery was known sin.

**[[Genesis 39#^v10|Genesis 39:10-12]]**

![[Genesis 39#^v10]]
![[Genesis 39#^v11]]
![[Genesis 39#^v12]]

- She spake to Joseph day by day, and he hearkened not. Then she arranged the empty house, caught him by his garment, and he left his garment in her hand and fled.
- That is the example: flee from sin as from the face of a serpent, for if thou comest too near it, it will bite thee; the teeth thereof are as the teeth of a lion. Joseph knew thirty more seconds of entertaining her would be the bite.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 21#^v1|Ecclesiasticus 21:1-2]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 21#^v1]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 21#^v2]]
    Flee from sin as from the face of a serpent; come too near and it will bite thee. Joseph fled.

**[[Genesis 39#^v13|Genesis 39:13-18]]**

![[Genesis 39#^v13]]
![[Genesis 39#^v14]]
![[Genesis 39#^v15]]
![[Genesis 39#^v16]]
![[Genesis 39#^v17]]
![[Genesis 39#^v18]]

- When she saw he was fled, she lied and cried that the Hebrew came in to mock her, holding his garment as evidence.
- The scriptures warn of that spirit: the woman whose heart is snares and nets and her hands as bands; whoso pleaseth God shall escape from her. The lips of a strange woman drop as an honeycomb, but her end is bitter, and her ways are moveable; the same mouth that flattered turns and accuses.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiastes 7#^v26|Ecclesiastes 7:26]]**
    ![[Ecclesiastes 7#^v26]]
    The woman whose heart is snares and nets, and her hands as bands; whoso pleaseth God shall escape from her, as Joseph escaped.
  - **[[Proverbs 5#^v3|Proverbs 5:3-6]]**
    ![[Proverbs 5#^v3]]
    ![[Proverbs 5#^v4]]
    ![[Proverbs 5#^v5]]
    ![[Proverbs 5#^v6]]
    The lips of a strange woman drop as an honeycomb, but her end is bitter as wormwood; her ways are moveable.

**[[Genesis 39#^v19|Genesis 39:19-20]]**

![[Genesis 39#^v19]]
![[Genesis 39#^v20]]

- When his master heard the words of his wife, his wrath was kindled and he put Joseph into the prison. He did not seek understanding, for jealousy is the rage of a man: he will not spare in the day of vengeance, nor regard any ransom.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Proverbs 6#^v32|Proverbs 6:32-35]]**
    ![[Proverbs 6#^v32]]
    ![[Proverbs 6#^v33]]
    ![[Proverbs 6#^v34]]
    ![[Proverbs 6#^v35]]
    Jealousy is the rage of a man; he will not spare in the day of vengeance nor regard any ransom; that is why no plea could reach Potiphar.

**[[Genesis 39#^v21|Genesis 39:21-23]]**

![[Genesis 39#^v21]]
![[Genesis 39#^v22]]
![[Genesis 39#^v23]]

- But the Lord was with Joseph, and shewed him mercy, and gave him favour in the sight of the keeper of the prison, and everything in the prison was committed to Joseph's hand.
- Everywhere Joseph went the favor followed, because the Most High was ordering his steps the whole way, so that Joseph would stand where he could save the whole house of Israel alive.
