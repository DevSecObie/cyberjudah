# Judges 5: The Song of Deborah and Barak: A Willing People

Read the chapter → [[Judges 5]]

## Introduction

This session covers [[Judges 5|Judges 5]] through 8: the song of Deborah and Barak after the victory over Sisera, the call of Gideon while Israel is wasted under Midian, the three hundred men by whom the Lord saves Israel, and Israel's return to Baal worship as soon as Gideon is dead. The class opens under the order of [[1 Corinthians 14#^v34|1 Corinthians 14:34-35]], that the women keep silence in the assembly, for it is not permitted unto them to speak, but they are commanded to be under obedience, as also saith the law. The running thread is that everything Israel does for the Most High must come from a willing heart, and that the victory of battle stands not in the multitude of an host, but strength comes from heaven.

- Deborah and Barak sing praise to the Lord for the avenging of Israel, when the people willingly offered themselves. That is what the Most High wants from us: a willing heart in all that we do, in the keeping of the commandments, in the keeping of the feast days, and in the praise of him.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Exodus 35#^v22|Exodus 35:22]]**
    ![[Exodus 35#^v22]]
    At the building of the tabernacle in the wilderness, both men and women as many as were willing hearted brought their gold unto the Lord; we have to have that same willing heart today.

- How do we willingly offer ourselves to the Lord in the twenty-first century? By giving up whatever presents itself as an obstacle between us and the Lord. It may be father, mother, husband, wife, or children; it may be drugs, money, or working on the sabbath. If you willingly offer yourself, you will not let any stumbling block keep you from the Lord.

# Judges 6: The Call of Gideon: Why Is All This Befallen Us

![[Judges 6#^v1]]

- Israel did evil in the sight of the Lord, and the Lord delivered them into the hand of Midian seven years. That is our manner: the Most High delivers us, we get a little bit of freedom, and we go right back to wickedness. The same thing goes on today; we get relaxed, and the Lord has to remind us, putting us right back into hard bondage, that this is not our rest.
- Because of the Midianites, the children of Israel made them the dens in the mountains, and caves, and strongholds; Israel hid in the mountains of their own land.

# Judges 7: The Three Hundred: The Victory Is the Lord's

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 8#^v11|Deuteronomy 8:11]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 8#^v11]]
    Beware that thou forget not the Lord thy God in not keeping his commandments; the warning was given before they ever entered the land.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 8#^v14|Deuteronomy 8:14-18]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 8#^v14]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 8#^v15]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 8#^v16]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 8#^v17]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 8#^v18]]
    Lest thine heart be lifted up and thou say in thine heart, my power and the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth; thou shalt remember the Lord thy God, for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth.

- Whosoever is fearful and afraid, let him return: twenty and two thousand bounced back to Gilead, and ten thousand remained. That is why God always lowered the numbers going into battle, so Israel would know it was by the Lord's strength that we overcame our enemies; and it is going to be the same thing again, few numbers going into battle against the nations when that time comes.

**[[Judges 7#^v4|Judges 7:4-6]]**

![[Judges 7#^v4]]

- The people are yet too many; the Lord tries them at the water. Some lapped the water with their hand to their mouth as a dog lappeth, and all the rest bowed down upon their knees to drink; the lappers were three hundred men.
- What can we learn from this? You have to be willing to let go of some people. The Lord said, of whom I say, this shall not go with thee, the same shall not go: some people are not going to be beneficial to the war ahead of you, and the Lord will move them out of the way, but you have to be ready to let them go.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 12#^v1|Genesis 12:1]]**
    ![[Genesis 12#^v1]]
    The same word came to Abraham: get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred; there were things the Lord had to do for him that required letting go.
  - **[[Matthew 10#^v34|Matthew 10:34-36]]**
    ![[Matthew 10#^v34]]
    ![[Matthew 10#^v35]]
    ![[Matthew 10#^v36]]
    Christ came to divide and separate, father, mother, husband, wife, family; let that ring in your mind, you have to be willing to let go of some people for the battle ahead.

**[[Judges 7#^v7|Judges 7:7-8]]**

![[Judges 7#^v7]]
![[Judges 7#^v8]]

- By the three hundred men that lapped will I save you, and deliver the Midianites into thine hand. The Lord sent the rest of Israel every man unto his tent and retained the three hundred, with victuals and trumpets in their hands.
- Now you know where the famous story of the three hundred came from: they got it from our ancestors, from Gideon. The nations take our history and make it entertainment, and even the artwork drew those three hundred with locks and braids and gave the other host the same, because the artist knew whose history he was drawing; those three hundred were Israelites.

When Gideon heard the dream and the interpretation, he worshipped, and returned to say, arise; for the Lord hath delivered into your hand the host of Midian.

    If ye go to war in your land against the enemy that oppresseth you, then ye shall blow an alarm with the trumpets, and ye shall be remembered before the Lord your God, and saved from your enemies. This is the history behind the shofars we blow; those trumpets are not just for looks and thumbnails, there is a deep meaning behind them going into the memorial of blowing of trumpets.

- The three companies blew the trumpets, brake the pitchers, held the lamps in their left hands and the trumpets in their right, and cried, the sword of the Lord, and of Gideon; and every man stood in his place round about the camp. Three hundred against thousands, you have to do something to mess with their mind: those horns blowing in unison put fear in the nations, and all the host ran, and cried, and fled.
- And the Lord set every man's sword against his fellow, even throughout all the host. The enemy destroyed itself; that is the Lord fighting the battle.

# Judges 8: Gideon's Pursuit and Israel's Return to Baal

- The men of Ephraim chode with Gideon sharply: why hast thou served us thus, that thou calledst us not when thou wentest to fight with the Midianites? Ephraim has an attitude because they got called late to the battle and wanted in from the beginning.
- Gideon answers with wisdom: is not the gleaning of the grapes of Ephraim better than the vintage of Abiezer? God delivered into your hands the princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb; what was I able to do in comparison of you? He gave them the best possible situation, the enemy fed right into their hands with nowhere to go, and then their anger was abated toward him.

We have all had that moment of getting the nudge from pops and being nervous; the son was young, and that is understandable, but you see Gideon being a father, taking his son to the war and teaching him the ways he must walk.
