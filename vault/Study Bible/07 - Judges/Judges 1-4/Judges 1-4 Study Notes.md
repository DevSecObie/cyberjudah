# Judges 1: Judah Goes Up First, but the Inhabitants Are Not Driven Out

Read the chapter → [[Judges 1]]

## Introduction

This session covers [[Judges 1|Judges 1]] through 4, reading through the Bible from Genesis through Revelation, four chapters a day, so the whole Bible is read in one year. It is not a class, but highlighted parts of the chapters are touched on with basic precepts to help with the understanding. Much of Judges reads straightforward: after the death of Joshua the tribes go up against the Canaanites, but tribe after tribe does not drive out the inhabitants of the land as the Most High commanded. Chapter 2 shows what dwelling among them cost Israel, chapter 3 opens the cycle of the judges with Othniel, Ehud, and Shamgar, and chapter 4 gives Deborah, Barak, and Jael. As you read, it helps to look at a map of the dividing of the land, such as the dividing-of-the-land map in the rear of the Zondervan's Compact Bible Dictionary, or an ancient twelve tribes chart, to see where these tribes and cities sat.

- An angel of the Most High comes up from Gilgal to Bochim and rehearses the covenant: I made you to go up out of Egypt, I brought you unto the land I sware unto your fathers, and I said I will never break my covenant with you. The command was plain: ye shall make no league with the inhabitants of this land, ye shall throw down their altars. But ye have not obeyed my voice. Why have ye done this?
- The same is true today in the places where we are scattered: we have made leagues with the inhabitants of the land. We find ourselves in their sports leagues and bowling leagues, at their dinner parties, when the scripture says envy not the oppressor and choose none of his ways.
- The Most High warned against letting them dwell in the land because they would make us sin against him. Dwelling among them breeds curiosity after their gods: how did these nations serve their gods, what is that thing they are doing with that tree, what are they hanging on the tree, why are they giving each other gifts? That curiosity is the snare, and we are not to add to his commandments or take away from them.
- Throwing down their altars meant burning their graven images with fire, not even desiring the silver or gold on them, lest it become a snare. The Most High keeps reiterating that we will be snared and trapped by serving what these nations serve.
- Because Israel disobeyed, the sentence falls: I will not drive them out from before you, but they shall be as thorns in your sides, and their gods shall be a snare unto you. The people lifted up their voice and wept, called the place Bochim, and sacrificed there unto the Most High.

- She calls Barak the son of Abinoam and gives him the commandment of the Most High: draw toward mount Tabor with ten thousand men of Naphtali and Zebulun, and I will draw unto thee Sisera with his chariots and his multitude to the river Kishon, and deliver him into thine hand.
- Barak answers, if thou wilt go with me, then I will go, but if thou wilt not go with me, then I will not go. Deborah agrees to go, but tells him the journey shall not be for his honour, for the Most High shall sell Sisera into the hand of a woman.

- Barak calls Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh and goes up with ten thousand men at his feet, and Deborah goes up with him. Heber the Kenite, of the children of Hobab the father in law of Moses, had severed himself from the Kenites and pitched his tent by Kedesh.
- Sisera gathers all nine hundred chariots of iron unto the river of Kishon, and Deborah says unto Barak, up, for this is the day in which the Lord hath delivered Sisera into thine hand; is not the Lord gone out before thee?
- The Most High discomfits Sisera and all his chariots and all his host with the edge of the sword before Barak; Sisera lights down off his chariot and flees away on his feet, and all his host falls upon the edge of the sword; there was not a man left.

- Sisera flees on foot to the tent of Jael the wife of Heber the Kenite, for there was peace between Jabin the king of Hazor and the house of Heber. Jael invites him in, covers him with a mantle, and gives him milk to drink when he asks for water.
- He tells her to stand in the door and say no man is there; then Jael takes a nail of the tent and a hammer in her hand, goes softly unto him as he sleeps fast and weary, and smites the nail into his temples and fastens it into the ground, and he dies. The word through Deborah stands: Sisera was sold into the hand of a woman.
- When Barak comes pursuing, Jael shows him Sisera lying dead with the nail in his temples. So God subdued that day Jabin the king of Canaan before the children of Israel, and their hand prospered and prevailed until they had destroyed him.
