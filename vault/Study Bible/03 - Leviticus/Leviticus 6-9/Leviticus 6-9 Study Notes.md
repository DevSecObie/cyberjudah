# Leviticus 6: The Trespass Offering and the Fire That Shall Never Go Out

Read the chapter → [[Leviticus 6]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Leviticus 6|Leviticus 6]] through 9: the trespass offering and restitution, the laws of the burnt, meat, sin, trespass, and peace offerings, the consecration of Aaron and his sons, and the eighth day when the glory of the Lord appeared to all the people. Leviticus is straightforward like the rest of the law, and these chapters pertain heavily to the book of Hebrews: the law of sacrifice is the volume of the book that is written of Christ, and once you understand the law you begin to understand the letters of Paul. The session opened by backtracking into [[Leviticus 5|Leviticus 5]] on the witness law, unclean things, and oaths; that teaching belongs to the [[Leviticus 5|Leviticus 5]] chapter set and is logged there.

- If a soul lie unto his neighbour in that which was delivered him to keep, or in fellowship, or deceive his neighbour, or find that which was lost and lie concerning it, and swear falsely, the Lord counts all of it as a thing taken away by violence: verse 4 says he shall restore that which he took violently away. Deceiving your neighbour, going into agreement and not holding up your end, or lying about what was given you to hold is a violent act toward your neighbour, and a Ponzi scheme or pyramid scheme sits right in that law.
- He shall restore it in the principal: the value of what was taken had to be given back, with more added on top. A sorry would not settle it with the Lord; you could end up in debt for years over sticky fingers.
- Stealing was not a sin unto death, but the Lord made the restitution heavy so you would never commit it again. It goes back to the commandment, thou shalt not steal, and to the civil law where an ox was restored fivefold and a sheep fourfold.

- This is the law of the sacrifice of peace offerings. If it was offered for a thanksgiving, the flesh was eaten the same day it was offered; none was left until the morning.
- If the sacrifice was a vow or a voluntary offering, it was eaten the same day and the remainder on the morrow also; that is the difference between the two. But flesh remaining on the third day was burnt with fire, and if any was eaten on the third day it was an abomination, and the offering was not accepted.

- All the touching of unclean and defiled things pertains to sacrifice: the point of the uncleanness laws was to separate the children of Israel from their uncleanness, that they die not when they defile the tabernacle. There is no more sacrifice, so those defilement laws do not apply today; a brother can brush his hair with a brush of boar bristles, because that law was about coming in to sacrifice.

No man can condemn you because you are not sacrificing animals any more, or for how you observe a holyday, new moon, or sabbath pertaining to sacrifice, for those are a shadow of things to come, but the body is of Christ.

If the law of sacrifice still stood, there would be no sacrifice for worshipping other gods, and we would be done as a people; it was taken out of the way so Israel could come back to God.

That is how Satan and all the nations are overcome; the blood of the sacrifices went all the way back to Christ.
