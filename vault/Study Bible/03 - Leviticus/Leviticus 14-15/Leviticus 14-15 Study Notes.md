# Leviticus 14: The Law of the Leper in the Day of His Cleansing

Read the chapter → [[Leviticus 14]]

## Introduction

This session is a reading-only recording covering [[Leviticus 14|Leviticus 14]] and 15 only. The chapters were read straight through without exposition or question and answer, so these notes carry the reading itself with short breakdowns and no precepts. Chapter 14 gives the law of the leper in the day of his cleansing and the law of the plague of leprosy in a house; chapter 15 gives the law of issues, the running issues of the flesh and the separation of the woman, ending with the charge to separate the children of Israel from their uncleanness. Note a gap in the series: no session was ever recorded for [[Leviticus 16|Leviticus 16]] and 17, so this book passes from chapter 15 to chapter 18.

- This is the law of the leper in the day of his cleansing. The leper stays outside the camp; the priest goes forth out of the camp to him and looks whether the plague of leprosy be healed in the leper.
- The priest does not heal the plague; he examines the man after the healing and carries out the cleansing the Most High commanded.

- For him that is to be cleansed the priest commands two birds alive and clean, cedar wood, scarlet, and hyssop. One bird is killed in an earthen vessel over running water.
- The living bird, with the cedar wood, the scarlet, and the hyssop, is dipped in the blood of the bird that was killed over the running water; the priest sprinkles him that is to be cleansed seven times, pronounces him clean, and lets the living bird loose into the open field.

- He that is to be cleansed washes his clothes, shaves off all his hair, and washes himself in water, and comes into the camp; but he tarries abroad out of his tent seven days.
- On the seventh day he shaves all his hair off his head, his beard, and his eyebrows, washes his clothes and his flesh in water, and is clean.

- On the eighth day he brings two he lambs without blemish, one ewe lamb of the first year without blemish, three tenth deals of fine flour mingled with oil for a meat offering, and one log of oil.
- The priest that makes him clean presents him with these things before the Most High at the door of the tabernacle. One he lamb is offered for a trespass offering with the log of oil, waved for a wave offering, and slain in the holy place: as the sin offering is the priest's, so is the trespass offering; it is most holy.

- The priest takes of the blood of the trespass offering and puts it upon the tip of the right ear of him that is to be cleansed, upon the thumb of his right hand, and upon the great toe of his right foot.
- Of the log of oil the priest pours into his own left hand, sprinkles of it with his finger seven times before the Lord, and puts oil upon the ear, the thumb, and the toe, upon the blood of the trespass offering; the remnant of the oil he pours upon the head of him that is to be cleansed, to make an atonement for him before the Lord.

- If he be poor and cannot get so much, the law makes provision: one lamb for a trespass offering to be waved, one tenth deal of fine flour mingled with oil, a log of oil, and two turtledoves or two young pigeons, such as he is able to get, the one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering.
- The same order is kept for the poor man: the blood of the trespass offering upon the right ear, the right thumb, and the right great toe, the oil sprinkled seven times and put upon the same places, and the rest of the oil upon his head. This is the law of him in whom is the plague of leprosy, whose hand is not able to get that which pertaineth to his cleansing.

- The Most High tells Moses and Aaron that when Israel comes into the land of Canaan, and he puts the plague of leprosy in a house of the land of their possession, the owner shall come and tell the priest, saying, It seemeth to me there is as it were a plague in the house.
- The priest commands that the house be emptied before he goes in, that all that is in it be not made unclean; then he looks on the plague, and if it be in the walls with hollow strakes, greenish or reddish, in sight lower than the wall, he shuts up the house seven days.
- If at the seventh day the plague be spread in the walls, the stones in which the plague is are taken away and cast into an unclean place without the city; the house is scraped within round about, the dust poured out without the city into an unclean place, and other stones and other mortar are put in their place.

- If the plague come again and break out in the house after the stones were taken away and the house scraped and plastered, the priest looks, and if the plague be spread, it is a fretting leprosy in the house: it is unclean.
- He breaks down the house, the stones of it, the timber, and all the mortar, and carries them forth out of the city into an unclean place. He that goes into the house while it is shut up is unclean until the even, and he that lies or eats in the house washes his clothes.

# Leviticus 15: The Law of Issues and Separation

![[Leviticus 15#^v2]]

![[Leviticus 15#^v11]]

- When any man hath a running issue out of his flesh, because of his issue he is unclean, whether his flesh run with his issue or be stopped from his issue.
- Every bed whereon he lies and every thing whereon he sits is unclean. Whoever touches his bed, sits where he sat, touches his flesh, or is touched by him before he has rinsed his hands, washes his clothes, bathes himself in water, and is unclean until the even; the same goes for his spittle and his saddle.
- The vessel of earth that he touches shall be broken, and every vessel of wood shall be rinsed in water.

**[[Leviticus 15#^v13|Leviticus 15:13-15]]**

![[Leviticus 15#^v13]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v14]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v15]]

- When he that hath an issue is cleansed of his issue, he numbers to himself seven days for his cleansing, washes his clothes, and bathes his flesh in running water, and shall be clean.
- On the eighth day he takes two turtledoves or two young pigeons and comes before the Lord unto the door of the tabernacle, and gives them unto the priest; the priest offers the one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering, and makes an atonement for him before the Lord for his issue.

**[[Leviticus 15#^v16|Leviticus 15:16-18]]**

![[Leviticus 15#^v16]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v17]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v18]]

- If any man's seed of copulation go out from him, he washes all his flesh in water and is unclean until the even; every garment and every skin whereon is the seed is washed with water and unclean until the even.
- The woman also with whom a man lies with seed of copulation: they both bathe themselves in water and are unclean until the even.

**[[Leviticus 15#^v19|Leviticus 15:19-24]]**

![[Leviticus 15#^v19]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v20]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v21]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v22]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v23]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v24]]

- When a woman has an issue, and her issue in her flesh be blood, she is put apart seven days for her separation, and whosoever toucheth her is unclean until the even.
- Every thing that she lies upon or sits upon in her separation is unclean; whoever touches her bed or any thing she sat upon washes his clothes, bathes himself in water, and is unclean until the even.
- If any man lie with her at all, and her flowers be upon him, he is unclean seven days, and all the bed whereon he lies is unclean.

**[[Leviticus 15#^v25|Leviticus 15:25-27]]**

![[Leviticus 15#^v25]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v26]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v27]]

- If a woman have an issue of her blood many days out of the time of her separation, or if it run beyond the time of her separation, all the days of the issue of her uncleanness are as the days of her separation: she is unclean.
- Every bed she lies on and every thing she sits upon in those days is as the uncleanness of her separation, and whosoever touches those things washes his clothes, bathes in water, and is unclean until the even.

**[[Leviticus 15#^v28|Leviticus 15:28-30]]**

![[Leviticus 15#^v28]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v29]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v30]]

- When she is cleansed of her issue, she numbers to herself seven days, and after that she shall be clean.
- On the eighth day she takes two turtles or two young pigeons and brings them unto the priest to the door of the tabernacle; the priest offers the one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering, and makes an atonement for her before the Lord for the issue of her uncleanness.

**[[Leviticus 15#^v31|Leviticus 15:31-33]]**

![[Leviticus 15#^v31]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v32]]
![[Leviticus 15#^v33]]

- The purpose of the whole chapter is stated plainly: thus shall ye separate the children of Israel from their uncleanness, that they die not in their uncleanness when they defile the tabernacle of the Most High that is among them.
- This is the law of him that hath an issue, of him whose seed goeth from him, of her that is sick of her flowers, of him and her that have issues, and of him that lieth with her that is unclean.
