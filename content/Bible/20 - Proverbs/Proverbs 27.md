# Proverbs 27

1 Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth. ^v1

2 Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth; a stranger, and not thine own lips. ^v2

3 A stone is heavy, and the sand weighty; but a fool’s wrath is heavier than them both. ^v3

4 Wrath is cruel, and anger is outrageous; but who is able to stand before envy? ^v4

5 Open rebuke is better than secret love. ^v5

6 Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful. ^v6

7 The full soul loatheth an honeycomb; but to the hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet. ^v7

8 As a bird that wandereth from her nest, so is a man that wandereth from his place. ^v8

9 Ointment and perfume rejoice the heart: so doth the sweetness of a man’s friend by hearty counsel. ^v9

10 Thine own friend, and thy father’s friend, forsake not; neither go into thy brother’s house in the day of thy calamity: for better is a neighbour that is near than a brother far off. ^v10

11 My son, be wise, and make my heart glad, that I may answer him that reproacheth me. ^v11

12 A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself; but the simple pass on, and are punished. ^v12

13 Take his garment that is surety for a stranger, and take a pledge of him for a strange woman. ^v13

14 He that blesseth his friend with a loud voice, rising early in the morning, it shall be counted a curse to him. ^v14

15 A continual dropping in a very rainy day and a contentious woman are alike. ^v15

16 Whosoever hideth her hideth the wind, and the ointment of his right hand, which bewrayeth itself. ^v16

17 Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend. ^v17

18 Whoso keepeth the fig tree shall eat the fruit thereof: so he that waiteth on his master shall be honoured. ^v18

19 As in water face answereth to face, so the heart of man to man. ^v19

20 Hell and destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are never satisfied. ^v20

21 As the fining pot for silver, and the furnace for gold; so is a man to his praise. ^v21

22 Though thou shouldest bray a fool in a mortar among wheat with a pestle, yet will not his foolishness depart from him. ^v22

23 Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds. ^v23

24 For riches are not for ever: and doth the crown endure to every generation? ^v24

25 The hay appeareth, and the tender grass sheweth itself, and herbs of the mountains are gathered. ^v25

26 The lambs are for thy clothing, and the goats are the price of the field. ^v26

27 And thou shalt have goats’ milk enough for thy food, for the food of thy household, and for the maintenance for thy maidens. ^v27
