# Job 13

1 Lo, mine eye hath seen all this, mine ear hath heard and understood it. ^v1

2 What ye know, the same do I know also: I am not inferior unto you. ^v2

3 Surely I would speak to the Almighty, and I desire to reason with God. ^v3

4 But ye are forgers of lies, ye are all physicians of no value. ^v4

5 O that ye would altogether hold your peace! and it should be your wisdom. ^v5

6 Hear now my reasoning, and hearken to the pleadings of my lips. ^v6

7 Will ye speak wickedly for God? and talk deceitfully for him? ^v7

8 Will ye accept his person? will ye contend for God? ^v8

9 Is it good that he should search you out? or as one man mocketh another, do ye so mock him? ^v9

10 He will surely reprove you, if ye do secretly accept persons. ^v10

11 Shall not his excellency make you afraid? and his dread fall upon you? ^v11

12 Your remembrances are like unto ashes, your bodies to bodies of clay. ^v12

13 Hold your peace, let me alone, that I may speak, and let come on me what will. ^v13

14 Wherefore do I take my flesh in my teeth, and put my life in mine hand? ^v14

15 Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him: but I will maintain mine own ways before him. ^v15

16 He also shall be my salvation: for an hypocrite shall not come before him. ^v16

17 Hear diligently my speech, and my declaration with your ears. ^v17

18 Behold now, I have ordered my cause; I know that I shall be justified. ^v18

19 Who is he that will plead with me? for now, if I hold my tongue, I shall give up the ghost. ^v19

20 Only do not two things unto me: then will I not hide myself from thee. ^v20

21 Withdraw thine hand far from me: and let not thy dread make me afraid. ^v21

22 Then call thou, and I will answer: or let me speak, and answer thou me. ^v22

23 How many are mine iniquities and sins? make me to know my transgression and my sin. ^v23

24 Wherefore hidest thou thy face, and holdest me for thine enemy? ^v24

25 Wilt thou break a leaf driven to and fro? and wilt thou pursue the dry stubble? ^v25

26 For thou writest bitter things against me, and makest me to possess the iniquities of my youth. ^v26

27 Thou puttest my feet also in the stocks, and lookest narrowly unto all my paths; thou settest a print upon the heels of my feet. ^v27

28 And he, as a rotten thing, consumeth, as a garment that is moth eaten. ^v28
