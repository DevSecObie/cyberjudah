## Introduction

Thumbnail video: "For I would that ye knew what great conflict I have for you and for them at Laodicea, and for as many as have not seen my face in the flesh. It is good to be back in Austin, Texas. We are doing a study on Colossians chapter 2: Deceived by Enticing Words. The reason for touching on Colossians is that there have been some wacky breakdowns of this chapter going around, so we are going to go through it somewhat slowly and gain some understanding.

Books read to open the class:

- From Diaspora Themes: rabbinic literature almost totally ignores the vast western diaspora that existed at the time, particularly compared with the relatively large number of places in this diaspora mentioned in Acts chapter 2, in the chapters describing the journeys of Paul (Acts 13), and in the Pauline epistles, which give a comprehensive picture of the geography of the western Jewish Greek-speaking diaspora in the first century. That Greek-speaking diaspora was around Asia Minor. Paul was born in Tarsus; they were Greek-speaking, and his father sent him to Jerusalem to learn Hebrew and become a scholar in the scriptures.
- From a second book, on the results of Paul's teachings: it is not what happened to Paul personally that is important, but what happened to his teachings. Even while he lived, ample evidence proves that the people to whom he preached had misunderstood him. Most of the people who listened to Paul remembered only the connection between believing in Jesus and meriting God's mercy and ultimate resurrection, and they skipped the middle step: the need to live godly lives, the need to keep the commandments. Thus faith in Jesus came to be the only and entire basis of the religion adopted by the pagan Christians, while the Judeo-Christians who lived in Palestine continued to observe the law. But it was the pagan Christian attitude which won in the end, and Christianity spread most easily and rapidly in those cities in which Jews had lived for a long time. That is the church today: believe in Jesus and mercy is all they think or talk about. Christianity spread worldwide because it was a pagan religion with no commandments.
