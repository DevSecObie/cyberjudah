# The Covenant

The covenants of the Most High from Noah and Abraham through Sinai and the plains of Moab.

## Genesis

**[[Genesis 6#^v8|Genesis 6:8-9]]**  taught in [[Genesis 5-8 Study Notes|Genesis 5-8]]

- Noah found grace in the eyes of the Lord; he was a just man and perfect in his generations, and Noah walked with God.
- Without Noah none of us would be here; Shem, Ham, and Japheth repopulated the earth after the flood.
- Precept **[[Ecclesiasticus 44#^v17|Ecclesiasticus 44:17-18]]**: Noah was found perfect and righteous and was left as a remnant unto the earth when the flood came.

**[[Genesis 8#^v20|Genesis 8:20-22]]**  taught in [[Genesis 5-8 Study Notes|Genesis 5-8]]

- An everlasting covenant was made with Noah that all flesh should perish no more by a flood. Before it was water; the next time it is going to be fire, so let us get our minds right.

**[[Genesis 9#^v9|Genesis 9:9-11]]**  taught in [[Genesis 9-12 Study Notes|Genesis 9-12]]

- The covenant established here is with Noah, his seed, and every living creature: all flesh shall never again be cut off by the waters of a flood.
- This is a general covenant with all flesh. The covenant with Israel is found in [[Jeremiah 31|Jeremiah 31]].

**[[Genesis 9#^v12|Genesis 9:12-13]]**  taught in [[Genesis 9-12 Study Notes|Genesis 9-12]]

- The bow in the cloud, the rainbow, is the token of the covenant between the Most High and the earth; it compasses the heaven about with a glorious circle, and His hands bended it.
- The world has taken that same bow, the token of the covenant, and turned it into the symbol of sodomy. The spiritual eyes will see what that means: the great city is spiritually called Sodom and Egypt.

**[[Genesis 9#^v14|Genesis 9:14-15]]**  taught in [[Genesis 9-12 Study Notes|Genesis 9-12]]

- The waters shall no more become a flood to destroy all flesh. The next time the destruction is not water but fire, when the day of the Lord comes and the elements melt with fervent heat.

**[[Genesis 9#^v20|Genesis 9:20-23]]**  taught in [[Genesis 9-12 Study Notes|Genesis 9-12]]

- Noah was still perfect and righteous. A just man falleth seven times and riseth up again; one fall does not undo righteousness.
- Understand Noah's heart: he preached repentance for around a hundred years, and everyone he knew outside his house drowned. Strong drink is for him that is ready to perish and wine for those of heavy hearts; Noah drank too much, but he remained righteous in the eyes of the Most High.
- Ham saw the nakedness of his father and did not cover him; Shem and Japheth went backward and covered their father without looking. The commandments concerning nakedness were being applied; [[Leviticus 18|Leviticus 18]] opens those laws.
- Precept **[[Ecclesiasticus 44#^v17|Ecclesiasticus 44:17-18]]**: Noah was found perfect and righteous and was left as a remnant unto the earth.

**[[Genesis 12#^v2|Genesis 12:2-3]]**  taught in [[Genesis 9-12 Study Notes|Genesis 9-12]]

- In thee shall all families of the earth be blessed is not talking about everybody. The children of the prophets and of the covenant are Israel, and Israel would be scattered into all nations of the earth; through Abraham's seed the kindreds of the earth are blessed because scattered Israel gets the chance to repent and return, circumcised or not.
- Precept **[[Acts 3#^v25|Acts 3:25-26]]**: Ye are the children of the prophets and of the covenant made with Abraham; unto you first God sent His Son to bless you in turning you from your iniquities.

**[[Genesis 13#^v14|Genesis 13:14-17]]**  taught in [[Genesis 13-16 Study Notes|Genesis 13-16]]

- This is the same covenant language spoken over the children of Israel: as the sand of the sea which cannot be measured nor numbered.

**[[Genesis 15#^v13|Genesis 15:13-16]]**  taught in [[Genesis 13-16 Study Notes|Genesis 13-16]]

- The twelve tribes scattered among the nations today, the people called Blacks, Hispanics, and Native Americans, are the seed this covenant history belongs to.

**[[Genesis 16#^v15|Genesis 16:15-16]]**  taught in [[Genesis 13-16 Study Notes|Genesis 13-16]]

- Abram was fourscore and six years old, eighty six, when Hagar bare Ishmael. Ishmael was Abram's firstborn, but the covenant did not go to Ishmael; the covenant goes through the twelve tribes of Israel.

**[[Genesis 17#^v4|Genesis 17:4-6]]**  taught in [[Genesis 17-20 Study Notes|Genesis 17-20]]

- When the Most High began dealing with Abram on a covenant level, his name was changed: Abram became Abraham, a father of many nations, and kings would come out of him.
- The blessing of all men and the covenant were made to rest upon the head of Jacob and divided among the twelve tribes. Kings come out of that seed, including Christ, the King of kings.
- Precept **[[Genesis 35#^v10|Genesis 35:10-11]]**: The same covenant words passed to Jacob, named Israel: nations and kings out of his loins.
- Precept **[[Ecclesiasticus 44#^v19|Ecclesiasticus 44:19-23]]**: The blessing of all men and the covenant rested on the head of Jacob and was divided among the twelve tribes.

**[[Genesis 17#^v7|Genesis 17:7]]**  taught in [[Genesis 17-20 Study Notes|Genesis 17-20]]

- The covenant is with Abraham's seed after him, and seed is literal offspring, not a spiritual metaphor. The Most High promised to be a God to Abraham and to his seed after him.

**[[Genesis 17#^v10|Genesis 17:10-14]]**  taught in [[Genesis 17-20 Study Notes|Genesis 17-20]]

- Circumcision is the token of the covenant: every man child circumcised at eight days, and the uncircumcised soul cut off from his people.
- It still has to be done. A covenant is a contract with terms; the covenant was confirmed to Jacob for a law, and circumcision is one of the laws within the covenant.
- Precept **[[1 Chronicles 16#^v15|1 Chronicles 16:15-17]]**: Be mindful always of His covenant, confirmed to Jacob for a law; the covenant carries terms.

**[[Genesis 17#^v15|Genesis 17:15-19]]**  taught in [[Genesis 17-20 Study Notes|Genesis 17-20]]

- Sarai's name was changed to Sarah, and she shall be a mother of nations; those are the same nations of the covenant, and she and Abraham had one child together, Isaac.

**[[Genesis 17#^v20|Genesis 17:20-21]]**  taught in [[Genesis 17-20 Study Notes|Genesis 17-20]]

- Ishmael received a blessing: fruitful, multiplied exceedingly, twelve princes, a great nation. But the word to Abraham was kings shall come out of thee, and the covenant passes to Isaac: my covenant will I establish with Isaac.

**[[Genesis 21#^v22|Genesis 21:22-32]]**  taught in [[Genesis 21-24 Study Notes|Genesis 21-24]]

- Abraham reproved Abimelech over a well his servants had taken violently, and they made a covenant; the seven ewe lambs stood as a witness that Abraham digged the well.

**[[Genesis 22#^v15|Genesis 22:15-18]]**  taught in [[Genesis 21-24 Study Notes|Genesis 21-24]]

- The seed possessing the gate of his enemies is part of the covenant: the seed of Jacob shall possess the remnant of Edom and of all the heathen, and Edom shall be a possession. That is why all nations cannot be inside this covenant; the covenant has Israel possessing their enemies.

**[[Genesis 24#^v58|Genesis 24:58-60]]**  taught in [[Genesis 21-24 Study Notes|Genesis 21-24]]

- The covenant narrows again here: it runs through Rebekah's children, and of her two sons it is Jacob who carries it, for Esau sold his birthright. The covenants in Genesis cannot be broken.

**[[Genesis 26#^v1|Genesis 26:1-5]]**  taught in [[Genesis 24-27 Study Notes|Genesis 24-27]]

- In thy seed shall all nations be blessed does not put everybody in the covenant. The seed is Christ, and the children of the prophets and of the covenant are Israel, scattered into every nation; through Christ scattered Israel in all kindreds of the earth receives repentance and comes back to the Father.
- Precept **[[Acts 3#^v25|Acts 3:25-26]]**: Ye are the children of the prophets and of the covenant; unto you first God sent His Son to bless you in turning every one from his iniquities.

**[[Genesis 26#^v26|Genesis 26:26-31]]**  taught in [[Genesis 24-27 Study Notes|Genesis 24-27]]

- Isaac was not simple; he separated from the people that hated him rather than assimilating among them, and he dealt with their covenant with open eyes. The words can be like butter while war is in the heart.

**[[Genesis 28#^v6|Genesis 28:6-9]]**  taught in [[Genesis 28-31 Study Notes|Genesis 28-31]]

- Esau saw that the daughters of Canaan pleased not Isaac his father, but instead of following Jacob's path he went to Ishmael and took Mahalath to add to the wives he had. He answered his father's grief by doing his own thing.
- Esau had already taken Hittite wives that were a grief of mind unto Isaac and Rebekah, and his generations show wives of the daughters of Canaan. He was hardheaded from the beginning; that is the spirit of the wicked, who hate instruction and cast the words of the Most High behind them.
- Precept **[[Psalms 50#^v16|Psalms 50:16-17]]**: Unto the wicked God saith, Thou hatest instruction, and castest my words behind thee; that is Esau's spirit.

**[[Genesis 32#^v9|Genesis 32:9-12]]**  taught in [[Genesis 32-35 Study Notes|Genesis 32-35]]

- He pleaded the covenant: thou saidst, I will make thy seed as the sand of the sea. That is the pattern for our prayers; plead the promise made to the fathers and ask the Most High to remember His covenant.

**[[Genesis 35#^v9|Genesis 35:9-11]]**  taught in [[Genesis 32-35 Study Notes|Genesis 32-35]]

- The nation and company of nations of Abraham's covenant is Israel, plain and clear, and kings come out of Israel's loins. Christ washed us from our sins and made us kings and priests unto God; the men of Israel are kings in the sight of the Most High.

**[[Genesis 38#^v24|Genesis 38:24-26]]**  taught in [[Genesis 36-39 Study Notes|Genesis 36-39]]

- When Judah heard Tamar was with child by whoredom he said, Bring her forth, and let her be burnt. A woman promised in the covenant that plays the harlot and brings an heir by another disobeys the law of the Most High, and her reproach is not blotted out; that is why the judgment was so heavy.

**[[Genesis 41#^v15|Genesis 41:15-16]]**  taught in [[Genesis 40-43 Study Notes|Genesis 40-43]]

- Pharaoh told Joseph he had heard he could understand a dream to interpret it, and Joseph gave all the praise to the Most High: it is not in me; God shall give Pharaoh an answer of peace.
- The Most High reveals his secrets to his servants, and the secret of the Lord is with them that fear him. That is why the interpretation was with Joseph and not with the magicians of Egypt.
- Precept **[[Psalms 25#^v14|Psalm 25:14]]**: The secret of the Lord is with them that fear him; the Most High shows those things unto his servants.

## Exodus

**[[Exodus 2#^v23|Exodus 2:23-25]]**  taught in [[Exodus 2-5 Study Notes|Exodus 2-5]]

- God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob. The bondage itself was prophesied to Abraham: his seed would be a stranger in a land not theirs and be afflicted four hundred years, and afterward come out with great substance. That prophecy is about Egypt, not a date of release in this present day.

**[[Exodus 4#^v24|Exodus 4:24-26]]**  taught in [[Exodus 2-5 Study Notes|Exodus 2-5]]

- This shows the laws did not begin at Sinai. Circumcision was the covenant given to Abraham for every man child in his generations, on the eighth day, and the uncircumcised man child whose flesh is not circumcised, that soul shall be cut off from his people: he hath broken my covenant. That is why Moses was worthy of death.
- Precept **[[Genesis 17#^v9|Genesis 17:9-14]]**: Every man child among you shall be circumcised, he that is eight days old, for an everlasting covenant; the uncircumcised man child shall be cut off from his people; he hath broken my covenant.
- Precept **[[Genesis 17#^v23|Genesis 17:23-24]]**: Abraham was ninety years old and nine when he was circumcised; the covenant is kept at any age.
- Precept **[[Acts 16#^v1|Acts 16:1-3]]**: Paul took Timothy, whose father was a Greek, and circumcised him because of the covenant.
- Precept **[[Leviticus 18#^v3|Leviticus 18:3]]**: After the doings of the land of Egypt, wherein ye dwelt, shall ye not do; Egypt did not keep the covenant of circumcision.
- Precept **[[Romans 2#^v25|Romans 2:25-29]]**: Circumcision verily profiteth, if thou keep the law; he is a Jew, which is one inwardly, and circumcision is that of the heart; it speaks to lawbreaking, not to ending the covenant of the flesh.
- Precept **[[Matthew 23#^v25|Matthew 23:25-28]]**: Ye make clean the outside of the cup, but within ye are full of hypocrisy and iniquity; the outward show is not the covenant.

**[[Exodus 6#^v4|Exodus 6:4-5]]**  taught in [[Exodus 6-9 Study Notes|Exodus 6-9]]

- The covenant stands to give Israel the land of Canaan, the land of their pilgrimage wherein they were strangers; and the Most High heard the groaning of the children of Israel whom the Egyptians kept in bondage.

**[[Exodus 19#^v5|Exodus 19:5-8]]**  taught in [[Exodus 18-21 Study Notes|Exodus 18-21]]

- If ye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant, then ye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me above all people, a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation.
- Moses laid all these words before the elders, and all the people answered together, all that the Lord hath spoken we will do. Right there Israel made a covenant, an agreement, with the Most High.

**[[Exodus 21#^v2|Exodus 21:2-6]]**  taught in [[Exodus 18-21 Study Notes|Exodus 18-21]]

- Zedekiah's covenant in Jeremiah confirms the law in the prophets: every man was to let his Hebrew manservant and maidservant go free at the appointed release.
- Precept **[[Jeremiah 34#^v8|Jeremiah 34:8-10]]**: Zedekiah made a covenant to proclaim liberty, that every man let his Hebrew manservant and maidservant go free; the law of release stood in the prophets.

**[[Exodus 23#^v13|Exodus 23:13]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- The Lord says this same thing three ways in this one chapter, and the other two places show the meaning: verse 24, thou shalt not bow down to their gods nor serve them nor do after their works; and verses 32 and 33, thou shalt make no covenant with them nor with their gods, for if thou serve their gods it will surely be a snare unto thee. Make no mention means do not learn how to serve and worship them.

**[[Exodus 23#^v32|Exodus 23:32-33]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- Thou shalt make no covenant with them, nor with their gods. They shall not dwell in thy land, lest they make thee sin against me: for if thou serve their gods, it will surely be a snare unto thee. This is the third way the Lord states the command of verse 13 in this chapter: no learning their worship, no serving, no covenant.

**[[Exodus 24#^v4|Exodus 24:4-8]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- Moses took half of the blood in basons and half he sprinkled on the altar; then he took the book of the covenant and read in the audience of the people, and they said, All that the Lord hath said will we do, and be obedient. And he sprinkled the blood on the people: Behold the blood of the covenant, which the Lord hath made with you concerning all these words. This sprinkling ties in later to the book of Hebrews and its teaching on sacrifice.
- Notice verse seven: Moses had a book of the covenant written and read it before he ever went up into the mount for the forty days. It was more than ten commandments; a whole book of what was to be done was already broken down before he went up.

**[[Exodus 24#^v13|Exodus 24:13-18]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- If you have been actively listening: Moses already had the book of the covenant written before he went up. In the forty days and forty nights he came down with even more; it was never just the ten commandments.

**[[Exodus 25#^v3|Exodus 25:3-7]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- The offering was gold, silver, brass, blue, purple, and scarlet, fine linen, goats' hair, rams' skins dyed red, badgers' skins, shittim wood, oil for the light, spices, and onyx stones for the ephod and the breastplate. Thank the Most High he requires no gold and silver now, for half of us would have nothing to give; under the new covenant the offering is the body presented a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable.

**[[Exodus 28#^v39|Exodus 28:39-43]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- The broidered coat of fine linen, the mitre of fine linen, and the girdle of needlework; and for Aaron's sons coats, girdles, and bonnets, for glory and for beauty, to be anointed and consecrated in.
- Linen breeches to cover their nakedness, from the loins even unto the thighs, upon Aaron and his sons when they come near the altar to minister, that they bear not iniquity and die: a statute for ever unto him and his seed after him.
- Breeches are pants; the grandmothers of the south said pull your britches up, and the hosen of [[Daniel 3|Daniel 3]] is defined with one word, pants. This is where pants were ordained in the scriptures, and the statute for ever means that from beginning to end of time pants were created for men, period. Women are not to wear what pertaineth unto a man.
- Why they would bear iniquity without the breeches: before the breeches were instituted, going up by steps unto the altar would have discovered their nakedness, so the Most High commanded the covering.
- Ecclesiasticus gives the second account of these garments and confirms the breeches under the robe of glory. It also shows the pomegranates and bells compassed him, all around the hem and not four corners only, and that the crown of gold sat upon the mitre before Israel had any kings, because mediator between the Most High and His people is the highest honor; for the brothers given the honor to lead and teach among the nation, do not take it for granted or be slothful in it, for there is nothing more honorable to do on this earth.
- Precept **[[Ecclesiasticus 45#^v6|Ecclesiasticus 45:6-13]]**: The second account of Aaron's garments: rich garments with breeches, pomegranates and bells compassing him round about, the whole world in the writing of the tribes, and a crown of gold upon the mitre; no stranger ever put them on.

**[[Exodus 29#^v5|Exodus 29:5-9]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- Ecclesiasticus records the same consecration: Moses anointed him with holy oil, an everlasting covenant to Aaron and to his seed so long as the heavens should remain, to minister unto Him, execute the office of the priesthood, offer the sacrifices, and teach Jacob the testimonies and inform Israel in His laws. The priest's job was the offerings and teaching the nation the law, statutes, and commandments.
- What happened to that covenant is answered in Malachi, where the commandment is spoken to the priests: the covenant with Levi was of life and peace, the priest's lips should keep knowledge and they should seek the law at his mouth, for he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts; but ye are departed out of the way, ye have caused many to stumble at the law, ye have corrupted the covenant of Levi. The priests were finessing the offerings, keeping the best animals for themselves and sacrificing the blind and the broken, and they perverted the teachings of the law for their own benefit.
- That is why leadership today is not walking around requiring Levite lineage of Aaron. Christ is the high priest now, and the nation is a whole nation of kings and priests; Malachi and Hebrews are two good books to study on the changing of the priesthood, against the doctrine that the Aaronic covenant could never change.
- Precept **[[Ecclesiasticus 45#^v15|Ecclesiasticus 45:15-17]]**: The everlasting covenant of the priesthood with Aaron and his seed: to offer the sacrifices and to teach Jacob the testimonies and inform Israel in His laws.
- Precept **[[Malachi 2#^v5|Malachi 2:5-8]]**: The covenant with Levi was of life and peace and the priest is the messenger of the Lord of hosts; but ye are departed out of the way, caused many to stumble at the law, and have corrupted the covenant of Levi.

**[[Exodus 31#^v16|Exodus 31:16-17]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- The children of Israel shall keep the sabbath, to observe the sabbath throughout their generations, for a perpetual covenant; it is a sign between the Most High and the children of Israel for ever, for in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed.

**[[Exodus 32#^v11|Exodus 32:11-14]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- And the LORD repented of the evil which he thought to do unto his people. The Most High does not repent as a man; he has no one to answer to, he simply turned from the anger of that moment. He was never going to break his covenant, for consuming the people would have destroyed all the seed of Jacob except Moses; Moses was there to remind him of his own word.

**[[Exodus 34#^v10|Exodus 34:10]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- The Most High made a covenant to do marvels such as have not been done in all the earth, nor in any nation, and all the people among which Israel dwells shall see the work of the Lord. One of those marvels is laid out in the Revelation.

**[[Exodus 34#^v11|Exodus 34:11-16]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- Take heed to thyself, lest thou make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land, lest it be for a snare, a trap, in the midst of thee. Ye shall destroy their altars, break their images, and cut down their groves, just as the law states it again in Deuteronomy; for the Lord, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God.

**[[Exodus 34#^v27|Exodus 34:27-28]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- Write thou these words: for after the tenor of these words I have made a covenant with thee and with Israel. The words are the laws the Lord had just given.
- Moses was there with the Lord forty days and forty nights; he did neither eat bread, nor drink water. And he wrote upon the tables the words of the covenant, the ten commandments; the words of the covenant take in the directions of the sacrifices, which animals were to be offered and how it was to be done, together with the commandments.

**[[Exodus 35#^v10|Exodus 35:10-19]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- The list runs through the whole work: the tabernacle, his tent, his covering, his taches, boards, bars, pillars, and sockets; the ark of the covenant with the staves and the mercy seat, and the vail of the covering; the table with his staves and vessels and the shewbread; the candlestick, which is the menorah, with his lamps and the oil for the light; the incense altar; the altar of burnt offering, where the burnt offering was burnt, with his brasen grate; the laver; the hangings of the court; and the cloths of service, the holy garments for Aaron the priest and the garments of his sons, to minister in the priest's office.

**[[Exodus 37#^v6|Exodus 37:6-9]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- The most holy place, where the ark of the covenant and the mercy seat sat, was the central focus of the entire tabernacle: there the Most High spoke to the high priest above the mercy seat, in the area where the wings of the cherubims faced each other; the tablets of the law were placed inside the ark.

**[[Exodus 37#^v10|Exodus 37:10-16]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- This is the table of shewbread: on it Aaron and his sons placed twelve loaves of bread made from fine flour, and these twelve loaves represented the twelve tribes of Israel, a continual reminder of the everlasting promises, the covenant between the Most High and the children of Israel, and a memorial of His provision of food; the bread was eaten by Aaron and his sons and replaced every week on the sabbath.

## Leviticus

**[[Leviticus 2#^v13|Leviticus 2:13]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 2-5 Study Notes|Leviticus 2-5]]

- Every oblation of the meat offering had to be seasoned with salt, the salt of the covenant of thy God; with all thine offerings thou shalt offer salt. It is physical too: you offer something up, you whip something up, you better season that thing.
- Israel has a covenant of salt with the Lord. When Abijah king of Judah stood on the mount to defuse the war between the northern kingdom and the southern kingdom, around the 900s BC, he told Jeroboam: ought ye not to know that the Lord God of Israel gave the kingdom over Israel to David for ever, even to him and to his sons by a covenant of salt? The northern kingdom far exceeded the southern in numbers, but the numbers did not matter.
- Salt is a preservative; it keeps a thing fresh. The covenant of salt goes into the brotherhood: we are supposed to preserve our relationships, keep our alliance fresh, and grow together. Everything given to the Levites for sacrifice carried that symbolism.
- Precept **[[2 Chronicles 13#^v1|2 Chronicles 13:1-5]]**: Abijah told Jeroboam that the Lord gave the kingdom to David and his sons by a covenant of salt; the salt is the preserving of the bond.

**[[Leviticus 4#^v1|Leviticus 4:1-3]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 2-5 Study Notes|Leviticus 2-5]]

- But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God. Christ took away the first covenant to establish the second, and we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all. Men had been sacrificing since the days of Adam; when Christ came, one sacrifice ended it, because that lamb had no blemish. The law of sacrifice was always temporary, a shadow of good things to come, and the good thing that came was Christ.
- Precept **[[Hebrews 10#^v7|Hebrews 10:7-12]]**: Sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not; he taketh away the first covenant to establish the second, and this man offered one sacrifice for sins for ever and sat down.

**[[Leviticus 8#^v1|Leviticus 8:1-5]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 6-9 Study Notes|Leviticus 6-9]]

- Mark the list: if you want to live under the Old Covenant, you had better have a priest, the garments, the anointing oil, a bullock for the sin offering, two rams, a basket of unleavened bread, and a temple to gather at. The covenant of sacrifice cannot be kept today.

**[[Leviticus 12#^v3|Leviticus 12:3]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 10-13 Study Notes|Leviticus 10-13]]

- In the eighth day the flesh of his foreskin shall be circumcised. The law is Genesis 17: every man child among you shall be circumcised at eight days old, a token of the covenant, and my covenant shall be in your flesh for an everlasting covenant; the uncircumcised man child whose flesh is not circumcised, that soul shall be cut off from his people. Brothers coming into the truth must be circumcised.
- Precept **[[Genesis 17#^v10|Genesis 17:10-14]]**: Every man child eight days old shall be circumcised, an everlasting covenant in the flesh; the uncircumcised is cut off.

**[[Leviticus 21#^v10|Leviticus 21:10-12]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 18-21 Study Notes|Leviticus 18-21]]

- The high priest, upon whose head the anointing oil was poured, was not to uncover his head nor rend his clothes, nor go in to any dead body, nor go out of the sanctuary; the crown of the anointing oil of his God was upon him. Under the old covenant his head was covered with the mitre.

**[[Leviticus 24#^v5|Leviticus 24:5-9]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 22-25 Study Notes|Leviticus 22-25]]

- Twelve cakes of fine flour were set in two rows, six on a row, upon the pure table before the Lord, with pure frankincense upon each row, every sabbath, taken from the children of Israel by an everlasting covenant.

**[[Leviticus 25#^v35|Leviticus 25:35-38]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 22-25 Study Notes|Leviticus 22-25]]

- I am the Lord your God, which brought you forth out of the land of Egypt, to give you the land of Canaan. The Most High is not the God of everybody: to bring Israel out He judged Egypt, and to give Israel Canaan He cast out the seven nations, greater and mightier than Israel, delivered them before Israel to be smitten, with no covenant and no mercy shewed, during that time period.
- Precept **[[Deuteronomy 7#^v1|Deuteronomy 7:1-2]]**: Seven nations greater and mightier than Israel were cast out and delivered before Israel to be utterly destroyed, with no covenant made and no mercy shewed; that is how the Most High gave the land of Canaan.

**[[Leviticus 26#^v3|Leviticus 26:3-4]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 26-27 Study Notes|Leviticus 26-27]]

**[[Joshua 7#^v10|Joshua 7:10-12]]**  taught in [[Joshua 5-8 Study Notes|Joshua 5-8]]

- The Lord said, Get thee up; wherefore liest thou upon thy face? Israel hath sinned. It does not say Achan sinned; it says Israel. This walk is not selfish or individual: when you are in sin you hurt your wife, your husband, your children, the congregation, and the leadership, for we are all our brother's keeper and responsible for one another.
- They have taken of the accursed thing, and stolen, and dissembled, and put it among their own stuff: the Lord says they. As a father who finds mischief chastens all his children together when none confesses, so the Most High deals with his children as one house; we pay for one another's sins.
- Therefore the children of Israel could not stand before their enemies: the Lord was not going to let them win, and he put a mightier warring spirit on the men of Ai. However well you train with your sword and shield, the Most High controls the outcome.
- His power cannot be comprehended: great things doeth he, which we cannot comprehend; touching the Almighty, we cannot find him out. Stop trying to figure the Creator out and questioning why he created what he created; you are the creation, so keep the commandments and play your role.
- Neither will I be with you any more, except ye destroy the accursed from among you: what is accursed must be destroyed; that is the only way to remove it.

**[[Joshua 7#^v13|Joshua 7:13-15]]**  taught in [[Joshua 5-8 Study Notes|Joshua 5-8]]

- Up, sanctify the people against tomorrow: there is an accursed thing in the midst of thee, O Israel; thou canst not stand before thine enemies until ye take away the accursed thing from among you.
- The search would narrow by the Lord's own taking: tribe, then family, then household, then man by man; and he that is taken with the accursed thing shall be burnt with fire, he and all that he hath.
- The folly was wrought against a plain commandment: Joshua had already charged them in [[Joshua 6#^v18|Joshua 6:18-19]] to keep from the accursed thing lest they trouble the camp, and adjured them in [[Joshua 6#^v26|Joshua 6:26]]; the transgressor troubled the camp, the army and congregation of Israel.

**[[Joshua 8#^v33|Joshua 8:33]]**  taught in [[Joshua 5-8 Study Notes|Joshua 5-8]]

- All Israel, and their elders, and officers, and their judges stood on this side the ark and on that side, before the priests the Levites, the stranger as well as he that was born among them, half over against mount Gerizim and half over against mount Ebal, as Moses commanded, that they should bless the people of Israel.
- Why does the body set up rank instead of saying I only follow Christ? Because we are commanded to rehearse the righteous acts of the Lord: reading the wars and works of Joshua, Moses, and the officers, judges, and elders, we practice what our forefathers did, in order, as an organized army.
- Rehearse means practice, and practice makes perfect: there are rehearsals every day, classes and meetings for the men, the women, and the children, because this is not our rest.

## 1 Samuel

**[[1 Samuel 2#^v3|1 Samuel 2:3]]**  taught in [[1 Samuel 1-3 Study Notes|1 Samuel 1-3]]

- All of us in this walk, keeping the commandments and the faith in Christ, have taken covenant and proclaimed out of our mouths that we are going to endure to the end; we broke the bread and drank the wine and took the covenant with the Lord. Now the Lord is judging you by your actions: are you going to endure to the end, whether to Christ's return or until you leave this earth, if you truly love him?

**[[1 Samuel 3#^v2|1 Samuel 3:2-3]]**  taught in [[1 Samuel 1-3 Study Notes|1 Samuel 1-3]]

- Eli was laid down in his place, his eyes beginning to wax dim, and ere the lamp of God went out in the temple of the Lord, where the ark of God was, Samuel was laid down to sleep. Ere means before: before the lamp of God went out. That light in the temple by the ark was never to go out, though at a point in time it did go out.
- Precept **[[Exodus 19#^v3|Exodus 19:3-6]]**: Before the Most High even gave the ten commandments he made this plain: if ye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant, then ye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me above all people, a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation. Obedience is what he desired most from the beginning.

**[[1 Samuel 3#^v4|1 Samuel 3:4-7]]**  taught in [[1 Samuel 1-3 Study Notes|1 Samuel 1-3]]

- Do not have your children going into a covenant with Christ that he will judge them for if they break it; the scripture says, for this cause many are weak and sickly among you, and many sleep. You do not know if your kids are going to serve the Lord when they go out on their own, or what kind of lusts are in them that will draw them away into the world when they come of age. It is your job as a parent to train them up and pray they stay in it: kids learn by watching, so let them see you take that covenant over and over again until they are ready, and make them rehearse the righteous acts. When the child comes to the age of reasoning and knows the Lord for himself, and the Lord reveals himself to him, you will know it; do not rush it.
- Precept **[[1 Corinthians 11#^v29|1 Corinthians 11:29-30]]**: He that eateth and drinketh unworthily, eateth and drinketh damnation to himself; for this cause many are weak and sickly among you, and many sleep. That covenant is not to be forced on children who do not yet know the Lord.

**[[1 Samuel 4#^v3|1 Samuel 4:3-5]]**  taught in [[1 Samuel 4-7 Study Notes|1 Samuel 4-7]]

- The elders of Israel asked the right question, Wherefore hath the Lord smitten us to day before the Philistines? But instead of examining the nation, they had an idea: fetch the ark of the covenant out of Shiloh, and when it comes among us it will save us out of the hand of our enemies.
- The two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, came with the ark, and when it entered the camp all Israel shouted with a great shout so that the earth rang again. That is how excited and ecstatic Israel was to have the ark of the covenant among them; they felt their enemies could not destroy them now.
- So what is the ark of the covenant, and what does it contain? The Lord told Moses to hew two tables of stone like the first ones he broke, and the Lord rewrote the ten commandments on them with his own finger, and Moses put the tables in the ark. The ark of the covenant contained the commandments that God wrote.

**[[1 Samuel 4#^v6|1 Samuel 4:6-9]]**  taught in [[1 Samuel 4-7 Study Notes|1 Samuel 4-7]]

- When the Philistines heard the noise of the shout and understood that the ark of the Lord was come into the camp, they were afraid, saying, God is come into the camp; woe unto us, for there hath not been such a thing heretofore. They had never had to battle Israel with the ark of the covenant in the camp.

**[[1 Samuel 6#^v19|1 Samuel 6:19-21]]**  taught in [[1 Samuel 4-7 Study Notes|1 Samuel 4-7]]

- He smote the men of Beth-shemesh, because they had looked into the ark of the Lord, even fifty thousand and threescore and ten men. It was not just the other nations that had to keep discretion with the ark of the covenant; it was forbidden to touch it or look into it unless the Lord gave you authority, no matter what tribe you were, even Levi.
