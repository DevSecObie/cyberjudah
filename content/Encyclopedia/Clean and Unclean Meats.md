# Clean and Unclean Meats

The dietary laws: what may be eaten, what is an abomination, and the precepts behind them.

## Genesis

**[[Genesis 1#^v26|Genesis 1:26-27]]**  taught in [[Genesis 1-4 Study Notes|Genesis 1-4]]

- Notice it says let them have dominion. The Most High did not create just one man; the man spoken of here is mankind.
- The words created he him point to a particular person, who begins to be described in [[Genesis 2|Genesis 2]]. Male and female created he them is mankind.

**[[Genesis 5#^v1|Genesis 5:1-2]]**  taught in [[Genesis 5-8 Study Notes|Genesis 5-8]]

- The words made he him speak of the first Adam. This Adam was made in the likeness of God, a direct descendant of the Most High.
- Male and female created he them, and called their name Adam: they were not that Adam; these were the other people on the earth. When the Most High creates a fish He does not create only a salmon; there is mackerel and tuna and all types of fish. In the same way, He created Adam and He created the other people.
- Chapter 5 is the direct lineage of Adam. That lineage is the sons of God you read of in chapter 6, and the later chapters of Genesis tie the Israelites directly to the lineage of Adam through Shem.
- When Christ said, Ye are gods, He was saying we come from that line of Adam.
- Precept **[[Genesis 1#^v26|Genesis 1:26-27]]**: The man made in our image, created he him, is the first Adam; male and female created he them is the other people.

**[[Genesis 7#^v2|Genesis 7:2-3]]**  taught in [[Genesis 5-8 Study Notes|Genesis 5-8]]

- The dietary law was not yet instituted, but the Most High had already defined what was a clean beast and what was an unclean beast, all the way back in Genesis; Moses reiterated that law to the children of Israel.
- Today this gets confused with the doctrine that says I can eat all things through Christ who strengthens me, and that is not true; the laws were instituted for us to discern between the unclean and the clean.
- Precept **[[Leviticus 11#^v45|Leviticus 11:45-47]]**: The law of the beasts makes a difference between the unclean and the clean, the beast that may be eaten and the beast that may not be eaten.
- Precept **[[Ezekiel 44#^v23|Ezekiel 44:23]]**: The people are taught to discern between the unclean and the clean; that is what the laws were instituted for.

**[[Genesis 7#^v21|Genesis 7:21-23]]**  taught in [[Genesis 5-8 Study Notes|Genesis 5-8]]

- This was the first time the Most High destroyed the earth for the wickedness that was being done. The flood came because they despised the commandments; destruction came because of sin.
- Of them that dwelt in the world only Noah and his household were left, of whom came all righteous men: through Shem's seed to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and through their line the Israelites.
- The breath of life is twofold. One is the commandments, which were given to Adam; the other is the breathing life, the oxygen. Here everything on the dry land that breathed died; the fish were not touched.

**[[Genesis 8#^v20|Genesis 8:20-22]]**  taught in [[Genesis 5-8 Study Notes|Genesis 5-8]]

- Noah built an altar and offered burnt offerings of every clean beast and every clean fowl, and the Most High smelled a sweet savour and said He would not again curse the ground any more for man's sake.

**[[Genesis 9#^v3|Genesis 9:3]]**  taught in [[Genesis 9-12 Study Notes|Genesis 9-12]]

- This is the introduction of man eating meat. The Most High did not give man a new stomach; the body was already made for it and it lay dormant until after the flood.
- Having the liberty to eat meat does not mean eating without balance; be not too greedy upon meats, for excess of meats brings sickness.
- The law of what may be eaten was already understood; you could not eat everything. The doctrine that says you can pray over anything and eat it is a seducing spirit and a doctrine of devils.
- Fish is meat; all flesh is not the same flesh, but the flesh of beasts, of fishes, and of birds are all flesh. Every creature of God is good for what it was made for, and it is sanctified by the word of God, and the word that sanctifies what may be eaten is the dietary law.
- Precept **[[Leviticus 11#^v46|Leviticus 11:46-47]]**: The law of the beasts makes a difference between the beast that may be eaten and the beast that may not be eaten.

**[[Genesis 9#^v4|Genesis 9:4]]**  taught in [[Genesis 9-12 Study Notes|Genesis 9-12]]

- The law against eating flesh with the blood was given to Adam and is still in place with Noah; the word is true from the beginning.

## Exodus

**[[Exodus 4#^v24|Exodus 4:24-26]]**  taught in [[Exodus 2-5 Study Notes|Exodus 2-5]]

- By the way in the inn, the LORD met him, and sought to kill him, because Moses had not circumcised his son. Zipporah took a sharp stone, cut off the foreskin of her son, and said, Surely a bloody husband art thou to me: bloody because of the circumcision she had to perform herself.
- This shows the laws did not begin at Sinai. Circumcision was the covenant given to Abraham for every man child in his generations, on the eighth day, and the uncircumcised man child whose flesh is not circumcised, that soul shall be cut off from his people: he hath broken my covenant. That is why Moses was worthy of death.
- Precept **[[Leviticus 12#^v1|Leviticus 12:1-3]]**: In the eighth day the flesh of his foreskin shall be circumcised; the law Moses himself later carried to Israel.
- Precept **[[Revelation 21#^v27|Revelation 21:27]]**: There shall in no wise enter into it any thing that defileth; the unclean do not enter the kingdom.

**[[Exodus 21#^v28|Exodus 21:28-29]]**  taught in [[Exodus 18-21 Study Notes|Exodus 18-21]]

- If an ox gore a man or a woman that they die, the ox is stoned and his flesh not eaten, and the owner is quit. But if the ox were wont to push with his horns in time past, and it hath been testified to his owner, and he hath not kept him in, and it kill a man or a woman, the ox is stoned and his owner also put to death.
- Most do not have oxen today, but the same judgment reaches the yard dogs, the pit bulls, German shepherds, and rottweilers. If the dog has bitten before and it was reported to you, and it gets out again through a snapped chain or a breach in the fence and mauls or kills somebody, the scripture holds the owner responsible unto death; that is why animal control writes the ticket and warns you to keep your dogs in.
- The law is slack right now, as Habakkuk says, so many punishments worthy of death in the scriptures, like rape or this judgment, come out today as a fine or a slap on the hand.

**[[Exodus 25#^v3|Exodus 25:3-7]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- The badgers' skins settle a confusion: the Lord commanded Israel to handle animal skins and hair to build his own tabernacle. So the laws about not touching that which is defiled, the carcass of the unclean beast, go into the matter of sacrifice and coming into the temple, not into everyday handling. A boar-bristle brush is not sin; brush your hair, brothers.

**[[Exodus 26#^v7|Exodus 26:7-14]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- The badgers' skins settle a doctrine question. [[Leviticus 11|Leviticus 11]] says not to touch the carcase of the unclean beast, and the badger goes on paws, yet the tabernacle had to be built with badger skins by hand. The law goes into sacrificing the unclean thing and eating it, touching it with your mouth; it is not saying a dead animal in your path cannot be moved out of the way with your hands.
- Precept **[[Leviticus 11#^v8|Leviticus 11:8]]**: The carcase not to be touched is spoken of the beasts as food; the command sits in the dietary law.
- Precept **[[Leviticus 11#^v27|Leviticus 11:27]]**: Whatsoever goeth upon his paws is unclean, which is what makes the badger an unclean beast.

**[[Exodus 29#^v29|Exodus 29:29-34]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- The holy garments of Aaron pass to his sons after him, to be anointed and consecrated in them; the son that is priest in his stead shall put them on seven days when he cometh into the tabernacle to minister.
- Aaron and his sons eat the flesh of the ram and the bread at the door of the tabernacle, the things wherewith the atonement was made; a stranger shall not eat thereof, because they are holy. And whatever of the flesh or the bread remains unto the morning is burnt with fire and not eaten: no eating the leftovers of the offering, because it is holy.

**[[Exodus 30#^v17|Exodus 30:17-21]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- Aaron and his sons washed their hands and their feet thereat when they went into the tabernacle of the congregation and when they came near to the altar to minister, that they die not; the priests could not go in unclean.

## Leviticus

**[[Leviticus 5#^v2|Leviticus 5:2-3]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 2-5 Study Notes|Leviticus 2-5]]

- Touching any unclean thing, the carcase of an unclean beast or the uncleanness of man, leaves a soul unclean and guilty. This goes into eating, not mere contact; the classes have touched on this already, and it stands.
- The touching itself pertained to the sacrifice: Israel was separated from their uncleanness that they die not when they defile the tabernacle, so a man that had touched the unclean thing could not come in to sacrifice. There is no more sacrifice, so the touching-defilement laws do not apply today; a brush of boar bristles is not sin.

**[[Leviticus 6#^v19|Leviticus 6:19-23]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 6-9 Study Notes|Leviticus 6-9]]

- This is the offering of Aaron and of his sons in the day when he is anointed: the tenth part of an ephah of fine flour for a meat offering perpetual, half in the morning and half at night, baked with oil in a pan. The meat offering is again actual flour, grain.
- Every meat offering for the priest was wholly burnt; it was not eaten. The priest's own offering went completely to the Lord.

**[[Leviticus 7#^v19|Leviticus 7:19-21]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 6-9 Study Notes|Leviticus 6-9]]

- The flesh that touched any unclean thing was not eaten but burnt with fire, and the soul that ate of the peace offerings having his uncleanness upon him was cut off from his people. An unclean man coming into the tabernacle to sacrifice or a priest eating unclean was facing death before the Lord.
- All the touching of unclean and defiled things pertains to sacrifice: the point of the uncleanness laws was to separate the children of Israel from their uncleanness, that they die not when they defile the tabernacle. There is no more sacrifice, so those defilement laws do not apply today; a brother can brush his hair with a brush of boar bristles, because that law was about coming in to sacrifice.

**[[Leviticus 10#^v8|Leviticus 10:8-11]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 10-13 Study Notes|Leviticus 10-13]]

**[[Leviticus 11#^v1|Leviticus 11:1-3]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 10-13 Study Notes|Leviticus 10-13]]

- [[Leviticus 11|Leviticus 11]] tells you what you can and cannot eat. Among all the beasts of the earth, ye shall eat whatsoever parteth the hoof, and is clovenfooted, and cheweth the cud.
- Chewing the cud means the animal has more than one stomach: it chews its food, brings it back up into its mouth to be chewed again, and then digests it through another stomach.

**[[Leviticus 11#^v4|Leviticus 11:4-8]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 10-13 Study Notes|Leviticus 10-13]]

- Both marks must be present. There are animals that chew the cud and animals that divide the hoof but do not have both; those are unclean. The camel cheweth the cud but divideth not the hoof: you cannot eat camel.
- The coney is the pika, a small mountain dwelling mammal that resembles its close relative the rabbit, found at heights among the highest of any mammal. The scriptures already had it right: the rocks are a refuge for the conies, and the conies, a feeble folk, make their houses in the rocks. You cannot eat coney, and the hare likewise is unclean.
- The swine has only the one mark: he divideth the hoof and is clovenfooted, yet he cheweth not the cud; he is unclean unto you. You cannot eat pork; leave it alone.
- Their carcase shall ye not touch sits inside the dietary law: it goes into sacrificing the unclean thing and eating it, touching it with the mouth. It does not mean a dead animal in your path cannot be moved out of the way with the hands; the tabernacle itself had to be built by hand with badgers' skins.

**[[Leviticus 11#^v9|Leviticus 11:9-12]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 10-13 Study Notes|Leviticus 10-13]]

- Of all that are in the waters, ye shall eat whatsoever hath fins and scales; whatever has not fins and scales is an abomination. No shrimp, no crab, no lobster, no bottom dwellers.
- The Most High gave an excellent dietary law: high blood pressure and the like come from eating shellfish. He made these animals and knows what they do; if you eat what he allowed you will be fine. It is simple: if it does not have both fins and scales, leave it alone.

**[[Leviticus 11#^v13|Leviticus 11:13-19]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 10-13 Study Notes|Leviticus 10-13]]

- These are the fowls in abomination that shall not be eaten: the eagle, the ossifrage, the osprey, the vulture and the kite after his kind, every raven after his kind, the owl, the nighthawk, the cuckow, the hawk, the little owl, the cormorant, the great owl, the swan, the pelican, the gier eagle, the stork, the heron after her kind, the lapwing, and the bat.
- On duck: the swan's closest relatives include the geese and ducks. The swan is unclean, and duck falls in the family of the swan, so you cannot eat duck; they are related, and it is the same thing.

**[[Leviticus 11#^v20|Leviticus 11:20-23]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 10-13 Study Notes|Leviticus 10-13]]

- All fowls that creep, going upon all four, are an abomination, yet those which have legs above their feet to leap withal upon the earth may be eaten: the locust, the bald locust, the beetle, and the grasshopper after his kind. You can eat locusts; John the Baptist ate locusts.

An earthen vessel it falls into is broken and thrown out, and ovens and ranges for pots are broken down.

**[[Leviticus 11#^v39|Leviticus 11:39-43]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 10-13 Study Notes|Leviticus 10-13]]

## Numbers

**[[Numbers 4#^v7|Numbers 4:7-14]]**  taught in [[Numbers 3-6 Study Notes|Numbers 3-6]]

- Badger skin is something we would consider unclean today, yet the Lord had his holy things covered in it. Under the law of sacrifice, defilement came by touching unclean things to eat them; touching them to make something for the tabernacle still left you unclean until sundown, unable to sacrifice, but the skins themselves served the sanctuary.

**[[Numbers 6#^v6|Numbers 6:6-8]]**  taught in [[Numbers 3-6 Study Notes|Numbers 3-6]]

- All the days of his separation he came at no dead body, and made not himself unclean even for his father, his mother, his brother, or his sister when they died, because the consecration of his God was upon his head. It was a heavy thing to fall under the Nazarite vow; you had to separate yourself from everybody, even family, so you would not be defiled.

**[[Numbers 9#^v9|Numbers 9:9-12]]**  taught in [[Numbers 7-10 Study Notes|Numbers 7-10]]

- The answer came: if any man be unclean by reason of a dead body, or be in a journey afar off, he shall still keep the passover unto the Lord, on the fourteenth day of the second month at even, eating it with unleavened bread and bitter herbs.

**[[Numbers 18#^v15|Numbers 18:15-17]]**  taught in [[Numbers 15-18 Study Notes|Numbers 15-18]]

**[[Numbers 19#^v10|Numbers 19:10]]**  taught in [[Numbers 19-22 Study Notes|Numbers 19-22]]

**[[Numbers 19#^v11|Numbers 19:11]]**  taught in [[Numbers 19-22 Study Notes|Numbers 19-22]]

- Any man that touches the dead body of any man is unclean seven days. There is history on this law: in [[Numbers 9|Numbers 9]] certain men were defiled by a dead body around the passover and could not keep it on that day because of this very statute.
- The Most High answered through Moses with a second keeping: the man unclean by reason of a dead body, or far off on a journey, keeps the passover the fourteenth day of the second month at even, eating it with unleavened bread and bitter herbs.
- Precept **[[Numbers 9#^v10|Numbers 9:10-11]]**: The unclean by a dead body or far off on a journey keep the passover the fourteenth day of the second month with unleavened bread and bitter herbs.

**[[Numbers 19#^v12|Numbers 19:12-13]]**  taught in [[Numbers 19-22 Study Notes|Numbers 19-22]]

- The unclean man purifies himself with the water of separation on the third day and on the seventh day, and then he is clean; if he purifies not himself the third day, then the seventh day he shall not be clean.

**[[Numbers 19#^v14|Numbers 19:14-22]]**  taught in [[Numbers 19-22 Study Notes|Numbers 19-22]]

- This is the law when a man dies in a tent: all that come into the tent and all that is in the tent are unclean seven days, and every open vessel with no covering bound upon it is unclean.
- For the unclean person they take of the ashes of the burnt heifer of purification for sin, with running water in a vessel; a clean person dips hyssop in the water and sprinkles the tent, the vessels, and the persons on the third day and on the seventh day, and on the seventh day he washes his clothes, bathes in water, and is clean at even.
- The man that stays unclean and will not purify himself is cut off from among the congregation, because he has defiled the sanctuary of the Lord.
- Chapter 19 as a whole gives the water of separation, which is the purification for sin: the first half gives the specifics of the sacrifice and what the priest must do, and the rest gives the examples of how a man becomes unclean, whether by sin, by touching the dead, or by being around the dead.

**[[Numbers 30#^v1|Numbers 30:1-2]]**  taught in [[Numbers 27-30 Study Notes|Numbers 27-30]]

- A vow is promising the Lord you will do a thing. Say a man vows, Lord, if you bring this to pass I will never eat swine again; he must keep that vow, and he cannot go back on it and simply ask forgiveness afterward.

**[[Numbers 31#^v19|Numbers 31:19-24]]**  taught in [[Numbers 31-34 Study Notes|Numbers 31-34]]

- Whoever killed any person or touched any slain had to abide without the camp seven days and purify himself and his captives on the third day and on the seventh day. That is the law going back to [[Numbers 5#^v2|Numbers 5:2]], putting the defiled outside the camp.
- Purifying the raiment, all that is made of skins, all work of goats' hair, and all things made of wood goes back to the law of purification in [[Numbers 19|Numbers 19]].
- Eleazar the priest gave the ordinance: gold, silver, brass, iron, tin, and lead, everything that may abide the fire, goes through the fire and is purified with the water of separation; whatever abides not the fire goes through the water; and on the seventh day they wash their clothes, are clean, and come into the camp.
- Precept **[[Numbers 19#^v11|Numbers 19:11-12]]**: He that touches the dead purifies himself on the third day and on the seventh day; the men of war purified themselves by that law.

## Deuteronomy

**[[Deuteronomy 4#^v14|Deuteronomy 4:14-16]]**  taught in [[Deuteronomy 3-6 Study Notes|Deuteronomy 3-6]]

- No similitude means he spoke plainly and clearly, not in dark speeches: when the commandments were given there was no arguing over what they meant. Fringes on the borders of the garments, no swine, thou shalt not kill; it was simple and easy to understand.

**[[Deuteronomy 12#^v15|Deuteronomy 12:15]]**  taught in [[Deuteronomy 11-14 Study Notes|Deuteronomy 11-14]]

- Precept **[[Deuteronomy 15#^v22|Deuteronomy 15:22]]**: The unclean and the clean person shall eat it alike, as the roebuck and as the hart; the unclean and clean here are persons.

**[[Deuteronomy 14#^v3|Deuteronomy 14:3]]**  taught in [[Deuteronomy 11-14 Study Notes|Deuteronomy 11-14]]

- The chapter then lists the clean beasts, the beasts that part the hoof and chew the cud, the fish with fins and scales, and the clean fowls; the rest of chapter 14 is clear cut on the laws of the clean and the unclean foods.
- Precept **[[Deuteronomy 12#^v15|Deuteronomy 12:15]]**: Whatsoever thy soul lusteth after, as of the roebuck and as of the hart: the lusting of the soul is bounded by the clean beasts of this chapter.

**[[Deuteronomy 15#^v19|Deuteronomy 15:19-23]]**  taught in [[Deuteronomy 15-18 Study Notes|Deuteronomy 15-18]]

- If there be any blemish therein, as if it be lame or blind, thou shalt not sacrifice it unto the Lord thy God; thou shalt eat it within thy gates, the unclean and the clean person alike, as the roebuck and as the hart.

**[[Deuteronomy 23#^v10|Deuteronomy 23:10-11]]**  taught in [[Deuteronomy 23-26 Study Notes|Deuteronomy 23-26]]

- Any man that was not clean by reason of uncleanness that chanceth him by night went abroad out of the camp; when evening came he washed himself with water, and when the sun was down he came into the camp again.
- Precept **[[Leviticus 15#^v16|Leviticus 15:16]]**: The law of the uncleanness that chanceth a man by night: he shall wash all his flesh in water and be unclean until the even.

**[[Deuteronomy 23#^v12|Deuteronomy 23:12-14]]**  taught in [[Deuteronomy 23-26 Study Notes|Deuteronomy 23-26]]

- Verse 14 explains why: the Lord thy God walketh in the midst of thy camp, to deliver thee and to give up thine enemies before thee; therefore shall thy camp be holy, that he see no unclean thing in thee and turn away from thee.
- That is what we just read happened with Achan the son of Carmi: he brought an unclean thing into the camp, and the Most High turned from Israel in battle.
- God says he is in the midst of Israel and none else; the Lord would go before us and fight our battles, and that is why he wanted no unclean thing in the camp, for we are holy and the camp was to be holy.

**[[Deuteronomy 28#^v18|Deuteronomy 28:18]]**  taught in [[Deuteronomy 27-30 Study Notes|Deuteronomy 27-30]]

- Cursed shall be the fruit of thy land, thy kine, and thy sheep: the food we eat is cursed. Fast food fills our neighborhoods, and where there is no fast food there is a corner store selling unclean meat that causes diseases in us.
