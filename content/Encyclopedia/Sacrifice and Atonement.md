# Sacrifice and Atonement

The offerings and what each one means: burnt, sin, trespass, peace, and the day of atonement.

## Genesis

**[[Genesis 3#^v21|Genesis 3:21]]**  taught in [[Genesis 1-4 Study Notes|Genesis 1-4]]

- This is animal sacrifice to cover the sins. This is where the Most High ordained the sacrificial law; this is when they started to sacrifice.
- The first sacrifice is twofold. A literal lamb was slain here to clothe Adam and his wife, and that lamb slain from the foundation of the world represents Christ, slain for our sins.
- Precept **[[Leviticus 16#^v27|Leviticus 16:27]]**: The sin offering yields skins, as the Most High made coats of skins to cover sin.

**[[Genesis 4#^v3|Genesis 4:3-5]]**  taught in [[Genesis 1-4 Study Notes|Genesis 1-4]]

- The Most High had respect to Abel's offering and not to Cain's, and Cain was wroth.

**[[Genesis 4#^v7|Genesis 4:7]]**  taught in [[Genesis 1-4 Study Notes|Genesis 1-4]]

- The Most High is calling Cain to repent. Cain knew that the Most High required animal sacrifice, because the sacrificial law was ordained at [[Genesis 3#^v21|Genesis 3:21]].

**[[Genesis 8#^v20|Genesis 8:20-22]]**  taught in [[Genesis 5-8 Study Notes|Genesis 5-8]]

- Noah built an altar and offered burnt offerings of every clean beast and every clean fowl, and the Most High smelled a sweet savour and said He would not again curse the ground any more for man's sake.

**[[Genesis 9#^v5|Genesis 9:5]]**  taught in [[Genesis 9-12 Study Notes|Genesis 9-12]]

- The Most High requiring blood at the hand of every beast is animal sacrifice, still in place with Noah, passed down generation to generation.
- That is the scapegoat: it takes the wrongdoings, mistakes, and faults of others and places them on the animal. That is why Cain and Abel were bringing offerings, because they knew of the sacrifice from [[Genesis 3|Genesis 3]].
- Precept **[[Genesis 3#^v21|Genesis 3:21]]**: The coats of skins were the first sacrifice; the beast stood in place of Adam and Eve.
- Precept **[[Genesis 4#^v3|Genesis 4:3-5]]**: Cain and Abel brought offerings, proving the sacrifice was known and passed down.
- Precept **[[Leviticus 16#^v8|Leviticus 16:8-10]]**: The scapegoat is presented alive to make an atonement, standing in for the faults of others.

**[[Genesis 15#^v9|Genesis 15:9-12]]**  taught in [[Genesis 13-16 Study Notes|Genesis 13-16]]

- Abram divided the heifer, the goat, and the ram, and drove the fowls from the carcases; he was offering sacrifice.

**[[Genesis 20#^v9|Genesis 20:9-13]]**  taught in [[Genesis 17-20 Study Notes|Genesis 17-20]]

- Abimelech called it what it is: a great sin. Adultery is one of the weightier matters; there is no animal sacrifice that atones for it.

**[[Genesis 22#^v1|Genesis 22:1-3]]**  taught in [[Genesis 21-24 Study Notes|Genesis 21-24]]

- God did tempt Abraham: Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac. Ishmael was alive, but the Most High counted Isaac the only son, because Ishmael was born of the flesh, of a plan the Lord never spoke, while Isaac was the child of the promise.
- Abraham rose up early in the morning and went. No hesitation, no debating with the word; that is obedience.

**[[Genesis 22#^v7|Genesis 22:7-8]]**  taught in [[Genesis 21-24 Study Notes|Genesis 21-24]]

- Isaac asked, Where is the lamb for a burnt offering? Abraham answered, My son, God will provide himself a lamb. He trusted the Most High with what was missing.

**[[Genesis 22#^v13|Genesis 22:13-14]]**  taught in [[Genesis 21-24 Study Notes|Genesis 21-24]]

- Once the obedience was fulfilled, God provided the sacrifice: a ram caught in a thicket by his horns, offered in the stead of his son, just as Abraham had told Isaac. Abraham called the place Jehovahjireh: in the mount of the Lord it shall be seen.

## Exodus

**[[Exodus 3#^v16|Exodus 3:16-18]]**  taught in [[Exodus 2-5 Study Notes|Exodus 2-5]]

- They shall hearken to thy voice; and thou and the elders shall say unto the king of Egypt, The LORD God of the Hebrews hath met with us: let us go, we beseech thee, three days' journey into the wilderness, that we may sacrifice to the LORD our God.

**[[Exodus 5#^v3|Exodus 5:3]]**  taught in [[Exodus 2-5 Study Notes|Exodus 2-5]]

- They said, The God of the Hebrews hath met with us: let us go, we pray thee, three days' journey into the desert, and sacrifice unto the LORD our God; lest he fall upon us with pestilence, or with the sword.

**[[Exodus 5#^v6|Exodus 5:6-9]]**  taught in [[Exodus 2-5 Study Notes|Exodus 2-5]]

- Pharaoh commanded the taskmasters the same day: ye shall no more give the people straw to make brick; let them go and gather straw for themselves; and the tale of the bricks ye shall lay upon them, ye shall not diminish ought thereof. For they be idle; therefore they cry, saying, Let us go and sacrifice to our God.

**[[Exodus 5#^v15|Exodus 5:15-19]]**  taught in [[Exodus 2-5 Study Notes|Exodus 2-5]]

- Pharaoh answered, Ye are idle, ye are idle: therefore ye say, Let us go and do sacrifice to the LORD. The charge of idleness is the standing answer to any who would go serve God, whether it is the three days' journey or keeping the Sabbath and the new moons now.

**[[Exodus 8#^v5|Exodus 8:5-8]]**  taught in [[Exodus 6-9 Study Notes|Exodus 6-9]]

- Aaron stretched out his hand over the waters, and the frogs came up and covered the land of Egypt. With this second plague the Most High was mocking Heqet, the Egyptian idol god of the frogs.
- The magicians did so with their enchantments and brought up frogs, but just as with the water they could only add to the plague; they could not take the frogs away.
- So Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron and said, Entreat the Lord that he may take away the frogs. Why did Pharaoh not call on Heqet, the god of frogs? He had to go to the Lord.

**[[Exodus 8#^v25|Exodus 8:25-32]]**  taught in [[Exodus 6-9 Study Notes|Exodus 6-9]]

- Pharaoh said, Go sacrifice to your God in the land; but Moses answered, It is not meet, for we should sacrifice the abomination of the Egyptians before their eyes, and will they not stone us? Israel's sacrifices were the very animals Egypt held sacred, so Israel must go three days' journey into the wilderness.

**[[Exodus 10#^v24|Exodus 10:24-26]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- After three days of darkness Pharaoh called for Moses: go, serve the Lord, only let your flocks and your herds be stayed. Go, but leave all your possessions in Egypt.
- Moses refused: our cattle also shall go with us, there shall not an hoof be left behind. Not a hoof, a hair, or a heifer would stay in Egypt, for thereof must Israel take to serve the Lord, and we know not with what we must serve the Lord until we come thither.

**[[Exodus 12#^v3|Exodus 12:3-5]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- The lamb was to be without blemish, a male of the first year, taken from the sheep or from the goats. This is a foreshadowing: the feast days and sacrifices are a shadow of things to come, but the body is of Christ.
- The lamb without blemish and without spot points to Christ, who was sinless, verily foreordained before the foundation of the world but manifest in these last times for the nation of Israel.
- Precept **[[1 Peter 1#^v19|1 Peter 1:19-20]]**: The precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot, foreordained before the foundation of the world.

**[[Exodus 12#^v24|Exodus 12:24-28]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- This thing is an ordinance to thee and to thy sons for ever, kept even when Israel comes into the land the Lord promised. When the children ask, What mean ye by this service? the answer is, It is the sacrifice of the Lord's passover, who passed over the houses of the children of Israel in Egypt when he smote the Egyptians and delivered our houses.

**[[Exodus 13#^v14|Exodus 13:14-16]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- When thy son asketh thee in time to come, saying, What is this? thou shalt say, By strength of hand the Lord brought us out from Egypt, from the house of bondage: when Pharaoh would hardly let us go, the Lord slew all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, therefore the firstborn are sacrificed or redeemed.
- The children must be taught the righteousness of why the feast is kept. Ask the children of this land why they keep Christmas and they answer that Santa Claus is coming down the chimney; Israel's children are to be taught the works of the Most High instead.
- It is for a token upon thine hand and for frontlets between thine eyes, for by strength of hand the Lord brought us forth out of Egypt.

**[[Exodus 16#^v22|Exodus 16:22-30]]**  taught in [[Exodus 14-17 Study Notes|Exodus 14-17]]

- For the brothers and sisters far from a congregation: you may have to travel and you may have to be the firstfruits in your area. The first assemblies met in houses, and that is how camps start; a man will drive hours to a club, so drive for the truth, and the Lord honors that faith.
- Precept **[[Romans 16#^v3|Romans 16:3-5]]**: Greet the church that is in their house, and Epaenetus the firstfruits of Achaia; congregations begin with the diligent few.
- Precept **[[Matthew 6#^v33|Matthew 6:33]]**: Seek ye first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things shall be added; the Lord sees the sacrifice it takes to get to the congregation.

**[[Exodus 18#^v9|Exodus 18:9-11]]**  taught in [[Exodus 18-21 Study Notes|Exodus 18-21]]

- Naaman the Syrian, healed of his leprosy in Jordan, confessed that there is no God in all the earth but in Israel. His faith was so strong he vowed to offer neither burnt offering nor sacrifice unto other gods, and asked pardon for even bowing in the house of Rimmon with his master.
- Precept **[[Psalms 96#^v5|Psalms 96:5-8]]**: All the gods of the nations are idols, but the Lord made the heavens; give unto the Lord the glory due unto his name.
- Precept **[[2 Kings 5#^v14|2 Kings 5:14-18]]**: Naaman dipped seven times in Jordan and was clean, and confessed there is no God in all the earth but in Israel; a heathen vowed to sacrifice to no other god but the Lord.

**[[Exodus 23#^v14|Exodus 23:14-17]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- The feast of harvest, the firstfruits of thy labours, is also known as Pentecost and the feast of weeks; and the feast of ingathering, in the end of the year, is also known as the feast of tabernacles and the feast of booths.

**[[Exodus 23#^v18|Exodus 23:18-19]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- Thou shalt not offer the blood of my sacrifice with leavened bread, neither shall the fat of my sacrifice remain until the morning. The first of the firstfruits of thy land thou shalt bring into the house of the Lord thy God.

**[[Exodus 24#^v4|Exodus 24:4-8]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- Moses wrote all the words of the Lord, rose up early, and builded an altar under the hill with twelve pillars according to the twelve tribes of Israel; young men offered burnt offerings and peace offerings of oxen.
- Moses took half of the blood in basons and half he sprinkled on the altar; then he took the book of the covenant and read in the audience of the people, and they said, All that the Lord hath said will we do, and be obedient. And he sprinkled the blood on the people: Behold the blood of the covenant, which the Lord hath made with you concerning all these words. This sprinkling ties in later to the book of Hebrews and its teaching on sacrifice.

**[[Exodus 25#^v1|Exodus 25:1-2]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- Speak unto the children of Israel, that they bring me an offering: of every man that giveth it willingly with his heart ye shall take my offering. The Lord wants a willing heart in giving, not grudgingly or of necessity or only because the law says so, but because you know your giving benefits someone in need or the house of God, whatever it needs, the lights, the roof, the floor, the tables, the leadership's travel.

**[[Exodus 25#^v3|Exodus 25:3-7]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- The offering was gold, silver, brass, blue, purple, and scarlet, fine linen, goats' hair, rams' skins dyed red, badgers' skins, shittim wood, oil for the light, spices, and onyx stones for the ephod and the breastplate. Thank the Most High he requires no gold and silver now, for half of us would have nothing to give; under the new covenant the offering is the body presented a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable.
- The badgers' skins settle a confusion: the Lord commanded Israel to handle animal skins and hair to build his own tabernacle. So the laws about not touching that which is defiled, the carcass of the unclean beast, go into the matter of sacrifice and coming into the temple, not into everyday handling. A boar-bristle brush is not sin; brush your hair, brothers.
- Precept **[[Romans 12#^v1|Romans 12:1]]**: Present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God; that is the offering of him that has no gold nor silver.

**[[Exodus 27#^v1|Exodus 27:1-8]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- Verses one through eight go into the altar in which the sacrifices were done in the tabernacle: the altar of shittim wood foursquare, its horns of the same, overlaid with brass, with its pans, shovels, basons, fleshhooks, and firepans, the grate of network of brass, and the staves to bear it.

**[[Exodus 29#^v1|Exodus 29:1-4]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- To hallow Aaron and his sons to minister in the priest's office: one young bullock and two rams without blemish, with unleavened bread, cakes tempered with oil, and wafers anointed with oil, brought in one basket to the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

**[[Exodus 29#^v5|Exodus 29:5-9]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- Ecclesiasticus records the same consecration: Moses anointed him with holy oil, an everlasting covenant to Aaron and to his seed so long as the heavens should remain, to minister unto Him, execute the office of the priesthood, offer the sacrifices, and teach Jacob the testimonies and inform Israel in His laws. The priest's job was the offerings and teaching the nation the law, statutes, and commandments.
- What happened to that covenant is answered in Malachi, where the commandment is spoken to the priests: the covenant with Levi was of life and peace, the priest's lips should keep knowledge and they should seek the law at his mouth, for he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts; but ye are departed out of the way, ye have caused many to stumble at the law, ye have corrupted the covenant of Levi. The priests were finessing the offerings, keeping the best animals for themselves and sacrificing the blind and the broken, and they perverted the teachings of the law for their own benefit.
- Precept **[[Ecclesiasticus 45#^v15|Ecclesiasticus 45:15-17]]**: The everlasting covenant of the priesthood with Aaron and his seed: to offer the sacrifices and to teach Jacob the testimonies and inform Israel in His laws.

**[[Exodus 29#^v10|Exodus 29:10-14]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- The bullock is killed before the Lord by the door of the tabernacle, its blood put upon the horns of the altar with the finger, the fat burnt upon the altar, and the flesh, skin, and dung burnt with fire without the camp: it is a sin offering. This is the introduction to the categories of offerings; there is the sin offering.

**[[Exodus 29#^v15|Exodus 29:15-18]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- The first ram is slain, its blood sprinkled round about upon the altar, and the whole ram burnt upon the altar: it is a burnt offering unto the Lord, a sweet savour, an offering made by fire. There is the second category, the burnt offering.

**[[Exodus 29#^v19|Exodus 29:19-28]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- The bread and the parts of the ram are put in the hands of Aaron and his sons and waved for a wave offering before the Lord, then burnt upon the altar; and the breast of the wave offering and the shoulder of the heave offering are sanctified for Aaron and his sons by a statute for ever. There go the wave offering and the heave offering, which you will read about throughout the first five books, Leviticus predominantly.

**[[Exodus 29#^v29|Exodus 29:29-34]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- Aaron and his sons eat the flesh of the ram and the bread at the door of the tabernacle, the things wherewith the atonement was made; a stranger shall not eat thereof, because they are holy. And whatever of the flesh or the bread remains unto the morning is burnt with fire and not eaten: no eating the leftovers of the offering, because it is holy.

**[[Exodus 29#^v35|Exodus 29:35-42]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- Seven days shalt thou consecrate them, offering every day a bullock for a sin offering for atonement, cleansing the altar and anointing it to sanctify it, that it be an altar most holy.
- Then the continual offering: two lambs of the first year day by day continually, one lamb in the morning and the other at even, with the tenth deal of flour mingled with beaten oil and the drink offering of wine; a continual burnt offering throughout your generations at the door of the tabernacle, where the Lord meets and speaks with Israel.

**[[Exodus 29#^v43|Exodus 29:43-46]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- The tabernacle shall be sanctified by His glory, and Aaron and his sons sanctified to minister in the priest's office; for the Most High to enter and deal with Israel, everything had to be sanctified. That was serious.
- I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God. He was in no other nation's tabernacle, temple, or synagogue, and though there is no tabernacle today it is still the same case: He is in the midst of Israel and none else, which excludes everybody else from the benefits of having the Most High as their God. There is nothing to be ashamed of in the fact that He chose Israel only, and nothing changed across testaments: blessed be the Lord God of Israel, for he hath visited and redeemed his people, and that is the purpose of Christ, to redeem His people.
- The other nations knew it. When the adversaries of Judah and Benjamin heard the temple was being rebuilt they came saying, Let us build with you, for we seek your God; and Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the chief of the fathers answered, Ye have nothing to do with us to build an house unto our God, but we ourselves together will build unto the Lord God of Israel. That is the mindset for these last days: Israel does not need the other nations' assistance, but rebuilds one another spiritually as a nation while awaiting Christ's return to restore the kingdom.
- He brought them forth out of the land of Egypt that He may dwell among them: I am the Lord their God. Israel should walk confident, special, and chosen above all nations of people, for the Most High is faithful and belongs to no one else; repent from spiritual fornication and return to the Most High.
- Precept **[[Ezra 4#^v1|Ezra 4:1-3]]**: The adversaries said, Let us build with you, for we seek your God; the fathers answered, Ye have nothing to do with us, but we ourselves together will build unto the Lord God of Israel.

**[[Exodus 30#^v6|Exodus 30:6-9]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- No strange incense went on it, and no burnt sacrifice, meat offering, or drink offering; this altar was for one specific incense only. The service had to be exactly as prescribed, and deviation carried death, which is a reason to thank the Lord for Christ.

**[[Exodus 30#^v10|Exodus 30:10]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- Aaron made an atonement upon the horns of it once in a year with the blood of the sin offering of atonements; it is most holy unto the Lord. This altar served the day of atonement.
- On the tenth day of the seventh month Israel afflicted their souls and did no work at all, and the priest made an atonement for the sanctuary, the tabernacle, the altar, the priests, and all the people of the congregation, to cleanse them from all their sins before the Lord; an everlasting statute, once a year.
- The priests went always into the first tabernacle accomplishing the service of God, but into the second went the high priest alone once every year, not without blood. Christ offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, so the day of atonement is still kept by afflicting the soul and doing no work, but without the animal sacrifice, because Christ took the place of the animal.
- Precept **[[Leviticus 16#^v29|Leviticus 16:29-34]]**: On the tenth day of the seventh month ye shall afflict your souls, and the priest makes an atonement to cleanse the children of Israel from all their sins, an everlasting statute.
- Precept **[[Hebrews 10#^v10|Hebrews 10:10-12]]**: The daily sacrifices can never take away sins, but this man offered one sacrifice for sins for ever and sat down on the right hand of God.

**[[Exodus 30#^v11|Exodus 30:11-16]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- The ransom was half a shekel after the shekel of the sanctuary, from every one that passed among them that were numbered, from twenty years old and above; the rich gave no more and the poor gave no less, to make an atonement for their souls.
- The atonement money was appointed for the service of the tabernacle of the congregation. The gold, the wood, the curtains, and the fabrics all had to be bought and kept up, and the tabernacle moved with the nation through the wilderness; that is the same reason offerings are gathered in the congregation today, because the house of the Most High cannot be built or kept without it.

**[[Exodus 30#^v17|Exodus 30:17-21]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- The laver of brass, with his foot also of brass, stood between the tabernacle of the congregation and the altar, with water in it; a great brass bowl set on a brass base.
- Aaron and his sons washed their hands and their feet thereat when they went into the tabernacle of the congregation and when they came near to the altar to minister, that they die not; the priests could not go in unclean.
- It was a statute for ever to Aaron and to his seed throughout their generations.

**[[Exodus 30#^v22|Exodus 30:22-33]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- With it were anointed the tabernacle of the congregation, the ark of the testimony, the table and all his vessels, the candlestick and his vessels, the altar of incense, the altar of burnt offering with all his vessels, and the laver and his foot, that they be most holy; whatsoever toucheth them shall be holy. Aaron and his sons were anointed and consecrated with it to minister in the priest's office.

**[[Exodus 31#^v7|Exodus 31:7-11]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- The wise hearted made all that was commanded: the tabernacle of the congregation, the ark of the testimony, the mercy seat that is thereupon, all the furniture of the tabernacle, the table and his furniture, the pure candlestick with all his furniture, the altar of incense, the altar of burnt offering with all his furniture, and the laver and his foot.

**[[Exodus 32#^v1|Exodus 32:1-6]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- Aaron built an altar before it and made proclamation, To morrow is a feast to the LORD; and the people rose up early, offered burnt offerings and peace offerings, sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play. Israel kept provoking God, and the Lord still brought the nation into the promised land as he promised Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

**[[Exodus 32#^v7|Exodus 32:7-10]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- The Lord told Moses, Go, get thee down; for thy people, which thou broughtest out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. They turned aside quickly out of the way, made a molten calf, worshipped it, and sacrificed thereunto. Not forty days gone, and they had already left his commandments.
- The spirit that was in Israel in Egypt was still in Israel when they left. It is the same coming into this truth: the evil carried in from the world will corrupt you and turn you from the Lord if it is not put off.
- I have seen this people, and, behold, it is a stiffnecked people. That has been Israel's reputation with the Father from the beginning; without the laws of God we are a carnal, rebellious people, which is why these last days call for humbleness and repentance, not bringing the world's rhetoric into the congregation.
- Now therefore let me alone, that my wrath may wax hot against them, and that I may consume them: and I will make of thee a great nation. Moses was of the tribe of Levi, of the seed of Jacob, so the Lord could have started over with him.
- Precept **[[Ecclesiastes 5#^v1|Ecclesiastes 5:1]]**: Keep thy foot when thou goest to the house of God; the evil still in a man from the world will corrupt him as it did our forefathers.

**[[Exodus 32#^v30|Exodus 32:30-35]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- Moses said unto the people, Ye have sinned a great sin. They had broken the first two commandments just given at Sinai: no other gods before him, and no graven image to bow down to and serve.
- Moses returned unto the LORD to mediate: yet now, if thou wilt forgive their sin; and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book which thou hast written. That is a real leader, putting himself in the way of harm for his people.
- The Lord answered, Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book; therefore now go, lead the people, and mine Angel shall go before thee; nevertheless in the day when I visit I will visit their sin upon them. And the LORD plagued the people, because they made the calf, which Aaron made.

**[[Exodus 34#^v11|Exodus 34:11-16]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- The snare is spelled out: they go a whoring after their gods, they call thee, thou eat of their sacrifice and partake in serving their gods; thou take of their daughters unto thy sons, and their daughters go a whoring after their gods and make thy sons go a whoring after their gods.

**[[Exodus 34#^v22|Exodus 34:22-24]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- Thrice in the year shall all your men children appear before the Lord GOD, the God of Israel. The law is written again in Deuteronomy: the feast of unleavened bread, which is the Passover season, the feast of weeks, which is Pentecost, and the feast of tabernacles; and they shall not appear before the Lord empty, for each had to bring his sacrifice.

**[[Exodus 34#^v25|Exodus 34:25-26]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- Thou shalt not offer the blood of my sacrifice with leaven; neither shall the sacrifice of the feast of the passover be left unto the morning: it had to be eaten up or burned.
- The first of the firstfruits of thy land thou shalt bring unto the house of the LORD thy God. And thou shalt not seethe a kid in his mother's milk: a young one still in his mother's milk, as veal is taken for instance, is not ready to be eaten; it is unlawful.

**[[Exodus 34#^v27|Exodus 34:27-28]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- Moses was there with the Lord forty days and forty nights; he did neither eat bread, nor drink water. And he wrote upon the tables the words of the covenant, the ten commandments; the words of the covenant take in the directions of the sacrifices, which animals were to be offered and how it was to be done, together with the commandments.

**[[Exodus 35#^v4|Exodus 35:4-9]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- Whosoever is of a willing heart, let him bring the Lord's offering: gold, silver, and brass; blue, purple, scarlet, fine linen, and goats' hair; rams' skins dyed red and badgers' skins; shittim wood; oil for the light and spices for the anointing oil and the sweet incense; and onyx stones and stones to be set for the ephod and the breastplate. These are the offerings the people would bring unto the Lord.

**[[Exodus 35#^v10|Exodus 35:10-19]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

**[[Leviticus 1#^v1|Leviticus 1:1-3]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 1 Study Notes|Leviticus 1]]

**[[Leviticus 2#^v1|Leviticus 2:1-3]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 2-5 Study Notes|Leviticus 2-5]]

- The meat offering is of fine flour, with oil poured upon it and frankincense put thereon; the priest burns his handful of it as a memorial upon the altar, an offering made by fire of a sweet savour unto the Lord, and the remnant belongs to Aaron and his sons.
- Five primary offerings run through these chapters. The burnt offering, usually a male burnt at the altar, with the skin for the priests, was glorified by the Most High. The meal offering, the one read here, was made of fine flour with oil and frankincense; no leaven and no honey were permitted, it had to be seasoned with salt, and it was baked in an oven, a pan, or a frying pan.
- The peace offering could be either male or female: the blood, the fat, and the kidneys were the Lord's, and the breast and the right shoulder were for the priest. The sin offering was blood and fat on the altar, with the bullock burned without the camp, for the judgment of sin. The trespass offering was for atonement and forgiveness, the blood and fat of a ram on the altar, with the principal restored and the fifth part added. There are also wave offerings and heave offerings, but these five are the primary offerings.

It is the same spirit today, wicked all weekend and a hundred dollars in the plate on Sunday; the Most High does not want your money, he wants you to repent. The law of sacrifice was always temporary, a shadow of good things to come, and the good thing that came was Christ.

**[[1 Samuel 2#^v27|1 Samuel 2:27-29]]**  taught in [[1 Samuel 1-3 Study Notes|1 Samuel 1-3]]

Did I choose him out of all the tribes of Israel to be my priest, to offer upon mine altar, to burn incense, to wear an ephod before me? and did I give unto the house of thy father all the offerings made by fire of the children of Israel? When they are of age they can go their own way; but they should not be living under your roof and breaking the commandments of God. You honour your children above God when you allow that.

**[[1 Samuel 7#^v3|1 Samuel 7:3-4]]**  taught in [[1 Samuel 4-7 Study Notes|1 Samuel 4-7]]

- Who is Ashtoreth? Astaroth in demonology is a chief figure of hell, named after the Near Eastern goddess Astarte, and Astarte is the Hellenized form of Ashtoreth, a form of Ishtar, the goddess called the queen of heaven, associated with love, beauty, sex, and war. Samuel was exhorting the people to depart from these fake gods and goddesses of the nations, and to this day there are Israelites that have not put away Ashtoreth from among them.
- Easter is at its roots the celebration of Ishtar, the goddess of fertility and sex. When Constantine set out to merge paganism with the faith of the apostles to bring everything under one empire, pagan rituals like Easter and Saturnalia were dressed up as honoring Christ, and that was the birth of Roman Catholicism. Nothing about it mathematically adds up to Christ rising, the apostles never celebrated it or taught it, and a bunny laying eggs has nothing to do with your Lord and Saviour; it is not of Christ.
- Then the children of Israel did put away Baalim and Ashtaroth, and served the Lord only. That is repentance.

That is Ishtar worship, and it is the same spirit that says, when I was in the world celebrating her holidays I was good, and now in the truth I am catching hell.
