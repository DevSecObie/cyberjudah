# The Feasts of the Most High

Passover and the appointed times: the holy convocations and how they are kept.

## Genesis

**[[Genesis 4#^v25|Genesis 4:25-26]]**  taught in [[Genesis 1-4 Study Notes|Genesis 1-4]]

- Seth was appointed another seed instead of Abel. Through Seth men began to call upon the name of the Lord; they started to worship the Most High and keep the commandments, while the line of Cain went on in evil and wickedness. That is why the Most High chose the line of Seth.

**[[Genesis 18#^v9|Genesis 18:9-15]]**  taught in [[Genesis 17-20 Study Notes|Genesis 17-20]]

- Sarah was barren from the beginning and it had ceased to be with her after the manner of women; she laughed within herself at the promise of a son.
- The Lord answered the thought she never spoke: Wherefore did Sarah laugh? She denied it out of fear, and He said, Nay, but thou didst laugh. The Most High sees what is within the mind.
- Is any thing too hard for the Lord? The barren woman is made a joyful mother of children. Through faith Sarah received strength to conceive when she was past age, and Abraham, as good as dead at a hundred, staggered not at the promise but was strong in faith, fully persuaded that what He had promised He was able to perform.

**[[Genesis 19#^v1|Genesis 19:1-3]]**  taught in [[Genesis 17-20 Study Notes|Genesis 17-20]]

- Lot sat in the gate and pressed the two angels into his house, because he knew the men of Sodom. He made them a feast, and they did eat; angels have bodies and functions like men.

**[[Genesis 24#^v12|Genesis 24:12-16]]**  taught in [[Genesis 21-24 Study Notes|Genesis 21-24]]

- The servant prayed for a sign at the well: the damsel who offers to draw for the camels also is the one appointed. Before he had done speaking, Rebekah came out.

**[[Genesis 24#^v12|Genesis 24:12-20]]**  taught in [[Genesis 24-27 Study Notes|Genesis 24-27]]

- The servant asked a sign, and before he had done speaking Rebekah came out: very fair, a virgin, and kind. She gave a stranger drink and drew for all his camels; that kind and easily entreated spirit is the pattern for the daughters of Israel.

**[[Genesis 25#^v20|Genesis 25:20-21]]**  taught in [[Genesis 24-27 Study Notes|Genesis 24-27]]

- Isaac was forty years old when he took Rebekah to wife. Brothers waiting on a wife, be patient and stay virtuous; Isaac waited forty years, and the Most High had the right sister appointed.

## Exodus

**[[Exodus 5#^v1|Exodus 5:1-2]]**  taught in [[Exodus 2-5 Study Notes|Exodus 2-5]]

- Moses and Aaron went in and told Pharaoh, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto me in the wilderness. That feast was the Passover.

**[[Exodus 9#^v1|Exodus 9:1-7]]**  taught in [[Exodus 6-9 Study Notes|Exodus 6-9]]

- A grievous murrain fell upon the cattle of Egypt, upon the horses, asses, camels, oxen, and sheep; and with this fifth plague the Most High was mocking Hathor, the Egyptian idol goddess of cattle.
- The Lord severed between the cattle of Israel and the cattle of Egypt: all the cattle of Egypt died, but of the cattle of the children of Israel died not one. Pharaoh sent, and behold, there was not one of the cattle of the Israelites dead; and his heart was hardened.
- Picture what was taking place in the land: flies all over Egypt, but where Israel dwelt there were no flies; the water was blood, but where Israel dwelt the water was good; the cattle of Egypt died, but Israel's cattle lived. The plagues touched Pharaoh, his servants, and the Egyptians only; they did not touch the Israelites at all, because this was the Lord redeeming the Israelites.
- The Egyptians could not pray to their idols to help them, because the Lord was mocking them, showing them that Hathor does not exist, Sobek does not exist, Heqet does not exist, and Beelzebub does not exist.

**[[Exodus 10#^v2|Exodus 10:2]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- The fathers are to tell in the ears of their sons and their sons' sons what things the Most High wrought in Egypt. That is what Israel does every year at the Passover, giving honour to the things he did in Egypt and the way he delivered the people out of the hand of the oppressors.

**[[Exodus 10#^v7|Exodus 10:7-11]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- Pharaoh's own servants pleaded with him: how long shall this man be a snare unto us? Knowest thou not yet that Egypt is destroyed?
- Moses answered that all would go, young and old, sons and daughters, flocks and herds, for we must hold a feast unto the Lord. The Most High loves to see Israel gathered together in unity; that is why so many feast days bring the nation together to honour him.
- Pharaoh offered to let only the men go, and Moses and Aaron were driven out from his presence.
- Precept **[[Psalms 133#^v1|Psalm 133:1]]**: Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity; the feasts gather the nation.

**[[Exodus 10#^v27|Exodus 10:27-29]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- The same delusion falls on Israel when the people go reprobate in their sins: believing the Sabbath begins when the sun comes up, that anything can be eaten on Passover, or that the leaven to be put away is only the heart. Sin blinds.

**[[Exodus 12#^v3|Exodus 12:3-5]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- In the tenth day of the month every man was to take a lamb according to the house of his fathers; if the household was too little for the lamb, he and his neighbour next unto his house were to take it according to the number of the souls.
- The lamb was to be without blemish, a male of the first year, taken from the sheep or from the goats. This is a foreshadowing: the feast days and sacrifices are a shadow of things to come, but the body is of Christ.
- The lamb without blemish and without spot points to Christ, who was sinless, verily foreordained before the foundation of the world but manifest in these last times for the nation of Israel.
- Precept **[[Colossians 2#^v16|Colossians 2:16-17]]**: The meat, drink, holydays, and sabbaths are a shadow of things to come, but the body is of Christ; the Passover lamb was a precursor of him.

**[[Exodus 12#^v6|Exodus 12:6-8]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- The flesh was to be eaten that night, roast with fire, with unleavened bread and with bitter herbs. That is the Passover menu in layman's terms: lamb roast with fire, unleavened bread, bitter herbs; no fried chicken, no birthday cake, no mashed potatoes and gravy, none of the thirteen herbs and spices.

**[[Exodus 12#^v9|Exodus 12:9-11]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- It was to be eaten with loins girded, shoes on the feet, and staff in hand, in haste: it is the Lord's passover, and that very night Israel was leaving Egypt.

**[[Exodus 12#^v12|Exodus 12:12-13]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- The blood was a token upon the houses: when I see the blood, I will pass over you. That is where the term Passover comes from; the houses with the blood on the doorposts were passed over from death while the firstborn of Egypt were slain.

**[[Exodus 12#^v14|Exodus 12:14]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- This day is a memorial, a feast to the Lord throughout your generations, kept by an ordinance for ever. It was not done away; even in the New Testament the Passover is still kept: Christ our passover is sacrificed for us, therefore let us keep the feast. The lamb of the Passover represents Christ, and the apostle's instruction is to keep the feast, not to spiritualize it away.
- Precept **[[1 Corinthians 5#^v7|1 Corinthians 5:7-8]]**: Christ our passover is sacrificed for us; therefore let us keep the feast, with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.

**[[Exodus 12#^v15|Exodus 12:15-17]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- Seven days shall ye eat unleavened bread; even the first day the leaven is put out of the houses, and whosoever eats leavened bread from the first day until the seventh, that soul shall be cut off from Israel.
- The feast of unleavened bread is observed because in this selfsame day the Most High brought the armies of Israel out of Egypt. He deals with armies, and when he brings Israel out of this place he is bringing out an army again: when the spirit of life enters them and they stand upon their feet, great fear falls on them which see them, for they stand up an exceeding great army, not choir boys.

**[[Exodus 12#^v18|Exodus 12:18-20]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month at even, ye shall eat unleavened bread, until the one and twentieth day of the month at even. The Passover begins and ends at even because the day begins and ends at even; it is not difficult.

**[[Exodus 12#^v21|Exodus 12:21-23]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- The elders were to draw out a lamb for their families and kill the passover, take a bunch of hyssop, dip it in the blood in the bason, and strike the lintel and the two side posts; the hyssop bush was used like a paintbrush to paint the blood of the lamb over the doorposts.

**[[Exodus 12#^v24|Exodus 12:24-28]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- This thing is an ordinance to thee and to thy sons for ever, kept even when Israel comes into the land the Lord promised. When the children ask, What mean ye by this service? the answer is, It is the sacrifice of the Lord's passover, who passed over the houses of the children of Israel in Egypt when he smote the Egyptians and delivered our houses.
- The feast days commemorate the Most High delivering his people and bringing judgment on their enemies; that is the same pattern the feast of dedication commemorates. The people bowed the head and worshipped, and the children of Israel went and did as the Lord had commanded Moses and Aaron.

**[[Exodus 12#^v37|Exodus 12:37]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- The children of Israel journeyed from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand on foot that were men, beside children. Israel departed from Rameses in the first month; the exodus is tied to Rameses by name.
- Precept **[[Numbers 33#^v3|Numbers 33:3]]**: They departed from Rameses in the first month, on the fifteenth day of the first month, on the morrow after the passover.

**[[Exodus 12#^v39|Exodus 12:39-42]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- It is a night to be much observed unto the Lord for bringing them out from the land of Egypt: this is the Passover night, which all the children of Israel are to observe in their generations.

**[[Exodus 12#^v43|Exodus 12:43-51]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- This is the ordinance of the passover: no stranger eats of it. A bought servant may eat when he is circumcised; a foreigner and an hired servant shall not eat thereof. When a stranger will keep the passover of the Lord, let all his males be circumcised, and he shall be as one born in the land; one law is to the homeborn and to the stranger.
- It is eaten in one house, none of the flesh carried outside, neither shall ye break a bone thereof. That also goes to Christ, the Passover lamb, whose bones were not broken on the cross.
- Precept **[[John 19#^v36|John 19:36]]**: These things were done that the scripture should be fulfilled, A bone of him shall not be broken; Christ is the Passover lamb.

**[[Exodus 13#^v3|Exodus 13:3-4]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- This day came ye out in the month Abib: the first month of the year, falling at the first new moon of the spring.

**[[Exodus 13#^v5|Exodus 13:5-10]]**  taught in [[Exodus 10-13 Study Notes|Exodus 10-13]]

- When the Lord brings Israel into the land of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites, a land flowing with milk and honey, this service is kept in this month: seven days of unleavened bread, and in the seventh day a feast to the Lord, with no leaven seen in all thy quarters.
- Thou shalt therefore keep this ordinance in his season from year to year: the Passover is kept every year.

**[[Exodus 16#^v22|Exodus 16:22-30]]**  taught in [[Exodus 14-17 Study Notes|Exodus 14-17]]

- Abide ye every man in his place on the seventh day is about not going out to gather food on the sabbath. Some foolish Israelites read verse 29 out of context and say you never have to leave your house or join a congregation on the sabbath; that is not what it says. The sabbath is a holy convocation, a large gathering, in all your dwellings, and we are to come together on it.
- For the brothers and sisters far from a congregation: you may have to travel and you may have to be the firstfruits in your area. The first assemblies met in houses, and that is how camps start; a man will drive hours to a club, so drive for the truth, and the Lord honors that faith.
- Precept **[[Leviticus 23#^v1|Leviticus 23:1-3]]**: The seventh day is a sabbath of rest, an holy convocation in all your dwellings; Exodus 16:29 does not undo the gathering.
- Precept **[[Romans 16#^v3|Romans 16:3-5]]**: Greet the church that is in their house, and Epaenetus the firstfruits of Achaia; congregations begin with the diligent few.

**[[Exodus 19#^v1|Exodus 19:1-2]]**  taught in [[Exodus 18-21 Study Notes|Exodus 18-21]]

- In the third month after going forth out of Egypt, Israel departed from Rephidim, came into the wilderness of Sinai, and camped before the mount. The journeys are recorded in Numbers 33: Israel departed from Rameses on the fifteenth day of the first month, on the morrow after the Passover, and later pitched in the wilderness of Sinai.
- Precept **[[Numbers 33#^v1|Numbers 33:1-4]]**: These are the journeys of Israel by their armies, and Moses wrote their goings out by the commandment of the Lord, departing from Rameses on the morrow after the Passover.

**[[Exodus 21#^v2|Exodus 21:2-6]]**  taught in [[Exodus 18-21 Study Notes|Exodus 18-21]]

- Zedekiah's covenant in Jeremiah confirms the law in the prophets: every man was to let his Hebrew manservant and maidservant go free at the appointed release.

**[[Exodus 21#^v13|Exodus 21:13-14]]**  taught in [[Exodus 18-21 Study Notes|Exodus 18-21]]

- If a man lie not in wait, but God deliver him into his hand, a place was appointed whither he shall flee. That is the case of the slayer that kills his neighbour ignorantly, as when a man hews wood with his neighbour and the axe head slips from the helve and lights upon him that he die: he flees to the appointed city and lives, lest the avenger of blood overtake him while his heart is hot, for he was not worthy of death, having hated him not in time past.

**[[Exodus 22#^v29|Exodus 22:29-30]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- Likewise with the oxen and the sheep: seven days the firstling stayed with his dam, his mother, and on the eighth day it was given. The Lord did not want it too young, taken as soon as it popped out; it had to be its appointed time with the mother, then brought to the priest.

**[[Exodus 23#^v14|Exodus 23:14-17]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- The feast of unleavened bread is the Passover, kept in the time appointed of the month Abib, the first month of the year, as it is written, the feast of unleavened bread drew nigh, which is called the Passover.
- The feast of harvest, the firstfruits of thy labours, is also known as Pentecost and the feast of weeks; and the feast of ingathering, in the end of the year, is also known as the feast of tabernacles and the feast of booths.
- Precept **[[Luke 22#^v1|Luke 22:1]]**: The feast of unleavened bread drew nigh, which is called the Passover; the two names are one feast.

**[[Exodus 23#^v18|Exodus 23:18-19]]**  taught in [[Exodus 22-25 Study Notes|Exodus 22-25]]

- Thou shalt not offer the blood of my sacrifice with leavened bread, neither shall the fat of my sacrifice remain until the morning. The first of the firstfruits of thy land thou shalt bring into the house of the Lord thy God.

**[[Exodus 28#^v1|Exodus 28:1-2]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- Aaron and his sons Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar are taken from among the children of Israel to minister unto the Most High in the priest's office.
- The Most High commanded holy garments to be made for Aaron for glory and for beauty. Garments were ordained for the men of Israel; when the world hears [[Deuteronomy 22#^v5|Deuteronomy 22:5]] and asks why the men wear garments, the answer is here: the most holy and regarded men of the nation, the priests, were arrayed in long garments down to the foot.
- The sealed of the Lord receive the same. The hundred forty and four thousand of [[Revelation 7|Revelation 7]] that must be sealed before destruction comes are read of again in 2 Esdras: they departed from the shadow of the world, leaving spiritual Egypt and the ways Babylon taught behind, and have received glorious garments of the Lord. In the kingdom of heaven the Most High will clothe His men in glorious garments as in ancient times, on another level than anything worn today.
- Precept **[[2 Esdras 2#^v38|2 Esdras 2:38-39]]**: The sealed that departed from the shadow of the world receive glorious garments of the Lord.

**[[Exodus 28#^v30|Exodus 28:30]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- The Urim and the Thummim go in the breastplate of judgment, upon Aaron's heart before the Lord; Aaron bears the judgment of the children of Israel upon his heart continually.
- Urim and Thummim means lights and perfections: objects not specifically described in the scriptures, perhaps stones since they went in the breastplate, by which the high priest ascertained the will of the Most High in important matters affecting the nation. To ascertain is to find out for certain, a divine discerning beyond what a man can determine by his own thinking.
- One theory holds they were cast as lots, but the apostles, who were not dealing with the Levitical priesthood, cast lots between Joseph called Barsabas and Matthias without any Urim and Thummim, which is an issue with that theory. The better theory: they served as a symbol of the high priest's authority to seek counsel of the Most High, His will being revealed to him through inner illumination.
- The significance shows in Nehemiah's day: priests whose register was not found among those reckoned by genealogy were put from the priesthood as polluted, and the Tirshatha, who is Nehemiah the governor, said they should not eat of the most holy things till there stood up a priest with Urim and with Thummim; determining that lineage was outside human ability, so it waited on the priest with the divine discerner.
- Joshua likewise was to stand before Eleazar the priest, who shall ask counsel for him after the judgment of Urim before the Lord; the Most High gave revelation through it.
- Precept **[[Acts 1#^v23|Acts 1:23-26]]**: The apostles cast lots between Joseph called Barsabas and Matthias, and the lot fell upon Matthias; lots were cast without the Urim and Thummim.

**[[Exodus 29#^v1|Exodus 29:1-4]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- To hallow Aaron and his sons to minister in the priest's office: one young bullock and two rams without blemish, with unleavened bread, cakes tempered with oil, and wafers anointed with oil, brought in one basket to the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

**[[Exodus 29#^v5|Exodus 29:5-9]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- The garments are put upon Aaron, the coat, the robe of the ephod, the ephod, and the breastplate, with the curious girdle; the mitre upon his head and the holy crown upon the mitre; the anointing oil poured upon his head; and his sons in coats, girdles, and bonnets. The priest's office shall be theirs for a perpetual statute: thou shalt consecrate Aaron and his sons.
- Ecclesiasticus records the same consecration: Moses anointed him with holy oil, an everlasting covenant to Aaron and to his seed so long as the heavens should remain, to minister unto Him, execute the office of the priesthood, offer the sacrifices, and teach Jacob the testimonies and inform Israel in His laws. The priest's job was the offerings and teaching the nation the law, statutes, and commandments.
- What happened to that covenant is answered in Malachi, where the commandment is spoken to the priests: the covenant with Levi was of life and peace, the priest's lips should keep knowledge and they should seek the law at his mouth, for he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts; but ye are departed out of the way, ye have caused many to stumble at the law, ye have corrupted the covenant of Levi. The priests were finessing the offerings, keeping the best animals for themselves and sacrificing the blind and the broken, and they perverted the teachings of the law for their own benefit.
- That is why leadership today is not walking around requiring Levite lineage of Aaron. Christ is the high priest now, and the nation is a whole nation of kings and priests; Malachi and Hebrews are two good books to study on the changing of the priesthood, against the doctrine that the Aaronic covenant could never change.
- Precept **[[Ecclesiasticus 45#^v15|Ecclesiasticus 45:15-17]]**: The everlasting covenant of the priesthood with Aaron and his seed: to offer the sacrifices and to teach Jacob the testimonies and inform Israel in His laws.

**[[Exodus 29#^v19|Exodus 29:19-28]]**  taught in [[Exodus 26-29 Study Notes|Exodus 26-29]]

- The second ram is the ram of consecration: its blood is put upon the tip of the right ear of Aaron and of his sons, upon the thumb of their right hand and the great toe of their right foot, and sprinkled upon the altar round about; the blood and the anointing oil are sprinkled upon Aaron, his garments, his sons, and their garments, and he shall be hallowed.
- The bread and the parts of the ram are put in the hands of Aaron and his sons and waved for a wave offering before the Lord, then burnt upon the altar; and the breast of the wave offering and the shoulder of the heave offering are sanctified for Aaron and his sons by a statute for ever. There go the wave offering and the heave offering, which you will read about throughout the first five books, Leviticus predominantly.
- These ordinances belonged to the Levitical priesthood, and the doctrines that would put men back under pieces of that office today do not even keep what they claim; they sound right because they use scriptures strongly and confidently, but they are in error. Be not tossed to and fro with every wind of doctrine, nor carried about with strange doctrines; do not ever fall away from the Messiah.

**[[Exodus 30#^v11|Exodus 30:11-16]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- The atonement money was appointed for the service of the tabernacle of the congregation. The gold, the wood, the curtains, and the fabrics all had to be bought and kept up, and the tabernacle moved with the nation through the wilderness; that is the same reason offerings are gathered in the congregation today, because the house of the Most High cannot be built or kept without it.

**[[Exodus 30#^v22|Exodus 30:22-33]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- The Most High gave specific orders down to the spices and the weights, and none could deviate from them or they would die. Our forefathers carried out that tedious, exact work, yet we complain about the smallest things set in place for order, the uniform, the new moon, and not cooking on the sabbath; the spirit has to get right.

**[[Exodus 31#^v12|Exodus 31:12-15]]**  taught in [[Exodus 30-33 Study Notes|Exodus 30-33]]

- Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep, for it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations, that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you. Keeping the weekly sabbath and the feast days shows that Israel are his children and he is their God; the nations do not have this sabbath, and adopted Sunday worship is not the true sabbath given to the children of Israel.
- Every one that defileth the sabbath shall surely be put to death. The stoning was done away in Christ, but the judgment for breaking the sabbath still stands, and people still die on the sabbath day, Friday sundown to Saturday sundown; the Most High is justified, because it is his sign between him and his people.
- Six days may work be done, but in the seventh is the sabbath of rest, holy to the LORD. It is more than a day of rest and congregating; it is the sign of who Israel's God is, which is why saying the sabbath is done away takes away the very sign between Israel and God.
- Precept **[[Ecclesiasticus 33#^v7|Ecclesiasticus 33:7-9]]**: Every day has the same sun, but by the knowledge of the Lord some days were made high days and hallowed; the sabbath is distinguished by God, not by man.

**[[Exodus 34#^v18|Exodus 34:18-20]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- The feast of unleavened bread shalt thou keep seven days, in the time of the month Abib, for in the month Abib thou camest out from Egypt; this goes to the Passover season.

**[[Exodus 34#^v22|Exodus 34:22-24]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- The feast of weeks goes to Pentecost, and the feast of ingathering at the year's end goes to the feast of tabernacles.
- Thrice in the year shall all your men children appear before the Lord GOD, the God of Israel. The law is written again in Deuteronomy: the feast of unleavened bread, which is the Passover season, the feast of weeks, which is Pentecost, and the feast of tabernacles; and they shall not appear before the Lord empty, for each had to bring his sacrifice.
- Precept **[[Deuteronomy 16#^v16|Deuteronomy 16:16]]**: Three times in a year shall all thy males appear before the LORD in the place which he shall choose, at the three feasts, and they shall not appear before the LORD empty.

**[[Exodus 34#^v25|Exodus 34:25-26]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- The first of the firstfruits of thy land thou shalt bring unto the house of the LORD thy God. And thou shalt not seethe a kid in his mother's milk: a young one still in his mother's milk, as veal is taken for instance, is not ready to be eaten; it is unlawful.

**[[Exodus 36#^v20|Exodus 36:20-34]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- The whole tent was built to be carried. Any time Israel moved through the wilderness, the men appointed as carriers would break everything down and travel with it toward the land of Canaan; wherever the Most High moved, Israel followed, and where the Most High stopped, they set up again.

**[[Exodus 37#^v25|Exodus 37:25-29]]**  taught in [[Exodus 34-37 Study Notes|Exodus 34-37]]

- Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, took either of them his censer, put fire therein and incense thereon, and offered strange fire before the Lord, which he commanded them not; and there went out fire from the Lord and devoured them, and they died before the Lord. They were not the ones authorized to burn the incense; in the tabernacle service you had to be appointed to do certain services, and you could not just take it upon yourself to do them.
- Precept **[[Leviticus 10#^v1|Leviticus 10:1-2]]**: Nadab and Abihu offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not, and fire from the LORD devoured them; only the appointed could burn the incense on this altar.

## Leviticus

**[[Leviticus 1#^v4|Leviticus 1:4-9]]**  taught in [[Leviticus 1 Study Notes|Leviticus 1]]

- He put his hand upon the head of the burnt offering, and it was accepted for him to make an atonement for him; the bullock was killed before the Lord, the blood sprinkled round about upon the altar by the priests, and the parts laid in order upon the wood on the fire.
- Notice the fat is laid on the altar. Do not stumble when chapter 3 says not to eat fat: that deals with the fat of the sacrifice that the priest sent up in the offering, not the fat on a steak off your grill.
- The offering made by fire is a sweet savour unto the Lord. Christ walked in love and gave himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour: he fulfilled and replaced all these offerings, once and for all, in his death and resurrection.
- This is the context so much of Paul's letters sit in: he was convincing the congregations that they no longer had to sacrifice animals, because Pharisees were creeping in trying to turn them back to those things; therefore let no man judge you in meat or in drink.
- In our captivity, with no Levitical priesthood and no temple, keeping the commandments is the offering and the reasonable service: he that keepeth the law bringeth offerings enough, and we present our bodies a living sacrifice, sacrificing the lusts and pleasures of the flesh to follow the footsteps of Christ. And [[Ezekiel 20|Ezekiel 20]] shows that when Israel is brought back through the wilderness, offerings will be required again in the holy mountain.
- Precept **[[Colossians 2#^v16|Colossians 2:16]]**: Let no man judge you in meat, or in drink; Paul defended the congregations from those pushing them back to the sacrifices.
- Precept **[[Ezekiel 20#^v40|Ezekiel 20:40]]**: In the holy mountain, at the return, all Israel shall serve the Most High, and there He will require the offerings again.
