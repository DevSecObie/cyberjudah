# Judges 9: Abimelech and the Fire Out of the Bramble

Read the chapter → [[Judges 9]]

## Introduction

This session covers [[Judges 9|Judges 9]] through 12: Abimelech's bloody rise and fall at Shechem, the judges Tola and Jair, Israel sold into the hands of the Philistines and Ammon for serving other gods, Jephthah the Gileadite and his answer to the king of Ammon, Jephthah's vow and his daughter, the slaughter of the Ephraimites at the passages of Jordan, and the judges Ibzan, Elon, and Abdon. The main threads of the teaching: the Most High is in the midst of every war and sends the spirits that stir them; your sin will find you out; and know your history, because Jephthah knew the scriptures and could not be deceived about what actually happened.

- Remember also that I am your bone and your flesh: he reminded them that he was family. When people do that, they want you to remember that blood is thicker than water, so you will back them whether or not it is right.

- They gave him threescore and ten pieces of silver out of the house of Baal-berith, money out of their idol's house to back his rise. Politics has always worked this way: people will put a man in office not because he is best for the job, but because he is family or because they know him.
- Baal was the chief god of the Canaanites, worshipped as the god of the elements who brought rain and made the ground fruitful, and sometimes as the god of war. Baal-berith, also called El-berith, was the Canaanite god of Shechem in the twelfth century BC, a sub god under Baal.

- In his parable the trees went forth on a time to anoint a king over them. The olive tree would not leave its fatness, wherewith by it they honour God and man; the fig tree would not forsake its sweetness and good fruit; the vine would not leave its wine, which cheereth God and man. The profitable trees refused to be promoted over the trees.
- Only the bramble accepted: if in truth ye anoint me king over you, then come and put your trust in my shadow; and if not, let fire come out of the bramble and devour the cedars of Lebanon. The worthless bush took the crown, and its answer carried the threat of fire.

- He reminded them of what they owed his father: my father fought for you, and adventured his life far, and delivered you out of the hand of Midian; and ye are risen up against my father's house this day, and have slain his sons, threescore and ten persons upon one stone, and have made Abimelech, the son of his maidservant, king over the men of Shechem, because he is your brother. They did it only because he was kin to them.
- But if not, let fire come out from Abimelech and devour the men of Shechem and the house of Millo; and let fire come out from the men of Shechem and from the house of Millo, and devour Abimelech. That is what the parable of the bramble was about: he prophesied that they would destroy one another, and it came to pass.

- When Abimelech had reigned three years over Israel, God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and the men of Shechem, and the men of Shechem dealt treacherously with Abimelech. Read that carefully: the Lord sent the evil spirit. When somebody's spirit suddenly changes toward you, we tend to think it is just that person; the scripture shows the Most High is the one who stirs these things.
- War and strife between nations are not coincidence, karma, or mother nature; those are the world's false ideas and idols. The Most High is in the midst of these things, mustering the host, the armies above and below, and he even puts it on the spirit of one wicked man to go against another.
- The reason is given: that the cruelty done to the threescore and ten sons of Jerubbaal might come, and their blood be laid upon Abimelech their brother, which slew them, and upon the men of Shechem, which aided him in the killing of his brethren. The hired men and the backers carried the blood guilt too. Abimelech ran for three years and everything looked good, but you are not getting away with evil.

# Judges 11: Jephthah and the Search of the Fathers

- Now Jephthah the Gileadite was a mighty man of valour, and he was the son of an harlot: and Gilead begat Jephthah. His mother was a harlot, but he himself was a mighty man of valour.
- Gilead's wife's sons grew up and thrust out Jephthah: thou shalt not inherit in our father's house, for thou art the son of a strange woman. Strange woman here does not mean his mother was of another nation; the scripture says only that she was a harlot. His brothers cast him out of his father's house over his mother, though he had as much right to that house as they did.
- Jephthah fled from his brethren and dwelt in the land of Tob, and there were gathered vain men to Jephthah, and went out with him. Watch what that shows: when we do our brothers and sisters wrong, when the ones trying to do right do not get the love and support they need from their own family, they go looking for love in the wrong places, and you see the kind of men they gravitate to.

The king of Ammon could have pulled that on somebody ignorant and been given the land; Jephthah said no, that is not what happened, the book of Numbers does not say that. People try to confuse us about the Bible in the street the same way, so prepare yourself to the search of the fathers.

- And the LORD God of Israel delivered Sihon and all his people into the hand of Israel, and they smote them: so Israel possessed all the land of the Amorites, from Arnon even unto Jabbok, and from the wilderness even unto Jordan. That is how Israel got the land of the Amorites: the Lord gave it in battle when they came out against his people.

- So now the LORD God of Israel hath dispossessed the Amorites from before his people Israel, and shouldest thou possess it? The Lord moved the Amorites out by moving Israel in; why should Ammon possess what the Most High gave to Israel?
- Wilt not thou possess that which Chemosh thy god giveth thee? So whomsoever the LORD our God shall drive out from before us, them will we possess. You take what your god gives you; we will possess whatever the Most High gives us, and we are not backing down from anybody.
- Art thou any thing better than Balak the son of Zippor, king of Moab? did he ever strive against Israel, or did he ever fight against them? While Israel dwelt in Heshbon and her towns, and in Aroer and her towns, and in all the cities along the coasts of Arnon, three hundred years, why did ye not recover them within that time? Three hundred years Israel held that land and nobody came to claim it.

The Lord is in the midst of every battle; nothing goes down without the Most High letting it go down. World War One, the Lord was in the midst; World War Two, the Lord was in the midst; whoever he has destined to win is going to win.
- Howbeit the king of the children of Ammon hearkened not unto the words of Jephthah: the Lord had hardened their hearts not to listen, the same as he did with Sihon.

- His daughter answered, My father, if thou hast opened thy mouth unto the LORD, do to me according to that which hath proceeded out of thy mouth; forasmuch as the LORD hath taken vengeance for thee of thine enemies, even of the children of Ammon. She was not pleading for her life; she accepted that her life was now vowed to the Lord.
- She asked one thing: let me alone two months, that I may go up and down upon the mountains, and bewail my virginity, I and my fellows. To bewail is to weep and mourn; she went with her companions and mourned her virginity, because being given over to the Lord she would never know a man.
- Jephthah did not burn his daughter as a human sacrifice. Israel did not do human sacrifices; that was Molech worship among the Canaanites. Read the end of the passage: at the end of two months she returned unto her father, who did with her according to his vow which he had vowed, and she knew no man. If she had been put to death, there would be no reason for the scripture to say she knew no man; her life was committed to the Lord and she remained a virgin.
- Why would she mourn her virginity if she were about to die? Because the sorrow was that she could never marry or bear children. In those days our women wanted to bring children into the world and continue the lineage; to be without children was counted a shame and a dishonour among the daughters of Israel.
- A lot of people try to say Jephthah sacrificed his daughter, because our people do not understand the scriptures; keep reading and the text itself answers it. It is a translation difference around the word offering: she was presented to the Lord to serve him, not sent up as a burnt sacrifice.
- And it was a custom in Israel, that the daughters of Israel went yearly to lament the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite four days in a year.
