# Judges 17: Micah's House of Idols and the Hired Levite

Read the chapter → [[Judges 17]]

## Introduction

This session covers [[Judges 17|Judges 17]] through 20, some of the wildest history in the book: Micah of mount Ephraim and his house of idols, the Levite he hired to be his personal priest, the Danites stealing Micah's gods and conquering Laish, the Levite's concubine and the sons of Belial at Gibeah, and the war of all Israel against Benjamin. The thread that ties these chapters together is stated four times: in those days there was no king in Israel, and every man did that which was right in his own eyes. Israel came out of the wilderness with the commandments, but without a leadership structure they were sheep without a shepherd, and the nation ran wild in all manner of wickedness, primarily idolatry.

![[Judges 17#^v1]]
![[Judges 17#^v2]]

- A man of mount Ephraim named Micah stole eleven hundred shekels of silver from his own mother, and when he heard her cursing about the missing silver he confessed, I took it, and restored it to her.
- Instead of rebuking her son, his mother said blessed be thou of the LORD, and told him she had wholly dedicated the silver for him to make a graven image and a molten image. She refused to keep what he returned, took two hundred shekels to the founder, and had idols made to set up in her son's house.
- A mother buying her son idols was nothing new in Israel; there were mothers who steered a son down the wrong path.

  Precepts:
  - **[[2 Chronicles 22#^v3|2 Chronicles 22:3]]**
    ![[2 Chronicles 22#^v3]]
    Later in history king Ahaziah walked in the ways of the house of Ahab, for his mother was his counsellor to do wickedly; the same pattern seen with Micah's mother.

![[Judges 17#^v5]]

- Micah had a whole house of gods. He made an ephod and teraphim and consecrated one of his own sons to be his priest, a counterfeit priesthood serving counterfeit gods.

**[[Judges 17#^v6|Judges 17:6]]**

![[Judges 17#^v6]]

- This verse is the key to understanding the whole book of Judges. With no king and no government, Israel was sheep without a shepherd, and when you see how the people carry on in these chapters, that is why: every man did that which was right in his own eyes.
- The same individual spirit is around today in those who say join no camp and let no man teach you. Every time the scripture shows Israel without a leadership structure, all manner of wickedness follows, primarily idolatry.

- When Micah learned the man was a Levite, of the tribe known for the priesthood and the knowledge of the sacrifices, he offered him a deal: dwell with me, be unto me a father and a priest, ten shekels of silver by the year, a suit of apparel, and thy victuals. He hired himself a personal priest for his idols.

- Then Micah said, now know I that the LORD will do me good, seeing I have a Levite to my priest. A house full of idols and a hired priest over them, yet he expected the Most High to bless him because a Levite was on the payroll. That is how sick in the mind Israel was without leadership.

# Judges 19: The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah

Read the chapter → [[Judges 19]]

![[Judges 19#^v1]]

- His concubine played the whore against him, laid with another man, then went away from him unto her father's house to Bethlehemjudah and was there four whole months.

**[[Judges 19#^v3|Judges 19:3]]**

![[Judges 19#^v3]]

- By the law, a woman who is put away and becomes another man's is defiled to her former husband, and taking her back defiles the land. The Most High does not permit it, but with no king in the land and no enforcement of the law, this is what men did.

- The father's words on the fifth day cut a popular doctrine. They tarried until afternoon, and he said, behold, the day draweth toward evening, and then, behold, the day groweth to an end. According to the Bible the day ends at evening; and if it ends at evening it begins at evening, so a day runs from evening to evening, including the sabbath. That buries the sunrise to sunrise doctrine, which has no works and no fruits to show it was ever right.

- Jerusalem is called Jebus here because the Jebusites, the original inhabitants of Jerusalem, still dwelt in it; this is before David rose up and took it from them. When Israel came out of the wilderness, the promised land was inhabited primarily by Canaanites, and the children of Judah could not drive the Jebusites out.

Even in those wild days he would not lodge with heathens, only with his brothers.

- Six hundred men turned and fled to the wilderness unto the rock Rimmon and abode there four months; only that remnant kept the tribe from being utterly destroyed. The men of Israel turned again upon the children of Benjamin and smote them with the edge of the sword, men of every city, and beast, and all that came to hand, and set on fire all the cities they came to.
- Benjamin was judged big time; the Most High pushed the reset button on the tribe for the sodomite and rape activity that came out of Gibeah. Chapter 21 shows the drastic measures that had to be taken so Benjamin could continue producing as a tribe.
- That judgment is why Saul, in the days of the first king, calls himself a Benjamite of the smallest of the tribes of Israel; this war is what minimized the tribe. There have been mighty and righteous Benjamites since, Paul chief among them, but it is written, and we learn the history as it is written.
