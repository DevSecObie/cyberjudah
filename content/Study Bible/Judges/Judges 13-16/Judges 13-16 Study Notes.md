# Judges 13: A Nazarite from the Womb

Read the chapter → [[Judges 13]]

## Introduction

This session covers the whole history of Samson, one of the most famous forefathers and one of the most illustrated stories in the Bible: his birth announced by the angel of the Lord in chapter 13, his marriage among the Philistines and the riddle in chapter 14, the jawbone of an ass in chapter 15, and Delilah and his death in chapter 16. In the book of Judges, after Joshua led Israel into the promised land and died, Israel immediately started going off; before the major captivities of Assyria and Babylon came about, Israel was oppressed by the Moabites, the Philistines, and other nations round about. Here Israel is under the hand of the Philistines, and the Most High raises up a deliverer from the tribe of Dan.

**[[Judges 13#^v1|Judges 13:1]]**

![[Judges 13#^v1]]

- The children of Israel did evil again in the sight of the Lord, and the Lord delivered them into the hand of the Philistines forty years. This is the pattern of the book of Judges: after Joshua's death Israel went off, and nation after nation was given power over them before the great captivities ever came.

**[[Judges 13#^v2|Judges 13:2]]**

![[Judges 13#^v2]]

- There was a certain man of Zorah, of the family of the Danites, whose name was Manoah. He was married, but his wife was barren; she was not able to have any children.

**[[Judges 13#^v3|Judges 13:3]]**

![[Judges 13#^v3]]

- The angel of the Lord appeared unto the woman and told her plainly: you are barren now, but you shall conceive and bear a son, a man child.
- This is one of many examples in the Bible of our foremothers going through barrenness, and when the Most High was ready he enabled them to conceive. Sisters who have tried for years without children should in patience possess their souls, take comfort of the scriptures, meditate on the foremothers, and believe what the Bible says.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Psalms 113#^v9|Psalms 113:9]]**
    ![[Psalms 113#^v9]]
    The Most High maketh the barren woman to keep house and to be a joyful mother of children.

**[[Judges 13#^v4|Judges 13:4-5]]**

![[Judges 13#^v5]]

- The angel commanded the mother herself to drink no wine nor strong drink and to eat no unclean thing, because the child would be a Nazarite unto God from the womb; even in the womb he was to be kept pure.
- No razor was to come on his head, for the child would be a Nazarite unto God from the womb, and he would begin to deliver Israel out of the hand of the Philistines. This is one of the first examples in scripture of somebody actually living the vow that was laid out in [[Numbers 6|Numbers 6]].
- Understand the difference between a Nazarite and a Nazarene. Christ was a Nazarene because he was from the city of Nazareth, not a Nazarite; he drank wine, and Nazarites could not drink the blood of the grape at all.
- The locks of the Nazarite also witness to who Israel is; the hair of Israel naturally grows into locks.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Numbers 6#^v1|Numbers 6:1-4]]**
    ![[Numbers 6#^v1]]
    ![[Numbers 6#^v2]]
    ![[Numbers 6#^v3]]
    ![[Numbers 6#^v4]]

  - **[[Numbers 6#^v5|Numbers 6:5]]**
    ![[Numbers 6#^v5]]
    All the days of the vow no razor shall come upon his head; he shall be holy and shall let the locks of the hair of his head grow, which is why the angel gave Manoah's wife these instructions.

In verse 3 the scripture says it was the angel of the Lord, but he did not tell her, so she was not sure; she only knew his face was terrible like an angel's.
- The popular pictures of angels as cute babies with smooth cheeks and little white wings do not match the biblical descriptions at all. To see an angel of the Most High was a terrifying sight, not something to jump for joy about.
- She repeated the message: the child shall be a Nazarite to God from the womb to the day of his death, and she was to drink no wine nor strong drink nor eat any unclean thing.

- No flesh can look upon the Most High God; he is far too fierce and terrible. The furthest anybody got was Moses seeing his back parts.
- His wife reasoned rightly: if the Lord were pleased to kill us, he would not have received a burnt offering and a meat offering at our hands, neither would he have shewed us all these things.

# Judges 14: An Occasion Against the Philistines

Read the chapter → [[Judges 14]]

**[[Judges 14#^v1|Judges 14:1-3]]**

![[Judges 14#^v1]]
![[Judges 14#^v2]]
![[Judges 14#^v3]]

- Samson went down to Timnath and saw a woman of the daughters of the Philistines, and told his father and mother, get her for me to wife. As he grew up this brother had a lust issue with heathen women; that is the infamy the story of Samson is known for.
- His parents answered him rightly: is there never a woman among the daughters of thy brethren, or among all my people, that thou goest to take a wife of the uncircumcised Philistines? There were twelve tribes; there was no shortage of women in all the coasts of Israel.
- Samson insisted, get her for me, for she pleaseth me well. The forefathers always charged their sons not to do this.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 28#^v1|Genesis 28:1]]**
    ![[Genesis 28#^v1]]
    Isaac called Jacob, blessed him, and charged him: thou shalt not take a wife of the daughters of Canaan; the fathers commanded their sons to choose from their own people.

**[[Judges 14#^v4|Judges 14:4]]**

![[Judges 14#^v4]]

- His father and his mother knew not that it was of the Lord, that he sought an occasion against the Philistines, for at that time the Philistines had dominion over Israel. The Lord was moving Samson's spirit, and the parents did not understand, just as we often question the Most High when we have no idea the depths of his wisdom and what he is really doing.
- The Most High was using Samson's flaw, his lust after Philistine women, as an occasion against the Philistines, to destroy them for oppressing his people. That is the basis of the whole story: the Lord was behind all of it, pulling the strings.
- The Most High is in control of everything, good and evil alike. Even affliction that comes on his people is his tactic to make them seek him and repent; nothing anybody does can alter the outcome he has already ordained, for he declared the end from the beginning.

  - **[[Isaiah 55#^v8|Isaiah 55:8-9]]**
    ![[Isaiah 55#^v8]]
    ![[Isaiah 55#^v9]]
    His thoughts are not our thoughts, neither are our ways his ways; his thoughts are higher than our thoughts.
  - **[[Job 12#^v16|Job 12:16]]**
    ![[Job 12#^v16]]
    With him is strength and wisdom; the deceived and the deceiver are his. The Most High used these heathen women, and later Delilah, to get Samson caught up with the Philistines so that he would end up destroying them.
  - **[[Proverbs 21#^v30|Proverbs 21:30]]**
    ![[Proverbs 21#^v30]]
    There is no wisdom nor understanding nor counsel against the Lord; everything ultimately works within his plan.
  - **[[Amos 3#^v6|Amos 3:6]]**
    ![[Amos 3#^v6]]
    Shall there be evil in a city, and the Lord hath not done it? The evil is wrong, but the Lord is behind it, working his purpose.
  - **[[Hosea 5#^v15|Hosea 5:15]]**
    ![[Hosea 5#^v15]]
    In their affliction they will seek me early; affliction is how the Most High wakes his people up to repent.

**[[Judges 14#^v5|Judges 14:5-6]]**

![[Judges 14#^v5]]
![[Judges 14#^v6]]

- As Samson went down to the vineyards of Timnath, a young lion roared against him, challenging him. Here you begin to see the divine power on Samson.
- The spirit of the Lord came mightily upon him, and he rent the lion as he would have rent a kid, and he had nothing in his hand: no weapon, no sword, nothing. He tore up a young lion, a prince of the jungle, like it was a little goat. This is one of the forms of the spirit of the Lord, spiritual power.
- He told not his father or his mother what he had done; he did not boast about it.

**[[Judges 14#^v7|Judges 14:7-9]]**

![[Judges 14#^v7]]
![[Judges 14#^v8]]
![[Judges 14#^v9]]

- He went down and talked with the woman, and she pleased Samson well.
- After a time he returned to take her, and he turned aside to see the carcase of the lion he had slain, and behold, there was a swarm of bees and honey in the carcase.
- He took of the honey in his hands and went on eating, and gave to his father and mother and they did eat, but he told them not that he had taken the honey out of the carcase of the lion.

**[[Judges 14#^v10|Judges 14:10-14]]**

![[Judges 14#^v10]]
![[Judges 14#^v11]]
![[Judges 14#^v12]]
![[Judges 14#^v13]]
![[Judges 14#^v14]]

- His father went down unto the woman, and Samson made there a feast, as the young men used to do, and when the Philistines saw him they brought thirty companions to be with him.
- Samson put forth a riddle: if they could declare it within the seven days of the feast, he would give them thirty sheets and thirty change of garments, and if they could not, they would give him the same.
- The riddle: out of the eater came forth meat, and out of the strong came forth sweetness. In three days they could not expound it.

**[[Judges 14#^v15|Judges 14:15-17]]**

![[Judges 14#^v15]]
![[Judges 14#^v16]]
![[Judges 14#^v17]]

- On the seventh day they said unto Samson's wife, entice thy husband that he may declare unto us the riddle, lest we burn thee and thy father's house with fire. They threatened to burn their own countrywoman in her father's house over thirty outfits.
- She turned to manipulation and wept before him: thou dost but hate me, and lovest me not; thou hast put forth a riddle unto the children of my people and hast not told it me. He answered, I have not told it my father nor my mother, and shall I tell it thee?
- She wept before him the seven days while their feast lasted, and on the seventh day he told her, because she lay sore upon him. This big strong mighty brother caved in because of the tears of a woman.

**[[Judges 14#^v18|Judges 14:18]]**

![[Judges 14#^v18]]

- She told the riddle to the children of her people, and the men of the city answered him before the sun went down: what is sweeter than honey, and what is stronger than a lion?
- Samson knew exactly what happened: if ye had not plowed with my heifer, ye had not found out my riddle.
- Never trust your enemy. That was his wife, and her loyalty belonged to him, but when she got the riddle out of him she went and told it to her heathen people, his enemy. That is why it makes no sense to marry the enemy.

**[[Judges 14#^v19|Judges 14:19-20]]**

![[Judges 14#^v19]]
![[Judges 14#^v20]]

- The spirit of the Lord came upon him, and he went down to Ashkelon and slew thirty men of the Philistines, took their spoil, and gave the change of garments unto them which expounded the riddle; the spirit of the Lord came upon him even for this, against the nation that oppressed Israel.
- His anger was kindled, and he went up to his father's house, and Samson's wife was given to his companion, whom he had used as his friend; she was his wife to be, for they had not yet consummated the marriage.

# Judges 15: The Jawbone of an Ass

Read the chapter → [[Judges 15]]

**[[Judges 15#^v1|Judges 15:1-3]]**

![[Judges 15#^v1]]
![[Judges 15#^v2]]
![[Judges 15#^v3]]

- Within a while, in the time of wheat harvest, Samson visited his wife with a kid and said, I will go in to my wife into the chamber. He knew she had betrayed him and still went back to her; a weakness for a woman really does beat on Israelite men.
- Her father would not suffer him to go in, saying, I verily thought that thou hadst utterly hated her, therefore I gave her to thy companion; is not her younger sister fairer than she? take her, I pray thee, instead of her.
- Samson said concerning them, now shall I be more blameless than the Philistines, though I do them a displeasure; he was set to avenge himself.

**[[Judges 15#^v4|Judges 15:4-5]]**

![[Judges 15#^v4]]
![[Judges 15#^v5]]

- Samson went and caught three hundred foxes by himself, took firebrands, turned the foxes tail to tail, and put a firebrand in the midst between two tails.
- When he had set the brands on fire, he let them go into the standing corn of the Philistines and burnt up both the shocks and the standing corn, with the vineyards and olives; their food source and their wine source, all of it, because they crossed him.

**[[Judges 15#^v6|Judges 15:6]]**

![[Judges 15#^v6]]

- The Philistines asked who had done this, and were told, Samson, the son in law of the Timnite, because he had taken his wife and given her to his companion.
- The Philistines came up and burnt her and her father with fire; the very thing she betrayed her husband to escape came upon her from her own people.

**[[Judges 15#^v7|Judges 15:7-8]]**

![[Judges 15#^v7]]
![[Judges 15#^v8]]

- Samson said unto them, though ye have done this, yet will I be avenged of you, and after that I will cease; he would get the last word.
- He smote them hip and thigh with a great slaughter, dropping them like flies, and went down and dwelt in the top of the rock Etam.

**[[Judges 15#^v9|Judges 15:9-13]]**

![[Judges 15#^v9]]
![[Judges 15#^v10]]
![[Judges 15#^v11]]
![[Judges 15#^v12]]
![[Judges 15#^v13]]

- The Philistines went up and pitched in Judah and spread themselves in Lehi, and the men of Judah asked, why are ye come up against us? We are already under tribute; what is the problem? They answered, to bind Samson are we come up, to do to him as he hath done to us. Samson was not even of Judah; he was a Danite.
- Three thousand men of Judah went to the top of the rock Etam and said to Samson, knowest thou not that the Philistines are rulers over us? what is this that thou hast done unto us? Instead of standing with their brother, the whole tribe came to hand him over to the oppressor.
- He answered, as they did unto me, so have I done unto them. He let them bind him, only making them swear that they would not fall upon him themselves, and they bound him with two new cords and brought him up from the rock.

**[[Judges 15#^v14|Judges 15:14-15]]**

![[Judges 15#^v14]]
![[Judges 15#^v15]]

- When he came unto Lehi, the Philistines shouted against him, and the spirit of the Lord came mightily upon him: the cords that were upon his arms became as flax that was burnt with fire, and his bands loosed from off his hands.
- He found a new jawbone of an ass, put forth his hand and took it, and slew a thousand men therewith. This is not normal; you have not seen this in any comic book. One man against a thousand, and all he had was the jawbone of a donkey, and he laid all of them down.
- This is the superpower class of Israel's history, like walking with the giants; Israel really was nothing to play with when the Most High was dealing with us, and the nations knew it.

**[[Judges 15#^v16|Judges 15:16-17]]**

![[Judges 15#^v16]]
![[Judges 15#^v17]]

- Samson said, with the jawbone of an ass, heaps upon heaps, with the jaw of an ass have I slain a thousand men.
- When he had made an end of speaking, he cast away the jawbone out of his hand and called that place Ramath-lehi.

**[[Judges 15#^v18|Judges 15:18-19]]**

![[Judges 15#^v18]]
![[Judges 15#^v19]]

- He was sore athirst and called on the Lord: thou hast given this great deliverance into the hand of thy servant, and now shall I die for thirst, and fall into the hand of the uncircumcised? You kill a thousand men with a jawbone, you are going to be parched.
- God clave an hollow place that was in the jaw, and there came water thereout; and when he had drunk, his spirit came again, and he revived. It is the same as after a fast, when the first food and drink make you feel like a whole new person.
- He called the name of that place En-hakkore, which is in Lehi unto this day.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Acts 9#^v19|Acts 9:19]]**
    ![[Acts 9#^v19]]
    Paul went days without eating, and when he had received meat, he was strengthened; food and drink after the trial revive the servant of the Most High.

**[[Judges 15#^v20|Judges 15:20]]**

![[Judges 15#^v20]]

- Samson judged Israel in the days of the Philistines twenty years; he ended up a leadership figure, a judge over Israel.

# Judges 16: Samson and Delilah

Read the chapter → [[Judges 16]]

**[[Judges 16#^v1|Judges 16:1-3]]**

![[Judges 16#^v1]]
![[Judges 16#^v2]]
![[Judges 16#^v3]]

- Samson went to Gaza, the chief city of the Philistines, and saw there an harlot and went in unto her; once again the whoremongering spirit had hold of this brother.
- It was told the Gazites that Samson was come, and they compassed him in and laid wait for him all night in the gate of the city, quiet all the night, saying, in the morning, when it is day, we shall kill him.
- Samson lay till midnight, arose at midnight, and took the doors of the gate of the city and the two posts, and went away with them, bar and all, upon his shoulders, and carried them up to the top of an hill that is before Hebron. They wanted to wait at the gate for him, so he took the gates.

**[[Judges 16#^v4|Judges 16:4-5]]**

![[Judges 16#^v4]]
![[Judges 16#^v5]]

- Afterward he loved a woman in the valley of Sorek whose name was Delilah; she was seductive and promiscuous, and Samson started leaning on this other heathen woman.
- The lords of the Philistines came up unto her and said, entice him, and see wherein his great strength lieth, and by what means we may prevail against him, that we may bind him to afflict him; and we will give thee every one of us eleven hundred pieces of silver.
- They knew what it took to get to Samson, just as the serpent knew what it took to get to Adam. The scheme never changed: when the devil wants to attack a strong Israelite man, he uses the woman.

**[[Judges 16#^v6|Judges 16:6-9]]**

![[Judges 16#^v6]]
![[Judges 16#^v7]]
![[Judges 16#^v8]]
![[Judges 16#^v9]]

- Delilah asked Samson outright, tell me, I pray thee, wherein thy great strength lieth, and wherewith thou mightest be bound to afflict thee. She was literally asking him for the formula to get him killed.
- He told her, if they bind me with seven green withs that were never dried, then shall I be weak and be as another man. The lords of the Philistines brought them up, she bound him, with liers in wait abiding in the chamber, and said, the Philistines be upon thee, Samson.
- He brake the withs as a thread of tow is broken when it toucheth the fire, so his strength was not known. A man of war should have recognized right there that this was a setup, and cut her off, but he stayed.

**[[Judges 16#^v10|Judges 16:10-12]]**

![[Judges 16#^v10]]
![[Judges 16#^v11]]
![[Judges 16#^v12]]

- Delilah pressed again: thou hast mocked me and told me lies, now tell me, I pray thee, wherewith thou mightest be bound. Think how backwards that is: she was openly complaining that he would not hand her the way to bind him. His instinct should have been to get away from her, but he stayed.
- He said, if they bind me fast with new ropes that never were occupied, then shall I be weak; but he was playing with her, for he had already burst through new cords in chapter 15.
- Delilah took new ropes and bound him, with liers in wait in the chamber, and said, the Philistines be upon thee, Samson; and he brake them from off his arms like a thread.

**[[Judges 16#^v13|Judges 16:13-14]]**

![[Judges 16#^v13]]
![[Judges 16#^v14]]

- She pressed the third time, and he told her, if thou weavest the seven locks of my head with the web. She fastened it with the pin and said, the Philistines be upon thee, Samson; he awaked out of his sleep and went away with the pin of the beam and with the web.
- Three times she had attempted to have his life taken, and he was still dealing with this harlot; it made no sense, but that is the strength of this snare.

**[[Judges 16#^v15|Judges 16:15-17]]**

![[Judges 16#^v15]]
![[Judges 16#^v16]]
![[Judges 16#^v17]]

- She said, how canst thou say, I love thee, when thine heart is not with me? and she pressed him daily with her words and urged him, so that his soul was vexed unto death. Day after day she would not let up.
- He told her all his heart, and that is where he messed up: there hath not come a razor upon mine head, for I have been a Nazarite unto God from my mother's womb; if I be shaven, then my strength will go from me, and I shall become weak and be like any other man.
- This is a scriptural example of why a man cannot tell his wife every single thing in his heart. We do not go by the philosophies of men that say your wife is your best friend you tell everything to; we do what the Bible says.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Micah 7#^v5|Micah 7:5]]**
    ![[Micah 7#^v5]]
    Trust ye not in a friend, put ye not confidence in a guide: keep the doors of thy mouth from her that lieth in thy bosom; you cannot share everything on your mind even with the woman you lie down with.

**[[Judges 16#^v18|Judges 16:18-20]]**

![[Judges 16#^v18]]
![[Judges 16#^v19]]
![[Judges 16#^v20]]

- When Delilah saw that he had told her all his heart, she knew she had him, and sent and called for the lords of the Philistines, and they came up and brought the money in their hand.
- She made him sleep upon her knees, and she called for a man and caused him to shave off the seven locks of his head, and she began to afflict him, and his strength went from him.
- She said, the Philistines be upon thee, Samson; and he awoke out of his sleep and said, I will go out as at other times before, and shake myself. And he wist not that the Lord was departed from him. He thought he was going into beast mode like before, but it was over, because he had broken his vow; the hair was shaved from off his head.

**[[Judges 16#^v21|Judges 16:21-22]]**

![[Judges 16#^v21]]
![[Judges 16#^v22]]

- The Philistines took him and put out his eyes, brought him down to Gaza, bound him with fetters of brass, and he did grind in the prison house: a blind slave, put on display like an exhibit, the man that had been slaying them now unable even to see.
- Howbeit the hair of his head began to grow again after he was shaven; they did not keep it cut.

**[[Judges 16#^v23|Judges 16:23-25]]**

![[Judges 16#^v23]]

- The lords of the Philistines gathered together to offer a great sacrifice unto Dagon their god, an idolatrous ritual, and to rejoice, for they said, our god hath delivered Samson our enemy into our hand.
- They gave the praise and the glory to their idol, a devil that had not done a thing. Really it was Samson's own transgression that caused the Most High to allow the Philistines to bind and afflict him; that praise to a fake god did not please the Most High.
- When their hearts were merry, they said, call for Samson, that he may make us sport; and they called for him out of the prison house and set him between the pillars, using the Israelite man for entertainment and mockery while they honoured their idol.

- Samson said unto the lad that held him by the hand, suffer me that I may feel the pillars whereupon the house standeth, that I may lean upon them: the two main pillars that bore up the whole building, one at his right hand and one at his left.
- Now the house was full of men and women, all the lords of the Philistines were there, and there were upon the roof about three thousand men and women that beheld while Samson made sport.

**[[Judges 16#^v28|Judges 16:28-30]]**

![[Judges 16#^v29]]

- Samson called unto the Lord and said, O Lord God, remember me, I pray thee, and strengthen me, I pray thee, only this once, O God, that I may be at once avenged of the Philistines for my two eyes.
- He took hold of the two middle pillars upon which the house stood, of the one with his right hand and of the other with his left, and said, let me die with the Philistines; and he bowed himself with all his might, and the house fell upon the lords and upon all the people that were therein.
- So the dead which he slew at his death were more than they which he slew in his life. Remember [[Judges 14#^v4|Judges 14:4]]: the Lord sought an occasion against the Philistines, and even with Samson's failings and flaws the Most High used him as a vessel of vengeance on the heathen who oppressed his people and gave the glory of their victory to a fake god.
- Israel's salvation in scripture is always joined with the destruction of the enemy. The avenging of what has been done to Israel does not come by striving for equality; the righteous justice and judgment is the destruction the Most High brings on the oppressor.

    ![[Wisdom of Solomon 18#^v7]]
    Of the Most High's people is accepted both the salvation of the righteous and the destruction of the enemies; the two always go together.
