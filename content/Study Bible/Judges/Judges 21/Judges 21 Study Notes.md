# Judges 21: The Oath at Mizpeh and Wives for Benjamin

Read the chapter → [[Judges 21]]

## Introduction

This session covers [[Judges 21|Judges 21]] together with Ruth chapters 1 through 3; these notes cover [[Judges 21|Judges 21]], the closing chapter of the book. After the war against Benjamin in the previous chapters, Israel weeps that a whole tribe is nearly cut off, but the men of Israel had sworn at Mizpeh not to give their daughters to Benjamin to wife, and an oath sworn to the Most High had to be performed. The chapter shows how Israel provided wives for the remnant of Benjamin, first from Jabeshgilead and then from the daughters of Shiloh, and it closes the book on the key of the whole era: in those days there was no king in Israel, and every man did that which was right in his own eyes.

- The men of Israel had sworn at Mizpeh that not one of them would give his daughter unto Benjamin to wife. This follows the previous chapter, where the tribes went to war against Benjamin.
- The people came to the house of God and sat there till evening before God, lifted up their voices and wept sore, asking why it had come to pass that there should be one tribe lacking in Israel. The next day they rose early, built an altar, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings.
- The children of Israel repented them for Benjamin their brother, for there was one tribe cut off from Israel that day. They had also made a great oath that whoever came not up to the Lord at Mizpeh would surely be put to death.

**[[Judges 21#^v7|Judges 21:7]]**

![[Judges 21#^v7]]

- The question of the chapter: how shall we do for wives for them that remain, seeing we have sworn by the Lord not to give them our daughters? They made a vow, they swore to the Lord, and the law says a sworn oath must be performed; that is why they could not simply turn around and give their daughters.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Numbers 30#^v1|Numbers 30:1-2]]**
    ![[Numbers 30#^v1]]
    ![[Numbers 30#^v2]]
    If a man vow a vow unto the Lord or swear an oath, he shall not break his word; he shall do according to all that proceedeth out of his mouth. This is the law Israel was bound by when they swore concerning their daughters.

- They searched which tribe came not up to Mizpeh, and behold, none had come from Jabeshgilead to the assembly. So under the great oath the congregation sent twelve thousand of the valiantest men to smite Jabeshgilead with the edge of the sword, destroying every male and every woman that had lain by man.
- Among the inhabitants of Jabeshgilead they found four hundred young virgins that had not known man by lying with any male, and brought them to the camp at Shiloh in the land of Canaan.
- Verse 12 is the precept for what it means in scripture to know somebody: to lie with them, meaning to have sex with them.

- The whole congregation sent to speak peaceably to the children of Benjamin at the rock Rimmon, and Benjamin came again. They gave them the wives saved alive of the women of Jabeshgilead, but they sufficed not; there were not enough for all the men that remained.
- The people repented them for Benjamin because the Lord had made a breach in the tribes of Israel, and the elders said there must be an inheritance for them that be escaped of Benjamin, that a tribe be not destroyed out of Israel.
- Verse 18 restates why they could not just give their own daughters: the children of Israel had sworn, saying, Cursed be he that giveth a wife to Benjamin. That is the same oath read in verse 7.

- The solution: there was a yearly feast of the Lord in Shiloh, so they commanded the children of Benjamin to lie in wait in the vineyards, and when the daughters of Shiloh came out to dance in the dances, every man was to catch him a wife of the daughters of Shiloh and go to the land of Benjamin.
- When the fathers or brethren came to complain, the elders would answer, Be favourable unto them for our sakes; because the fathers did not give their daughters, they would not be guilty of breaking the oath.
- The children of Benjamin did so and took wives according to their number of them that danced, then returned to their inheritance, repaired the cities, and dwelt in them.

- The children of Israel departed thence at that time, every man to his tribe, to his family, and to his inheritance.
- In those days there was no king in Israel; every man did that which was right in his own eyes. We see that same thing today: most of our people are still in that state, doing what is right in their own eyes. There is nothing new under the sun.
