# Numbers 1: The Numbering of the Armies of Israel

Read the chapter → [[Numbers 1]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Numbers 1|Numbers 1]] and 2, read in the same session as [[Leviticus 26|Leviticus 26]] and 27. The book of Numbers opens with the numbering of the armies of the Most High, the armies of the children of Israel: the census of the men of war in the wilderness of Sinai, and then the order of the camps about the tabernacle. Some think this reading is boring, but the Bible is not for them; there is beauty in everything read here, for the Most High was forming the armies of His people, and it is a beautiful thing that He found us fit and chose us to be His people.

**[[Numbers 1#^v1|Numbers 1:1-3]]**

![[Numbers 1#^v1]]
![[Numbers 1#^v2]]
![[Numbers 1#^v3]]

- The book of Numbers is the numbering of the armies of the Lord, the armies of the children of Israel. Moses and the elders numbered the men from twenty years old and upward, every one able to go forth to war, by their armies.
- Every man went to war unless the law released him: a man that had newly married, or built a house and not dedicated it, did not have to go; and a fearful man did not have to go, because that spirit of fear would jump on the other brothers.
- The Most High chose able men. In the book of Judges He cut the army down from the thousands to three hundred; and when Moses held up his hands over the battle, mighty men had to hold his arms up.

- With Moses and Aaron stood a man of every tribe, each one head of the house of his fathers: the renowned of the congregation, princes of the tribes, heads of thousands in Israel.
- The Most High always chose the men He wanted. It is not up to us to choose who is going to be leadership or who will be in this or that position; it is never us. Promotion cometh neither from the east, nor from the west, nor from the south: God is the judge, He putteth down one and setteth up another, and He elected these men as renowned men.

- The congregation declared their pedigrees after their families, by the house of their fathers. That is the Bible's rule of lineage: the line runs through the father, not the mother.
- Those called Jewish today reckon you Jewish by your mother, but that is not what the Bible says. The father plants the seed; the woman is the soil, and the seed planted in the soil grows into the child. The Most High established the pedigrees of the children of Israel by the house of their fathers.

- Tribe by tribe, by their generations, after their families, by the house of their fathers, every male from twenty years old and upward able to go forth to war was numbered: Reuben 46,500; Simeon 59,300; Gad 45,650; Judah 74,600; Issachar 54,400; Zebulun 57,400; Ephraim 40,500; Manasseh 32,200; Benjamin 35,400; Dan 62,700; Asher 41,500; Naphtali 53,400.
- The counting is by scores: a score is twenty, so two score is forty, threescore is sixty, and fourscore is twenty times four, which is eighty. Threescore and two thousand and seven hundred is 62,700.

- These are those whom Moses and Aaron numbered with the twelve princes of Israel, each one for the house of his fathers: all the men of war from twenty years old and upward came to six hundred thousand and three thousand and five hundred and fifty.
- This was the forming of Israel's armies in the wilderness, because the nation was going to have to be prepared to go to war with the nations ahead of them.

- The Levites pitched round about the tabernacle, that there be no wrath upon the congregation of the children of Israel; and the children of Israel did according to all that the Most High commanded Moses.

# Numbers 2: The Order of the Camps About the Tabernacle

Read the chapter → [[Numbers 2]]

**[[Numbers 2#^v1|Numbers 2:1-2]]**

![[Numbers 2#^v1]]
![[Numbers 2#^v2]]

- Every man of the children of Israel pitched by his own standard, with the ensign of his father's house, far off about the tabernacle of the congregation.
- This is the picture of the tribes set in their sections around the tabernacle, with the tabernacle in the middle and the pillar of fire coming down by night: the setting of the Lord's army in formation. That is how we are supposed to be set in formation; that is heavy.
