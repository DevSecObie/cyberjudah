# Numbers 35: The Cities of the Levites and the Cities of Refuge

Read the chapter → [[Numbers 35]]

## Introduction

This session covered Numbers 35-36 together with Deuteronomy 1-2, closing the book of Numbers and opening the book of Deuteronomy; the Deuteronomy chapters sit in their own chapter set. [[Numbers 35|Numbers 35]] gives the cities of the Levites and one of the more interesting subjects in the books of Moses, the cities of refuge, where the Most High taught Israel how to discern murder from manslaughter. [[Numbers 36|Numbers 36]] settles the inheritance of the daughters of Zelophehad and the law of marrying within the tribe.

**[[Numbers 35#^v1|Numbers 35:1-2]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v1]]
![[Numbers 35#^v2]]

- Moses commands the tribes to separate cities out of their own inheritance for the Levites to dwell in, with suburbs round about them.
- The Levites needed cities inside every tribe's territory because the Most High took them from among the children of Israel as a gift, to do the service of the tabernacle of the congregation. Every tribe needed that service done among them, so the Levites had to be spread out through all the children of Israel.
- The commandment was fulfilled when Israel came into the land: the heads of the fathers of the Levites came to Eleazar the priest and Joshua at Shiloh and claimed the cities the Lord commanded by the hand of Moses.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Numbers 18#^v6|Numbers 18:6]]**
    ![[Numbers 18#^v6]]
    The Levites are taken from among the children of Israel as a gift to do the service of the tabernacle; that is why every tribe gave them cities.
  - **[[Joshua 21#^v1|Joshua 21:1-3]]**
    ![[Joshua 21#^v1]]
    ![[Joshua 21#^v2]]
    ![[Joshua 21#^v3]]
    When Israel reached the land, the Levites claimed the commandment of Numbers 35 and the children of Israel gave them cities and suburbs out of their inheritance.

**[[Numbers 35#^v3|Numbers 35:3-5]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v3]]
![[Numbers 35#^v4]]
![[Numbers 35#^v5]]

- The cities were for the Levites to dwell in, and the suburbs were for their cattle, their goods, and all their beasts.
- The suburbs were measured out: a thousand cubits from the wall of the city round about, and two thousand cubits on the east, south, west, and north sides, with the city in the midst.

**[[Numbers 35#^v6|Numbers 35:6-8]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v6]]
![[Numbers 35#^v7]]
![[Numbers 35#^v8]]

- Among the Levite cities six were set apart for refuge, appointed for the manslayer to flee there, with forty and two more added: forty and eight cities in all, given from every tribe according to its inheritance, many from them that had many and few from them that had few.
- Why would the Lord permit a manslayer refuge? The Most High gave Moses the understanding of how to discern manslaughter from murder. For murder there was no refuge, but for manslaughter cities of refuge were given.
- The law itself was already instituted in [[Exodus 21|Exodus 21]]; [[Numbers 35|Numbers 35]] answers where the manslayer goes: the Most High appointed him a place to flee.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Exodus 21#^v12|Exodus 21:12-14]]**
    ![[Exodus 21#^v12]]
    ![[Exodus 21#^v13]]
    ![[Exodus 21#^v14]]
    He that smiteth a man that he die is put to death; but for him that lay not in wait, God delivering him into his hand, a place was appointed to flee; the presumptuous slayer is taken even from the altar to die.

**[[Numbers 35#^v9|Numbers 35:9-12]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v9]]
![[Numbers 35#^v10]]
![[Numbers 35#^v11]]
![[Numbers 35#^v12]]

- Killeth any person unawares means he did not mean to do it; it was an accident that resulted in a death. Accidental deaths are of the Lord, beyond our control.
- Refuge means a condition of being safe or sheltered from pursuit, danger, or trouble. The cities of refuge exist because the manslayer is being pursued: someone has the right to avenge the death of the one who was killed.
- The city of refuge keeps the manslayer alive until he stands before the congregation in judgment, so the trial can discern between murder and manslaughter before the avenger reaches him.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 19#^v6|Deuteronomy 19:6]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 19#^v6]]
    The avenger pursues the slayer while his heart is hot; without refuge he would overtake and slay a man who was not worthy of death.

**[[Numbers 35#^v13|Numbers 35:13-15]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v13]]
![[Numbers 35#^v14]]
![[Numbers 35#^v15]]

- Six cities of refuge stood, three on this side Jordan and three in the land of Canaan, and they served the children of Israel, the stranger, and the sojourner among them alike.
- The chapter lays out three categories when someone dies by the hand of another: he meant to hurt him but not kill him, yet he died, and that is murder; he hated him and killed him with premeditation, and that is murder; or he had no quarrel and never meant it, and that is manslaughter, not worthy of death.

**[[Numbers 35#^v16|Numbers 35:16-18]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v16]]
![[Numbers 35#^v17]]
![[Numbers 35#^v18]]

- Smite him with an instrument of iron, with a stone, or with a hand weapon of wood so that he die, and he is a murderer: even if the intent was only to hurt him and not to kill him, if he dies, the judgment is death.
- This is a precept for the street: the music culture glorifies slinging an iron, but the word says if you sling that iron and the brother dies, you are a murderer and your judgment is to die.

**[[Numbers 35#^v19|Numbers 35:19]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v19]]

- The revenger of blood himself slays the murderer when he meets him; there was no refuge and no mercy for murder.
- This is the law given to Israel at that time, an understanding of those judgments; it is not license for anyone to go about killing today.

**[[Numbers 35#^v20|Numbers 35:20-21]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v20]]
![[Numbers 35#^v21]]

- Laying of wait means he was lurking and stalking, waiting to catch him slipping because of hatred between them; that is premeditated murder.
- Even without a weapon, smiting him in enmity with the hand so that he dies is murder; he that smote him shall surely be put to death.

**[[Numbers 35#^v22|Numbers 35:22-24]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v22]]
![[Numbers 35#^v23]]
![[Numbers 35#^v24]]

- The law repeats its key words on purpose, the way legal statutes do, because the details decide the case: suddenly, without enmity, without laying of wait, and seeing him not.
- Seeing him not matters because it is nearly impossible to kill a man by accident while looking straight at him; the victim was out of sight when the thing flew and struck him.
- He was not his enemy and sought not his harm: there could be no documented past hatred, and he could not even have been seeking to harm him, since seeking only to harm still makes the death murder. Then the congregation judges between the slayer and the revenger of blood according to these judgments.

**[[Numbers 35#^v25|Numbers 35:25]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v25]]

- Once the congregation clears him of murder, it delivers the slayer out of the hand of the revenger and restores him to his city of refuge; he still must abide there.
- He stays within the city until the death of the high priest which was anointed with the holy oil, whoever held that office when the incident happened.

**[[Numbers 35#^v26|Numbers 35:26-28]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v26]]
![[Numbers 35#^v27]]
![[Numbers 35#^v28]]

- If the slayer steps outside the borders of his city of refuge before that high priest dies and the revenger of blood finds him there and kills him, the revenger is not guilty of blood, because the slayer was instructed to remain within the borders.
- After the death of the high priest the slayer returns to the land of his possession, and the avenger can no longer touch him.

**[[Numbers 35#^v29|Numbers 35:29-30]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v29]]
![[Numbers 35#^v30]]

- The murderer is put to death by the mouth of witnesses, plural: multiple people must testify, and their stories must match in the details for the murder to be established.
- One witness alone cannot cause a man to die, because a single witness could lie in the interest of the avenger and put an innocent man to death.
- The rule stood first for sins worthy of death and then had to become the principle for every matter: two or three witnesses establish any accusation of iniquity or sin.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 17#^v6|Deuteronomy 17:6]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 17#^v6]]
    At the mouth of two or three witnesses shall he that is worthy of death be put to death; one witness is never enough.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 19#^v15|Deuteronomy 19:15]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 19#^v15]]
    One witness shall not rise up against a man for any iniquity or any sin; at the mouth of two or three witnesses is the matter established.

**[[Numbers 35#^v31|Numbers 35:31-32]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v31]]
![[Numbers 35#^v32]]

- The judges could take no satisfaction for the life of a murderer guilty of death, and no satisfaction for the manslayer to come home before the death of the high priest; a guilty man cannot buy himself off.
- No matter who stands before the judge or what money is offered, if they are guilty of death they are guilty of death; a gift blinds the eyes of the wise and perverts the words of the righteous.
- Israel's judges later fell into exactly this: they became covetous and took bribes to clear the guilty and turn aside the poor in the gate.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 16#^v18|Deuteronomy 16:18-19]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 16#^v18]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 16#^v19]]
    Judges in all thy gates must judge with just judgment, respect no persons, and take no gift, for a gift doth blind the eyes of the wise.
  - **[[Amos 5#^v12|Amos 5:12]]**
    ![[Amos 5#^v12]]
    The judges of Israel came to afflict the just and take a bribe, turning aside the poor in the gate from their right.

**[[Numbers 35#^v33|Numbers 35:33-34]]**

![[Numbers 35#^v33]]
![[Numbers 35#^v34]]

- Blood defileth the land, and the land cannot be cleansed of the blood shed in it but by the blood of him that shed it; the murderer who escapes his judgment leaves the land polluted.
- That judgment was instituted back in the days of Noah, and Moses carried it forward in the statutes given to Israel, for the Lord himself dwells among the children of Israel.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 9#^v6|Genesis 9:6]]**
    ![[Genesis 9#^v6]]
    Whoso sheddeth man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed; the only cleansing of the land is the blood of the shedder.

# Numbers 36: The Inheritance of the Daughters of Zelophehad

Read the chapter → [[Numbers 36]]

**[[Numbers 36#^v1|Numbers 36:1-4]]**

![[Numbers 36#^v1]]
![[Numbers 36#^v2]]
![[Numbers 36#^v3]]
![[Numbers 36#^v4]]

- The chief fathers of the families of Gilead of the tribe of Manasseh bring an issue rising out of what was already ruled in Numbers 27: when Zelophehad died in the wilderness with no sons, the Lord ruled that his inheritance pass to his daughters.
- Their concern is real: if the daughters marry men of another tribe, their father's inheritance passes with them to the husband's tribe, and at the jubilee it is fixed to that tribe, so the tribe of Manasseh loses that land forever.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Numbers 27#^v3|Numbers 27:3-4]]**
    ![[Numbers 27#^v3]]
    ![[Numbers 27#^v4]]
    The daughters of Zelophehad pleaded that their father died in his own sin with no son, and asked why his name should be done away from among his family.
  - **[[Numbers 27#^v6|Numbers 27:6-8]]**
    ![[Numbers 27#^v6]]
    ![[Numbers 27#^v7]]
    ![[Numbers 27#^v8]]
    The Lord ruled the daughters spoke right: if a man die and have no son, his inheritance passes to his daughter; that ruling set up the question of Numbers 36.

**[[Numbers 36#^v5|Numbers 36:5-7]]**

![[Numbers 36#^v5]]
![[Numbers 36#^v6]]
![[Numbers 36#^v7]]

- The Most High affirms the tribe of the sons of Joseph: they have said well. The solution: the daughters of Zelophehad may marry whom they think best, only within the family of the tribe of their father.
- The purpose is stated plainly: so shall not the inheritance of the children of Israel remove from tribe to tribe; the marriage boundary here was instituted because of land inheritance.

**[[Numbers 36#^v8|Numbers 36:8-9]]**

![[Numbers 36#^v8]]
![[Numbers 36#^v9]]

- Every daughter that possessed an inheritance had to marry within her father's tribe, so every man of Israel would enjoy the inheritance of his fathers and no inheritance would remove from one tribe to another.
- The tribal restriction belonged to the time when Israel dwelt in the land; it existed because of inheritance. Today Israel has no inheritance in the lands of captivity, so the rule that still applies is marrying within the nation of Israel, not tribe-only marriage.
- No one today knows his exact tribe with certainty; a Judahite could have been dropped off anywhere in the captivity. Without the history of [[Numbers 36|Numbers 36]], [[Tobit 4#^v12|Tobit 4:12]] gets twisted into a doctrine that a man may only marry his own tribe, and that is a heresy born of not knowing why the law was given.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Tobit 4#^v12|Tobit 4:12]]**
    ![[Tobit 4#^v12]]
    Take a wife of the seed of thy fathers and not a strange woman; the seed-of-thy-fathers command still stands, and the tribe clause belonged to the inheritance law of Numbers 36.

**[[Numbers 36#^v10|Numbers 36:10-13]]**

![[Numbers 36#^v10]]
![[Numbers 36#^v11]]
![[Numbers 36#^v12]]
![[Numbers 36#^v13]]

- The daughters of Zelophehad did as the Lord commanded Moses: Mahlah, Tirzah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Noah married their father's brothers' sons, within the families of Manasseh, and their inheritance remained in the tribe of the family of their father.
- These are the commandments and the judgments which the Lord commanded by the hand of Moses in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho; with this verse the book of Numbers is completed.
