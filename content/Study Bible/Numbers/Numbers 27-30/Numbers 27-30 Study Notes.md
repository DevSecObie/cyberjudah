# Numbers 27: The Daughters of Zelophehad and the Ordination of Joshua

Read the chapter → [[Numbers 27]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Numbers 27|Numbers 27]] through 30: the inheritance won by the daughters of Zelophehad, the ordination of Joshua to lead after Moses, the continual burnt offering and the offerings of every sabbath, new moon, and feast, the high holy days of the seventh month, and the law of vows. To answer the inheritance question the whole of [[Numbers 36|Numbers 36]] was also read, precept upon precept, and the Q&A after the reading is folded into the verses it belongs to.

**[[Numbers 27#^v1|Numbers 27:1-4]]**

![[Numbers 27#^v1]]
![[Numbers 27#^v2]]
![[Numbers 27#^v3]]
![[Numbers 27#^v4]]

- The daughters of Zelophehad, of the tribe of Manasseh, stood before Moses, Eleazar the priest, the princes, and all the congregation at the door of the tabernacle.
- Their father died in the wilderness in his own sin; he was not in the company of Korah that gathered together against the Lord. He had no sons, only these five daughters.
- Their question was the question of the lineage: since the man puts the seed in the woman, if a man had only daughters, why should his name be done away from among his family? So they asked for a possession among the brethren of their father.

**[[Numbers 27#^v5|Numbers 27:5-7]]**

![[Numbers 27#^v5]]
![[Numbers 27#^v6]]
![[Numbers 27#^v7]]

- Moses brought their cause before the Lord. He did not do his own thing; he sought counsel and asked the Most High what to do in this instance.
- The Lord said the daughters of Zelophehad speak right: give them a possession of an inheritance among their father's brethren, and cause the inheritance of their father to pass unto them.

**[[Numbers 27#^v8|Numbers 27:8-11]]**

![[Numbers 27#^v8]]
![[Numbers 27#^v9]]
![[Numbers 27#^v10]]
![[Numbers 27#^v11]]

- Here the Lord set the order of inheritance for all Israel: if a man die and have no son, it passes to his daughter; if no daughter, to his brethren; if no brethren, to his father's brethren; and if his father had no brethren, to the kinsman that is next to him of his family. Every angle was covered so the inheritance stayed within the tribe.
- Israel always had questions, just as we do when we read, and the Lord put these statutes in place to answer them before they were asked.
- But what if a daughter who received the inheritance married into another tribe? You have to go precept upon precept: [[Numbers 36|Numbers 36]] takes up that very case with these same daughters. The chief fathers of Gilead brought it to Moses, and the word came back that the daughters of Zelophehad may marry to whom they think best, only to the family of the tribe of their father shall they marry.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Numbers 36#^v6|Numbers 36:6-7]]**
    ![[Numbers 36#^v6]]
    ![[Numbers 36#^v7]]
    Let them marry to whom they think best, only within the tribe of their father, so the inheritance does not remove from tribe to tribe.
  - **[[Numbers 36#^v8|Numbers 36:8-9]]**
    ![[Numbers 36#^v8]]
    ![[Numbers 36#^v9]]
    Every daughter that possesses an inheritance shall be wife unto one of the family of the tribe of her father, that every man enjoy the inheritance of his fathers.
  - **[[Numbers 36#^v10|Numbers 36:10-12]]**
    ![[Numbers 36#^v10]]
    ![[Numbers 36#^v11]]
    ![[Numbers 36#^v12]]
    The daughters of Zelophehad were married unto their father's brothers' sons, and the inheritance remained in the tribe of the family of their father.

- This is not saying no one ever married from Judah to Benjamin or Levi to Ephraim; it is showing that where an inheritance rode on the marriage, the woman married within her father's tribe.
- It also goes into marriage in the nation as a whole: there was never a case where an inheritance in Israel passed to an Edomite, an Ammonite, or a Moabite. The fathers from the beginning took wives of their own kindred, and interracial marriage is not of the scriptures.

# Numbers 28: The Continual Burnt Offering and the Offerings of the Feasts

- The Lord commanded the offerings made by fire to be observed in their due season: two lambs of the first year without spot, day by day, one lamb in the morning and the other lamb at even, a continual burnt offering which was ordained in mount Sinai.
- With it went a meat offering of a tenth deal of flour mingled with the fourth part of an hin of beaten oil, and a drink offering of the fourth part of an hin; in the holy place the strong wine was poured unto the Lord.
- The fire and the sacrifice continued morning and evening; it burned through the day and through the night, every single day.

**[[Numbers 28#^v9|Numbers 28:9-10]]**

- On the sabbath day two more lambs were offered, with their meat and drink offerings, the burnt offering of every sabbath, beside the continual burnt offering. That is four lambs every sabbath, with the drink offerings attached.
- Precept upon precept this answers what Christ told the Pharisees: the priests in the temple profane the sabbath and are blameless. Israel did not kindle fire for themselves on the sabbath, but this altar work was the ordinance of the Lord for every sabbath day.
- This is also why Christ had to come. In captivity we have no lambs, no farms, no temple, no most holy place, no veil, no high priest; the handwriting of ordinances that was against us was taken out of the way and nailed to his cross. Those who deny Christ and claim the Old Testament only are not keeping these sacrifices, and without Christ the whole law, drink offering to meat offering, still stands over them.

- That day is also the new moon, so the offering of the trumpets came beside the burnt offering of the month, the daily burnt offering, and their meat and drink offerings. In a span of fifteen days this month holds the memorial of blowing of trumpets coupled with the new moon, the day of atonement, and the feast of tabernacles.

- To afflict your soul is to fast. The fast the Lord has chosen is a day for a man to afflict his soul, to loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy burdens, to let the oppressed go free, and to break every yoke; that is why we still fast on this day once a year, and why we go out and teach on the day of atonement.
- We fast without the sacrifices, because the atonement is now Christ. He was brought as a lamb to the slaughter, wounded for our transgressions, and the Lord laid on him the iniquity of us all, just as the priest laid the sins of the nation on the sacrifice of [[Leviticus 16|Leviticus 16]]; that is why the veil rent in two when he died, and why John said, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.
- [[Isaiah 53|Isaiah 53]] cannot be speaking of David, who was already dead in Isaiah's day; it speaks of Jesus Christ, cut off out of the land of the living for the transgression of the people, the ultimate sacrifice.

# Numbers 30: The Law of Vows

Read the chapter → [[Numbers 30]]

**[[Numbers 30#^v1|Numbers 30:1-2]]**

![[Numbers 30#^v1]]
![[Numbers 30#^v2]]

- If a man vow a vow unto the Lord, or swear an oath to bind his soul with a bond, he shall not break his word; he shall do according to all that proceedeth out of his mouth.
- A vow is promising the Lord you will do a thing. Say a man vows, Lord, if you bring this to pass I will never eat swine again; he must keep that vow, and he cannot go back on it and simply ask forgiveness afterward.
- If thou shalt forbear to vow, it shall be no sin in thee; but that which is gone out of thy lips thou shalt keep and perform. Do not suffer thy mouth to cause thy flesh to sin, and do not say before the angel that it was an error.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Leviticus 27#^v2|Leviticus 27:2]]**
    ![[Leviticus 27#^v2]]
    When a man shall make a singular vow, the persons shall be for the Lord by thy estimation; the law already valued what was vowed.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 23#^v21|Deuteronomy 23:21-23]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 23#^v21]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 23#^v22]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 23#^v23]]
    When thou shalt vow a vow, thou shalt not slack to pay it, for it would be sin in thee; but if thou forbear to vow, it shall be no sin in thee.
  - **[[Ecclesiastes 5#^v3|Ecclesiastes 5:3-7]]**
    ![[Ecclesiastes 5#^v3]]
    ![[Ecclesiastes 5#^v4]]
    ![[Ecclesiastes 5#^v5]]
    ![[Ecclesiastes 5#^v6]]
    ![[Ecclesiastes 5#^v7]]
    Pay that which thou hast vowed; better not to vow than to vow and not pay; suffer not thy mouth to cause thy flesh to sin, neither say before the angel that it was an error.

- Christ took it further: swear not at all, neither by heaven, nor by the earth, nor by Jerusalem; let your communication be Yea, yea, Nay, nay, for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil. That is why we say the Lord's will: the Lord's will I will be there, the Lord's will I will teach that day.
- A vow made before you came into this truth does not stand, for you were not vowing to the God of Israel then; if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature, and old things are passed away. And if you vowed ignorantly and could not fulfill it, all manner of sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven unto men; only blasphemy against the Holy Ghost, speaking evil of this truth after receiving it, shall not be forgiven.
- Swearing on the Bible in the court system is another matter: be subject unto the higher powers, for the powers that be are ordained of God, and you also have the option to affirm; either way, tell the truth.

**[[Numbers 30#^v3|Numbers 30:3-5]]**

![[Numbers 30#^v3]]
![[Numbers 30#^v4]]
![[Numbers 30#^v5]]

- A daughter in her father's house in her youth who vows a vow is under her father's covering. If her father hears her vow and holds his peace, all her vows stand and every bond wherewith she hath bound her soul stands.
- But if her father disallow her in the day that he heareth, not any of her vows or bonds shall stand, and the Lord shall forgive her, because her father disallowed her. That shows the power the Most High put in the man of the house: a father can hear his daughter bind herself and say no, and the Lord releases her.

**[[Numbers 30#^v6|Numbers 30:6-8]]**

![[Numbers 30#^v6]]
![[Numbers 30#^v7]]
![[Numbers 30#^v8]]

- The same covering passes to the husband. If a woman with a husband vows, or utters ought out of her lips wherewith she binds her soul, and her husband hears it and holds his peace in the day that he hears it, her vows stand.
- But if her husband disallow her on the day that he heard it, he makes her vow of none effect, and the Lord shall forgive her. A wife could be binding herself to something she cannot perform, and her husband can step in and say, Lord, do not hear that vow, and the Most High releases her because of her husband.
- That is the power of the head of the house: the husband keeping the commandments stands between his wife and a bond that would jack her up. That is a reason to love your hedge, not to despise it.

**[[Numbers 30#^v9|Numbers 30:9]]**

![[Numbers 30#^v9]]

- Every vow of a widow, and of her that is divorced, wherewith they have bound their souls, shall stand against her. She has no father or husband to disallow it, so what she vows she must perform.
- The husband is a hedge of protection; where no hedge is, the possession is spoiled. The married woman has a covering that the widow and the divorced woman do not have.

**[[Numbers 30#^v10|Numbers 30:10-15]]**

- If a wife vowed in her husband's house and her husband heard it and held his peace and disallowed her not, then all her vows stand. Her husband may establish every vow and every binding oath to afflict the soul, or her husband may make it void.
- If he altogether holds his peace at her from day to day, then he establishes all her vows and confirms all her bonds, because he held his peace in the day that he heard them. Holding your peace is confirming it.
- But if he shall any ways make them void after that he hath heard them, then he shall bear her iniquity. He cannot hear her vow, hold his peace day after day, and then come later to void it; he had the power to stop it and did not, so the iniquity falls to him. And this runs one way: a wife cannot disallow or nullify her husband's vow.
- This is the order of the house: the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church, and he is the saviour of the body; so let the wives be subject to their own husbands in every thing, even in what is vowed in prayer.
