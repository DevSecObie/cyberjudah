# Deuteronomy 23: Who May Enter the Congregation and the Holy Camp

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 23]]

## Introduction

This session covers [[Deuteronomy 23|Deuteronomy 23]] through 26. Many of these laws should sound familiar, because we have already been through Exodus, Leviticus, and Numbers, and some of these laws are simply being repeated in Deuteronomy. Chapter 23 sets who may enter the congregation of the Lord and requires the camp to be holy because the Most High walks in the midst of it; chapter 24 covers the bill of divorcement, pledges, and mercy toward the poor; chapter 25 covers just judgment, the duty toward a dead brother's wife, and the memorial against Amalek; chapter 26 covers the firstfruits confession and the Most High avouching Israel to be his peculiar people.

# Deuteronomy 26: The Firstfruits Confession and a Peculiar People

Read the chapter → [[Deuteronomy 26]]

**[[Deuteronomy 26#^v5|Deuteronomy 26:5]]**

![[Deuteronomy 26#^v5]]

- The firstfruits confession spoken before the Lord begins: a Syrian ready to perish was my father, and he went down into Egypt and sojourned there with a few, and became there a nation, great, mighty, and populous.
- The father is Jacob: Jacob fled into the country of Syria, and Israel served for a wife, and for a wife he kept sheep, as we read in Genesis.

- This day the Lord thy God hath commanded thee to do these statutes and judgments: thou shalt keep and do them with all thine heart and with all thy soul.
- Thou hast avouched the Lord this day to be thy God: avouch means affirm or confirm. We confirm, thou art our God and we are going to be thy people, walking in his ways and keeping his statutes, his commandments, and his judgments, and hearkening unto his voice.

**[[Deuteronomy 26#^v18|Deuteronomy 26:18]]**

![[Deuteronomy 26#^v18]]

- The Lord hath avouched thee this day to be his peculiar people, as he hath promised thee: the Lord hath chosen Jacob unto himself, and Israel for his peculiar treasure.
- Ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people, that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light: we were called out of our sins into the light, and the light is the commandments and the law.
- Which in time past were not a people, but are now the people of God: we were called by other names, African American, Dominican, Negro, but now we are coming into the understanding that we are actually the children of Israel.
