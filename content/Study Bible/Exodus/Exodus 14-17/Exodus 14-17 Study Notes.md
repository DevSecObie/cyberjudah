# Exodus 14: The Salvation of the Lord at the Red Sea

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 14]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Exodus 14|Exodus 14]] through 17: the Red Sea opened and the Egyptians overthrown, the song of Moses, the manna and the sabbath proved in the wilderness, and the first war with Amalek. The session was a straight reading with brief commentary and questions afterward; the answers are folded in under the verses they belong to.

**[[Exodus 14#^v1|Exodus 14:1-5]]**

![[Exodus 14#^v1]]
![[Exodus 14#^v2]]
![[Exodus 14#^v3]]
![[Exodus 14#^v4]]
![[Exodus 14#^v5]]

- The Most High is not done with the Egyptians. He destroyed their firstborn and brought Israel out, but He kept one more judgment in store for that wicked nation, and He will do the same to the wicked nation that has done the same things to Israel today.
- The heart of Pharaoh and of his servants turned as soon as the people were gone: why have we done this, that we have let Israel go from serving us? The oppressor does not release his slaves gladly.

**[[Exodus 14#^v10|Exodus 14:10-12]]**

![[Exodus 14#^v10]]
![[Exodus 14#^v11]]
![[Exodus 14#^v12]]

- At the first sight of the Egyptians marching, Israel lost all faith. The Lord had done miracle after miracle before their eyes and brought them out with an high hand, yet at the first sign of danger they said He brought them out to die. Our people have always been a rebellious people of little faith: a very froward generation, children in whom is no faith.
- Let us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians: that is the same word our people speak today. Why are you stirring up trouble, why tell us to come out of the ways of Babylon; let us alone to serve the slave master, because serving him feels easier than having faith in the Most High.
- The same spirit was in Israel in the days of Christ: if we let him thus alone, all men will believe on him, and the Romans shall come. The fear of the oppressor outweighed belief in the miracles.
- In captivity the people wax old in mind and will not depart: woe to the bloody city, the prey departed not. Israel was the prey in Egypt and did not want to leave, the same as now.

**[[Exodus 14#^v13|Exodus 14:13-14]]**

![[Exodus 14#^v13]]
![[Exodus 14#^v14]]

- Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord. The Egyptians whom ye have seen today, ye shall see them again no more for ever; that sounds like the same word that will stand over Babylon.
- The Lord shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace. The battle was never Israel's to win by strength.

- The angel of God that went before the camp, the angel in the chariot whom we know to be Christ, removed and went behind them, standing between the camp of Egypt and the camp of Israel.
- The pillar was a cloud and darkness to the Egyptians but gave light by night to Israel, so that the one came not near the other; Egypt could not see clearly where they were going.
- The Lord caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night; Israel went through on dry ground, and the waters were a wall on the right hand and on the left.

- In the morning watch the Lord troubled the host of the Egyptians and took off their chariot wheels, so that they said, Let us flee from the face of Israel, for the Lord fighteth for them.
- Moses stretched out his hand and the sea returned in his strength. All that water that had stood up as a wall came down on the Egyptians, their chariots, and their horsemen, and consumed them. All praises to the Most High for loving His children enough to save us by such a mighty salvation.

**[[Exodus 14#^v29|Exodus 14:29-31]]**

![[Exodus 14#^v29]]
![[Exodus 14#^v30]]

- Israel saw the Egyptians dead upon the sea shore and saw the great work of the Lord, and the people feared the Lord and believed the Lord and His servant Moses.
- The Lord who brought us out the first time with great signs and wonders will save Israel out of this captivity the same way; that is why the brothers and sisters must have faith in these last days, because a trial is the true test of faith.
- We have to accept that our salvation was their destruction. The Red Sea opened for Israel and closed on Egypt in the same hour; whoever hates that the Most High saves one nation by destroying the others will have to come to grips with it.
- Even the ground testified: where water stood before, dry land appeared, and a green field came up out of the violent stream; grass sprang up at the bottom of the sea for the people to pass.

# Exodus 15: The Song of Moses: The Lord Is a Man of War

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 15]]

**[[Exodus 15#^v1|Exodus 15:1-3]]**

![[Exodus 15#^v1]]
![[Exodus 15#^v2]]
![[Exodus 15#^v3]]

- Then sang Moses and the children of Israel this song unto the Lord. The whole chapter is a song and a prophecy, speaking not only of the coming out of Egypt but in the future tense as well.
- The Lord is a man of war: the Lord is His name. He loves war against His enemies; our carnal minds cannot fathom it, but the word says what it says. He mustereth the host of the battle: He brought the Babylonians, the Persians and Medes, the Greeks, and the Romans against Israel, and He has stood in the midst of the wars of the nations ever since.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Isaiah 13#^v4|Isaiah 13:4]]**
    ![[Isaiah 13#^v4]]
    The Lord of hosts mustereth the host of the battle; He is a man of war.

**[[Exodus 15#^v14|Exodus 15:14-16]]**

![[Exodus 15#^v14]]
![[Exodus 15#^v15]]
![[Exodus 15#^v16]]

- The people shall hear and be afraid; the dukes of Edom shall be amazed, trembling shall take hold of the mighty men of Moab, and the inhabitants of Canaan shall melt away. This goes into the future, when the nations would hear what the Lord had done for Israel.
- Fear and dread fall upon them by the greatness of His arm till the people pass over which the Lord purchased.

**[[Exodus 15#^v20|Exodus 15:20-21]]**

![[Exodus 15#^v20]]
![[Exodus 15#^v21]]

- Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a timbrel, and all the women went out after her with timbrels and with dances, answering the song: sing ye to the Lord, for he hath triumphed gloriously.
- The prophetesses guided the women, teaching the young women to be sober and to love their husbands, and Miriam had her part in bringing Israel out of Egypt by keeping the sisters in order; that is the charge on the older sisters today toward the younger.

**[[Exodus 15#^v23|Exodus 15:23-26]]**

![[Exodus 15#^v23]]
![[Exodus 15#^v24]]
![[Exodus 15#^v25]]
![[Exodus 15#^v26]]

- At Marah the waters were bitter, and the people murmured; the Lord shewed Moses a tree, which when he had cast into the waters, the waters were made sweet.
- There He made a statute and an ordinance, and there He proved them: hearken diligently to the voice of the Lord thy God, do that which is right in His sight, and keep all His statutes, and none of the diseases of Egypt will be put on thee: for I am the Lord that healeth thee.

# Exodus 16: Manna From Heaven and the Sabbath Proved

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 16]]

**[[Exodus 16#^v1|Exodus 16:1-3]]**

![[Exodus 16#^v1]]
![[Exodus 16#^v2]]
![[Exodus 16#^v3]]

- The whole congregation murmured against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness: would to God we had died by the hand of the Lord in Egypt, when we sat by the flesh pots. The Lord commanded Moses to bring the people out, and when things got tough they made it his fault.
- That is us today. When the deliverance does not come easy, the people turn on the man sent to them instead of trusting the Most High who sent him.

**[[Exodus 16#^v4|Exodus 16:4-5]]**

![[Exodus 16#^v4]]
![[Exodus 16#^v5]]

- Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you; and the people shall gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no. The Lord proves His people by the daily portion.
- On the sixth day they were to prepare twice as much as they gathered daily; that word becomes important later in the chapter.

**[[Exodus 16#^v6|Exodus 16:6-8]]**

![[Exodus 16#^v6]]
![[Exodus 16#^v7]]
![[Exodus 16#^v8]]

- Your murmurings are not against us, but against the Lord. Moses and Aaron were nobody of themselves; complaining against the leadership the Most High sets is murmuring against the Most High Himself.
- That is a heavy point for today. A man says he has no problem with the Bible, only with leadership, but it was the word coming out that made him mad; the Lord hears every murmur of the complainers and doubters.

**[[Exodus 16#^v13|Exodus 16:13-18]]**

![[Exodus 16#^v13]]
![[Exodus 16#^v14]]
![[Exodus 16#^v15]]
![[Exodus 16#^v16]]
![[Exodus 16#^v17]]
![[Exodus 16#^v18]]

- At even the quails came up and covered the camp, and in the morning the manna lay like a small round thing, small as the hoar frost. They said one to another, It is manna: for they wist not what it was.
- An omer is a measure, a portion, like measuring cups of flour; every man gathered according to his eating, an omer for every man. He that gathered much had nothing over, and he that gathered little had no lack.

**[[Exodus 16#^v19|Exodus 16:19-21]]**

![[Exodus 16#^v19]]
![[Exodus 16#^v20]]
![[Exodus 16#^v21]]

- Moses said, Let no man leave of it till the morning; some hearkened not and left it, and it bred worms and stank, and Moses was wroth with them.
- They gathered it every morning, and when the sun waxed hot it melted; the portion was for the day the Lord gave it.

**[[Exodus 16#^v22|Exodus 16:22-30]]**

![[Exodus 16#^v22]]
![[Exodus 16#^v23]]
![[Exodus 16#^v24]]
![[Exodus 16#^v25]]
![[Exodus 16#^v26]]
![[Exodus 16#^v27]]
![[Exodus 16#^v28]]
![[Exodus 16#^v29]]
![[Exodus 16#^v30]]

- On the sixth day they gathered twice as much, and Moses said, Tomorrow is the rest of the holy sabbath unto the Lord: bake that which ye will bake today. This is the scripture we always use to show that we do not cook on the sabbath; everything is prepared friday before sundown, and the leftovers are eaten the next day.
- What was laid up did not stink, neither was there any worm therein, because the Lord had given the bread of two days on the sixth day; the same manna that bred worms on other nights was preserved for the sabbath.
- Some of the people still went out on the seventh day to gather and found none, and the Lord said, How long refuse ye to keep my commandments and my laws? The sabbath was already binding in the wilderness.
- Abide ye every man in his place on the seventh day is about not going out to gather food on the sabbath. Some foolish Israelites read verse 29 out of context and say you never have to leave your house or join a congregation on the sabbath; that is not what it says. The sabbath is a holy convocation, a large gathering, in all your dwellings, and we are to come together on it.
- For the brothers and sisters far from a congregation: you may have to travel and you may have to be the firstfruits in your area. The first assemblies met in houses, and that is how camps start; a man will drive hours to a club, so drive for the truth, and the Lord honors that faith.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Leviticus 23#^v1|Leviticus 23:1-3]]**
    ![[Leviticus 23#^v1]]
    ![[Leviticus 23#^v2]]
    ![[Leviticus 23#^v3]]
    The seventh day is a sabbath of rest, an holy convocation in all your dwellings; Exodus 16:29 does not undo the gathering.
  - **[[Romans 16#^v3|Romans 16:3-5]]**
    ![[Romans 16#^v3]]
    ![[Romans 16#^v4]]
    ![[Romans 16#^v5]]
    Greet the church that is in their house, and Epaenetus the firstfruits of Achaia; congregations begin with the diligent few.
  - **[[Matthew 6#^v33|Matthew 6:33]]**
    ![[Matthew 6#^v33]]
    Seek ye first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things shall be added; the Lord sees the sacrifice it takes to get to the congregation.

**[[Exodus 16#^v31|Exodus 16:31-36]]**

![[Exodus 16#^v31]]
![[Exodus 16#^v32]]
![[Exodus 16#^v33]]
![[Exodus 16#^v34]]
![[Exodus 16#^v35]]
![[Exodus 16#^v36]]

- The house of Israel called the name thereof Manna: like coriander seed, white, and the taste of it like wafers made with honey. An omer is the tenth part of an ephah.
- Aaron laid up an omer of manna before the Testimony, to be kept for generations, that they may see the bread wherewith the Lord fed Israel in the wilderness. The manna that spoiled overnight on other days was preserved for generations; that is the power of the Most High.
- Israel ate manna forty years, until they came unto the borders of the land of Canaan.
- Stop reading over the power in these books with the colonizer's picture of the fathers in mind. Esau paints our fathers as a backward people in rags, but the Most High shewed mighty wonders through Genesis and Exodus; stop believing that image.

# Exodus 17: Water Out of the Rock and War With Amalek

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 17]]

**[[Exodus 17#^v1|Exodus 17:1-4]]**

![[Exodus 17#^v1]]
![[Exodus 17#^v2]]
![[Exodus 17#^v3]]
![[Exodus 17#^v4]]

- At Rephidim there was no water, and the people did chide with Moses: wherefore is this that thou hast brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our cattle with thirst? At the first sign of trial the people jumped off the Lord's bandwagon again, the same as we do today when tribulation comes.
- Moses cried unto the Lord, What shall I do unto this people? they be almost ready to stone me. They were ready to kill the prophet over water, blaming the brother for what only the Most High controls; we have to build our faith up like Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, who trusted the Lord even when trials came.

**[[Exodus 17#^v5|Exodus 17:5-7]]**

![[Exodus 17#^v5]]
![[Exodus 17#^v6]]
![[Exodus 17#^v7]]

- The Lord stood before Moses upon the rock in Horeb; Moses smote the rock, and water came out of it in the sight of the elders of Israel.
- He called the place Massah and Meribah, temptation and chiding, because they tempted the Lord, saying, Is the Lord among us, or not? That is wickedness: He had just made Egypt dark, slain their firstborn, and sent His angel in the chariot to guide us, and the people still asked whether He was with them. The mercy of the Most High in bearing that is beyond us.

**[[Exodus 17#^v8|Exodus 17:8-13]]**

![[Exodus 17#^v8]]
![[Exodus 17#^v9]]
![[Exodus 17#^v10]]
![[Exodus 17#^v11]]
![[Exodus 17#^v12]]
![[Exodus 17#^v13]]

- Then came Amalek, and fought with Israel in Rephidim. Amalek was the first nation to come against Israel after Egypt; Amalek was Esau's grandson, born to Eliphaz of Timna the concubine, a duke in the land of Edom.
- When Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed; when he let down his hand, Amalek prevailed. Aaron and Hur stayed up his hands until the going down of the sun. Moses was about eighty years old holding a rod above his head all day; the fathers were mighty men, stronger than we are today.
- The sign shewed the people whose hand their life was in: Moses is the man to hearken to, so do not speak against him. And Joshua discomfited Amalek with the edge of the sword; Joshua was a mighty warrior even then.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 36#^v12|Genesis 36:12]]**
    ![[Genesis 36#^v12]]
    Timna bare to Eliphaz Amalek; Amalek is the grandson of Esau, a duke of Edom.

**[[Exodus 17#^v14|Exodus 17:14-16]]**

![[Exodus 17#^v14]]
![[Exodus 17#^v15]]
![[Exodus 17#^v16]]

- Write this for a memorial in a book, and rehearse it in the ears of Joshua: for I will utterly put out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven. Moses wrote it, and [[Deuteronomy 25|Deuteronomy 25]] is that rehearsal: remember what Amalek did unto thee by the way.
- Amalek smote the hindmost, all that were feeble, when Israel was faint and weary, and he feared not God. He did not meet the mighty men in the front; he went to the back to slay the women, the children, and the old ones. That same spirit works against the nation's young and old today.
- The Lord hath sworn that the Lord will have war with Amalek from generation to generation. By the time Moses rehearsed it in Deuteronomy, forty years had passed and the children of Amalek had done nothing themselves; hundreds of years after that the Lord sent Saul by Samuel to smite Amalek for what was done coming out of Egypt. God be true and every man a liar: the nations do not get away with what they did to His people, and the judgment carries to the seed.
- Saul did not finish Agag's line, and in Esther's day Haman the Agagite of that same seed rose against Israel; the war truly runs from generation to generation.
- Today that seed says they are Jews and are not, but are the synagogue of Satan, robbing Israel of identity and prospering off the oppression. The Lord says He will make them come and worship before thy feet, and to know that I have loved thee.
- The whole world is set up between Jacob and Esau, and Esau is the end of this world, the so-called white man, with Amalek leading; it is not speaking of the Arab man, for if Ishmael had a rulership still to come, Israel would have another captivity to enter, and Christ has already redeemed us from the curse of the law.
- The enmity was set from the garden between the serpent's seed and the woman's seed. They bruised Christ's heel, but at the end of the world Jacob bruises the head: the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly.
