# Exodus 30: The Altar of Incense and the Holy Anointing Oil

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 30]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Exodus 30|Exodus 30]] through 33: the altar of incense, the ransom of souls, the laver of brass, the holy anointing oil and perfume, Bezaleel and Aholiab filled with wisdom to build, the sabbath as a sign between the Most High and Israel, the golden calf at Horeb, and Moses pleading for the presence of the Lord and seeing his glory pass by. This was a reading session with teaching along the way; the questions answered at the end are merged under the verses they belong to.

- The altar to burn incense upon was made of shittim wood, a cubit in length and a cubit in breadth, foursquare, and two cubits high, with horns of the same. A cubit has been read to be about seventeen inches, so this stood about waist high.
- It was overlaid with pure gold, the top, the sides round about, and the horns, with a crown of gold round about, two golden rings under the crown, and staves of shittim wood overlaid with gold. The staves through the rings were how the priests bore it as Israel moved through the wilderness.

- The altar was put before the vail that is by the ark of the testimony, before the mercy seat where the Most High would meet with Moses.
- Aaron burned sweet incense on it every morning when he dressed the lamps, and again at even when he lighted the lamps, a perpetual incense before the Lord throughout the generations; it burned continually, morning and evening.
- No strange incense went on it, and no burnt sacrifice, meat offering, or drink offering; this altar was for one specific incense only. The service had to be exactly as prescribed, and deviation carried death, which is a reason to thank the Lord for Christ.

**[[Exodus 30#^v10|Exodus 30:10]]**

- Aaron made an atonement upon the horns of it once in a year with the blood of the sin offering of atonements; it is most holy unto the Lord. This altar served the day of atonement.
- On the tenth day of the seventh month Israel afflicted their souls and did no work at all, and the priest made an atonement for the sanctuary, the tabernacle, the altar, the priests, and all the people of the congregation, to cleanse them from all their sins before the Lord; an everlasting statute, once a year.
- The priests went always into the first tabernacle accomplishing the service of God, but into the second went the high priest alone once every year, not without blood. Christ offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, so the day of atonement is still kept by afflicting the soul and doing no work, but without the animal sacrifice, because Christ took the place of the animal.

- The ransom was half a shekel after the shekel of the sanctuary, from every one that passed among them that were numbered, from twenty years old and above; the rich gave no more and the poor gave no less, to make an atonement for their souls.

The gold, the wood, the curtains, and the fabrics all had to be bought and kept up, and the tabernacle moved with the nation through the wilderness; that is the same reason offerings are gathered in the congregation today, because the house of the Most High cannot be built or kept without it.

- Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep, for it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations, that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you. Keeping the weekly sabbath and the feast days shows that Israel are his children and he is their God; the nations do not have this sabbath, and adopted Sunday worship is not the true sabbath given to the children of Israel.
- Every one that defileth the sabbath shall surely be put to death. The stoning was done away in Christ, but the judgment for breaking the sabbath still stands, and people still die on the sabbath day, Friday sundown to Saturday sundown; the Most High is justified, because it is his sign between him and his people.
- Six days may work be done, but in the seventh is the sabbath of rest, holy to the LORD. It is more than a day of rest and congregating; it is the sign of who Israel's God is, which is why saying the sabbath is done away takes away the very sign between Israel and God.

- From the questions: eating is allowed on the sabbath, but not cooking. What is baked and seethed is prepared the day before and laid up, so the sabbath meal is leftovers or simple food; no microwaving, no coffee pots, no kindling the cooking fire.

- The Lord gives no man license to sin, but Christ is Lord of the sabbath and brought grace for necessities out of our control, a blown tire or an empty tank on the way to keep the sabbath, or medicine when the body is in danger; it is lawful to do well on the sabbath days.

- When the people saw that Moses delayed to come down out of the mount, they gathered unto Aaron: Up, make us gods, which shall go before us; for as for this Moses, we wot not what is become of him. Any time deliverance did not come when Israel wanted it, the nation rebelled, and Moses and the leadership got the blame; the same spirit is against the leadership of Israel to this day.
- Aaron said break off the golden earrings which are in the ears of your wives, of your sons, and of your daughters. Men and women both wore earrings in the scriptures, and it is not sin.
- Aaron received the gold, fashioned it with a graving tool, and made a molten calf, and they said, These be thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt. Aaron saw the miracles in Egypt and was set up as high priest, yet he succumbed to the pressure of the people; that is why the Lord chose Moses specifically to lead, because Moses could not be moved by any means.
- Aaron built an altar before it and made proclamation, To morrow is a feast to the LORD; and the people rose up early, offered burnt offerings and peace offerings, sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play. Israel kept provoking God, and the Lord still brought the nation into the promised land as he promised Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

- The Lord told Moses, Go, get thee down; for thy people, which thou broughtest out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. They turned aside quickly out of the way, made a molten calf, worshipped it, and sacrificed thereunto. Not forty days gone, and they had already left his commandments.
- The spirit that was in Israel in Egypt was still in Israel when they left. It is the same coming into this truth: the evil carried in from the world will corrupt you and turn you from the Lord if it is not put off.
- I have seen this people, and, behold, it is a stiffnecked people. That has been Israel's reputation with the Father from the beginning; without the laws of God we are a carnal, rebellious people, which is why these last days call for humbleness and repentance, not bringing the world's rhetoric into the congregation.
- Now therefore let me alone, that my wrath may wax hot against them, and that I may consume them: and I will make of thee a great nation. Moses was of the tribe of Levi, of the seed of Jacob, so the Lord could have started over with him.

That Angel is Christ.
- I will not go up in the midst of thee, for thou art a stiffnecked people, lest I consume thee in the way.
- From the questions: a land flowing with milk and honey is not a magical land of milk. It means a plenteous, fertile land of great landscape, valleys, grass, and fields, with cattle and goats grazing for the milk, and flowers and plants in abundance for the bees to make the honey; a land blessed as the days of heaven upon the earth.

- Moses took the tabernacle and pitched it without the camp, afar off from the camp, and called it the Tabernacle of the congregation. The Lord dwelt there, and he would not sit in the midst of a camp that had corrupted itself; every one which sought the LORD went out unto the tabernacle without the camp.
- When Moses went out unto the tabernacle, all the people rose up and stood every man at his tent door and looked after Moses until he was gone in; and the cloudy pillar descended and stood at the door of the tabernacle, and the LORD talked with Moses, and all the people rose up and worshipped, every man in his tent door.
- The LORD spake unto Moses face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend. That is not Moses looking eye to eye at the Lord; it means there was no mediator between Moses and the Lord, for Moses himself was the mediator between the Lord and the people, and the Lord spoke to him directly.
