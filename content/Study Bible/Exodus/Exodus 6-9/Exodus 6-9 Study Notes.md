# Exodus 6: The Promise Renewed and the Generations of Moses and Aaron

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 6]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Exodus 6|Exodus 6]] through 9: the Most High renews His promise to bring Israel out of Egypt, the genealogy of Reuben, Simeon, and Levi of whom came Moses and Aaron, Aaron's rod turned serpent, and the first seven plagues upon Egypt. The thread of the session is that through each plague the Most High was mocking an idol god of Egypt: Sobek the god of the rivers, Heqet the god of the frogs, Beelzebub the lord of flies and uncleanness, Hathor the goddess of cattle, Sekhmet the goddess of health and beauty, and Nut and Geb the gods of the sky and the earth.

**[[Exodus 6#^v1|Exodus 6:1]]**

![[Exodus 6#^v1]]

- The Lord renews His promise: Pharaoh will not merely let Israel go, but with a strong hand he shall drive them out of his land.

**[[Exodus 6#^v2|Exodus 6:2-3]]**

![[Exodus 6#^v2]]
![[Exodus 6#^v3]]

- The Most High appeared unto Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob by the name of God Almighty, but by His name JEHOVAH was He not known to them. Some say Jehovah and some say Yahawah; you can say whichever name you want to say.

**[[Exodus 6#^v4|Exodus 6:4-5]]**

![[Exodus 6#^v4]]
![[Exodus 6#^v5]]

- The covenant stands to give Israel the land of Canaan, the land of their pilgrimage wherein they were strangers; and the Most High heard the groaning of the children of Israel whom the Egyptians kept in bondage.
- The land of Canaan is in the land of Ham. So when people say we are Africans, the answer is that we come from Northeast Africa, from that continent, but we are not Africans; our land sits on the continent, and we are the Israelites.

**[[Exodus 6#^v9|Exodus 6:9-12]]**

![[Exodus 6#^v9]]
![[Exodus 6#^v10]]
![[Exodus 6#^v11]]
![[Exodus 6#^v12]]

- The children of Israel hearkened not unto Moses for anguish of spirit and for cruel bondage.
- Moses answered, Behold, the children of Israel have not hearkened unto me; how then shall Pharaoh hear me, who am of uncircumcised lips? This is why Aaron was given him for a mouthpiece in the next chapter.

**[[Exodus 6#^v16|Exodus 6:16-20]]**

![[Exodus 6#^v16]]
![[Exodus 6#^v17]]
![[Exodus 6#^v18]]
![[Exodus 6#^v19]]
![[Exodus 6#^v20]]

- Chapter 6 is dealing with the genealogy: the families of Reuben, Simeon, and Levi, and out of Levi came Moses and Aaron. Levi begat Kohath, Kohath begat Amram, and Amram took Jochebed to wife, and she bare him Aaron and Moses.

**[[Exodus 6#^v26|Exodus 6:26-27]]**

![[Exodus 6#^v26]]
![[Exodus 6#^v27]]

- These are that Aaron and Moses to whom the Lord said, Bring out the children of Israel from the land of Egypt; these are they which spake to Pharaoh king of Egypt.

# Exodus 7: Aaron's Rod and the River Turned to Blood

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 7]]

**[[Exodus 7#^v1|Exodus 7:1-2]]**

![[Exodus 7#^v1]]
![[Exodus 7#^v2]]

- The Lord made Moses a god to Pharaoh, and Aaron his brother was his prophet. Moses was as God and Aaron was the mouthpiece, because Moses kept saying he was of uncircumcised lips.
- This is why you do not move in a spirit of speaking against your leadership. Airing your complaints in front of everybody instead of bringing them in order only shows what type of spirit you are moving in; it is completely out of order.

**[[Exodus 7#^v8|Exodus 7:8-12]]**

![[Exodus 7#^v8]]
![[Exodus 7#^v9]]
![[Exodus 7#^v10]]
![[Exodus 7#^v11]]
![[Exodus 7#^v12]]

- It was Aaron who cast down the rod before Pharaoh, and it became a serpent. A lot of people to this day say it was Moses with the rod, because they do not read the Bible; Moses was telling Aaron what to do, and it was Aaron's rod that turned into the serpent.
- The magicians of Egypt did in like manner with their enchantments, and their rods became serpents; but Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods.

**[[Exodus 7#^v19|Exodus 7:19-22]]**

![[Exodus 7#^v19]]
![[Exodus 7#^v20]]
![[Exodus 7#^v21]]
![[Exodus 7#^v22]]

- All the waters of Egypt were turned to blood, in the streams, the rivers, the ponds, the pools, and in vessels of wood and vessels of stone; the fish died and the river stank.
- With this first plague the Most High was mocking Sobek, the Egyptian idol god of the rivers, showing that he could not defend his own waters.
- The magicians did so with their enchantments, but they could only copy the plague; they could not turn the water back clear. Seven days were fulfilled after the Lord had smitten the river.
- Many Egyptologists claim this never happened, because to admit it happened is to admit that the idols they claim to be gods and goddesses were powerless before our God, and that those idols do not exist at all.

# Exodus 8: Frogs, Lice, and Swarms of Flies

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 8]]

**[[Exodus 8#^v5|Exodus 8:5-8]]**

![[Exodus 8#^v5]]
![[Exodus 8#^v6]]
![[Exodus 8#^v7]]
![[Exodus 8#^v8]]

- Aaron stretched out his hand over the waters, and the frogs came up and covered the land of Egypt. With this second plague the Most High was mocking Heqet, the Egyptian idol god of the frogs.
- The magicians did so with their enchantments and brought up frogs, but just as with the water they could only add to the plague; they could not take the frogs away.
- So Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron and said, Entreat the Lord that he may take away the frogs. Why did Pharaoh not call on Heqet, the god of frogs? He had to go to the Lord.

**[[Exodus 8#^v16|Exodus 8:16-19]]**

![[Exodus 8#^v16]]
![[Exodus 8#^v17]]
![[Exodus 8#^v18]]
![[Exodus 8#^v19]]

- All the dust of the land became lice in man and in beast throughout all the land of Egypt. Imagine that: the dust of the whole land of Egypt turned to lice.
- The magicians did so with their enchantments to bring forth lice, but this time they could not; and the magicians said unto Pharaoh, This is the finger of God. Yet Pharaoh's heart was hardened.
- With the third and fourth plagues, the lice and the swarms of flies, the Most High was mocking Beelzebub, the idol lord of flies and uncleanness.

**[[Exodus 8#^v21|Exodus 8:21-24]]**

![[Exodus 8#^v21]]
![[Exodus 8#^v22]]
![[Exodus 8#^v23]]
![[Exodus 8#^v24]]

- These were no ordinary flies. They were biting and killing men and animals; the scripture records that the bitings of flies killed and no remedy was found.
- The Lord severed the land of Goshen, where His people dwelt, so that no swarms of flies were there, and put a division between His people and Pharaoh's people, to the end Pharaoh may know that the Lord is in the midst of the earth.
- Goshen was Israel's own quarter in Egypt, where we had our own houses and our own cattle; it was given to us in the days of Joseph, as we read in the book of Genesis.

  - **[[Genesis 47#^v6|Genesis 47:6]]**
    ![[Genesis 47#^v6]]
    Goshen, the best of the land, was given to Israel in Joseph's day; that is where the children of Israel dwelt apart from Egypt.

**[[Exodus 8#^v25|Exodus 8:25-32]]**

![[Exodus 8#^v25]]
![[Exodus 8#^v26]]
![[Exodus 8#^v27]]
![[Exodus 8#^v28]]

![[Exodus 8#^v30]]

Israel's sacrifices were the very animals Egypt held sacred, so Israel must go three days' journey into the wilderness.
- Moses warned Pharaoh, Let not Pharaoh deal deceitfully any more. The Lord removed the swarms so that there remained not one; and Pharaoh hardened his heart at this time also, and would not let the people go.

# Exodus 9: Murrain, Boils, and Hail; Goshen Set Apart

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 9]]

**[[Exodus 9#^v1|Exodus 9:1-7]]**

![[Exodus 9#^v1]]
![[Exodus 9#^v2]]
![[Exodus 9#^v3]]
![[Exodus 9#^v4]]
![[Exodus 9#^v5]]
![[Exodus 9#^v6]]
![[Exodus 9#^v7]]

- A grievous murrain fell upon the cattle of Egypt, upon the horses, asses, camels, oxen, and sheep; and with this fifth plague the Most High was mocking Hathor, the Egyptian idol goddess of cattle.
- The Lord severed between the cattle of Israel and the cattle of Egypt: all the cattle of Egypt died, but of the cattle of the children of Israel died not one. Pharaoh sent, and behold, there was not one of the cattle of the Israelites dead; and his heart was hardened.
- Picture what was taking place in the land: flies all over Egypt, but where Israel dwelt there were no flies; the water was blood, but where Israel dwelt the water was good; the cattle of Egypt died, but Israel's cattle lived. The plagues touched Pharaoh, his servants, and the Egyptians only; they did not touch the Israelites at all, because this was the Lord redeeming the Israelites.
- The Egyptians could not pray to their idols to help them, because the Lord was mocking them, showing them that Hathor does not exist, Sobek does not exist, Heqet does not exist, and Beelzebub does not exist.

**[[Exodus 9#^v8|Exodus 9:8-12]]**

![[Exodus 9#^v8]]
![[Exodus 9#^v9]]
![[Exodus 9#^v10]]
![[Exodus 9#^v11]]
![[Exodus 9#^v12]]

- Moses sprinkled ashes of the furnace toward heaven, and it became a boil breaking forth with blains upon man and upon beast throughout all the land of Egypt.
- The magicians could not stand before Moses because of the boils, for the boils were upon the magicians themselves and upon all the Egyptians. With this sixth plague the Most High was mocking Sekhmet, the Egyptian idol goddess of health and beauty.

**[[Exodus 9#^v13|Exodus 9:13-17]]**

![[Exodus 9#^v13]]
![[Exodus 9#^v14]]
![[Exodus 9#^v15]]
![[Exodus 9#^v16]]
![[Exodus 9#^v17]]

- Some mock and ask, What type of God says let my people go and then keeps hardening Pharaoh's heart so he will not let them go? That is exactly the God we serve, and the Lord tells you Himself why He did it: for this cause have I raised thee up, for to shew in thee my power, and that my name may be declared throughout all the earth.
- The same thing is happening now, right here in this captivity. The powers of Babylon know what is being taught, yet the Lord hardens their hearts so that they keep passing laws and pressing against His people, so that the Most High can show His power again, just as He did in Egypt.

**[[Exodus 9#^v18|Exodus 9:18-21]]**

![[Exodus 9#^v18]]
![[Exodus 9#^v19]]
![[Exodus 9#^v20]]
![[Exodus 9#^v21]]

- Before the hail the Lord gave warning: gather thy cattle and all that thou hast in the field, for upon every man and beast found in the field the hail shall come down, and they shall die.
- He that feared the word of the Lord among the servants of Pharaoh made his servants and his cattle flee into the houses; they began to recognize the God of heaven and earth, the God of the Israelites.
- He that regarded not the word of the Lord left his servants and his cattle in the field. Many in Egypt held certain idols in more favor than others, and these trusted that Nut, the idol goddess of the sky, would keep them safe.

**[[Exodus 9#^v22|Exodus 9:22-26]]**

![[Exodus 9#^v22]]
![[Exodus 9#^v23]]
![[Exodus 9#^v24]]
![[Exodus 9#^v25]]
![[Exodus 9#^v26]]

- This was no regular hail. There was hail, and fire mingled with the hail, very grievous, such as there was none like it in all the land of Egypt since it became a nation; the fire ran along upon the ground.
- With this seventh plague the Most High was mocking Nut and Geb, the Egyptian idol gods of the sky and the earth, striking from the sky and burning along the ground they were supposed to rule.
- Only in the land of Goshen, where the children of Israel were, was there no hail. The Lord does not play; our God is a terrible God.

**[[Exodus 9#^v27|Exodus 9:27-30]]**

![[Exodus 9#^v27]]
![[Exodus 9#^v28]]
![[Exodus 9#^v29]]
![[Exodus 9#^v30]]

- Pharaoh said, I have sinned this time: the Lord is righteous, and I and my people are wicked. This time? So all the other times, none of that was sin? Only now, under the thunder and hail, does he see it.
- Moses answered that the thunder and hail would cease, that thou mayest know how that the earth is the Lord's; but as for thee and thy servants, I know that ye will not yet fear the Lord God.

**[[Exodus 9#^v31|Exodus 9:31-32]]**

![[Exodus 9#^v31]]
![[Exodus 9#^v32]]

- The flax and the barley were smitten, for the barley was in the ear and the flax was bolled; but the wheat and the rie were not smitten, for they were not grown up.

**[[Exodus 9#^v34|Exodus 9:34-35]]**

![[Exodus 9#^v34]]
![[Exodus 9#^v35]]

- When Pharaoh saw that the rain and the hail and the thunders were ceased, he sinned yet more and hardened his heart, he and his servants. He still had the wheat and the rie in the ground, because they were not grown up yet, and that is part of why he went right back to hardening himself.
