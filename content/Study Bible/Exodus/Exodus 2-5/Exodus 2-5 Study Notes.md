# Exodus 2: The Birth of Moses

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 2]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Exodus 2|Exodus 2]] through 5: the birth of Moses and his flight to Midian, the angel in the burning bush, the three signs given to Moses and the circumcision of his son, and the first appearance before Pharaoh with the answer, Let my people go. The session opened with prayer out of [[Psalms 56|Psalms 56]], be merciful unto me, O God, for man would swallow me up; in God I have put my trust, I will not fear what flesh can do unto me; and with thanksgiving for the reawakening prophesied in [[Ezekiel 37|Ezekiel 37]], that the people of the Most High will return unto Him and stand upon their feet, an exceeding great army.

**[[Exodus 2#^v11|Exodus 2:11-12]]**

![[Exodus 2#^v11]]
![[Exodus 2#^v12]]

- When Moses was grown he went out unto his brethren the Israelites and looked on their burdens, and he spied an Egyptian smiting an Hebrew, one of his brethren. Moses knew the Israelites were his brothers; he knew he was not of the Egyptian lineage and that there was something different about him.
- He looked this way and that way, and when he saw that there was no man, he slew the Egyptian and hid him in the sand. He did not just marvel at the oppression; he used his position for his people, unlike many who hold a position of power today and will not risk it for their people. Moses is the ultimate revolutionary, as Christ is.

In Egypt his head was shaved, his beard cut, his dress and customs Egyptian; he laid all of it down to be what he was called to be, an Israelite.
- The same claim stands now: the remnant does not want the names of the captivity but the natural heritage of Israel, esteeming the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures of this land.

Go, get thee to them of the captivity, and speak unto them, whether they will hear or whether they will forbear. That stubbornness is itself a mark of who the Israelites are among the nations today.

- The first sign: the rod cast on the ground became a serpent, and Moses fled from before it; he caught it by the tail and it became a rod in his hand.
- The sign was given that they may believe that the LORD God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, hath appeared unto thee. Israel would not believe on a man's word alone; it took a miracle, because we were a people of little faith.

**[[Exodus 4#^v6|Exodus 4:6-8]]**

![[Exodus 4#^v6]]

- The second sign: Moses put his hand into his bosom, and when he took it out, his hand was leprous as snow; he put it in again, and it was turned again as his other flesh.
- This was a miracle precisely because Moses was a very dark-skinned man; the daughters of the priest of Midian had taken him for an Egyptian. The hand came out white as snow and went back to his natural dark skin; had he been white already there would have been no sign in it.
- If they will not believe the first sign, they will believe the voice of the latter sign; the magicians of Egypt could imitate serpents, but not this.

Nothing that defileth enters the kingdom.
- The Egyptians did not practice circumcision; they were a nation of uncleanness, and the covenant was between the Lord and Israel: after the doings of the land of Egypt, wherein ye dwelt, shall ye not do.

# Exodus 5: Let My People Go

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 5]]

**[[Exodus 5#^v1|Exodus 5:1-2]]**

![[Exodus 5#^v1]]
![[Exodus 5#^v2]]

- Moses and Aaron went in and told Pharaoh, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto me in the wilderness. That feast was the Passover.
- Pharaoh said, Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice to let Israel go? I know not the LORD, neither will I let Israel go. The Lord had already said He would harden Pharaoh's heart.

**[[Exodus 5#^v3|Exodus 5:3]]**

![[Exodus 5#^v3]]

- They said, The God of the Hebrews hath met with us: let us go, we pray thee, three days' journey into the desert, and sacrifice unto the LORD our God; lest he fall upon us with pestilence, or with the sword.

**[[Exodus 5#^v4|Exodus 5:4-5]]**

![[Exodus 5#^v4]]
![[Exodus 5#^v5]]

- The king of Egypt said, Wherefore do ye, Moses and Aaron, let the people from their works? get you unto your burdens. That is the standing complaint of the oppressor: the prophets are pulling the people from their labor and getting their hopes up that their God will deliver them.

**[[Exodus 5#^v6|Exodus 5:6-9]]**

![[Exodus 5#^v6]]
![[Exodus 5#^v7]]
![[Exodus 5#^v8]]
![[Exodus 5#^v9]]

- Pharaoh commanded the taskmasters the same day: ye shall no more give the people straw to make brick; let them go and gather straw for themselves; and the tale of the bricks ye shall lay upon them, ye shall not diminish ought thereof. For they be idle; therefore they cry, saying, Let us go and sacrifice to our God.
- Let there more work be laid upon the men, that they may labour therein; and let them not regard vain words. The same tactic runs in this captivity: burdens are increased and extra wages dangled for working the Sabbath and the feast days, to wear the people down so they will not regard the words of the Most High.
- The work of the Lord is the true calling over any occupation held in Babylon: consider the Apostle and High Priest of our profession, Christ Jesus; fight the good fight of faith, lay hold on eternal life, whereunto thou art also called, and hast professed a good profession before many witnesses.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Hebrews 3#^v1|Hebrews 3:1]]**
    ![[Hebrews 3#^v1]]
    Consider the Apostle and High Priest of our profession, Christ Jesus; the work of the Lord is the profession of the called.
  - **[[1 Timothy 6#^v12|1 Timothy 6:12]]**
    ![[1 Timothy 6#^v12]]
    Fight the good fight of faith, lay hold on eternal life, whereunto thou art also called, and hast professed a good profession before many witnesses.

**[[Exodus 5#^v10|Exodus 5:10-14]]**

- The people were scattered abroad throughout all the land of Egypt to gather stubble instead of straw, and the taskmasters hasted them, saying, Fulfil your works, your daily tasks, as when there was straw. Fewer resources, the same production and the same deadline: the pattern of oppression has not changed in this captivity.
- The officers of the children of Israel, which Pharaoh's taskmasters had set over them, were beaten. Israelites were set as officers over their own people, so that the anger of the oppressed lands on their own brother, the supervisor they can see, instead of on the power above him; it has always been an agenda, and it is the same one today.
- This place is the same house of bondage in the spirit: the great city is spiritually called Sodom and Egypt, and the afflictions Egypt laid on Israel are the same tactics laid on Israel here.

- The officers of the children of Israel came and cried unto Pharaoh, saying, Wherefore dealest thou thus with thy servants? There is no straw given unto thy servants, and they say to us, Make brick: and, behold, thy servants are beaten; but the fault is in thine own people.
- Pharaoh answered, Ye are idle, ye are idle: therefore ye say, Let us go and do sacrifice to the LORD. The charge of idleness is the standing answer to any who would go serve God, whether it is the three days' journey or keeping the Sabbath and the new moons now.
- The officers did see that they were in evil case. That is when they realized no appeal to Pharaoh would deliver them; deliverance was not coming from Egypt.
