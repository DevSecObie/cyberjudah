# Exodus 10: The Most High Hardens Pharaoh to Shew His Signs

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 10]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Exodus 10|Exodus 10]] through 13: the plagues of locusts and darkness, the death of the firstborn announced and executed, the institution of the Lord's Passover and the feast of unleavened bread, Israel thrust out of Egypt after 430 years, the sanctifying of the firstborn, and the pillar of cloud and fire leading the people. The session opens with a precursor from [[Proverbs 21|Proverbs 21]] and Romans 9: the things that were written aforetime were written for our learning, and the Most High is in absolute control of the kings of the earth.

**[[Exodus 10#^v1|Exodus 10:1]]**

![[Exodus 10#^v1]]

- The Most High hardened the heart of Pharaoh and of his servants so that he might show his signs and exemplify his power in the earth. After he brought down Egypt and delivered the people, every nation feared Israel and the God of Israel.
- The king's heart is in the hand of the Lord; he turns it whithersoever he will. He is in control of the mind of the kings of the world, so there is no need to worry over who becomes president or who gets put in office; either way the Most High is in control.
- The scripture said to Pharaoh, Even for this same purpose have I raised thee up, that I might show my power in thee, and that my name might be declared throughout all the earth. He has mercy on whom he will have mercy, and whom he will he hardeneth.
- The potter has power over the clay, of the same lump to make one vessel unto honour and another unto dishonour. Some nations are honourable, which are the Israelites, and some are dishonourable; Jacob have I loved, but Esau have I hated. The vessels of wrath were fitted to destruction, endured with much longsuffering so the Most High could show how powerful he is; that is why Esau had to be set on such a high level, so that when he brings these people down all will know God is truly all powerful.
- The same holds in spiritual Egypt today: the Most High will harden the hearts of the rulers of this kingdom so they will not let Israel go and will continue to oppress, because in the affliction the people will seek him early. It does not matter who is voted into office; whoever sits in the seat, the Most High turns him about like the rivers of water.
- The things that were written aforetime were written for our learning, and the thing that hath been is that which shall be. That is why so many similarities appear between the captivities: this captivity carries the marks of Egypt, of Babylon, and of the Greeks, because these things must come to pass.

# Exodus 11: The Death of the Firstborn Announced

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 11]]

**[[Exodus 11#^v1|Exodus 11:1]]**

![[Exodus 11#^v1]]

- The Most High already knew what was going down: yet will I bring one plague more upon Pharaoh and upon Egypt. This last plague would end it, and when Pharaoh let the people go he would thrust them out hence altogether, with the quickness.

**[[Exodus 11#^v2|Exodus 11:2-3]]**

![[Exodus 11#^v2]]
![[Exodus 11#^v3]]

- Every man and woman was to borrow of their neighbours jewels of silver and jewels of gold, and the Lord gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians.
- Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh's servants and in the sight of the people; he had a good report. Israel in spiritual Egypt must deal the same way: have the respect of the people, and if people despise you, let it be because they hate God and the word you teach, not because you are in wickedness, shady dealings, deceit, or disrespect.

**[[Exodus 11#^v4|Exodus 11:4-6]]**

![[Exodus 11#^v4]]
![[Exodus 11#^v5]]
![[Exodus 11#^v6]]

- About midnight the Lord would go out into the midst of Egypt, and all the firstborn in the land would die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh on his throne to the firstborn of the maidservant behind the mill, and all the firstborn of beasts.
- There would be a great cry throughout all the land of Egypt, such as there was none like it, nor shall be like it any more.

**[[Exodus 11#^v7|Exodus 11:7]]**

![[Exodus 11#^v7]]

- Against the children of Israel not a dog would move his tongue, that ye may know how that the Lord doth put a difference between the Egyptians and Israel.
- A difference had to be shown because Israel and the Egyptians looked alike. Moses was mistaken for an Egyptian, Paul was mistaken for an Egyptian, Joseph was mistaken for an Egyptian by his own brothers, and at Jacob's funeral the mourners were called Egyptians because no one could tell the difference.
- The difference was made plain in the plague itself: the houses whose firstborn died were shown not to be his people.

# Exodus 12: The Lord's Passover

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 12]]

**[[Exodus 12#^v1|Exodus 12:1-2]]**

![[Exodus 12#^v1]]
![[Exodus 12#^v2]]

- This month shall be unto you the beginning of months, the first month of the year. The month is Abib, the month in which Israel came out of Egypt.
- Abib in the Bible dictionary is the pre-exilic name for the first month of the year; after the exile the name was changed to Nisan, and it falls between March and April. So the new year begins in the springtime, around March and April, not on January the first in the dead of winter; that is another lie taught in Babylon.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Exodus 13#^v4|Exodus 13:4]]**
    ![[Exodus 13#^v4]]

![[Exodus 12#^v4]]
![[Exodus 12#^v5]]

- In the tenth day of the month every man was to take a lamb according to the house of his fathers; if the household was too little for the lamb, he and his neighbour next unto his house were to take it according to the number of the souls.
- The lamb was to be without blemish, a male of the first year, taken from the sheep or from the goats. This is a foreshadowing: the feast days and sacrifices are a shadow of things to come, but the body is of Christ.
- The lamb without blemish and without spot points to Christ, who was sinless, verily foreordained before the foundation of the world but manifest in these last times for the nation of Israel.

    ![[1 Peter 1#^v19]]

**[[Exodus 12#^v6|Exodus 12:6-8]]**

![[Exodus 12#^v6]]
![[Exodus 12#^v7]]
![[Exodus 12#^v8]]

- The whole assembly of the congregation of Israel was to kill the lamb in the evening, and take of the blood and strike it on the two side posts and on the upper door post of the houses.
- The flesh was to be eaten that night, roast with fire, with unleavened bread and with bitter herbs. That is the Passover menu in layman's terms: lamb roast with fire, unleavened bread, bitter herbs; no fried chicken, no birthday cake, no mashed potatoes and gravy, none of the thirteen herbs and spices.

**[[Exodus 12#^v9|Exodus 12:9-11]]**

![[Exodus 12#^v9]]
![[Exodus 12#^v10]]
![[Exodus 12#^v11]]

- Eat not of it raw, nor sodden at all with water: do not boil it, but roast it with fire, the head with the legs and the purtenance thereof, and let nothing of it remain until the morning; what remains is burned with fire.
- It was to be eaten with loins girded, shoes on the feet, and staff in hand, in haste: it is the Lord's passover, and that very night Israel was leaving Egypt.

**[[Exodus 12#^v12|Exodus 12:12-13]]**

![[Exodus 12#^v12]]
![[Exodus 12#^v13]]

- The Lord would pass through the land that night and smite all the firstborn of man and beast, and against all the gods of Egypt he would execute judgment. Each plague put an Egyptian god to shame: when he smote the land with darkness he showed that the sun god Ra is no god at all, for the God of heaven and earth blotted out the sun three days. Israel had to learn that those gods are nothing, because the nation was about to come out and serve the one true God.
- The blood was a token upon the houses: when I see the blood, I will pass over you. That is where the term Passover comes from; the houses with the blood on the doorposts were passed over from death while the firstborn of Egypt were slain.

**[[Exodus 12#^v14|Exodus 12:14]]**

![[Exodus 12#^v14]]

- This day is a memorial, a feast to the Lord throughout your generations, kept by an ordinance for ever. It was not done away; even in the New Testament the Passover is still kept: Christ our passover is sacrificed for us, therefore let us keep the feast. The lamb of the Passover represents Christ, and the apostle's instruction is to keep the feast, not to spiritualize it away.

  Precepts:
  - **[[1 Corinthians 5#^v7|1 Corinthians 5:7-8]]**
    ![[1 Corinthians 5#^v7]]

- The first day and the seventh day are holy convocations; no manner of work is done in them save that which every man must eat, meaning the food may be cooked.
- The feast of unleavened bread is observed because in this selfsame day the Most High brought the armies of Israel out of Egypt. He deals with armies, and when he brings Israel out of this place he is bringing out an army again: when the spirit of life enters them and they stand upon their feet, great fear falls on them which see them, for they stand up an exceeding great army, not choir boys.

- When the Lord brings Israel into the land of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites, a land flowing with milk and honey, this service is kept in this month: seven days of unleavened bread, and in the seventh day a feast to the Lord, with no leaven seen in all thy quarters.
- Thou shalt shew thy son in that day, saying, This is done because of that which the Lord did unto me when I came forth out of Egypt. It is a sign upon thine hand and a memorial between thine eyes, that the Lord's law may be in thy mouth.
- Thou shalt therefore keep this ordinance in his season from year to year: the Passover is kept every year.

- When thy son asketh thee in time to come, saying, What is this? thou shalt say, By strength of hand the Lord brought us out from Egypt, from the house of bondage: when Pharaoh would hardly let us go, the Lord slew all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, therefore the firstborn are sacrificed or redeemed.
- The children must be taught the righteousness of why the feast is kept. Ask the children of this land why they keep Christmas and they answer that Santa Claus is coming down the chimney; Israel's children are to be taught the works of the Most High instead.
- It is for a token upon thine hand and for frontlets between thine eyes, for by strength of hand the Lord brought us forth out of Egypt.

**[[Exodus 13#^v17|Exodus 13:17-18]]**

![[Exodus 13#^v17]]

- When Pharaoh let the people go, God led them not through the way of the land of the Philistines, although that was near, lest peradventure the people repent when they see war and return to Egypt. Israel would have been fearful at the sight of the Philistine army and turned back.
- The people felt a false security in Egypt: in the wilderness they murmured for the fish, the leeks, the onions, and the garlick of Egypt and loathed the light bread; and when the Assyrians came, Israel again ran down to Egypt for help. The same spirit holds the people in America today: a little job, government assistance, a car, food from the store, and they never want this kingdom to come down. But the Most High has something much greater in store: the world, the earth, and many mansions.
- So God led the people about, through the way of the wilderness of the Red Sea, and the children of Israel went up harnessed out of the land of Egypt.
