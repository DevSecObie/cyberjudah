# 1 Samuel 12: Turn Not Aside From Following the Lord

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 12]]

## Introduction

This session covers [[1 Samuel 12|1 Samuel 12]] through 15, heavy chapters in the rise and fall of king Saul. In chapter 12 Samuel gives his farewell address: he establishes his own blamelessness, rehearses the righteous acts of the Most High from Egypt through the judges, rebukes Israel for demanding a king when the Lord was their king, and calls down thunder and rain in wheat harvest to prove the wickedness of that request. In chapter 13 Saul begins to show he is not fit to be king, making haste in time of trouble by offering the burnt offering instead of waiting for Samuel. In chapter 14 Jonathan and his armor bearer take on the Philistine garrison alone, showing there is no restraint to the Lord to save by many or by few. In chapter 15 the Lord commands Saul to utterly destroy Amalek; Saul obeys partially, and partial obedience is counted as rebellion: to obey is better than sacrifice, and rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft.

# 1 Samuel 13: Make Not Haste in Time of Trouble

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 13]]

- Chapter 13 is the beginning of Saul showing his simplicity, the beginning of the proof that he is not fit to be king over Israel.
- Saul chose three thousand men, two thousand with him in Michmash and mount Bethel and a thousand with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin. Jonathan smote the garrison of the Philistines in Geba, and the Philistines heard of it; Saul blew the trumpet throughout all the land, and Israel was had in abomination with the Philistines. His son popped off the battle, so now it is war time.

- The Philistines gathered thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen, and people as the sand on the seashore, far deeper than the three thousand Israel had prepared. When the men of Israel saw they were in a strait, they hid themselves in caves, thickets, rocks, high places, and pits; some Hebrews fled over Jordan, and the people that followed Saul followed him trembling.
- The sons of God, the mighty men of valor, saw the enemy coming and started hiding. As the chosen people, if we really have faith we should be fearless; the Lord had just defeated the Philistines for Israel in chapter 7. To still fear the enemy shows a lack of faith, that you really do not believe in your God.
- Nehemiah shows the right spirit. In the Persian captivity he had liberty from the king to rebuild the walls of Jerusalem, and the enemies Sanballat, Tobiah, and Geshem plotted to scare him off the work. A hired man urged him to hide in the temple because they were coming to slay him, and Nehemiah refused; operating in the spirit of fear is sin according to the scripture.

  - **[[2 Timothy 1#^v7|2 Timothy 1:7]]**
    ![[2 Timothy 1#^v7]]

- Samuel had instructed Saul in chapter 10: when you get to Gilgal, tarry seven days, make no moves until I arrive. Saul tarried seven days according to the set time, but Samuel came not at the moment expected, and the people were scattered from him and acting frantic.
- Saul said, Bring hither a burnt offering to me, and he offered the burnt offering, and Saul is not the priest; that is Samuel's office. And it came to pass that as soon as he had made an end of offering the burnt offering, behold, Samuel came. He was barely late, and Saul could not hold his patience.
- Brothers do the same thing today: leadership said it would be here at ten, it is five past ten, so we cancel the plan and go home. Be humble and tarry like you were instructed; it may run some minutes over, and the Lord may be testing your patience.

  Precepts:

    ![[Ecclesiasticus 2#^v2]]
    Saul made haste in a time of trouble; he panicked and did unadvisedly, and he is going to suffer consequences for it.

- Samuel asks, What hast thou done? Saul answers with excuses: the people were scattered from me, thou camest not within the days appointed, the Philistines gathered at Michmash, so I forced myself and offered a burnt offering. This is part of Saul's pattern of behavior; he never wants to hold himself accountable, he always deflects onto whoever else.

  - **[[Isaiah 58#^v9|Isaiah 58:9]]**

- For now would the Lord have established thy kingdom upon Israel for ever; but now thy kingdom shall not continue. For that one act of foolishness the Lord withdrew what he would have given: Saul's lineage ruling Israel.
- The Lord wants to see whether you will perform what he told you to do even when things do not work out how you planned; that is what proves you faithful in temptation and worthy of being exalted. Saul failed that test in the time of his distress.
- The Lord hath sought him a man after his own heart to be captain over his people. Samuel prophesied this without ever having met the replacement; that man is David, predestined before he is even introduced in the scriptures.

  Precepts:
  - **[[1 Maccabees 2#^v52|1 Maccabees 2:52-53]]**

    ![[1 Maccabees 2#^v53]]

  - **[[Acts 13#^v22|Acts 13:22]]**

- When they told Saul, he said, Ye have transgressed: roll a great stone unto me this day; bring every man his ox and his sheep, slay them here, and eat, and sin not against the Lord in eating with the blood. And Saul built an altar unto the Lord, the first altar that he built unto the Lord.

- If you are spiritual, when the Lord ignores you, you understand at that point what it reveals: it is sin. It has to be; the scriptures give only that reason.

# 1 Samuel 15: To Obey Is Better Than Sacrifice

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 15]]

**[[1 Samuel 15#^v1|1 Samuel 15:1-3]]**

![[1 Samuel 15#^v1]]
![[1 Samuel 15#^v2]]
![[1 Samuel 15#^v3]]

- Samuel said unto Saul, The Lord sent me to anoint thee to be king over his people; now therefore hearken thou unto the voice of the words of the Lord. Thus saith the Lord of hosts, I remember that which Amalek did to Israel, how he laid wait for him in the way when he came up from Egypt. Generations after the wilderness, the Most High still remembers; the generation of Amalekites had returned, and it was time to fulfil what was written.
- Now go and smite Amalek, and utterly destroy all that they have, and spare them not; but slay both man and woman, infant and suckling, ox and sheep, camel and ass. This is a specific instruction: kill all of them, spare none. Note that the Most High commanded this against a generation that had not personally done the deed in the wilderness; that answers the argument, I never did anything to you, so why hold it against me? The Most High himself commanded recompense for what was done to the forefathers.
- Christianity says forgive and forget, but the Most High God said remember; this is a righteous grudge, and it shows [[Leviticus 19#^v18|Leviticus 19:18]] applies to the children of thy people.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 25#^v17|Deuteronomy 25:17-18]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v17]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v18]]
    Remember what Amalek did unto thee by the way, how he met thee and smote the hindmost of thee, all that were feeble behind thee, when thou wast faint and weary; and he feared not God; the detail is written to stir you up: they preyed on the weak and the tired.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 25#^v19|Deuteronomy 25:19]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 25#^v19]]
    When the Lord thy God hath given thee rest from all thine enemies round about, thou shalt blot out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven; thou shalt not forget it. Israel now had rest and a king; the time had come.
  - **[[Exodus 17#^v16|Exodus 17:16]]**
    ![[Exodus 17#^v16]]
    The Lord hath sworn that the Lord will have war with Amalek from generation to generation; that war did not end in the wilderness.
  - **[[Leviticus 19#^v18|Leviticus 19:18]]**
    ![[Leviticus 19#^v18]]
    Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people; the grudge law is toward your own people, not toward the enemies of Israel whom the Most High has sworn against.

- The teaching applies [[Exodus 17#^v16|Exodus 17:16]] to this day: the same war continues from generation to generation, and it is now more spiritual than ever, because the adversary has established the imagination that they are the Jews, and we go out and cast down that imagination every day. Edom cheered Babylon on when Jerusalem burned, and the scripture pronounces the payback.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Psalms 137#^v7|Psalms 137:7]]**
    ![[Psalms 137#^v7]]
    Remember, O Lord, the children of Edom in the day of Jerusalem, who said, Rase it, rase it, even to the foundation thereof; they were the cheerleaders when Babylon destroyed the temple.
  - **[[Psalms 137#^v8|Psalms 137:8-9]]**
    ![[Psalms 137#^v8]]
    ![[Psalms 137#^v9]]
    O daughter of Babylon, who art to be destroyed, happy shall he be that rewardeth thee as thou hast served us; happy shall he be that taketh and dasheth thy little ones against the stones; the Most High calls him happy that pays her back for everything she has done.
  - **[[Revelation 17#^v5|Revelation 17:5]]**
    ![[Revelation 17#^v5]]
    Edom grew up from being the daughter of Babylon into Mystery, Babylon the Great, the mother of harlots; that is why the great woman of Revelation carries Babylon's name.

- Understand the frame: we live peaceably with all men and lay hands on no one; we do not incite or condone any violence, and if we hear of any we report it. The day of [[Psalms 137|Psalms 137]] has not yet come; it belongs to the return of the Lord, so chill out and relax, none of that is for now.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Romans 12#^v18|Romans 12:18]]**
    ![[Romans 12#^v18]]
    If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men; that is the walk commanded now.
  - **[[Leviticus 5#^v1|Leviticus 5:1]]**
    ![[Leviticus 5#^v1]]
    If a soul hear the voice of swearing and is a witness, he must utter it; hence if we hear of violence we make it known.

So the Kenites departed. The Most High discriminates righteously: the people who showed Israel kindness were spared out of the judgment.

- Saul smote the Amalekites from Havilah until thou comest to Shur, and utterly destroyed the people with the edge of the sword; but he took Agag the king of the Amalekites alive. He halfway fulfilled the order, reasoning like the king might be valuable as a hostage, as if the Lord forgot that detail.
- But Saul and the people spared Agag, and the best of the sheep, and of the oxen, and of the fatlings, and the lambs, and all that was good, and would not utterly destroy them; but every thing that was vile and refuse, that they destroyed utterly. They killed what looked like it deserved killing, so they could say they did some killing, and kept the healthy, good looking animals, telling themselves surely the Lord could not have meant these.

**[[1 Samuel 15#^v10|1 Samuel 15:10-11]]**

- Then came the word of the Lord unto Samuel: It repenteth me that I have set up Saul to be king, for he is turned back from following me, and hath not performed my commandments. Mark the language: Saul did most of what was said, yet the Lord counts it as turning back and not performing his commandments at all.
- For everybody who says, well, I did do this part, understand: whenever there is a but in your obedience, you did not perform the orders given unto you. That is what the Most High is showing us in this story.
- And it grieved Samuel, and he cried unto the Lord all night. Pride had to be in operation for Saul to transgress on that level, and pride is where the throne is lost.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 10#^v12|Ecclesiasticus 10:12-14]]**

    ![[Ecclesiasticus 10#^v13]]

    That is exactly what happened: Saul's heart turned from his Maker, he poured out sin upon sin from there, and the Lord cast down the proud prince's throne and set up the meek in his stead; that is the whole story we are about to read.

- Samuel rose early to meet Saul, and it was told him that Saul came to Carmel and set him up a place, a monument to himself, and is gone down to Gilgal. When your transgression becomes the lesson, be thankful when the correction comes quietly and nobody knows your name; do not take the spirit of bitterness, because you deserved a lot more.
- Saul greets Samuel first with a cover: Blessed be thou of the Lord: I have performed the commandment of the Lord. The first thing out of his mouth is a fabrication, knowing full well he transgressed.
- Samuel answers, What meaneth then this bleating of the sheep in mine ears, and the lowing of the oxen which I hear? If you performed the commandment, why do I hear animals?
- Saul deflects again, the same blame game as chapter 13: They have brought them from the Amalekites, for the people spared the best of the sheep and of the oxen, to sacrifice unto the Lord thy God; and the rest we have utterly destroyed. Verse 9 already told us Saul and the people spared them; now suddenly it is only the people, and for a good reason, a sacrifice, as though the Lord ought to be pleased.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 12#^v32|Deuteronomy 12:32]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 12#^v32]]
    What thing soever I command you, observe to do it: thou shalt not add thereto, nor diminish from it; the Most High made a law for the bright ideas that come along the way of following instructions, and it is plain.

- Samuel said, Stay, and I will tell thee what the Lord hath said to me this night. When thou wast little in thine own sight, wast thou not made the head of the tribes of Israel, and the Lord anointed thee king over Israel? Samuel brings Saul back to his remembrance: when you thought nothing of yourself, with no confidence to be a ruler, the Most High still chose you.
- And the Lord sent thee on a journey, and said, Go and utterly destroy the sinners the Amalekites, and fight against them until they be consumed; the errand was the vengeance written in [[Deuteronomy 25|Deuteronomy 25]]. Wherefore then didst thou not obey the voice of the Lord, but didst fly upon the spoil, and didst evil in the sight of the Lord? Keeping the orders partially is evil in the sight of the Lord.

  Precepts:
  - **[[1 Samuel 9#^v21|1 Samuel 9:21]]**
    ![[1 Samuel 9#^v21]]
    Saul had answered, Am not I a Benjamite, of the smallest of the tribes of Israel, and my family the least of all the families of the tribe of Benjamin? wherefore then speakest thou so to me? That was the little-in-his-own-sight attitude Saul started with.

**[[1 Samuel 15#^v20|1 Samuel 15:20-21]]**

![[1 Samuel 15#^v20]]
![[1 Samuel 15#^v21]]

- Saul is in denial: Yea, I have obeyed the voice of the lord, and have gone the way which the Lord sent me, and have brought Agag the king of Amalek, and have utterly destroyed the Amalekites. Samuel breaks down the rebuke and Saul tells the prophet he is wrong.
- In justifying himself over the animals, Saul volunteers even more of his rebellion, boasting that he spared the king, as if that was the smart move everyone really wanted; then he lays the spoil on the people again: the people took of the spoil, sheep and oxen, the chief of the things which should have been utterly destroyed, to sacrifice unto the Lord thy God in Gilgal.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Job 9#^v20|Job 9:20]]**
    ![[Job 9#^v20]]
    If I justify myself, mine own mouth shall condemn me; when you plead your own righteousness against correction, you end up revealing more of your rebellion out of your own mouth, which is exactly what Saul did.

**[[1 Samuel 15#^v22|1 Samuel 15:22]]**

![[1 Samuel 15#^v22]]

- Samuel makes it plain: Hath the Lord as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the Lord? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams. Do you think the Lord prefers a sacrifice to atone for broken commandments over you simply keeping the commandments? That is a heavy verse.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 34#^v18|Ecclesiasticus 34:18-19]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 34#^v18]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 34#^v19]]
    The spoil was wrongfully gotten, taken against the will of the Lord, so the offering is ridiculous; all those animals were not going to pacify his anger.
  - **[[Proverbs 21#^v27|Proverbs 21:27]]**
    ![[Proverbs 21#^v27]]
    The sacrifice of the wicked is abomination: how much more, when he bringeth it with a wicked mind? It does not merely do nothing; it makes the Most High more angry, and Saul probably would have kept those animals to eat had it never been pointed out.
  - **[[Psalms 51#^v16|Psalms 51:16-17]]**
    ![[Psalms 51#^v16]]
    ![[Psalms 51#^v17]]
    Thou desirest not sacrifice, else would I give it: the sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise. David wrote this in his repentance: the true sacrifice is being sorry and remorseful for what you did, not pleading why you were not wrong for doing wrong; that is the spirit Saul does not have.

**[[1 Samuel 15#^v23|1 Samuel 15:23]]**

![[1 Samuel 15#^v23]]

- For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and stubbornness is as iniquity and idolatry. That is heavy. A brother who would never touch voodoo will sit through a whole counsel of do not do this, and call two weeks later saying he did it anyway; according to this verse, that rebellion is the same as witchcraft.
- Stubbornness is idolatry because the instruction does not come from the man; it is the commandment of the Lord that leadership carries. When you are stubborn against it, you are saying what I want to do comes before what God wants me to do, and you have put yourself in the midst of idolatry as surely as circling the stone at Mecca or bowing before a cross.
- Because thou hast rejected the word of the Lord, he hath also rejected thee from being king: you will lose whatever position the Most High put you in.
- This stubborn heart is what fares evil at the last; we will read Saul's end in the last chapter of 1 Samuel, and in chapter 16 the evil spirit enters into him and the Spirit of the Lord departs. The plant of wickedness took root here.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 3#^v26|Ecclesiasticus 3:26-27]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 3#^v26]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 3#^v27]]
    Obstinate is another word for stubborn: the mind set on doing what it wants contrary to counsel will be laden with sorrows, and Saul heaped sin upon sin, lying, justifying himself, and blaming the people, which is what carried him into the witchcraft and idolatry of rebellion.

- Then he asks, Pardon my sin, and turn again with me, that I may worship the Lord; take me back to the regular Saul and Samuel connection, as though a fearful excuse settles it.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Proverbs 29#^v25|Proverbs 29:25]]**

    The fear of man bringeth a snare, but whoso putteth his trust in the Lord shall be safe; if you fear what the people will say, you will fall into the trap and transgress, which is why a leader cannot fold when the people become too much to handle; Saul would have been safe had he trusted the Lord.

**[[1 Samuel 15#^v26|1 Samuel 15:26]]**

![[1 Samuel 15#^v26]]

- Samuel refuses: I will not return with thee; for thou hast rejected the word of the Lord, and the Lord hath rejected thee from being king over Israel. Think it through: Samuel gave Saul the opening at the bleating of the sheep, and Saul would not humble down; he asked why Saul disobeyed the voice of the Lord, and Saul justified himself again. Two admonitions were given and refused before this rejection.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Titus 3#^v10|Titus 3:10]]**
    ![[Titus 3#^v10]]
    A man that is an heretick after the first and second admonition reject; Saul had two chances to repent and confess and still justified himself, so Samuel did exactly what the scripture says and rejected him.
  - **[[Titus 1#^v16|Titus 1:16]]**
    ![[Titus 1#^v16]]
    They profess that they know God, but in works they deny him, being abominable, and disobedient, and unto every good work reprobate; Saul professed, restore me that I may worship the Lord, while every work denied him; this is the starting point of Saul losing the Spirit and going reprobate.

Understand the spirit of the title: whenever you go off on your own mind, pride has you departing from your strength, and once you have left your strength you are open to Satan; if a brother tells you that you are battling with pride, take heed and examine yourself.

- That is the spirit of vanity, caught up in a rank and a title while forgetting that the title came for pleasing the Lord; once you discontinue pleasing the Lord, the title is going to leave as well. So Samuel turned again after Saul, and Saul worshipped the Lord.

- And Samuel said, As thy sword hath made women childless, so shall thy mother be childless among women. And Samuel hewed Agag in pieces before the Lord in Gilgal. The old prophet did not play; he finished the commandment himself, measure for measure for what Amalek's sword had done.
