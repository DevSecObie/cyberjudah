# 1 Samuel 16: The Lord Looketh on the Heart

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 16]]

## Introduction

This session covers 1 Samuel chapters 16 through 19: David selected and anointed as Saul's replacement, his humble beginnings as a keeper of sheep and a player on the harp, his victory over Goliath, Saul's descent into envy and murder once the women sang David's praise, and the faithfulness of Jonathan and Michal in delivering David from Saul's snares. The thread that ties it together is the spirit: the Most High looks not on the outward appearance but on the heart, his spirit came upon David from the day of his anointing, and it departed from Saul, who was given over to his own ruin.

**[[1 Samuel 16#^v1|1 Samuel 16:1]]**

![[1 Samuel 16#^v1]]

- Recall the class on chapters 12 through 15: in chapter 15 Saul rebelled against the commandment, and after two admonitions he still would not humble down and repent, so the Most High rejected him from reigning over Israel and told him through Samuel that his kingdom would not continue.
- Samuel had groomed Saul up and saw potential in the brother, so he mourned over him; the Most High asked how long he would keep crying over the man he had rejected.
- Samuel is told to fill his horn with oil and go to Jesse the Bethlehemite, for the Most High had already provided himself a king from among Jesse's sons.

![[1 Samuel 16#^v2]]

- Samuel feared that Saul would kill him if he heard of it. Remember the influence and love Samuel had among the people; even so, this shows the power of the king. Saul raised no hand against Samuel yet, but a king had the authority to command a prophet's death.
- The Most High told Samuel to go in with a heifer, as if coming only to sacrifice; he would go incognito, not letting it be known that he was coming to anoint Saul's replacement, and the Most High would show him whom to anoint.

- When Samuel looked on Eliab he judged through his own lens as a man: surely this is the Lord's anointed. The Most High corrected him: look not on his countenance or on the height of his stature, because I have refused him. Looking the mightiest and the tallest does not make a man the one the Most High picked.
- The Lord seeth not as man seeth; man looketh on the outward appearance, but the Lord looketh on the heart. This scripture is heavy: it is showing the spirit of Christ before Christ walked the earth. The Pharisees could not understand why he dealt with the lower class instead of the mighty men; he was looking on the heart, and he knew the Pharisees' hearts were corrupt while the humble were willing to devote their lives to the Most High.
- This still happens today: brothers will look at a group and assume the biggest one must be the leader, not knowing that brother might be the soldier while the leader is the slim one beside him.

Saul was put through a trial, whether he would keep the commandment given through the mouth of the prophet, and he went wrong; so the spirit forsook him and he was given over to his own ruin, that abyss of jealousy, envy, and hatred.

# 1 Samuel 17: Not by Sword and Spear

- As we covered before, the Philistines always wanted smoke with the Israelites; they would not leave us alone. They gathered to battle at Shochoh and pitched between Shochoh and Azekah.
- Saul and the men of Israel pitched by the valley of Elah and set the battle in array; the Philistines stood on a mountain on one side, Israel on a mountain on the other side, with a valley between them.

- He wore a helmet of brass and a coat of mail weighing five thousand shekels of brass, greaves of brass on his legs, a target of brass between his shoulders, a spear like a weaver's beam with a head of six hundred shekels of iron; a big man in a mighty costume of armor.
- He did not even carry his own shield; one bearing a shield went before him.

- When Saul and all Israel heard the words of the Philistine, they were dismayed and greatly afraid. They were in a carnal spirit, measuring his height, his armor, and his reach; the spirit had departed, and fear is not the spirit of the Most High.
- The Most High put laws in the book about going to battle, and this fear showed that Saul and the people lacked faith; the same God that destroyed Pharaoh and the Egyptians who pursued Israel was with them, and a trial of faith was set before them in the form of one big intimidating man with a big mouth.

  Precepts:

  - **[[2 Kings 19#^v34|2 Kings 19:34-35]]**

    The fulfillment in Hezekiah's day: the angel of the Lord went out and smote in the camp of the Assyrians an hundred fourscore and five thousand, and Judah woke up to the battle already won; that is how the Lord gets down.

David had precise aim and power; that stone left the sling with the velocity of a bullet, and it took one shot.

- When the Philistines saw their champion was dead, they fled. They ran from a young youth because they knew something supernatural was going on; he was in the spirit of the Lord.

# 1 Samuel 18: The Envious Eye of Saul

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 18]]

**[[1 Samuel 18#^v1|1 Samuel 18:1-4]]**

![[1 Samuel 18#^v1]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v2]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v3]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v4]]

- The soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul, and they made a covenant; Jonathan stripped himself of his robe and gave it to David, with his garments, his sword, his bow, and his girdle.
- Address the perversion head on, as we did last class: souls being knit is not talking about anything unnatural between the two men. The same word describes the church: hearts knit together in love, as joints and bands in one body. Jonathan and David were on one accord; they had the same spirit.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Colossians 2#^v2|Colossians 2:2]]**
    ![[Colossians 2#^v2]]
    That their hearts might be comforted, being knit together in love; the will of Christ for the church at Colosse, the same knitting of souls.
  - **[[Colossians 2#^v19|Colossians 2:19]]**
    ![[Colossians 2#^v19]]
    The body, by joints and bands knit together, increaseth with the increase of God; as muscles and joints are knit into one body, so were Jonathan and David of one spirit.

**[[1 Samuel 18#^v5|1 Samuel 18:5-7]]**

![[1 Samuel 18#^v5]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v6]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v7]]

- David went whithersoever Saul sent him and behaved himself wisely, and Saul set him over the men of war, and he was accepted in the sight of all the people and of Saul's servants.
- When they returned from the slaughter of the Philistine, the women came out of all cities of Israel singing and dancing to meet king Saul, with tabrets, with joy, and with instruments of musick.
- The women answered one another as they played: Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands. The first line honored Saul, but the song made David exponentially the greater warrior, and brothers catch spirits behind what women say and do.

**[[1 Samuel 18#^v8|1 Samuel 18:8-9]]**

![[1 Samuel 18#^v8]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v9]]

- Saul was very wroth and the saying displeased him: they have ascribed unto David ten thousands, and to me they have ascribed but thousands; and what can he have more but the kingdom? Wroth is the same word used in [[Genesis 4|Genesis 4]] of how Cain fell toward Abel, and Abel had done nothing to him but what the Lord required; David likewise did nothing to Saul but what Saul himself asked of him.
- Instead of appreciating the song, give credit where it is due, for the labourer is worthy of his reward, Saul was displeased at his brother's praise; that is the same spirit of envy.
- And Saul eyed David from that day and forward. What was behind that eye? Envy. The envious man is not satisfied with his own portion; Saul had his thousands and still had the kingdom, but his eye was on David's portion, and iniquity dried up his soul from that point forward.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 4#^v5|Genesis 4:5]]**
    ![[Genesis 4#^v5]]
    Cain was very wroth, and his countenance fell; the same word, the same spirit, kindled against a brother who had done nothing wrong.
  - **[[Matthew 5#^v21|Matthew 5:21-22]]**
    ![[Matthew 5#^v21]]
    ![[Matthew 5#^v22]]
    Whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment, the same judgment as a murderer; anger without a cause is counted as murder in the spirit.
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 14#^v8|Ecclesiasticus 14:8-9]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 14#^v8]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 14#^v9]]
    The envious man hath a wicked eye; that is the eye Saul eyed David with, and the iniquity of the wicked dried up his soul.

**[[1 Samuel 18#^v10|1 Samuel 18:10-12]]**

![[1 Samuel 18#^v10]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v11]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v12]]

- On the morrow the evil spirit from God came upon Saul, and this time it came back with more power: there was a javelin in Saul's hand, and he cast it, saying, I will smite David even to the wall with it. David avoided out of his presence twice.
- Saul was afraid of David, because the Lord was with him and was departed from Saul. When Saul saw how quickly David escaped, that spirit on David made the hunter the scared one.

  Precepts:
  - **[[James 2#^v19|James 2:19]]**
    ![[James 2#^v19]]
    The devils also believe, and tremble; when Saul saw the spirit on David, he who sought David's life became the one afraid.

**[[1 Samuel 18#^v13|1 Samuel 18:13-16]]**

![[1 Samuel 18#^v13]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v14]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v15]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v16]]

- Saul removed David from him and made him captain over a thousand; he basically demoted the brother, putting his own armourbearer away from his presence for no reason.
- David behaved himself wisely in all his ways, and the Lord was with him, and when Saul saw it he was the more afraid. David's continued righteousness heaped coals of fire on Saul; when your righteousness makes the wicked afraid, that is how you know you are in the spirit.
- But all Israel and Judah loved David, because he went out and came in before them; everybody knew he was the one really putting his life on the line and doing the work, even though he had no throne.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Luke 6#^v22|Luke 6:22]]**
    ![[Luke 6#^v22]]
    Blessed are ye, when men shall hate you, and when they shall separate you from their company; Saul separated David from his company for no reason, a mark of blessing on David.
  - **[[Romans 12#^v20|Romans 12:20]]**
    ![[Romans 12#^v20]]
    In so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire on his head; David's faithful service under a hateful king did exactly that.
  - **[[1 Peter 2#^v15|1 Peter 2:15]]**
    ![[1 Peter 2#^v15]]
    With well doing ye put to silence the ignorance of foolish men; David kept behaving wisely and left Saul nothing to charge him with.

**[[1 Samuel 18#^v17|1 Samuel 18:17-19]]**

- Saul offered his elder daughter Merab to wife, only be thou valiant for me and fight the Lord's battles; for Saul said, Let not mine hand be upon him, but let the hand of the Philistines be upon him. Remember, the king's daughter was already the promise attached to killing Goliath; Saul aged him out of that and reattached conditions, thinking the Philistines would finish David.
- David answered humbly: who am I, and what is my life, or my father's family in Israel, that I should be son in law to the king?
- When the time came that Merab should have been given to David, she was given to Adriel the Meholathite; Saul tricked David out of her.

**[[1 Samuel 18#^v20|1 Samuel 18:20-27]]**

![[1 Samuel 18#^v20]]

- Michal, Saul's daughter, loved David, and the thing pleased Saul: I will give him her, that she may be a snare to him, and that the hand of the Philistines may be against him. He set his own daughter as a trap.
- David again answered humbly, that he was a poor man and lightly esteemed; so Saul waived the dowry money and named his price: an hundred foreskins of the Philistines, thinking to make David fall by the hand of the Philistines, for there was no way he should survive that errand.
- David arose with his men, slew two hundred Philistines, and brought their foreskins in full tale to the king; he doubled the order. That is how devoted a warrior he was for his king and his nation, and Saul gave him Michal his daughter to wife.

**[[1 Samuel 18#^v28|1 Samuel 18:28-30]]**

![[1 Samuel 18#^v28]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v29]]
![[1 Samuel 18#^v30]]

- Saul saw and knew that the Lord was with David, and that Michal loved him, and Saul was yet the more afraid of David; and Saul became David's enemy continually. Put the verses together: he knew the Lord was with the man, and he chose to be that man's enemy anyway. If you know the Lord is with somebody and you make yourself their enemy, you have made yourself the adversary.
- David was righteous, obedient, and humble; Saul was envious, wicked, and rebellious, and he could not stand him. As sin is an abomination to the Most High, so godliness is an abomination to a sinner; the sinner cannot bear to look at righteousness.
- The princes of the Philistines went forth, and David behaved himself more wisely than all the servants of Saul, so that his name was much set by; harmless as a dove, wise as a serpent.

  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 1#^v25|Ecclesiasticus 1:25]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 1#^v25]]
    Godliness is an abomination to a sinner; that is why Saul became David's enemy continually.
  - **[[1 John 3#^v12|1 John 3:12]]**
    ![[1 John 3#^v12]]
    Cain, who was of that wicked one, slew his brother because his own works were evil and his brother's righteous; the same spirit that was on Saul, the root of bitterness that says, I have to take that man's life.

# 1 Samuel 19: A Faithful Friend Is a Strong Defence

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 19]]

**[[1 Samuel 19#^v1|1 Samuel 19:1-3]]**

![[1 Samuel 19#^v1]]
![[1 Samuel 19#^v2]]
![[1 Samuel 19#^v3]]

- Saul spake to Jonathan his son and to all his servants that they should kill David. The Philistines could not get him, so now he wants Israelites to kill an Israelite; that is how far down the slope this man has gone.
- But Jonathan delighted much in David and told him: my father seeketh to kill thee; take heed to thyself until the morning, abide in a secret place, and hide thyself. I will commune with my father of thee, and what I see, that I will tell thee.
- Jonathan would have been next in line for the throne; you would think his loyalty lay with his daddy, but he saw the spirit was not on his father, it was on his brother David. His loyalty exceeded his own desires. That is the kind of friend to look for, and the lesson to learn from Jonathan.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 6#^v14|Ecclesiasticus 6:14]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 6#^v14]]
    A faithful friend is a strong defence, and he that hath found such an one hath found a treasure; that is what Jonathan was to David.
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 22#^v25|Ecclesiasticus 22:25]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 22#^v25]]
    I will not be ashamed to defend a friend, neither will I hide myself from him; even with his father the king gone crazy, Jonathan would not abandon David.

**[[1 Samuel 19#^v4|1 Samuel 19:4-7]]**

![[1 Samuel 19#^v4]]
![[1 Samuel 19#^v5]]
![[1 Samuel 19#^v6]]
![[1 Samuel 19#^v7]]

- Jonathan spake good of David to Saul: let not the king sin against his servant, because he hath not sinned against thee, and his works have been to thee-ward very good; he put his life in his hand and slew the Philistine, and thou sawest it and didst rejoice. Why wilt thou sin against innocent blood, to slay David without a cause?
- Without a cause: in chapter 18 it was just a feeling, wrath at David for nothing; as Christ explained in the spirit, that anger without a cause leads to actually wanting to murder, which is why it carries the same judgment.
- Saul hearkened unto Jonathan and sware, As the Lord liveth, he shall not be slain; and Jonathan brought David back to Saul, and he was in his presence as in times past.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Exodus 23#^v7|Exodus 23:7]]**
    ![[Exodus 23#^v7]]
    The innocent and righteous slay thou not; the law in the book that Saul was set on breaking.
  - **[[Proverbs 6#^v16|Proverbs 6:16-17]]**
    ![[Proverbs 6#^v16]]
    ![[Proverbs 6#^v17]]
    Among the six things the Lord hates, yea seven that are an abomination unto him, are hands that shed innocent blood; that is the boat Saul put himself in.

**[[1 Samuel 19#^v8|1 Samuel 19:8-10]]**

![[1 Samuel 19#^v8]]
![[1 Samuel 19#^v9]]
![[1 Samuel 19#^v10]]

- There was war again, and David went out and fought with the Philistines and slew them with a great slaughter. Everything was back to normal until David's mighty spirit was displayed on the battlefield again.
- The evil spirit from the Lord was upon Saul as he sat in his house with his javelin in his hand, and he sought to smite David even to the wall while David played with his hand. The evil spirit had leveled up: in times past the harp made it depart, now Saul throws the javelin at the man playing the music.
- David slipped away out of Saul's presence, and the javelin smote into the wall, and David fled and escaped that night, just as in chapter 18.

**[[1 Samuel 19#^v11|1 Samuel 19:11-17]]**

![[1 Samuel 19#^v11]]
![[1 Samuel 19#^v12]]
![[1 Samuel 19#^v13]]
![[1 Samuel 19#^v14]]
![[1 Samuel 19#^v15]]
![[1 Samuel 19#^v16]]

- Saul sent messengers unto David's house to watch him and to slay him in the morning. Having tried to turn his son against David, he now leans on his daughter, David's own wife; but Michal warned David, If thou save not thy life to night, to morrow thou shalt be slain.
- Michal let David down through a window, and he fled and escaped; she laid an image in the bed with a pillow of goats' hair for his bolster and said, He is sick. Saul said, Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may slay him, and found the image; Michal covered herself, saying David threatened her, but she was looking out for her husband.
- At this time Michal was a righteous example, a ride or die; she was a tower against death to her husband. She turns sour some chapters down the line, but here she saved his life.
- Note Saul's words: why hast thou sent away mine enemy? Saul and David were brothers, both Israel, Benjamin and Judah; Saul made his brother his enemy, and David loved him anyway. That is the understanding of Christ's words on loving your enemy: David exemplified it with Saul.

    A married woman is a tower against death to her husband; that is what Michal was to David that night.
  - **[[Matthew 5#^v44|Matthew 5:44]]**
    ![[Matthew 5#^v44]]
    Love your enemies; Saul was David's enemy, but David loved him, and that is the understanding Christ was going into.

- David fled and escaped and came to Samuel to Ramah and told him all that Saul had done, and he and Samuel went and dwelt in Naioth. David was smart: he ran to the prophet, into the tower of the Lord.
- Saul sent messengers to take David, and when they saw the company of the prophets prophesying and Samuel standing as appointed over them, the spirit of God was upon the messengers of Saul, and they also prophesied; a second and a third set of messengers were sent, and the same thing happened each time they entered that atmosphere.
- Then Saul went himself, fully set in his mind to take David's life himself, and the spirit of God came upon him also; he prophesied before Samuel, stripped off his clothes, and lay down naked all that day and all that night, wherefore they say, Is Saul also among the prophets?
- That is the close of the session: when you are fighting for the Most High, nothing can be done unto you; if the eyes of the wicked are against you and you are ordained and predestined, his enemies came to take David and the spirit of God shut the evil down every time.
