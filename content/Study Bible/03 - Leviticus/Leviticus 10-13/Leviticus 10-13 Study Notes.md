# Leviticus 10: Strange Fire and the Charge of the Priesthood

Read the chapter → [[Leviticus 10]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Leviticus 10|Leviticus 10]] through 13: the strange fire of Nadab and Abihu and the charge laid on the priesthood, the dietary law of clean and unclean beasts, the law of purification after childbirth, and the law of the plague of leprosy.

**[[Leviticus 10#^v1|Leviticus 10:1-2]]**

![[Leviticus 10#^v1]]
![[Leviticus 10#^v2]]

- Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, took either of them his censer, put fire therein and incense thereon, and offered strange fire before the Lord, which he commanded them not; and fire went out from the Lord and devoured them.
- The instructions for the incense were already given: Aaron shall burn sweet incense every morning and at even, a perpetual incense before the Lord throughout your generations, and ye shall offer no strange incense thereon. There were instructions on what could be burned and what could not; the strange fire is that Nadab and Abihu put strange incense before the Lord.
- When the Most High gave instructions through Moses on what to sacrifice and how, it was with specificity; you were to do it exactly the way he told you.
- They were also not the ones authorized to burn the incense; in the tabernacle service you had to be appointed to certain services and could not take a service upon yourself. Zacharias shows the pattern throughout the generations: he executed the priest's office in the order of his course, and his lot, his appointed job, was to burn incense when he went into the temple of the Lord.
- The fire that went out from the Lord was the same fire that came out from before the Lord in [[Leviticus 9|Leviticus 9]] and consumed the burnt offering upon the altar.

# Leviticus 11: The Dietary Law of Clean and Unclean Beasts

Read the chapter → [[Leviticus 11]]

**[[Leviticus 11#^v1|Leviticus 11:1-3]]**

- [[Leviticus 11|Leviticus 11]] tells you what you can and cannot eat. Among all the beasts of the earth, ye shall eat whatsoever parteth the hoof, and is clovenfooted, and cheweth the cud.
- A cloven hoof is a hoof split into two toes; mammals with this hoof include cattle, deer, antelopes, gazelles, goats, and sheep.
- Chewing the cud means the animal has more than one stomach: it chews its food, brings it back up into its mouth to be chewed again, and then digests it through another stomach.

- Both marks must be present. There are animals that chew the cud and animals that divide the hoof but do not have both; those are unclean. The camel cheweth the cud but divideth not the hoof: you cannot eat camel.
- The coney is the pika, a small mountain dwelling mammal that resembles its close relative the rabbit, found at heights among the highest of any mammal. The scriptures already had it right: the rocks are a refuge for the conies, and the conies, a feeble folk, make their houses in the rocks. You cannot eat coney, and the hare likewise is unclean.
- The swine has only the one mark: he divideth the hoof and is clovenfooted, yet he cheweth not the cud; he is unclean unto you. You cannot eat pork; leave it alone.
- Their carcase shall ye not touch sits inside the dietary law: it goes into sacrificing the unclean thing and eating it, touching it with the mouth. It does not mean a dead animal in your path cannot be moved out of the way with the hands; the tabernacle itself had to be built by hand with badgers' skins.

- The Most High gave an excellent dietary law: high blood pressure and the like come from eating shellfish. He made these animals and knows what they do; if you eat what he allowed you will be fine. It is simple: if it does not have both fins and scales, leave it alone.
- A fish must have both scales and fins. Tilapia has scales and fins, so it may be eaten if you want it; sardines do not show both, so it looks like sardines are a no.

- Look these up. The ossifrage, the bone breaker, is an older name for the bearded vulture, a scavenger famous for dropping bones on rocks to crack them open. The osprey is the seahawk or fishhawk, a fish eating bird of prey. The kite is a bird of prey in the family of the eagle.
- What is interesting is that all of these are scavengers and hunters; even in the waters the Most High did not want you to have the scavengers, and no shark, a hunter. He gave you this diet because he made these animals and knows what they do.
- After his kind: when the Most High made one, he made many of them, so the rule covers the whole family.
- On duck: the swan's closest relatives include the geese and ducks. The swan is unclean, and duck falls in the family of the swan, so you cannot eat duck; they are related, and it is the same thing.

- If a woman have conceived seed and born a man child, she shall be unclean seven days, according to the days of the separation for her infirmity. Conceived seed means the sperm has met the egg: at intercourse about 300 million sperm enter, most die on the journey, and fertilization is one sperm uniting with the egg to form new life. All of that has to happen; that is conceiving seed.
- At the moment of fertilization a unique genetic code arises, instantly determining gender: an X with an X chromosome is a female, and an X with a Y chromosome is a male. Gender is determined at conception; you do not get to choose it, and there is no changing it afterward.
- Even inside the woman it is a male and a female pronucleus that come together to form the baby: male and female, not two males.
- The seven days match the law of the woman's issue: when her issue is blood, she is put apart seven days; those are the days of her separation spoken of here.

- The plague of leprosy is discerned by the priest. When the hair in the plague is turned white and the plague in sight is deeper than the skin of the flesh, it is a plague of leprosy and the priest pronounces him unclean; if not, the priest shuts him up seven days, and seven days more if the plague is at a stay, and if the plague is somewhat dark and spread not, it is but a scab and he is pronounced clean; but a scab that spreads much abroad after his cleansing is a leprosy.

- That is the albino: if the leprosy covers all the skin, as in an albino, he is still clean. A man can be albino and still be Israel; if his father is Judah, he is clean. It is not about your skin, it is about your nationality.

When Israel, blacks, Hispanics, and Native Americans dye their hair blonde, they are taking after the customs of Esau, emulating who they want to be like, and the word calls that yellow thin hair a plague on our people.
- There is no law against color as such: the scripture names yellow thin hair; but go with what the word says.

- The man whose hair is fallen off his head is bald, yet is he clean; and he whose hair is fallen off toward his face is forehead bald, yet is he clean.
- So do not assume against a brother: if he goes bald naturally, the word says the man is clean, and a brother may be forehead bald with hair further back you cannot see.

**[[Leviticus 13#^v45|Leviticus 13:45-46]]**

![[Leviticus 13#^v45]]

- The leper in whom the plague is, his clothes shall be rent, his head bare, a covering upon his upper lip, and he shall cry, Unclean, unclean; all the days the plague is in him he is defiled, and he shall dwell alone, without the camp.
- Miriam is the example. She and Aaron spake against Moses because of the Ethiopian woman whom he had married, Miriam trying to usurp authority over the man, and the Lord heard it; the cloud departed, and behold, Miriam became leprous, white as snow.
- The Lord said, if her father had but spit in her face, should she not be ashamed seven days? Let her be shut out from the camp seven days, and after that let her be received in again. That is the same law working here: the leper is put without the camp.

- The plague of leprosy can be in a garment, woollen or linen, in the warp or woof or in any thing made of skin, greenish or reddish; it is shewed unto the priest and shut up seven days, and if the plague be spread in the garment it is a fretting leprosy: the garment is burnt in the fire. As with Miriam, the clothing wherein the plague is had to be dealt with.

- If the plague be not spread, the thing is washed and shut up seven days more; if the plague have not changed his colour it is burnt, and if it be somewhat dark after washing, the piece is rent out; if it appear still, it is a spreading plague and burnt with fire; and the garment that the plague departs from is washed the second time and shall be clean. This is the law of the plague of leprosy in a garment, to pronounce it clean or to pronounce it unclean.
