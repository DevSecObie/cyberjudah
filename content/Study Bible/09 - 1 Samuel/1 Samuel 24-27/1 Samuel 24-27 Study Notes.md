# 1 Samuel 24: David Spares Saul in the Cave at Engedi

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 24]]

## Introduction

This session covers 1 Samuel chapters 24 through 27. In the prior chapters Saul was chasing after David, hunting him to kill him; here David twice has Saul delivered into his hand and twice refuses to touch the Lord's anointed. The teaching follows David sparing Saul in the cave at Engedi, the folly of Nabal and the wisdom of Abigail who stayed David from shedding blood, David sparing Saul a second time in the wilderness of Ziph, and David's escape into the land of the Philistines, where Achish gave him Ziklag. The main threads are David's integrity, restraint, and fear of the Most High toward his elder and king, and the lesson of a wife of good understanding who protects her house with her behavior instead of her mouth.

**[[1 Samuel 24#^v1|1 Samuel 24:1-3]]**

![[1 Samuel 24#^v1]]
![[1 Samuel 24#^v2]]
![[1 Samuel 24#^v3]]

- Coming out of the prior chapters, Saul was hunting after David to kill him. When Saul returned from following the Philistines and was told David was in the wilderness of Engedi, he took three thousand chosen men out of all Israel to seek David and his men upon the rocks of the wild goats.
- Saul went into the cave to cover his feet: that means he was in there relieving himself. See the vulnerable position Saul was in, and yet David spared his life; that is why David could say, thou wast delivered into my hand, but I did not take thy life.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Judges 3#^v24|Judges 3:24-25]]**
    ![[Judges 3#^v24]]
    ![[Judges 3#^v25]]
    Eglon's servants saw the doors of the parlour locked and said, Surely he covereth his feet in his summer chamber, and tarried till they were ashamed; covering the feet is the scripture's phrase for relieving yourself.

**[[1 Samuel 24#^v4|1 Samuel 24:4-7]]**

![[1 Samuel 24#^v4]]
![[1 Samuel 24#^v5]]
![[1 Samuel 24#^v6]]
![[1 Samuel 24#^v7]]

- Behold, I will deliver thine enemy into thine hand: who is being referred to as the enemy? His own people; Saul was of the tribe of Benjamin, and David was of the tribe of Judah.
- On the skirt: the Zondervan Compact Bible Dictionary (page 136) shows the basic garments in general use among men of biblical times were the inner tunic, the tunic coat, the girdle, and the cloak, with the headdress and sandals. The word skirt in the KJV usually refers to an article of male, not female, clothing, with meanings of extremity and corner. Where we have our fringes attached to the borders of our garments, that is the bottom part of Saul's robe that David cut off privily.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ruth 3#^v9|Ruth 3:9]]**
    ![[Ruth 3#^v9]]
    The dictionary's own example of the skirt: spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; the skirt is the extremity of the garment.
  - **[[Exodus 28#^v33|Exodus 28:33]]**
    ![[Exodus 28#^v33]]
    The dictionary's other example: the hem of the priestly robe, the border of a male garment.

- Afterward David's heart smote him because he had cut off Saul's skirt, and he said, The Lord forbid that I should do this thing unto my master, the Lord's anointed. So David stayed his servants with these words and suffered them not to rise against Saul.

- When Saul rose out of the cave, David went out after him, cried, My lord the king, and stooped with his face to the earth and bowed himself. He reverenced Saul even though Saul was trying to kill him; that is why the Most High said David is a man after mine own heart. David maintained his integrity.
- David laid the proof before Saul: this day thine eyes have seen how the Lord delivered thee into mine hand in the cave, and some bade me kill thee, but mine eye spared thee, for he is the Lord's anointed. See the skirt of thy robe in my hand: I cut off the skirt and killed thee not; there is neither evil nor transgression in mine hand, yet thou huntest my soul to take it.
- David left the judgment with the Most High: the Lord judge between me and thee, and the Lord avenge me of thee, but mine hand shall not be upon thee; as saith the proverb of the ancients, Wickedness proceedeth from the wicked. After whom is the king of Israel come out? after a dead dog, after a flea.

- Saul made an acknowledgement there because he saw the righteousness of David: I know well that thou shalt surely be king, and that the kingdom of Israel shall be established in thine hand.

  - **[[Isaiah 32#^v1|Isaiah 32:1]]**

- Saul asked David to swear that he would not cut off his seed after him nor destroy his name out of his father's house, and David sware unto Saul; Saul went home, and David and his men gat them up unto the hold.
- David showed so much restraint and respect unto his elder; that is something some of us struggle with today, and we need to use this as an example. The Most High was dealing with David: even though he saw Saul acting wickedly and strangely with him, when he had the opportunity to kill Saul he would not do it.

# 1 Samuel 25: Nabal's Folly and Abigail's Wisdom

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 25]]

**[[1 Samuel 25#^v1|1 Samuel 25:1-3]]**

![[1 Samuel 25#^v2]]

- Samuel died, and all the Israelites gathered together and lamented him and buried him in his house at Ramah; and David arose and went down to the wilderness of Paran.
- There was a very great man in Maon whose possessions were in Carmel, three thousand sheep and a thousand goats, shearing his sheep in Carmel. His name was Nabal, and his wife was Abigail, a woman of good understanding and of a beautiful countenance; but the man was churlish and evil in his doings, and he was of the house of Caleb.

- The request was righteous: thy shepherds were with us, we hurt them not, neither was there ought missing unto them all the while they were in Carmel; ask thy young men and they will shew thee. We come in a good day; give whatsoever cometh to thine hand unto thy servants and to thy son David.

- Nabal answered, Who is David? and who is the son of Jesse? there be many servants now a days that break away every man from his master. He was talking much smack, as if he did not even know who David was, and refused to give of his bread, his water, and his flesh to men he knew not.
- When his young men told David, he said, Gird ye on every man his sword; about four hundred men went up after David, and two hundred abode by the stuff.

**[[1 Samuel 25#^v14|1 Samuel 25:14-17]]**

- One of the young men told Abigail: David sent messengers out of the wilderness to salute our master, and he railed on them. But the men were very good unto us; we were not hurt, neither missed we any thing as long as we were conversant with them; they were a wall unto us both by night and day while we were keeping the sheep. David's men had been giving Nabal's shepherds protection from their enemies.
- Evil is determined against our master and against all his household, for he is such a son of Belial that a man cannot speak to him. Some of us know people like that: you cannot talk to them, they will not reason with you. Consider that Nabal was rich, a very wealthy man, and a lot of times what comes with that is an arrogant spirit.

**[[1 Samuel 25#^v18|1 Samuel 25:18-22]]**

- Abigail made haste and took two hundred loaves, two bottles of wine, five sheep ready dressed, parched corn, clusters of raisins, and cakes of figs, laid them on asses, and sent her servants on before her; but she told not her husband Nabal.
- David had said, Surely in vain have I kept all that this fellow hath in the wilderness; he hath requited me evil for good. He swore to leave Nabal none by the morning light of any that pisseth against the wall; David was coming with intent to destroy the men of that house.

**[[1 Samuel 25#^v23|1 Samuel 25:23-25]]**

![[1 Samuel 25#^v23]]
![[1 Samuel 25#^v24]]
![[1 Samuel 25#^v25]]

- When Abigail saw David she hasted, lighted off the ass, fell at his feet, and said, Upon me, my lord, upon me let this iniquity be. She was not the one who did anything wrong, but she took that iniquity upon herself; she was being that pillar of her house, using wisdom to protect it, for the scripture says she was a woman of good understanding.
- She did not turn her lord over to be destroyed, as some women today would be ready to do; instead she pleaded, Let not my lord regard this man of Belial, even Nabal: for as his name is, so is he; Nabal is his name, and folly is with him. She had not seen the young men David sent, or she would have stepped in then to protect their house.

  Precepts:
  - **[[1 Peter 2#^v18|1 Peter 2:18-19]]**
    ![[1 Peter 2#^v18]]
    ![[1 Peter 2#^v19]]
    Servants, be subject to your masters with all fear, not only to the good and gentle but also to the froward, to those that are like Nabal; enduring grief wrongfully for conscience toward God is thankworthy.
  - **[[1 Peter 3#^v1|1 Peter 3:1-2]]**
    ![[1 Peter 3#^v1]]
    ![[1 Peter 3#^v2]]
    Likewise, ye wives, be in subjection to your own husbands, that if any obey not the word, they may without the word be won by the conversation of the wives, beholding your chaste conversation coupled with fear; this is what Abigail was putting in place.

- Her chaste conversation was her behavior: when she heard David was coming she made that feast and brought it down to meet him, to spare her house. That is the lesson for wives: if your lord is out of the spirit, instead of going railing for railing with him, your behavior should be able to win him over, to make him say, I am tripping, I have a good wife keeping the commandments who stays in a humble spirit. Provoking with the mouth just leaves an unhappy household where people living under the same roof are set against each other.

She asked, Forgive the trespass of thine handmaid; it was Nabal who was out of the spirit, but again that chaste conversation, stepping up for her household.
- The soul of my lord shall be bound in the bundle of life with the Lord thy God, and the souls of thine enemies, them shall he sling out as out of the middle of a sling. When the Lord shall have appointed thee ruler over Israel, this shall be no grief unto thee, that thou hast shed blood causeless or that my lord hath avenged himself; then remember thine handmaid.

- In the morning, when the wine was gone out of Nabal, his wife told him these things, and his heart died within him, and he became as a stone. Remember he had railed, Who is David? but when he sobered up and heard that the coming king had come down to destroy everybody in his house, his heart died within him. About ten days after, the Lord smote Nabal, that he died.

Then David sent and communed with Abigail, to take her to him to wife.
- Abigail bowed herself on her face to the earth and said, Behold, let thine handmaid be a servant to wash the feet of the servants of my lord, and she arose and washed the feet of the servants who delivered the good tidings; that is a very humble act.
- She was probably very joyous and glad to go: a woman living with a man who is hard to deal with, who will not listen, will not take advice, arrogant, maybe verbally abusive, was relieved of that thing when Nabal died, and she hasted and arose with five damsels of hers and became David's wife.

# 1 Samuel 26: David Spares Saul a Second Time

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 26]]

**[[1 Samuel 26#^v1|1 Samuel 26:1-4]]**

![[1 Samuel 26#^v1]]
![[1 Samuel 26#^v2]]
![[1 Samuel 26#^v3]]
![[1 Samuel 26#^v4]]

- The Ziphites came unto Saul to Gibeah, saying, Doth not David hide himself in the hill of Hachilah, which is before Jeshimon? Right here we are going back: chapter 26 is picking up on what we read in chapter 23, when the Ziphites first went up to Saul to deliver David into the king's hand.

  Precepts:
  - **[[1 Samuel 23#^v19|1 Samuel 23:19-20]]**
    ![[1 Samuel 23#^v19]]
    ![[1 Samuel 23#^v20]]
    Then came up the Ziphites to Saul to Gibeah, saying, Doth not David hide himself with us in the strongholds, in the hill of Hachilah? come down, O king, and our part shall be to deliver him into the king's hand; chapter 26 picks this thread back up.

- Saul arose and went down to the wilderness of Ziph, having three thousand chosen men of Israel with him, and pitched in the hill of Hachilah; but David abode in the wilderness, sent out spies, and understood that Saul was come in very deed.

**[[1 Samuel 26#^v5|1 Samuel 26:5-7]]**

- David beheld the place where Saul lay, with Abner the son of Ner, the captain of his host; Saul lay in the trench with his spear stuck in the ground at his bolster, and the people pitched round about him as protection to the king.
- David asked Ahimelech the Hittite and Abishai the son of Zeruiah, the brother of Joab, Who will go down with me to Saul to the camp? and Abishai said, I will go down with thee; and they came to the people by night, and all lay sleeping.

But David said, Destroy him not: for who can stretch forth his hand against the Lord's anointed, and be guiltless?
- David left Saul's end with the Most High: as the Lord liveth, the Lord shall smite him, or his day shall come to die, or he shall descend into battle and perish; the Lord forbid that I should stretch forth mine hand against the Lord's anointed.
- They took the spear and the cruse of water from Saul's bolster and got them away, and no man saw it, nor knew it, neither awaked, for they were all asleep, because a deep sleep from the Lord was fallen upon them.

- As the Lord liveth, ye are worthy to die, because ye have not kept your master, the Lord's anointed; and now see where the king's spear is, and the cruse of water that was at his bolster.

- Let not my blood fall to the earth before the face of the Lord: for the king of Israel is come out to seek a flea, as when one doth hunt a partridge in the mountains.

**[[1 Samuel 26#^v21|1 Samuel 26:21-25]]**

- Saul said, I have sinned: return, my son David, for I will no more do thee harm, because my soul was precious in thine eyes this day: behold, I have played the fool, and have erred exceedingly. Saul was acknowledging once more David's righteousness, because again David had an opportunity to kill Saul and did not put forth his hand.
- David answered, The Lord render to every man his righteousness and his faithfulness: for the Lord delivered thee into my hand to day, but I would not stretch forth mine hand against the Lord's anointed. Again he showed compassion, restraint, and the fear of the Most High.
- That is what we have to get to as far as brotherly love: when we see each other, we see Christ in each other. That is what our nation is lacking, and that is why we have to be that example, as David was in sparing Saul's life.

# 1 Samuel 27: David Dwells in the Land of the Philistines

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 27]]

**[[1 Samuel 27#^v1|1 Samuel 27:1-4]]**

![[1 Samuel 27#^v1]]
![[1 Samuel 27#^v2]]
![[1 Samuel 27#^v3]]
![[1 Samuel 27#^v4]]

- David said in his heart, I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul: there is nothing better for me than that I should speedily escape into the land of the Philistines, and Saul shall despair of me, to seek me any more in any coast of Israel.
- David passed over with the six hundred men that were with him unto Achish the son of Maoch, king of Gath, every man with his household, David with his two wives, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess and Abigail the Carmelitess, Nabal's wife. When it was told Saul that David was fled to Gath, he sought no more again for him.

**[[1 Samuel 27#^v5|1 Samuel 27:5-7]]**

![[1 Samuel 27#^v5]]
![[1 Samuel 27#^v6]]
![[1 Samuel 27#^v7]]

- David said to Achish, If I have found grace in thine eyes, let them give me a place in some town in the country, that I may dwell there: for why should thy servant dwell in the royal city with thee? So Achish gave him Ziklag that day; wherefore Ziklag pertaineth unto the kings of Judah unto this day.
- The time that David dwelt in the country of the Philistines was a full year and four months.

**[[1 Samuel 27#^v8|1 Samuel 27:8-12]]**

- David and his men went up and invaded the Geshurites, and the Gezrites, and the Amalekites, for those nations were of old the inhabitants of the land, as thou goest to Shur, even unto the land of Egypt; and David smote the land, left neither man nor woman alive, took away the sheep, the oxen, the asses, the camels, and the apparel, and returned to Achish.
- When Achish asked, Whither have ye made a road to day? David said, Against the south of Judah, and against the south of the Jerahmeelites, and against the south of the Kenites; and he saved neither man nor woman alive to bring tidings to Gath, saying, Lest they should tell on us.
- Achish believed David, saying, He hath made his people Israel utterly to abhor him; therefore he shall be my servant for ever. So the session closes with David departed out of the presence of Saul, dwelling with Achish in the land of the Philistines at Gath.
