# 1 Samuel 20: David's Empty Seat at the New Moon

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 20]]

## Introduction

This session covers 1 Samuel chapters 20 through 23, the history of David fleeing from Saul: Jonathan's covenant with David and the sign of the arrows, David's empty seat at the new moon, the hallowed bread at Nob and the sword of Goliath, David feigning madness before Achish king of Gath, Doeg the Edomite slaying the priests of the Lord, and Saul pursuing David to Keilah and through the wilderness of Ziph and Maon. The history is pretty straightforward reading, so the teaching pulls in only a few precepts: Christ's own recounting of the shewbread, and the witness of scripture on trusting an enemy, shown in Doeg the Edomite.

- That first day Saul spake not anything, for he thought something had befallen David, that surely he was not clean; but on the second day of the month, with David's place empty again, Saul asked Jonathan, Wherefore cometh not the son of Jesse to meat?

- Saul got wind that his own son was hiding David out. When Jonathan covered for David, Saul's anger was kindled against Jonathan: as long as the son of Jesse liveth upon the ground, thou shalt not be established, nor thy kingdom.
- Saul cast a javelin at Jonathan to smite him, whereby Jonathan knew that it was determined of his father to slay David; from here the history follows Saul pursuing David.

# 1 Samuel 21: The Hallowed Bread and the Feigned Madness

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 21]]

**[[1 Samuel 21#^v6|1 Samuel 21:6]]**

![[1 Samuel 21#^v6]]

- The priest gave David hallowed bread, for there was no bread there but the shewbread that was taken from before the Lord. Remember David had been out in the field three days, fearful of king Saul, when he came to Nob in need.
- Link this history with the words of Christ: when the Pharisees challenged his disciples for plucking the ears of corn on the sabbath day, Christ recounted this very history of David and the shewbread. Precept this in your bibles if you do not have it already.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Mark 2#^v23|Mark 2:23-24]]**
    ![[Mark 2#^v23]]
    ![[Mark 2#^v24]]
    The Pharisees challenged Christ's disciples for plucking the ears of corn on the sabbath day, saying it was not lawful.
  - **[[Mark 2#^v25|Mark 2:25-26]]**
    ![[Mark 2#^v25]]
    ![[Mark 2#^v26]]
    Christ answered, Have ye never read what David did, when he had need, and was an hungred? This is that history, David eating the shewbread which is not lawful to eat but for the priests.

# 1 Samuel 22: Never Trust Thine Enemy

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 22]]

**[[1 Samuel 22#^v9|1 Samuel 22:9-10]]**

![[1 Samuel 22#^v9]]
![[1 Samuel 22#^v10]]

- Here you see that Saul had placed an Edomite over his servants: Doeg the Edomite, which was set over the servants of Saul, answered and reported that David had come to Nob and that Ahimelech had inquired of the Lord for him, given him victuals, and given him the sword of Goliath the Philistine.
- The scriptures tell us we are never supposed to trust our enemies, yet what we are reading is Saul with an Edomite set over his servants.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 12#^v10|Ecclesiasticus 12:10]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 12#^v10]]
    Never trust thine enemy; but Saul had placed an Edomite over his servants.

**[[1 Samuel 22#^v17|1 Samuel 22:17-19]]**

![[1 Samuel 22#^v17]]
![[1 Samuel 22#^v18]]
![[1 Samuel 22#^v19]]

- Saul commanded his footmen to turn and slay the priests of the Lord, but the servants of the king would not put forth their hand to fall upon the priests of the Lord. Saul was able to get the Edomite to do what his own men would not do: Doeg turned and fell upon the priests, and slew on that day fourscore and five persons that did wear a linen ephod, and smote Nob, the city of the priests, with the edge of the sword.
- Go back to Genesis and see the spirit that has been placed on this man from the beginning: Esau comforted himself with the purpose of killing his brother Jacob. Just as in the time of Saul, even to this very day, when Israel suffers violence at the hands of the other nations, especially Esau, he gets comfort out of killing us; when Saul appointed Doeg to that task, you can best believe he was quite happy over it and most likely found much pleasure in killing the priests.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 27#^v41|Genesis 27:41-42]]**
    ![[Genesis 27#^v41]]
    ![[Genesis 27#^v42]]
    Esau hated Jacob because of the blessing, and purposed in his heart to slay his brother; Rebekah warned Jacob that Esau, as touching thee, doth comfort himself, purposing to kill thee. That comfort in killing is the spirit seen again in Doeg the Edomite.

# 1 Samuel 23: Saul Hunts David in the Wilderness

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 23]]

**[[1 Samuel 23#^v21|1 Samuel 23:21-23]]**

![[1 Samuel 23#^v21]]
![[1 Samuel 23#^v22]]
![[1 Samuel 23#^v23]]

- When the Ziphites offered to deliver David up, Saul blessed them and sent them back: go, prepare yet, and know and see his place where his haunt is, and who hath seen him there, and come ye again to me with the certainty.
- Saul is asking the Ziphites for intel. He knows David is a man of war, and he wants to be absolutely sure David is exactly where they say he is before he comes down; for it was told him that David dealeth very subtilly.
