# 1 Samuel 4: The Ark of God Is Taken

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 4]]

## Introduction

This session covers 1 Samuel chapters 4 through 7, the war between Israel and one of our main enemies in the Old Testament, the Philistines, and their weak fake god Dagon. Samson had already crushed Dagon's house down on the Philistines in the book of Judges, yet they rebuilt him, and the prophets keep having to humiliate their gods. Here Israel loses the ark of the covenant in battle, the Most High plagues the Philistines from city to city until they send it back, and the prophet Samuel rises up for the nation of Israel, showing that prayer is a spiritual power: when a righteous man sends up prayer, the Most High will come down in thunder against the enemies of his people. Reading about our forefathers being attacked by the other nations prepares us for the same persecution today; as the works increase, so does the attack, so stay girded up and stay focused.

- The two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, came with the ark, and when it entered the camp all Israel shouted with a great shout so that the earth rang again. That is how excited and ecstatic Israel was to have the ark of the covenant among them; they felt their enemies could not destroy them now.
- So what is the ark of the covenant, and what does it contain? The Lord told Moses to hew two tables of stone like the first ones he broke, and the Lord rewrote the ten commandments on them with his own finger, and Moses put the tables in the ark. The ark of the covenant contained the commandments that God wrote.

- The law is spiritual, and the law has literal spiritual power; the commandments have always been our source of power as a nation. The church teaches that the laws are done away with, but watch in these chapters how seriously the Lord regarded the ark that contained his commandments.
- Watch also why the ark did not have the effect Israel expected in this battle: there were still Israelites in the midst of idolatry with idols among them, and until those idols were put away we could not be delivered from our enemies. That is the lesson Samuel drives home in chapter 7.

- When the Philistines heard the noise of the shout and understood that the ark of the Lord was come into the camp, they were afraid, saying, God is come into the camp; woe unto us, for there hath not been such a thing heretofore. They had never had to battle Israel with the ark of the covenant in the camp.
- Think on what they said: Who shall deliver us out of the hand of these mighty Gods? These are the Gods that smote the Egyptians with all the plagues. The nations knew what the Most High did to Egypt; it was known throughout the earth, and to this day real history about what the Hebrews and their God did to the Egyptians is still taught on the other side of the earth. With the ark of the commandments among us, the nations understood they were dealing with something above a regular people.
- Everything our people try to glory in about Kemet, the Lord smote down. The ten plagues humiliated ten plus different gods that the Egyptians served, all their power, all their magicians and wizards. Come out of that Kemet movement; the Lord destroyed it and said Egypt would not rise again as a power in the earth.
- Still the Philistines rallied: be strong, and quit yourselves like men, O ye Philistines, that ye be not servants unto the Hebrews, as they have been to you. They remembered serving Israel in the days of the Judges and refused to be slaves again, so they got ready to fight.

- The ark of God was taken, and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were slain. Those two were wicked as hell, sleeping with the women that assembled at the door of the congregation and finessing the people's offerings, so in this battle the Most High allowed their lives to be taken, exactly as had been prophesied against the house of Eli.

- A man of Benjamin ran out of the army to Shiloh the same day with his clothes rent and earth upon his head, and found Eli sitting by the wayside watching, for his heart trembled for the ark of God. Eli was very protective of the ark; he did not want anything to happen to it.
- Eli was the high priest who groomed Samuel up, and he was righteous as far as himself, but his sons were devils, and he honoured his sons above the Most High by not rebuking them like a true leader should have.
- Eli was ninety and eight years old and his eyes were dim, and when the messenger told him Israel fled, the slaughter was great, thy two sons are dead, and the ark of God is taken, at the mention of the ark of God he fell from off the seat backward by the side of the gate, and his neck brake, and he died, for he was an old man, and heavy. The shock of the ark being taken is what took him off that seat.
- He had judged Israel forty years, and his death was ultimately part of the judgment for how he dealt with his sons, in that he did not correct them as he should have.

# 1 Samuel 5: Dagon Falls Before the Ark

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 5]]

- The Philistines took the ark of God from Ebenezer to Ashdod, and had the audacity to bring it into the house of Dagon and set it by Dagon, a fake god the Lord had already exposed. Now watch what the ark did to their idol.
- When they of Ashdod arose early on the morrow, Dagon was fallen upon his face to the earth before the ark of the Lord. This was no little souvenir you could knock over; it was a large statue, and nobody had been in there to touch it.
- They set him in his place again, as if maybe the wind blew a little harder, and the next morning Dagon was fallen on his face again before the ark, with the head of Dagon and both the palms of his hands cut off upon the threshold; only the stump of Dagon was left to him. No man did this: that is the power of the ark of God. Therefore the priests of Dagon tread not on the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod unto this day.

- The punishment followed the ark: the hand of the Lord was against Gath with a very great destruction, and he smote the men of the city, both small and great, and they had emerods in their secret parts. Moving it to another city changed nothing; the affliction went with it.

- The lords of the Philistines were told, Send away the ark of the God of Israel, and let it go again to his own place; for there was a deadly destruction throughout all the city, and the hand of God was very heavy there. The men that died not were smitten with the emerods, and the cry of the city went up to heaven. That was the great suffering and affliction the Philistines earned for thinking they had done something by taking the ark.

- The ark of the Lord was in the country of the Philistines seven months, and the Philistines called for the priests and the diviners, asking how to send it back to his place. Seven months of that plague and they had to get it out of the land.
- The priests answered, send it not empty, but in any wise return him a trespass offering; then ye shall be healed. A trespass offering acknowledges you did wrong. The offering was five golden emerods and five golden mice, according to the number of the lords of the Philistines, images of the very things that marred the land, and ye shall give glory unto the God of Israel; peradventure he will lighten his hand from off you, and from off your gods, and from off your land.
- Their own priests warned them from Israel's history: wherefore then do ye harden your hearts, as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts? They did not want to end up like Egypt, so they said, let us learn the lesson and get this ark back.

That shows us we have to have a level of discipline over our eyes; with these devices today it is very easy to take a two second look at what you know you should not be looking at. Guard your eyes.

# 1 Samuel 7: Put Away the Strange Gods, and the Lord Thundered

Read the chapter → [[1 Samuel 7]]

**[[1 Samuel 7#^v1|1 Samuel 7:1-2]]**

![[1 Samuel 7#^v1]]
![[1 Samuel 7#^v2]]

- The men of Kirjath-jearim fetched up the ark of the Lord into the house of Abinadab in the hill, and sanctified Eleazar his son to keep it. While the ark abode in Kirjath-jearim the time was long, twenty years, and all the house of Israel lamented after the Lord.

**[[1 Samuel 7#^v3|1 Samuel 7:3-4]]**

![[1 Samuel 7#^v3]]
![[1 Samuel 7#^v4]]

- Samuel spake unto all the house of Israel: If ye do return unto the Lord with all your hearts, then put away the strange gods and Ashtaroth from among you, and prepare your hearts unto the Lord, and serve him only, and he will deliver you out of the hand of the Philistines. Go back to how we lost the battle even with the ark of God among us: we had strange gods among us. Deliverance is not just having the ark, it is applying the commandments that are in the ark, thou shalt have no other gods before me.
- Who is Ashtoreth? Astaroth in demonology is a chief figure of hell, named after the Near Eastern goddess Astarte, and Astarte is the Hellenized form of Ashtoreth, a form of Ishtar, the goddess called the queen of heaven, associated with love, beauty, sex, and war. Samuel was exhorting the people to depart from these fake gods and goddesses of the nations, and to this day there are Israelites that have not put away Ashtoreth from among them.
- Easter is at its roots the celebration of Ishtar, the goddess of fertility and sex. When Constantine set out to merge paganism with the faith of the apostles to bring everything under one empire, pagan rituals like Easter and Saturnalia were dressed up as honoring Christ, and that was the birth of Roman Catholicism. Nothing about it mathematically adds up to Christ rising, the apostles never celebrated it or taught it, and a bunny laying eggs has nothing to do with your Lord and Saviour; it is not of Christ.
- Then the children of Israel did put away Baalim and Ashtaroth, and served the Lord only. That is repentance.

- As Samuel was offering up the burnt offering, the Philistines drew near to battle, but the Lord thundered with a great thunder on that day upon the Philistines, and discomfited them, and they were smitten before Israel. That was no regular thunder; it was the Lord entering the battle himself. They had repented, put away the false gods, and the prayer of the righteous man reached his ears, so now the Philistines had to deal with the Most High.
- The Philistines were so terrified they put up no fight; the men of Israel went out of Mizpeh and pursued them, smiting them until they came under Beth-car. Then Samuel took a stone, set it between Mizpeh and Shen, and called the name of it Ebenezer, saying, Hitherto hath the Lord helped us.
- Samuel was one of the most respected prophets in the whole history of the prophets: he established the kingdom and anointed Saul, and by his faithfulness he was found a true prophet. Faithfulness translates loyalty. He came up under Eli, watched Eli's sons go wicked, Eli die, and the nation take losses, but Samuel stayed loyal to the Most High, just as Abraham's faith was tried again and again and he remained faithful.

- That yearly circuit is leadership making its rounds, establishing the people and coming back to check on them, the same example Paul followed with the churches. These chapters close with the ark lost and regained, and Samuel rising up for the nation: prayer is a superpower, and that is how our mighty forefathers rolled, from Moses to Samuel to Judas Maccabeus all the way to Christ. Keep the commandments and send up that prayer, and the Most High will come down in thunder against your enemies.
