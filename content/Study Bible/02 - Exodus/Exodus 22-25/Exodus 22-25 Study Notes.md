# Exodus 22: The Civil Laws of Restitution

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 22]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Exodus 22|Exodus 22]] through 25: the civil laws of restitution, the laws of righteous judgment and the three feasts in the year, the blood of the covenant sworn at Sinai, and the pattern of the tabernacle with the ark, the table, and the candlestick. The session opens on [[Matthew 5#^v17|Matthew 5:17]], that Christ came not to destroy the law but to fulfil, so the mind is right going into the reading: these laws still apply, though some are in a different form or in suspension because of the captivity and because of what Christ took away.

**[[Exodus 22#^v1|Exodus 22:1]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v1]]

- If a man steal an ox or a sheep and kill it or sell it, he restores five oxen for an ox and four sheep for a sheep. We are in a different situation where we do not keep oxen and sheep, but the principle of restitution still applies among brothers and sisters in the body: if something is stolen, the law written here governs the payback. Do not be stealing.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Matthew 5#^v17|Matthew 5:17-18]]**
    ![[Matthew 5#^v17]]
    ![[Matthew 5#^v18]]
    Christ came not to destroy the law or the prophets but to fulfil; not one jot or tittle passes from the law till all be fulfilled.

**[[Exodus 22#^v2|Exodus 22:2-4]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v2]]
![[Exodus 22#^v3]]
![[Exodus 22#^v4]]

- If a thief be found breaking up means breaking in. He entered your home and you defended yourself and he died: that is what is called self-defense today, and no blood is shed for him, meaning there is no judgment. The judgment was already passed in the breaking in.
- If the sun be risen upon him, the thief got away, and there shall be blood shed for him means there shall be a judgment; then the verse gives the judgment. He makes full restitution, and if he have nothing he is sold for his theft: not sold into slavery, but into work servitude until he pays off the debt of what he stole.
- If the theft be found in his hand alive, he restores double. Verse three covers the thief who already got rid of what he stole; verse four covers the one still holding it.

**[[Exodus 22#^v5|Exodus 22:5-6]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v5]]
![[Exodus 22#^v6]]

- These are self-explanatory: if a man lets his beast feed in another man's field or vineyard, he makes restitution of the best of his own field and vineyard; and he that kindles a fire that consumes another man's corn surely makes restitution.

**[[Exodus 22#^v7|Exodus 22:7-9]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v7]]
![[Exodus 22#^v8]]
![[Exodus 22#^v9]]

- If a man deliver money or stuff to his neighbor to keep and it be stolen, the thief found pays double. If the thief be not found, the master of the house is brought unto the judges, to see whether he put his hand unto his neighbor's goods, whether he was part of a scheme to deceive the owner out of his goods.
- For all manner of trespass, whether for ox, ass, sheep, raiment, or any lost thing, both parties come before the judges, and whom the judges condemn pays double unto his neighbor.

**[[Exodus 22#^v10|Exodus 22:10-11]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v10]]
![[Exodus 22#^v11]]

- If a beast delivered to a neighbor to keep die or be hurt or driven away, no man seeing it, an oath of the Lord is between them both. In layman's terms he swears: I did not take the stuff, I did not set you up, I do not know where it is. The owner accepts the oath and the matter ends there; it is not to be spoken of again.
- Paul uses this civil law to show how the Most High is unchangeable. An oath for confirmation is the end of all strife; God confirmed his counsel by an oath, and by two immutable things, that it is impossible for God to lie and that he will not take back what went out of his mouth, we have strong consolation. The same way, once the oath of this law is applied, the matter is over.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Hebrews 6#^v16|Hebrews 6:16-18]]**
    ![[Hebrews 6#^v16]]
    ![[Hebrews 6#^v17]]
    ![[Hebrews 6#^v18]]
    An oath for confirmation is to men an end of all strife; God, willing to shew unto the heirs of promise the immutability of his counsel, confirmed it by an oath.

**[[Exodus 22#^v12|Exodus 22:12-15]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v12]]
![[Exodus 22#^v13]]
![[Exodus 22#^v14]]
![[Exodus 22#^v15]]

- If the thing kept be stolen from him, he makes restitution; if it be torn in pieces, he brings it for witness and does not make it good.
- If a man borrow ought of his neighbor, ought meaning anything, and it be hurt or die with the owner not present, he surely makes it good. You borrowed it, it got messed up, you owe the man.
- But if the owner be with it, or if it be an hired thing, it came for its hire. If you came to plow a man's field for ten shekels with your own donkey and your donkey breaks his leg, you cannot expect the owner to pay for your own faulty equipment; the same as a business loan today, the property owner is not liable when your own equipment breaks down.

**[[Exodus 22#^v16|Exodus 22:16-17]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v16]]
![[Exodus 22#^v17]]

- If a man entice a maid that is not betrothed and lie with her, he shall surely endow her to be his wife; he has to sign the papers. He is at fault for not going through the proper channels of marriage, and she is at fault for being enticed and laying down.
- The proper channel was that the father married off the daughter. To marry thy daughter is to perform a weighty matter, for the father gives her over to that man for the rest of her life and is out of all watching over her; so she is to be given to a man of understanding, one keeping the commandments as a wise man. In our own land the daughter went from her father's house to a husband's house with the blessing of the father.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 7#^v25|Ecclesiasticus 7:25]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 7#^v25]]
    Marrying off the daughter was the father's weighty matter; she was given to a man of understanding.

- If her father utterly refuse to give her unto him, he does not agree with the backdoor marriage, the man still pays money according to the dowry of virgins. [[Genesis 34|Genesis 34]] gives the example with Dinah: Shechem and his father Hamor begged Jacob for her and offered never so much dowry, for Shechem understood the union was not supposed to take place, the Hebrews with the Hamites.
- The dowry of virgins was fifty shekels of silver, paid by the man who laid hold on a damsel not betrothed, and she became his wife whom he could not put away all his days. And if a husband afterward brought up an evil name upon a virgin of Israel, saying he found her not a maid, the tokens of her virginity, the cloth from the bedchamber her father kept, were spread before the elders; the man was chastised and amerced in an hundred shekels of silver for the false report.

- He that sacrificeth unto any god save unto the Lord only shall be utterly destroyed.

**[[Exodus 22#^v21|Exodus 22:21]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v21]]

- This is twofold, and first and foremost the stranger is your brother from a different area, our own people. [[Leviticus 25|Leviticus 25]] shows it: if thy brother be waxen poor, though he be a stranger or a sojourner, sojourn meaning travel, thou shalt relieve him that he may live with thee. A brother of another tribe who falls on hard times and travels down to live with you is that stranger; neither vex him nor oppress him, for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt.

![[Exodus 22#^v22]]
![[Exodus 22#^v23]]
![[Exodus 22#^v24]]

- Ye shall not afflict any widow or fatherless child. A wife without a husband already has a hard life, and a son or daughter without a father the same; do not make their life harder than it already is.
- If thou afflict them in any wise and they cry unto the Most High, he will surely hear their cry, and his wrath shall wax hot: he will kill you with the sword, and your own wives shall be widows and your children fatherless. That is the Lord's plain warning against mistreating those less fortunate.

**[[Exodus 22#^v25|Exodus 22:25]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v25]]

- Usury means interest. You cannot charge your brother or sister interest: if you lend twenty dollars, you get twenty dollars back, nothing more, nothing less; no little bit more off the top. This is the same law read in [[Leviticus 25#^v36|Leviticus 25:36-37]].

**[[Exodus 22#^v26|Exodus 22:26-27]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v26]]
![[Exodus 22#^v27]]

- If thou take thy neighbor's raiment to pledge, deliver it unto him by that the sun goeth down, for that is his covering only; wherein shall he sleep? The tunics and overcoats worn then were also sleeping garments to keep a man warm at night, especially the poor.
- A well-off man does not have to work for you; this speaks of the less fortunate who extend their services for money and give what is most valuable to them, their raiment, as the pledge. If he cries unto the Most High, he will hear, for he is gracious.

**[[Exodus 22#^v28|Exodus 22:28]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v28]]

- Thou shalt not revile the gods means do not hate or speak evil of your leaders. The word gods here is the leaders over you: the Most High says in the psalm, I have said, Ye are gods, and all of you are children of the Most High, speaking of us.
- [[Acts 23|Acts 23]] is the example: when Paul spoke against the high priest, they that stood by said, Revilest thou God's high priest? And Paul answered, I wist not, brethren, that he was the high priest: for it is written, Thou shalt not speak evil of the ruler of thy people, quoting this very verse.

**[[Exodus 22#^v29|Exodus 22:29-30]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v29]]
![[Exodus 22#^v30]]

- Thou shalt not delay to offer the first of thy ripe fruits and of thy liquors, and the firstborn of thy sons shalt thou give unto me. The firstborn sons that opened the matrix were consecrated unto the Lord, and the father redeemed them back from the priest with shekels.
- Likewise with the oxen and the sheep: seven days the firstling stayed with his dam, his mother, and on the eighth day it was given. The Lord did not want it too young, taken as soon as it popped out; it had to be its appointed time with the mother, then brought to the priest.

**[[Exodus 22#^v31|Exodus 22:31]]**

![[Exodus 22#^v31]]

- Ye shall be holy men unto me: neither shall ye eat any flesh that is torn of beasts in the field; ye shall cast it to the dogs. This is twofold, and the dogs are the strangers of other nations, the aliens and foreigners.
- Always get the context of the law, because sometimes the stranger is your brother and sometimes it is literally the other nations. [[Deuteronomy 14#^v21|Deuteronomy 14:21]] doubles this law: that which dieth of itself thou shalt give unto the stranger that is in thy gates, or sell it unto an alien, for thou art an holy people unto the Lord thy God. Cast the torn flesh to the other nations; ye shall not eat it, because ye are holy people.

# Exodus 23: Righteous Judgment, the Three Feasts, and the Angel of the Covenant

- Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil. Enter not into the path of the wicked; sinners entice, saying come with us, cast in thy lot among us, but walk not thou in the way with them, for their feet run to evil and make haste to shed blood. Many of our young men's lives end short because they follow a multitude to do evil. To be in the midst of the singing, like the whole school hollering fight, fight, fight, is to be in the multitude the Most High warns of; a soul without knowledge of the laws of God hastes with his feet and sins.

**[[Exodus 23#^v3|Exodus 23:3]]**

![[Exodus 23#^v3]]

- Neither shalt thou countenance a poor man in his cause. This deals with judgment: do not be a respecter of persons because a man is poor, as if he is automatically wrong when he brings a matter against a rich man. The judges were charged to hear the small as well as the great.
- In this corrupt system in Babylon, how innocent or guilty you come out depends on how much lawyer you can afford. Our law was holy and righteous because none of that made a difference: either you were right or you were wrong, whether poor or rich. The Pharisees learned the ways of the heathen and began respecting persons, which the book of James speaks against.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Deuteronomy 1#^v16|Deuteronomy 1:16-17]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 1#^v16]]
    ![[Deuteronomy 1#^v17]]
    Judge righteously between every man and his brother; ye shall not respect persons in judgment, but hear the small as well as the great.

![[Exodus 23#^v4]]
![[Exodus 23#^v5]]

- If thou meet thine enemy's ox or his ass going astray, thou shalt surely bring it back to him again; if thou see the ass of him that hateth thee lying under his burden, thou shalt surely help with him. This enemy that hates you is your brother, your own nation, not the other nations as [[Matthew 5#^v44|Matthew 5:44]] is misconstrued to mean.
- [[Deuteronomy 22#^v1|Deuteronomy 22:1-4]] proves it, saying the same law with thy brother in the place of thine enemy: thou shalt not see thy brother's ox or sheep go astray; in any case, no matter how you feel, bring them again unto thy brother. And if thou know him not, keep it in thine own house until thy brother seek after it. He is still your brother even if you never grew up with him; that is real love for your own people.
- Deuteronomy means the second law; it speaks the law plain in case you did not understand it here. Keep [[Deuteronomy 22#^v1|Deuteronomy 22:1-4]] and [[Leviticus 19#^v17|Leviticus 19:17-18]] in your hip pocket with this verse.

- Keep thee far from a false matter; the innocent and righteous slay thou not, for the Most High will not justify the wicked.
- Thou shalt take no gift, for the gift blindeth the wise and perverteth the words of the righteous: do not take a bribe when it comes to judgment; be upright and honest.

![[Exodus 23#^v9]]

- The seventh year ties into the count of the jubilee: seven sabbaths of years to the forty-ninth year, and in the fiftieth year no plowing of the land, which the law takes up later.

**[[Exodus 23#^v12|Exodus 23:12]]**

- When the Lord sets Israel back up to rule, the nations will kick and scream at first, but once these laws are applied there will be righteousness reigning over the earth like they have never seen, and all will be refreshed on the sabbath, spiritually and physically.

**[[Exodus 23#^v13|Exodus 23:13]]**

![[Exodus 23#^v13]]

- The Lord says this same thing three ways in this one chapter, and the other two places show the meaning: verse 24, thou shalt not bow down to their gods nor serve them nor do after their works; and verses 32 and 33, thou shalt make no covenant with them nor with their gods, for if thou serve their gods it will surely be a snare unto thee. Make no mention means do not learn how to serve and worship them.
- Joshua and Deuteronomy double it: neither make mention of the name of their gods, nor cause to swear by them, neither serve them, nor bow yourselves unto them; and if thy brother or son or wife entice thee secretly, saying, Let us go and serve other gods, thou shalt not consent. Wherever you are, do not go trying to find out how the nations worship their gods.

# Exodus 24: The Blood of the Covenant

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 24]]

**[[Exodus 24#^v1|Exodus 24:1-3]]**

- Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel were called up to worship afar off, and Moses alone came near the Lord.
- Moses told the people all the words of the Lord and all the judgments, and all the people answered with one voice: All the words which the Lord hath said will we do. That was us making a verbal agreement with the Lord; we are our ancestors back on the earth again.
- All the hell we catch today, we have no excuse for: it is our fault, because we agreed to do all the words the Lord had spoken. The Lord hates for us to go through it, but where he said all of this would come back on us, God is not a man that he should lie.

- Moses took half of the blood in basons and half he sprinkled on the altar; then he took the book of the covenant and read in the audience of the people, and they said, All that the Lord hath said will we do, and be obedient. And he sprinkled the blood on the people: Behold the blood of the covenant, which the Lord hath made with you concerning all these words. This sprinkling ties in later to the book of Hebrews and its teaching on sacrifice.
- Notice verse seven: Moses had a book of the covenant written and read it before he ever went up into the mount for the forty days. It was more than ten commandments; a whole book of what was to be done was already broken down before he went up.
- Because we swore the oath, all we can do now is pray the Lord have mercy on us and return to redeem us from those that hate us.

The Corinthians Paul wrote were Israelites, for Greece was not even a power on the earth in Moses' day. It was Christ dealing with Israel in the wilderness on behalf of the Most High.

# Exodus 25: The Pattern of the Tabernacle

Read the chapter → [[Exodus 25]]

**[[Exodus 25#^v1|Exodus 25:1-2]]**

![[Exodus 25#^v1]]
![[Exodus 25#^v2]]

- Speak unto the children of Israel, that they bring me an offering: of every man that giveth it willingly with his heart ye shall take my offering. The Lord wants a willing heart in giving, not grudgingly or of necessity or only because the law says so, but because you know your giving benefits someone in need or the house of God, whatever it needs, the lights, the roof, the floor, the tables, the leadership's travel.

**[[Exodus 25#^v3|Exodus 25:3-7]]**

![[Exodus 25#^v3]]
![[Exodus 25#^v4]]

- The offering was gold, silver, brass, blue, purple, and scarlet, fine linen, goats' hair, rams' skins dyed red, badgers' skins, shittim wood, oil for the light, spices, and onyx stones for the ephod and the breastplate. Thank the Most High he requires no gold and silver now, for half of us would have nothing to give; under the new covenant the offering is the body presented a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable.
- The badgers' skins settle a confusion: the Lord commanded Israel to handle animal skins and hair to build his own tabernacle. So the laws about not touching that which is defiled, the carcass of the unclean beast, go into the matter of sacrifice and coming into the temple, not into everyday handling. A boar-bristle brush is not sin; brush your hair, brothers.
