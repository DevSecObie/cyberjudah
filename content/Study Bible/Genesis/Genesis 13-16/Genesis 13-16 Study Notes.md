# Genesis 13: The Peacemaker and the Promise

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 13]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Genesis 13|Genesis 13]] through 16. Genesis is the book of the genetics and the genealogy: it shows how Israel comes from the lineage of Shem, and where every other nation finds its beginning.

**[[Genesis 13#^v1|Genesis 13:1-2]]**

![[Genesis 13#^v1]]
![[Genesis 13#^v2]]

- When the scriptures speak of our forefathers being rich, it is not carnal wealth. Abram was rich in cattle, silver, and gold: livestock, land, and the things that take care of a family. That is true wealth.
- We have to be retaught what wealth is: build houses, dwell in them, plant gardens, and eat the fruit of them, and learn to provide for ourselves.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Jeremiah 29#^v5|Jeremiah 29:5]]**
    ![[Jeremiah 29#^v5]]
    Build houses and plant gardens and eat the fruit of them; that is the wealth the Most High teaches.

**[[Genesis 13#^v5|Genesis 13:5-7]]**

![[Genesis 13#^v5]]
![[Genesis 13#^v6]]
![[Genesis 13#^v7]]

- Lot was Abram's nephew: Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran, and Haran begat Lot.
- Their substance was so great they could not dwell together. That substance came from Egypt, where Pharaoh entreated Abram well for Sarai's sake and gave him sheep, oxen, servants, and camels; the maidservants from Egypt are where Hagar comes from.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 11#^v27|Genesis 11:27]]**
    ![[Genesis 11#^v27]]
    Haran begat Lot; Lot was Abram's nephew.
  - **[[Genesis 12#^v16|Genesis 12:16]]**
    ![[Genesis 12#^v16]]
    Pharaoh gave Abram livestock and menservants and maidservants; this is where the household's wealth and Hagar came from.

**[[Genesis 13#^v8|Genesis 13:8-9]]**

![[Genesis 13#^v8]]
![[Genesis 13#^v9]]

- Abram said, Let there be no strife, for we be brethren. That was the spirit of Abram: he would de-escalate and look for solutions. If you go left I go right; if you go right I go left; you cannot argue with that.
- Be solution minded and fix problems using the scriptures; blessed are the peacemakers.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Matthew 5#^v9|Matthew 5:9]]**
    ![[Matthew 5#^v9]]
    Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called the children of God; Abram was a peacemaker.

**[[Genesis 13#^v10|Genesis 13:10-13]]**

![[Genesis 13#^v10]]
![[Genesis 13#^v11]]
![[Genesis 13#^v12]]
![[Genesis 13#^v13]]

- Lot lifted up his eyes and chose the well watered plain of Jordan, fertile like the garden of the Lord, and pitched his tent toward Sodom. Toward Sodom became in Sodom; [[Genesis 14#^v12|Genesis 14:12]] shows Lot dwelt in Sodom.
- The men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the Lord exceedingly: they were lawless, without principles, and given to sexual freedom. That is why the city was overthrown.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 14#^v12|Genesis 14:12]]**
    ![[Genesis 14#^v12]]
    Lot dwelt in Sodom; pitching toward Sodom ended with dwelling in it.

**[[Genesis 13#^v14|Genesis 13:14-17]]**

![[Genesis 13#^v14]]
![[Genesis 13#^v15]]
![[Genesis 13#^v16]]
![[Genesis 13#^v17]]

- The Most High reiterated the promise He made when Abram first entered the land: unto thy seed will I give this land, and thy seed shall be as the dust of the earth.
- This is the same covenant language spoken over the children of Israel: as the sand of the sea which cannot be measured nor numbered.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Genesis 12#^v7|Genesis 12:7]]**
    ![[Genesis 12#^v7]]
    Unto thy seed will I give this land; the promise was made at Abram's first altar.
  - **[[Hosea 1#^v10|Hosea 1:10]]**
    ![[Hosea 1#^v10]]
    The number of the children of Israel shall be as the sand of the sea; they shall be called the sons of the living God.

# Genesis 14: The War of the Kings and Melchizedek

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 14]]

**[[Genesis 14#^v1|Genesis 14:1-3]]**

![[Genesis 14#^v1]]
![[Genesis 14#^v2]]
![[Genesis 14#^v3]]

- Four kings, the Mesopotamian kings, made an allegiance against five kings; this was a world war, nations and kingdoms making alliances to war against other nations. Shinar is modern day Iraq.
- The king of Bela is the one king whose name is not given; Bela is the city, which is Zoar. The vale of Siddim is the salt sea, known today as the Dead Sea.

**[[Genesis 14#^v4|Genesis 14:4-11]]**

![[Genesis 14#^v4]]
![[Genesis 14#^v5]]
![[Genesis 14#^v6]]
![[Genesis 14#^v7]]
![[Genesis 14#^v8]]
![[Genesis 14#^v9]]
![[Genesis 14#^v10]]
![[Genesis 14#^v11]]

- The five kings served Chedorlaomer twelve years and rebelled in the thirteenth; the four kings defeated them and took the goods of Sodom and Gomorrah.
- The vale of Siddim was full of slimepits: bitumen, tar, petroleum. The same region is rich in oil to this day.

**[[Genesis 14#^v12|Genesis 14:12-16]]**

![[Genesis 14#^v12]]
![[Genesis 14#^v13]]
![[Genesis 14#^v14]]
![[Genesis 14#^v15]]
![[Genesis 14#^v16]]

- Because Lot dwelt in Sodom he was carried away captive with his goods, and one that escaped told Abram the Hebrew.
- Abram armed his trained servants, three hundred and eighteen, born in his own house. That tells you Abram trained them: he was a warrior fit for battle who knew how to conduct war, nothing like the frail figure in the whitewashed images.
- He divided his men and moved by night; that is military strategy. With three hundred and eighteen men and his three confederates he smote four kingdoms and brought back Lot, the goods, and the people.
- The victory of battle stands not in the multitude of an host; strength comes from heaven. Whether with many or with them that have no power, the battle is not yours but God's; our forefathers proved it, as when Asa cried to the Lord and a million Ethiopians fell before Judah and Benjamin.

  Precepts:
  - **[[1 Maccabees 3#^v18|1 Maccabees 3:18-19]]**
    ![[1 Maccabees 3#^v18]]
    ![[1 Maccabees 3#^v19]]
    It is no hard matter for many to be shut up in the hands of a few; the victory of battle standeth not in the multitude of an host.
  - **[[2 Chronicles 20#^v15|2 Chronicles 20:15]]**
    ![[2 Chronicles 20#^v15]]
    Be not afraid by reason of this great multitude; the battle is not yours, but God's.
  - **[[2 Chronicles 14#^v9|2 Chronicles 14:9-11]]**
    ![[2 Chronicles 14#^v9]]
    ![[2 Chronicles 14#^v10]]
    ![[2 Chronicles 14#^v11]]
    Asa rested on the Lord against a thousand thousand Ethiopians; it is nothing with the Lord to help, whether with many or with them that have no power.

**[[Genesis 14#^v18|Genesis 14:18-20]]**

![[Genesis 14#^v18]]
![[Genesis 14#^v19]]
![[Genesis 14#^v20]]

- Melchizedek king of Salem brought forth bread and wine; Salem goes into Shalom, peace, so he is the king of peace, and he was the priest of the Most High God.
- Abram paid tithes to Melchizedek. This is way before the Levitical priesthood; the twelve tribes were not yet born. For the order of Melchisedec, study [[Hebrews 7|Hebrews 7]] and [[John 8#^v56|John 8:56]].

**[[Genesis 14#^v21|Genesis 14:21-24]]**

![[Genesis 14#^v21]]
![[Genesis 14#^v22]]
![[Genesis 14#^v23]]
![[Genesis 14#^v24]]

- The king of Sodom asked only for the persons and offered Abram all the goods, and Abram refused from a thread even to a shoelatchet, lest the king of Sodom say, I have made Abram rich. He would not give him that satisfaction.
- The young men's portion was not refused: Aner, Eshcol, and Mamre fought, and they earned their portion.

# Genesis 15: The Covenant and the Four Hundred Years

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 15]]

**[[Genesis 15#^v1|Genesis 15:1-4]]**

![[Genesis 15#^v1]]
![[Genesis 15#^v2]]
![[Genesis 15#^v3]]
![[Genesis 15#^v4]]

- Abram asked what the Lord would give him, seeing he went childless and the steward of his house, Eliezer of Damascus, stood to inherit everything.
- The word came: This shall not be thine heir; he that shall come forth out of thine own bowels shall be thine heir. The Most High prophesied children to Abram out of his own seed.

**[[Genesis 15#^v5|Genesis 15:5-6]]**

![[Genesis 15#^v5]]
![[Genesis 15#^v6]]

- Look now toward heaven and tell the stars: so shall thy seed be. Abram believed the Lord, and it was counted to him for righteousness.
- Righteousness is immortal, and righteousness is defined by the law: it shall be our righteousness if we observe to do all these commandments. Abram's faith showed itself in his works.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Wisdom of Solomon 1#^v15|Wisdom of Solomon 1:15]]**
    ![[Wisdom of Solomon 1#^v15]]
    Righteousness is immortal; the righteous seed endures forever.
  - **[[Deuteronomy 6#^v25|Deuteronomy 6:25]]**
    ![[Deuteronomy 6#^v25]]
    It shall be our righteousness if we observe to do all these commandments; righteousness goes into keeping the laws.
  - **[[Romans 4#^v1|Romans 4:1-3]]**
    ![[Romans 4#^v1]]
    ![[Romans 4#^v2]]
    ![[Romans 4#^v3]]
    Abraham believed God and it was counted unto him for righteousness; his faith was shown in his works.

**[[Genesis 15#^v9|Genesis 15:9-12]]**

![[Genesis 15#^v9]]
![[Genesis 15#^v10]]
![[Genesis 15#^v11]]
![[Genesis 15#^v12]]

- Abram divided the heifer, the goat, and the ram, and drove the fowls from the carcases; he was offering sacrifice.
- When the sun went down a deep sleep and a horror of great darkness fell on Abram: he was about to receive a vision of future prophecy. The Most High speaks in a dream, in a vision of the night, and reveals His secret to His servants the prophets.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Job 33#^v15|Job 33:15-16]]**
    ![[Job 33#^v15]]
    ![[Job 33#^v16]]
    In a dream, in a vision of the night, He openeth the ears of men and sealeth their instruction.
  - **[[Amos 3#^v7|Amos 3:7]]**
    ![[Amos 3#^v7]]
    The Lord God revealeth His secret unto His servants the prophets.

**[[Genesis 15#^v13|Genesis 15:13-16]]**

![[Genesis 15#^v13]]
![[Genesis 15#^v14]]
![[Genesis 15#^v15]]
![[Genesis 15#^v16]]

- Know of a surety: thy seed shall be a stranger in a land that is not theirs, shall serve them, and be afflicted four hundred years. This prophecy was fulfilled in Egypt; the sojourning of the children of Israel in Egypt was four hundred and thirty years, and the four hundred was an approximation of that time.
- This verse is not the doctrine about America and 1619. The northern kingdom's captivity on this side began in 1492, and by that counting the years pass four hundred and Israel is still in captivity and still scattered this day; the prophecy of [[Genesis 15#^v13|Genesis 15:13]] was fulfilled at the Exodus.
- They came out with great substance: the children of Israel required jewels of silver and gold and raiment of the Egyptians, and they spoiled Egypt as they left.
- The twelve tribes scattered among the nations today, the people called Blacks, Hispanics, and Native Americans, are the seed this covenant history belongs to.
- Abram himself would go to his fathers in peace and be buried in a good old age; and the seed would return in the fourth generation, for the iniquity of the Amorites was not yet full.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Acts 7#^v6|Acts 7:6]]**
    ![[Acts 7#^v6]]
    His seed should sojourn in a strange land, in bondage, and be evil entreated four hundred years.
  - **[[Exodus 12#^v40|Exodus 12:40-41]]**
    ![[Exodus 12#^v40]]
    ![[Exodus 12#^v41]]
    The sojourning of the children of Israel in Egypt was four hundred and thirty years, and it ended the selfsame day; the prophecy was fulfilled at the Exodus.
  - **[[Baruch 3#^v8|Baruch 3:8]]**
    ![[Baruch 3#^v8]]
    Israel is yet this day in captivity, scattered for a reproach; the scattering did not end on a four hundred year clock.
  - **[[Exodus 12#^v33|Exodus 12:33-36]]**
    ![[Exodus 12#^v33]]
    ![[Exodus 12#^v34]]
    ![[Exodus 12#^v35]]
    ![[Exodus 12#^v36]]
    Egypt urged them out in haste, and the Lord gave the people favour, and they spoiled the Egyptians; they came out with great substance.

# Genesis 16: Hagar and Ishmael

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 16]]

**[[Genesis 16#^v1|Genesis 16:1-2]]**

![[Genesis 16#^v1]]
![[Genesis 16#^v2]]

- Hagar was Sarai's Egyptian handmaid, from the servants Pharaoh gave in [[Genesis 12#^v16|Genesis 12:16]].
- Sarai, restrained from bearing, gave her maid to Abram that she might obtain children by her.

**[[Genesis 16#^v3|Genesis 16:3-6]]**

![[Genesis 16#^v3]]
![[Genesis 16#^v4]]
![[Genesis 16#^v5]]
![[Genesis 16#^v6]]

- When Hagar conceived, her mistress was despised in her eyes. Hagar got high minded; she stopped submitting because she carried Abram's child.
- Sarai laid the wrong on Abram, and Abram answered, Behold, thy maid is in thy hand. He did not say my wife; he had no attachment to Hagar. His sole purpose was the seed, not multiple wives.
- Abram kept the laws of the Most High. Do not use this account to justify taking multiple wives; the purpose here was the promised inheritance, and Sarai dealt with her maid as mistress over her.

**[[Genesis 16#^v7|Genesis 16:7-9]]**

![[Genesis 16#^v7]]
![[Genesis 16#^v8]]
![[Genesis 16#^v9]]

- The angel of the Lord found Hagar by the fountain in the way to Shur and sent her back: Return to thy mistress, and submit thyself under her hands. Humble yourself and go back.

**[[Genesis 16#^v10|Genesis 16:10-12]]**

![[Genesis 16#^v10]]
![[Genesis 16#^v11]]
![[Genesis 16#^v12]]

- Ishmael would be a wild man, his hand against every man and every man's hand against him, and he would dwell in the presence of all his brethren.
- Ishmael became the ancestor of the nomad desert tribes inhabiting the area from the Sinai desert across the Negev to southern Jordan, and the Arabs venerate Ishmael as their forefather; Muslim tradition holds that Ishmael and Hagar are buried at the Kaaba in Mecca. The reference work Who's Who in the Bible lays this history out.
- They dwell in the presence of all their brethren to this day, nation beside nation through that whole region.

**[[Genesis 16#^v15|Genesis 16:15-16]]**

![[Genesis 16#^v15]]
![[Genesis 16#^v16]]

- Abram was fourscore and six years old, eighty six, when Hagar bare Ishmael. Ishmael was Abram's firstborn, but the covenant did not go to Ishmael; the covenant goes through the twelve tribes of Israel.
