# Genesis 44: The Cup in Benjamin's Sack and Judah's Surety

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 44]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Genesis 44|Genesis 44]] through 47: the silver cup planted in Benjamin's sack, Joseph made known unto his brethren, Israel's going down into Egypt with threescore and ten souls, and the family planted in Goshen through the famine years. The session opens back at [[Genesis 43#^v30|Genesis 43:30-34]] to bring the account up to speed: the Egyptians might not eat bread with the Hebrews, for that is an abomination unto the Egyptians; even while Israel sojourned in Egypt the Lord put a difference between the Egyptians and Israel, as [[Exodus 11#^v7|Exodus 11:7]] shows. The exposition after the reading draws out the characteristics of Joseph: forgiveness, favor, righteousness, mercy, and perseverance through adversity.

**[[Genesis 44#^v1|Genesis 44:1-2]]**

![[Genesis 44#^v1]]
![[Genesis 44#^v2]]

- Joseph commanded his steward to fill the men's sacks with food, return every man's money, and put his own silver cup in the sack's mouth of the youngest. The cup was planted so Joseph could hold Benjamin for a short period of time; he was not seeking harm, he was drawing out word of his father Jacob and whether he was yet alive.

**[[Genesis 44#^v11|Genesis 44:11-13]]**

![[Genesis 44#^v11]]
![[Genesis 44#^v12]]
![[Genesis 44#^v13]]

- The steward searched, beginning at the eldest and leaving at the youngest, and the cup was found in Benjamin's sack; they rent their clothes and returned to the city. Benjamin found with the cup gave Joseph cause to hold him, and it set the stage for Judah to speak the whole history.

**[[Genesis 44#^v27|Genesis 44:27-29]]**

![[Genesis 44#^v27]]
![[Genesis 44#^v28]]
![[Genesis 44#^v29]]

- Thy servant my father said unto us, Ye know that my wife bare me two sons: that is Jacob speaking of Rachel, who bare Joseph and Benjamin, the eleventh and twelfth sons.
- Ye shall bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to the grave: Jacob never got over Joseph being taken away. Sorrow kills; give not over thy mind to heaviness, for the gladness of the heart is the life of man, and losing Benjamin also would have brought their father to the grave.

- Judah became surety for the lad unto his father, and offered to abide instead of the lad as a bondman: let the lad go up with his brethren. He knew his father's life was bound up in the lad's life, and he would not see the evil come on his father.
- Joseph in all of this was interrogating his brothers about the whereabouts and welfare of their father; holding Benjamin was the device that drew it out of Judah.

# Genesis 45: Joseph Made Known unto His Brethren

- Joseph made himself known, and his brethren could not answer him, for they were troubled at his presence. They could not recognize their own brother: he was sold as a teenager, and he stood before them now a grown man in his years.
- He was also clothed in the customs of Egypt: shaved, in Egyptian raiment, decked out as the second in command, and he had made himself strange to them and spoken by an interpreter though he understood their tongue.
- This does not prove the Israelites looked like the Egyptians; the peoples were all dark people. The change was his age, his dress, and the customs of the land he ruled in.

- Joseph is yet alive, and he is governor over all the land of Egypt. Everything Egypt was at that point in time came through the influence of Joseph; the prosperity of that land was the favor the Lord had with Israel.
- The word of the Lord tried Joseph. Until the time that his word came he sat in the prison; then the king sent and loosed him, made him lord of his house and ruler of all his substance, and Joseph taught his senators wisdom. He was the teacher in Egypt, second in command, a king in all but title.
- To go from prison to governor is favor: the Lord causes the enemy to entreat his remnant well in the time of evil. Keep the commandments, for rulers are not a terror to good works.
- These are the characteristics of Joseph: my son, if thou come to serve the Lord, prepare thy soul for temptation; set thy heart aright and constantly endure, and make not haste in time of trouble. Do not be quick to leave the truth or find excuses in the time of trouble. Gold is tried in the fire, and acceptable men in the furnace of adversity.
- Better is the end of a thing than the beginning thereof: he began sold into slavery and put in prison under false pretenses, and the end of the thing was governor of Egypt, a father to Pharaoh. In the day of prosperity be joyful, but in the day of adversity consider the days that were good, and be patient in spirit: read the Bible more, study more, pray more, apply more.
- If thou faint in the day of adversity, thy strength is small. When trials come, read the afflictions of the forefathers and the attributes they had in those times, and apply them: forgiveness, favor, righteousness, mercy. There is no other way into the kingdom but through tribulation.
- Jacob knew there was corn in Egypt because Joseph had already told Pharaoh exactly what to do to save up in the time of tribulation; when Jacob heard, he sent out the fathers. Applying these attributes makes better men, women, brothers, and sisters, to bring forth the nation unto the kingdom of God.

# Genesis 46: Israel Comes into Egypt, Threescore and Ten Souls

Read the chapter → [[Genesis 46]]

**[[Genesis 46#^v26|Genesis 46:26-27]]**

![[Genesis 46#^v26]]
![[Genesis 46#^v27]]

- All the souls of the house of Jacob which came into Egypt were threescore and ten. A score is twenty, so three score is sixty, and ten more makes seventy souls.

**[[Genesis 46#^v31|Genesis 46:31-34]]**

![[Genesis 46#^v31]]
![[Genesis 46#^v32]]
![[Genesis 46#^v33]]
![[Genesis 46#^v34]]

- Joseph instructed his brethren to tell Pharaoh their trade hath been about cattle from their youth, both they and also their fathers, that they might dwell in the land of Goshen; for every shepherd is an abomination unto the Egyptians.
- There is history behind that instruction. Somewhere between the fifteenth and seventeenth dynasties Egypt was ruled by the Hyksos, called the shepherd kings, a group of Canaanites or Hittites that occupied the land; the Egyptians did not deal with shepherds.
- Naming their trade set Israel apart in Goshen, which was fertile land, and the favor Joseph had was bestowed upon him through Pharaoh to give them that land.

**[[Genesis 47#^v11|Genesis 47:11-12]]**

- Joseph placed his father and his brethren in the best of the land, in the land of Rameses, and nourished them with bread. Goshen and the land of Rameses are the exact same place.
- Rameses was the king at the time Israel left Egypt, and Moses wrote the first five books of the Bible; he wrote the place under the name of his own day. In the bush the Most High manifestly revealed himself to Moses, showed him the secrets of the times and the end, the beginning to the end, and the words were written again with Esdras.

- Joseph teaches how to save money: pay the fifth part, and lay up for the years of trouble. That wisdom is how Egypt got through the famine, and it is the same perseverance to practice now in times of tribulation.

- Jacob made Joseph swear to carry him out of Egypt and bury him in the burying place of his fathers. By faith Jacob, when he was a dying, blessed both the sons of Joseph; and by faith Joseph made mention of the departing of the children of Israel and gave commandment concerning his bones. [[Hebrews 11|Hebrews 11]] is the hall of faith, the faithful men of the sons of God, a chapter to read.
- Neither was there a man born like unto Joseph, a governor of his brethren, a stay of the people, whose bones were regarded of the Lord. To grow in spirit and be like him, put on the spirit of the forefathers.
