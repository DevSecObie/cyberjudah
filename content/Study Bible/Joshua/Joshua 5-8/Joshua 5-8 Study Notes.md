# Joshua 5: Circumcision of the Flesh and of the Heart

Read the chapter → [[Joshua 5]]

## Introduction

This session covered [[Joshua 5|Joshua 5]] through 8: the circumcision of the wilderness generation at Gilgal and what the uncircumcised heart means, the Captain of the host of the Lord, the fall of Jericho and the faith of Rahab, the accursed thing and the sin of Achan, the taking of Ai, and the blessings and cursings read at mount Ebal. By way of recap of chapters 1 through 4: after the death of Moses, Joshua was set over the host of Israel, the Lord dried up the river Jordan so the people crossed on dry ground, and the spies Joshua sent had linked up with Rahab the harlot in Jericho. Rahab worked as counterintelligence for Israel; she dwelt on the side of the wall in the poor quarter of the city, and such people know the secret goings-on of a city, so she gave the spies information to carry back to Joshua.

**[[Joshua 5#^v1|Joshua 5:1]]**

![[Joshua 5#^v1]]

- The kings of the Amorites and the Canaanites heard that the Lord had dried up the waters of Jordan from before the children of Israel, and their heart melted: they were paralyzed with fear.
- They had heard how the Most High split the Red sea in Exodus, and now he had done it again at Jordan, so neither was there spirit in them any more because of the children of Israel.

- Circumcise again the children of Israel the second time: the children of Israel had not been keeping the commandments, being stiffnecked and hardheaded, and the Lord told Joshua to fix them.
- Circumcision is the covenant the Most High instituted with our forefather Abraham: every man child circumcised at eight days old, and the servant born in the house or bought with money circumcised likewise and keeping the laws as well.
- When Abraham received the commandment he performed it, circumcising every male of his house the selfsame day; he showed the Lord by his actions that he believed. So the men in Joshua who had not been circumcised showed a form of repentance by applying the law of circumcision.

- The eighth day is the wisdom of the Most High for the body: before then a baby's blood has not begun to coagulate as it needs to, and the child could bleed out; by the eighth day he is strong enough to withstand the process and heal.
- There are physical benefits to the law: when the foreskin remains, moisture gets trapped and creates an environment for bacteria, raising the risk of infection and urinary tract infections, and being circumcised even reduces a wife's risk of cervical cancer. This is the Most High as a Father giving his children counsel on how to live and stay clear of disease.
- The physical circumcision is symbolic of spiritual circumcision. The heart in scripture is the mind, for out of the heart of men proceed evil thoughts; sin is what makes a man dirty, nasty, and filthy, so the uncircumcised heart is the mind that will not keep the commandments.
- No surgery can circumcise the brain: circumcision of the heart is repentance. You wash you and make you clean by ceasing to do evil, and a young man cleanses his way by taking heed thereto according to the word of the Most High.

- That they had not circumcised themselves means they did not obey the voice of the Lord: they should have been circumcised physically and also mentally and spiritually.
- The land that floweth with milk and honey is understood by the Bible's own dictionary, precept upon precept: the rebels in Numbers called Egypt the land flowing with milk and honey, and Israel was brought out of the land of Egypt, so the phrase describes a land abundant in food. The milk goes into your cattle and your meats, and the honey goes into your vegetation.
- The mixed multitude and the children of Israel wept for the fish, cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions, and garlic they ate in Egypt: they were comfortable in their oppression and did not understand they were not free, the same mindset among our people in captivity today who say they are free and living their best life.
- The honey teaching carries a dietary lesson: hast thou found honey, eat so much as is sufficient for thee. Do not overeat; balance your salts, sugars, meats, and dainties moderately.

- The blowing of the trumpets activates the armies of the Lord, the angels, to help in battle. Remember Elisha's servant: when his eyes were opened he saw the host of angels that were there warring and protecting Israel.
- This is the spirit behind the men of Israel rehearsing the righteous acts with trumpets and shofars today, at the camps and at the new moons; it is spiritual and an act of faith.

- The men apply [[Joshua 6|Joshua 6]] to this day: when a scoffer will not hear the word and thinks to outcry the truth, the brothers say the scripture together with one mind, one body, one spirit. That unity is spiritual, a rehearsal of the righteous acts.

- Rahab was a Canaanite, a heathen, yet the heathen can be blessed when they do it to benefit Israel; blessed is he that blesseth thee.

Was not Rahab the harlot justified by works, when she had received the messengers and sent them out another way? As the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also.

- Keep yourselves from the accursed thing, lest ye make yourselves accursed and make the camp of Israel a curse, and trouble it: your individual sins affect the whole nation of Israel, your brothers, your sisters, and the children. That is why we must examine ourselves whether we be in the faith.
- All the silver and gold and vessels of brass and iron were consecrated unto the Lord, separated and sanctified for his treasury alone: nobody was to gain personally from this war.

# Joshua 7: The Accursed Thing and the Sin of Achan

**[[Joshua 7#^v1|Joshua 7:1]]**

![[Joshua 7#^v1]]

- Who committed the trespass? Many will answer Achan, but read the first sentence: the children of Israel committed a trespass in the accursed thing. Achan the son of Carmi, the son of Zabdi, the son of Zerah, of the tribe of Judah, took of the accursed thing, and the anger of the Lord was kindled against all the children of Israel.
- Your individual sins can hurt the rest of the nation. You can be the hindrance to why your camp is not growing, why your family, your marriage, or your children have issues, when you have a spirit on you that you know about and keep secret, not praying, not fasting, not seeking counsel. That is the spirit of Achan, and the children of Israel pay for it; we are supposed to be one mind, one body, one spirit.

- Hearts melting as water is the curse for disobedience: the Most High sends a faintness into the heart, so that the mighty flee as fleeing from a sword and fall when none pursueth, like the athlete in the scary movie suddenly stumbling from sheer fear.

- Joshua sent messengers, and they ran unto the tent, and behold, it was hid in his tent, and the silver under it; they laid it all out before the Lord with the whole congregation present. The Lord revealed it, and the scripture cannot be broken: as Jericho was made a curse, so the one who took of it.
- Joshua and all Israel took Achan, the silver, the garment, the wedge of gold, and his sons, and his daughters, and his oxen, asses, sheep, tent, and all that he had, unto the valley of Achor. His sons and daughters did not steal it, yet because he lacked knowledge and understanding they paid for the covetous spirit he carried.
- Why hast thou troubled us? the Lord shall trouble thee this day. Achan was the reason men died in that battle and the nation was nearly overtaken; his selfish, individual spirit never weighed what his family would pay.
- All Israel stoned him with stones, and burned them with fire, after they had stoned them with stones: them, because his household went with him. My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge: reject knowledge and the Lord will reject thee, and the end of [[Hosea 4#^v6|Hosea 4:6]] that we seldom quote says, I will also forget thy children. The Lord hits where it hurts, and your sin reaches your children, your wife, your husband, your brothers and sisters.
- It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God, and that is where Achan fell, and unfortunately his sons and daughters and all he had with him. Some of our people in the islands and elsewhere still ignorantly carry out such public executions to this day; we do not condone it, for we are not under the law of sacrifice now but under grace, and the judgment is of the Lord.
- Consider the contrast that closes these chapters: Achan, an Israelite of Judah, lacked faith and was put to death, while Rahab the Canaanite harlot was saved alive for her faith. When Israel will not obey his voice, the Lord likens the house of Judah unto all the heathen; so we must have the fear of the Lord in our hearts.
- They raised over him a great heap of stones unto this day, and the Lord turned from the fierceness of his anger; wherefore the name of that place was called the valley of Achor unto this day.

# Joshua 8: The Taking of Ai and the Blessings and Cursings

Read the chapter → [[Joshua 8]]

**[[Joshua 8#^v1|Joshua 8:1-2]]**

![[Joshua 8#^v1]]
![[Joshua 8#^v2]]

- With the accursed thing purged, the Lord said unto Joshua, Fear not, neither be thou dismayed: take all the people of war with thee and go up to Ai; see, I have given into thy hand the king of Ai, and his people, and his city, and his land.
- This time the spoil thereof and the cattle shall ye take for a prey unto yourselves, unlike Jericho where everything was consecrated to the treasury of the Lord; and the command was to lay an ambush for the city behind it.

**[[Joshua 8#^v3|Joshua 8:3-8]]**

![[Joshua 8#^v3]]

![[Joshua 8#^v8]]

- Joshua chose out thirty thousand mighty men of valour and sent them away by night to lie in ambush behind the city, not very far from it, always ready. These were warriors, men of courage, but none of that matters unless the spirit is right with the Lord first.
- The strategy: Joshua and the people with him approach the city, and when the men of Ai come out, Israel flees before them as at the first, drawing them away from the city; then the ambush rises up and seizes the city and sets it on fire, according to the commandment of the Lord.
- Note the wording: for the Lord your God will deliver it into your hand. He did not say ye shall win the battle; the Most High is the one who delivers the enemy into Israel's hand.

- The ambush went and abode between Bethel and Ai, on the west side of Ai, while Joshua lodged that night among the people; he rose early, numbered the people, and went up with the elders of Israel and pitched on the north side of Ai, with a valley between them and the city.
- He took about five thousand men and set them to lie in ambush between Bethel and Ai on the west side: every position was assigned, everything strategic. Joshua was carrying out the playbook, gifted in war strategy and technique.

**[[Joshua 8#^v14|Joshua 8:14-17]]**

![[Joshua 8#^v14]]
![[Joshua 8#^v15]]

![[Joshua 8#^v17]]

- When the king of Ai saw it, the men of the city hasted and rose up early and went out against Israel to battle at a time appointed, not knowing there were liers in ambush behind the city.
- Joshua and all Israel made as if they were beaten before them, and fled by the way of the wilderness: part of the strategy, feigning defeat so Ai would chase them straight into the ambush.
- All the people in Ai were called together to pursue, and they were drawn away from the city: not a man was left in Ai or Bethel that went not out after Israel, and they left the city open.

**[[Joshua 8#^v18|Joshua 8:18-19]]**

- The Lord said unto Joshua, Stretch out the spear that is in thy hand toward Ai; for I will give it into thine hand.
- As soon as he stretched out his hand, the ambush arose quickly out of their place, ran, entered into the city, took it, and hasted and set the city on fire.

- When the men of Ai looked behind them, the smoke of their city ascended up to heaven, and they had no power to flee this way or that way; the people that fled to the wilderness turned back upon the pursuers.
- The men of Ai were in the midst of Israel, some on this side and some on that side, and Israel smote them until none remained or escaped; they were surrounded, exactly as drawn up. The king of Ai they took alive and brought him to Joshua.

- When Israel had made an end of slaying all the inhabitants of Ai in the field and in the wilderness, all that fell that day, both of men and women, were twelve thousand, even all the men of Ai. Women are casualties of war.
- That point stands as a warning for the sisters serving unrepented in the militaries of the nations, standing stiffly for the oppressor: when Christ comes back he is coming to kill, and a uniform and a weapon will not save; men and women will be dying alike. It is best in these last days for a woman to stand by and support her own man and nation, not another nation.
- Joshua drew not his hand back until he had utterly destroyed all the inhabitants of Ai, meaning the children were put to death also. This was not new: at Sihon's cities Israel utterly destroyed the men, the women, and the little ones. The children's song teaching that Christ loves all the children of every nation alike does not stand with these scriptures; the Lord's love is toward the children of Israel.

- Then Joshua built an altar unto the Lord God of Israel in mount Ebal, as Moses commanded and as it is written in the book of the law: an altar of whole stones, over which no man hath lift up any iron; and they offered burnt offerings and sacrificed peace offerings, and he wrote upon the stones a copy of the law of Moses.
- We are still under the law of sacrifice today, but the sacrifice is your body, not the blood of bulls and goats: you sacrifice time, money, pain, and labor to push this truth and do the work, spiritual sacrifices through your works by keeping the laws.
- The peace offering today is keeping the commandments: he that keepeth the law bringeth offerings enough, and he that taketh heed to the commandment offereth a peace offering. Under grace, peace is a dispensation: the more sin you commit, the less peace you will have, so keep the commandments and do not frustrate the grace and peace the Most High has with you.
- The Most High hath a controversy with the inhabitants of the land; if you do not want the Lord to have a controversy with you, keep the laws. And keeping the laws is more than beards, fringes, borders of blue, and not eating pork: it is purging the unfruitful spirits you know dwell inside you, the jealousy, envy, covetousness, malice, hatred, grudging, backbiting, murmuring, and gossip.

- Why does the body set up rank instead of saying I only follow Christ? Because we are commanded to rehearse the righteous acts of the Lord: reading the wars and works of Joshua, Moses, and the officers, judges, and elders, we practice what our forefathers did, in order, as an organized army.
- Rehearse means practice, and practice makes perfect: there are rehearsals every day, classes and meetings for the men, the women, and the children, because this is not our rest.

- Afterward he read all the words of the law, the blessings and cursings, according to all that is written in the book of the law. We tell our neighbor God bless you, but by the same book the Most High and Christ will curse you too if you do not keep the laws.
- Heaven and earth are called to record: everything we do is recorded, and life and death, blessing and cursing, are set before us. Life is the keeping of the commandments of the Most High, and death is the wages of sin; it is the one thing left optional, so choose life, for it is not just you that pays but thy seed as well.
- How do you become blessed? Read the last page of the book: blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life. A few verses before your Bible says the end, it says keep the commandments, so no man in his right mind can say the laws are done away with; that is justifying wickedness. Keep the sabbath holy, leave the swine alone, dress in modesty, and keep the commandments if you want the blessings.
- There was not a word of all that Moses commanded which Joshua read not before all the congregation, with the women, and the little ones, and the strangers that were conversant among them: those were the strangers that fell in line with Israel, with the fear of the Lord in them, as Rahab had.
