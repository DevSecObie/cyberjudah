# Numbers 7: The Offering of the Princes at the Dedication of the Altar

Read the chapter → [[Numbers 7]]

## Introduction

This reading circles back to catch [[Numbers 7|Numbers 7]] through 10: the offerings of the twelve princes at the dedication of the altar, the cleansing of the Levites and the years of their service, the passover of the second month and the cloud over the tabernacle, and the two silver trumpets with the order of Israel's journeys. This is a reading session, so the teaching is brief precepts given along the reading, together with the questions answered after it.

**[[Numbers 7#^v1|Numbers 7:1-3]]**

![[Numbers 7#^v1]]
![[Numbers 7#^v2]]
![[Numbers 7#^v3]]

- On the day that Moses had fully set up the tabernacle and anointed and sanctified it with all the instruments thereof, the princes of Israel, heads of the house of their fathers and over them that were numbered, brought their offering before the Lord: six covered wagons and twelve oxen, a wagon for two of the princes and for each one an ox.

- The Lord told Moses to take the wagons and oxen for the service of the tabernacle of the congregation and give them to the Levites, to every man according to his service: two wagons and four oxen to the sons of Gershon, and four wagons and eight oxen to the sons of Merari, under the hand of Ithamar the son of Aaron the priest.
- Unto the sons of Kohath he gave none, because the service of the sanctuary belonging unto them was that they should bear upon their shoulders. [[Numbers 4|Numbers 4]] shows what that bearing was: Aaron and his sons covered the ark and the vessels first, and after that the sons of Kohath came to bear them, not touching any holy thing lest they die.
- The Kohathites were in charge of carrying the sanctuary to and fro, and the reading further on shows them doing it once the camp moved.

    When Aaron has made an end of covering the sanctuary, the sons of Kohath come to bear it, but they shall not touch any holy thing, lest they die.

- The twelve days went in the order of the camps: Judah, Issachar, Zebulun, Reuben, Simeon, Gad, Ephraim, Manasseh, Benjamin, Dan, Asher, and Naphtali, closing with Ahira the son of Enan on the twelfth day.

- Israel does not do the animal sacrifices anymore. When Christ came into the world he said, sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not; the Most High had no pleasure in them, because the people's minds were not changed when they offered them, and Christ was the ultimate sacrifice who took away the first that he might establish the second.
- There is also no tabernacle today to do them at. The sacrifices had to be done at the tabernacle, and Israel is scattered everywhere in captivity, not able to go to Jerusalem to a temple that is not standing.

**[[Numbers 8#^v5|Numbers 8:5-14]]**

![[Numbers 8#^v5]]

- The Levites were taken from among the children of Israel and cleansed: water of purifying sprinkled upon them, all their flesh shaven, and their clothes washed.
- The whole assembly of the children of Israel was gathered, the children of Israel put their hands upon the Levites, and Aaron offered the Levites before the Lord for an offering of the children of Israel, with the two bullocks for a sin offering and a burnt offering to make an atonement for the Levites.
- Thus were the Levites separated from among the children of Israel: and the Levites shall be mine.

**[[Numbers 8#^v15|Numbers 8:15-19]]**

![[Numbers 8#^v15]]
![[Numbers 8#^v16]]
![[Numbers 8#^v17]]
![[Numbers 8#^v18]]
![[Numbers 8#^v19]]

- After the cleansing the Levites went in to do the service of the tabernacle of the congregation. Mark the word instead in verse 16: instead of such as open every womb, instead of the firstborn of all the children of Israel, the Most High took the Levites unto himself.
- At the first, all the firstborn of all the tribes were sanctified for the office, from the day the Lord smote every firstborn in the land of Egypt. Here he makes it clear again, as he has done in multiple places, that the one tribe of Levi now performs the service of the tabernacle.
- The Levites were given as a gift to Aaron and to his sons, to do the service of the children of Israel and to make an atonement for them, that there be no plague among the children of Israel when they come nigh unto the sanctuary.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Exodus 13#^v1|Exodus 13:1-2]]**
    ![[Exodus 13#^v1]]
    ![[Exodus 13#^v2]]
    Sanctify unto me all the firstborn, whatsoever openeth the womb among the children of Israel; that was the office before the Levites were taken instead.

**[[Numbers 8#^v20|Numbers 8:20-22]]**

![[Numbers 8#^v20]]
![[Numbers 8#^v21]]

- Moses, Aaron, and all the congregation of the children of Israel did to the Levites according unto all that the Lord commanded Moses; the Levites were purified, and after that they went in to do their service in the tabernacle of the congregation before Aaron and before his sons.

**[[Numbers 8#^v23|Numbers 8:23-26]]**

![[Numbers 8#^v23]]
![[Numbers 8#^v24]]

![[Numbers 8#^v26]]

- The service of the Levites had an age: from twenty and five years old and upward they went in to wait upon the service of the tabernacle of the congregation, and up until that point they were not allowed to do the service.
- From the age of fifty years they ceased waiting upon the service; it ran from twenty five to fifty. After fifty they ministered with their brethren in the tabernacle to keep the charge, but did no service.

# Numbers 9: The Second Passover and the Cloud over the Tabernacle

Read the chapter → [[Numbers 9]]

**[[Numbers 9#^v1|Numbers 9:1-5]]**

![[Numbers 9#^v1]]
![[Numbers 9#^v2]]
![[Numbers 9#^v3]]
![[Numbers 9#^v4]]
![[Numbers 9#^v5]]

- In the first month of the second year after they were come out of the land of Egypt, the Lord commanded that the children of Israel keep the passover at his appointed season, the fourteenth day of the month at even; and they kept it in the wilderness of Sinai, according to all that the Lord commanded Moses.
- The passover carried its rites and its ceremonies: there were things they had to sacrifice and do according to all the ordinances of it, to keep everything in order for the congregation.

**[[Numbers 9#^v6|Numbers 9:6-8]]**

![[Numbers 9#^v6]]
![[Numbers 9#^v7]]
![[Numbers 9#^v8]]

- Certain men were defiled by the dead body of a man, so they could not keep the passover on that day. The law had already put out of the camp whosoever was defiled by the dead, so they could not come in with the congregation to make their offering.
- They asked, wherefore are we kept back, that we may not offer an offering of the Lord in his appointed season? And Moses said, stand still, and I will hear what the Lord will command concerning you.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Numbers 5#^v1|Numbers 5:1-2]]**
    ![[Numbers 5#^v1]]
    ![[Numbers 5#^v2]]
    Put out of the camp every leper, every one that hath an issue, and whosoever is defiled by the dead; that is why those men could not come in.

**[[Numbers 9#^v9|Numbers 9:9-12]]**

![[Numbers 9#^v9]]
![[Numbers 9#^v10]]
![[Numbers 9#^v11]]
![[Numbers 9#^v12]]

- The answer came: if any man be unclean by reason of a dead body, or be in a journey afar off, he shall still keep the passover unto the Lord, on the fourteenth day of the second month at even, eating it with unleavened bread and bitter herbs.
- Defilement by the dead and traveling afar off were the only two reasons given to miss the appointed day, and even then the passover was kept the following month, not neglected.
- The same holds today: many drive trucks and work jobs they cannot get off, and the Most High understands the captivity, but that does not mean you neglect the passover; keep it the second month if it is possible.

**[[Numbers 9#^v13|Numbers 9:13]]**

![[Numbers 9#^v13]]

- But the man that is clean, and is not in a journey, and forbeareth to keep the passover, shall be cut off from among his people; he brought not the offering of the Lord in his appointed season, and that man shall bear his sin.
- This speaks to the one that likes to make excuses. There were no excuses then without being cut off, and there are none now when the holy days come around; if the reason is not according to the bible, it does not stand.

**[[Numbers 9#^v14|Numbers 9:14]]**

![[Numbers 9#^v14]]

- The stranger that sojourns with Israel and will keep the passover keeps it according to the ordinance of the passover and the manner thereof: one ordinance both for the stranger and for him that was born in the land.
- The stranger here is not unbelieving family; it is one of another nation that dwelt among Israel. Even the nations among us had to keep the laws just as we did; they were not getting a pass around us.

**[[Numbers 9#^v15|Numbers 9:15-16]]**

![[Numbers 9#^v15]]
![[Numbers 9#^v16]]

- On the day that the tabernacle was reared up the cloud covered it, and at even there was upon the tabernacle as it were the appearance of fire until the morning.
- That cloud is the chariot of the Most High: he maketh the clouds his chariot, and that is what sat over the top of the tabernacle.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Psalms 104#^v3|Psalms 104:3]]**
    ![[Psalms 104#^v3]]
    He maketh the clouds his chariot; the cloud over the tabernacle was his chariot.

**[[Numbers 9#^v17|Numbers 9:17-23]]**

![[Numbers 9#^v17]]
![[Numbers 9#^v18]]
![[Numbers 9#^v19]]
![[Numbers 9#^v20]]
![[Numbers 9#^v21]]
![[Numbers 9#^v22]]
![[Numbers 9#^v23]]

- When the cloud was taken up from the tabernacle, the children of Israel journeyed; and in the place where the cloud abode, there they pitched their tents. Wherever the chariot went, Israel got up and followed it, the same way it led them across the Red Sea.
- Whether it were two days, or a month, or a year that the cloud tarried upon the tabernacle, they abode in their tents and journeyed not; and when it was taken up, they journeyed, whether by day or by night. That is how they knew where they needed to stay and when to move.
- At the commandment of the Lord they rested in the tents, and at the commandment of the Lord they journeyed; they kept the charge of the Lord by the hand of Moses.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Exodus 40#^v36|Exodus 40:36]]**
    ![[Exodus 40#^v36]]
    When the cloud was taken up from over the tabernacle, the children of Israel went onward in all their journeys; Numbers revisits what Exodus recorded.

# Numbers 10: The Silver Trumpets and the Order of the Journeys

Read the chapter → [[Numbers 10]]

**[[Numbers 10#^v1|Numbers 10:1-4]]**

![[Numbers 10#^v1]]
![[Numbers 10#^v2]]
![[Numbers 10#^v3]]
![[Numbers 10#^v4]]

- Two trumpets of silver, of a whole piece, were made for the calling of the assembly and for the journeying of the camps. When they blew with both, all the assembly gathered to the door of the tabernacle of the congregation; when they blew but with one, the princes, the heads of the thousands of Israel, gathered.
- There were different ways the Levites had to blow the trumpets: one way to call the whole congregation, another for just the leaders, and another to let everybody know it was war.

**[[Numbers 10#^v5|Numbers 10:5-6]]**

![[Numbers 10#^v5]]
![[Numbers 10#^v6]]

- When ye blow an alarm, the camps that lie on the east parts go forward first: that was Judah, Issachar, and Zebulun, under the standard of the camp of Judah.
- When the alarm is blown the second time, the camps that lie on the south side take their journey: that was Reuben, Simeon, and Gad. The alarm for the journeys was short bursts.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Numbers 2#^v3|Numbers 2:3-7]]**
    ![[Numbers 2#^v3]]
    ![[Numbers 2#^v4]]
    ![[Numbers 2#^v5]]
    ![[Numbers 2#^v6]]
    ![[Numbers 2#^v7]]
    On the east side toward the rising of the sun pitch the standard of the camp of Judah, with Issachar and Zebulun beside him.
  - **[[Numbers 2#^v10|Numbers 2:10-14]]**
    ![[Numbers 2#^v10]]
    ![[Numbers 2#^v11]]
    ![[Numbers 2#^v12]]
    ![[Numbers 2#^v13]]
    ![[Numbers 2#^v14]]
    On the south side is the standard of the camp of Reuben, with Simeon and Gad beside him.

**[[Numbers 10#^v7|Numbers 10:7-9]]**

![[Numbers 10#^v7]]
![[Numbers 10#^v8]]
![[Numbers 10#^v9]]

- When the congregation is to be gathered together, ye shall blow, but ye shall not sound an alarm: a longer sound, a different tone from the short alarm bursts, letting everybody know this is the gathering.
- If the trumpet give an uncertain sound, who shall prepare himself to the battle? The sound had to be certain, or people would sit still or try to gather when it was time to go to war.
- The sons of Aaron the priests blew the trumpets, an ordinance for ever throughout your generations. In war in your land against the enemy that oppresseth you, blow an alarm, and ye shall be remembered before the Lord your God and saved from your enemies. As Israel marched through the wilderness against the nations, the Most High remembered them and destroyed the enemy, as long as they were keeping the commandments.

  Precepts:
  - **[[1 Corinthians 14#^v8|1 Corinthians 14:8]]**
    ![[1 Corinthians 14#^v8]]
    For if the trumpet give an uncertain sound, who shall prepare himself to the battle?

**[[Numbers 10#^v10|Numbers 10:10]]**

![[Numbers 10#^v10]]

- The trumpets were also blown in the day of your gladness, in your solemn days, and in the beginnings of your months, over the burnt offerings and the sacrifices of the peace offerings, for a memorial before your God.
- The feast days must be a glad moment even to this day. Coming to congregate with your brothers and sisters grudgingly is one of the reasons Israel went into captivity: we were not glad to do what the Most High said.
- The beginnings of the months are marked by the moon: the Most High made the moon to serve in her season for a declaration of times, a light that decreaseth in her perfection and then increaseth in her changing, and the month is called after her name. When the light is full again, that is the new moon.

  Precepts:
  - **[[Ecclesiasticus 43#^v6|Ecclesiasticus 43:6-8]]**
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 43#^v6]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 43#^v7]]
    ![[Ecclesiasticus 43#^v8]]
    The moon declares the times and seasons and is the sign of feasts; her cycle marks the beginnings of the months.

- On the twentieth day of the second month in the second year the cloud was taken up from off the tabernacle of the testimony, and the children of Israel took their journeys out of the wilderness of Sinai; and the cloud rested in the wilderness of Paran.
- The testimony is the commandments of the Most High. If the children keep his covenant and his testimony, their children shall sit upon the throne for evermore; and it is his testimony, not our own testimony, that is righteous and very faithful and that changes the people.

  - **[[Psalms 19#^v7|Psalms 19:7]]**
    ![[Psalms 19#^v7]]
    The testimony of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple; it is the laws of the Most High that must be taught, for his laws change the people, not our testimony.

- In the first place went the standard of the camp of the children of Judah. The standard is the banner, like the flags of the different tribes held up in the schools today.
- The order of the march is given camp by camp: Judah with Issachar and Zebulun first; the tabernacle taken down and borne by the sons of Gershon and Merari; then Reuben with Simeon and Gad; then the Kohathites bearing the sanctuary, and the other did set up the tabernacle against they came; then Ephraim with Manasseh and Benjamin; and last Dan with Asher and Naphtali, the rereward of all the camps.
- The tabernacle went ahead so that it would be set up before the whole congregation of the twelve tribes arrived where they were going. This was the order of the armies whenever they got up and moved.

**[[Numbers 10#^v29|Numbers 10:29-32]]**

![[Numbers 10#^v29]]
![[Numbers 10#^v30]]

- Moses said unto Hobab the son of Raguel the Midianite, his kinsman by marriage, we are journeying unto the place of which the Lord said, I will give it you: come thou with us, and we will do thee good. Hobab answered that he would depart to his own land and to his kindred, to go back to his people.
- Leave us not, for thou mayest be to us instead of eyes: Hobab knew the land and how they ought to encamp in the wilderness, so Moses asked him to stay and be their eyes.
- What goodness the Lord shall do unto us, the same will we do unto thee: Moses treated him as one born among Israel, as a brother; whatever we get, you are going to get. That is the law of the stranger, and it is why Moses dealt so with his kinsman.

- Every time the ark set forward Moses said, rise up, Lord, and let thine enemies be scattered, and let them that hate thee flee before thee; and when it rested he said, return, O Lord, unto the many thousands of Israel.
- The Most High was fighting Israel's battles and winning the wars. It was not because Israel was a great nation of itself that the other nations were defeated; it was because God was with them, the God that brought them out of Egypt and multiplied them into a great nation.
