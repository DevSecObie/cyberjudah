# Numbers 23: How Shall I Curse, Whom God Hath Not Cursed?

Read the chapter → [[Numbers 23]]

## Introduction

This reading covers [[Numbers 23|Numbers 23]] through 26: Balaam's parables that bless Israel instead of cursing her, the star and sceptre prophecy of the latter days, Israel joined unto Baal-peor and the zeal of Phinehas, and the second census of the families of Israel in the plains of Moab. This is a reading session, with the chapters read straight through and teaching given along the way.

**[[Numbers 23#^v1|Numbers 23:1-2]]**

![[Numbers 23#^v1]]
![[Numbers 23#^v2]]

- Sacrificing animals was not solely a thing pertaining to Israel; sacrificing for sin is what pertained to Israel. All the ancient nations of old sacrificed animals when they worshipped their gods, so Balak and Balaam offering bullocks and rams on altars was nothing new to them.
- Offering animals on altars had been done since the time of Adam. It was commonplace in that time, so do not be confused at why Balaam keeps calling for altars and offerings through this chapter.

**[[Numbers 23#^v7|Numbers 23:7-8]]**

![[Numbers 23#^v7]]
![[Numbers 23#^v8]]

- Balak the king of Moab brought Balaam from Aram to curse Jacob and defy Israel, and Balaam could not do it: how shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed?
- Understand this: we are a blessed people, but we are living under the curses of God for our disobedience. We are the sons and daughters of God, above all nations, yet on the bottom for our sin, and we remain there until we repent as a nation.

- The people shall dwell alone and shall not be reckoned among the nations; who can count the dust of Jacob? Israel is as the sand of the sea.
- Let me die the death of the righteous, and let my last end be like his. The last end of Israel is a crown of glory in the hand of the Lord: the land married, double for our shame, everlasting joy, and a seed acknowledged among the Gentiles as the seed which the Lord hath blessed. That is why we cannot wait for those days.

- The house of Israel is impudent and hardhearted, so the Lord makes the face and forehead strong, meaning the mind: whatever excuse the people raise, He gives an answer through the scriptures on the spot. The rebellious spirits on our people are only cast down by the word of God, which is why we speak what the Lord puts in our mouth.

**[[Numbers 23#^v19|Numbers 23:19]]**

![[Numbers 23#^v19]]

- God is not a man, that he should lie. Whatever the Lord speaks out of His mouth, once the judgment is passed there is no taking it back, whether in this life or the one to come.
- The parable of Lazarus shows it: between us and you there is a great gulf fixed, so that none can pass. The judgment has been set and there is no changing it.
- In the day of doom no man shall be able to save him that is destroyed. This present life is not the end, and asking the righteous to pray for those that outright despise God and His laws is praying for the weak; Esdras tried that with the Most High and was told to concern himself with the righteous.
- That is what we doubted back in Deuteronomy when Moses read the curses over us; we thought the Lord would not make good on His word. But the Lord is longsuffering and patient and will in no wise acquit the wicked; we will not escape our sins.

- That does not make the other nations prophets. Prophets are raised up of the sons of Israel; the Lord used Balaam for one specific purpose, to show the nations there is nothing they can do against Israel when the Lord is on our side, and to have Moses write it down in a book.

# Numbers 25: Baal-peor and the Zeal of Phinehas

- Israel abode in Shittim, and the people began to commit whoredom with the daughters of Moab. Why? Balaam could not curse us, because God said you cannot curse what is blessed; so his counsel was the best the enemy can ever do: throw another god in front of Israel and send the women, and let Israel bring the trespass on himself. That counsel is why the Lord put Balaam to death.
- They called the people unto the sacrifices of their gods, and the people did eat and bowed down; Israel joined himself unto Baal-peor, and the anger of the Lord was kindled. The worship of Baal-peor went into whoredom, that orgy worship: we started worshipping another god and messing with the women together.

Every one his man means every judge by his rank: the officer over ten took ten, the officer over fifty took fifty, and so on. With the Most High it is two options only, life and death.

- Do not take that mercy lightly, and do not frustrate the grace of God until He is finished with you. He can cut a man's days down whenever He wants; do not press Him to it.

- Phinehas turned the wrath of the Most High away from the children of Israel, because he was zealous for his God's sake among them; wherefore the Lord gave him His covenant of peace, and to his seed after him the covenant of an everlasting priesthood, because he made an atonement for the children of Israel.
- That covenant of peace is what all of us are striving for, rest from this servitude of bondage; but the wisdom in reading the acts of Phinehas is that you must be zealous for the law of the Most High to obtain it. Be ye zealous for the law, and give your lives for the covenant of your fathers; Phinehas our father, in being zealous and fervent, obtained the covenant of an everlasting priesthood.
- Being zealous today is confessing Christ before men, and you confess him mainly through your actions. Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example that we should follow his steps, and his steps were keeping the commandments of God; the first way you deny Christ is by rejecting the commandments.
- Christ came not to send peace on earth but a sword, setting a man at variance against his own household; he that loveth father or mother more than him is not worthy of him. We stay zealous by letting nothing separate us from following Christ in his steps: keeping the laws of God, teaching the gospel, and spreading the word with wisdom.

# Numbers 26: The Second Census of the Families of Israel

Read the chapter → [[Numbers 26]]

**[[Numbers 26#^v1|Numbers 26:1-2]]**

![[Numbers 26#^v1]]
![[Numbers 26#^v2]]

- After the plague the Lord commanded a new numbering of the congregation, from twenty years old and upward, all that are able to go to war in Israel. Twenty is the biblical age of a man: Israel sent men to war, not children, so the numbering of them able for war fixes the age of manhood at twenty years old.

**[[Numbers 26#^v5|Numbers 26:5-7]]**

![[Numbers 26#^v5]]
![[Numbers 26#^v6]]
![[Numbers 26#^v7]]

- Of Hanoch, the family of the Hanochites; of Pallu, the family of the Palluites. The ending ites means the descendants of, the people you belong to: Israelites are the descendants of Israel, Edomites the descendants of Edom, and so it goes all down the line through the scriptures.
- Reading these family names aloud loosens the tongue to say the names of the scriptures fluently and without hesitation. [[1 Chronicles 1|1 Chronicles 1]] serves the same practice, and it carries the names of Israel's families together with Edom and Ishmael, the nations that come out of Abraham's line.
