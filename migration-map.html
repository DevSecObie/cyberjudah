Which copy is the source

You asked why your Docusaurus site deviates from how Docusaurus works. It does, measurably. But the fork that matters isn't vault or MDX — it's which copy of your content is allowed to win when two disagree.

Migrating to standard Docusaurus would not give you web editing, because you author in a separate Obsidian vault. That vault would keep overwriting whatever you typed on the web.

Every architecture below is really an answer to one question: is your Obsidian vault the source of truth, or is the repo? You can have durable web editing, or you can have an upstream vault that wins. Not both. Everything else follows from that.

Why migration doesn't buy what you wanted

The decisive finding

You author in a separate Obsidian vault. So converting the repo to MDX does not remove the conversion step. It relocates it:

After migrating, with Obsidian still upstream

your Obsidian vault          still wikilinks — that is why you use Obsidian
      │  converter            the same generator, moved here
      ▼
docs/*.mdx                  committed, standard, tool-friendly
      │  docusaurus build
      ▼
   the site

Two things go wrong, and neither is fixable by choosing a better CMS.

  • The repo stops holding what you edit. Today vault/ in the repo is your working copy. After migration the repo holds only derived MDX, and you gain a manual conversion step before every commit.
  • Web edits still die. Edit a page through github.dev or Decap and the change lands in docs/*.mdx. Your next Obsidian sync regenerates that file and your edit is gone. Same drift you have now, pointed the other direction.

Durable web editing requires exactly one source of truth, and it must be the one the web editor can reach. That is the whole problem, and MDX has nothing to do with it.

The three real options

Named by what wins when two copies disagree

Obsidian keeps winning

Change nothing structural

You get: your current authoring loop, untouched. Obsidian's live preview already renders your verse embeds as real scripture, which is the WYSIWYG you were asking for, on a phone, today.

You give up: web editing, permanently. Not as a missing feature but as a structural impossibility. Every tool in this conversation stays a dead end.

The repo wins, as MDX

Full migration · retire Obsidian

You get: a genuinely standard Docusaurus site. Committed docs/, "Edit this page" on every page, and github.dev, Decap, Tina and Editsaurus all working as advertised.

You give up: Obsidian on this content, or you accept two sources that drift. Plus 80 files of hand-written HTML to make JSX-valid, and the repo grows by the generated trees you'd now commit.

The repo wins, and it is the vault

Recommended · smallest change, most of the benefit

Open vault/ in Obsidian directly. Commit a .obsidian folder, add obsidian-git, and delete the off-repo copy.

You get: one source of truth that is simultaneously your Obsidian vault and your git repo. Obsidian keeps its live preview and backlinks. Nothing converts, nothing drifts, and web editing becomes possible for the first time, because a custom editor writing to vault/ is writing to the only copy that exists.

You give up: off-the-shelf CMS support, since the format stays non-standard. The web editor has to be built rather than installed.

Option three is the one your situation actually points at. It costs an afternoon instead of a month, it removes a whole class of drift bug you currently live with, and it turns "edit from the web" from impossible into merely unbuilt.

If you take option three

Reversible order of operations

  1. Confirm the two vaults still match. Diff your off-repo Obsidian vault against vault/ in the repo. Any file that differs is drift you are already carrying, and it needs resolving before the copies are merged.

  2. Make the repo vault the real one. Open vault/ in Obsidian, commit the .obsidian folder, and archive the off-repo copy rather than deleting it. Fully reversible up to this point.

  3. Add obsidian-git. Commit and sync from desktop and phone. This alone closes your original request: mobile editing with live-rendered scripture.

  4. Fold finalize.py into npm run sync. Nav lines, Topics and book indexes then regenerate on every build, so a note added from a phone can't leave its neighbours stale.

  5. Stamp custom_edit_url in generate.mjs. Every page gets an "Edit this page" link pointing at its real vault source on GitHub, not at the throwaway docs/ file. Roughly twenty lines.

  6. Turn on build-failure notifications. With onBrokenLinks: "throw", a bad reference typed on a phone fails the deploy silently and the site quietly serves the last good build.

  7. Then, only if you still want it, build /admin. A verse picker over static/search/verses.json, live embed rendering, and commits through the GitHub API. Worth doing after the steps above, because they may make it unnecessary.

Steps one through three are the whole answer to the question you started with. Four through six are cheap insurance. Seven is optional.
