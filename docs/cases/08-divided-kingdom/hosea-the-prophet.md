<p class="casehead"><span class="badge badge--primary verdict">Kept the law</span> <span class="charge">Obeying the Lord's command to take a wife of whoredoms as a sign; prophesying Israel's scattering and restoration</span><br/><span class="era">Divided Kingdom</span></p>

The word of the Lord came to Hosea in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, and of Jeroboam the son of Joash. He was commanded to marry Gomer, a wife of whoredoms, as a living sign of how Israel committed spiritual fornication against the Lord. Three children bore prophetic names — Jezreel, Lo-ruhamah, Lo-ammi — yet the promise followed: the children of Israel shall be as the sand of the sea.

## The obedience

The Lord told Hosea to go take a wife of whoredoms and children of whoredoms, for the land had committed great whoredom, departing from the Lord. So he went and took Gomer the daughter of Diblaim (Hosea 1:2–3). The prophet's own household became the picture of the nation's unfaithfulness.

Three children were born, each named by the Lord's word. Jezreel: the Lord would avenge the blood of Jezreel upon the house of Jehu and cause to cease the kingdom of Israel (Hosea 1:4). Lo-ruhamah: the Lord would no more have mercy upon the house of Israel but utterly take them away (Hosea 1:6). Lo-ammi: ye are not my people and I will not be your God (Hosea 1:9). The names declared scattering, the withdrawal of mercy, and the severing of the covenant.

Yet the promise followed: the number of the children of Israel shall be as the sand of the sea, which cannot be measured nor numbered, and in the place where it was said, Ye are not my people, there it shall be said, Ye are the sons of the living God (Hosea 1:10). They would be gathered under one head (Hosea 1:11).

## The blessing

The Lord commanded Hosea again to love a woman beloved of her friend, yet an adulteress, according to the love of the Lord toward the children of Israel. He bought her for fifteen pieces of silver and a homer and a half of barley and told her to abide many days (Hosea 3:1–3).

For the children of Israel shall abide many days without a king, without a prince, without a sacrifice, without an image, without an ephod, and without teraphim. Afterward shall the children of Israel return and seek the Lord their God, and David their king, and shall fear the Lord and his goodness in the latter days (Hosea 3:4–5). The long dispersion would end in a return.
