<p class="casehead"><span class="badge badge--warning verdict">Cursed</span> <span class="charge">Doing worse than all that were before him; walking in the way of Jeroboam and in his sin</span><br/><span class="era">Divided Kingdom</span></p>

After Zimri burned the palace over himself, Israel divided: half followed Tibni and half followed Omri. Omri prevailed, reigned twelve years, bought the hill Samaria, and built the city that became the northern kingdom's capital. He wrought evil in the eyes of the Lord and did worse than all that were before him.

## The offense

After Zimri's death the people of Israel divided into two parts: half followed Tibni the son of Ginath, to make him king, and half followed Omri. The people that followed Omri prevailed, Tibni died, and Omri reigned (1 Kings 16:21–22). The throne was gained through civil struggle, and the cost of the division left no visible impression on the rule that followed.

Omri reigned twelve years over Israel, six of them in Tirzah. He bought the hill Samaria of Shemer for two talents of silver, built on it, and named the city after the owner of the hill (1 Kings 16:23–24). Samaria became the seat of the northern kingdom and the symbol of its departure from the Lord.

## The judgment

Omri wrought evil in the eyes of the Lord and did worse than all that were before him. He walked in all the way of Jeroboam the son of Nebat and in his sin wherewith he made Israel to sin, to provoke the Lord God of Israel to anger with their vanities (1 Kings 16:25–26). Each king surpassed his predecessor, yet the standard of comparison was always the original departure: the sin of Jeroboam.

Omri slept with his fathers and was buried in Samaria, and Ahab his son reigned in his stead (1 Kings 16:28). His son then exceeded him, as if walking in Jeroboam's sins were a light thing (1 Kings 16:31). The house Omri built became the house through which Baal worship entered Israel, and the prophet Micah later named the accumulated corruption as the statutes of Omri (Micah 6:16).
