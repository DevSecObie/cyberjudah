In the year King Uzziah died, Isaiah saw the Lord high and lifted up upon a throne, with seraphim crying Holy, holy, holy. His unclean lips were cleansed with a coal from the altar, and he answered, Here am I; send me. He walked naked and barefoot three years as a sign against Egypt and Ethiopia, counseled Hezekiah against Sennacherib, and prophesied the angel that smote 185,000 Assyrians in one night.

## The obedience

In the year that King Uzziah died, Isaiah saw the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up, and his train filled the temple. Above it stood the seraphims, each with six wings, crying one to another, Holy, holy, holy, is the Lord of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory. The posts of the door moved at the voice, and the house was filled with smoke (Isaiah 6:1–4).

Isaiah cried, Woe is me, for I am undone, because I am a man of unclean lips and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips, for mine eyes have seen the King, the Lord of hosts. A seraphim flew with a live coal from the altar and laid it upon his mouth, saying his iniquity was taken away and his sin purged (Isaiah 6:5–7). He heard the voice of the Lord saying, Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? And he answered, Here am I; send me (Isaiah 6:8).

He was sent to a people whose hearts were made fat, whose ears were heavy, whose eyes were shut, lest they see, hear, understand, convert, and be healed. The blindness was decreed until the cities be wasted and the land utterly desolate (Isaiah 6:9–11).

At the Lord's command, Isaiah walked naked and barefoot three years as a sign and wonder upon Egypt and Ethiopia, that the king of Assyria would lead them away captive, young and old, naked and barefoot, to the shame of Egypt (Isaiah 20:2–4). The prophet's body became the sign, and the sign endured for three years.

When Sennacherib sent Rabshakeh to blaspheme the living God and threaten Jerusalem, Hezekiah sent to Isaiah, who answered: Be not afraid of the words with which the servants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed me. I will send a blast upon him, and he shall hear a rumour, and return to his own land, and I will cause him to fall by the sword in his own land (Isaiah 37:6–7).

## The blessing

The Lord declared through Isaiah that the king of Assyria should not come into Jerusalem, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with shields, nor cast a bank against it. He would defend the city for His own sake and for His servant David's sake (Isaiah 37:33–35). Then the angel of the Lord went forth and smote in the camp of the Assyrians a hundred and fourscore and five thousand; and when they arose early in the morning, behold, they were all dead corpses (Isaiah 37:36). Sennacherib departed and was slain by his own sons in the house of his god, fulfilling the word spoken through Isaiah.

Tradition holds that Isaiah was sawn asunder under Manasseh (Hebrews 11:37). The prophet who saw the Lord and answered the call served through the reigns of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, carrying the word through a people whose hearts were fat and through kings who alternated between reform and ruin. His faithfulness was maintained not because the people received the word, but because the Lord who sent it had touched his mouth and purged his sin.
