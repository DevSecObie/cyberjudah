<p class="casehead"><span class="badge badge--secondary verdict">Sentence deferred</span> <span class="charge">Doing evil in the sight of the Lord; not departing from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat</span><br/><span class="era">Divided Kingdom</span></p>

Jeroboam the son of Joash reigned forty-one years over Israel and restored the coast from Hamath to the sea of the plain, as the Lord had spoken by Jonah the prophet. The Lord saw that Israel's affliction was bitter and saved them by his hand, yet Jeroboam did evil and departed not from Jeroboam's sins.

## The offense

Jeroboam the son of Joash began to reign in the fifteenth year of Amaziah and reigned forty-one years in Samaria. He did that which was evil in the sight of the Lord: he departed not from all the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin (2 Kings 14:23–24). The longest reign of any northern king carried no reform with it.

He restored the coast of Israel from the entering of Hamath unto the sea of the plain, according to the word of the Lord spoken by the hand of his servant Jonah the son of Amittai (2 Kings 14:25). The restoration was accomplished through him, yet it was the Lord's doing, because the Lord saw the affliction of Israel, that it was very bitter: there was not any shut up, nor any left, nor any helper for Israel (2 Kings 14:26).

## The judgment

The Lord said not that he would blot out the name of Israel from under heaven but saved them by the hand of Jeroboam the son of Joash (2 Kings 14:27). The deliverance was not because of Jeroboam's righteousness but because of the Lord's mercy toward His afflicted people.

Jeroboam slept with his fathers, and Zechariah his son reigned in his stead (2 Kings 14:29). The prosperity of his reign did not secure his house: Zechariah was slain after six months, fulfilling the word that Jehu's house would sit on the throne unto the fourth generation (2 Kings 15:12). The kingdom was saved through an evil king and then taken from his descendants according to a limit already spoken.
