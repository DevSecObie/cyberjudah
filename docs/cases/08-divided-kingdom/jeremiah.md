<p class="casehead"><span class="badge badge--primary verdict">Kept the law</span> <span class="charge">Speaking the word of the Lord faithfully despite persecution; not holding back when commanded to prophesy</span><br/><span class="era">Divided Kingdom</span></p>

Before Jeremiah was formed in the belly the Lord knew him, sanctified him, and ordained him a prophet unto the nations — set over kingdoms to root out, pull down, destroy, throw down, build, and plant. His own city conspired to kill him. The word burned like fire shut up in his bones. He was threatened with death, saved by Ahikam, and cast into a miry dungeon before Ebed-melech the Ethiopian drew him out.

## The obedience

The word of the Lord came to Jeremiah the son of Hilkiah: Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee, and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations (Jeremiah 1:5). When Jeremiah protested he was a child, the Lord told him to go wherever he was sent and speak whatever was commanded. Be not afraid of their faces, for I am with thee to deliver thee (Jeremiah 1:7–8).

The Lord put forth His hand, touched Jeremiah's mouth, and said, I have put my words in thy mouth. See, I have this day set thee over the nations and over the kingdoms, to root out, and to pull down, and to destroy, and to throw down, to build, and to plant (Jeremiah 1:9–10). The commission was both destructive and constructive, and neither part could be refused.

When Jeremiah prophesied in the temple court that this house would become like Shiloh, the priests and the prophets and the people took him and said, Thou shalt surely die. He answered that the Lord had sent him and that if they put him to death they would bring innocent blood upon themselves (Jeremiah 26:8–15). The hand of Ahikam the son of Shaphan was with him, and they did not give him to the people (Jeremiah 26:24).

Mocked and derided daily, Jeremiah resolved to stop speaking in the Lord's name. But his word was in mine heart as a burning fire shut up in my bones, and I was weary with forbearing, and I could not stay (Jeremiah 20:9). The word did not allow itself to be contained.

King Jehoiakim cut the prophetic scroll with a penknife and burned it leaf by leaf upon the hearth. The Lord commanded Jeremiah to take another roll and write all the former words again, with added curses against the king (Jeremiah 36:23–32). The burning of the scroll did not extinguish the word; it increased the sentence upon the one who burned it.

## The blessing

The princes cast Jeremiah into the dungeon of Malchiah, letting him down with cords into a place where there was no water but mire, and he sank in the mire (Jeremiah 38:6). Ebed-melech the Ethiopian spoke to the king and said these men have done evil in all they have done to Jeremiah. The king commanded him to take thirty men and draw the prophet up before he died. Ebed-melech let down old rags by cords, told Jeremiah to put them under his arms, and they drew him up (Jeremiah 38:7–13).

Jeremiah abode in the court of the prison until the day that Jerusalem was taken (Jeremiah 38:28). He witnessed the fall of the city and the fulfillment of every warning he had spoken. The Lord made him a defenced city, an iron pillar, and brasen walls: they fought against him but did not prevail, for the Lord was with him to deliver him (Jeremiah 1:18–19). The promise spoken at his ordination was kept to the end.
