**Lending on interest; taking brethren's lands and children for debt** · Restitution · Exile and Return

During the rebuilding of the wall, the poor cried out that they had mortgaged their fields and vineyards and sold their sons and daughters into bondage to pay the king's tribute and the nobles' interest. Nehemiah was very angry and set a great assembly against the nobles and rulers.

## The offense

Ye exact usury, every one of his brother. The law forbids taking usury from a poor brother or holding him as a bondservant; Nehemiah adds that they were buying back Jews sold to the heathen while their own class was selling them again.

## The judgment

Restore, I pray you, to them, even this day, their lands, their vineyards, their oliveyards, and their houses, also the hundredth part of the money. They said, we will restore them, and swore before the priests. Nehemiah shook his lap: so God shake out every man that performeth not this promise.

## Laws broken

- [16C.1](/law/16-economic-and-monetary-laws/16c#16C.1) If you lend to any of your brethren in need, you are not to take or lay usury upon them.
- [16C.5](/law/16-economic-and-monetary-laws/16c#16C.5) If one of your brethren becomes poor, you shall exact no usury or interest from him; you shall not lend him your money for usury, nor lend him your food at a profit.
- [16C.4](/law/16-economic-and-monetary-laws/16c#16C.4) Usury and the usurer are condemned.
- [15B.6](/law/15-general-welfare/15b#15B.6) You shall not oppress the poor.
- [21B.4](/law/21-servants-employees-etc/21b#21B.4) Your own brethren are not to be compelled to serve as a slave but shall be treated as a hired servant.
- [19C.20](/law/19-judgment-execution-of-law/19c#19C.20) A personal trespass that results in a loss of property is to be repaid in the principal amount plus an additional fifth part added to it.
