**Taking his brother's wife; beheading John for saying so** · Sentence not recorded · Gospels

Herod the tetrarch married Herodias, his brother Philip's wife, and John told him it was not lawful. Herodias wanted John dead; Herod feared him and heard him gladly, but at his birthday feast swore to give her dancing daughter whatever she asked. She asked for John's head on a charger.

## The offense

Leviticus twice: thou shalt not uncover the nakedness of thy brother's wife. Then a rash oath kept at the cost of a prophet's life, for the sake of them which sat with him. Christ called him that fox and would not answer him at his trial.

## The judgment

Scripture records no sentence on Herod or Herodias; the gospels leave them at Christ's trial, mocking him. History records Antipas exiled by Caligula at his wife's ambition. The record here is the crime and the statute.
