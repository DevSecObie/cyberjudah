**Extortion as chief of the publicans** · Restitution · Gospels

A rich chief tax-collector of Jericho climbed a sycamore to see Jesus and was called down by name. Standing in his house he said: the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.

## The offense

The publican's trade as commonly practiced: assessment by false accusation and extortion, which Leviticus 6 classes with theft and fraud. Fourfold was the law's rate for a stolen sheep, the highest ordinary restitution, and Zacchaeus chose it for himself.

## The judgment

This day is salvation come to this house, forsomuch as he also is a son of Abraham. The restitution was not the price of the salvation but its evidence: fruits meet for repentance, the very thing John had told the publicans to bring.

## Laws broken

- [19C.2](/law/19-judgment-execution-of-law/19c#19C.2) Stolen livestock which is slaughtered is to be repaid four or five times.
- [19C.15](/law/19-judgment-execution-of-law/19c#19C.15) If one gains property or money by way of a false report, lying, deceit, or extortion, he shall make full restitution plus an additional fifth part of its value.
- [19C.19](/law/19-judgment-execution-of-law/19c#19C.19) In civil trespasses involving property deceitfully obtained, the person guilty shall confess his crime and make restitution in full for his wrong, and add to it one-fifth of it, and give it to him whom he has wronged.
- [19C.20](/law/19-judgment-execution-of-law/19c#19C.20) A personal trespass that results in a loss of property is to be repaid in the principal amount plus an additional fifth part added to it.
- [16E](/law/16-economic-and-monetary-laws/16e) Taxes and Revenue (section)
- [2L.7](/law/02-relationship-to-god/2l#2L.7) True repentance is evidenced by one's fruits.
