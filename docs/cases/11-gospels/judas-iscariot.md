**Theft from the common purse; betraying innocent blood for thirty pieces of silver** · Put to death · Gospels

One of the twelve, keeper of the bag, and a thief who took what was put in it. He went to the chief priests and asked what they would give him; they weighed thirty pieces of silver. He delivered Christ with a kiss. In the morning he brought the silver back: I have sinned in that I have betrayed the innocent blood.

## The offense

Covetousness ripened into treachery. Zechariah had priced the shepherd at thirty pieces of silver, the price of a gored slave. His own confession names the statute: cursed be he that taketh reward to slay an innocent person. Christ: good were it for that man if he had never been born.

## The judgment

The priests would not take the money back; he cast it down in the temple, went and hanged himself, and the silver bought the potter's field. Acts adds that he fell headlong and burst asunder. Peter, citing the Psalms, says he went to his own place, and his bishoprick passed to another.
