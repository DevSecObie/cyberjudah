**Attempted adultery by extortion; false witness against an innocent woman** · Put to death · Second Temple (Apocrypha)

Two elders of Babylon's Jews, appointed judges, hid in Joacim's garden, tried to force his wife Susanna, and when she cried out, accused her of adultery with a young man. The assembly believed them, as elders and judges, and condemned her to death. Daniel stopped the execution and examined them apart.

## The offense

Lust turned to extortion and then to perjury: they perverted their own hearts and turned away their eyes that they might not look unto heaven nor remember just judgments. Examined separately, one said the pair were under a mastick tree and the other under a holm tree. Their own mouths convicted them of false witness.

## The judgment

According to the law of Moses they did unto them in such sort as they maliciously intended to do to their neighbour: and they put them to death. Thus the innocent blood was saved the same day. Deuteronomy 19 is the statute; this is its clearest case.
