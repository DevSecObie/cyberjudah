**Attempting to plunder the temple treasury** · Reprieve · Second Temple (Apocrypha)

Sent by King Seleucus on the information of Simon the temple governor, Heliodorus came to seize the money laid up in the treasury, though the high priest Onias told him it was the relief of widows and fatherless children and deposits held in trust. The whole city lay in sackcloth. He went in anyway.

## The offense

Sacrilege against the place, and theft from the poor the place protected. The law that forbids afflicting widows and the fatherless, and the law that makes God's house holy, were both in front of him. He proceeded as he had resolved.

## The judgment

A horse with a terrible rider in golden armor struck him with its forefeet, and two young men scourged him without ceasing; he fell to the ground in darkness and was carried out on a litter. Onias offered sacrifice for his life, and he lived. He returned to the king and testified: if thou hast any enemy or traitor, send him thither, and thou shalt receive him well scourged.
