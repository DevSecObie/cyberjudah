**Speaking of God the thing that is not right; condemning the righteous** · Restitution · Patriarchal

Eliphaz, Bildad, and Zophar came to comfort Job and spent thirty chapters insisting his suffering proved hidden sin. Eliphaz went so far as to invent the sins: thou hast taken a pledge from thy brother for nought, and stripped the naked of their clothing. When the Lord answered out of the whirlwind, he answered them too.

## The offense

Job had asked them earlier: will ye speak wickedly for God? and talk deceitfully for him? They had judged a man on suspicion, condemned the just, and defended God with false accusations against his servant. Job's cause was right; their theology was not.

## The judgment

My wrath is kindled against thee and against thy two friends, for ye have not spoken of me the thing that is right, as my servant Job hath. They were ordered to bring seven bullocks and seven rams to Job and have him pray for them, lest I deal with you after your folly. He did, and the Lord accepted Job.
