## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Galatians 3 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-29, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Galatians 3:1](/bible/galatians/3#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/galatians/3#v1)</sup> O foolish Galatians, who hath bewitched you, that ye should not obey the truth, before whose eyes Jesus Christ hath been evidently set forth, crucified among you?

- The works of the law is the law of sacrifice, the use of animals. Christ's death freed us from having to atone with animals: that is the liberty spied out in Galatians 2. Otherwise we are condemned, because the temple is gone and the priests are scattered, stupid and lost, so we can't atone that way.
- If you seek to be justified by animals, you are a condemned man, because there are sins no animal can cover. If you were out here selling drugs, weed, psychedelics, LSD, cocaine, crack, you were a witch, a sorcerer: that is death. If you are a pimp, you are a whoremonger: death for that too. There is no atoning for witchcraft. So those who came from that background, thank Christ for dying for you; otherwise your ass would be finished. Idolatry: done. Catholic, condemned. Baptist, condemned. Muslim, condemned. Say you're still under the old covenant, and you're done, because you need an animal and a priest to atone, and there is no animal for that.
- And you are not David, you are not Moses, you are not Solomon; you don't get the pass they did. The exception does not change the rule; it proves the rule. When you commit adultery or witchcraft, you get death. There are exceptions, but they are exceptions because most of the time there are none.

**[Galatians 3:2](/bible/galatians/3#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/galatians/3#v2)</sup> This only would I learn of you, Received ye the Spirit by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?

- “Received ye the Spirit by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?” Did you receive the spirit of Christ by offering animals, or by hearing of Christ? How did you learn the gospel? The works of the law in verse 2 is the law of sacrifice, the same works of the law by which no man is justified in Galatians 2:16.
- “Received ye the Spirit by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?” Did you receive the Spirit, the understanding of Christ, by the works of the law, or by hearing and understanding the faith?

**[Galatians 3:3](/bible/galatians/3#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/galatians/3#v3)</sup> Are ye so foolish? having begun in the Spirit, are ye now made perfect by the flesh?

- “Are ye so foolish?” Are you stupid or something? Something wrong with you? Are you slow? Paul is cursing them out. “Having begun in the Spirit”, having begun learning about Christ, “are ye now made perfect by the flesh?” The flesh of animals.
- “Having begun in the Spirit”, as believers in Christ, “are ye now made perfect by the flesh?” By the flesh means by the law: the flesh of the animals. It is the same thing.

**[Galatians 3:4](/bible/galatians/3#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/galatians/3#v4)</sup> Have ye suffered so many things in vain? if it be yet in vain.

- “Have ye suffered so many things in vain?” Are you going through persecution and being hated for following Christ for nothing?
- “Have ye suffered so many things in vain?” Have you suffered following Christ for nothing?

**[Galatians 3:5](/bible/galatians/3#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/galatians/3#v5)</sup> He therefore that ministereth to you the Spirit, and worketh miracles among you, doeth he it by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?

- “Doeth he it by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?” The works of the law in verse 5 is the same as in verse 2: sacrifice. These are the Christians' go-tos: they run to Galatians 2:16, to 3:2 and to 3:5, and all three go into the same thing.
- Right back to verse 2: he that “worketh miracles among you”, does he do it “by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?”

**[Galatians 3:6-9](/bible/galatians/3#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/galatians/3#v6)</sup> Even as Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/galatians/3#v7)</sup> Know ye therefore that they which are of faith, the same are the children of Abraham.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/galatians/3#v8)</sup> And the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the heathen through faith, preached before the gospel unto Abraham, saying, In thee shall all nations be blessed.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/galatians/3#v9)</sup> So then they which be of faith are blessed with faithful Abraham.

- “Even as Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness.” Abraham's faith came first. God told Abraham, I'm going to give you a son, a whole lot of land, and children like the sand of the sea, and Abraham said, all right, sounds good to me. Then he offered sacrifices, after he believed what God told him. His faith came first and God counted that for righteousness, not his offering up an animal. That was secondary.
- “They which are of faith, the same are the children of Abraham.” The scripture foresaw “that God would justify the heathen through faith”. Heathen is another word for Gentiles: God would justify the Israelites scattered among the Gentiles through faith. “In thee shall all nations be blessed.” Here they say, see, brother, all nations will be blessed. That is twisting the scripture, doing what the Bible says not to do.
- Esau already got his blessing: blood and death, weaponry, war, bloodshed, nukes, witchcraft. Isaac blessed Esau; he isn't getting blessed again, and he is dropping his blessings on Iran right now. The blessing for the nations is to work forever: to build up our kingdom and survive the destruction, to be a slave.

You will not find anywhere in the law and the prophets that Christ would justify, atone for or reconcile all the heathen Gentile nations through his death.

**[Galatians 3:10](/bible/galatians/3#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/galatians/3#v10)</sup> For as many as are of the works of the law are under the curse: for it is written, Cursed is every one that continueth not in all things which are written in the book of the law to do them.

- “For as many as are of the works of the law are under the curse”. Those four words again: works of the law, in verse 2, in verse 5, in verse 10 and in Galatians 2:16. They go to the law of sacrifice, Nehemiah 10:33. Under the curse of the covenant: everyone who confirmed to do everything under that covenant, which is offering up animals. If you violate laws that you can't use an animal for, you are cursed: you get death.
- “For as many as are of the works of the law are under the curse”. The works of the law are in verse 2, in verse 5, in Galatians 2:16, and here again. The curse is condemnation and death: cursed be he that does this, cursed be he that does that, cursed be he that commits adultery, that kills, that steals, that bears false witness, that disrespects his mother and father, that worships idols. There is no repentance for those things.
- “Cursed is every one that continueth not in all things which are written in the book of the law to do them”: all things means the commandments coupled with sacrificing. In Paul’s time the temple still stood and people were still accustomed to offering up animals. But the Galatians did not come into this truth by offering up animals. They came in because they heard that Christ died for them, redeemed them from all the sins the animals could not atone for, granted them eternal life if they keep his commandments, and grafted them back in with their brothers, the southern kingdom.

**[Galatians 3:11](/bible/galatians/3#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/galatians/3#v11)</sup> But that no man is justified by the law in the sight of God, it is evident: for, The just shall live by faith.

- “No man is justified by the law in the sight of God”: justified by the works of the law and justified by the law are the same, the same law as in verse 10, verse 5, verse 2 and Galatians 2:16. “The just shall live by faith.”
- “But that no man is justified by the law in the sight of God”: the law of sacrifice again. Where remission of sins is, there is no more offering for sin. “It is evident: for, The just shall live by faith.” Paul is quoting Habakkuk 2:4.

**[Galatians 3:12](/bible/galatians/3#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/galatians/3#v12)</sup> And the law is not of faith: but, The man that doeth them shall live in them.

- “The law is not of faith”, because faith came first. “The man that doeth them shall live in them”: the man who does them, offering sacrifice, lives until he commits a sin worthy of death. Then he can't live anymore.
- “And the law is not of faith”: not of the promise. The law here is sacrifice, as in Hebrews 9 and Hebrews 10. Abraham’s faith came first; his faith and the promise to him go hand in hand, and the promise predates the law and sacrifice. “The man that doeth them shall live in them.”

**[Galatians 3:13](/bible/galatians/3#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/galatians/3#v13)</sup> Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:

- “Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us”. Many read right past it. The curse of the law is death: the death we all confirmed and agreed to, and then violated. Being made a curse for us means he died for us. “Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree”: Christ took that curse on himself when he was hung.
- “Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law”: Christ bought us, Christ freed us. The curse of the law is in Deuteronomy 28, and one of those curses is going into slavery again with ships and being sold unto our enemies, with no man to buy us. Christ has redeemed us from slavery; the curse of the law is slavery.
- “Being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree”. He was made a curse for us when he was murdered, crucified: they hanged him on a tree and killed him. In his dying, his blood redeemed us. That was the price paid for us to be redeemed and freed. The Lord said we sold ourselves for nought and would be redeemed without money, and we were redeemed with Christ’s blood, not with shekels, silver and gold.

- “That the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ”: the Gentiles are the northern kingdom.

**[Galatians 3:15](/bible/galatians/3#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/galatians/3#v15)</sup> Brethren, I speak after the manner of men; Though it be but a man’s covenant, yet if it be confirmed, no man disannulleth, or addeth thereto.

- “I speak after the manner of men”: hypothetically speaking, in layman's terms. “Though it be but a man’s covenant”, covenant or testament, which are the same thing, “yet if it be confirmed, no man disannulleth, or addeth thereto”. No man can take away from it or add to it: it is written, it is in stone.

**[Galatians 3:16](/bible/galatians/3#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/galatians/3#v16)</sup> Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ.

- “Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ.” That’s why the holy seed starts with Christ. It does not start with us; it starts with Christ. He is the first of the holy seed, the holy seed Christ, of the tribe of Judah, for Israel.
- That’s what Matthew chapter 1, verse 1 all the way down to 16, is all about: it gives you his generations, his genealogy. The holy seed would come through that lineage.
- “Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ.” The promise passed on to Abraham went on to Christ our king.

**[Galatians 3:17-18](/bible/galatians/3#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/galatians/3#v17)</sup> And this I say, that the covenant, that was confirmed before of God in Christ, the law, which was four hundred and thirty years after, cannot disannul, that it should make the promise of none effect.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/galatians/3#v18)</sup> For if the inheritance be of the law, it is no more of promise: but God gave it to Abraham by promise.

- “The law, which was four hundred and thirty years after, cannot disannul” the covenant. That law is sacrificial law: the works of the law in verses 2, 5 and 10, and justified by the law in verse 11. Abraham's promise predates the Mosaic law. The Lord promised Abraham a seed, which goes to Christ, and promised him land, before Moses.
- “For if the inheritance be of the law, it is no more of promise”: if the inheritance came by the sacrifice, the law would supersede the promise. But how can that be, when the promise came first? It can't. “God gave it to Abraham by promise.”

**[Galatians 3:19-20](/bible/galatians/3#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/galatians/3#v19)</sup> Wherefore then serveth the law? It was added because of transgressions, till the seed should come to whom the promise was made; and it was ordained by angels in the hand of a mediator.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/galatians/3#v20)</sup> Now a mediator is not a mediator of one, but God is one.

- “Wherefore then serveth the law?” What is the purpose of the animal sacrifice? “It was added because of transgressions”: because of the sins within us, to keep us in check, as Romans 5 showed. “Till the seed should come to whom the promise was made”: the sacrifice was added until the seed arrived, and the seed is Christ, the one seed of verse 16. “In the hand of a mediator”: Moses was our mediator.

**[Galatians 3:21](/bible/galatians/3#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/galatians/3#v21)</sup> Is the law then against the promises of God? God forbid: for if there had been a law given which could have given life, verily righteousness should have been by the law.

- “Is the law then against the promises of God? God forbid”: the promises came first. No law was given which could have given life, because we were sinful by nature.

**[Galatians 3:22](/bible/galatians/3#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/galatians/3#v22)</sup> But the scripture hath concluded all under sin, that the promise by faith of Jesus Christ might be given to them that believe.

- “But the scripture hath concluded all under sin”. Here they say, see, all nations are under sin. All nations are sinful, yes, but they are not all under sin. Those under sin are the ones under the law: all Israel, Jews and Gentiles. “That the promise by faith of Jesus Christ might be given to them that believe”: all Israel.

**[Galatians 3:23](/bible/galatians/3#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/galatians/3#v23)</sup> But before faith came, we were kept under the law, shut up unto the faith which should afterwards be revealed.

- “Before faith came”, before Christ arrived, “we were kept under the law”. Paul, writing to the Galatians, says we were kept under the law; and the ones under the law are the ones under sin. “Shut up unto the faith which should afterwards be revealed”: kept, shut up, locked into that covenant until Christ came, died, and freed us from it, granting us liberty.
- “But before faith came, we were kept under the law”. The we is Israel, the Israelites. Before Christ came on the scene and died for us, we were kept under the law of sacrifice, “shut up unto the faith which should afterwards be revealed”: the same as the carnal ordinances imposed on them until the time of reformation in Hebrews 9.

  Precepts:
  - **[Romans 3:19](/bible/romans/3#v19)**
    > <sup>[19](/bible/romans/3#v19)</sup> Now we know that what things soever the law saith, it saith to them who are under the law: that every mouth may be stopped, and all the world may become guilty before God.

    “Now we know that what things soever the law saith, it saith to them who are under the law”. Whatever the law says, it says to those under the law. Who is under the law? The same ones who are under sin. That is the we Paul says was kept under the law.
  - **[Hebrews 9:9-10](/bible/hebrews/9#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/hebrews/9#v9)</sup> Which was a figure for the time then present, in which were offered both gifts and sacrifices, that could not make him that did the service perfect, as pertaining to the conscience;
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/hebrews/9#v10)</sup> Which stood only in meats and drinks, and divers washings, and carnal ordinances, imposed on them until the time of reformation.

    This is an example of the works of the law. It “was a figure for the time then present, in which were offered both gifts and sacrifices, that could not make him that did the service perfect, as pertaining to the conscience”. The gifts and sacrifices could not make the one who did the sacrificing perfect as pertaining to the mind, because sin was already in us since Adam. So the animals had to be temporary.

**[Galatians 3:24](/bible/galatians/3#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/galatians/3#v24)</sup> Wherefore the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ, that we might be justified by faith.

- “Wherefore the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ”. The law that was our schoolmaster is sacrifice. The sacrificing of animals was a foreshadowing of Christ's death, to prepare us for his body and his death. It was temporary, because we would all eventually have been condemned through it: we were too sinful to keep doing it over and over, and grew weary and rebellious behind it. “That we might be justified by faith”: by Christ's death.
- “Wherefore the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ”: our mentor, our stepping stone, a placeholder for Christ’s body being offered in the end according to his will and testament, the old testament. “That we might be justified by faith”: justification is in Christ’s death, not in the law of sacrifice, which is no more according to Hebrews 9 and 10.

**[Galatians 3:25](/bible/galatians/3#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/galatians/3#v25)</sup> But after that faith is come, we are no longer under a schoolmaster.

- “But after that faith is come”, the time of reformation, “we are no longer under a schoolmaster”: no longer under the law of sacrifice.
- “But after that faith is come”, now that Christ has come and gone, “we are no longer under a schoolmaster”. The schoolmaster is the law of sacrifice.

**[Galatians 3:26-27](/bible/galatians/3#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/galatians/3#v26)</sup> For ye are all the children of God by faith in Christ Jesus.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/galatians/3#v27)</sup> For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ.

- “For ye are all the children of God by faith in Christ Jesus.” Remember, he said we were kept under the law. All the ye's and you's and our's are the same we that were kept under the law. Don't escape the context: start at verse 23 and read down. “As many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ”: the same people who were kept under the law.
- “For ye are all the children of God by faith in Christ Jesus”: ye who were kept under the law, Israel. You are the children of God by faith in Christ now, not by animal sacrifice. That was the bewitching earlier in the chapter: believing you can be justified without Christ, that all you need is an animal.

**[Galatians 3:28](/bible/galatians/3#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/galatians/3#v28)</sup> There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus.

- “There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female”. Christians say it like a soft effeminate man: neither Jew nor Greek, brother; praise white Jesus. They have no idea about the history, so they read it as anybody.
- But the ye is the same ye of verse 23 that was kept under the law. Those kept under the law are called Jews and Greeks, bond and free, male and female. The Greeks are Israelites who were made Greeks, as 2 Maccabees shows. So whatever title you go under, free or bond, Jew or Greek, “ye are all one in Christ Jesus”: you are all God's chosen people, whatever your title or status in the world of that time. Paul was free and some were bond, but they were all God's people.
- “There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female”. The ye is Israel, the same we that was kept under the law. They were under many different statuses: some were called Jews, some Greeks, some were bond, some free, some male, some female, but they are all the same people of God in Christ.

**[Galatians 3:29](/bible/galatians/3#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/galatians/3#v29)</sup> And if ye be Christ’s, then are ye Abraham’s seed, and heirs according to the promise.

- “And if ye be Christ’s, then are ye Abraham’s seed, and heirs according to the promise.” The heirs according to the promise are the Israelites.
- “And if ye be Christ’s”, if you have faith in Christ and are not bewitched into animal sacrifice, “then are ye Abraham’s seed”: Abraham is the father of faith, so you should be children of faith like your father Abraham. “And heirs according to the promise.” The promise pertains to the Israelites. The Galatians are the children of promise. All nations are not.
- If they are, show a nation by its biblical name in the old covenant that God calls his people and to whom the promise pertains alongside Israel. Fill in the blank. Moab? Ammon? The Elamites dropping bombs over there in Israel today? The Canaanites? The Philistines? There is no name.
