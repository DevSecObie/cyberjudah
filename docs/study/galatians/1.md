**[Galatians 1:1](/bible/galatians/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/galatians/1#v1)</sup> Paul, an apostle, not of men, neither by man, but by Jesus Christ, and God the Father, who raised him from the dead;

- Paul was not taught Christ from the disciples and was not awakened by them. Paul was literally awakened and taught by Christ himself.
- Paul's credentials were astounding, above any other person. The things revealed to him were not revealed to many.
- There were mysteries revealed to him on a level beyond the other disciples. Paul was able to go to scriptures and history and show the correlation between the past and the present.
- Christ himself stopped Paul in his tracks and revealed himself to him even after his death, because Paul played an extremely important role in the gospel. If you are of the elect, if you were commissioned to do a job from the beginning, no matter what point of life you are in, you are going to wake up and fulfill the scriptures.
- There are certain people chosen from the foundation of the world to preach this gospel.

  Precepts:
  - **[Acts 9:1-6](/bible/acts/9#v1)**
  - **[Acts 9:15-16](/bible/acts/9#v15)**

- **Precept:** Paul's apostleship came directly from Christ, not from any man or group of men
- **Precept:** Those chosen from the foundation of the world will fulfill their role regardless of their prior walk of life

**[Galatians 1:2](/bible/galatians/1#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/galatians/1#v2)</sup> And all the brethren which are with me, unto the churches of Galatia:

- The word 'brethren' is key. Paul's brethren are Israelites.
- Romans 9:3-4 proves this: Paul wished he could take on the curses of Christ for his brethren, his kinsmen according to the flesh, meaning according to nationality, according to race, who are Israelites. External sources claim the people of Galatia were Gauls or Celts that migrated there, but you must go precept upon precept, line upon line.
- No other people were called brethren outside of the nation of Israel. The churches of Galatia were Israelites congregating.
- A church just means a gathering or assembly of people. Paul visited Galatia in Acts 16:6 when they went through the region of Galatia, and in Acts 18:23 he went back to strengthen the disciples there.

  Precepts:
  - **[Romans 9:3-4](/bible/romans/9#v3)**
  - **[Acts 16:6](/bible/acts/16#v6)**
  - **[Acts 18:23-24](/bible/acts/18#v23)**

- **Precept:** The term 'brethren' in Paul's epistles refers specifically to Israelites
- **Precept:** A church means a gathering or assembly of people

**[Galatians 1:3](/bible/galatians/1#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/galatians/1#v3)</sup> Grace be to you and peace from God the Father, and from our Lord Jesus Christ,

- The grace Paul speaks about is grace and mercy given by Christ. Prior to Christ, under the old covenant, there was no mercy given.
- John 1:17 states the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ. The law was not done away with; it was also given by Jesus Christ, and the only difference is that mercy was given through the blood of Christ.
- This verse also shows that Christ and God are two separate entities: 'Grace be to you and peace from God the Father, and from our Lord Jesus Christ,' showing they are two separate entities. God is the Father and Christ is the Son of God.

  Precepts:
  - **[John 1:17](/bible/john/1#v17)**

- **Precept:** Grace means mercy, given through the blood of Christ
- **Precept:** The law was not done away with; mercy was added through Christ
- **Precept:** God the Father and Jesus Christ are two separate entities, not one

**[Galatians 1:4](/bible/galatians/1#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/galatians/1#v4)</sup> Who gave himself for our sins, that he might deliver us from this present evil world, according to the will of God and our Father:

- Christ gave himself for his people's sins. Matthew 1:21 says 'he shall save his people from their sins.' His people are the Israelites. 'Our sins' means the Israelite sins.
- Luke 1:68-74 confirms this: 'Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, for he hath visited and redeemed his people.' The horn of salvation is Christ from the lion of Judah, raised up in the house of David. It was prophesied from the beginning of the world for the deliverance of the nation of Israel.
- The whole purpose of salvation is to be saved from our enemies, all those who had a hand in our slavery and oppression. This whole world, this whole system, is based upon sin.
- Titus 2:11-12 explains the grace of God teaches us to deny ungodliness and worldly lust. Grace gives us a chance and opportunity to overcome sins through mercy.
- If you keep striving and fighting, eventually you will overcome. Grace also teaches us to keep the commandments in this present world.
- When it says 'according to the will of God and our Father,' God refers to Christ (who is called God in Hebrews 1:8) and Father refers to the Most High.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 1:21](/bible/matthew/1#v21)**
  - **[Luke 1:68-74](/bible/luke/1#v68)**
  - **[Titus 2:11-12](/bible/titus/2#v11)**
  - **[Hebrews 1:8](/bible/hebrews/1#v8)**
  - **[Revelation 1:6](/bible/revelation/1#v6)**
  - **[1 Samuel 4:8](/bible/1-samuel/4#v8)**
  - **[Isaiah 9:6](/bible/isaiah/9#v6)**

- **Precept:** Christ died specifically for his people, the Israelites
- **Precept:** Salvation's purpose is deliverance from enemies and from this evil world
- **Precept:** Grace teaches us to deny ungodliness and to keep God's commandments
- **Precept:** Grace gives repeated chances to overcome sin through mercy
- **Precept:** Christ is called God (Hebrews 1:8); there is a Godhead structure with the Most High as the highest power and Christ as the second power

**[Galatians 1:5](/bible/galatians/1#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/galatians/1#v5)</sup> To whom be glory for ever and ever. Amen.

- Brief doxology before Paul moves into his rebuke of the Galatians.

**[Galatians 1:6-7](/bible/galatians/1#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/galatians/1#v6)</sup> I marvel that ye are so soon removed from him that called you into the grace of Christ unto another gospel: Which is not another; but there be some that trouble you, and would pervert the gospel of Christ.

- Paul was speaking to the Israelites in Galatia, saying he was surprised they were so far removed from the gospel of Christ, from the teachings. We teach brothers and sisters and expect them to hold fast to what was taught, not to be persuaded otherwise.
- But Israelites that came out from among them were persuading and beguiling those Israelites in different regions of the diaspora. It is no different from today.
- What happened then is happening now, but under a different name. Different doctrines come out, different names, but the same spirits doing the same things. 'Which is not another' because they use the same Bibles and same scriptures, but the wrong understanding.
- They bring the same scriptures but change the meaning. Acts 15:24 confirms: 'certain which went out from us have troubled you with words, subverting your souls.' Subverted means destroying, changing, altering.
- The doctrine back then was insisting on circumcision and keeping the law of Moses meaning animal sacrifice. Today the doctrines are different heresies but the same spirit.

  Precepts:
  - **[Acts 15:24-27](/bible/acts/15#v24)**

- **Precept:** False teachers use the same scriptures but change the understanding
- **Precept:** Those who pervert the gospel came out from among the believers
- **Precept:** History repeats itself: the same spirits work under different names in every era
- **Precept:** There must be heresies to prove those which are of God

**[Galatians 1:8](/bible/galatians/1#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/galatians/1#v8)</sup> But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed.

- Paul was going to an extreme. He says even if you see us the disciples, or even an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel than what was brought to you by the disciples, let that man be cursed, let that man die.
- There is no hope for a man like that. There is no repentance for a heretic that learned Christ and changed the doctrine.
- Matthew 12:31-32 says all manner of sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven unto men, but the blasphemy against the Holy Ghost shall not be forgiven, neither in this world nor in the world to come. The blasphemy against the Holy Ghost is subverting the doctrine, speaking heresies, bringing blasphemous doctrines, going against the laws of God.
- Once they go to heresy and don't want to repent, after the first and second admonition reject the heretic. The Most High will take away their spirit.
- They are reprobate. It is impossible to repent after that.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 12:31-32](/bible/matthew/12#v31)**
  - **[Titus 3:10](/bible/titus/3#v10)**

- **Precept:** Changing the gospel carries the curse of spiritual death
- **Precept:** Blasphemy against the Holy Ghost is subverting the doctrine and bringing heresies
- **Precept:** There is no forgiveness for unrepentant heretics who change the doctrine
- **Precept:** After the first and second admonition, reject the heretic

**[Galatians 1:9](/bible/galatians/1#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/galatians/1#v9)</sup> As we said before, so say I now again, If any man preach any other gospel unto you than that ye have received, let him be accursed.

- Paul had to reiterate it. That is how serious it is.
- When you change the gospel, when you change the meaning of the scriptures even though it was taught to you, when you bring heresies into the body, let him be a curse. You are not going to be forgiven in this world, neither are you going to be forgiven in the world to come.
- You are dead twice.

- **Precept:** Paul repeats the warning to emphasize the severity of changing the gospel
- **Precept:** Perverting the received gospel results in being cursed, dead twice

**[Galatians 1:10](/bible/galatians/1#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/galatians/1#v10)</sup> For do I now persuade men, or God? or do I seek to please men? for if I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ.

- Paul asks whether he seeks to persuade or please men. The Pharisees were all about the appearance of men, looking righteous before men.
- They were hypocrites. They did things that were easily seen: they focused on circumcision and sacrifices to appear righteous, wore phylacteries, broadened their fringes.
- They wanted preeminence. They wanted to look like the God-sent comforters.
- None of us in this truth should seek to please men. Do this for yourself, for your salvation.
- Some do things in the body just for rank. Your rank has nothing to do with your works or keeping the commandments.
- The scripture says strive lawfully, meaning your spirit and your works will match the rank given to you. If you yet please men, you cannot be the servant of Christ.
- You cannot fake this truth. The Most High knows.
- He tries the hearts and reins of man. He knows who is sincere.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Timothy 2:5](/bible/2-timothy/2#v5)**

- **Precept:** A servant of Christ cannot seek to please men
- **Precept:** You cannot fake it in this truth; the Most High tries the hearts and reins of man
- **Precept:** Rank should not be the motivation; strive lawfully and the spirit will match

**[Galatians 1:11-12](/bible/galatians/1#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/galatians/1#v11)</sup> But I certify you, brethren, that the gospel which was preached of me is not after man. For I neither received it of man, neither was I taught it, but by the revelation of Jesus Christ.

- The gospel preached by Paul is not after man. No man revealed it to him.
- Christ himself gave him the mysteries and teachings. That is why Peter says some things in Paul's writings were hard to be understood.
- There were things the other disciples could not understand because Paul was a chosen vessel specifically chosen of Christ. He was given an abundance of revelations above all the others. 2 Corinthians 12:1-7 shows Paul was caught up to the third heaven, into paradise, and heard unspeakable words which it was not lawful for a man to utter.
- He was revealed things he was forbidden to speak. The deep things revealed to us through Paul are already profound, yet there are things on a level we cannot even comprehend that he was not allowed to utter.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Peter 3:16](/bible/2-peter/3#v16)**
  - **[2 Corinthians 12:1-7](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v1)**

- **Precept:** Paul's gospel came directly by revelation from Jesus Christ, not from any man
- **Precept:** Paul was given an abundance of revelations above all other apostles
- **Precept:** Paul was caught up to the third heaven and told things unlawful for man to utter

**[Galatians 1:13-14](/bible/galatians/1#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/galatians/1#v13)</sup> For ye have heard of my conversation in time past in the Jews' religion, how that beyond measure I persecuted the church of God, and wasted it: And profited in the Jews' religion above many my equals in mine own nation, being more exceedingly zealous of the traditions of my fathers.

- Paul's conversation in times past in the Jews' religion refers to the laws of his fathers. Religion comes from the Latin word 'religio' meaning to bind, to restrain, to withhold you from your lust. 1 Maccabees 2:20-22 defines religion as the law and the ordinances: 'God forbid that we should forsake the law and the ordinances.
- We will not hearken to the king's words, to go from our religion.' Paul was a Pharisee. Philippians 3:4-6 shows his credentials: circumcised the eighth day, of the stock of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews, as touching the law a Pharisee.
- He was the top of his class. His conversation in times past under the law was as a Pharisee, a separated one, of the Pharisaical order.
- Acts 22:3 shows he was brought up at the feet of Gamaliel, taught according to the perfect manner of the law of the fathers. Paul was close to being perfect but battled just like we battle.
- He called himself the chief of sinners. 2 Corinthians 12:7 says a thorn in the flesh was given to him, the messenger of Satan, lest he be exalted above measure.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Maccabees 2:20-22](/bible/1-maccabees/2#v20)**
  - **[Philippians 3:4-7](/bible/philippians/3#v4)**
  - **[Acts 22:3](/bible/acts/22#v3)**
  - **[2 Corinthians 12:7](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v7)**
  - **[1 Timothy 1:15](/bible/1-timothy/1#v15)**

- **Precept:** The Jews' religion is the law and the ordinances, the traditions of the fathers
- **Precept:** Religion means to bind, restrain, and withhold from lust (from Latin 'religio')
- **Precept:** Even Paul, close to perfection, was given a thorn in the flesh to keep him humble

**[Galatians 1:15-16](/bible/galatians/1#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/galatians/1#v15)</sup> But when it pleased God, who separated me from my mother's womb, and called me by his grace, To reveal his Son in me, that I might preach him among the heathen; immediately I conferred not with flesh and blood:

- Paul was always ordained from his mother's womb, even though he was persecuting and killing the church, chasing them from city to city, locking them in jail. It was already ordained for him to fulfill his role.
- Jeremiah 1:5 says the same thing: 'Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee, and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations.' Many of us before we came from our mother's womb, the Most High separated us to be prophets. 'That I might preach him among the heathen' refers to Israel. 2 Esdras 2:33 shows Ezra called Israel heathens because when you do not keep the laws, you follow after the other nations. Ezekiel 20:30-32 confirms: when Israel is in sin, they are saying 'we will be as the heathen.' Paul was a minister unto the heathen, meaning the Israelites that were following the customs of other nations.
- When Paul says he conferred not with flesh and blood, he means no man taught him the doctrine or the gospel; Christ revealed it directly.

  Precepts:
  - **[Jeremiah 1:5](/bible/jeremiah/1#v5)**
  - **[2 Esdras 2:33](/bible/2-esdras/2#v33)**
  - **[Ezekiel 20:30-32](/bible/ezekiel/20#v30)**

- **Precept:** Many are ordained as prophets from the womb
- **Precept:** The term 'heathen' can refer to Israelites who follow the customs of other nations
- **Precept:** Paul did not consult with any man after his calling; Christ taught him directly

**[Galatians 1:17-18](/bible/galatians/1#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/galatians/1#v17)</sup> Neither went I up to Jerusalem to them which were apostles before me; but I went into Arabia, and returned again unto Damascus. Then after three years I went up to Jerusalem to see Peter, and abode with him fifteen days.

- Paul started preaching the gospel immediately after the spirit of Christ came upon him. He did not go to Jerusalem to consult with the apostles first.
- He went into Arabia because there were Israelites living in Arabia, then returned to Damascus where he was confounding the elders, preaching boldly that Christ was the Lord. Acts 9 covers this period.
- After three years of teaching, Paul went up to Jerusalem to see Peter and stayed with him fifteen days. The apostles were not aware of what Paul had been doing; he was doing these things having not been taught by Peter and the others.

  Precepts:
  - **[Acts 9:19-28](/bible/acts/9#v19)**

- **Precept:** Paul preached for three years before consulting the apostles in Jerusalem
- **Precept:** There were Israelites living in Arabia

**[Galatians 1:19-20](/bible/galatians/1#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/galatians/1#v19)</sup> But other of the apostles saw I none, save James the Lord's brother. Now the things which I write unto you, behold, before God, I lie not.

- Paul identifies which apostles he saw when he went to Jerusalem: only Peter and James the Lord's brother. He swears before God that he is telling the truth about his history and his calling.

**[Galatians 1:21-24](/bible/galatians/1#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/galatians/1#v21)</sup> Afterwards I came into the regions of Syria and Cilicia; And was unknown by face unto the churches of Judaea which were in Christ: But they had heard only, That he which persecuted us in times past now preacheth the faith which once he destroyed. And they glorified God in me.

- Nobody knew Paul at that time. He was a newly appointed apostle.
- The churches of Judea had only heard of him, that the one who persecuted them now preached the faith he once tried to destroy, and they glorified God because of him. News traveled about his conversion.

- **Precept:** Paul's conversion from persecutor to preacher caused the churches to glorify God
