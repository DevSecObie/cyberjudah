**[Numbers 36:1-4](/bible/numbers/36#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/numbers/36#v1)</sup> And the chief fathers of the families of the children of Gilead, the son of Machir, the son of Manasseh, of the families of the sons of Joseph, came near, and spake before Moses, and before the princes, the chief fathers of the children of Israel:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/numbers/36#v2)</sup> And they said, The Lord commanded my lord to give the land for an inheritance by lot to the children of Israel: and my lord was commanded by the Lord to give the inheritance of Zelophehad our brother unto his daughters.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/numbers/36#v3)</sup> And if they be married to any of the sons of the other tribes of the children of Israel, then shall their inheritance be taken from the inheritance of our fathers, and shall be put to the inheritance of the tribe whereunto they are received: so shall it be taken from the lot of our inheritance.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/numbers/36#v4)</sup> And when the jubile of the children of Israel shall be, then shall their inheritance be put unto the inheritance of the tribe whereunto they are received: so shall their inheritance be taken away from the inheritance of the tribe of our fathers.

- The chief fathers of the families of Gilead of the tribe of Manasseh bring an issue rising out of what was already ruled in Numbers 27: when Zelophehad died in the wilderness with no sons, the Lord ruled that his inheritance pass to his daughters.
- Their concern is real: if the daughters marry men of another tribe, their father's inheritance passes with them to the husband's tribe, and at the jubilee it is fixed to that tribe, so the tribe of Manasseh loses that land forever.

  Precepts:
  - **[Numbers 27:3-4](/bible/numbers/27#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/numbers/27#v3)</sup> Our father died in the wilderness, and he was not in the company of them that gathered themselves together against the Lord in the company of Korah; but died in his own sin, and had no sons.
    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/numbers/27#v4)</sup> Why should the name of our father be done away from among his family, because he hath no son? Give unto us therefore a possession among the brethren of our father.

    The daughters of Zelophehad pleaded that their father died in his own sin with no son, and asked why his name should be done away from among his family.
  - **[Numbers 27:6-8](/bible/numbers/27#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/numbers/27#v6)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Moses, saying,
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/numbers/27#v7)</sup> The daughters of Zelophehad speak right: thou shalt surely give them a possession of an inheritance among their father’s brethren; and thou shalt cause the inheritance of their father to pass unto them.
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/numbers/27#v8)</sup> And thou shalt speak unto the children of Israel, saying, If a man die, and have no son, then ye shall cause his inheritance to pass unto his daughter.

    The Lord ruled the daughters spoke right: if a man die and have no son, his inheritance passes to his daughter; that ruling set up the question of Numbers 36.

**[Numbers 36:5-7](/bible/numbers/36#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/numbers/36#v5)</sup> And Moses commanded the children of Israel according to the word of the Lord, saying, The tribe of the sons of Joseph hath said well.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/numbers/36#v6)</sup> This is the thing which the Lord doth command concerning the daughters of Zelophehad, saying, Let them marry to whom they think best; only to the family of the tribe of their father shall they marry.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/numbers/36#v7)</sup> So shall not the inheritance of the children of Israel remove from tribe to tribe: for every one of the children of Israel shall keep himself to the inheritance of the tribe of his fathers.

- The Most High affirms the tribe of the sons of Joseph: they have said well. The solution: the daughters of Zelophehad may marry whom they think best, only within the family of the tribe of their father.
- The purpose is stated plainly: so shall not the inheritance of the children of Israel remove from tribe to tribe; the marriage boundary here was instituted because of land inheritance.

**[Numbers 36:8-9](/bible/numbers/36#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/numbers/36#v8)</sup> And every daughter, that possesseth an inheritance in any tribe of the children of Israel, shall be wife unto one of the family of the tribe of her father, that the children of Israel may enjoy every man the inheritance of his fathers.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/numbers/36#v9)</sup> Neither shall the inheritance remove from one tribe to another tribe; but every one of the tribes of the children of Israel shall keep himself to his own inheritance.

- Every daughter that possessed an inheritance had to marry within her father's tribe, so every man of Israel would enjoy the inheritance of his fathers and no inheritance would remove from one tribe to another.
- The tribal restriction belonged to the time when Israel dwelt in the land; it existed because of inheritance. Today Israel has no inheritance in the lands of captivity, so the rule that still applies is marrying within the nation of Israel, not tribe-only marriage.
- No one today knows his exact tribe with certainty; a Judahite could have been dropped off anywhere in the captivity. Without the history of [Numbers 36](/bible/numbers/36), [Tobit 4:12](/bible/tobit/4#v12) gets twisted into a doctrine that a man may only marry his own tribe, and that is a heresy born of not knowing why the law was given.

  Precepts:
  - **[Tobit 4:12](/bible/tobit/4#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/tobit/4#v12)</sup> Beware of all whoredom, my son, and chiefly take a wife of the seed of thy fathers, and take not a strange woman to wife, which is not of thy father’s tribe: for we are the children of the prophets, Noe, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob: remember, my son, that our fathers from the beginning, even that they all married wives of their own kindred, and were blessed in their children, and their seed shall inherit the land.

    Take a wife of the seed of thy fathers and not a strange woman; the seed-of-thy-fathers command still stands, and the tribe clause belonged to the inheritance law of Numbers 36.

**[Numbers 36:10-13](/bible/numbers/36#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/numbers/36#v10)</sup> Even as the Lord commanded Moses, so did the daughters of Zelophehad:
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/numbers/36#v11)</sup> For Mahlah, Tirzah, and Hoglah, and Milcah, and Noah, the daughters of Zelophehad, were married unto their father’s brothers’ sons:
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/numbers/36#v12)</sup> And they were married into the families of the sons of Manasseh the son of Joseph, and their inheritance remained in the tribe of the family of their father.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/numbers/36#v13)</sup> These are the commandments and the judgments, which the Lord commanded by the hand of Moses unto the children of Israel in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho.

- The daughters of Zelophehad did as the Lord commanded Moses: Mahlah, Tirzah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Noah married their father's brothers' sons, within the families of Manasseh, and their inheritance remained in the tribe of the family of their father.
- These are the commandments and the judgments which the Lord commanded by the hand of Moses in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho; with this verse the book of Numbers is completed.
