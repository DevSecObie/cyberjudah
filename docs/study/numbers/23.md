## Introduction

This reading covers [Numbers 23](/bible/numbers/23) through 26: Balaam's parables that bless Israel instead of cursing her, the star and sceptre prophecy of the latter days, Israel joined unto Baal-peor and the zeal of Phinehas, and the second census of the families of Israel in the plains of Moab. This is a reading session, with the chapters read straight through and teaching given along the way.

**[Numbers 23:1-2](/bible/numbers/23#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/numbers/23#v1)</sup> And Balaam said unto Balak, Build me here seven altars, and prepare me here seven oxen and seven rams.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/numbers/23#v2)</sup> And Balak did as Balaam had spoken; and Balak and Balaam offered on every altar a bullock and a ram.

- Sacrificing animals was not solely a thing pertaining to Israel; sacrificing for sin is what pertained to Israel. All the ancient nations of old sacrificed animals when they worshipped their gods, so Balak and Balaam offering bullocks and rams on altars was nothing new to them.
- Offering animals on altars had been done since the time of Adam. It was commonplace in that time, so do not be confused at why Balaam keeps calling for altars and offerings through this chapter.

**[Numbers 23:7-8](/bible/numbers/23#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/numbers/23#v7)</sup> And he took up his parable, and said, Balak the king of Moab hath brought me from Aram, out of the mountains of the east, saying, Come, curse me Jacob, and come, defy Israel.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/numbers/23#v8)</sup> How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed? or how shall I defy, whom the Lord hath not defied?

- Balak the king of Moab brought Balaam from Aram to curse Jacob and defy Israel, and Balaam could not do it: how shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed?
- Understand this: we are a blessed people, but we are living under the curses of God for our disobedience. We are the sons and daughters of God, above all nations, yet on the bottom for our sin, and we remain there until we repent as a nation.

**[Numbers 23:9-10](/bible/numbers/23#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/numbers/23#v9)</sup> For from the top of the rocks I see him, and from the hills I behold him: lo, the people shall dwell alone, and shall not be reckoned among the nations.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/numbers/23#v10)</sup> Who can count the dust of Jacob, and the number of the fourth part of Israel? Let me die the death of the righteous, and let my last end be like his!

- The people shall dwell alone and shall not be reckoned among the nations; who can count the dust of Jacob? Israel is as the sand of the sea.
- Let me die the death of the righteous, and let my last end be like his. The last end of Israel is a crown of glory in the hand of the Lord: the land married, double for our shame, everlasting joy, and a seed acknowledged among the Gentiles as the seed which the Lord hath blessed. That is why we cannot wait for those days.

**[Numbers 23:11-12](/bible/numbers/23#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/numbers/23#v11)</sup> And Balak said unto Balaam, What hast thou done unto me? I took thee to curse mine enemies, and, behold, thou hast blessed them altogether.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/numbers/23#v12)</sup> And he answered and said, Must I not take heed to speak that which the Lord hath put in my mouth?

- Balak charged Balaam with blessing his enemies altogether, and Balaam answered: must I not take heed to speak that which the Lord hath put in my mouth?
- That is the same charge given to Ezekiel, and it is what every man must do who speaks on behalf of the Most High, especially the brothers: go unto the house of Israel and speak with His words unto them.
- The house of Israel is impudent and hardhearted, so the Lord makes the face and forehead strong, meaning the mind: whatever excuse the people raise, He gives an answer through the scriptures on the spot. The rebellious spirits on our people are only cast down by the word of God, which is why we speak what the Lord puts in our mouth.

    Go, get thee unto the house of Israel, and speak with my words unto them.
  - **[Ezekiel 3:7-9](/bible/ezekiel/3#v7)**

**[Numbers 23:19](/bible/numbers/23#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/numbers/23#v19)</sup> God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent: hath he said, and shall he not do it? or hath he spoken, and shall he not make it good?

- God is not a man, that he should lie. Whatever the Lord speaks out of His mouth, once the judgment is passed there is no taking it back, whether in this life or the one to come.
- The parable of Lazarus shows it: between us and you there is a great gulf fixed, so that none can pass. The judgment has been set and there is no changing it.
- In the day of doom no man shall be able to save him that is destroyed. This present life is not the end, and asking the righteous to pray for those that outright despise God and His laws is praying for the weak; Esdras tried that with the Most High and was told to concern himself with the righteous.
- That is what we doubted back in Deuteronomy when Moses read the curses over us; we thought the Lord would not make good on His word. But the Lord is longsuffering and patient and will in no wise acquit the wicked; we will not escape our sins.

  Precepts:
  - **[Luke 16:25-26](/bible/luke/16#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/luke/16#v25)</sup> But Abraham said, Son, remember that thou in thy lifetime receivedst thy good things, and likewise Lazarus evil things: but now he is comforted, and thou art tormented.
    >
    > <sup>[26](/bible/luke/16#v26)</sup> And beside all this, between us and you there is a great gulf fixed: so that they which would pass from hence to you cannot; neither can they pass to us, that would come from thence.

    Between us and you there is a great gulf fixed; once judgment is passed there is no crossing it, because God is not a man that he should lie.
  - **[2 Esdras 7:42-45](/bible/2-esdras/7#v42)**
    > <sup>[42](/bible/2-esdras/7#v42)</sup> He answered me, and said, This present life is not the end where much glory doth abide; therefore have they prayed for the weak.
    >
    > <sup>[43](/bible/2-esdras/7#v43)</sup> But the day of doom shall be the end of this time, and the beginning of the immortality for to come, wherein corruption is past,
    >
    > <sup>[44](/bible/2-esdras/7#v44)</sup> Intemperance is at an end, infidelity is cut off, righteousness is grown, and truth is sprung up.
    >
    > <sup>[45](/bible/2-esdras/7#v45)</sup> Then shall no man be able to save him that is destroyed, nor to oppress him that hath gotten the victory.

    In the day of doom no man shall be able to save him that is destroyed; the sentence stands for ever.

**[Numbers 23:20-21](/bible/numbers/23#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/numbers/23#v20)</sup> Behold, I have received commandment to bless: and he hath blessed; and I cannot reverse it.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/numbers/23#v21)</sup> He hath not beheld iniquity in Jacob, neither hath he seen perverseness in Israel: the Lord his God is with him, and the shout of a king is among them.

- He hath received commandment to bless, and it cannot be reversed: he hath not beheld iniquity in Jacob, neither seen perverseness in Israel. Israel had done wickedly since Egypt, and princes rose up to overthrow Moses, but the Lord forgave us over and over again.
- No nation could pull up before God and demand He do away with His people. In His eyes Israel was good compared to the accusers; the shout of a King is among them.

**[Numbers 23:22-23](/bible/numbers/23#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/numbers/23#v22)</sup> God brought them out of Egypt; he hath as it were the strength of an unicorn.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/numbers/23#v23)</sup> Surely there is no enchantment against Jacob, neither is there any divination against Israel: according to this time it shall be said of Jacob and of Israel, What hath God wrought!

- God brought them out of Egypt; he hath as it were the strength of an unicorn. The unicorn in biblical times is the rhinoceros, the beast with the one great horn on the front.
- Surely there is no enchantment against Jacob, neither is there any divination against Israel. For Levi and all the tribes, that voodoo stuff is nothing: once you repent, there is no hex, divination, or curse of bad luck anyone can put on you. Do not even worry about it.

**[Numbers 23:24](/bible/numbers/23#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/numbers/23#v24)</sup> Behold, the people shall rise up as a great lion, and lift up himself as a young lion: he shall not lie down until he eat of the prey, and drink the blood of the slain.

- Behold, the people shall rise up as a great lion, and he shall not lie down until he eat of the prey and drink the blood of the slain: Israel shall destroy his enemies. Deuteronomy carries that same promise, and [Genesis 49](/bible/genesis/49) touches it as well in the blessings of the tribes.
