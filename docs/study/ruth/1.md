## Introduction

This session covers [Judges 21](/bible/judges/21) together with Ruth chapters 1 through 3; these notes cover [Ruth 1](/bible/ruth/1) through 3. The book of Ruth opens in the days when the judges ruled, the same era Judges just closed with the words, there was no king in Israel, and every man did that which was right in his own eyes. That setting explains how the sons of Elimelech came to take wives of the women of Moab, which the law forbade. The teaching follows Naomi and Ruth from Moab back to Bethlehem and into the field of Boaz, and its main threads are the rest a widow finds in the house of her husband, the inheritance a man is supposed to leave behind, the law of raising up the name of the dead, the idol gods Orpah returned to, the place of the stranger who joins herself under Israel, and the near kinsman at the threshingfloor.

**[Ruth 1:1-2](/bible/ruth/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ruth/1#v1)</sup> Now it came to pass in the days when the judges ruled, that there was a famine in the land. And a certain man of Beth–lehem–judah went to sojourn in the country of Moab, he, and his wife, and his two sons.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ruth/1#v2)</sup> And the name of the man was Elimelech, and the name of his wife Naomi, and the name of his two sons Mahlon and Chilion, Ephrathites of Beth–lehem–judah. And they came into the country of Moab, and continued there.

- It came to pass in the days when the judges ruled: the scripture is putting Ruth in a time frame. Ruth takes place in the same time as the book of Judges.
- There was a famine in the land, and Elimelech of Bethlehemjudah went to sojourn in the country of Moab with his wife Naomi and his two sons Mahlon and Chilion, Ephrathites of Bethlehemjudah.

**[Ruth 1:3-5](/bible/ruth/1#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/ruth/1#v3)</sup> And Elimelech Naomi’s husband died; and she was left, and her two sons.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/ruth/1#v4)</sup> And they took them wives of the women of Moab; the name of the one was Orpah, and the name of the other Ruth: and they dwelled there about ten years.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ruth/1#v5)</sup> And Mahlon and Chilion died also both of them; and the woman was left of her two sons and her husband.

- Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died, and she was left with her two sons. The sons took them wives of the women of Moab, Orpah and Ruth, and after they dwelt there about ten years Mahlon and Chilion died also, leaving Naomi without her husband and without her sons.
- Were the sons of Israel supposed to take wives of the women of Moab while sojourning there? No; but remember what was just read at the end of Judges, that every man did that which was right in his own eyes. And this was not the first time Israel dealt with the women of Moab.

  Precepts:
  - **[Judges 21:25](/bible/judges/21#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/judges/21#v25)</sup> In those days there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes.

    There was no king in Israel and every man did that which was right in his own eyes; that is the state Israel was in when these marriages happened.
  - **[Deuteronomy 23:3](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v3)</sup> An Ammonite or Moabite shall not enter into the congregation of the Lord; even to their tenth generation shall they not enter into the congregation of the Lord for ever:

    An Ammonite or Moabite shall not enter into the congregation of the Lord for ever; Israel was not supposed to be marrying the women of Moab.
  - **[Numbers 25:1-2](/bible/numbers/25#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/numbers/25#v1)</sup> And Israel abode in Shittim, and the people began to commit whoredom with the daughters of Moab.
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/numbers/25#v2)</sup> And they called the people unto the sacrifices of their gods: and the people did eat, and bowed down to their gods.

    Israel had already committed whoredom with the daughters of Moab at Shittim, and the people did eat and bowed down to their gods.
  - **[1 Esdras 8:69](/bible/1-esdras/8#v69)**
    > <sup>[69](/bible/1-esdras/8#v69)</sup> The nation of Israel, the princes, the priests and Levites, have not put away from them the strange people of the land, nor the pollutions of the Gentiles to wit, of the Canaanites, Hittites, Pheresites, Jebusites, and the Moabites, Egyptians, and Edomites.

    Even in the time of Ezra, Israel had not put away the strange people of the land, the Moabites among them; remember that it says the strange people of the land.

**[Ruth 1:6-9](/bible/ruth/1#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/ruth/1#v6)</sup> Then she arose with her daughters in law, that she might return from the country of Moab: for she had heard in the country of Moab how that the Lord had visited his people in giving them bread.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/ruth/1#v7)</sup> Wherefore she went forth out of the place where she was, and her two daughters in law with her; and they went on the way to return unto the land of Judah.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/ruth/1#v8)</sup> And Naomi said unto her two daughters in law, Go, return each to her mother’s house: the Lord deal kindly with you, as ye have dealt with the dead, and with me.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/ruth/1#v9)</sup> The Lord grant you that ye may find rest, each of you in the house of her husband. Then she kissed them; and they lifted up their voice, and wept.

- Naomi heard in the country of Moab that the Lord had visited his people and given them bread, so she arose with her daughters in law to return unto the land of Judah, and told them, Go, return each to her mother's house; the Lord deal kindly with you, as ye have dealt with the dead and with me.
- The Lord grant you that ye may find rest, each of you in the house of her husband: that rest is an inheritance. The man is supposed to leave an inheritance; you are not supposed to leave your wife and your children desolate with a gang of bills. To leave an inheritance you first have to work, and you have to be wise with how you deal with your money.

  - **[Proverbs 13:22](/bible/proverbs/13#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/proverbs/13#v22)</sup> A good man leaveth an inheritance to his children’s children: and the wealth of the sinner is laid up for the just.

    A good man leaveth an inheritance to his children's children.

    > <sup>[8](/bible/1-timothy/5#v8)</sup> But if any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an infidel.

  - **[Ruth 4:5](/bible/ruth/4#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/ruth/4#v5)</sup> Then said Boaz, What day thou buyest the field of the hand of Naomi, thou must buy it also of Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of the dead, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance.

    Reading ahead for understanding: whoever bought the field of Naomi had to buy it also of Ruth, the wife of the dead, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance.
  - **[Judith 8:4-7](/bible/judith/8#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/judith/8#v4)</sup> So Judith was a widow in her house three years and four months.
    >
    > <sup>[5](/bible/judith/8#v5)</sup> And she made her a tent upon the top of her house, and put on sackcloth upon her loins and ware her widow’s apparel.
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/judith/8#v6)</sup> And she fasted all the days of her widowhood, save the eves of the sabbaths, and the sabbaths, and the eves of the new moons, and the new moons and the feasts and solemn days of the house of Israel.
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/judith/8#v7)</sup> She was also of a goodly countenance, and very beautiful to behold: and her husband Manasses had left her gold, and silver, and menservants and maidservants, and cattle, and lands; and she remained upon them.

    The example of a widow finding rest in the house of her husband: Manasses left Judith gold, silver, servants, cattle, and lands, and she remained upon them.

- Behind the words the wife of the dead stands the law of the husband's brother: if brethren dwell together and one dies having no child, his brother takes the widow to wife, and the firstborn she bears succeeds in the name of the dead brother, that his name be not put out of Israel. That first child is basically the dead brother's child; he gets the rights of inheritance as if the brother were still alive, so the brother's name stays in the earth and his house is built up.

  - **[Genesis 49:3](/bible/genesis/49#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/49#v3)</sup> Reuben, thou art my firstborn, my might, and the beginning of my strength, the excellency of dignity, and the excellency of power:

    The firstborn is the might and the beginning of strength, the excellency of dignity and power; you want somebody to carry on your name.
  - **[1 Chronicles 5:1](/bible/1-chronicles/5#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/1-chronicles/5#v1)</sup> Now the sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel, (for he was the firstborn; but, forasmuch as he defiled his father’s bed, his birthright was given unto the sons of Joseph the son of Israel: and the genealogy is not to be reckoned after the birthright.

    Reuben was the firstborn, but because he defiled his father's bed his birthright was given unto the sons of Joseph; the birthright shows why the firstborn and the inheritance mattered so much in Israel.

- We are not under that law anymore; we do not have that land inheritance in captivity. It is wicked to pull that scripture today to try to get with your brother's wife after he passes away; do not be looking at your brother's wife.
- It is important for us to build our legacy and pass it down; that is one of the things we lack in Israel today. Some of us use captivity as an excuse not to better ourselves or build, but that is not what the scriptures say: get the job, further your education, do more for yourself, your family, and the body. Building up your house also means spiritually building it, teaching your wife and children.

  - **[Jeremiah 29:5-6](/bible/jeremiah/29#v5)**

**[Ruth 1:10-13](/bible/ruth/1#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/ruth/1#v10)</sup> And they said unto her, Surely we will return with thee unto thy people.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/ruth/1#v11)</sup> And Naomi said, Turn again, my daughters: why will ye go with me? are there yet any more sons in my womb, that they may be your husbands?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/ruth/1#v12)</sup> Turn again, my daughters, go your way; for I am too old to have an husband. If I should say, I have hope, if I should have an husband also to night, and should also bear sons;
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/ruth/1#v13)</sup> Would ye tarry for them till they were grown? would ye stay for them from having husbands? nay, my daughters; for it grieveth me much for your sakes that the hand of the Lord is gone out against me.

- The daughters in law said, Surely we will return with thee unto thy people; but Naomi answered, Are there yet any more sons in my womb, that they may be your husbands? Even if she had a husband that night and bore sons, would they tarry until the boys were grown? They were grown women; they were not going to wait.
- Naomi was free of her responsibility toward them: her husband had died, her sons their husbands had died, and their husbands had left them inheritances in Moab. She would not put them under an oath to stay and wait for sons that would likely never come.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Timothy 5:11](/bible/1-timothy/5#v11)**
    > <sup>[11](/bible/1-timothy/5#v11)</sup> But the younger widows refuse: for when they have begun to wax wanton against Christ, they will marry;

    In Israel the younger widows are refused, for when they begin to wax wanton against Christ they will marry; a young widow still has years to bear children and that urge will most likely come upon her, which is why Naomi released them.

**[Ruth 1:16-17](/bible/ruth/1#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/ruth/1#v16)</sup> And Ruth said, Intreat me not to leave thee, or to return from following after thee: for whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God:
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/ruth/1#v17)</sup> Where thou diest, will I die, and there will I be buried: the Lord do so to me, and more also, if ought but death part thee and me.

- Ruth was determined: Intreat me not to leave thee; whither thou goest, I will go; thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God; where thou diest, will I die. She was saying, do not put me away; Orpah may have gone back, but I am going to cleave unto you.
- Does that mean she would inherit the kingdom as a Moabitess? No. She joined herself under Israel and acknowledged her place in the hierarchy under Israel, and the scriptures give examples of strangers who did the same.
