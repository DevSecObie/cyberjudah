**[Ruth 3:1-5](/bible/ruth/3#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ruth/3#v1)</sup> Then Naomi her mother in law said unto her, My daughter, shall I not seek rest for thee, that it may be well with thee?
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ruth/3#v2)</sup> And now is not Boaz of our kindred, with whose maidens thou wast? Behold, he winnoweth barley to night in the threshingfloor.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/ruth/3#v3)</sup> Wash thyself therefore, and anoint thee, and put thy raiment upon thee, and get thee down to the floor: but make not thyself known unto the man, until he shall have done eating and drinking.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/ruth/3#v4)</sup> And it shall be, when he lieth down, that thou shalt mark the place where he shall lie, and thou shalt go in, and uncover his feet, and lay thee down; and he will tell thee what thou shalt do.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ruth/3#v5)</sup> And she said unto her, All that thou sayest unto me I will do.

- Naomi said, My daughter, shall I not seek rest for thee, that it may be well with thee? This is the same rest spoken of in chapter 1, rest in the house of a husband; and Boaz of their kindred was winnowing barley that night in the threshingfloor.
- Her instructions: wash thyself, anoint thee, put thy raiment upon thee, get thee down to the floor, and make not thyself known unto the man until he has done eating and drinking; mark where he lies, uncover his feet, and lay thee down, and he will tell thee what thou shalt do. Ruth answered, All that thou sayest unto me I will do.

**[Ruth 3:6-9](/bible/ruth/3#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/ruth/3#v6)</sup> And she went down unto the floor, and did according to all that her mother in law bade her.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/ruth/3#v7)</sup> And when Boaz had eaten and drunk, and his heart was merry, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of corn: and she came softly, and uncovered his feet, and laid her down.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/ruth/3#v8)</sup> And it came to pass at midnight, that the man was afraid, and turned himself: and, behold, a woman lay at his feet.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/ruth/3#v9)</sup> And he said, Who art thou? And she answered, I am Ruth thine handmaid: spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman.

- Ruth did according to all her mother in law bade her. When Boaz had eaten and drunk and his heart was merry, he lay down at the end of the heap of corn, and she came softly, uncovered his feet, and laid her down. At midnight the man was afraid, turned himself, and behold, a woman lay at his feet.
- She answered, I am Ruth thine handmaid: spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman.
- On the skirt: Zondervan's Compact Bible Dictionary, page 137, explains that the basic garments of the men of biblical times were the inner tunic, the tunic coat, the girdle, and the cloak, with the headdress and the shoes or sandals; the word skirt in the Bible usually refers to an article of male, not female, clothing, and carries meanings such as extremity, as in [Ruth 3:9](/bible/ruth/3#v9). Asking Boaz to spread his skirt over her was asking the near kinsman to take her under his covering.

**[Ruth 3:10](/bible/ruth/3#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/ruth/3#v10)</sup> And he said, Blessed be thou of the Lord, my daughter: for thou hast shewed more kindness in the latter end than at the beginning, inasmuch as thou followedst not young men, whether poor or rich.

- Boaz blessed her: thou hast shewed more kindness in the latter end than at the beginning, inasmuch as thou followedst not young men, whether poor or rich. Ruth could have gone after other men, but even as a Moabitess she was following the customs of Israel, trying to find someone to raise up seed for her dead lord and keep his name in the earth; she was trying to do the right thing and keep the laws of Israel.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ruth 1:12-13](/bible/ruth/1#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/ruth/1#v12)</sup> Turn again, my daughters, go your way; for I am too old to have an husband. If I should say, I have hope, if I should have an husband also to night, and should also bear sons;
    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/ruth/1#v13)</sup> Would ye tarry for them till they were grown? would ye stay for them from having husbands? nay, my daughters; for it grieveth me much for your sakes that the hand of the Lord is gone out against me.

    Naomi had asked, Would ye tarry for sons till they were grown? Boaz's words point back to this: Ruth did what no young widow was expected to do, seeking the kinsman of her dead husband instead of following young men.

**[Ruth 3:11-13](/bible/ruth/3#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/ruth/3#v11)</sup> And now, my daughter, fear not; I will do to thee all that thou requirest: for all the city of my people doth know that thou art a virtuous woman.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/ruth/3#v12)</sup> And now it is true that I am thy near kinsman: howbeit there is a kinsman nearer than I.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/ruth/3#v13)</sup> Tarry this night, and it shall be in the morning, that if he will perform unto thee the part of a kinsman, well; let him do the kinsman’s part: but if he will not do the part of a kinsman to thee, then will I do the part of a kinsman to thee, as the Lord liveth: lie down until the morning.

- Fear not, my daughter; I will do to thee all that thou requirest: for all the city of my people doth know that thou art a virtuous woman.
- Boaz was honest: it is true that I am thy near kinsman, howbeit there is a kinsman nearer than I. Tarry this night, and in the morning, if he will perform unto thee the part of a kinsman, well; but if not, then will I do the part of a kinsman to thee, as the Lord liveth.

**[Ruth 3:14-18](/bible/ruth/3#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/ruth/3#v14)</sup> And she lay at his feet until the morning: and she rose up before one could know another. And he said, Let it not be known that a woman came into the floor.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/ruth/3#v15)</sup> Also he said, Bring the vail that thou hast upon thee, and hold it. And when she held it, he measured six measures of barley, and laid it on her: and she went into the city.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/ruth/3#v16)</sup> And when she came to her mother in law, she said, Who art thou, my daughter? And she told her all that the man had done to her.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/ruth/3#v17)</sup> And she said, These six measures of barley gave he me; for he said to me, Go not empty unto thy mother in law.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/ruth/3#v18)</sup> Then said she, Sit still, my daughter, until thou know how the matter will fall: for the man will not be in rest, until he have finished the thing this day.

- She lay at his feet until the morning and rose up before one could know another; that is the same precept of knowing read in [Judges 21](/bible/judges/21), meaning in this case to lie down and have sex with each other. That is also why Boaz said, Let it not be known that a woman came into the floor.
- Boaz measured six measures of barley into her vail and she went into the city; she told Naomi, These six measures of barley gave he me; for he said to me, Go not empty unto thy mother in law.
- Naomi answered, Sit still, my daughter, until thou know how the matter will fall: for the man will not be in rest until he have finished the thing this day.

  Precepts:
  - **[Judges 21:12](/bible/judges/21#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/judges/21#v12)</sup> And they found among the inhabitants of Jabesh–gilead four hundred young virgins, that had known no man by lying with any male: and they brought them unto the camp to Shiloh, which is in the land of Canaan.

    The virgins of Jabeshgilead had not known man by lying with any male; to know somebody in scripture means to lie with them.
