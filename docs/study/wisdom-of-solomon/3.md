## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Wisdom of Solomon 3 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-8, 10-12, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Wisdom of Solomon 3:1](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v1)</sup> But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and there shall no torment touch them.

- “But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and there shall no torment touch them.” When it says no torment touch them, that’s talking about the lake of fire, that eternal condemnation.
- “But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God”: the righteous never dies. “And there shall no torment touch them”: that is the same torment we read earlier, the lake of fire, the second death. That is not going to touch us.
- “But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and there shall no torment touch them.” The torment that will not touch us is that lake of fire, that condemnation. No torment shall touch us.

  Precepts:
  - **[Revelation 14:9-11](/bible/revelation/14#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/revelation/14#v9)</sup> And the third angel followed them, saying with a loud voice, If any man worship the beast and his image, and receive his mark in his forehead, or in his hand,
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/revelation/14#v10)</sup> The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation; and he shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels, and in the presence of the Lamb:
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/revelation/14#v11)</sup> And the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever and ever: and they have no rest day nor night, who worship the beast and his image, and whosoever receiveth the mark of his name.

    Look at the torment, so you understand the difference between you being beheaded, crucified, COVID-19, diabetes, affliction, and the torment. “If any man worship the beast”: any man worship the white man, “and his image, and receive his mark in his forehead”: you receive his mark, his policies, in your mind, “or in his hand”: you support it. “The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God”: you’re going to get judged. Let’s see what kind of judgment.
  - **[Matthew 10:28](/bible/matthew/10#v28)**
    > <sup>[28](/bible/matthew/10#v28)</sup> And fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell.

    Don’t fear them that kill the body, because that’s the fear we all fear; but the Lord says no, that ain’t the fear. “And fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul”: Revelation 14 was talking about your soul. When I mentioned John the Baptist and Peter, I was talking about their body, not their soul. “But rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell.” Hell is the lake of fire; that’s what you don’t want.
  - **[Revelation 20:6](/bible/revelation/20#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/revelation/20#v6)</sup> Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection: on such the second death hath no power, but they shall be priests of God and of Christ, and shall reign with him a thousand years.

    “Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection”: meaning when Christ comes back. “On such the second death hath no power.” The second death is that final judgment, and it will have no power on us. That is the torment that shall not touch the souls of the righteous.

**[Wisdom of Solomon 3:2-3](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v2)</sup> In the sight of the unwise they seemed to die: and their departure is taken for misery,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v3)</sup> And their going from us to be utter destruction: but they are in peace.

- “In the sight of the unwise they seemed to die”: they said let’s kill them, torture them; the wicked put us to death. “But they are in peace.”
- When you go through bad times, wicked family members say if you weren’t in that cult this wouldn’t happen to you; come back to the Catholic Church, come back to the Baptist church. Anything that goes wrong, which is just a trial, they say it is because you are reading that Bible, because you are in that group.
- “In the sight of the unwise they seemed to die”. This year alone over 20 brothers and sisters died in IUIC, and the unwise see that they die and say, God judged them, because he’s wicked, she’s wicked; that’s why God did that to them. “And their departure is taken for misery”: your departure, your death, is taken as misery.
- “In the sight of the unwise they seemed to die”: the unwise is the ADL. We seem to die because our body dies; our bodies perish. “And their departure is taken for misery”: oh, you had a miserable life, all you did was read that Bible and teach that Bible, you had a miserable life and now you’re dead. That’s what it means.

**[Wisdom of Solomon 3:4](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v4)</sup> For though they be punished in the sight of men, yet is their hope full of immortality.

- “For though they be punished in the sight of men”: it looked like they were punished in the sight of man, “yet is their hope full of immortality.”
- “For though they be punished in the sight of men”: through incarceration, through torment, whether we get shot, stabbed, beheaded, whatever it is. One of the apostles was beat down by a group of people with clubs. That’s a terrible death, a long death. That ain’t like getting beheaded, where you die quick; getting beat with sticks takes some time, that’s pain.
- “Yet is their hope full of immortality”: they are in godhood, they are immortal.
- “For though they be punished in the sight of men”, as we just read in Wisdom of Solomon 2, “yet is their hope full of immortality.” That is what no torment shall touch us means.

**[Wisdom of Solomon 3:5](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v5)</sup> And having been a little chastised, they shall be greatly rewarded: for God proved them, and found them worthy for himself.

- “And having been a little chastised”. You mean John the Baptist being beheaded was a little chastised? Yes. You mean Peter and his wife being crucified upside down was a little chastised? Yes. Why was it little? Because of what verse one said: the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and torment shall not touch them. Being beheaded, crucified, COVID-19, diabetes, affliction, whatever it is, touches the body, not the soul. “They shall be greatly rewarded: for God proved them, and found them worthy for himself.”
- “And having been a little chastised”: the clubbing you got, the shooting, the beheading, they burned you to death, they drowned you, they tied you up in the back of a car and dragged your body down the street. God says that’s a little chastised. No matter what form of death we go through, whether it’s sickness and pain or not, it is a little chastisement. That is what we are going to go through in these last days: no matter what the torment, torture or incarceration period is, God calls it a little chastised.
- “They shall be greatly rewarded”: we’re going to get eternal life, salvation, godhood. There’s no comparison between the godhood and immortality we shall get and the chastisement we suffer. That’s what King Solomon is telling us.
- “For God proved them, and found them worthy for himself”: that’s the point right there. The Lord said, I’m going to test you, I’m going to try you, I’m going to prove you. God proved us, he tested us, and the ones he found worthy are those that confessed him before men that were putting them to death.

  - **[2 Maccabees 7:14](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v14)</sup> So when he was ready to die he said thus, It is good, being put to death by men, to look for hope from God to be raised up again by him: as for thee, thou shalt have no resurrection to life.

    See the mindset of our forefathers, how they thought. “So when he was ready to die he said thus, It is good, being put to death by men, to look for hope from God to be raised up again by him.” His understanding and his faith said it is good to lose my life being put to death by men, to look for hope from God to be raised up again, knowing that Christ holds the keys of hell and death.

**[Wisdom of Solomon 3:6](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v6)</sup> As gold in the furnace hath he tried them, and received them as a burnt offering.

- “As gold in the furnace hath he tried them, and received them as a burnt offering.” This brings it to the next level of understanding. Received as a burnt offering: that’s death. The death of the saint is an offering to the Most High God.
- We see death different than what the Bible teaches. That Sam Cooke song, ‘I don’t know what’s up there beyond the sky’: everybody’s scared. Our forefathers said no: the time of my departure is at hand, it’s time for me to go. They didn’t fear, oh, I’m going to be killed. Read of the seven sons: they kept saying, cut my hands off, and it’s like, what’s wrong with these dudes? But at the end they said, ‘we shall have a resurrection, but you shall have no resurrection to life’. Our forefathers knew something we have forgotten.
- In this time of persecution, this time of Jacob’s trouble, the Most High is going to take us to that understanding. He said, you’ve been looking at death wrong; there’s a lot of things you don’t see too clear, but I’m going to show you now, because you’re crying out to me.
- “As gold in the furnace hath he tried them, and received them as a burnt offering.” The death of the saints is like a burnt offering, and God loves that smell. That’s some heavy stuff. Christ fulfilled the law of sacrifice, and our death is doing the same thing: that’s the sacrifice I’m looking for, he said.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Timothy 4:6](/bible/2-timothy/4#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/2-timothy/4#v6)</sup> For I am now ready to be offered, and the time of my departure is at hand.

    “For I am now ready to be offered, and the time of my departure is at hand.” This is heavy. Paul used the same words: I’m ready to be offered, and the time of my departure, the time of my death, is at hand. So that death is an offering: received as a burnt offering, the death of the saint is an offering to the Most High God.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 2:5](/bible/sirach/2#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/sirach/2#v5)</sup> For gold is tried in the fire, and acceptable men in the furnace of adversity.

    “For gold is tried in the fire, and acceptable men in the furnace of adversity.” It uses the same words as Wisdom of Solomon 3:6. When we break it down, we always leave it at you’re being tempted by lust or money, sex, drugs, alcohol, and we leave it there. It’s deeper than that: Wisdom of Solomon brings it to the next level of understanding.
  - **[Psalms 116:15](/bible/psalms/116#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/psalms/116#v15)</sup> Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints.

    “Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints.” That’s the precious thing. The Lord said, here’s another offering. John got beheaded: that’s an offering to me. Peter and his wife got crucified upside down: I love that smell. Christ was the greatest; his offering took it all.

**[Wisdom of Solomon 3:7](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v7)</sup> And in the time of their visitation they shall shine, and run to and fro like sparks among the stubble.

- “And in the time of their visitation they shall shine”: we shall shine. That means we are going to get that eternal life, that godhood, that new body. “And run to and fro like sparks among the stubble”: that’s power, supernatural power. The stubble is the nations.
- “And in the time of their visitation”, when the Lord returns, “they shall shine”: we shall shine, because we are going to have a new body, corruption putting on incorruption, mortal putting on immortality. “And run to and fro like sparks among the stubble”: we are going to have spiritual power.

  Precepts:
  - **[Daniel 12:3](/bible/daniel/12#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/daniel/12#v3)</sup> And they that be wise shall shine as the brightness of the firmament; and they that turn many to righteousness as the stars for ever and ever.

    “They shall shine” in Wisdom of Solomon is the same thing Daniel 12:3 said. “And they that be wise”: those that keep the commandments, “shall shine as the brightness of the firmament”. That’s godhood, another level: the immortality, the celestial body we seek for. “And they that turn many to righteousness as the stars for ever and ever.”

**[Wisdom of Solomon 3:8](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v8)</sup> They shall judge the nations, and have dominion over the people, and their Lord shall reign for ever.

- “They shall judge the nations”: we are going to judge the nations, rule the nations, dominate the nations, “and have dominion over the people.” “And their Lord shall reign for ever”: Christ shall reign forever.
- “They shall judge the nations, and have dominion over the people”: we are going to rule the nations, brothers and sisters, and dominate all people on this earth. “And their Lord shall reign for ever”: Christ, the King of kings and Lord of lords, shall reign forever.

**[Wisdom of Solomon 3:10](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v10)</sup> But the ungodly shall be punished according to their own imaginations, which have neglected the righteous, and forsaken the Lord.

- Many sisters, and brothers too, kick against the pricks in their marriage: God set forth the ordained order of the man and the woman, the woman kicks against it, and when the issues come she blames the man, without putting in the proper effort in her divine order.
- “But the ungodly shall be punished according to their own imaginations”: their own mindset of how they deem marriage, according to the world and not according to God. “Which have neglected the righteous”: this man is putting forth his best effort to be a righteous man, fixing himself and getting himself together, and she neglects him. “And forsaken the Lord”: by doing that she forsakes the Lord, because in order for a woman to fear the Lord she must honor her husband, and she says, I’m not doing none of that.

**[Wisdom of Solomon 3:11](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v11)</sup> For whoso despiseth wisdom and nurture, he is miserable, and their hope is vain, their labours unfruitful, and their works unprofitable:

- “For whoso despiseth wisdom and nurture”: she despises God’s wisdom and the nurture that comes from that wisdom, so “he is miserable.” Now your life is miserable, and you complain about your marriage. A sister commented that marriage seems miserable in the Israelite community, like the women are slaves. These women are not slaves; you just don’t want to come under the wisdom and nurture of God, you want to do your own thing according to the world.
- “And their hope is vain, their labours unfruitful, and their works unprofitable”: the things you are trying to bring forth, your connection in marriage, your jobs, your business with your husband, none of it works.

**[Wisdom of Solomon 3:12](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/3#v12)</sup> Their wives are foolish, and their children wicked:

- “Their wives are foolish, and their children wicked”: you bring forth wicked children.
- This cannot be left on the back burner. All of the great works that men and women seek to do will falter at some level, and will not have the potency they should have in going for the kingdom, if this problem with marriage is not fixed. Some brothers are not married at all and do well to a degree, without the distraction, as Paul said, but not everybody has that strength; most of the time brothers need that comfort, that solace.
