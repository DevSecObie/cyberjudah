## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Judith 16 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 9, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Judith 16:9](/bible/judith/16#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/judith/16#v9)</sup> Her sandals ravished his eyes, her beauty took his mind prisoner, and the fauchion passed through his neck.

- Beauty is deceitful, and brothers have to be on their game. Judith was on a righteous cause for her people, and her objective was to get Holofernes’ head cut off, which is a beautiful thing. But this shows the power of beauty and how it can deceive.
- “Her sandals ravished his eyes”: we have never seen Judith, but reading it, men imagine how the sandals and the feet looked. It tore him up. “Her beauty took his mind prisoner”: her beauty captivated his brain. “And the fauchion passed through his neck”: a fauchion is a broad, single-edged sword, wider near the point, like a heavy cleaver or machete. Like a sickle, it took his head. She was able to cut his head off because he was so blinded and captivated by her beauty.
- Many men have gone out of their wits for women, because that is the power women have over you when you are weak. Women are beautiful and men love nothing better, but you can’t let that deceive you. Judith ravished his eyes and never laid with him; she did just enough to win victory for her people.
- She had been mourning the death of her husband, and she took off her mourning garments and put on her beauty for that occasion, to ravish his mind and eyes and get her chance to cut off his head. She knew the power of beauty in righteousness. Sisters, you can do that for your husband, and you will be surprised at the motivation that comes out of a brother who feels he has somebody in his corner. Some brothers work double shifts because they don’t want to go home.
