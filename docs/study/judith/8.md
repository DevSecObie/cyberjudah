## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Judith 8 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 2-3, 6, 12, 15, 24-26, 32-35, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Judith 8:2-3](/bible/judith/8#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/judith/8#v2)</sup> And Manasses was her husband, of her tribe and kindred, who died in the barley harvest.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/judith/8#v3)</sup> For as he stood overseeing them that bound sheaves in the field, the heat came upon his head, and he fell on his bed, and died in the city of Bethulia: and they buried him with his fathers in the field between Dothaim and Balamo.

- Judith's husband Manasses was of her own tribe and kindred and died in the barley harvest while overseeing the work in the fields. He had responsibility, workers and resources. The household he left and Judith's understanding show a woman who had been well established and taught. Judith is identified with the tribe of Simeon through her family history.

**[Judith 8:6](/bible/judith/8#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/judith/8#v6)</sup> And she fasted all the days of her widowhood, save the eves of the sabbaths, and the sabbaths, and the eves of the new moons, and the new moons, and the feasts and solemn days of the house of Israel.

- She fasted throughout her widowhood except for the eves of the Sabbaths, the Sabbaths, the eves of the new moons, the new moons and the feast and solemn days of Israel. This shows discipline joined to honouring the holy days. Her continued honour for her husband is presented as part of the character that made her trustworthy.

**[Judith 8:12](/bible/judith/8#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/judith/8#v12)</sup> And now who are ye that have tempted God this day, and stand instead of God among the children of men?

- Judith challenged the decision that effectively put a deadline on God. “who are ye that have tempted God this day,” She feared tempting him. Such fortitude came through being raised in the fear of God and understanding his commandments. It contradicts the idea that God's people could dispense with keeping them.

**[Judith 8:15](/bible/judith/8#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/judith/8#v15)</sup> For if he will not help us within these five days, he hath power to defend us when he will, even every day, or to destroy us before our enemies.

- “he hath power to defend us when he will, even every day, or to destroy us before our enemies.” The elders had spoken of waiting five days. God's power was not confined to their timetable. The faithful response was to wait upon him rather than make surrender the inevitable conclusion if he did not act on command.

**[Judith 8:24-26](/bible/judith/8#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/judith/8#v24)</sup> Now therefore, O brethren, let us shew an example to our brethren, because their hearts depend upon us, and the sanctuary, and the house, and the altar, rest upon us.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/judith/8#v25)</sup> Moreover let us give thanks to the Lord our God, which trieth us, even as he did our fathers.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/judith/8#v26)</sup> Remember what things he did to Abraham, and how he tried Isaac, and what happened to Jacob in Mesopotamia of Syria, when he kept the sheep of Laban his mother’s brother.

- The brethren depended upon their leaders' example, and the sanctuary, house and altar rested upon them. Judith urged thanksgiving in the trial and remembrance of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob's experience while keeping Laban's sheep in Mesopotamia. She drew strength from the same history Mattathias would later use with his sons.
- The nation had been taught to fear God and remember how the fathers overcame. Even its enemies knew that history. The degraded descriptions now placed upon our people do not define what Israel is. The history supplies a greater identity and a reason to remain faithful under pressure.
- “Let us shew an example to our brethren.” That is the question to ask yourself: you want to be a leader, but is your house in order? What are your works, to be an example to others? This truth is about being an example. Christ is the ultimate example; he is the most famous man on the face of the earth because of his works, the one man who did nothing wrong and was put to death for it.
- “Because their hearts depend upon us”: the nation depends on its leaders. That is why America went straight after the leaders, because that is where your hope and faith come from, and why they took out Martin and Malcolm when they were getting too big. This movement is different because all of us have the same guideline and the same instructions; everybody runs the same playbook over and over, and it keeps working. The commandments are the secret sauce.

**[Judith 8:32-34](/bible/judith/8#v32)**

> <sup>[32](/bible/judith/8#v32)</sup> Then said Judith unto them, Hear me, and I will do a thing, which shall go throughout all generations to the children of our nation.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/judith/8#v33)</sup> Ye shall stand this night in the gate, and I will go forth with my waiting-woman: and within the days that ye have promised to deliver the city to our enemies the Lord will visit Israel by mine hand.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/judith/8#v34)</sup> But enquire not ye of mine act: for I will not declare it unto you, till the things be finished that I do.

- Judith proposed an act that would be remembered through the generations. She would go out with her waitingwoman, her handmaid, while the men stood at the gate, and trusted that God would visit Israel through her hand. She withheld the details until the act was finished.
- Her plan used her beauty, discipline and understanding of people to help the men and deliver the nation. As with Ehud, gaining access depended on more than physical force. If she had bluntly announced that she would cut that nigga's head off, the men seeking to protect her would not have wanted her to go. Instead she asked them to trust her, supported by her character and reputation.
- She spoke respectfully to the governors, not as a loud ass woman demanding her own way. Her femininity and restraint were part of the historical example. This is not a command for sisters to carry out such an act today.

**[Judith 8:35](/bible/judith/8#v35)**

> <sup>[35](/bible/judith/8#v35)</sup> Then said Ozias and the princes unto her, Go in peace, and the Lord God be before thee, to take vengeance on our enemies.

- “Go in peace, and the Lord God be before thee, to take vengeance on our enemies.” The princes allowed Judith and her maid to leave the defended city even though the hostile army surrounded it. They trusted her and desired deliverance and vengeance against the oppressor. Praying for the overthrow of those oppressing Israel is therefore part of the nation's recorded history. The later songs about Judith remember that deliverance.
