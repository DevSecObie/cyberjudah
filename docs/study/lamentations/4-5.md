Read the chapter → [Lamentations 4](/bible/lamentations/4)

This session covers Lamentations chapters 4 and 5, the closing of the book of mourning over the fall of Jerusalem and the Babylonian captivity. Chapter 4 walks through the ruin of the city and plainly identifies the complexion of the people the Most High chose, and chapter 5 is the closing prayer that remembers all that is come upon us and pleads for the Most High to turn us back to Him. Through it all our forefathers confess their sin and yet look for the visitation of the enemy and the mercy of the Most High.

**[Lamentations 4:1-6](/bible/lamentations/4#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/lamentations/4#v1)</sup> How is the gold become dim! how is the most fine gold changed! the stones of the sanctuary are poured out in the top of every street.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/lamentations/4#v2)</sup> The precious sons of Zion, comparable to fine gold, how are they esteemed as earthen pitchers, the work of the hands of the potter!
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/lamentations/4#v3)</sup> Even the sea monsters draw out the breast, they give suck to their young ones: the daughter of my people is become cruel, like the ostriches in the wilderness.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/lamentations/4#v4)</sup> The tongue of the sucking child cleaveth to the roof of his mouth for thirst: the young children ask bread, and no man breaketh it unto them.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/lamentations/4#v5)</sup> They that did feed delicately are desolate in the streets: they that were brought up in scarlet embrace dunghills.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/lamentations/4#v6)</sup> For the punishment of the iniquity of the daughter of my people is greater than the punishment of the sin of Sodom, that was overthrown as in a moment, and no hands stayed on her.

- The gold has become dim and the most fine gold changed; the stones of the sanctuary are poured out at the top of every street, and the precious sons of Zion, once comparable to fine gold, are now esteemed as earthen pitchers, the work of the potter's hands.
- Even the sea monsters draw out the breast and give suck to their young, but the daughter of the people is become cruel like the ostrich in the wilderness; the tongue of the suckling cleaves to the roof of his mouth for thirst, and the young children ask bread and no man breaks it to them.
- They that fed delicately are desolate in the streets, and they that were brought up in scarlet embrace dunghills, for the punishment of the iniquity of the people is greater than the punishment of Sodom, which was overthrown in a moment.
- The famine of the siege was so severe that a nation once raised in the finest things was reduced to nothing, and their judgment for their sin exceeded even the sin of Sodom.

**[Lamentations 4:7-11](/bible/lamentations/4#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/lamentations/4#v7)</sup> Her Nazarites were purer than snow, they were whiter than milk, they were more ruddy in body than rubies, their polishing was of sapphire:
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/lamentations/4#v8)</sup> Their visage is blacker than a coal; they are not known in the streets: their skin cleaveth to their bones; it is withered, it is become like a stick.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/lamentations/4#v9)</sup> They that be slain with the sword are better than they that be slain with hunger: for these pine away, stricken through for want of the fruits of the field.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/lamentations/4#v10)</sup> The hands of the pitiful women have sodden their own children: they were their meat in the destruction of the daughter of my people.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/lamentations/4#v11)</sup> The Lord hath accomplished his fury; he hath poured out his fierce anger, and hath kindled a fire in Zion, and it hath devoured the foundations thereof.

- Her Nazarites were purer than snow, whiter than milk, more ruddy in body than rubies, their polishing of sapphire, but their visage is now blacker than a coal, and they are not known in the streets.
- The verse says their visage is blacker than a coal, which shows plainly that the Israelites are a black people; the coal is the natural color of the nation and the burning of the famine only made it darker.
- They that are slain with the sword are better than they that are slain with hunger, for these pine away, stricken through for want of the fruits of the field.
- The hands of the pitiful women sodden their own children for meat in the destruction of the people, and the Most High accomplished His fury and poured out His fierce anger, kindling a fire in Zion that devoured its foundations.

**[Lamentations 4:12-16](/bible/lamentations/4#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/lamentations/4#v12)</sup> The kings of the earth, and all the inhabitants of the world, would not have believed that the adversary and the enemy should have entered into the gates of Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/lamentations/4#v13)</sup> For the sins of her prophets, and the iniquities of her priests, that have shed the blood of the just in the midst of her,
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/lamentations/4#v14)</sup> They have wandered as blind men in the streets, they have polluted themselves with blood, so that men could not touch their garments.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/lamentations/4#v15)</sup> They cried unto them, Depart ye; it is unclean; depart, depart, touch not: when they fled away and wandered, they said among the heathen, They shall no more sojourn there.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/lamentations/4#v16)</sup> The anger of the Lord hath divided them; he will no more regard them: they respected not the persons of the priests, they favoured not the elders.

- The kings of the earth and all the inhabitants of the world would not have believed that the adversary and enemy could enter the gates of Jerusalem, but it came to pass for the sins of her prophets and the iniquities of her priests that shed the blood of the just in her midst.
- They wandered as blind men in the streets, polluted with blood so that men could not touch their garments, and the people cried unto them, Depart, it is unclean, depart.
- The anger of the Most High divided them and He would no more regard them; they respected not the persons of the priests and favored not the elders, for the leadership that was supposed to guide the people had defiled itself.

**[Lamentations 4:17-20](/bible/lamentations/4#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/lamentations/4#v17)</sup> As for us, our eyes as yet failed for our vain help: in our watching we have watched for a nation that could not save us.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/lamentations/4#v18)</sup> They hunt our steps, that we cannot go in our streets: our end is near, our days are fulfilled; for our end is come.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/lamentations/4#v19)</sup> Our persecutors are swifter than the eagles of the heaven: they pursued us upon the mountains, they laid wait for us in the wilderness.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/lamentations/4#v20)</sup> The breath of our nostrils, the anointed of the Lord, was taken in their pits, of whom we said, Under his shadow we shall live among the heathen.

- Their eyes failed watching for a vain help; they watched for a nation that could not save them, looking to other countries to deliver them instead of to the Most High.
- Their persecutors were swifter than the eagles of heaven, pursuing them upon the mountains and laying wait for them in the wilderness, so that they could not go in their streets and their end was come.
- The breath of their nostrils, the anointed of the Most High, was taken in their pits, the very one under whose shadow they thought to live among the heathen.

**[Lamentations 4:21-22](/bible/lamentations/4#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/lamentations/4#v21)</sup> Rejoice and be glad, O daughter of Edom, that dwellest in the land of Uz; the cup also shall pass through unto thee: thou shalt be drunken, and shalt make thyself naked.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/lamentations/4#v22)</sup> The punishment of thine iniquity is accomplished, O daughter of Zion; he will no more carry thee away into captivity: he will visit thine iniquity, O daughter of Edom; he will discover thy sins.

- Edom is told to rejoice and be glad in the land of Uz, but the cup shall pass through unto her also; she shall be drunken and make herself naked, for her turn of judgment is coming.
- The punishment of the iniquity of Zion is accomplished and the Most High will no more carry her away into captivity, but He will visit the iniquity of Edom and discover her sins.

# Lamentations 5: Turn Thou Us Unto Thee

Read the chapter → [Lamentations 5](/bible/lamentations/5)

**[Lamentations 5:1-10](/bible/lamentations/5#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/lamentations/5#v1)</sup> Remember, O Lord, what is come upon us: consider, and behold our reproach.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/lamentations/5#v2)</sup> Our inheritance is turned to strangers, our houses to aliens.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/lamentations/5#v3)</sup> We are orphans and fatherless, our mothers are as widows.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/lamentations/5#v4)</sup> We have drunken our water for money; our wood is sold unto us.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/lamentations/5#v5)</sup> Our necks are under persecution: we labour, and have no rest.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/lamentations/5#v6)</sup> We have given the hand to the Egyptians, and to the Assyrians, to be satisfied with bread.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/lamentations/5#v7)</sup> Our fathers have sinned, and are not; and we have borne their iniquities.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/lamentations/5#v8)</sup> Servants have ruled over us: there is none that doth deliver us out of their hand.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/lamentations/5#v9)</sup> We gat our bread with the peril of our lives because of the sword of the wilderness.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/lamentations/5#v10)</sup> Our skin was black like an oven because of the terrible famine.

- The prayer asks the Most High to remember what is come upon them and behold their reproach: their inheritance turned to strangers and their houses to aliens, orphans and fatherless, their mothers as widows.
- They drink their own water for money and buy their own wood; their necks are under persecution, they labor and have no rest, and they gave the hand to the Egyptians and the Assyrians just to be satisfied with bread.
- The fathers sinned and are gone, and the children bear their iniquities; servants rule over them and there is none to deliver them out of that hand, and they get their bread with the peril of their lives because of the sword of the wilderness.
- Their skin was black like an oven because of the terrible famine, the heat of the hunger burning the natural color of the nation.

**[Lamentations 5:11-18](/bible/lamentations/5#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/lamentations/5#v11)</sup> They ravished the women in Zion, and the maids in the cities of Judah.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/lamentations/5#v12)</sup> Princes are hanged up by their hand: the faces of elders were not honoured.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/lamentations/5#v13)</sup> They took the young men to grind, and the children fell under the wood.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/lamentations/5#v14)</sup> The elders have ceased from the gate, the young men from their musick.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/lamentations/5#v15)</sup> The joy of our heart is ceased; our dance is turned into mourning.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/lamentations/5#v16)</sup> The crown is fallen from our head: woe unto us, that we have sinned!
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/lamentations/5#v17)</sup> For this our heart is faint; for these things our eyes are dim.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/lamentations/5#v18)</sup> Because of the mountain of Zion, which is desolate, the foxes walk upon it.

- The women in Zion are ravished and the maids in the cities of Judah; princes are hung up by their hands and the faces of elders are not honored.
- The young men are taken to grind and the children fall under the wood; the elders have ceased from the gate and the young men from their music, for the whole order of the nation is broken.
- The joy of their heart is ceased and their dance is turned into mourning; the crown is fallen from their head, and they cry woe unto us that we have sinned.
- Their heart is faint and their eyes dim because of the mountain of Zion which is desolate, where the foxes now walk.

**[Lamentations 5:19-22](/bible/lamentations/5#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/lamentations/5#v19)</sup> Thou, O Lord, remainest for ever; thy throne from generation to generation.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/lamentations/5#v20)</sup> Wherefore dost thou forget us for ever, and forsake us so long time?
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/lamentations/5#v21)</sup> Turn thou us unto thee, O Lord, and we shall be turned; renew our days as of old.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/lamentations/5#v22)</sup> But thou hast utterly rejected us; thou art very wroth against us.

- Yet the Most High remains forever and His throne from generation to generation, so the prayer turns from the desolation to the One who never changes.
- They ask why He would forget them forever and forsake them so long, pleading, Turn thou us unto thee, O Lord, and we shall be turned; renew our days as of old.
- The book closes on a hard note, confessing that He has utterly rejected them and is very wroth against them, an honest cry that leaves the nation looking to the Most High to turn them back.
