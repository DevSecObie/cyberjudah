Read the chapter → [2 Kings 15](/bible/2-kings/15)

This session covers [2 Kings 15](/bible/2-kings/15) through 18. It follows the fast turnover of kings in Israel, marked by conspiracy and murder, while Judah's kings walk in mixed obedience. It moves through the rise of Assyria against the land, the idolatry of Ahaz, the carrying away of Israel into captivity for breaking the covenant, and the reforms of Hezekiah as Assyria begins to posture against Judah. The thread throughout is that the Most High keeps His word: He blesses those who keep His commandments and delivers into the hand of spoilers those who forsake them.

**[2 Kings 15:1-7](/bible/2-kings/15#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/15#v1)</sup> In the twenty and seventh year of Jeroboam king of Israel began Azariah son of Amaziah king of Judah to reign.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-kings/15#v2)</sup> Sixteen years old was he when he began to reign, and he reigned two and fifty years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Jecholiah of Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/15#v3)</sup> And he did that which was right in the sight of the Lord, according to all that his father Amaziah had done;
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/15#v4)</sup> Save that the high places were not removed: the people sacrificed and burnt incense still on the high places.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/15#v5)</sup> And the Lord smote the king, so that he was a leper unto the day of his death, and dwelt in a several house. And Jotham the king’s son was over the house, judging the people of the land.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/15#v6)</sup> And the rest of the acts of Azariah, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/15#v7)</sup> So Azariah slept with his fathers; and they buried him with his fathers in the city of David: and Jotham his son reigned in his stead.

- Azariah, also called Uzziah, begins to reign over Judah and does that which is right in the sight of the Most High as his father Amaziah had done.
- The high places were not removed, so the people still sacrificed and burned incense there, an incomplete obedience.
- The Most High smote him so that he was a leper until his death, and he dwelt in a separate house while Jotham his son judged the people.
- Azariah's fuller history is written elsewhere, and the teacher directs the reader to [2 Chronicles 26](/bible/2-chronicles/26) for a more detailed account of his life and acts.
  - **[2 Chronicles 26:1](/bible/2-chronicles/26#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/2-chronicles/26#v1)</sup> Then all the people of Judah took Uzziah, who was sixteen years old, and made him king in the room of his father Amaziah.

    Azariah is named Uzziah, the same king given in greater detail in Chronicles.

# 2 Kings 16: Ahaz and the Altar of Damascus

**[2 Kings 16:1-4](/bible/2-kings/16#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/16#v1)</sup> In the seventeenth year of Pekah the son of Remaliah Ahaz the son of Jotham king of Judah began to reign.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-kings/16#v2)</sup> Twenty years old was Ahaz when he began to reign, and reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem, and did not that which was right in the sight of the Lord his God, like David his father.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/16#v3)</sup> But he walked in the way of the kings of Israel, yea, and made his son to pass through the fire, according to the abominations of the heathen, whom the Lord cast out from before the children of Israel.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/16#v4)</sup> And he sacrificed and burnt incense in the high places, and on the hills, and under every green tree.

- Ahaz son of Jotham begins to reign over Judah and does not that which is right, but walks in the way of the kings of Israel.
- He makes his son to pass through the fire after the abominations of the heathen whom the Most High cast out before Israel.
- He sacrifices and burns incense in the high places, on the hills, and under every green tree, the very idolatry the law forbade.
  - **[Deuteronomy 12:2-4](/bible/deuteronomy/12#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/12#v2)</sup> Ye shall utterly destroy all the places, wherein the nations which ye shall possess served their gods, upon the high mountains, and upon the hills, and under every green tree:
    >
    > <sup>[3](/bible/deuteronomy/12#v3)</sup> And ye shall overthrow their altars, and break their pillars, and burn their groves with fire; and ye shall hew down the graven images of their gods, and destroy the names of them out of that place.
    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/deuteronomy/12#v4)</sup> Ye shall not do so unto the Lord your God.

    The nations' places of worship on the mountains, hills, and under every green tree were to be utterly destroyed, not imitated.

**[2 Kings 16:5-9](/bible/2-kings/16#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/16#v5)</sup> Then Rezin king of Syria and Pekah son of Remaliah king of Israel came up to Jerusalem to war: and they besieged Ahaz, but could not overcome him.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/16#v6)</sup> At that time Rezin king of Syria recovered Elath to Syria, and drave the Jews from Elath: and the Syrians came to Elath, and dwelt there unto this day.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/16#v7)</sup> So Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath–pileser king of Assyria, saying, I am thy servant and thy son: come up, and save me out of the hand of the king of Syria, and out of the hand of the king of Israel, which rise up against me.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/16#v8)</sup> And Ahaz took the silver and gold that was found in the house of the Lord, and in the treasures of the king’s house, and sent it for a present to the king of Assyria.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/16#v9)</sup> And the king of Assyria hearkened unto him: for the king of Assyria went up against Damascus, and took it, and carried the people of it captive to Kir, and slew Rezin.

- Rezin king of Syria and Pekah king of Israel come up to war against Jerusalem and besiege Ahaz but cannot overcome him.
- Syria recovers Elath and drives the Jews out, and Ahaz is left weakened.
- Rather than trust the Most High, Ahaz sends to Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria saying, I am thy servant and thy son, come up and save me.
- This is much like the nation today looking to Esau as though he were a savior instead of turning to the Most High.
- Ahaz strips the silver and gold from the house of the Most High and the king's treasures and sends it as a present to Assyria, who then takes Damascus and slays Rezin.

**[2 Kings 16:10-16](/bible/2-kings/16#v10)**

>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/16#v11)</sup> And Urijah the priest built an altar according to all that king Ahaz had sent from Damascus: so Urijah the priest made it against king Ahaz came from Damascus.

> <sup>[12](/bible/2-kings/16#v12)</sup> And when the king was come from Damascus, the king saw the altar: and the king approached to the altar, and offered thereon.
>

>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-kings/16#v14)</sup> And he brought also the brasen altar, which was before the Lord, from the forefront of the house, from between the altar and the house of the Lord, and put it on the north side of the altar.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-kings/16#v15)</sup> And king Ahaz commanded Urijah the priest, saying, Upon the great altar burn the morning burnt offering, and the evening meat offering, and the king’s burnt sacrifice, and his meat offering, with the burnt offering of all the people of the land, and their meat offering, and their drink offerings; and sprinkle upon it all the blood of the burnt offering, and all the blood of the sacrifice: and the brasen altar shall be for me to enquire by.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-kings/16#v16)</sup> Thus did Urijah the priest, according to all that king Ahaz commanded.

- Ahaz goes to Damascus to meet the king of Assyria and sees an altar there, then sends its pattern and workmanship to Urijah the priest.
- Urijah builds the altar according to the pattern before Ahaz returns, and the king offers his burnt offering, meat offering, drink offering, and peace offering upon it.
- The true altar was made after the pattern of heavenly things given through Moses, but Ahaz copies a heathen altar he admired in Damascus.
- Ahaz moves the brazen altar and commands Urijah to make the new altar the place of all the offerings, keeping the brazen altar for himself to inquire by.
  - **[Hebrews 8:5](/bible/hebrews/8#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/hebrews/8#v5)</sup> Who serve unto the example and shadow of heavenly things, as Moses was admonished of God when he was about to make the tabernacle: for, See, saith he, that thou make all things according to the pattern shewed to thee in the mount.

    Moses was warned to make all things according to the pattern shown in the mount, not after the fashion of the nations.

# 2 Kings 17: The Carrying Away of Israel

Read the chapter → [2 Kings 17](/bible/2-kings/17)

**[2 Kings 17:1-6](/bible/2-kings/17#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/17#v1)</sup> In the twelfth year of Ahaz king of Judah began Hoshea the son of Elah to reign in Samaria over Israel nine years.

> <sup>[2](/bible/2-kings/17#v2)</sup> And he did that which was evil in the sight of the Lord, but not as the kings of Israel that were before him.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/17#v3)</sup> Against him came up Shalmaneser king of Assyria; and Hoshea became his servant, and gave him presents.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/17#v4)</sup> And the king of Assyria found conspiracy in Hoshea: for he had sent messengers to So king of Egypt, and brought no present to the king of Assyria, as he had done year by year: therefore the king of Assyria shut him up, and bound him in prison.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/17#v5)</sup> Then the king of Assyria came up throughout all the land, and went up to Samaria, and besieged it three years.
>

- Hoshea son of Elah reigns nine years over Israel in Samaria and does evil, though not as the kings before him.
- Shalmaneser king of Assyria comes up, and Hoshea becomes his servant and pays tribute.
- The king of Assyria finds conspiracy in Hoshea because he sent messengers to So king of Egypt and stopped his yearly tribute, so he shuts him up in prison.
- Assyria besieges Samaria three years, then in the ninth year of Hoshea takes it and carries Israel away to Assyria, placing them in Halah, Habor, and the cities of the Medes.

>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/17#v9)</sup> And the children of Israel did secretly those things that were not right against the Lord their God, and they built them high places in all their cities, from the tower of the watchmen to the fenced city.

> <sup>[10](/bible/2-kings/17#v10)</sup> And they set them up images and groves in every high hill, and under every green tree:

- They walked in the statutes of the heathen, built high places in all their cities, and set up images and groves on every high hill and under every green tree.
- They wrought wicked things to provoke the Most High and served idols, though He had said plainly, ye shall not do this thing.
  - **[Deuteronomy 12:2-3](/bible/deuteronomy/12#v2)**

**[2 Kings 17:13-17](/bible/2-kings/17#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-kings/17#v13)</sup> Yet the Lord testified against Israel, and against Judah, by all the prophets, and by all the seers, saying, Turn ye from your evil ways, and keep my commandments and my statutes, according to all the law which I commanded your fathers, and which I sent to you by my servants the prophets.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-kings/17#v14)</sup> Notwithstanding they would not hear, but hardened their necks, like to the neck of their fathers, that did not believe in the Lord their God.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-kings/17#v15)</sup> And they rejected his statutes, and his covenant that he made with their fathers, and his testimonies which he testified against them; and they followed vanity, and became vain, and went after the heathen that were round about them, concerning whom the Lord had charged them, that they should not do like them.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-kings/17#v16)</sup> And they left all the commandments of the Lord their God, and made them molten images, even two calves, and made a grove, and worshipped all the host of heaven, and served Baal.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-kings/17#v17)</sup> And they caused their sons and their daughters to pass through the fire, and used divination and enchantments, and sold themselves to do evil in the sight of the Lord, to provoke him to anger.

- The Most High testified against Israel and Judah by all the prophets and seers, calling them to turn from evil and keep His commandments.
- They would not hear but hardened their necks like their fathers who did not believe in the Most High.
- To believe in the Most High is to take heed to the commandments, so those who claim belief while breaking the commandments do not truly believe.
- They rejected His statutes and covenant, followed vanity, made molten images and two calves, worshipped the host of heaven, and served Baal.
- They caused their sons and daughters to pass through the fire, used divinations and enchantments, and sold themselves to do evil.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 32:24](/bible/sirach/32#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/sirach/32#v24)</sup> He that believeth in the Lord taketh heed to the commandment; and he that trusteth in him shall fare never the worse.

    He that believeth in the Most High taketh heed to the commandments, tying true belief to obedience.
  - **[Deuteronomy 4:16-19](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v16)</sup> Lest ye corrupt yourselves, and make you a graven image, the similitude of any figure, the likeness of male or female,
    >
    > <sup>[17](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v17)</sup> The likeness of any beast that is on the earth, the likeness of any winged fowl that flieth in the air,
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v18)</sup> The likeness of any thing that creepeth on the ground, the likeness of any fish that is in the waters beneath the earth:
    >
    > <sup>[19](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v19)</sup> And lest thou lift up thine eyes unto heaven, and when thou seest the sun, and the moon, and the stars, even all the host of heaven, shouldest be driven to worship them, and serve them, which the Lord thy God hath divided unto all nations under the whole heaven.

    Israel was warned not to corrupt themselves by worshipping the host of heaven.

**[2 Kings 17:18-23](/bible/2-kings/17#v18)**
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-kings/17#v18)</sup> Therefore the Lord was very angry with Israel, and removed them out of his sight: there was none left but the tribe of Judah only.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-kings/17#v19)</sup> Also Judah kept not the commandments of the Lord their God, but walked in the statutes of Israel which they made.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-kings/17#v20)</sup> And the Lord rejected all the seed of Israel, and afflicted them, and delivered them into the hand of spoilers, until he had cast them out of his sight.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-kings/17#v21)</sup> For he rent Israel from the house of David; and they made Jeroboam the son of Nebat king: and Jeroboam drave Israel from following the Lord, and made them sin a great sin.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-kings/17#v22)</sup> For the children of Israel walked in all the sins of Jeroboam which he did; they departed not from them;
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-kings/17#v23)</sup> Until the Lord removed Israel out of his sight, as he had said by all his servants the prophets. So was Israel carried away out of their own land to Assyria unto this day.

- The Most High is very angry and removes Israel out of His sight, leaving only the tribe of Judah.
- Judah also fails to keep the commandments and walks in the statutes that Israel made.
- The Most High rejects all the seed of Israel, afflicts them, and delivers them into the hand of spoilers until He casts them out of His sight.
- This rejection is why the seed of Israel are later called heathens and Gentiles in the New Testament, which resolves much of the struggle over Jews and Gentiles there.
  - **[Deuteronomy 32:21](/bible/deuteronomy/32#v21)**
    > <sup>[21](/bible/deuteronomy/32#v21)</sup> They have moved me to jealousy with that which is not God; they have provoked me to anger with their vanities: and I will move them to jealousy with those which are not a people; I will provoke them to anger with a foolish nation.

    The Most High moved Israel to jealousy and anger, foretelling their rejection among the nations.

**[2 Kings 17:24-28](/bible/2-kings/17#v24)**
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-kings/17#v24)</sup> And the king of Assyria brought men from Babylon, and from Cuthah, and from Ava, and from Hamath, and from Sepharvaim, and placed them in the cities of Samaria instead of the children of Israel: and they possessed Samaria, and dwelt in the cities thereof.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-kings/17#v25)</sup> And so it was at the beginning of their dwelling there, that they feared not the Lord: therefore the Lord sent lions among them, which slew some of them.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-kings/17#v26)</sup> Wherefore they spake to the king of Assyria, saying, The nations which thou hast removed, and placed in the cities of Samaria, know not the manner of the God of the land: therefore he hath sent lions among them, and, behold, they slay them, because they know not the manner of the God of the land.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-kings/17#v27)</sup> Then the king of Assyria commanded, saying, Carry thither one of the priests whom ye brought from thence; and let them go and dwell there, and let him teach them the manner of the God of the land.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/2-kings/17#v28)</sup> Then one of the priests whom they had carried away from Samaria came and dwelt in Beth–el, and taught them how they should fear the Lord.

- The king of Assyria brings men from Babylon, Cuthah, Ava, Hamath, and Sepharvaim to dwell in the cities of Samaria in Israel's place.
- Because they do not fear the Most High, He sends lions among them that slay some of them.
- One of the carried-away priests is brought back to Bethel to teach the settlers how they should fear the Most High.

**[2 Kings 17:29-34](/bible/2-kings/17#v29)**
> <sup>[29](/bible/2-kings/17#v29)</sup> Howbeit every nation made gods of their own, and put them in the houses of the high places which the Samaritans had made, every nation in their cities wherein they dwelt.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/2-kings/17#v30)</sup> And the men of Babylon made Succoth–benoth, and the men of Cuth made Nergal, and the men of Hamath made Ashima,
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/2-kings/17#v31)</sup> And the Avites made Nibhaz and Tartak, and the Sepharvites burnt their children in fire to Adrammelech and Anammelech, the gods of Sepharvaim.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/2-kings/17#v32)</sup> So they feared the Lord, and made unto themselves of the lowest of them priests of the high places, which sacrificed for them in the houses of the high places.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/2-kings/17#v33)</sup> They feared the Lord, and served their own gods, after the manner of the nations whom they carried away from thence.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/2-kings/17#v34)</sup> Unto this day they do after the former manners: they fear not the Lord, neither do they after their statutes, or after their ordinances, or after the law and commandment which the Lord commanded the children of Jacob, whom he named Israel;

- Every nation makes gods of its own and sets them in the houses of the high places the Samaritans had made.
- The men of Babylon made Succoth-benoth, Cuth made Nergal, Hamath made Ashima, the Avites made Nibhaz and Tartak, and the Sepharvites burnt their children to Adrammelech and Anammelech.
- They feared the Most High and also served their own gods after the manner of the nations they came from.
- The Most High made His covenant and charged the children of Jacob whom He named Israel, showing the covenant belongs to Israel and not to all nations.

**[2 Kings 17:35-41](/bible/2-kings/17#v35)**
> <sup>[35](/bible/2-kings/17#v35)</sup> With whom the Lord had made a covenant, and charged them, saying, Ye shall not fear other gods, nor bow yourselves to them, nor serve them, nor sacrifice to them:
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/2-kings/17#v36)</sup> But the Lord, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt with great power and a stretched out arm, him shall ye fear, and him shall ye worship, and to him shall ye do sacrifice.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/2-kings/17#v37)</sup> And the statutes, and the ordinances, and the law, and the commandment, which he wrote for you, ye shall observe to do for evermore; and ye shall not fear other gods.
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/2-kings/17#v38)</sup> And the covenant that I have made with you ye shall not forget; neither shall ye fear other gods.
>
> <sup>[39](/bible/2-kings/17#v39)</sup> But the Lord your God ye shall fear; and he shall deliver you out of the hand of all your enemies.
>
> <sup>[40](/bible/2-kings/17#v40)</sup> Howbeit they did not hearken, but they did after their former manner.
>
> <sup>[41](/bible/2-kings/17#v41)</sup> So these nations feared the Lord, and served their graven images, both their children, and their children’s children: as did their fathers, so do they unto this day.

- The Most High commanded Israel not to fear, bow to, serve, or sacrifice to other gods, but to fear and worship Him alone who brought them out of Egypt with a stretched out arm.
- They were to observe His statutes, ordinances, and commandments forevermore, and He would deliver them out of the hand of all their enemies.
- No nation and no treaty can redeem Israel from their condition, only turning to the Most High can.
- These settler nations feared the Most High yet served their graven images to this day, and as a nation Israel does the same, serving the gods of the nations.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)</sup> But it shall come to pass, if thou wilt not hearken unto the voice of the Lord thy God, to observe to do all his commandments and his statutes which I command thee this day; that all these curses shall come upon thee, and overtake thee:

    No man can buy or redeem the nation out of its condition, for the curses come from forsaking the Most High.

# 2 Kings 18: Hezekiah and the Assyrian Threat

Read the chapter → [2 Kings 18](/bible/2-kings/18)

**[2 Kings 18:1-4](/bible/2-kings/18#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/18#v1)</sup> Now it came to pass in the third year of Hoshea son of Elah king of Israel, that Hezekiah the son of Ahaz king of Judah began to reign.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-kings/18#v2)</sup> Twenty and five years old was he when he began to reign; and he reigned twenty and nine years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name also was Abi, the daughter of Zachariah.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/18#v3)</sup> And he did that which was right in the sight of the Lord, according to all that David his father did.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/18#v4)</sup> He removed the high places, and brake the images, and cut down the groves, and brake in pieces the brasen serpent that Moses had made: for unto those days the children of Israel did burn incense to it: and he called it Nehushtan.

- Hezekiah son of Ahaz begins to reign over Judah in the third year of Hoshea, the last king of Israel before the captivity.
- He does that which is right according to all that David his father did, removing the high places, breaking the images, and cutting down the groves.
- He breaks in pieces the brazen serpent Moses made in the wilderness, which had stood from that time and was now being burned incense to, and he calls it Nehushtan.

**[2 Kings 18:5-8](/bible/2-kings/18#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/18#v5)</sup> He trusted in the Lord God of Israel; so that after him was none like him among all the kings of Judah, nor any that were before him.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/18#v6)</sup> For he clave to the Lord, and departed not from following him, but kept his commandments, which the Lord commanded Moses.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/18#v7)</sup> And the Lord was with him; and he prospered whithersoever he went forth: and he rebelled against the king of Assyria, and served him not.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/18#v8)</sup> He smote the Philistines, even unto Gaza, and the borders thereof, from the tower of the watchmen to the fenced city.

- Hezekiah trusts in the Most High so that none among the kings of Judah, before or after, was like him.
- He clave to the Most High, kept the commandments, and the Most High was with him so that he prospered.
- Prospering here does not mean a mansion or luxury, but the blessing that comes from keeping and doing the commandments.
- He rebels against the king of Assyria and smites the Philistines even unto Gaza.
  - **[Deuteronomy 29:9](/bible/deuteronomy/29#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/deuteronomy/29#v9)</sup> Keep therefore the words of this covenant, and do them, that ye may prosper in all that ye do.

    Keep the words of the covenant and do them, that ye may prosper in all that ye do.

**[2 Kings 18:9-12](/bible/2-kings/18#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/18#v9)</sup> And it came to pass in the fourth year of king Hezekiah, which was the seventh year of Hoshea son of Elah king of Israel, that Shalmaneser king of Assyria came up against Samaria, and besieged it.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-kings/18#v10)</sup> And at the end of three years they took it: even in the sixth year of Hezekiah, that is the ninth year of Hoshea king of Israel, Samaria was taken.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/18#v11)</sup> And the king of Assyria did carry away Israel unto Assyria, and put them in Halah and in Habor by the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes:
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-kings/18#v12)</sup> Because they obeyed not the voice of the Lord their God, but transgressed his covenant, and all that Moses the servant of the Lord commanded, and would not hear them, nor do them.

- In Hezekiah's fourth year Shalmaneser besieges Samaria, and after three years, in Hezekiah's sixth year, Samaria is taken.
- Israel was not removed all at once but carried away in stages into Assyria.
- The reason given again is that they obeyed not the voice of the Most High but transgressed His covenant and all Moses commanded.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)</sup> But it shall come to pass, if thou wilt not hearken unto the voice of the Lord thy God, to observe to do all his commandments and his statutes which I command thee this day; that all these curses shall come upon thee, and overtake thee:

    The captivity comes because Israel would not hearken to the voice of the Most High.

**[2 Kings 18:13-16](/bible/2-kings/18#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-kings/18#v13)</sup> Now in the fourteenth year of king Hezekiah did Sennacherib king of Assyria come up against all the fenced cities of Judah, and took them.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-kings/18#v14)</sup> And Hezekiah king of Judah sent to the king of Assyria to Lachish, saying, I have offended; return from me: that which thou puttest on me will I bear. And the king of Assyria appointed unto Hezekiah king of Judah three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-kings/18#v15)</sup> And Hezekiah gave him all the silver that was found in the house of the Lord, and in the treasures of the king’s house.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-kings/18#v16)</sup> At that time did Hezekiah cut off the gold from the doors of the temple of the Lord, and from the pillars which Hezekiah king of Judah had overlaid, and gave it to the king of Assyria.

- Sennacherib king of Assyria comes up against the fenced cities of Judah and takes them.
- Hezekiah sends to Lakish acknowledging offense and agrees to bear whatever tribute is laid on him.
- Assyria appoints three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold, so Hezekiah gives all the silver of the house of the Most High and even cuts the gold from the temple doors.

**[2 Kings 18:17-25](/bible/2-kings/18#v17)**
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-kings/18#v17)</sup> And the king of Assyria sent Tartan and Rabsaris and Rab–shakeh from Lachish to king Hezekiah with a great host against Jerusalem. And they went up and came to Jerusalem. And when they were come up, they came and stood by the conduit of the upper pool, which is in the highway of the fuller’s field.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-kings/18#v18)</sup> And when they had called to the king, there came out to them Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, which was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah the son of Asaph the recorder.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-kings/18#v19)</sup> And Rab–shakeh said unto them, Speak ye now to Hezekiah, Thus saith the great king, the king of Assyria, What confidence is this wherein thou trustest?
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-kings/18#v20)</sup> Thou sayest, (but they are but vain words,) I have counsel and strength for the war. Now on whom dost thou trust, that thou rebellest against me?
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-kings/18#v21)</sup> Now, behold, thou trustest upon the staff of this bruised reed, even upon Egypt, on which if a man lean, it will go into his hand, and pierce it: so is Pharaoh king of Egypt unto all that trust on him.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-kings/18#v22)</sup> But if ye say unto me, We trust in the Lord our God: is not that he, whose high places and whose altars Hezekiah hath taken away, and hath said to Judah and Jerusalem, Ye shall worship before this altar in Jerusalem?
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-kings/18#v23)</sup> Now therefore, I pray thee, give pledges to my lord the king of Assyria, and I will deliver thee two thousand horses, if thou be able on thy part to set riders upon them.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-kings/18#v24)</sup> How then wilt thou turn away the face of one captain of the least of my master’s servants, and put thy trust on Egypt for chariots and for horsemen?
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-kings/18#v25)</sup> Am I now come up without the Lord against this place to destroy it? The Lord said to me, Go up against this land, and destroy it.

- The king of Assyria sends Tartan, Rabsaris, and Rabshakeh with a great host to Jerusalem, and they stand by the conduit of the upper pool.
- Rabshakeh challenges Hezekiah's confidence, mocking his trust and calling Egypt a bruised reed that pierces the hand of any who lean on it.
- He argues that because Hezekiah removed the high places, even Judah's own worship is now confined to one altar, twisting the reform into a weakness.
- He boasts that Assyria came up against the land by the word of the Most High Himself, claiming divine backing to destroy it.

**[2 Kings 18:26-37](/bible/2-kings/18#v26)**
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-kings/18#v26)</sup> Then said Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, and Shebna, and Joah, unto Rab–shakeh, Speak, I pray thee, to thy servants in the Syrian language; for we understand it: and talk not with us in the Jews’ language in the ears of the people that are on the wall.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-kings/18#v27)</sup> But Rab–shakeh said unto them, Hath my master sent me to thy master, and to thee, to speak these words? hath he not sent me to the men which sit on the wall, that they may eat their own dung, and drink their own piss with you?
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/2-kings/18#v28)</sup> Then Rab–shakeh stood and cried with a loud voice in the Jews’ language, and spake, saying, Hear the word of the great king, the king of Assyria:
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/2-kings/18#v29)</sup> Thus saith the king, Let not Hezekiah deceive you: for he shall not be able to deliver you out of his hand:
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/2-kings/18#v30)</sup> Neither let Hezekiah make you trust in the Lord, saying, The Lord will surely deliver us, and this city shall not be delivered into the hand of the king of Assyria.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/2-kings/18#v31)</sup> Hearken not to Hezekiah: for thus saith the king of Assyria, Make an agreement with me by a present, and come out to me, and then eat ye every man of his own vine, and every one of his fig tree, and drink ye every one the waters of his cistern:
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/2-kings/18#v32)</sup> Until I come and take you away to a land like your own land, a land of corn and wine, a land of bread and vineyards, a land of oil olive and of honey, that ye may live, and not die: and hearken not unto Hezekiah, when he persuadeth you, saying, The Lord will deliver us.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/2-kings/18#v33)</sup> Hath any of the gods of the nations delivered at all his land out of the hand of the king of Assyria?
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/2-kings/18#v34)</sup> Where are the gods of Hamath, and of Arpad? where are the gods of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivah? have they delivered Samaria out of mine hand?
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/2-kings/18#v35)</sup> Who are they among all the gods of the countries, that have delivered their country out of mine hand, that the Lord should deliver Jerusalem out of mine hand?
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/2-kings/18#v36)</sup> But the people held their peace, and answered him not a word: for the king’s commandment was, saying, Answer him not.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/2-kings/18#v37)</sup> Then came Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, which was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah the son of Asaph the recorder, to Hezekiah with their clothes rent, and told him the words of Rab–shakeh.

- Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah ask Rabshakeh to speak in the Syrian language so the people on the wall will not understand, but he refuses.
- Rabshakeh cries aloud in the Jews' language, warning the people not to let Hezekiah deceive them or make them trust in the Most High.
- He promises the people their own vines, fig trees, and waters if they surrender, then a land like their own, seeking to turn them from their king.
- He asks whether the gods of Hamath, Arpad, Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivah delivered their lands, claiming no god can deliver Jerusalem out of his hand.
- The people hold their peace and answer not a word, for Hezekiah had commanded silence.
- Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah come to Hezekiah with their clothes rent and tell him the words of Rabshakeh.
