Read the chapter → [2 Kings 3](/bible/2-kings/3)

This session covers [2 Kings 3](/bible/2-kings/3) through 6, following the acts and ministry of Elisha after he received a double portion of the spirit of Elijah. It moves through the war of the three kings against Moab, the miracles among the sons of the prophets and the Shunammite woman, the cleansing of Naaman the Syrian and the judgment on Gehazi, the floating axe head, the blinding of the Syrian host, and the famine and siege of Samaria. These chapters show the hand of the Most High working through his prophet, a witness to Israel of how great a people they were when the Lord was with them, even while they remained a rebellious nation.

>

- Nevertheless he cleaves to the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin, and departs not from them. The house of Ahab had reared up images and led the kingdom into idolatry.

> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/3#v4)</sup> And Mesha king of Moab was a sheepmaster, and rendered unto the king of Israel an hundred thousand lambs, and an hundred thousand rams, with the wool.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/3#v5)</sup> But it came to pass, when Ahab was dead, that the king of Moab rebelled against the king of Israel.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/3#v6)</sup> And king Jehoram went out of Samaria the same time, and numbered all Israel.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/3#v7)</sup> And he went and sent to Jehoshaphat the king of Judah, saying, The king of Moab hath rebelled against me: wilt thou go with me against Moab to battle? And he said, I will go up: I am as thou art, my people as thy people, and my horses as thy horses.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/3#v8)</sup> And he said, Which way shall we go up? And he answered, The way through the wilderness of Edom.

- Mesha king of Moab is a sheepmaster who rendered to the king of Israel a hundred thousand lambs and a hundred thousand rams with the wool, as tribute. But when Ahab is dead, the king of Moab rebels against the king of Israel.
- King Jehoram goes out of Samaria, numbers all Israel, and sends to Jehoshaphat king of Judah, telling him Moab has rebelled and asking whether he will go up with him to battle. Jehoshaphat answers, I will go up; I am as thou art, my people as thy people, and my horses as thy horses.
- They agree to go up by the way through the wilderness of Edom.

> <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/3#v11)</sup> But Jehoshaphat said, Is there not here a prophet of the Lord, that we may enquire of the Lord by him? And one of the king of Israel’s servants answered and said, Here is Elisha the son of Shaphat, which poured water on the hands of Elijah.
>

- Jehoshaphat asks whether there is not a prophet of the Lord that they may inquire of the Lord by him. One of the king of Israel's servants answers that Elisha the son of Shaphat is there, who poured water on the hands of Elijah.
- Jehoshaphat says the word of the Lord is with him, so the three kings go down to Elisha.

**[2 Kings 3:13-14](/bible/2-kings/3#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-kings/3#v13)</sup> And Elisha said unto the king of Israel, What have I to do with thee? get thee to the prophets of thy father, and to the prophets of thy mother. And the king of Israel said unto him, Nay: for the Lord hath called these three kings together, to deliver them into the hand of Moab.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-kings/3#v14)</sup> And Elisha said, As the Lord of hosts liveth, before whom I stand, surely, were it not that I regard the presence of Jehoshaphat the king of Judah, I would not look toward thee, nor see thee.

- Elisha confronts the king of Israel, asking what he has to do with him, and sends him to the prophets of his father and of his mother. The king answers that the Lord has called the three kings together to deliver them into the hand of Moab.
- Elisha says, as the Lord of hosts liveth before whom I stand, that were it not that he regarded the presence of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, he would not look toward the king of Israel nor see him. He addresses the matter only for the sake of the righteous king of Judah.

- The Lord will also deliver the Moabites into their hand, and they shall smite every fenced city and every choice city, fell every good tree, stop all the wells of water, and mar every good piece of land with stones.

**[2 Kings 3:20-25](/bible/2-kings/3#v20)**
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-kings/3#v20)</sup> And it came to pass in the morning, when the meat offering was offered, that, behold, there came water by the way of Edom, and the country was filled with water.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-kings/3#v21)</sup> And when all the Moabites heard that the kings were come up to fight against them, they gathered all that were able to put on armour, and upward, and stood in the border.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-kings/3#v22)</sup> And they rose up early in the morning, and the sun shone upon the water, and the Moabites saw the water on the other side as red as blood:
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-kings/3#v23)</sup> And they said, This is blood: the kings are surely slain, and they have smitten one another: now therefore, Moab, to the spoil.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-kings/3#v24)</sup> And when they came to the camp of Israel, the Israelites rose up and smote the Moabites, so that they fled before them: but they went forward smiting the Moabites, even in their country.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-kings/3#v25)</sup> And they beat down the cities, and on every good piece of land cast every man his stone, and filled it; and they stopped all the wells of water, and felled all the good trees: only in Kir–haraseth left they the stones thereof; howbeit the slingers went about it, and smote it.

- In the morning, when the meat offering is offered, water comes by the way of Edom and the country is filled with water. When the Moabites hear the kings have come up to fight, they gather all who can put on armor and stand in the border.
- Rising early, the Moabites see the sun shining on the water on the other side red as blood, and conclude the kings are slain and have smitten one another. They cry, Moab to the spoil, and run toward the camp expecting to gather the plunder of dead men.
- When they come to the camp of Israel, the Israelites rise up and smite the Moabites so that they flee, and Israel goes forward smiting them even into their own country, beating down cities, casting stones on every good piece of land, stopping the wells, and felling the good trees, leaving only the stones of Kir-haraseth, which the slingers went about and smote.
- This victory is a testament to how great a people Israel was when the hand of the Lord was with them. When Israel was in its right mind and its prayers went forth, the Lord fought for them, which is part of why the heathen nations, and Moab in particular, have hated Israel to this day.

    The Lord causes the enemies that rise up against Israel to be smitten before their face when the nation keeps his commandments.
