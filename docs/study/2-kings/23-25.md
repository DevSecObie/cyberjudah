This session covers [2 Kings 23](/bible/2-kings/23) through 25. It follows Josiah as he hears the book of the law that was found in the house of the Most High, rends his clothes in the fear of the Lord, and leads the whole nation in a covenant to keep the commandments. He tears down the idols, altars, and high places that his fathers had set up, keeps a great Passover, and turns to the Lord with all his heart. Yet the wrath kindled by Manasseh's sins is not turned away: Josiah is slain at Megiddo, his sons do evil, and the reading moves through the fall of Judah, the burning of the temple, and the carrying away of the people to Babylon, closing with a note of mercy shown to Jehoiachin. The thread throughout is repentance: seeing in the book of the law why the nation is in its condition and turning back to keep what is written.

# 2 Kings 25: The Fall of Jerusalem and the Burning of the Temple

Read the chapter → [2 Kings 25](/bible/2-kings/25)

**[2 Kings 25:1-3](/bible/2-kings/25#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/25#v1)</sup> And it came to pass in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, in the tenth day of the month, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came, he, and all his host, against Jerusalem, and pitched against it; and they built forts against it round about.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-kings/25#v2)</sup> And the city was besieged unto the eleventh year of king Zedekiah.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/25#v3)</sup> And on the ninth day of the fourth month the famine prevailed in the city, and there was no bread for the people of the land.

- In the ninth year of Zedekiah's reign Nebuchadnezzar came with all his host against Jerusalem, pitched against it, and built forts round about it.
- The city was besieged until the eleventh year of Zedekiah, and the famine prevailed in the city.
- Because the city was besieged no food, water, or goods could get in, so there was no bread for the people of the land.

**[2 Kings 25:4-7](/bible/2-kings/25#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/25#v4)</sup> And the city was broken up, and all the men of war fled by night by the way of the gate between two walls, which is by the king’s garden: (now the Chaldees were against the city round about:) and the king went the way toward the plain.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/25#v5)</sup> And the army of the Chaldees pursued after the king, and overtook him in the plains of Jericho: and all his army were scattered from him.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/25#v6)</sup> So they took the king, and brought him up to the king of Babylon to Riblah; and they gave judgment upon him.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/25#v7)</sup> And they slew the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes, and put out the eyes of Zedekiah, and bound him with fetters of brass, and carried him to Babylon.

- The city was broken up and the men of war fled by night by the way of the gate between two walls by the king's garden, while the Chaldeans were round about the city.
- The army pursued the king, overtook him in the plains of Jericho, and his army was scattered from him, and they took him to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where judgment was given upon him.
- They slew Zedekiah's sons before his eyes, then put out his eyes, bound him with fetters of brass, and carried him to Babylon, just as Jeremiah had warned him.

**[2 Kings 25:8-12](/bible/2-kings/25#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/25#v8)</sup> And in the fifth month, on the seventh day of the month, which is the nineteenth year of king Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, came Nebuzar–adan, captain of the guard, a servant of the king of Babylon, unto Jerusalem:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/25#v9)</sup> And he burnt the house of the Lord, and the king’s house, and all the houses of Jerusalem, and every great man’s house burnt he with fire.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-kings/25#v10)</sup> And all the army of the Chaldees, that were with the captain of the guard, brake down the walls of Jerusalem round about.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/25#v11)</sup> Now the rest of the people that were left in the city, and the fugitives that fell away to the king of Babylon, with the remnant of the multitude, did Nebuzar–adan the captain of the guard carry away.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-kings/25#v12)</sup> But the captain of the guard left of the poor of the land to be vinedressers and husbandmen.

- In the fifth month of the nineteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard came to Jerusalem.
- He burnt the house of the Lord, the king's house, and every great man's house, and the army broke down the walls of Jerusalem round about.
- He carried away the rest of the people and the fugitives, but left the poor of the land to be vinedressers and husbandmen, since the poor could not fend for themselves.

- The two pillars, one sea, and the bases Solomon had made were of brass without weight, the one pillar eighteen cubits high with a brasen chapiter, wreathen work, and pomegranates round about.
