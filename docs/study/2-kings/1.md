Read the chapter → [2 Kings 1](/bible/2-kings/1)

This session covers [2 Kings 1](/bible/2-kings/1) and 2. The teaching follows Ahaziah, son of Ahab, who falls sick and sends to inquire of Beelzebub the god of Ekron rather than the Most High, and how Elijah pronounces the word that he shall surely die. It then follows the fire that comes down on the captains of fifty and the taking up of Elijah into heaven, the double portion that falls to Elisha, and the signs that follow him. Chapters 1 and 2 were taught in this session; the full exposition of chapter 3 belongs to the next session, 2 Kings 3-6.

**[2 Kings 1:1](/bible/2-kings/1#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/1#v1)</sup> Then Moab rebelled against Israel after the death of Ahab.

- Moab rebels against Israel after the death of Ahab. Nations that had been subdued under David and brought under tribute begin to break away, because Israel no longer walks in the blessing that came from serving the Most High.
  - **[2 Samuel 8:1-2](/bible/2-samuel/8#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/8#v1)</sup> And after this it came to pass, that David smote the Philistines, and subdued them: and David took Metheg–ammah out of the hand of the Philistines.
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/8#v2)</sup> And he smote Moab, and measured them with a line, casting them down to the ground; even with two lines measured he to put to death, and with one full line to keep alive. And so the Moabites became David’s servants, and brought gifts.

    David smote Moab and measured them with a line, so that Moab became David's servants and brought gifts, that is, tribute, when Israel served the Lord.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:1](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v1)</sup> And it shall come to pass, if thou shalt hearken diligently unto the voice of the Lord thy God, to observe and to do all his commandments which I command thee this day, that the Lord thy God will set thee on high above all nations of the earth:

    If Israel hearkens diligently to the voice of the Lord to do his commandments, the Most High will set them on high above all nations of the earth, as happened under David.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:7](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v7)</sup> The Lord shall cause thine enemies that rise up against thee to be smitten before thy face: they shall come out against thee one way, and flee before thee seven ways.

    The Lord causes the enemies that rise up against Israel to be smitten before their face when the nation keeps his commandments.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)</sup> But it shall come to pass, if thou wilt not hearken unto the voice of the Lord thy God, to observe to do all his commandments and his statutes which I command thee this day; that all these curses shall come upon thee, and overtake thee:

    But if Israel will not hearken to the voice of the Lord to do his commandments, all these curses shall come upon them and overtake them.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:25-26](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v25)</sup> The Lord shall cause thee to be smitten before thine enemies: thou shalt go out one way against them, and flee seven ways before them: and shalt be removed into all the kingdoms of the earth.
    >
    > <sup>[26](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v26)</sup> And thy carcase shall be meat unto all fowls of the air, and unto the beasts of the earth, and no man shall fray them away.

    The Lord causes a disobedient Israel to be smitten before their enemies and scattered, their carcasses meat for the fowls of the air, which is the same judgment pronounced on the house of Ahab.

**[2 Kings 1:2-8](/bible/2-kings/1#v2)**
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-kings/1#v2)</sup> And Ahaziah fell down through a lattice in his upper chamber that was in Samaria, and was sick: and he sent messengers, and said unto them, Go, enquire of Baal–zebub the god of Ekron whether I shall recover of this disease.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/1#v3)</sup> But the angel of the Lord said to Elijah the Tishbite, Arise, go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria, and say unto them, Is it not because there is not a God in Israel, that ye go to enquire of Baal–zebub the god of Ekron?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/1#v4)</sup> Now therefore thus saith the Lord, Thou shalt not come down from that bed on which thou art gone up, but shalt surely die. And Elijah departed.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/1#v5)</sup> And when the messengers turned back unto him, he said unto them, Why are ye now turned back?
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/1#v6)</sup> And they said unto him, There came a man up to meet us, and said unto us, Go, turn again unto the king that sent you, and say unto him, Thus saith the Lord, Is it not because there is not a God in Israel, that thou sendest to enquire of Baal–zebub the god of Ekron? therefore thou shalt not come down from that bed on which thou art gone up, but shalt surely die.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/1#v7)</sup> And he said unto them, What manner of man was he which came up to meet you, and told you these words?
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/1#v8)</sup> And they answered him, He was an hairy man, and girt with a girdle of leather about his loins. And he said, It is Elijah the Tishbite.

- Ahaziah falls down through a lattice in his upper chamber and is sick, and sends messengers to inquire of Beelzebub, the god of Ekron, the lord of the flies, whether he shall recover. He turns to an idol rather than to the Most High for his answer.
- The angel of the Lord sends Elijah the Tishbite to meet the messengers and ask whether it is because there is no God in Israel that they go to inquire of Beelzebub. The word of the Lord is that Ahaziah shall not come down from his bed but shall surely die.
- When the messengers describe the hairy man girt with a girdle of leather who met them, the king knows it is Elijah. Ahaziah should have known the history that would have kept him from trusting the god of Ekron.
  - **[1 Samuel 5:10-11](/bible/1-samuel/5#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/1-samuel/5#v10)</sup> Therefore they sent the ark of God to Ekron. And it came to pass, as the ark of God came to Ekron, that the Ekronites cried out, saying, They have brought about the ark of the God of Israel to us, to slay us and our people.
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/1-samuel/5#v11)</sup> So they sent and gathered together all the lords of the Philistines, and said, Send away the ark of the God of Israel, and let it go again to his own place, that it slay us not, and our people: for there was a deadly destruction throughout all the city; the hand of God was very heavy there.

    When the ark of God came to Ekron, the Ekronites cried out that it had been brought to slay them, for the hand of God was heavy there, so the god of Ekron could not even defend his own people against the God of Israel.

**[2 Kings 1:9-16](/bible/2-kings/1#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/1#v9)</sup> Then the king sent unto him a captain of fifty with his fifty. And he went up to him: and, behold, he sat on the top of an hill. And he spake unto him, Thou man of God, the king hath said, Come down.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-kings/1#v10)</sup> And Elijah answered and said to the captain of fifty, If I be a man of God, then let fire come down from heaven, and consume thee and thy fifty. And there came down fire from heaven, and consumed him and his fifty.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/1#v11)</sup> Again also he sent unto him another captain of fifty with his fifty. And he answered and said unto him, O man of God, thus hath the king said, Come down quickly.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-kings/1#v12)</sup> And Elijah answered and said unto them, If I be a man of God, let fire come down from heaven, and consume thee and thy fifty. And the fire of God came down from heaven, and consumed him and his fifty.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-kings/1#v13)</sup> And he sent again a captain of the third fifty with his fifty. And the third captain of fifty went up, and came and fell on his knees before Elijah, and besought him, and said unto him, O man of God, I pray thee, let my life, and the life of these fifty thy servants, be precious in thy sight.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-kings/1#v14)</sup> Behold, there came fire down from heaven, and burnt up the two captains of the former fifties with their fifties: therefore let my life now be precious in thy sight.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-kings/1#v15)</sup> And the angel of the Lord said unto Elijah, Go down with him: be not afraid of him. And he arose, and went down with him unto the king.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-kings/1#v16)</sup> And he said unto him, Thus saith the Lord, Forasmuch as thou hast sent messengers to enquire of Baal–zebub the god of Ekron, is it not because there is no God in Israel to enquire of his word? therefore thou shalt not come down off that bed on which thou art gone up, but shalt surely die.

- The king sends a captain of fifty with his fifty to Elijah, who sits on the top of a hill, and the captain commands the man of God to come down. Elijah answers that if he is a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and consume him and his fifty, and it does.
- A second captain of fifty is sent and speaks the same way, and fire from heaven consumes him and his fifty as well. The third captain goes up, falls on his knees, and pleads that his life and the lives of his fifty be precious in Elijah's sight.
- The angel of the Lord tells Elijah to go down with the third captain and not to fear him. Elijah delivers to the king the same word, that because he sent to inquire of Beelzebub as though there were no God in Israel, he shall surely die.

**[2 Kings 1:17-18](/bible/2-kings/1#v17)**
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-kings/1#v17)</sup> So he died according to the word of the Lord which Elijah had spoken. And Jehoram reigned in his stead in the second year of Jehoram the son of Jehoshaphat king of Judah; because he had no son.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-kings/1#v18)</sup> Now the rest of the acts of Ahaziah which he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

- Ahaziah dies according to the word of the Lord that Elijah had spoken, and Jehoram reigns in his stead because Ahaziah had no son. The rest of the acts of Ahaziah are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.
