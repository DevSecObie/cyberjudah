Read the chapter → [2 Kings 19](/bible/2-kings/19)

This session covers [2 Kings 19](/bible/2-kings/19) through 22. It opens with Hezekiah turning to the Most High when Assyria blasphemes the living God, and the prophet Isaiah returning the word that Sennacherib will fall by the sword in his own land. It shows the Most High schooling the proud king of Assyria, reminding him that his power to lay cities waste was given by the Most High for His own purposes. It moves through the healing of Hezekiah and his folly with the envoys of Babylon, the deep wickedness of Manasseh and Amon, and the reforms of Josiah who found the book of the law. The thread throughout is that the Most High keeps His word: He hears the prayer of the humble, defends His city for His own sake and for David's sake, and brings righteous judgment upon those who blaspheme and forsake Him. The prophets Isaiah, Micah, and Hosea speak the same thing in this same time period, because they were all sent to declare what would come upon the nation.

**[2 Kings 19:1-7](/bible/2-kings/19#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/19#v1)</sup> And it came to pass, when king Hezekiah heard it, that he rent his clothes, and covered himself with sackcloth, and went into the house of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-kings/19#v2)</sup> And he sent Eliakim, which was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and the elders of the priests, covered with sackcloth, to Isaiah the prophet the son of Amoz.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/19#v3)</sup> And they said unto him, Thus saith Hezekiah, This day is a day of trouble, and of rebuke, and blasphemy: for the children are come to the birth, and there is not strength to bring forth.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/19#v4)</sup> It may be the Lord thy God will hear all the words of Rab–shakeh, whom the king of Assyria his master hath sent to reproach the living God; and will reprove the words which the Lord thy God hath heard: wherefore lift up thy prayer for the remnant that are left.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/19#v5)</sup> So the servants of king Hezekiah came to Isaiah.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/19#v6)</sup> And Isaiah said unto them, Thus shall ye say to your master, Thus saith the Lord, Be not afraid of the words which thou hast heard, with which the servants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed me.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/19#v7)</sup> Behold, I will send a blast upon him, and he shall hear a rumour, and shall return to his own land; and I will cause him to fall by the sword in his own land.

- When Hezekiah hears the reproach of Assyria he rends his clothes, covers himself with sackcloth, and goes into the house of the Most High, then sends his servants to Isaiah the prophet.
- He calls it a day of trouble, rebuke, and blasphemy, and asks Isaiah to lift up prayer for the remnant that is left.
- This follows chapter 18, where Rabshakeh the messenger of Sennacherib told Judah their God was no different from the gods of the nations Assyria had already destroyed.
- Isaiah, Micah, and Hosea prophesied in this same period, so much of what is read in one is found in the others.
- Isaiah returns the word of the Most High: fear not the blasphemy, for the Most High will send a blast upon Sennacherib, and he shall hear a rumor, return to his own land, and fall by the sword there.

**[2 Kings 19:8-13](/bible/2-kings/19#v8)**
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/19#v8)</sup> So Rab–shakeh returned, and found the king of Assyria warring against Libnah: for he had heard that he was departed from Lachish.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/19#v9)</sup> And when he heard say of Tirhakah king of Ethiopia, Behold, he is come out to fight against thee: he sent messengers again unto Hezekiah, saying,
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-kings/19#v10)</sup> Thus shall ye speak to Hezekiah king of Judah, saying, Let not thy God in whom thou trustest deceive thee, saying, Jerusalem shall not be delivered into the hand of the king of Assyria.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/19#v11)</sup> Behold, thou hast heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all lands, by destroying them utterly: and shalt thou be delivered?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-kings/19#v12)</sup> Have the gods of the nations delivered them which my fathers have destroyed; as Gozan, and Haran, and Rezeph, and the children of Eden which were in Thelasar?
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-kings/19#v13)</sup> Where is the king of Hamath, and the king of Arpad, and the king of the city of Sepharvaim, of Hena, and Ivah?

- Rabshakeh returns and finds the king of Assyria warring against Libnah, having departed from Lachish.
- Hearing that Tirhakah king of Ethiopia has come out to fight against him, Sennacherib sends messengers again to Hezekiah with a threatening letter.
- The letter mocks Judah's trust, listing the nations and kings Assyria destroyed, saying their gods could not deliver them, so Jerusalem will not be delivered either.

**[2 Kings 19:14-19](/bible/2-kings/19#v14)**
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-kings/19#v14)</sup> And Hezekiah received the letter of the hand of the messengers, and read it: and Hezekiah went up into the house of the Lord, and spread it before the Lord.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-kings/19#v15)</sup> And Hezekiah prayed before the Lord, and said, O Lord God of Israel, which dwellest between the cherubims, thou art the God, even thou alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth; thou hast made heaven and earth.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-kings/19#v16)</sup> Lord, bow down thine ear, and hear: open, Lord, thine eyes, and see: and hear the words of Sennacherib, which hath sent him to reproach the living God.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-kings/19#v17)</sup> Of a truth, Lord, the kings of Assyria have destroyed the nations and their lands,
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-kings/19#v18)</sup> And have cast their gods into the fire: for they were no gods, but the work of men’s hands, wood and stone: therefore they have destroyed them.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-kings/19#v19)</sup> Now therefore, O Lord our God, I beseech thee, save thou us out of his hand, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that thou art the Lord God, even thou only.

- Hezekiah receives the letter, reads it, goes up to the house of the Most High, and spreads it before Him.
- He prays acknowledging the Most High as God alone of all the kingdoms of the earth, maker of heaven and earth, and asks Him to bow down His ear and hear the words that reproached the living God.
- He confesses that the gods Assyria cast into the fire were no gods but the work of men's hands, wood and stone, and asks the Most High to save them so all the nations may know He alone is the Lord God.
- The prayer of Hezekiah is that the Most High show His power and prove His people are the chosen and that He is the one true living God. This is a righteous prayer, the same praying for the downfall of enemies and for righteous judgment to come upon them.

**[2 Kings 19:20-28](/bible/2-kings/19#v20)**
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-kings/19#v20)</sup> Then Isaiah the son of Amoz sent to Hezekiah, saying, Thus saith the Lord God of Israel, That which thou hast prayed to me against Sennacherib king of Assyria I have heard.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-kings/19#v21)</sup> This is the word that the Lord hath spoken concerning him; The virgin the daughter of Zion hath despised thee, and laughed thee to scorn; the daughter of Jerusalem hath shaken her head at thee.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-kings/19#v22)</sup> Whom hast thou reproached and blasphemed? and against whom hast thou exalted thy voice, and lifted up thine eyes on high? even against the Holy One of Israel.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-kings/19#v23)</sup> By thy messengers thou hast reproached the Lord, and hast said, With the multitude of my chariots I am come up to the height of the mountains, to the sides of Lebanon, and will cut down the tall cedar trees thereof, and the choice fir trees thereof: and I will enter into the lodgings of his borders, and into the forest of his Carmel.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-kings/19#v24)</sup> I have digged and drunk strange waters, and with the sole of my feet have I dried up all the rivers of besieged places.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-kings/19#v25)</sup> Hast thou not heard long ago how I have done it, and of ancient times that I have formed it? now have I brought it to pass, that thou shouldest be to lay waste fenced cities into ruinous heaps.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-kings/19#v26)</sup> Therefore their inhabitants were of small power, they were dismayed and confounded; they were as the grass of the field, and as the green herb, as the grass on the housetops, and as corn blasted before it be grown up.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-kings/19#v27)</sup> But I know thy abode, and thy going out, and thy coming in, and thy rage against me.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/2-kings/19#v28)</sup> Because thy rage against me and thy tumult is come up into mine ears, therefore I will put my hook in thy nose, and my bridle in thy lips, and I will turn thee back by the way by which thou camest.

- Isaiah sends word that the Most High has heard the prayer against Sennacherib, and the virgin daughter of Zion despises and laughs him to scorn.
- The Most High asks whom Sennacherib has reproached and blasphemed, for he has lifted up his eyes on high against the Holy One of Israel. Israel is laughing at the enemy, for it is already written that they lose.
- Assyria boasted with the multitude of his chariots to cut down cedars and dry up rivers, the same as a nation today boasting of its army, military, and political system.
- The Most High answers that He did it and formed it of ancient times; He gave Assyria the very ability to lay fenced cities waste as ruinous heaps for His own purpose.
- Because their rage and tumult came up to His ears, the Most High will put His hook in Sennacherib's nose and His bridle in his lips, and turn him back the way he came, which joins with the blast promised in verse 7.

    > <sup>[6](/bible/micah/1#v6)</sup> Therefore I will make Samaria as an heap of the field, and as plantings of a vineyard: and I will pour down the stones thereof into the valley, and I will discover the foundations thereof.

    Micah, prophesying in the same time, says the Most High will make Samaria a heap, pouring down her stones and laying her idols desolate.

**[2 Kings 19:29-31](/bible/2-kings/19#v29)**
> <sup>[29](/bible/2-kings/19#v29)</sup> And this shall be a sign unto thee, Ye shall eat this year such things as grow of themselves, and in the second year that which springeth of the same; and in the third year sow ye, and reap, and plant vineyards, and eat the fruits thereof.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/2-kings/19#v30)</sup> And the remnant that is escaped of the house of Judah shall yet again take root downward, and bear fruit upward.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/2-kings/19#v31)</sup> For out of Jerusalem shall go forth a remnant, and they that escape out of mount Zion: the zeal of the Lord of hosts shall do this.

- The sign given to Hezekiah is that they shall eat what grows of itself two years, and in the third year sow, reap, and plant vineyards.
- The remnant of the house of Judah shall again take root downward and bear fruit upward, for out of Jerusalem and Mount Zion a remnant shall escape.
- This is twofold: a remnant came out of Assyria and into Babylon, and it also speaks of the remnant today, for the zeal of the Lord of hosts will perform it.

**[2 Kings 19:32-34](/bible/2-kings/19#v32)**
> <sup>[32](/bible/2-kings/19#v32)</sup> Therefore thus saith the Lord concerning the king of Assyria, He shall not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with shield, nor cast a bank against it.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/2-kings/19#v33)</sup> By the way that he came, by the same shall he return, and shall not come into this city, saith the Lord.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/2-kings/19#v34)</sup> For I will defend this city, to save it, for mine own sake, and for my servant David’s sake.

- The Most High declares the king of Assyria shall not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with shield, nor cast a bank against it.
- By the way that he came, by the same shall he return, and he shall not enter the city.
- The Most High will defend the city to save it for His own sake and for His servant David's sake; what He did to Samaria He will not do to Jerusalem.

**[2 Kings 19:35-37](/bible/2-kings/19#v35)**
> <sup>[35](/bible/2-kings/19#v35)</sup> And it came to pass that night, that the angel of the Lord went out, and smote in the camp of the Assyrians an hundred fourscore and five thousand: and when they arose early in the morning, behold, they were all dead corpses.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/2-kings/19#v36)</sup> So Sennacherib king of Assyria departed, and went and returned, and dwelt at Nineveh.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/2-kings/19#v37)</sup> And it came to pass, as he was worshipping in the house of Nisroch his god, that Adrammelech and Sharezer his sons smote him with the sword: and they escaped into the land of Armenia. And Esarhaddon his son reigned in his stead.

- That night the angel of the Most High goes out and smites the camp of the Assyrians, killing a hundred fourscore and five thousand, all dead corpses by morning. A score is twenty, so this is a hundred and eighty-five thousand slain.
- Israel did not lift a finger; the Most High handled the battle for them, for it is not by might but by His spirit. This is praying for the death of the enemy and for righteous judgment.
- Sennacherib departs and dwells at Nineveh, and as he worships in the house of Nisroch his god, his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer smite him with the sword and flee, and Esarhaddon his son reigns in his stead, fulfilling the word of verse 7.
