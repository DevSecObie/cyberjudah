## Introduction

This session covers [2 Kings 7](/bible/2-kings/7) through 10, the record of Elisha the prophet and the sweeping judgment poured out on the house of Ahab. It moves from the famine in Samaria broken overnight by the Most High, through the anointing of Jehu and the destruction of the wicked kings, to Jehu's zeal against Baal and his own falling short at the end. The thread running through these chapters is the surety of the word of the Most High spoken by his prophets, and the warning that even a man who does great things can turn away if he does not walk in the law with all his heart.

**[2 Kings 7:1-2](/bible/2-kings/7#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/7#v1)</sup> Then Elisha said, Hear ye the word of the Lord; Thus saith the Lord, To morrow about this time shall a measure of fine flour be sold for a shekel, and two measures of barley for a shekel, in the gate of Samaria.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-kings/7#v2)</sup> Then a lord on whose hand the king leaned answered the man of God, and said, Behold, if the Lord would make windows in heaven, might this thing be? And he said, Behold, thou shalt see it with thine eyes, but shalt not eat thereof.

- Elisha declares the word of the Most High that within a day the famine will break and fine flour and barley will sell cheaply in the gate of Samaria.
- The lord on whose hand the king leaned doubts the word, asking if the Most High would make windows in heaven. Elisha tells him he will see it with his eyes but not eat of it.
- The prophet speaks the word of the Most High plainly, without fear and without doubting, whether the message is blessing or judgment.

**[2 Kings 7:3-8](/bible/2-kings/7#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/7#v3)</sup> And there were four leprous men at the entering in of the gate: and they said one to another, Why sit we here until we die?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/7#v4)</sup> If we say, We will enter into the city, then the famine is in the city, and we shall die there: and if we sit still here, we die also. Now therefore come, and let us fall unto the host of the Syrians: if they save us alive, we shall live; and if they kill us, we shall but die.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/7#v5)</sup> And they rose up in the twilight, to go unto the camp of the Syrians: and when they were come to the uttermost part of the camp of Syria, behold, there was no man there.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/7#v6)</sup> For the Lord had made the host of the Syrians to hear a noise of chariots, and a noise of horses, even the noise of a great host: and they said one to another, Lo, the king of Israel hath hired against us the kings of the Hittites, and the kings of the Egyptians, to come upon us.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/7#v7)</sup> Wherefore they arose and fled in the twilight, and left their tents, and their horses, and their asses, even the camp as it was, and fled for their life.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/7#v8)</sup> And when these lepers came to the uttermost part of the camp, they went into one tent, and did eat and drink, and carried thence silver, and gold, and raiment, and went and hid it; and came again, and entered into another tent, and carried thence also, and went and hid it.

- Four leprous men at the gate reason that they will die either way, so they go over to the camp of the Syrians hoping to be spared.
- The Most High had made the host of the Syrians hear the noise of chariots and horses, so they fled in the twilight and left everything behind.
- The lepers eat and drink and carry off silver, gold, and raiment, hiding the spoil for themselves.

**[2 Kings 7:9-16](/bible/2-kings/7#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/7#v9)</sup> Then they said one to another, We do not well: this day is a day of good tidings, and we hold our peace: if we tarry till the morning light, some mischief will come upon us: now therefore come, that we may go and tell the king’s household.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-kings/7#v10)</sup> So they came and called unto the porter of the city: and they told them, saying, We came to the camp of the Syrians, and, behold, there was no man there, neither voice of man, but horses tied, and asses tied, and the tents as they were.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/7#v11)</sup> And he called the porters; and they told it to the king’s house within.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-kings/7#v12)</sup> And the king arose in the night, and said unto his servants, I will now shew you what the Syrians have done to us. They know that we be hungry; therefore are they gone out of the camp to hide themselves in the field, saying, When they come out of the city, we shall catch them alive, and get into the city.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-kings/7#v13)</sup> And one of his servants answered and said, Let some take, I pray thee, five of the horses that remain, which are left in the city, (behold, they are as all the multitude of Israel that are left in it: behold, I say, they are even as all the multitude of the Israelites that are consumed:) and let us send and see.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-kings/7#v14)</sup> They took therefore two chariot horses; and the king sent after the host of the Syrians, saying, Go and see.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-kings/7#v15)</sup> And they went after them unto Jordan: and, lo, all the way was full of garments and vessels, which the Syrians had cast away in their haste. And the messengers returned, and told the king.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-kings/7#v16)</sup> And the people went out, and spoiled the tents of the Syrians. So a measure of fine flour was sold for a shekel, and two measures of barley for a shekel, according to the word of the Lord.

- The lepers realize this is a day of good tidings and that they should not hold their peace, so they report to the king's household.
- The king suspects a trap, thinking the Syrians are hiding in the field to catch Israel coming out of the city.
- Messengers sent to investigate find the way to Jordan full of garments and vessels cast away in the Syrians' haste, and the people go out and spoil the tents, so the food is sold cheaply exactly as the word said.

**[2 Kings 7:17-20](/bible/2-kings/7#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/2-kings/7#v17)</sup> And the king appointed the lord on whose hand he leaned to have the charge of the gate: and the people trode upon him in the gate, and he died, as the man of God had said, who spake when the king came down to him.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-kings/7#v18)</sup> And it came to pass as the man of God had spoken to the king, saying, Two measures of barley for a shekel, and a measure of fine flour for a shekel, shall be to morrow about this time in the gate of Samaria:
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-kings/7#v19)</sup> And that lord answered the man of God, and said, Now, behold, if the Lord should make windows in heaven, might such a thing be? And he said, Behold, thou shalt see it with thine eyes, but shalt not eat thereof.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-kings/7#v20)</sup> And so it fell out unto him: for the people trode upon him in the gate, and he died.

- The king sets the doubting lord in charge of the gate, and the people trod upon him there and he died, just as the man of God had spoken.
- The lord saw the plenty with his eyes but did not eat of it, so the word of the Most High fell out to the letter.
- The trampling in the gate stands as a picture of a crowd stampeding after goods, hating one another over things, and here it became the judgment on a man who doubted the word.

# 2 Kings 8: The Prophet Weeps Over Israel's Coming Affliction

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/8#v1)</sup> Then spake Elisha unto the woman, whose son he had restored to life, saying, Arise, and go thou and thine household, and sojourn wheresoever thou canst sojourn: for the Lord hath called for a famine; and it shall also come upon the land seven years.

- Elisha warns the Shunammite woman whose son he restored to flee the coming seven year famine, and she sojourns in the land of the Philistines.
- When she returns to appeal to the king for her house and land, Gehazi is at that moment telling the king how Elisha restored a dead body to life.
- The timing is of the Most High, so the king restores all that was hers and the fruits of the field from the day she left.

- Ben-hadad king of Syria, sick, sends Hazael to inquire of Elisha whether he will recover, for the word of a prophet, also called a seer, was precious in those days.
- Elisha tells Hazael the king may recover but the Most High has shown that he shall surely die, then weeps because he sees the evil Hazael will do to Israel: burning strongholds, slaying young men, and ripping up women with child.
- Elisha tells Hazael he will be king over Syria, revealing that leadership can discern spirits at work in a man that the man does not see in himself. Hazael returns and smothers the king with a wet cloth and reigns in his place.

# 2 Kings 9: Jehu Anointed and the Wicked Kings Cut Off

- His servants carry him to Jerusalem and bury him in the sepulchre of his fathers in the city of David.

**[2 Kings 9:30-37](/bible/2-kings/9#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/2-kings/9#v30)</sup> And when Jehu was come to Jezreel, Jezebel heard of it; and she painted her face, and tired her head, and looked out at a window.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/2-kings/9#v31)</sup> And as Jehu entered in at the gate, she said, Had Zimri peace, who slew his master?
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/2-kings/9#v32)</sup> And he lifted up his face to the window, and said, Who is on my side? who? And there looked out to him two or three eunuchs.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/2-kings/9#v33)</sup> And he said, Throw her down. So they threw her down: and some of her blood was sprinkled on the wall, and on the horses: and he trode her under foot.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/2-kings/9#v34)</sup> And when he was come in, he did eat and drink, and said, Go, see now this cursed woman, and bury her: for she is a king’s daughter.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/2-kings/9#v35)</sup> And they went to bury her: but they found no more of her than the skull, and the feet, and the palms of her hands.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/2-kings/9#v36)</sup> Wherefore they came again, and told him. And he said, This is the word of the Lord, which he spake by his servant Elijah the Tishbite, saying, In the portion of Jezreel shall dogs eat the flesh of Jezebel:
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/2-kings/9#v37)</sup> And the carcase of Jezebel shall be as dung upon the face of the field in the portion of Jezreel; so that they shall not say, This is Jezebel.

- Jezebel paints her face and tires her head, returning to her seductive ways as Jehu enters the gate. Jehu calls out for who is on his side, and two or three eunuchs look out.
- At Jehu's word they throw her down, her blood is sprinkled on the wall and the horses, and he treads her under foot.
- When they go to bury her they find only the skull, the feet, and the palms of her hands, fulfilling the word spoken by Elijah that dogs would eat the flesh of Jezebel and her carcass would be as dung upon the field.

# 2 Kings 10: Jehu Destroys Baal and Falls Short of the Law

Read the chapter → [2 Kings 10](/bible/2-kings/10)

**[2 Kings 10:1-11](/bible/2-kings/10#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/10#v1)</sup> And Ahab had seventy sons in Samaria. And Jehu wrote letters, and sent to Samaria, unto the rulers of Jezreel, to the elders, and to them that brought up Ahab’s children, saying,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-kings/10#v2)</sup> Now as soon as this letter cometh to you, seeing your master’s sons are with you, and there are with you chariots and horses, a fenced city also, and armour;
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/10#v3)</sup> Look even out the best and meetest of your master’s sons, and set him on his father’s throne, and fight for your master’s house.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/10#v4)</sup> But they were exceedingly afraid, and said, Behold, two kings stood not before him: how then shall we stand?
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/10#v5)</sup> And he that was over the house, and he that was over the city, the elders also, and the bringers up of the children, sent to Jehu, saying, We are thy servants, and will do all that thou shalt bid us; we will not make any king: do thou that which is good in thine eyes.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/10#v6)</sup> Then he wrote a letter the second time to them, saying, If ye be mine, and if ye will hearken unto my voice, take ye the heads of the men your master’s sons, and come to me to Jezreel by to morrow this time. Now the king’s sons, being seventy persons, were with the great men of the city, which brought them up.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/10#v7)</sup> And it came to pass, when the letter came to them, that they took the king’s sons, and slew seventy persons, and put their heads in baskets, and sent him them to Jezreel.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/10#v8)</sup> And there came a messenger, and told him, saying, They have brought the heads of the king’s sons. And he said, Lay ye them in two heaps at the entering in of the gate until the morning.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/10#v9)</sup> And it came to pass in the morning, that he went out, and stood, and said to all the people, Ye be righteous: behold, I conspired against my master, and slew him: but who slew all these?
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-kings/10#v10)</sup> Know now that there shall fall unto the earth nothing of the word of the Lord, which the Lord spake concerning the house of Ahab: for the Lord hath done that which he spake by his servant Elijah.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/10#v11)</sup> So Jehu slew all that remained of the house of Ahab in Jezreel, and all his great men, and his kinsfolks, and his priests, until he left him none remaining.

- Jehu writes to the rulers of Jezreel who kept Ahab's seventy sons, challenging them to set up a son and fight, but they are exceedingly afraid because two kings could not stand before him.
- They submit as his servants, and Jehu's second letter commands them to take the heads of Ahab's seventy sons and bring them to Jezreel by the next day.
- The heads are laid in two heaps at the gate, and Jehu slays all that remained of the house of Ahab, his great men, kinsfolk, and priests, until none remained, according to the word spoken by Elijah.

**[2 Kings 10:12-17](/bible/2-kings/10#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/2-kings/10#v12)</sup> And he arose and departed, and came to Samaria. And as he was at the shearing house in the way,
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-kings/10#v13)</sup> Jehu met with the brethren of Ahaziah king of Judah, and said, Who are ye? And they answered, We are the brethren of Ahaziah; and we go down to salute the children of the king and the children of the queen.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-kings/10#v14)</sup> And he said, Take them alive. And they took them alive, and slew them at the pit of the shearing house, even two and forty men; neither left he any of them.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-kings/10#v15)</sup> And when he was departed thence, he lighted on Jehonadab the son of Rechab coming to meet him: and he saluted him, and said to him, Is thine heart right, as my heart is with thy heart? And Jehonadab answered, It is. If it be, give me thine hand. And he gave him his hand; and he took him up to him into the chariot.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-kings/10#v16)</sup> And he said, Come with me, and see my zeal for the Lord. So they made him ride in his chariot.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-kings/10#v17)</sup> And when he came to Samaria, he slew all that remained unto Ahab in Samaria, till he had destroyed him, according to the saying of the Lord, which he spake to Elijah.

- Jehu meets the brethren of Ahaziah going to salute the king's children and has all forty-two of them slain at the pit of the shearing house, guilty by association with the house of Ahab.
- He takes up Jehonadab the son of Rechab, whose heart is right with his, into the chariot to see his zeal for the Most High.
- At Samaria Jehu slays all that remained unto Ahab, destroying him according to the saying of the Most High spoken to Elijah.

**[2 Kings 10:18-27](/bible/2-kings/10#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/2-kings/10#v18)</sup> And Jehu gathered all the people together, and said unto them, Ahab served Baal a little; but Jehu shall serve him much.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-kings/10#v19)</sup> Now therefore call unto me all the prophets of Baal, all his servants, and all his priests; let none be wanting: for I have a great sacrifice to do to Baal; whosoever shall be wanting, he shall not live. But Jehu did it in subtilty, to the intent that he might destroy the worshippers of Baal.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-kings/10#v20)</sup> And Jehu said, Proclaim a solemn assembly for Baal. And they proclaimed it.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-kings/10#v21)</sup> And Jehu sent through all Israel: and all the worshippers of Baal came, so that there was not a man left that came not. And they came into the house of Baal; and the house of Baal was full from one end to another.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-kings/10#v22)</sup> And he said unto him that was over the vestry, Bring forth vestments for all the worshippers of Baal. And he brought them forth vestments.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-kings/10#v23)</sup> And Jehu went, and Jehonadab the son of Rechab, into the house of Baal, and said unto the worshippers of Baal, Search, and look that there be here with you none of the servants of the Lord, but the worshippers of Baal only.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-kings/10#v24)</sup> And when they went in to offer sacrifices and burnt offerings, Jehu appointed fourscore men without, and said, If any of the men whom I have brought into your hands escape, he that letteth him go, his life shall be for the life of him.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-kings/10#v25)</sup> And it came to pass, as soon as he had made an end of offering the burnt offering, that Jehu said to the guard and to the captains, Go in, and slay them; let none come forth. And they smote them with the edge of the sword; and the guard and the captains cast them out, and went to the city of the house of Baal.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-kings/10#v26)</sup> And they brought forth the images out of the house of Baal, and burned them.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-kings/10#v27)</sup> And they brake down the image of Baal, and brake down the house of Baal, and made it a draught house unto this day.

- Jehu gathers all the worshippers of Baal by pretending he will serve Baal much more than Ahab did, doing it in subtilty to destroy them.
- He fills the house of Baal, brings out vestments for every worshipper, and makes sure no servant of the Most High is among them, only Baal worshippers, as with the clergy garments and idolatry seen in false worship today.
- After the sacrifice he sends in the guard to slay them all, breaks down the images and the house of Baal, and makes it a draught house, so Jehu destroyed Baal out of Israel.

    > <sup>[25](/bible/deuteronomy/7#v25)</sup> The graven images of their gods shall ye burn with fire: thou shalt not desire the silver or gold that is on them, nor take it unto thee, lest thou be snared therein: for it is an abomination to the Lord thy God.
    >
    > <sup>[26](/bible/deuteronomy/7#v26)</sup> Neither shalt thou bring an abomination into thine house, lest thou be a cursed thing like it: but thou shalt utterly detest it, and thou shalt utterly abhor it; for it is a cursed thing.

    The graven images of their gods shall be burned with fire as a cursed and abominable thing.

> <sup>[28](/bible/2-kings/10#v28)</sup> Thus Jehu destroyed Baal out of Israel.

> <sup>[29](/bible/2-kings/10#v29)</sup> Howbeit from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin, Jehu departed not from after them, to wit, the golden calves that were in Beth–el, and that were in Dan.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/2-kings/10#v30)</sup> And the Lord said unto Jehu, Because thou hast done well in executing that which is right in mine eyes, and hast done unto the house of Ahab according to all that was in mine heart, thy children of the fourth generation shall sit on the throne of Israel.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/2-kings/10#v31)</sup> But Jehu took no heed to walk in the law of the Lord God of Israel with all his heart: for he departed not from the sins of Jeroboam, which made Israel to sin.

- Though Jehu destroys Baal out of Israel, he does not depart from the golden calves that Jeroboam set up in Bethel and in Dan; hold Dan in mind for later scriptures.
- The Most High commends Jehu for executing what is right against the house of Ahab and promises his children to the fourth generation shall sit on the throne of Israel.
- Yet Jehu takes no heed to walk in the law of the Most High with all his heart, showing that a man may do great things and still turn away and stay in sin.

- Jehu's life is a warning that the walk is a marathon, not a sprint, and the difference is whether a man gets back up and repents or stays down and departs from the law.
