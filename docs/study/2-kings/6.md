**[2 Kings 6:1-7](/bible/2-kings/6#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/6#v1)</sup> And the sons of the prophets said unto Elisha, Behold now, the place where we dwell with thee is too strait for us.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-kings/6#v2)</sup> Let us go, we pray thee, unto Jordan, and take thence every man a beam, and let us make us a place there, where we may dwell. And he answered, Go ye.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/6#v3)</sup> And one said, Be content, I pray thee, and go with thy servants. And he answered, I will go.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/6#v4)</sup> So he went with them. And when they came to Jordan, they cut down wood.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/6#v5)</sup> But as one was felling a beam, the axe head fell into the water: and he cried, and said, Alas, master! for it was borrowed.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/6#v6)</sup> And the man of God said, Where fell it? And he shewed him the place. And he cut down a stick, and cast it in thither; and the iron did swim.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/6#v7)</sup> Therefore said he, Take it up to thee. And he put out his hand, and took it.

- The sons of the prophets tell Elisha the place where they dwell with him is too small, and ask to go to Jordan, take every man a beam, and make a place to dwell. Elisha agrees to go with them.
- As they fell wood by Jordan, one man's axe head falls into the water, and he cries out to Elisha, alas master, for it was borrowed. The axe was not his own.
- Elisha asks where it fell, the man shows him the place, and Elisha cuts down a stick and casts it in, and the iron swims. Though iron sinks by nature, the Lord makes it float, and Elisha tells the man to take it up, and he reaches out his hand and takes it.

**[2 Kings 6:8-12](/bible/2-kings/6#v8)**
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/6#v8)</sup> Then the king of Syria warred against Israel, and took counsel with his servants, saying, In such and such a place shall be my camp.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/6#v9)</sup> And the man of God sent unto the king of Israel, saying, Beware that thou pass not such a place; for thither the Syrians are come down.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-kings/6#v10)</sup> And the king of Israel sent to the place which the man of God told him and warned him of, and saved himself there, not once nor twice.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/6#v11)</sup> Therefore the heart of the king of Syria was sore troubled for this thing; and he called his servants, and said unto them, Will ye not shew me which of us is for the king of Israel?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-kings/6#v12)</sup> And one of his servants said, None, my lord, O king: but Elisha, the prophet that is in Israel, telleth the king of Israel the words that thou speakest in thy bedchamber.

- The king of Syria wars against Israel and takes counsel with his servants, choosing where to set his camp. But the man of God sends to the king of Israel warning him to beware of passing a certain place, because the Syrians have come down there.
- The king of Israel sends to the place Elisha warned him of and saves himself, not once nor twice, so the heart of the king of Syria is sore troubled. He calls his servants and asks which of them is for the king of Israel.
- One servant answers that none of them is, but Elisha the prophet in Israel tells the king of Israel the very words the king of Syria speaks in his bedchamber.

**[2 Kings 6:13-17](/bible/2-kings/6#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-kings/6#v13)</sup> And he said, Go and spy where he is, that I may send and fetch him. And it was told him, saying, Behold, he is in Dothan.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-kings/6#v14)</sup> Therefore sent he thither horses, and chariots, and a great host: and they came by night, and compassed the city about.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-kings/6#v15)</sup> And when the servant of the man of God was risen early, and gone forth, behold, an host compassed the city both with horses and chariots. And his servant said unto him, Alas, my master! how shall we do?
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-kings/6#v16)</sup> And he answered, Fear not: for they that be with us are more than they that be with them.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-kings/6#v17)</sup> And Elisha prayed, and said, Lord, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see. And the Lord opened the eyes of the young man; and he saw: and, behold, the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha.

- The king of Syria says, go and spy where Elisha is, that he may send and fetch him, and it is told him that Elisha is in Dothan. He sends horses, chariots, and a great host by night, and they compass the city about.
- When the servant of the man of God rises early and sees the host with horses and chariots surrounding the city, he says, alas my master, how shall we do. Elisha answers, fear not, for they that be with us are more than they that be with them.
- Elisha prays that the Lord open the young man's eyes, and the Lord opens them so he sees the mountain full of horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha. When Israel goes out to war, there is more with them than with their enemies, for the angels of the Lord are with them.

**[2 Kings 6:18-23](/bible/2-kings/6#v18)**
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-kings/6#v18)</sup> And when they came down to him, Elisha prayed unto the Lord, and said, Smite this people, I pray thee, with blindness. And he smote them with blindness according to the word of Elisha.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-kings/6#v19)</sup> And Elisha said unto them, This is not the way, neither is this the city: follow me, and I will bring you to the man whom ye seek. But he led them to Samaria.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-kings/6#v20)</sup> And it came to pass, when they were come into Samaria, that Elisha said, Lord, open the eyes of these men, that they may see. And the Lord opened their eyes, and they saw; and, behold, they were in the midst of Samaria.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-kings/6#v21)</sup> And the king of Israel said unto Elisha, when he saw them, My father, shall I smite them? shall I smite them?
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-kings/6#v22)</sup> And he answered, Thou shalt not smite them: wouldest thou smite those whom thou hast taken captive with thy sword and with thy bow? set bread and water before them, that they may eat and drink, and go to their master.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-kings/6#v23)</sup> And he prepared great provision for them: and when they had eaten and drunk, he sent them away, and they went to their master. So the bands of Syria came no more into the land of Israel.

- When the Syrians come down to Elisha, he prays that the Lord smite them with blindness, and the Lord smites them according to the word of Elisha. Elisha tells them this is not the way nor the city, and leads them to Samaria.
- In Samaria, Elisha prays that the Lord open their eyes, and they see they are in the midst of the city. The king of Israel asks whether he shall smite them, calling Elisha my father.
- Elisha answers that he shall not smite those he did not take captive by his own sword and bow, but shall set bread and water before them to eat and drink and send them back to their master. A great feast is prepared, and after they eat and drink they are sent away, and the bands of Syria come no more into the land of Israel.

**[2 Kings 6:24-31](/bible/2-kings/6#v24)**
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-kings/6#v24)</sup> And it came to pass after this, that Ben–hadad king of Syria gathered all his host, and went up, and besieged Samaria.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-kings/6#v25)</sup> And there was a great famine in Samaria: and, behold, they besieged it, until an ass’s head was sold for fourscore pieces of silver, and the fourth part of a cab of dove’s dung for five pieces of silver.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-kings/6#v26)</sup> And as the king of Israel was passing by upon the wall, there cried a woman unto him, saying, Help, my lord, O king.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-kings/6#v27)</sup> And he said, If the Lord do not help thee, whence shall I help thee? out of the barnfloor, or out of the winepress?
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/2-kings/6#v28)</sup> And the king said unto her, What aileth thee? And she answered, This woman said unto me, Give thy son, that we may eat him to day, and we will eat my son to morrow.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/2-kings/6#v29)</sup> So we boiled my son, and did eat him: and I said unto her on the next day, Give thy son, that we may eat him: and she hath hid her son.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/2-kings/6#v30)</sup> And it came to pass, when the king heard the words of the woman, that he rent his clothes; and he passed by upon the wall, and the people looked, and, behold, he had sackcloth within upon his flesh.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/2-kings/6#v31)</sup> Then he said, God do so and more also to me, if the head of Elisha the son of Shaphat shall stand on him this day.

- Afterward Ben-hadad king of Syria gathers all his host and besieges Samaria, and there is a great famine, so that an ass's head is sold for eighty pieces of silver and the fourth part of a cab of dove's dung for five pieces of silver. As the king of Israel passes by upon the wall, a woman cries to him for help.
- The king answers, if the Lord does not help thee, from where shall I help thee, out of the barnfloor or the winepress. He asks what ails her.
- The woman tells how another woman said, give thy son that we may eat him today, and we will eat my son tomorrow, so they boiled and ate her son, but the next day the other woman hid her son. When the king hears this, he rends his clothes, and the people see he has sackcloth upon his flesh, and he swears that the head of Elisha shall not stand on him this day.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:53](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v53)**
    > <sup>[53](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v53)</sup> And thou shalt eat the fruit of thine own body, the flesh of thy sons and of thy daughters, which the Lord thy God hath given thee, in the siege, and in the straitness, wherewith thine enemies shall distress thee:

    In the siege and straitness of the enemy, a disobedient Israel shall eat the fruit of their own body, the flesh of their sons and daughters, which is the curse fulfilled in this famine.

**[2 Kings 6:32-33](/bible/2-kings/6#v32)**
> <sup>[32](/bible/2-kings/6#v32)</sup> But Elisha sat in his house, and the elders sat with him; and the king sent a man from before him: but ere the messenger came to him, he said to the elders, See ye how this son of a murderer hath sent to take away mine head? look, when the messenger cometh, shut the door, and hold him fast at the door: is not the sound of his master’s feet behind him?
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/2-kings/6#v33)</sup> And while he yet talked with them, behold, the messenger came down unto him: and he said, Behold, this evil is of the Lord; what should I wait for the Lord any longer?

- Elisha sits in his house with the elders, and the king sends a man before him, but before the messenger arrives Elisha tells the elders to see how this son of a murderer has sent to take away his head, and to shut the door and hold him fast, for the sound of his master's feet is behind him.
- While Elisha yet talks with them, the messenger comes down and says, behold, this evil is of the Lord; what should I wait for the Lord any longer. The king and his messenger blame the Lord for the evil and lose patience for his deliverance.
