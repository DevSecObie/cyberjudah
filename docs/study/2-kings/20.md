**[2 Kings 20:1-7](/bible/2-kings/20#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/20#v1)</sup> In those days was Hezekiah sick unto death. And the prophet Isaiah the son of Amoz came to him, and said unto him, Thus saith the Lord, Set thine house in order; for thou shalt die, and not live.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-kings/20#v2)</sup> Then he turned his face to the wall, and prayed unto the Lord, saying,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/20#v3)</sup> I beseech thee, O Lord, remember now how I have walked before thee in truth and with a perfect heart, and have done that which is good in thy sight. And Hezekiah wept sore.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/20#v4)</sup> And it came to pass, afore Isaiah was gone out into the middle court, that the word of the Lord came to him, saying,
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/20#v5)</sup> Turn again, and tell Hezekiah the captain of my people, Thus saith the Lord, the God of David thy father, I have heard thy prayer, I have seen thy tears: behold, I will heal thee: on the third day thou shalt go up unto the house of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/20#v6)</sup> And I will add unto thy days fifteen years; and I will deliver thee and this city out of the hand of the king of Assyria; and I will defend this city for mine own sake, and for my servant David’s sake.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/20#v7)</sup> And Isaiah said, Take a lump of figs. And they took and laid it on the boil, and he recovered.

- Hezekiah falls sick unto death, and Isaiah brings the word to set his house in order, for he shall die and not live.
- Hezekiah turns his face to the wall and prays, asking the Most High to remember how he walked before Him in truth and with a perfect heart, and he weeps sore.
- Before Isaiah leaves the middle court the word returns: the Most High has heard his prayer and seen his tears, will heal him, add fifteen years, and defend the city for His own sake and for David's sake.
- Isaiah applies a lump of figs to the boil and Hezekiah recovers, showing that remedies and herbs go together with prayer, for healing is in the hand of the Most High.
  - **[Wisdom of Solomon 16:12](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/16#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/16#v12)</sup> For it was neither herb, nor mollifying plaister, that restored them to health: but thy word, O Lord, which healeth all things.

    It is neither herb nor plaster that heals, but the word of the Most High, in whose hand is our healing.

**[2 Kings 20:8-11](/bible/2-kings/20#v8)**
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/20#v8)</sup> And Hezekiah said unto Isaiah, What shall be the sign that the Lord will heal me, and that I shall go up into the house of the Lord the third day?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/20#v9)</sup> And Isaiah said, This sign shalt thou have of the Lord, that the Lord will do the thing that he hath spoken: shall the shadow go forward ten degrees, or go back ten degrees?
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-kings/20#v10)</sup> And Hezekiah answered, It is a light thing for the shadow to go down ten degrees: nay, but let the shadow return backward ten degrees.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/20#v11)</sup> And Isaiah the prophet cried unto the Lord: and he brought the shadow ten degrees backward, by which it had gone down in the dial of Ahaz.

- Hezekiah asks for a sign that the Most High will heal him and that he shall go up to the house of the Most High on the third day.
- Isaiah offers the shadow going forward ten degrees or back ten degrees, and Hezekiah answers that going forward is a light thing, but let it return backward ten degrees.
- Isaiah cries to the Most High and He brings the shadow ten degrees backward on the dial of Ahaz, reversing the natural course of the sun as proof of His power.

**[2 Kings 20:12-19](/bible/2-kings/20#v12)**
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-kings/20#v12)</sup> At that time Berodach–baladan, the son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a present unto Hezekiah: for he had heard that Hezekiah had been sick.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-kings/20#v13)</sup> And Hezekiah hearkened unto them, and shewed them all the house of his precious things, the silver, and the gold, and the spices, and the precious ointment, and all the house of his armour, and all that was found in his treasures: there was nothing in his house, nor in all his dominion, that Hezekiah shewed them not.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-kings/20#v14)</sup> Then came Isaiah the prophet unto king Hezekiah, and said unto him, What said these men? and from whence came they unto thee? And Hezekiah said, They are come from a far country, even from Babylon.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-kings/20#v15)</sup> And he said, What have they seen in thine house? And Hezekiah answered, All the things that are in mine house have they seen: there is nothing among my treasures that I have not shewed them.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-kings/20#v16)</sup> And Isaiah said unto Hezekiah, Hear the word of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-kings/20#v17)</sup> Behold, the days come, that all that is in thine house, and that which thy fathers have laid up in store unto this day, shall be carried into Babylon: nothing shall be left, saith the Lord.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-kings/20#v18)</sup> And of thy sons that shall issue from thee, which thou shalt beget, shall they take away; and they shall be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-kings/20#v19)</sup> Then said Hezekiah unto Isaiah, Good is the word of the Lord which thou hast spoken. And he said, Is it not good, if peace and truth be in my days?

- Berodach-baladan son of Baladan king of Babylon sends letters and a present to Hezekiah, having heard he had been sick; this is the introduction of Babylon, still only a province under Assyria at this time.
- Hezekiah hearkens to them and shows them all his precious things, his silver, gold, spices, ointment, and armor, holding nothing back, trusting his enemies because of the gift.
- Isaiah asks what the men saw and where they came from, then declares that all in the house shall be carried into Babylon, and Hezekiah's own sons shall be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.
- Hezekiah answers that the word of the Most High is good, content that peace and truth be in his days. This same account is given in [Isaiah 39](/bible/isaiah/39).
  - **[Ecclesiastes 7:7](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v7)</sup> Surely oppression maketh a wise man mad; and a gift destroyeth the heart.

    A gift destroyeth the heart, which is why Hezekiah let his enemies in and showed them everything he owned.

    > <sup>[4](/bible/daniel/1#v4)</sup> Children in whom was no blemish, but well favoured, and skilful in all wisdom, and cunning in knowledge, and understanding science, and such as had ability in them to stand in the king’s palace, and whom they might teach the learning and the tongue of the Chaldeans.

    Isaiah's word is fulfilled when the children of Judah, including Daniel, are made eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.

**[2 Kings 20:20-21](/bible/2-kings/20#v20)**
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-kings/20#v20)</sup> And the rest of the acts of Hezekiah, and all his might, and how he made a pool, and a conduit, and brought water into the city, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-kings/20#v21)</sup> And Hezekiah slept with his fathers: and Manasseh his son reigned in his stead.

- The rest of the acts of Hezekiah, including how he made a pool and a conduit and brought water into the city, are written in the chronicles of the kings of Judah.
- Bringing water into the city is vision and planning like no other; this is nation building.
- Hezekiah sleeps with his fathers, and Manasseh his son reigns in his stead.
