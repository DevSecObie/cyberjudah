**[2 Kings 18:1-4](/bible/2-kings/18#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/2-kings/18#v1)</sup> Now it came to pass in the third year of Hoshea son of Elah king of Israel, that Hezekiah the son of Ahaz king of Judah began to reign.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-kings/18#v2)</sup> Twenty and five years old was he when he began to reign; and he reigned twenty and nine years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name also was Abi, the daughter of Zachariah.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-kings/18#v3)</sup> And he did that which was right in the sight of the Lord, according to all that David his father did.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-kings/18#v4)</sup> He removed the high places, and brake the images, and cut down the groves, and brake in pieces the brasen serpent that Moses had made: for unto those days the children of Israel did burn incense to it: and he called it Nehushtan.

- Hezekiah son of Ahaz begins to reign over Judah in the third year of Hoshea, the last king of Israel before the captivity.
- He does that which is right according to all that David his father did, removing the high places, breaking the images, and cutting down the groves.
- He breaks in pieces the brazen serpent Moses made in the wilderness, which had stood from that time and was now being burned incense to, and he calls it Nehushtan.

**[2 Kings 18:5-8](/bible/2-kings/18#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-kings/18#v5)</sup> He trusted in the Lord God of Israel; so that after him was none like him among all the kings of Judah, nor any that were before him.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-kings/18#v6)</sup> For he clave to the Lord, and departed not from following him, but kept his commandments, which the Lord commanded Moses.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-kings/18#v7)</sup> And the Lord was with him; and he prospered whithersoever he went forth: and he rebelled against the king of Assyria, and served him not.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/18#v8)</sup> He smote the Philistines, even unto Gaza, and the borders thereof, from the tower of the watchmen to the fenced city.

- Hezekiah trusts in the Most High so that none among the kings of Judah, before or after, was like him.
- He clave to the Most High, kept the commandments, and the Most High was with him so that he prospered.
- Prospering here does not mean a mansion or luxury, but the blessing that comes from keeping and doing the commandments.
- He rebels against the king of Assyria and smites the Philistines even unto Gaza.
  - **[Deuteronomy 29:9](/bible/deuteronomy/29#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/deuteronomy/29#v9)</sup> Keep therefore the words of this covenant, and do them, that ye may prosper in all that ye do.

    Keep the words of the covenant and do them, that ye may prosper in all that ye do.

**[2 Kings 18:9-12](/bible/2-kings/18#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/18#v9)</sup> And it came to pass in the fourth year of king Hezekiah, which was the seventh year of Hoshea son of Elah king of Israel, that Shalmaneser king of Assyria came up against Samaria, and besieged it.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-kings/18#v10)</sup> And at the end of three years they took it: even in the sixth year of Hezekiah, that is the ninth year of Hoshea king of Israel, Samaria was taken.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/18#v11)</sup> And the king of Assyria did carry away Israel unto Assyria, and put them in Halah and in Habor by the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes:
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-kings/18#v12)</sup> Because they obeyed not the voice of the Lord their God, but transgressed his covenant, and all that Moses the servant of the Lord commanded, and would not hear them, nor do them.

- In Hezekiah's fourth year Shalmaneser besieges Samaria, and after three years, in Hezekiah's sixth year, Samaria is taken.
- Israel was not removed all at once but carried away in stages into Assyria.
- The reason given again is that they obeyed not the voice of the Most High but transgressed His covenant and all Moses commanded.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)</sup> But it shall come to pass, if thou wilt not hearken unto the voice of the Lord thy God, to observe to do all his commandments and his statutes which I command thee this day; that all these curses shall come upon thee, and overtake thee:

    The captivity comes because Israel would not hearken to the voice of the Most High.

**[2 Kings 18:13-16](/bible/2-kings/18#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-kings/18#v13)</sup> Now in the fourteenth year of king Hezekiah did Sennacherib king of Assyria come up against all the fenced cities of Judah, and took them.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-kings/18#v14)</sup> And Hezekiah king of Judah sent to the king of Assyria to Lachish, saying, I have offended; return from me: that which thou puttest on me will I bear. And the king of Assyria appointed unto Hezekiah king of Judah three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-kings/18#v15)</sup> And Hezekiah gave him all the silver that was found in the house of the Lord, and in the treasures of the king’s house.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-kings/18#v16)</sup> At that time did Hezekiah cut off the gold from the doors of the temple of the Lord, and from the pillars which Hezekiah king of Judah had overlaid, and gave it to the king of Assyria.

- Sennacherib king of Assyria comes up against the fenced cities of Judah and takes them.
- Hezekiah sends to Lakish acknowledging offense and agrees to bear whatever tribute is laid on him.
- Assyria appoints three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold, so Hezekiah gives all the silver of the house of the Most High and even cuts the gold from the temple doors.

**[2 Kings 18:17-25](/bible/2-kings/18#v17)**
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-kings/18#v17)</sup> And the king of Assyria sent Tartan and Rabsaris and Rab–shakeh from Lachish to king Hezekiah with a great host against Jerusalem. And they went up and came to Jerusalem. And when they were come up, they came and stood by the conduit of the upper pool, which is in the highway of the fuller’s field.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-kings/18#v18)</sup> And when they had called to the king, there came out to them Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, which was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah the son of Asaph the recorder.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-kings/18#v19)</sup> And Rab–shakeh said unto them, Speak ye now to Hezekiah, Thus saith the great king, the king of Assyria, What confidence is this wherein thou trustest?
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-kings/18#v20)</sup> Thou sayest, (but they are but vain words,) I have counsel and strength for the war. Now on whom dost thou trust, that thou rebellest against me?
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-kings/18#v21)</sup> Now, behold, thou trustest upon the staff of this bruised reed, even upon Egypt, on which if a man lean, it will go into his hand, and pierce it: so is Pharaoh king of Egypt unto all that trust on him.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-kings/18#v22)</sup> But if ye say unto me, We trust in the Lord our God: is not that he, whose high places and whose altars Hezekiah hath taken away, and hath said to Judah and Jerusalem, Ye shall worship before this altar in Jerusalem?
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-kings/18#v23)</sup> Now therefore, I pray thee, give pledges to my lord the king of Assyria, and I will deliver thee two thousand horses, if thou be able on thy part to set riders upon them.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-kings/18#v24)</sup> How then wilt thou turn away the face of one captain of the least of my master’s servants, and put thy trust on Egypt for chariots and for horsemen?
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-kings/18#v25)</sup> Am I now come up without the Lord against this place to destroy it? The Lord said to me, Go up against this land, and destroy it.

- The king of Assyria sends Tartan, Rabsaris, and Rabshakeh with a great host to Jerusalem, and they stand by the conduit of the upper pool.
- Rabshakeh challenges Hezekiah's confidence, mocking his trust and calling Egypt a bruised reed that pierces the hand of any who lean on it.
- He argues that because Hezekiah removed the high places, even Judah's own worship is now confined to one altar, twisting the reform into a weakness.
- He boasts that Assyria came up against the land by the word of the Most High Himself, claiming divine backing to destroy it.

**[2 Kings 18:26-37](/bible/2-kings/18#v26)**
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-kings/18#v26)</sup> Then said Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, and Shebna, and Joah, unto Rab–shakeh, Speak, I pray thee, to thy servants in the Syrian language; for we understand it: and talk not with us in the Jews’ language in the ears of the people that are on the wall.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-kings/18#v27)</sup> But Rab–shakeh said unto them, Hath my master sent me to thy master, and to thee, to speak these words? hath he not sent me to the men which sit on the wall, that they may eat their own dung, and drink their own piss with you?
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/2-kings/18#v28)</sup> Then Rab–shakeh stood and cried with a loud voice in the Jews’ language, and spake, saying, Hear the word of the great king, the king of Assyria:
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/2-kings/18#v29)</sup> Thus saith the king, Let not Hezekiah deceive you: for he shall not be able to deliver you out of his hand:
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/2-kings/18#v30)</sup> Neither let Hezekiah make you trust in the Lord, saying, The Lord will surely deliver us, and this city shall not be delivered into the hand of the king of Assyria.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/2-kings/18#v31)</sup> Hearken not to Hezekiah: for thus saith the king of Assyria, Make an agreement with me by a present, and come out to me, and then eat ye every man of his own vine, and every one of his fig tree, and drink ye every one the waters of his cistern:
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/2-kings/18#v32)</sup> Until I come and take you away to a land like your own land, a land of corn and wine, a land of bread and vineyards, a land of oil olive and of honey, that ye may live, and not die: and hearken not unto Hezekiah, when he persuadeth you, saying, The Lord will deliver us.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/2-kings/18#v33)</sup> Hath any of the gods of the nations delivered at all his land out of the hand of the king of Assyria?
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/2-kings/18#v34)</sup> Where are the gods of Hamath, and of Arpad? where are the gods of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivah? have they delivered Samaria out of mine hand?
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/2-kings/18#v35)</sup> Who are they among all the gods of the countries, that have delivered their country out of mine hand, that the Lord should deliver Jerusalem out of mine hand?
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/2-kings/18#v36)</sup> But the people held their peace, and answered him not a word: for the king’s commandment was, saying, Answer him not.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/2-kings/18#v37)</sup> Then came Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, which was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah the son of Asaph the recorder, to Hezekiah with their clothes rent, and told him the words of Rab–shakeh.

- Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah ask Rabshakeh to speak in the Syrian language so the people on the wall will not understand, but he refuses.
- Rabshakeh cries aloud in the Jews' language, warning the people not to let Hezekiah deceive them or make them trust in the Most High.
- He promises the people their own vines, fig trees, and waters if they surrender, then a land like their own, seeking to turn them from their king.
- He asks whether the gods of Hamath, Arpad, Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivah delivered their lands, claiming no god can deliver Jerusalem out of his hand.
- The people hold their peace and answer not a word, for Hezekiah had commanded silence.
- Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah come to Hezekiah with their clothes rent and tell him the words of Rabshakeh.
