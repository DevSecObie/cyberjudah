**[2 Chronicles 12:1](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v1)</sup> And it came to pass, when Rehoboam had established the kingdom, and had strengthened himself, he forsook the law of the Lord, and all Israel with him.

- When Rehoboam establishes the kingdom and strengthens himself, he forsakes the law of the Lord, and all Israel with him.
- One who leads goes astray and carries many others with him; this is why the people must be examples to one another in righteousness, of one mind and one spirit in the rebuilding of the kingdom.

**[2 Chronicles 12:2-4](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v2)**
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v2)</sup> And it came to pass, that in the fifth year of king Rehoboam Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, because they had transgressed against the Lord,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v3)</sup> With twelve hundred chariots, and threescore thousand horsemen: and the people were without number that came with him out of Egypt; the Lubims, the Sukkiims, and the Ethiopians.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v4)</sup> And he took the fenced cities which pertained to Judah, and came to Jerusalem.

- In the fifth year of Rehoboam, Shishak king of Egypt comes up against Jerusalem because Rehoboam transgressed against the Lord.
- Shishak comes with twelve hundred chariots, threescore thousand horsemen, and people without number out of Egypt, the Lubims, the Sukkiims, and the Ethiopians.
- He takes the fenced cities of Judah and comes to Jerusalem; the invasion is the direct result of forsaking the law.

**[2 Chronicles 12:5-6](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v5)</sup> Then came Shemaiah the prophet to Rehoboam, and to the princes of Judah, that were gathered together to Jerusalem because of Shishak, and said unto them, Thus saith the Lord, Ye have forsaken me, and therefore have I also left you in the hand of Shishak.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v6)</sup> Whereupon the princes of Israel and the king humbled themselves; and they said, The Lord is righteous.

- Shemaiah the prophet comes to Rehoboam and the princes of Judah gathered at Jerusalem and declares the word of the Lord: because they have forsaken him, he has left them in the hand of Shishak.
- The princes and the king humble themselves and say the Lord is righteous.
- When correction comes, the right response is to confess that the Most High is righteous in what he does, acknowledging the punishment as just.

**[2 Chronicles 12:7-8](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v7)</sup> And when the Lord saw that they humbled themselves, the word of the Lord came to Shemaiah, saying, They have humbled themselves; therefore I will not destroy them, but I will grant them some deliverance; and my wrath shall not be poured out upon Jerusalem by the hand of Shishak.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v8)</sup> Nevertheless they shall be his servants; that they may know my service, and the service of the kingdoms of the countries.

- When the Lord sees that they humble themselves, his word comes to Shemaiah saying he will not destroy them but grant them some deliverance, and his wrath will not be poured on Jerusalem by the hand of Shishak.
- Nevertheless they will be Shishak's servants, that they may know the difference between serving the Most High and serving the kingdoms of the countries.
- The people today must likewise humble themselves, relearn to obey the commandments, and accept their condition as righteous judgment from the hand of the Most High.
  - **[Leviticus 26:40-42](/bible/leviticus/26#v40)**
    > <sup>[40](/bible/leviticus/26#v40)</sup> If they shall confess their iniquity, and the iniquity of their fathers, with their trespass which they trespassed against me, and that also they have walked contrary unto me;
    >
    > <sup>[41](/bible/leviticus/26#v41)</sup> And that I also have walked contrary unto them, and have brought them into the land of their enemies; if then their uncircumcised hearts be humbled, and they then accept of the punishment of their iniquity:
    >
    > <sup>[42](/bible/leviticus/26#v42)</sup> Then will I remember my covenant with Jacob, and also my covenant with Isaac, and also my covenant with Abraham will I remember; and I will remember the land.

    If they confess their iniquity and their uncircumcised hearts are humbled and they accept the punishment, then the Most High remembers his covenant; humbling brings deliverance.

**[2 Chronicles 12:9-11](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v9)</sup> So Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, and took away the treasures of the house of the Lord, and the treasures of the king’s house; he took all: he carried away also the shields of gold which Solomon had made.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v10)</sup> Instead of which king Rehoboam made shields of brass, and committed them to the hands of the chief of the guard, that kept the entrance of the king’s house.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v11)</sup> And when the king entered into the house of the Lord, the guard came and fetched them, and brought them again into the guard chamber.

- Shishak takes away the treasures of the house of the Lord and of the king's house, carrying off all the shields of gold Solomon had made.
- Rehoboam makes shields of brass in their place and commits them to the guard that keeps the entrance of the king's house.
- Whenever the king enters the house of the Lord the guard fetches the shields and returns them to the guard chamber.

**[2 Chronicles 12:12](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v12)**
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v12)</sup> And when he humbled himself, the wrath of the Lord turned from him, that he would not destroy him altogether: and also in Judah things went well.

- Because Rehoboam humbles himself, the wrath of the Lord turns from him so that he is not utterly destroyed, and in Judah things go well.
- Deliverance and the destruction of enemies come to the people when they humble themselves before the Most High.

**[2 Chronicles 12:13-14](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v13)</sup> So king Rehoboam strengthened himself in Jerusalem, and reigned: for Rehoboam was one and forty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city which the Lord had chosen out of all the tribes of Israel, to put his name there. And his mother’s name was Naamah an Ammonitess.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v14)</sup> And he did evil, because he prepared not his heart to seek the Lord.

- Rehoboam strengthens himself in Jerusalem and reigns; he is forty and one years old when he begins to reign and reigns seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city the Lord chose to put his name there.
- His mother's name is Naamah an Ammonitess.
- He does evil because he prepares not his heart to seek the Lord; the failure is a heart never set on the Most High.

**[2 Chronicles 12:15-16](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v15)**
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v15)</sup> Now the acts of Rehoboam, first and last, are they not written in the book of Shemaiah the prophet, and of Iddo the seer concerning genealogies? And there were wars between Rehoboam and Jeroboam continually.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-chronicles/12#v16)</sup> And Rehoboam slept with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David: and Abijah his son reigned in his stead.

- The acts of Rehoboam are written in the book of Shemaiah the prophet and of Iddo the seer concerning genealogies, and there were wars between Rehoboam and Jeroboam continually.
- Rehoboam sleeps with his fathers, is buried in the city of David, and his son Abijah reigns in his place.
- The account then turns to Abijah's rule over Judah in the next chapter, continuing this compressed history that is told more fully in 1 and 2 Kings.
