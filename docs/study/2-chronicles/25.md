## Introduction

This session covers 2 Chronicles 25-28, four kings of Judah in a row: Amaziah, Uzziah, Jotham, and Ahaz. The thread that runs through all four is that the king in Judah was held under very strict orders. He was to write the law, meditate in it, and take counsel from the prophets and the priests, because the king did not know everything on his own. As long as these men sought the Most High they prospered, and the moment they became lifted up, took bad counsel, or ran to other nations and their gods, judgment came. In these chapters the northern kingdom of Israel and the southern kingdom of Judah are treated as two separate nations at war, brother against brother, which is the same division we still see among the people today. Prosperity comes from the Most High, and no nation, no army, and no idol can deliver a man from the curses of God when he departs from the commandments.

**[2 Chronicles 25:1-4](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v1)</sup> Amaziah was twenty and five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned twenty and nine years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Jehoaddan of Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v2)</sup> And he did that which was right in the sight of the Lord, but not with a perfect heart.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v3)</sup> Now it came to pass, when the kingdom was established to him, that he slew his servants that had killed the king his father.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v4)</sup> But he slew not their children, but did as it is written in the law in the book of Moses, where the Lord commanded, saying, The fathers shall not die for the children, neither shall the children die for the fathers, but every man shall die for his own sin.

- Amaziah began to reign at twenty and five years old and reigned twenty and nine years in Jerusalem. He did that which was right in the sight of the Most High, but not with a perfect heart. As soon as the kingdom was established he slew the servants that had killed his father the king.
- He slew the murderers but he did not slay their children, because it is written in the law of Moses that the fathers shall not die for the children, nor the children for the fathers, but every man shall die for his own sin. This is a foundational law: a man answers for his own sin, not for another man's.

>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v6)</sup> He hired also an hundred thousand mighty men of valour out of Israel for an hundred talents of silver.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v7)</sup> But there came a man of God to him, saying, O king, let not the army of Israel go with thee; for the Lord is not with Israel, to wit, with all the children of Ephraim.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v8)</sup> But if thou wilt go, do it, be strong for the battle: God shall make thee fall before the enemy: for God hath power to help, and to cast down.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v9)</sup> And Amaziah said to the man of God, But what shall we do for the hundred talents which I have given to the army of Israel? And the man of God answered, The Lord is able to give thee much more than this.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v10)</sup> Then Amaziah separated them, to wit, the army that was come to him out of Ephraim, to go home again: wherefore their anger was greatly kindled against Judah, and they returned home in great anger.

- Amaziah gathered Judah and Benjamin, numbered them from twenty years old and upward, and found three hundred thousand choice men able to go to war. He also hired a hundred thousand mighty men out of Israel for a hundred talents of silver.
- A man of the Most High came and told him not to let the army of Israel go with him, for the Most High was not with Israel, with all the children of Ephraim. Because of the sin of Ephraim the Most High had cast them off and was not dealing with them, so their help would only bring defeat.
- When Amaziah asked what to do about the hundred talents he had already paid, the prophet told him the Most High is able to give him much more than that. Amaziah obeyed, separated the army of Ephraim to go home, and they went home in great anger.

**[2 Chronicles 25:11-16](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v11)</sup> And Amaziah strengthened himself, and led forth his people, and went to the valley of salt, and smote of the children of Seir ten thousand.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v12)</sup> And other ten thousand left alive did the children of Judah carry away captive, and brought them unto the top of the rock, and cast them down from the top of the rock, that they all were broken in pieces.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v13)</sup> But the soldiers of the army which Amaziah sent back, that they should not go with him to battle, fell upon the cities of Judah, from Samaria even unto Beth–horon, and smote three thousand of them, and took much spoil.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v14)</sup> Now it came to pass, after that Amaziah was come from the slaughter of the Edomites, that he brought the gods of the children of Seir, and set them up to be his gods, and bowed down himself before them, and burned incense unto them.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v15)</sup> Wherefore the anger of the Lord was kindled against Amaziah, and he sent unto him a prophet, which said unto him, Why hast thou sought after the gods of the people, which could not deliver their own people out of thine hand?
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-chronicles/25#v16)</sup> And it came to pass, as he talked with him, that the king said unto him, Art thou made of the king’s counsel? forbear; why shouldest thou be smitten? Then the prophet forbare, and said, I know that God hath determined to destroy thee, because thou hast done this, and hast not hearkened unto my counsel.

- Amaziah strengthened himself, led his people to the valley of salt, and smote ten thousand of the children of Seir, and cast down another ten thousand from the top of the rock. But he brought back the gods of the children of Seir, set them up to be his own gods, bowed to them, and burned incense to them.
- This is the divided heart in action. The Most High gave him the victory, and he responded by grabbing the idols of the people he had just defeated. So the anger of the Most High was kindled against him.
- The Most High sent a prophet who asked why he sought after gods that could not even deliver their own people out of his hand. This is why counsel is important. The prophet was trying to counsel Amaziah to destroy the idols, and instead Amaziah threatened him and told him to forbear, be quiet. The prophet forbore, but said the Most High had determined to destroy him because he had done this and had not hearkened to counsel.

He took all the gold and silver and the vessels found in the house of the Most High, and the treasures of the king's house, and hostages, and returned to Samaria. The two kingdoms had real animosity, calling each other another nation, brother stealing the vessels out of his own brother's temple.
