**[Song of Solomon 5:1](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v1)</sup> I am come into my garden, my sister, my spouse: I have gathered my myrrh with my spice; I have eaten my honeycomb with my honey; I have drunk my wine with my milk: eat, O friends; drink, yea, drink abundantly, O beloved.

- Her beloved comes into his garden, his sister, his spouse, gathering his myrrh with his spice and eating his honeycomb with his honey and drinking his wine with his milk, and he bids his friends eat and drink.

**[Song of Solomon 5:2-6](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v2)</sup> I sleep, but my heart waketh: it is the voice of my beloved that knocketh, saying, Open to me, my sister, my love, my dove, my undefiled: for my head is filled with dew, and my locks with the drops of the night.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v3)</sup> I have put off my coat; how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet; how shall I defile them?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v4)</sup> My beloved put in his hand by the hole of the door, and my bowels were moved for him.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v5)</sup> I rose up to open to my beloved; and my hands dropped with myrrh, and my fingers with sweet smelling myrrh, upon the handles of the lock.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v6)</sup> I opened to my beloved; but my beloved had withdrawn himself, and was gone: my soul failed when he spake: I sought him, but I could not find him; I called him, but he gave me no answer.

- She sleeps but her heart wakes; it is the voice of her beloved that knocks, calling her his sister, his love, his dove, his undefiled, for his head is filled with dew and his locks with the drops of the night.
- She has put off her coat and washed her feet; her beloved puts his hand by the hole of the door and her bowels are moved for him, but when she rises to open, her beloved has withdrawn himself and is gone, and she seeks him but cannot find him.

**[Song of Solomon 5:7-8](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v7)</sup> The watchmen that went about the city found me, they smote me, they wounded me; the keepers of the walls took away my veil from me.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v8)</sup> I charge you, O daughters of Jerusalem, if ye find my beloved, that ye tell him, that I am sick of love.

- The watchmen that go about the city find her, smite her, wound her, and the keepers of the walls take away her veil.
- She charges the daughters of Jerusalem that if they find her beloved they tell him she is sick of love.

**[Song of Solomon 5:9-11](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v9)</sup> What is thy beloved more than another beloved, O thou fairest among women? what is thy beloved more than another beloved, that thou dost so charge us?
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v10)</sup> My beloved is white and ruddy, the chiefest among ten thousand.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v11)</sup> His head is as the most fine gold, his locks are bushy, and black as a raven.

- The daughters ask what her beloved is more than another beloved, that she so charges them; here the teacher stops to answer the Christian argument that says these verses prove the beloved is white, that is, a white man.
- White means undefiled and unspotted, clean and clear, not white skin; there is nobody who is literally white, and the scriptures say Esau, whom Christianity tries to attribute the Israelites to, is the red man.
- Ruddy does not mean red either; it means a healthy, youthful appearance with clean, clear, beautiful skin, the same word used of young King David when he was called ruddy and goodly to look to and the Most High said arise and anoint him.
- His head is as the most fine gold, and his locks are bushy and black as a raven; this is the same as reading of the Christ having woolly hair and the Most High having hair of pure wool.
  - **[Psalms 51:7](/bible/psalms/51#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/51#v7)</sup> Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean: wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.

    Purge me and I shall be clean, wash me and I shall be whiter than snow, showing white in the scriptures means made clean and undefiled.
  - **[Genesis 25:25](/bible/genesis/25#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/genesis/25#v25)</sup> And the first came out red, all over like an hairy garment; and they called his name Esau.

    Esau came out red, all over like a hairy garment, so the red man is Esau, not the Israelites.
  - **[1 Samuel 16:12-13](/bible/1-samuel/16#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/1-samuel/16#v12)</sup> And he sent, and brought him in. Now he was ruddy, and withal of a beautiful countenance, and goodly to look to. And the Lord said, Arise, anoint him: for this is he.
    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/1-samuel/16#v13)</sup> Then Samuel took the horn of oil, and anointed him in the midst of his brethren: and the Spirit of the Lord came upon David from that day forward. So Samuel rose up, and went to Ramah.

    Young David was ruddy and of a beautiful countenance and goodly to look to, and the Most High said arise and anoint him, showing ruddy is a healthy youthful appearance.
  - **[Daniel 7:9](/bible/daniel/7#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/daniel/7#v9)</sup> I beheld till the thrones were cast down, and the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment was white as snow, and the hair of his head like the pure wool: his throne was like the fiery flame, and his wheels as burning fire.

    The Ancient of days sits with the hair of his head like the pure wool, matching the beloved's bushy hair.
  - **[Revelation 1:14](/bible/revelation/1#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/revelation/1#v14)</sup> His head and his hairs were white like wool, as white as snow; and his eyes were as a flame of fire;

    His head and his hairs are white like wool, showing the Christ has woolly hair like the beloved described here.
  - **[Judith 10:3](/bible/judith/10#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/judith/10#v3)</sup> And pulled off the sackcloth which she had on, and put off the garments of her widowhood, and washed her body all over with water, and anointed herself with precious ointment, and braided the hair of her head, and put on a tire upon it, and put on her garments of gladness, wherewith she was clad during the life of Manasses her husband.

    Judith braided the hair of her head, and this is why it matters for the sisters to return to their natural bushy hair and stop putting chemicals in it, laying down the fashions of the world.

**[Song of Solomon 5:12-16](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v12)</sup> His eyes are as the eyes of doves by the rivers of waters, washed with milk, and fitly set.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v13)</sup> His cheeks are as a bed of spices, as sweet flowers: his lips like lilies, dropping sweet smelling myrrh.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v14)</sup> His hands are as gold rings set with the beryl: his belly is as bright ivory overlaid with sapphires.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v15)</sup> His legs are as pillars of marble, set upon sockets of fine gold: his countenance is as Lebanon, excellent as the cedars.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v16)</sup> His mouth is most sweet: yea, he is altogether lovely. This is my beloved, and this is my friend, O daughters of Jerusalem.

- His eyes are as the eyes of doves by the rivers of waters, washed with milk and fitly set; his cheeks are as a bed of spices, his lips like lilies dropping sweet smelling myrrh.
- His hands are as gold rings set with the beryl, his belly as bright ivory overlaid with sapphires, his legs as pillars of marble, his countenance as Lebanon, excellent as the cedars, his mouth most sweet; he is altogether lovely, and this is her beloved and her friend, O daughters of Jerusalem.
