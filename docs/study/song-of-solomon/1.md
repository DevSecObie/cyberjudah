Read the chapter → [Song of Solomon 1](/bible/song-of-solomon/1)

This session closes with [Song of Solomon 1](/bible/song-of-solomon/1), read straight through as a single parable. The teacher stresses that this is a heavy book, not one to dig through casually in a four-chapters-a-day reading. In the questions afterward he settles the authorship, explaining that Solomon wrote it, and defines the plant names used for the perfumes and fragrances in the chapter.

**[Song of Solomon 1:1-4](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v1)</sup> The song of songs, which is Solomon’s.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v2)</sup> Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth: for thy love is better than wine.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v3)</sup> Because of the savour of thy good ointments thy name is as ointment poured forth, therefore do the virgins love thee.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v4)</sup> Draw me, we will run after thee: the king hath brought me into his chambers: we will be glad and rejoice in thee, we will remember thy love more than wine: the upright love thee.

- The book opens, "The song of songs, which is Solomon's." Solomon is the author, not a woman, though that is the doctrine some try to push. The plain words of verse one say these songs belong to Solomon.
- Solomon was a poet. The Most High gave him wisdom and largeness of heart, and he spoke three thousand proverbs and his songs were a thousand and five, which shows plainly who wrote this book.
- The bride longs for the king's love, calling it better than wine; the savor of his good ointments makes his name as ointment poured forth, and therefore the virgins love him. Drawn into the king's chambers, they are glad and rejoice in him.
  - **[1 Kings 4:29-32](/bible/1-kings/4#v29)**
    > <sup>[29](/bible/1-kings/4#v29)</sup> And God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding exceeding much, and largeness of heart, even as the sand that is on the sea shore.
    >
    > <sup>[30](/bible/1-kings/4#v30)</sup> And Solomon’s wisdom excelled the wisdom of all the children of the east country, and all the wisdom of Egypt.
    >
    > <sup>[31](/bible/1-kings/4#v31)</sup> For he was wiser than all men; than Ethan the Ezrahite, and Heman, and Chalcol, and Darda, the sons of Mahol: and his fame was in all nations round about.
    >
    > <sup>[32](/bible/1-kings/4#v32)</sup> And he spake three thousand proverbs: and his songs were a thousand and five.

    Solomon's wisdom and his three thousand proverbs and thousand and five songs mark him as the writer of this book.
  - **[Isaiah 34:16](/bible/isaiah/34#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/34#v16)</sup> Seek ye out of the book of the Lord, and read: no one of these shall fail, none shall want her mate: for my mouth it hath commanded, and his spirit it hath gathered them.

    Seek out of the book of the Most High and read, for the scriptures confirm one another and none fails.

**[Song of Solomon 1:5-6](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v5)</sup> I am black, but comely, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, as the tents of Kedar, as the curtains of Solomon.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v6)</sup> Look not upon me, because I am black, because the sun hath looked upon me: my mother’s children were angry with me; they made me the keeper of the vineyards; but mine own vineyard have I not kept.

- The bride says, "I am black, but comely, O ye daughters of Jerusalem," likening herself to the tents of Kedar and the curtains of Solomon.
- She says, look not upon me because I am black, because the sun has looked upon me; her mother's children were angry with her and made her keeper of the vineyards, but her own vineyard she has not kept.

**[Song of Solomon 1:7-8](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v7)</sup> Tell me, O thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou makest thy flock to rest at noon: for why should I be as one that turneth aside by the flocks of thy companions?
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v8)</sup> If thou know not, O thou fairest among women, go thy way forth by the footsteps of the flock, and feed thy kids beside the shepherds’ tents.

- She asks the one her soul loves where he feeds his flock and where he makes it rest at noon, so she need not wander as one who turns aside by the flocks of his companions.
- The answer: if she does not know, then O fairest among women, go by the footsteps of the flock and feed her kids beside the shepherds' tents.

**[Song of Solomon 1:9-11](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v9)</sup> I have compared thee, O my love, to a company of horses in Pharaoh’s chariots.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v10)</sup> Thy cheeks are comely with rows of jewels, thy neck with chains of gold.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v11)</sup> We will make thee borders of gold with studs of silver.

- He compares his love to a company of horses in Pharaoh's chariots, and praises her cheeks comely with rows of jewels and her neck with chains of gold.
- They will make her borders of gold with studs of silver.

**[Song of Solomon 1:12-14](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v12)</sup> While the king sitteth at his table, my spikenard sendeth forth the smell thereof.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v13)</sup> A bundle of myrrh is my wellbeloved unto me; he shall lie all night betwixt my breasts.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v14)</sup> My beloved is unto me as a cluster of camphire in the vineyards of En–gedi.

- While the king sits at his table, her spikenard sends forth its smell; her wellbeloved is a bundle of myrrh that shall lie all night between her breasts, and a cluster of camphire in the vineyards of Engedi.
- Spikenard is a flower or plant used to make oils and perfumes; camphire is a fragrant henna flower. The people back then used no artificial smells but took everything straight from the earth, and those are the best smells.

**[Song of Solomon 1:15-17](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v15)</sup> Behold, thou art fair, my love; behold, thou art fair; thou hast doves’ eyes.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v16)</sup> Behold, thou art fair, my beloved, yea, pleasant: also our bed is green.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v17)</sup> The beams of our house are cedar, and our rafters of fir.

- He tells her, behold you are fair, my love, you have doves' eyes; and she answers that he is fair and pleasant, and their bed is green.
- The beams of their house are cedar and their rafters of fir.
