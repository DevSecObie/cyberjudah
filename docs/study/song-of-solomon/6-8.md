Read the chapter → [Song of Solomon 6](/bible/song-of-solomon/6)

This session reads [Song of Solomon 6](/bible/song-of-solomon/6) through 8 straight through. Solomon writes in allegory, metaphor, and parable, so the chapters are read quickly and then opened by precept. Without the precepts the book cannot be understood: the vineyard named all through it is the house of Israel, and the breasts stand for understanding, for meditating on the milk, which is the laws of the Most High. Read this way, Song of Solomon is not about romance in a bed but about the bond between the Most High and His people, and about staying in the scriptures every day to understand, not just to knock out four chapters.

**[Song of Solomon 6:1-3](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v1)</sup> Whither is thy beloved gone, O thou fairest among women? whither is thy beloved turned aside? that we may seek him with thee.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v2)</sup> My beloved is gone down into his garden, to the beds of spices, to feed in the gardens, and to gather lilies.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v3)</sup> I am my beloved’s, and my beloved is mine: he feedeth among the lilies.

- The daughters ask where her beloved has gone so they may seek him with her, and she answers that he has gone down to his garden, to the beds of spices, to feed in the gardens and gather lilies. Verse one is the Shulamite's commitment being questioned.
- She declares, "I am my beloved's, and my beloved is mine: he feedeth among the lilies."
- The lily is the Most High's favorite plant, so the beloved feeding among the lilies is deliberate.

**[Song of Solomon 6:4-7](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v4)</sup> Thou art beautiful, O my love, as Tirzah, comely as Jerusalem, terrible as an army with banners.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v5)</sup> Turn away thine eyes from me, for they have overcome me: thy hair is as a flock of goats that appear from Gilead.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v6)</sup> Thy teeth are as a flock of sheep which go up from the washing, whereof every one beareth twins, and there is not one barren among them.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v7)</sup> As a piece of a pomegranate are thy temples within thy locks.

- The beloved praises her as beautiful as Jerusalem and terrible as an army with banners, so much that her eyes have overcome him and he asks her to turn them away.
- Her hair is a flock of goats from Gilead, her teeth a flock of sheep that bear twins with none barren, and her temples like a piece of pomegranate within her locks.

**[Song of Solomon 6:8-10](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v8)</sup> There are threescore queens, and fourscore concubines, and virgins without number.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v9)</sup> My dove, my undefiled is but one; she is the only one of her mother, she is the choice one of her that bare her. The daughters saw her, and blessed her; yea, the queens and the concubines, and they praised her.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v10)</sup> Who is she that looketh forth as the morning, fair as the moon, clear as the sun, and terrible as an army with banners?

- There are threescore queens, fourscore concubines, and virgins without number, but his dove, his undefiled, is but one, the only one of her mother and the choice one of her that bore her.
- The daughters saw her and blessed her, and the queens and concubines praised her, asking who she is that looks forth as the morning, fair as the moon, clear as the sun, and terrible as an army with banners.
- This reads like poetry. Solomon was a poet with skill long before Shakespeare or Langston Hughes or any of the celebrated poets, for the forefathers were masters of the craft.

**[Song of Solomon 6:11-13](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v11)</sup> I went down into the garden of nuts to see the fruits of the valley, and to see whether the vine flourished, and the pomegranates budded.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v12)</sup> Or ever I was aware, my soul made me like the chariots of Amminadib.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/song-of-solomon/6#v13)</sup> Return, return, O Shulamite; return, return, that we may look upon thee. What will ye see in the Shulamite? As it were the company of two armies.

- He went down into the garden of nuts to see the fruits of the valley, whether the vine flourished and the pomegranates budded, and his soul made him like the chariots of Amminadib.
- The call comes, "Return, return, O Shulamite; return, return, that we may look upon thee," and the answer asks what they will see in the Shulamite, "as it were the company of two armies."

# Song of Solomon 7: The Beauty of the Beloved

Read the chapter → [Song of Solomon 7](/bible/song-of-solomon/7)

**[Song of Solomon 7:1-4](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v1)</sup> How beautiful are thy feet with shoes, O prince’s daughter! the joints of thy thighs are like jewels, the work of the hands of a cunning workman.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v2)</sup> Thy navel is like a round goblet, which wanteth not liquor: thy belly is like an heap of wheat set about with lilies.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v3)</sup> Thy two breasts are like two young roes that are twins.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v4)</sup> Thy neck is as a tower of ivory; thine eyes like the fishpools in Heshbon, by the gate of Bath–rabbim: thy nose is as the tower of Lebanon which looketh toward Damascus.

- Her feet are beautiful with shoes, O prince's daughter, the joints of her thighs like jewels, her navel like a round goblet, and her belly like a heap of wheat set about with lilies.
- Her two breasts are like two young roes that are twins, her neck a tower of ivory, her eyes like the fishpools in Heshbon, and her nose as the tower of Lebanon looking toward Damascus.

**[Song of Solomon 7:5-9](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v5)</sup> Thine head upon thee is like Carmel, and the hair of thine head like purple; the king is held in the galleries.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v6)</sup> How fair and how pleasant art thou, O love, for delights!
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v7)</sup> This thy stature is like to a palm tree, and thy breasts to clusters of grapes.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v8)</sup> I said, I will go up to the palm tree, I will take hold of the boughs thereof: now also thy breasts shall be as clusters of the vine, and the smell of thy nose like apples;
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v9)</sup> And the roof of thy mouth like the best wine for my beloved, that goeth down sweetly, causing the lips of those that are asleep to speak.

- Her head is like Carmel and her hair like purple, the king held in the galleries; he calls her fair and pleasant, her stature like a palm tree and her breasts like clusters of grapes.
- He will go up to the palm tree and take hold of its boughs, and her breasts shall be as clusters of the vine, the smell of her nose like apples, and the roof of her mouth like the best wine that goes down sweetly.

**[Song of Solomon 7:10-13](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v10)</sup> I am my beloved’s, and his desire is toward me.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v11)</sup> Come, my beloved, let us go forth into the field; let us lodge in the villages.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v12)</sup> Let us get up early to the vineyards; let us see if the vine flourish, whether the tender grape appear, and the pomegranates bud forth: there will I give thee my loves.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/song-of-solomon/7#v13)</sup> The mandrakes give a smell, and at our gates are all manner of pleasant fruits, new and old, which I have laid up for thee, O my beloved.

- She says, "I am my beloved's, and his desire is toward me," and calls him to go forth into the field, to lodge in the villages, and to rise early to the vineyards to see whether the vine flourishes.
- There she will give him her loves; the mandrakes give a smell, and at their gates are all manner of pleasant fruits, new and old, laid up for her beloved.

# Song of Solomon 8: Love Strong as Death, the Vineyard of Israel

Read the chapter → [Song of Solomon 8](/bible/song-of-solomon/8)

**[Song of Solomon 8:1-4](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v1)</sup> O that thou wert as my brother, that sucked the breasts of my mother! when I should find thee without, I would kiss thee; yea, I should not be despised.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v2)</sup> I would lead thee, and bring thee into my mother’s house, who would instruct me: I would cause thee to drink of spiced wine of the juice of my pomegranate.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v3)</sup> His left hand should be under my head, and his right hand should embrace me.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v4)</sup> I charge you, O daughters of Jerusalem, that ye stir not up, nor awake my love, until he please.

- She wishes he were as her brother that nursed at her mother's breast, so that finding him outside she could kiss him and not be despised, and she would lead him into her mother's house to be instructed and give him spiced wine of her pomegranate.
- His left hand is under her head and his right hand embraces her, and she charges the daughters of Jerusalem not to stir up nor awake her love until he please.

**[Song of Solomon 8:5-7](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v5)</sup> Who is this that cometh up from the wilderness, leaning upon her beloved? I raised thee up under the apple tree: there thy mother brought thee forth: there she brought thee forth that bare thee.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v6)</sup> Set me as a seal upon thine heart, as a seal upon thine arm: for love is strong as death; jealousy is cruel as the grave: the coals thereof are coals of fire, which hath a most vehement flame.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v7)</sup> Many waters cannot quench love, neither can the floods drown it: if a man would give all the substance of his house for love, it would utterly be contemned.

- The question comes, "Who is this that cometh up from the wilderness, leaning upon her beloved?" He raised her up under the apple tree where her mother brought her forth.
- She asks to be set as a seal upon his heart and arm, "for love is strong as death; jealousy is cruel as the grave," for many waters cannot quench love, and if a man gave all the substance of his house for love it would utterly be contemned.

**[Song of Solomon 8:8-10](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v8)</sup> We have a little sister, and she hath no breasts: what shall we do for our sister in the day when she shall be spoken for?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v9)</sup> If she be a wall, we will build upon her a palace of silver: and if she be a door, we will inclose her with boards of cedar.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v10)</sup> I am a wall, and my breasts like towers: then was I in his eyes as one that found favour.

- "We have a little sister, and she hath no breasts": if she is a wall they will build a palace of silver upon her, and if a door they will enclose her with boards of cedar, and she answers, "I am a wall, and my breasts like towers."
- Throughout the book the breasts are not about cuddling in bed. The breasts stand for understanding, and lying all night betwixt the breasts, as in [Song of Solomon 1:13](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v13), means meditating on the milk, which is the laws of the Most High.
- The milk is the simplest, easiest things to be understood: keeping the laws, not harboring hatred, taking care of one another, being charitable and forgiving. This is what the new convert meditates on and grows in before reaching for the deep parables.
- The prophets spoke in similitudes, so not everyone will understand; the Most High shows His secrets through precepts, line upon line, a little here and a little there, to those He chooses to teach.
  - **[Hosea 12:10](/bible/hosea/12#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/hosea/12#v10)</sup> I have also spoken by the prophets, and I have multiplied visions, and used similitudes, by the ministry of the prophets.

    The Most High spoke by the prophets, Solomon and Isaiah among them, and used similitudes, which are illustrated stories, parables, and dark sayings.
  - **[Job 11:6](/bible/job/11#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/job/11#v6)</sup> And that he would shew thee the secrets of wisdom, that they are double to that which is! Know therefore that God exacteth of thee less than thine iniquity deserveth.

    The secrets of wisdom are double to that which is read, so the plain reading is only half of it.
  - **[Isaiah 28:9](/bible/isaiah/28#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/28#v9)</sup> Whom shall he teach knowledge? and whom shall he make to understand doctrine? them that are weaned from the milk, and drawn from the breasts.

    He teaches knowledge and doctrine to them that are weaned from the milk and drawn from the breasts.
  - **[Proverbs 4:2](/bible/proverbs/4#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/4#v2)</sup> For I give you good doctrine, forsake ye not my law.

    The good doctrine is His law, which He tells us not to forsake, so all denominations and philosophies are set aside for it.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 19:19](/bible/sirach/19#v19)**
    > <sup>[19](/bible/sirach/19#v19)</sup> The knowledge of the commandments of the Lord is the doctrine of life: and they that do things that please him shall receive the fruit of the tree of immortality.

    The doctrine of life is knowing and keeping the commandments of the Most High.
  - **[1 Peter 2:2](/bible/1-peter/2#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/1-peter/2#v2)</sup> As newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word, that ye may grow thereby:

    As newborn babes we are to desire the sincere milk of the word, the easy things to be understood, that we may grow thereby.

**[Song of Solomon 8:11-14](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v11)</sup> Solomon had a vineyard at Baal–hamon; he let out the vineyard unto keepers; every one for the fruit thereof was to bring a thousand pieces of silver.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v12)</sup> My vineyard, which is mine, is before me: thou, O Solomon, must have a thousand, and those that keep the fruit thereof two hundred.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v13)</sup> Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the companions hearken to thy voice: cause me to hear it.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/song-of-solomon/8#v14)</sup> Make haste, my beloved, and be thou like to a roe or to a young hart upon the mountains of spices.

- Solomon had a vineyard at Baalhamon and let it out to keepers, every one to bring a thousand pieces of silver for the fruit, and his own vineyard is before him.
- The vineyard repeated through the whole book is Israel; wherever Solomon names the vineyard, the fruit, or the garden, he is speaking of the house of Israel.
- The book closes, "Make haste, my beloved, and be thou like to a roe or to a young hart upon the mountains of spices."
  - **[Isaiah 5:7](/bible/isaiah/5#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/5#v7)</sup> For the vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah his pleasant plant: and he looked for judgment, but behold oppression; for righteousness, but behold a cry.

    The vineyard of the Most High of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah His pleasant plant.
