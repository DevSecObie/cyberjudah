Read the chapter → [Song of Solomon 2](/bible/song-of-solomon/2)

This session reads [Song of Solomon 2](/bible/song-of-solomon/2) through 5 straight through as parables between the beloved and the bride. The teacher says plainly that this is not a strong book for him, so he mainly reads and does not try to break down every parable, urging that questions be sent to the captains and that whoever does not understand should lay his hand upon his mouth. The one place he stops to teach is chapter 5, where he answers the Christian claim that "my beloved is white and ruddy" makes the beloved a white man, showing from the scriptures that white means undefiled and clean, ruddy means a healthy youthful appearance, that Esau is the red man, and that the beloved's bushy black hair as a raven matches the woolly hair of the Christ and the Most High.

**[Song of Solomon 2:1-3](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v1)</sup> I am the rose of Sharon, and the lily of the valleys.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v2)</sup> As the lily among thorns, so is my love among the daughters.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v3)</sup> As the apple tree among the trees of the wood, so is my beloved among the sons. I sat down under his shadow with great delight, and his fruit was sweet to my taste.

- The bride calls herself the rose of Sharon and the lily of the valleys, and among the daughters she is as the lily among thorns.
- She answers that as the apple tree is among the trees of the wood, so is her beloved among the sons; she sat under his shadow with great delight and his fruit was sweet to her taste.

**[Song of Solomon 2:4-7](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v4)</sup> He brought me to the banqueting house, and his banner over me was love.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v5)</sup> Stay me with flagons, comfort me with apples: for I am sick of love.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v6)</sup> His left hand is under my head, and his right hand doth embrace me.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v7)</sup> I charge you, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes, and by the hinds of the field, that ye stir not up, nor awake my love, till he please.

- He brought her to the banqueting house and his banner over her was love; she asks to be stayed with flagons and comforted with apples, for she is sick of love.
- His left hand is under her head and his right hand embraces her; she charges the daughters of Jerusalem by the roes and the hinds of the field not to stir up nor awake her love until he please.

**[Song of Solomon 2:8-9](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v8)</sup> The voice of my beloved! behold, he cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping upon the hills.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v9)</sup> My beloved is like a roe or a young hart: behold, he standeth behind our wall, he looketh forth at the windows, shewing himself through the lattice.

- The voice of her beloved comes leaping upon the mountains and skipping upon the hills, like a roe or a young hart.
- He stands behind their wall, looking forth at the windows and showing himself through the lattice.

**[Song of Solomon 2:10-13](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v10)</sup> My beloved spake, and said unto me, Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v11)</sup> For, lo, the winter is past, the rain is over and gone;
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v12)</sup> The flowers appear on the earth; the time of the singing of birds is come, and the voice of the turtle is heard in our land;
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v13)</sup> The fig tree putteth forth her green figs, and the vines with the tender grape give a good smell. Arise, my love, my fair one, and come away.

- Her beloved speaks and calls her to rise up and come away, for the winter is past, the rain is over and gone.
- The flowers appear on the earth, the time of the singing of birds is come, the voice of the turtle is heard, the fig tree puts forth her green figs and the vines with the tender grape give a good smell.

**[Song of Solomon 2:14-15](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v14)</sup> O my dove, that art in the clefts of the rock, in the secret places of the stairs, let me see thy countenance, let me hear thy voice; for sweet is thy voice, and thy countenance is comely.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v15)</sup> Take us the foxes, the little foxes, that spoil the vines: for our vines have tender grapes.

- He calls her his dove in the clefts of the rock and the secret places of the stairs, asking to see her countenance and hear her voice, for sweet is her voice and her countenance comely.
- He calls to take the little foxes that spoil the vines, for their vines have tender grapes.

**[Song of Solomon 2:16-17](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v16)</sup> My beloved is mine, and I am his: he feedeth among the lilies.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/song-of-solomon/2#v17)</sup> Until the day break, and the shadows flee away, turn, my beloved, and be thou like a roe or a young hart upon the mountains of Bether.

- She declares her beloved is hers and she is his; he feeds among the lilies until the day break and the shadows flee away.
- She bids him turn and be like a roe or a young hart upon the mountains of Bether.

# Song of Solomon 3: Seeking Him Whom My Soul Loveth

Read the chapter → [Song of Solomon 3](/bible/song-of-solomon/3)

**[Song of Solomon 3:1-3](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v1)</sup> By night on my bed I sought him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, but I found him not.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v2)</sup> I will rise now, and go about the city in the streets, and in the broad ways I will seek him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, but I found him not.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v3)</sup> The watchmen that go about the city found me: to whom I said, Saw ye him whom my soul loveth?

- By night on her bed she seeks him whom her soul loves but does not find him, so she rises to go about the city, through the streets and the broad ways, still seeking him.
- The watchmen that go about the city find her, and she asks them whether they saw him whom her soul loves.

**[Song of Solomon 3:4-5](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v4)</sup> It was but a little that I passed from them, but I found him whom my soul loveth: I held him, and would not let him go, until I had brought him into my mother’s house, and into the chamber of her that conceived me.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v5)</sup> I charge you, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes, and by the hinds of the field, that ye stir not up, nor awake my love, till he please.

- A little past the watchmen she finds him whom her soul loves, and she holds him and will not let him go until she brings him into her mother's house.
- Again she charges the daughters of Jerusalem by the roes and the hinds of the field not to stir up nor awake her love until he please.

**[Song of Solomon 3:6-8](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v6)</sup> Who is this that cometh out of the wilderness like pillars of smoke, perfumed with myrrh and frankincense, with all powders of the merchant?
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v7)</sup> Behold his bed, which is Solomon’s; threescore valiant men are about it, of the valiant of Israel.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v8)</sup> They all hold swords, being expert in war: every man hath his sword upon his thigh because of fear in the night.

- She asks who this is that comes out of the wilderness like pillars of smoke, perfumed with myrrh and frankincense and all powders of the merchant.
- She points to Solomon's bed, with threescore valiant men of Israel about it, every man with his sword upon his thigh because of fear in the night.

**[Song of Solomon 3:9-11](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v9)</sup> King Solomon made himself a chariot of the wood of Lebanon.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v10)</sup> He made the pillars thereof of silver, the bottom thereof of gold, the covering of it of purple, the midst thereof being paved with love, for the daughters of Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/song-of-solomon/3#v11)</sup> Go forth, O ye daughters of Zion, and behold king Solomon with the crown wherewith his mother crowned him in the day of his espousals, and in the day of the gladness of his heart.

- King Solomon made himself a chariot of the wood of Lebanon, its pillars of silver, its bottom of gold, its covering of purple, paved with love for the daughters of Jerusalem.
- She calls the daughters of Zion to go forth and behold King Solomon with the crown his mother crowned him with in the day of his espousals, the day of the gladness of his heart.

# Song of Solomon 4: Thou Art All Fair, My Love

Read the chapter → [Song of Solomon 4](/bible/song-of-solomon/4)

**[Song of Solomon 4:1-3](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v1)</sup> Behold, thou art fair, my love; behold, thou art fair; thou hast doves’ eyes within thy locks: thy hair is as a flock of goats, that appear from mount Gilead.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v2)</sup> Thy teeth are like a flock of sheep that are even shorn, which came up from the washing; whereof every one bear twins, and none is barren among them.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v3)</sup> Thy lips are like a thread of scarlet, and thy speech is comely: thy temples are like a piece of a pomegranate within thy locks.

- He praises her as fair, with doves' eyes within her locks, her hair as a flock of goats, her teeth as a flock of sheep even shorn, every one bearing twins.
- Her lips are like a thread of scarlet and her speech comely; her temples are like a piece of a pomegranate within her locks.

**[Song of Solomon 4:4-5](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v4)</sup> Thy neck is like the tower of David builded for an armoury, whereon there hang a thousand bucklers, all shields of mighty men.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v5)</sup> Thy two breasts are like two young roes that are twins, which feed among the lilies.

- Her neck is like the tower of David built for an armory, whereon hang a thousand bucklers, all shields of mighty men.
- Her two breasts are like two young roes that are twins, feeding among the lilies until the day break and the shadows flee away.

**[Song of Solomon 4:6-7](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v6)</sup> Until the day break, and the shadows flee away, I will get me to the mountain of myrrh, and to the hill of frankincense.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v7)</sup> Thou art all fair, my love; there is no spot in thee.

- He will get to the mountain of myrrh and to the hill of frankincense.
- He declares she is all fair, and there is no spot in her.

**[Song of Solomon 4:8-9](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v8)</sup> Come with me from Lebanon, my spouse, with me from Lebanon: look from the top of Amana, from the top of Shenir and Hermon, from the lions’ dens, from the mountains of the leopards.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v9)</sup> Thou hast ravished my heart, my sister, my spouse; thou hast ravished my heart with one of thine eyes, with one chain of thy neck.

- He calls his spouse to come with him from Lebanon, from the top of Amana, Shenir and Hermon, from the lions' dens and the mountains of the leopards.
- She has ravished his heart, his sister, his spouse, with one of her eyes and with one chain of her neck.

**[Song of Solomon 4:10-11](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v10)</sup> How fair is thy love, my sister, my spouse! how much better is thy love than wine! and the smell of thine ointments than all spices!
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v11)</sup> Thy lips, O my spouse, drop as the honeycomb: honey and milk are under thy tongue; and the smell of thy garments is like the smell of Lebanon.

- How fair is her love, better than wine, and the smell of her ointments better than all spices.
- Her lips drop as the honeycomb, honey and milk are under her tongue, and the smell of her garments is like the smell of Lebanon.

**[Song of Solomon 4:12-16](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v12)</sup> A garden inclosed is my sister, my spouse; a spring shut up, a fountain sealed.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v13)</sup> Thy plants are an orchard of pomegranates, with pleasant fruits; camphire, with spikenard,
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v14)</sup> Spikenard and saffron; calamus and cinnamon, with all trees of frankincense; myrrh and aloes, with all the chief spices:
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v15)</sup> A fountain of gardens, a well of living waters, and streams from Lebanon.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/song-of-solomon/4#v16)</sup> Awake, O north wind; and come, thou south; blow upon my garden, that the spices thereof may flow out. Let my beloved come into his garden, and eat his pleasant fruits.

- She is a garden enclosed, a spring shut up, a fountain sealed; her plants are an orchard of pomegranates with pleasant fruits, camphire, spikenard, saffron, calamus, cinnamon, frankincense, myrrh and aloes.
- She is a fountain of gardens, a well of living waters and streams from Lebanon; she calls the north wind and the south to blow upon her garden and bids her beloved come into his garden and eat his pleasant fruits.

# Song of Solomon 5: My Beloved Is White and Ruddy

Read the chapter → [Song of Solomon 5](/bible/song-of-solomon/5)

**[Song of Solomon 5:1](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v1)</sup> I am come into my garden, my sister, my spouse: I have gathered my myrrh with my spice; I have eaten my honeycomb with my honey; I have drunk my wine with my milk: eat, O friends; drink, yea, drink abundantly, O beloved.

- Her beloved comes into his garden, his sister, his spouse, gathering his myrrh with his spice and eating his honeycomb with his honey and drinking his wine with his milk, and he bids his friends eat and drink.

**[Song of Solomon 5:2-6](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v2)</sup> I sleep, but my heart waketh: it is the voice of my beloved that knocketh, saying, Open to me, my sister, my love, my dove, my undefiled: for my head is filled with dew, and my locks with the drops of the night.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v3)</sup> I have put off my coat; how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet; how shall I defile them?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v4)</sup> My beloved put in his hand by the hole of the door, and my bowels were moved for him.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v5)</sup> I rose up to open to my beloved; and my hands dropped with myrrh, and my fingers with sweet smelling myrrh, upon the handles of the lock.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v6)</sup> I opened to my beloved; but my beloved had withdrawn himself, and was gone: my soul failed when he spake: I sought him, but I could not find him; I called him, but he gave me no answer.

- She sleeps but her heart wakes; it is the voice of her beloved that knocks, calling her his sister, his love, his dove, his undefiled, for his head is filled with dew and his locks with the drops of the night.
- She has put off her coat and washed her feet; her beloved puts his hand by the hole of the door and her bowels are moved for him, but when she rises to open, her beloved has withdrawn himself and is gone, and she seeks him but cannot find him.

**[Song of Solomon 5:7-8](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v7)</sup> The watchmen that went about the city found me, they smote me, they wounded me; the keepers of the walls took away my veil from me.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v8)</sup> I charge you, O daughters of Jerusalem, if ye find my beloved, that ye tell him, that I am sick of love.

- The watchmen that go about the city find her, smite her, wound her, and the keepers of the walls take away her veil.
- She charges the daughters of Jerusalem that if they find her beloved they tell him she is sick of love.

**[Song of Solomon 5:9-11](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v9)</sup> What is thy beloved more than another beloved, O thou fairest among women? what is thy beloved more than another beloved, that thou dost so charge us?
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v10)</sup> My beloved is white and ruddy, the chiefest among ten thousand.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v11)</sup> His head is as the most fine gold, his locks are bushy, and black as a raven.

- The daughters ask what her beloved is more than another beloved, that she so charges them; here the teacher stops to answer the Christian argument that says these verses prove the beloved is white, that is, a white man.
- White means undefiled and unspotted, clean and clear, not white skin; there is nobody who is literally white, and the scriptures say Esau, whom Christianity tries to attribute the Israelites to, is the red man.
- Ruddy does not mean red either; it means a healthy, youthful appearance with clean, clear, beautiful skin, the same word used of young King David when he was called ruddy and goodly to look to and the Most High said arise and anoint him.
- His head is as the most fine gold, and his locks are bushy and black as a raven; this is the same as reading of the Christ having woolly hair and the Most High having hair of pure wool.
  - **[Psalms 51:7](/bible/psalms/51#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/51#v7)</sup> Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean: wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.

    Purge me and I shall be clean, wash me and I shall be whiter than snow, showing white in the scriptures means made clean and undefiled.
  - **[Genesis 25:25](/bible/genesis/25#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/genesis/25#v25)</sup> And the first came out red, all over like an hairy garment; and they called his name Esau.

    Esau came out red, all over like a hairy garment, so the red man is Esau, not the Israelites.
  - **[1 Samuel 16:12-13](/bible/1-samuel/16#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/1-samuel/16#v12)</sup> And he sent, and brought him in. Now he was ruddy, and withal of a beautiful countenance, and goodly to look to. And the Lord said, Arise, anoint him: for this is he.
    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/1-samuel/16#v13)</sup> Then Samuel took the horn of oil, and anointed him in the midst of his brethren: and the Spirit of the Lord came upon David from that day forward. So Samuel rose up, and went to Ramah.

    Young David was ruddy and of a beautiful countenance and goodly to look to, and the Most High said arise and anoint him, showing ruddy is a healthy youthful appearance.
  - **[Daniel 7:9](/bible/daniel/7#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/daniel/7#v9)</sup> I beheld till the thrones were cast down, and the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment was white as snow, and the hair of his head like the pure wool: his throne was like the fiery flame, and his wheels as burning fire.

    The Ancient of days sits with the hair of his head like the pure wool, matching the beloved's bushy hair.
  - **[Revelation 1:14](/bible/revelation/1#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/revelation/1#v14)</sup> His head and his hairs were white like wool, as white as snow; and his eyes were as a flame of fire;

    His head and his hairs are white like wool, showing the Christ has woolly hair like the beloved described here.
  - **[Judith 10:3](/bible/judith/10#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/judith/10#v3)</sup> And pulled off the sackcloth which she had on, and put off the garments of her widowhood, and washed her body all over with water, and anointed herself with precious ointment, and braided the hair of her head, and put on a tire upon it, and put on her garments of gladness, wherewith she was clad during the life of Manasses her husband.

    Judith braided the hair of her head, and this is why it matters for the sisters to return to their natural bushy hair and stop putting chemicals in it, laying down the fashions of the world.

**[Song of Solomon 5:12-16](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v12)</sup> His eyes are as the eyes of doves by the rivers of waters, washed with milk, and fitly set.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v13)</sup> His cheeks are as a bed of spices, as sweet flowers: his lips like lilies, dropping sweet smelling myrrh.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v14)</sup> His hands are as gold rings set with the beryl: his belly is as bright ivory overlaid with sapphires.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v15)</sup> His legs are as pillars of marble, set upon sockets of fine gold: his countenance is as Lebanon, excellent as the cedars.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/song-of-solomon/5#v16)</sup> His mouth is most sweet: yea, he is altogether lovely. This is my beloved, and this is my friend, O daughters of Jerusalem.

- His eyes are as the eyes of doves by the rivers of waters, washed with milk and fitly set; his cheeks are as a bed of spices, his lips like lilies dropping sweet smelling myrrh.
- His hands are as gold rings set with the beryl, his belly as bright ivory overlaid with sapphires, his legs as pillars of marble, his countenance as Lebanon, excellent as the cedars, his mouth most sweet; he is altogether lovely, and this is her beloved and her friend, O daughters of Jerusalem.
