## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 1 Corinthians 13 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-13, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[1 Corinthians 13:1-3](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v1)</sup> Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, and have not charity, I am become as sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v2)</sup> And though I have the gift of prophecy, and understand all mysteries, and all knowledge; and though I have all faith, so that I could remove mountains, and have not charity, I am nothing.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v3)</sup> And though I bestow all my goods to feed the poor, and though I give my body to be burned, and have not charity, it profiteth me nothing.

- “Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, and have not charity, I am become as sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal.” Some people are very good orators. They can speak, they have great retention of scripture, they can explain all mysteries; but if they don't have the spirit of charity, they are just making noise. “And though I have the gift of prophecy, and understand all mysteries, and all knowledge; and though I have all faith, so that I could remove mountains, and have not charity, I am nothing.” Give all your goods to feed the poor and your body to be burned, “and have not charity, it profiteth me nothing.”
- To speak with the tongues of men is to edify and bring forth the people. A man can teach and speak with tongues and gather the people, and they come in believing; and then he has no charity.

**[1 Corinthians 13:4-6](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v4)</sup> Charity suffereth long, and is kind; charity envieth not; charity vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up,
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v5)</sup> Doth not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own, is not easily provoked, thinketh no evil;
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v6)</sup> Rejoiceth not in iniquity, but rejoiceth in the truth;

- “Charity suffereth long, and is kind; charity envieth not; charity vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up, Doth not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own, is not easily provoked, thinketh no evil”. If you have the spirit of charity, you are going to suffer long, be kind, not envy, not vaunt yourself. As read in Matthew, we are meant to be ministers to the people, not to be ministered to.
- Charity “Rejoiceth not in iniquity, but rejoiceth in the truth”: it does not rejoice in sin. We rejoice in the application of God's laws. Leadership loves to come into a school and see it in order, the men in order and learning the scriptures. Sit down and ask a man who has been around nine years some questions, and he can't break down St. John 3:16 or Galatians 3? Then the leaders there are not doing their job; they are not preparing these men.
- This is how to fight the root of bitterness. “Charity suffereth long”: if you really love your brother, your wife, your spouse, you are going to suffer long, endure the bull crap; you are patient. “And is kind; charity envieth not; charity vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up.”
- “Is not easily provoked, thinketh no evil.” When Satan puts evil thoughts in your mind about someone else, charity is not going to think evil. Say, no, I am not going to think evil about that person; when that thought comes, shake it off. Repeat it over and over, even if people think you are crazy: charity thinketh no evil. Somebody said something? That person probably didn’t mean it like that. Charity thinketh no evil.

**[1 Corinthians 13:7](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v7)</sup> Beareth all things, believeth all things, hopeth all things, endureth all things.

- “Beareth all things, believeth all things, hopeth all things, endureth all things.” That is what charity does.

**[1 Corinthians 13:8](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v8)</sup> Charity never faileth: but whether there be prophecies, they shall fail; whether there be tongues, they shall cease; whether there be knowledge, it shall vanish away.

- “Charity never faileth: but whether there be prophecies, they shall fail; whether there be tongues, they shall cease; whether there be knowledge, it shall vanish away.” This is about the man. Whatever gift was given to him, the spirit of prophecy, tongues, knowledge, if he doesn't have charity he will fail. Say he used the gift of tongues in different countries to bring out the word, but he didn't deal right with the people: eventually the Lord will get him out of the way, and all the gifts he had are for nothing, because he didn't hold charity. The knowledge he was given is taken away from him, and he is out the door.
- It is like Christ telling the Pharisees they should have done the one and not left the other undone: good in the other things, but failing in charity, which a leader obviously has to have. Regardless of his gifts, he becomes a hypocrite. The people gather in, but without charity you lack meekness, you're not longsuffering, you're not kind, and those things will run the people away. That is why Paul said to restore such an one in the spirit of meekness. You can teach all these scriptures and all these breakdowns with a photographic memory, but if you don't know how to deal right with the people, the people will not stay.

**[1 Corinthians 13:9](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v9)</sup> For we know in part, and we prophesy in part.

- “For we know in part, and we prophesy in part.” You don't know as much as you think you know. God has given all of us a dispensation of this, and we have what is needful for salvation, but there are parts of this Bible we don't fully understand, not concerning salvation. It is to humble us: we are not as great as we think we are. And this is Paul, to whom God showed incredible revelations he didn't show many of the other apostles.

**[1 Corinthians 13:10-11](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v10)</sup> But when that which is perfect is come, then that which is in part shall be done away.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v11)</sup> When I was a child, I spake as a child, I understood as a child, I thought as a child: but when I became a man, I put away childish things.

- “But when that which is perfect is come”: Christ. “Then that which is in part shall be done away.”
- “When I was a child, I spake as a child, I understood as a child, I thought as a child: but when I became a man, I put away childish things.” The childish things are the things just talked about. We put them away and are renewed in the mind.
- “When I was a child, I spake as a child”: Paul is giving a metaphor of the old covenant and the New Covenant. The old covenant of animal sacrifice is described as him being a child, and the man is the New Covenant under Christ. “But when I became a man, I put away childish things”: put away the old covenant, the understanding of the old covenant.
- “When I was a child, I spake as a child, I understood as a child, I thought as a child: but when I became a man, I put away childish things.” We have to continue to grow. And when we tap into that understanding, we have to have the mindset that we’re not going to let anything or anyone jeopardize us from losing that potential. It’s not about feelings or emotions; it’s not about pleasures or self-satisfaction. Make sure once you tap in, you don’t let that thing go.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Corinthians 16:13](/bible/1-corinthians/16#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/1-corinthians/16#v13)</sup> Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong.

    “Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong.” The quit right there means to convey or behave. So it says behave like men, and be strong, with courage, without fear.
  - **[1 Peter 1:13](/bible/1-peter/1#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/1-peter/1#v13)</sup> Wherefore gird up the loins of your mind, be sober, and hope to the end for the grace that is to be brought unto you at the revelation of Jesus Christ;

    “Wherefore gird up the loins of your mind”: of your mind. It’s not carnal. In this world we think being a man is the brother that can turn up, go from zero to 100 and put hands on you. God is telling us to be strong men according to the Bible, and a strong man is all about his mind, his spirit. He can control his spirit, no matter where people are trying to sway you, whether to the left or to the right.

**[1 Corinthians 13:12](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v12)</sup> For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known.

- “For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known.” When we are with Christ, he is going to make this clear to us.
- “Now I know in part”: that’s why Christ said hold fast to what you have until I come.
- Back to the veil. “For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face.” Christ's body was a veil, our flesh is a veil, this body is a tabernacle we groan in while we wait for the redemption of our bodies. We see through this veil. Then we will see face to face, when we have our real body and are no longer obstructed by the groanings of this physical body.
- The only way to have clarity in this body is to feed your spirit every day in some way: audio classes, a few verses, even when the day was too intense for your four chapters. Four chapters is the standard, but whatever happens, engage with God's word every day. We don't perceive everything perfectly: the flesh obscures, distracts, and presses down the soul.

  Precepts:
  - **[Wisdom of Solomon 7:24-26](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/7#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/7#v24)</sup> For wisdom is more moving than any motion: she passeth and goeth through all things by reason of her pureness.
    >
    > <sup>[25](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/7#v25)</sup> For she is the breath of the power of God, and a pure influence flowing from the glory of the Almighty: therefore can no defiled thing fall into her.
    >
    > <sup>[26](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/7#v26)</sup> For she is the brightness of the everlasting light, the unspotted mirror of the power of God, and the image of his goodness.

    What is the glass? “For wisdom is more moving than any motion: she passeth and goeth through all things by reason of her pureness. For she is the breath of the power of God, and a pure influence flowing from the glory of the Almighty: therefore can no defiled thing fall into her.”
  - **[Ephesians 4:18](/bible/ephesians/4#v18)**
    > <sup>[18](/bible/ephesians/4#v18)</sup> Having the understanding darkened, being alienated from the life of God through the ignorance that is in them, because of the blindness of their heart:

    “Having the understanding darkened, being alienated from the life of God through the ignorance that is in them”. That is what it means to see “through a glass, darkly”: we don't have the full understanding of all these scriptures. The glass is the mirror, the unspotted image of the power of God, and we look into it with the understanding darkened, until then, face to face.

**[1 Corinthians 13:13](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/1-corinthians/13#v13)</sup> And now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these is charity.

- “And now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these is charity.” Charity is the greatest because that is what keeps the body growing. It keeps people coming in, because they see how you move, how you conduct yourself, how you speak.
- “But the greatest of these is charity”: love your brother as you love yourself, because Christ is in your brother.
