## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 1 Corinthians 7 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 2-3, 5, 7-12, 14-15, 28-29, 33, 35, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

- “Nevertheless, to avoid fornication”: to avoid sexual sins, “let every man have his own wife, and let every woman have her own husband.” That’s thus saith the Lord. Some Israelite congregations reject that, and they’re rejecting Christ the King.

King Solomon said to be always ravished with her love. A lack of sex in a relationship causes contention: why is he angry? He never got a release. Why is she angry? The water's backed up.

Write it down, brothers and sisters. Study it, acknowledge it, and fight.

**[1 Corinthians 7:5](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v5)</sup> Defraud ye not one the other, except it be with consent for a time, that ye may give yourselves to fasting and prayer; and come together again, that Satan tempt you not for your incontinency.

Sometimes she might not be feeling well, and if a sister truly is not feeling well, brothers understand that. But some sisters fake it, and that’s evil.

Now you become bitter, you ain’t getting none, and now you on the job looking at another woman. Or the sister on the job looking at another man, because, brother, you ain’t doing what you’re supposed to do as a husband.

**[1 Corinthians 7:7](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v7)</sup> For I would that all men were even as I myself. But every man hath his proper gift of God, one after this manner, and another after that.

- “For I would that all men were even as I myself.” This is Paul: I am so dedicated to the work, I don’t want to deal with women. “But every man hath his proper gift of God, one after this manner, and another after that.” Everyone has his own stamina; some can withhold from that because they want to dedicate their whole work to the Lord. He that has a wife has to care for his wife, but he that is by himself can put all his diligence into the Lord. One has this talent, another that.
- Asked whether it is a sin to take care of yourself when you don’t have a wife. “For I would that all men were even as I myself”: Paul had no wife, so he is saying, I wish that all you men could contain yourselves as me. “But every man hath his proper gift of God”: it’s a gift when you could serve the Lord and not desire companionship. “One after this manner, and another after that”: we all have different gifts. It’s a gift if you could be like, you know what, it’s not that big a deal to me, I’m just going to serve God by myself.

**[1 Corinthians 7:8](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v8)</sup> I say therefore to the unmarried and widows, It is good for them if they abide even as I.

- “It is good for them if they abide even as I”: good, because they would not have to deal with the cares of a spouse, male or female. But not everybody has that strength, and we understand that.
- “I say therefore to the unmarried”: if you are unmarried, “and widows”: you’ve lost your spouse. “It is good for them if they abide even as I”, because Paul was by himself to do the work of God. It’ll be easier to serve God by yourself.

**[1 Corinthians 7:9](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v9)</sup> But if they cannot contain, let them marry: for it is better to marry than to burn.

- “But if they cannot contain, let them marry: for it is better to marry than to burn.” Everybody has heard this one. It means that if a man cannot contain his sexual desires, it is better for him to go through the process of proving and then get married, than to end up burning in lust and dying in the lake of fire. It runs two ways: burning with desire, and the ultimate burn of destruction that comes when fornication comes.
- We will all say the right thing, but often the mind is somewhere else: I ain’t waiting years, look at that sister. Get married, but understand what it means. Make sure all the steps are in place so you marry properly. When you marry, you are marrying a daughter of the Lord, not a human condom, which is how some of these women are being treated in other camps.
- “But if they cannot contain”: you can’t contain because you have that desire and you don’t want to be lonely. “Let them marry”: nothing wrong with getting married. “For it is better to marry than to burn”: than to burn in lust.
- So there’s nothing wrong either way. If you have the gift of celibacy and you can stay by yourself, fine, nobody’s going to attack you. If you can’t contain, you can marry.

**[1 Corinthians 7:10-11](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v10)</sup> And unto the married I command, yet not I, but the Lord, Let not the wife depart from her husband:
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v11)</sup> But and if she depart, let her remain unmarried, or be reconciled to her husband: and let not the husband put away his wife.

- The Lord commands the wife not to depart from her husband. A godly man was supposed to marry a godly woman. Ordinary problems in a marriage, financial difficulties or occasional sexual problems, are different from physical violence. The command does not mean a woman must remain in danger with a violent man.
- Some sisters married a wicked man whose quick tongue, good looks or fighting first attracted them. The clever mouth is now turned on her, and the rough neck she admired now kicks and hits her. Some men also have violent wives, but the immediate counsel here is to the sisters.
- “But and if she depart, let her remain unmarried, or be reconciled to her husband”. If you are married to a violent man, separate from him. We are not proponents of divorce, but we advise separation when there is violence. Notice the or: remaining unmarried and reconciliation are distinct possibilities, not a command to hurry back.
- Reconcile means restore friendly relations and harmony. It comes after counseling and after going out with him under a chaperone so there is evidence that his spirit is in check. This takes months, plural, not a week or one month. Anger may be about his job, another woman he wanted or other frustrations, but none of that excuses hitting his wife. If he complains she has let herself go, take her to the gym; do not beat her.

**[1 Corinthians 7:12](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v12)</sup> But to the rest speak I, not the Lord: If any brother hath a wife that believeth not, and she be pleased to dwell with him, let him not put her away.

- “If any brother hath a wife that believeth not, and she be pleased to dwell with him”. Pleased to dwell means the unbelieving husband or wife is doing what the Bible says, including coming here, though that person does not yet believe. It does not mean a sister can call her husband pleased to dwell when she does not know where he is, he does not know where she is, and after two or three years nobody has ever seen him.
- Some boast about an unbelieving husband on social media because he provides a comfortable life. Another asked whether to stay with a husband who went to strip clubs every Thursday and wanted sex with her afterward. Do not simply accept his assurance that nothing happens in the champagne room. Be careful about the conduct and the danger to the marriage.
- The film Temptation: Confessions of a Marriage Counselor illustrates a woman leaving a good, ordinary man for a wealthy, attractive, fast-talking man and contracting AIDS, destroying her marriage. A couples' gathering can watch it with stopping points to discuss the choices. When courting, listen to how the other person responds; that can reveal how they think.
- The grass looking greener elsewhere does not mean the other relationship has no problems. There is dirt under that grass too, and it may be dirt you cannot handle. Mow, fertilize and maintain your own lawn. Do not abandon a good marriage for an imagined life on the other side.

**[1 Corinthians 7:14](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v14)</sup> For the unbelieving husband is sanctified by the wife, and the unbelieving wife is sanctified by the husband: else were your children unclean; but now are they holy.

- “For the unbelieving husband is sanctified by the wife.” Sometimes the wife comes into this truth believing and the husband does not, or the other way around: one spouse comes in believing and the other stays home. The believer sanctifies the house. “And the unbelieving wife is sanctified by the husband.” Why? Because one parent must anchor the family by an example of faith, so that the husband or wife who doesn’t believe will follow the believer in his or her conduct at home at least.
- “Else were your children unclean.” If one parent does not set the example and the other spouse follows, your children will be unclean. If this side of the room is keeping Hanukkah and Passover and that side keeps Christmas and Easter, the children will be confused and unclean: mommy believes one thing, daddy believes another. One brother said, my wife ain’t got to believe it, based on this verse, and his wife cooked pork on one side of the stove and beef on the other, Christmas on December 25th and then Hanukkah. That is confusion. Your children are unclean, your children are confused.
- These are the deciding factors on how your kids turn out, the things that follow troubled children into adulthood: physical abuse; emotional or psychological abuse, the name calling; sexual abuse, like the nasty husband that wants the daughter, or the nasty uncle that wants to bounce your son or daughter on his lap and molest them and always wants to babysit; neglect or lack of supervision; domestic violence; community violence, war or crime exposure; bullying; death, separation or abandonment by a caregiver; chronic family conflict; caregivers with untreated mental illness, or misusing alcohol or drugs; unstable housing, homelessness or poverty; food insecurity.
- If you can homeschool your kids, homeschool your own kids. Stop sending your children to your oppressor. If we continue to send our children to Caesar for their education, we should not be surprised when they return as Romans.

**[1 Corinthians 7:15](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v15)</sup> But if the unbelieving depart, let him depart. A brother or a sister is not under bondage in such cases: but God hath called us to peace.

- “But if the unbelieving depart, let him depart.” This is the man trying to change: he was a wicked man and he inherited a wicked woman. Why? To see if he will stay there. God orchestrated that, to reflect what is in us so we can change. The elect is what the Lord is interested in, so he puts us through trials and temptations, and the question is whether we will overcome them.
- “A brother or a sister is not under bondage in such cases”: the bondage is worldly marriage. We do not dissolve marriages in here; it is written in our rules, and biblically we are not doing that. This is for the one who is in the world, trying to repent and come into the truth, whose spouse says, I don’t care what you do, I don’t want anything to do with that Bible, and they are not bending: still cooking pork on one side of the stove, chitlins and hog maws, telling you that half is for you since you keep them laws.
- “But God hath called us to peace.” In those bad worldly marriages where the spouse says, I am not following that Bible, whoso pleaseth God shall escape from that situation, like Ecclesiastes and Ecclesiasticus say.
- Brothers and sisters coming in from these situations have a lot of work to do before they can even think about properly marrying, and some are impatient and jump on things. Your leadership are the fathers of the men and women; we have the records. When we tell you to lean back, wait and let the brother or the sister work on themselves, it is for a good reason, because the backdoor marriage stories are horrible and get worse every time.

**[1 Corinthians 7:28](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v28)</sup> But and if thou marry, thou hast not sinned; and if a virgin marry, she hath not sinned. Nevertheless such shall have trouble in the flesh: but I spare you.

- Scripture doesn’t define marriage by feelings; it defines it by function. Marriage came from God; culture later made it ceremonies and a business and put unrealistic expectations on it, and the traditions don’t replace the original design. Marriage is meant to function first, and through the process of aging together the feelings do what they do.
- But there will be conflict: “Nevertheless such shall have trouble in the flesh.” There will be trouble in the marriage, and how you deal with it depends on what your expectations were going into it.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 26:2](/bible/sirach/26#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/26#v2)</sup> A virtuous woman rejoiceth her husband, and he shall fulfil the years of his life in peace.

    “A virtuous woman rejoiceth her husband, and he shall fulfil the years of his life in peace.” The Bible calls for peace in the marriage: not war, not strife, not enmity, not division, not variance. The trouble in the flesh is dealt with as the wife rejoices her husband through reverence, and he stays in his integrity because there is peace in the household. Peace, not passion. Peace, not feelings.

**[1 Corinthians 7:29](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v29)</sup> But this I say, brethren, the time is short: it remaineth, that both they that have wives be as though they had none;

- We treat strangers better than those we love, and speak to our spouse and children in ways we would never speak to a stranger, because we can get away with it. We compliment a brother on his haircut, a brother cuts his dreads off and it is a celebration in the school, but our wives sit eight hours in the beauty salon with their head under the dome getting microwaved, come home and ask can you tell I cut it, and we never say nothing. When was the last time you told your wife she does a good job?
- But this is not a simp-out class. Don’t go home and start rubbing her feet all day and stop going to camp and stop travelling. “But this I say, brethren, the time is short: it remaineth, that both they that have wives be as though they had none.” We are in the last days. If you have a wife, you have to be as though you don’t; you have to do the Lord’s work and be about the Lord’s business. Brothers have a baby and the next week they are in Africa. The work has to be done, no matter what.
- But when you are not doing the work, you have to make sure you take care of your responsibilities at home, your prized possession, your pillar of rest. Build that thing up, or she will be a pillar of stress.

**[1 Corinthians 7:33](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v33)**

> <sup>[33](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v33)</sup> But he that is married careth for the things that are of the world, how he may please his wife.

- “But he that is married careth for the things that are of the world, how he may please his wife.” You have to please your wife and take care of that responsibility. We have to teach this truth and travel the world, gone a month, two, three at a time, but at the same time take care of home. Why? Because she is teaching your kids. Do you want an unhappy, mean, angry, bitter, emotional woman bringing up your kids while you are gone? Your son will pick up those traits, and your daughter will too. Take care of that possession at home, or you will destroy it.
