## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 1 Corinthians 10 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-33, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[1 Corinthians 10:1-4](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v1)</sup> Moreover, brethren, I would not that ye should be ignorant, how that all our fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through the sea;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v2)</sup> And were all baptized unto Moses in the cloud and in the sea;
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v3)</sup> And did all eat the same spiritual meat;
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v4)</sup> And did all drink the same spiritual drink: for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ.

- Paul reminds the Corinthians how “all our fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through the sea”: their fathers, the Israelites, passed through the sea with Moses, “And did all eat the same spiritual meat”.
- “All our fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through the sea; And were all baptized unto Moses in the cloud and in the sea.” Being baptized unto Moses means Moses taught us the laws.
- The one that was talking to Moses, that was in the wilderness with us, was Christ. It was always him right through the Bible. We never heard the Father’s voice; we never saw the Father.
- “All our fathers were under the cloud”: the cloud is the chariot, what they call UFOs, “and all passed through the sea”.

**[1 Corinthians 10:11](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v11)</sup> Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come.

- “And they are written for our admonition”: this is written for us to do right today. But how will you be tested? The Most High sends those encounters to see where your head is at: the divine orchestration and purpose of the people you encounter. Those who fail to learn from the mistakes of history are doomed to repeat them. You read it in the Bible and still don’t learn, so you repeat it, and the same spirits come and attach themselves to you.
- How many have moved from one town to another, or left a job, because they couldn’t get along with the people, only to find the same problem in the new city? Because you never corrected what was wrong with you, the Lord sent the same spirits everywhere you went to show you your mirror. A wicked woman is given as a portion to a wicked man: if you don’t change, you will keep getting those reflectors of you, and that is where the betrayals come in. We get betrayals too, but we are not deceived: we go to the Bible, the Most High casts the wicked out, and the Lord says, well done, my servants; inherit five and ten cities.
- There must be heresies to try you, and they latch on to certain spirits. A sister who comes in with a nasty gossiping habit won’t latch on to the senior sisters whose spiritual game is in order; she goes to the simple sisters, where those spirits can rest comfortably and not be chastised. People move from congregation to congregation for spiritual growth and the same thing happens again. The requirement is on you; the discipline is on you.
- “Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples”: examples to us. It happened to them in their actions; they passed away, but it is an example for us not to walk in the same footsteps. “And they are written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come.”

**[1 Corinthians 10:12](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v12)</sup> Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.

- “Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.” It’s that quick. It’s a thin line between standing and falling.
- “Upon whom the ends of the world are come. Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.” Pay attention to your own spirit. Examine yourselves and ask: why do these things keep happening to me? Is it something I am doing?
- “Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.” You think you are standing in the truth? Take heed, because it is easy for this thing to fall backwards on you, easy to get caught up in idolatry, fornication or murmuring if we are not disciplining our spirit and our mind, steadily practicing this Bible, reading our four chapters and praying three times a day. If we aren’t doing these things, Satan is going to slide up and pull us back out, and it could be any one of us in here.
- “Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.” Those who are in this truth and have repented: this walk is continual. Satan does not just leave; he will always come back. There is no break in this war, no lunch break, no taking a break from warring with Satan. If you think you stand, beware, because you could fall. Any one of us could fall.

**[1 Corinthians 10:13](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v13)</sup> There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.

- No matter what you go through in life, brothers and sisters, no matter how hard or how bad, be reminded of this. Sometimes when you are going through bad things you think you are the only one in the world, that nobody knows or understands. “There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able”. God knows all of our breaking points. He will not allow you to be tempted above what you are able; he gives Satan guidelines and parameters.
- “But will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.” The way to escape: number one is the scriptures. Number two, you have friends and family, brothers and sisters in this truth you can talk to: my mind is preoccupied with this sin, let's go over some scriptures, let's hang out. It might be a pull back to drug use, to alcohol, to some hoochie mama's house; and the Most High has that phone ring, and it's a brother: let's go hang out somewhere. That is the Most High giving you a door to escape while the home girl was right there at the door. You either take the way to escape or you don't. It's up to you.
- “There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able.” The Most High will not put more on you, because you are his elect. If you understood the joy of the elect, you would embrace the temptation, hard as that is to say. You won’t run from it; you will endure, because you know the Lord has your back. You go through the trial and the straits; enter ye in at the strait gate, and you still enter.
- He will not put more on you than you can handle, because you are in fact the righteous. He wants you to make it and doesn’t want any of us to perish, though some are appointed to die. So when those people encounter you, you that are part of the elect, men and women, recognize what you are dealing with: the Lord is trying to show you something in you so you can fix it, and then you don’t have to deal with that trial anymore.

**[1 Corinthians 10:14-15](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v14)**

>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v15)</sup> I speak as to wise men; judge ye what I say.

- Now he gets to the point. “Wherefore, my dearly beloved, flee from idolatry. I speak as to wise men”: to men who have understanding. “Judge ye what I say”: listen, pay attention.
- What are we called to do with these idolatrous celebrations? “Wherefore, my dearly beloved, flee from idolatry.” This is in the New Testament. This is for you black Christians, you black apologists, you black pastors. Why would it be recorded in the New Testament to flee from idolatry if the law is done away with? Valentine’s Day is idolatry, believe that.
- The Lord gave us our own high holy days. Last night at sundown began the day of Nicanor, the victory recorded in 1 Maccabees 7 and 2 Maccabees 14-15, when Judas Maccabeus defeated and killed Nicanor, the Seleucid Greek commander who came to crush Israel and threaten the temple. That is a victory over our enemies. Our people celebrate no victory over their enemies; instead they celebrate their enemies’ victories over us: the Fourth of July, Christmas, all these damnable holidays. Then comes Purim, when we were saved from being wiped out in the days of the Persian Empire, with Haman the enemy and the deliverance coming through Esther and Mordecai.
- “Wherefore, my dearly beloved, flee from idolatry.” Idolatry is so important in speaking of temptation because you idolize these temptations. That appetite, the lust within you, is an idol to you. You idolize the demon inside of you, you feed it, and it is never enough.

**[1 Corinthians 10:19-20](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v19)</sup> What say I then? that the idol is any thing, or that which is offered in sacrifice to idols is any thing?

But every decision we make, and every decision we ever made, has been filtered through something or somebody. Being in this truth we have to understand that either we are doing the work of the Lord or we are doing the work of Satan. We can’t play both.
- “The things which the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to devils, and not to God.” Paul was saying it at the time, but it is the Lord saying it: “I would not that ye should have fellowship with devils.”

It could be unclean, but sometimes it’s clean, so it is lawful to eat. But depending on the setting it is not appropriate. In the idol’s temple the meat is fine, but where you are eating it is not expedient, not suitable. “All things edify not”: as he said earlier, charity edifieth.

**[1 Corinthians 10:25](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v25)</sup> Whatsoever is sold in the shambles, that eat, asking no question for conscience sake:

- “Whatsoever is sold in the shambles, that eat”: the shambles are the meat markets. The meat left over in the idol’s temple was often sent off to the meat markets, a source of income for the pagan priests, and Israel would go and buy it there. Today you’ll say it’s unlawful to eat meat sacrificed to idols. Is it lawful to eat genetically modified food? That ain’t real meat, real beet, real lettuce. A strawberry this big, broccoli this big: is that real? Unless you’ve got your own land, your own farm and your own seeds, you don’t know, because they’ve got GMO seeds too. Seedless grapes and seedless watermelon are science, not God. So you’re caught between a rock and a hard place.
- “Asking no question for conscience sake”: what question would you ask? Was it sacrificed to an idol? There’s no reason to ask, because idols are nothing in the world. Dig deep enough into what’s in the food and you’ll find something; that’s the point. Is there gelatin in it? Is it pork? No? Then eat it, and don’t ask no damn questions. If the meat is lawful, buy it. Obviously, if it’s wrapped up and looks weird, or you get it home and it smells funny, put it down and get rid of it for conscience sake. But if it is clearly chicken, beef, lamb or salmon as far as you know, eat it, asking no question, because you are trying your best in captivity to eat what God says to eat and drink what God says to drink.
- The marketplace meat is disconnected from the idol’s temple worship. It is not the same place: it is sold for public use, like a Walmart, a ShopRite or a Costco today, so it is less offensive than eating at the actual temple. If they cut the meat, say all hail Zeus and put it on Costco’s truck, are you not going to eat the chicken because of Zeus? He ain’t a real guy in the first place. Paul permits eating meat bought in the shambles because it is no longer part of the idol feast; that is how the early believers lived in a pagan society without taking part in idolatry.
- There is no contradiction between chapters 8 and 10 when you keep God’s commandments. When you are in sin and wickedness, worldly, and you hate Paul’s letters, you’ll find fault. Some of you make things complicated for no reason.

The food those enemies provide is often offered to idols, or not real food at all: fake candy, fake water, fake rice, fake chicken, fake lamb, fake beef. The package says this is real cotton, this is real linen. Is it? You don’t really know. You go by faith; that’s all you can do.
  - **[Hosea 9:3](/bible/hosea/9#v3)**

    “They shall not dwell in the Lord’s land; but Ephraim shall return to Egypt”: back to slavery. “And they shall eat unclean things in Assyria”: we will eat unclean things in captivity.
  - **[Ezekiel 4:13](/bible/ezekiel/4#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/ezekiel/4#v13)</sup> And the Lord said, Even thus shall the children of Israel eat their defiled bread among the Gentiles, whither I will drive them.

    “Even thus shall the children of Israel eat their defiled bread among the Gentiles”: the food we eat is defiled. It is not the food God commanded: we cut the animal a certain kind of way and bled the chicken out. Now we eat whatever we buy in the market.

**[1 Corinthians 10:26-27](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v26)</sup> For the earth is the Lord’s, and the fulness thereof.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v27)</sup> If any of them that believe not bid you to a feast, and ye be disposed to go; whatsoever is set before you, eat, asking no question for conscience sake.

- “If any of them that believe not bid you to a feast, and ye be disposed to go”: say they want to learn the Bible with you and invite you to their house for dinner, and you want to go. “Whatsoever is set before you, eat, asking no question for conscience sake”: whatever lawful food is set before you. Don’t ask where the meat came from, is this real chicken, real lamb, real beef, is the water tap or alkaline. It’s water. Drink the damn water.

**[1 Corinthians 10:28](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v28)</sup> But if any man say unto you, This is offered in sacrifice unto idols, eat not for his sake that shewed it, and for conscience sake: for the earth is the Lord’s, and the fulness thereof:

- “But if any man say unto you, This is offered in sacrifice unto idols, eat not”: this is Thanksgiving dinner, a Christmas dinner, Halloween candy, a Valentine’s Day dinner. Don’t eat it. Not because the idol is anything, not because Thanksgiving means anything or Christmas is real, but because you’ll leave a bad example: teaching against it, but eating in honour of it. You’ll offend the unbelievers. “For his sake that shewed it, and for conscience sake”: because he told you what it is. I’m sorry, I don’t celebrate that; I can’t eat that. “For the earth is the Lord’s, and the fulness thereof”: the meat on that plate is from God anyway, not from the idol it is offered in honour of.
- Take Thanksgiving. If you eat it on Thanksgiving, you are destroying the people around you, because you are supposed to be an Israelite, and we don’t celebrate these days. But after Thanksgiving, after the idol worship is gone, if you have means to get food, get food; and if you don’t have the means, the food that is left has nothing to do with Thanksgiving any more, because the food is lawful. Still, for conscience sake, don’t destroy somebody weak in the spirit, because they are watching you as an Israelite.

**[1 Corinthians 10:29](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v29)</sup> Conscience, I say, not thine own, but of the other: for why is my liberty judged of another man’s conscience?

- “Conscience, I say, not thine own, but of the other”: by conscience Paul does not mean your own, but the conscience of the person who offers the food. “For why is my liberty judged of another man’s conscience?” You may ask among yourselves, why should it matter what he thinks? I know idols are nothing; what’s the big deal?

**[1 Corinthians 10:30](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v30)</sup> For if I by grace be a partaker, why am I evil spoken of for that for which I give thanks?

- “For if I by grace be a partaker, why am I evil spoken of for that for which I give thanks?” If I, knowing by the grace of God that idols are nothing, eat the food he tells me is a Christmas or Thanksgiving dinner, now I look like a hypocrite: teaching against it, but eating in honour of it, and making the person think it’s okay to sacrifice to idols. So you deny the food, not because the food is bad, but because of what it is in honour of.
- In the idol’s temple, don’t eat there, because you’ll cause offence to your brother. Here, don’t eat that food, because you’ll have them convinced you are eating in honour of the idol. The whole premise of both chapters, 8 and 10, is to be an example.

**[1 Corinthians 10:31-33](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v31)**

> <sup>[31](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v31)</sup> Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v32)</sup> Give none offence, neither to the Jews, nor to the Gentiles, nor to the church of God:
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v33)</sup> Even as I please all men in all things, not seeking mine own profit, but the profit of many, that they may be saved.

- “Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God”: whatever you do, make sure it is a good example to the glory of God. “Give none offence, neither to the Jews, nor to the Gentiles”, the northern kingdom, “nor to the church of God”: no one in the church, whether they have a strong conscience or a weak one regarding idolatry and meat sacrificed to idols. “Not seeking mine own profit”, not selfish or puffed up, “but the profit of many, that they may be saved”: winning souls by being a good example.
