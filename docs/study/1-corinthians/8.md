## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 1 Corinthians 8 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-13, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[1 Corinthians 8:1](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v1)</sup> Now as touching things offered unto idols, we know that we all have knowledge. Knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth.

- Knowledge puffs up. You taste that honey, it’s sweet, like damn, that’s good; but how do you get the reward from that?
- “Now as touching things offered unto idols, we know that we all have knowledge”: he’s dealing specifically with food offered to idols. We know there’s no power in that; the idols ain’t nothing. “Knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth.” Knowledge puffs up to the point where you start to not be honest with yourself: you lie to yourself, you become arrogant, and that’s when you start to burn the house down. Just knowing, with no application, doesn’t make you wise.
- This is where all the confusion about Paul lies. “Now as touching things offered unto idols”: that is the context, things offered to idols. “We know that we all have knowledge”: concerning idols, they all knew they had knowledge. Keep that in mind; Paul goes on to explain what that knowledge is.
- When you read Paul’s letters, the understanding comes either above the verse, within the verse or under the verse. If it is not found in those three places, he will often address the same situation in a different church, in a different letter, with more clarity, because the same situations tended to occur in multiple churches, and you cross reference them together.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 1:19-21](/bible/sirach/1#v19)**
    > <sup>[19](/bible/sirach/1#v19)</sup> Wisdom raineth down skill and knowledge of understanding, and exalteth them to honour that hold her fast.
    >
    > <sup>[20](/bible/sirach/1#v20)</sup> The root of wisdom is to fear the Lord, and the branches thereof are long life.
    >
    > <sup>[21](/bible/sirach/1#v21)</sup> The fear of the Lord driveth away sins: and where it is present, it turneth away wrath.

    Wisdom though raineth down skill, and that’s when you’re living in the mind of Christ, moving that way, abiding in him. “Wisdom raineth down skill and knowledge of understanding”, “and exalteth them to honour that hold her fast.” You get knowledge, but you don’t understand it fully; if you understand the power we have in our application, it’s transformative, and it opens up more things to you.
  - **[Proverbs 15:2](/bible/proverbs/15#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/15#v2)</sup> The tongue of the wise useth knowledge aright: but the mouth of fools poureth out foolishness.

    So what is the skill that comes with wisdom? How do you go from knowledge to wisdom? “The tongue of the wise useth knowledge aright”: you use the knowledge, and you use it correctly. “But the mouth of fools poureth out foolishness”: making excuses and justifications for your sin. That’s the difference between somebody who’s knowledgeable and puffed up and somebody who has wisdom. You use the knowledge correctly, you don’t lie to yourself, you don’t make excuses, you don’t make justifications.

**[1 Corinthians 8:2](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v2)</sup> And if any man think that he knoweth any thing, he knoweth nothing yet as he ought to know.

- “He knoweth nothing yet as he ought to know”: you know enough, but you don’t know everything. Don’t get arrogant. Don’t be puffed up.

**[1 Corinthians 8:3](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v3)</sup> But if any man love God, the same is known of him.

- “If any man love God, the same is known of him”: you will be known whether you love God by your actions.

**[1 Corinthians 8:4](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v4)</sup> As concerning therefore the eating of those things that are offered in sacrifice unto idols, we know that an idol is nothing in the world, and that there is none other God but one.

- “As concerning therefore the eating of those things that are offered in sacrifice unto idols”: the context has not changed from verse 1. Paul is addressing eating things offered to idols.
- “We know that an idol is nothing in the world, and that there is none other God but one.” The root of the word knowledge is to know. The knowledge the believers in Christ had in verse 1 is this: idols are nothing in the world. Idols are not gods; there is no God but one. Be mindful of what you know, and don’t get an ego from what you learn.
- Paul was born and raised in the perfect manner of the law; he was not a fool. When he wrote letters, his source material was the law and the prophets. So whatever he says in this letter, we can go back into the law and find it there.

  Precepts:
  - **[Deuteronomy 4:27-28](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v27)**
    > <sup>[27](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v27)</sup> And the Lord shall scatter you among the nations, and ye shall be left few in number among the heathen, whither the Lord shall lead you.
    >
    > <sup>[28](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v28)</sup> And there ye shall serve gods, the work of men’s hands, wood and stone, which neither see, nor hear, nor eat, nor smell.

    “And there ye shall serve gods, the work of men’s hands, wood and stone”: there, in the lands where we are scattered, we would serve gods made by men, carved in wood and stone, or molten in silver and gold. Back then those were the idols of old; in modern times it would be Christianity and Islam. Make-believe gods, action figures called gods: Ken and Barbie called gods, Ninja Turtles, Transformers, Power Rangers called gods. Made by men’s hands, “which neither see, nor hear, nor eat, nor smell”, because they’re not alive. They’re toys. Nothing in the world.
  - **[2 Kings 19:18](/bible/2-kings/19#v18)**
    > <sup>[18](/bible/2-kings/19#v18)</sup> And have cast their gods into the fire: for they were no gods, but the work of men’s hands, wood and stone: therefore they have destroyed them.

    “And have cast their gods into the fire: for they were no gods.” How do you cast a god into a fire? Because there isn’t a god. They were nothing in the world, “but the work of men’s hands, wood and stone: therefore they have destroyed them.” They are destroyable because they are toys. Put a toy in the microwave on the highest heat and it’ll melt. It is not a god.
  - **[Psalms 96:4-5](/bible/psalms/96#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/96#v4)</sup> For the Lord is great, and greatly to be praised: he is to be feared above all gods.
    >
    > <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/96#v5)</sup> For all the gods of the nations are idols: but the Lord made the heavens.

    “He is to be feared above all gods. For all the gods of the nations are idols: but the Lord made the heavens.” All the gods of the nations are idols; our God made the heavens.

**[1 Corinthians 8:5](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v5)</sup> For though there be that are called gods, whether in heaven or in earth, (as there be gods many, and lords many,)

- “For though there be that are called gods”: Zeus is called a god. Odin, Thor and Loki are called gods. Vishnu, Krishna and Allah are called gods. White Jesus is called a god. Bacchus, Mars, Mercury, Ares, Ashtoreth and Baal are called gods. “Whether in heaven”: some are known to dwell in heaven, paradise, Nirvana or Olympus; “or in earth”: some on earth, or under the water like Poseidon.
- “As there be gods many, and lords many”: the Hindus have 37,000 gods, and yoga calls upon those gods. Don’t do yoga. Its positions are bowing down to them, which the law says not to do.

**[1 Corinthians 8:6](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v6)</sup> But to us there is but one God, the Father, of whom are all things, and we in him; and one Lord Jesus Christ, by whom are all things, and we by him.

- “But to us there is but one God, the Father, of whom are all things, and we in him; and one Lord Jesus Christ, by whom are all things, and we by him.” By whom are all things: principalities, everything you see on the earth and in the heavens, the sun, the moon and the stars. Christ created everything, even us. “And we by him”, because Christ our Father created us.
- He created everything, but he prefers Israel.
- “But to us there is but one God”: to us, the Israelites. We are monotheistic; the other nations are polytheistic and worship many gods. “The Father, of whom are all things”: he is the architect. “And one Lord Jesus Christ, by whom are all things, and we by him”: his Son, whom he used to make everything, the master builder. Christ made us, and the Son was made by the Father. Grandfather, father: not the same entity. My dad is not my grandfather; my dad’s dad is my grandfather.
- The Unitarian in the video uses more Bible than the Trinitarians do: there is one God, the Father, and one Lord through whom all things exist, and everything beyond that is later development imposed on the Bible through Greek philosophy.

  Precepts:
  - **[John 1:1-3](/bible/john/1#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/john/1#v1)</sup> In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/john/1#v2)</sup> The same was in the beginning with God.
    >
    > <sup>[3](/bible/john/1#v3)</sup> All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.

    “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.” That is Christ. “All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.” All things were made by Christ: the one Lord by whom are all things.
  - **[Colossians 1:16-17](/bible/colossians/1#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/colossians/1#v16)</sup> For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him:
    >
    > <sup>[17](/bible/colossians/1#v17)</sup> And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.

    “For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible.” The sun, moon, stars and planets, everything on earth, and things you cannot see with the naked eye: all created by Jesus Christ, our Father, our Messiah, our God. “Whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him.”
  - **[Hebrews 1:2](/bible/hebrews/1#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/hebrews/1#v2)</sup> Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds;

    “Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds.” When Christ was on the scene, that was considered the last days; the last days started then.

**[1 Corinthians 8:7](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v7)</sup> Howbeit there is not in every man that knowledge: for some with conscience of the idol unto this hour eat it as a thing offered unto an idol; and their conscience being weak is defiled.

- “Howbeit there is not in every man that knowledge”: the knowledge of verse 4, that idols are nothing in the world. “For some with conscience of the idol unto this hour eat it as a thing offered unto an idol”: some, stronger in the faith, understood that idols are nothing; others ate meat offered to idols and really believed in their own mind they were honouring that god. “And their conscience being weak is defiled”: defiled in believing that these idols are actually real gods.
- Some of our people scattered among the nations were former idolaters, and others were raised heavily in Judaism to abhor and abstain from all idols at any cost. That was the dilemma here.

**[1 Corinthians 8:8](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v8)</sup> But meat commendeth us not to God: for neither, if we eat, are we the better; neither, if we eat not, are we the worse.

- “But meat commendeth us not to God”: to be commended is to gain acknowledgement, to be accepted, like being commendable for your service around the house or at your job. Paul explains it within the verse: “for neither, if we eat, are we the better; neither, if we eat not, are we the worse.” Eating meat sacrificed to idols, or not eating it, does not bring you closer to God or further from him, because idols are nothing in the world.
- If I set a Ninja Turtle toy by my plate and pray, oh heavenly Donatello, heavenly Leonardo, thank you for this chicken, does the food become defiled? No. I’m an idiot eating food. Heavenly Power Ranger, heavenly Zordon, heavenly Thundercats: the meat remains the meat. But some will say, you prayed to Ninja Turtles, that food is bad. That is the weak conscience. A lot of people really believe that doing that makes the food bad.

**[1 Corinthians 8:9](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v9)</sup> But take heed lest by any means this liberty of yours become a stumblingblock to them that are weak.

- “But take heed lest by any means this liberty of yours become a stumblingblock to them that are weak.” The liberty is the knowledge that idols are nothing in the world, action figures. Be mindful, because not all your brothers are as strong as you. If I eat food offered to a toy action figure and a brother with a weak conscience, from an idolatrous background, sees me, he may think I’m an idolater, or that maybe it’s okay to worship the turtle. If that offends your brother, don’t eat it, because it will make him fall.
- If you don’t know, you just don’t know: liberty means, once again, the knowledge that idols are nothing in the world. And you that know it are to be mindful of a brother or sister who is weak in the faith. Christians don’t understand that part, and it is the foolery they attack the prophets with today. Take good notes; if this is fluent to you, it will not be a problem.

**[1 Corinthians 8:10](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v10)</sup> For if any man see thee which hast knowledge sit at meat in the idol’s temple, shall not the conscience of him which is weak be emboldened to eat those things which are offered to idols;

- Paul is talking about eating foods offered to idols, but it shows us something. “If any man see thee which hast knowledge sit at meat in the idol’s temple, shall not the conscience of him which is weak be emboldened to eat those things which are offered to idols”. A new brother comes in, just came out of that life. You told him idols were wicked, but now you’re in the idol’s temple eating the food, and he’s watching you.
- “For if any man see thee which hast knowledge sit at meat in the idol’s temple”: in the Greco-Roman world Paul was writing to, in Corinth in Greece, temples were hubs for more than worship. Our own temple was multipurpose: sacrificing, feast days and worship, and business transactions too, until Christ got mad and threw the tables over. The heathen temples were also multipurpose: banquet halls, like the restaurants of today or the halls we rent for our feast days with a catering service, and community and business gathering spaces, rented out.
- After an animal was sacrificed to an idol, a portion of the meat was burned, a portion given to the priest, and the rest eaten in a meal on the temple grounds or sold in the public market. It was the same with Levi in our temple: the meat was burned, eaten by the priests and by those who offered it, and the rest put in the storehouse. Some believers in Corinth with a strong conscience went to those meals and ate, knowing the idols were not real and the meat was just food. They went there for the food.
- But the brother who comes from an idolatrous background sees you sitting there, and his conscience is “emboldened to eat those things which are offered to idols”: he is convinced it’s okay to worship and reverence idols. That was a real point of disagreement and a stumbling block for those with a weak conscience, who felt it was a compromise with idolatry. Paul is saying, I get it, you know the idols around here are nothing, but you are offending other believers who are weaker, and leading people to believe we partake in idolatry. If it is going to cause your brother to fall, don’t eat there.

    An example of meat in the idol’s temple. “Now the Babylonians had an idol, called Bel”: as Paul said, there be that are called gods, gods many and lords many. Beelzebub means lord of the flies; Baal-peor; Bel means lord. It all goes back to lords: gods are lords.

**[1 Corinthians 8:11](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v11)</sup> And through thy knowledge shall the weak brother perish, for whom Christ died?

- “And through thy knowledge shall the weak brother perish, for whom Christ died”. His conscience is messed up, because with all your knowledge you taught him or her idols are evil, and now you’re eating food offered to idols while he watches.
- “And through thy knowledge shall the weak brother perish, for whom Christ died?” You will either make him go back to idols or make him denounce the faith, because he sees you eating food offered to idols.

**[1 Corinthians 8:12](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v12)</sup> But when ye sin so against the brethren, and wound their weak conscience, ye sin against Christ.

- “But when ye sin so against the brethren, and wound their weak conscience, ye sin against Christ.” You sin by setting a bad example. I don’t celebrate New Year’s Eve, you told me it was evil, it was wicked, but here it is midnight, I cut on the TV and you are partying, dropping it like it’s hot. What the hell is going on here? You sin against Christ.
- With all of this, not one Bishop, not one Deacon, not one Captain, officer or soldier had the nerve to say this don’t look right, hey Dallas that’s adultery, we shouldn’t be partying like this, it’s not a good look. Nobody said that. That is why we all have to take inventory of ourselves.
- “But when ye sin so against the brethren, and wound their weak conscience, ye sin against Christ”: you sin against Christ for being a bad example to your brother. As verse 1 said, knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth. Look out for your brother.

**[1 Corinthians 8:13](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v13)</sup> Wherefore, if meat make my brother to offend, I will eat no flesh while the world standeth, lest I make my brother to offend.

- “Wherefore, if meat make my brother to offend, I will eat no flesh while the world standeth”: if eating there makes my brother offend, I will not eat that food.
