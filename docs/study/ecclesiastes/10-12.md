Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 10](/bible/ecclesiastes/10)

This is a "four chapters a day" session that reads [Ecclesiastes 10](/bible/ecclesiastes/10) through [Song of Solomon 1](/bible/song-of-solomon/1) straight through. The teacher moves verse by verse without heavy exposition through Ecclesiastes, then takes questions at the end on Song of Solomon. These notes carry the plain sense of each verse-group the preacher reads, and the affirmed precepts and word studies from the closing questions.

**[Ecclesiastes 10:1-4](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v1)</sup> Dead flies cause the ointment of the apothecary to send forth a stinking savour: so doth a little folly him that is in reputation for wisdom and honour.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v2)</sup> A wise man’s heart is at his right hand; but a fool’s heart at his left.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v3)</sup> Yea also, when he that is a fool walketh by the way, his wisdom faileth him, and he saith to every one that he is a fool.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v4)</sup> If the spirit of the ruler rise up against thee, leave not thy place; for yielding pacifieth great offences.

- Dead flies make the apothecary's ointment stink; so a little folly ruins the man who holds a reputation for wisdom and honor. A wise man's heart is at his right hand, but a fool's heart at his left.
- Even as the fool walks by the way his wisdom fails him, and he shows everyone that he is a fool. If the spirit of the ruler rises against you, leave not your place, for yielding pacifies great offenses.

**[Ecclesiastes 10:5-7](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v5)</sup> There is an evil which I have seen under the sun, as an error which proceedeth from the ruler:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v6)</sup> Folly is set in great dignity, and the rich sit in low place.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v7)</sup> I have seen servants upon horses, and princes walking as servants upon the earth.

- There is an evil under the sun, an error that proceeds from the ruler: folly is set in high dignity while the rich sit in a low place.
- He has seen servants riding upon horses and princes walking as servants upon the earth, the natural order turned upside down.

**[Ecclesiastes 10:8-11](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v8)</sup> He that diggeth a pit shall fall into it; and whoso breaketh an hedge, a serpent shall bite him.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v9)</sup> Whoso removeth stones shall be hurt therewith; and he that cleaveth wood shall be endangered thereby.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v10)</sup> If the iron be blunt, and he do not whet the edge, then must he put to more strength: but wisdom is profitable to direct.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v11)</sup> Surely the serpent will bite without enchantment; and a babbler is no better.

- He that digs a pit shall fall into it, and whoever breaks a hedge a serpent shall bite; he that removes stones is hurt by them, and he that cleaves wood is endangered by it.
- If the iron is blunt and the edge is not whetted, more strength is needed, but wisdom is profitable to direct. The serpent bites without enchantment, and a babbler is no better.

**[Ecclesiastes 10:12-15](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v12)</sup> The words of a wise man’s mouth are gracious; but the lips of a fool will swallow up himself.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v13)</sup> The beginning of the words of his mouth is foolishness: and the end of his talk is mischievous madness.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v14)</sup> A fool also is full of words: a man cannot tell what shall be; and what shall be after him, who can tell him?
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v15)</sup> The labour of the foolish wearieth every one of them, because he knoweth not how to go to the city.

- The words of a wise man's mouth are gracious, but the fool is swallowed up by his own lips. His talk begins in foolishness and ends in mischievous madness.
- A fool is full of words, though no man can tell what shall be after him. The labor of the foolish wearies every one of them because he does not even know how to go to the city.

**[Ecclesiastes 10:16-20](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v16)</sup> Woe to thee, O land, when thy king is a child, and thy princes eat in the morning!
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v17)</sup> Blessed art thou, O land, when thy king is the son of nobles, and thy princes eat in due season, for strength, and not for drunkenness!
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v18)</sup> By much slothfulness the building decayeth; and through idleness of the hands the house droppeth through.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v19)</sup> A feast is made for laughter, and wine maketh merry: but money answereth all things.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/ecclesiastes/10#v20)</sup> Curse not the king, no not in thy thought; and curse not the rich in thy bedchamber: for a bird of the air shall carry the voice, and that which hath wings shall tell the matter.

- Woe to the land whose king is a child and whose princes eat in the morning; blessed is the land whose king is the son of nobles and whose princes eat in due season, for strength and not for drunkenness.
- Slothfulness lets the building decay and idleness makes the house drop through. A feast is made for laughter and wine makes merry, but money answers all things.
- Curse not the king, not even in your thought, nor the rich in your bedchamber, for a bird of the air shall carry the voice and tell the matter.

# Ecclesiastes 11: Cast Thy Bread Upon the Waters

Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 11](/bible/ecclesiastes/11)

**[Ecclesiastes 11:1-4](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v1)</sup> Cast thy bread upon the waters: for thou shalt find it after many days.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v2)</sup> Give a portion to seven, and also to eight; for thou knowest not what evil shall be upon the earth.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v3)</sup> If the clouds be full of rain, they empty themselves upon the earth: and if the tree fall toward the south, or toward the north, in the place where the tree falleth, there it shall be.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v4)</sup> He that observeth the wind shall not sow; and he that regardeth the clouds shall not reap.

- Cast your bread upon the waters, for you shall find it after many days; give a portion to seven and also to eight, for you know not what evil shall be upon the earth.
- When the clouds are full of rain they empty themselves on the earth, and where the tree falls it stays. He that watches the wind will not sow, and he that regards the clouds will not reap; endless caution never acts.

**[Ecclesiastes 11:5-7](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v5)</sup> As thou knowest not what is the way of the spirit, nor how the bones do grow in the womb of her that is with child: even so thou knowest not the works of God who maketh all.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v6)</sup> In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thine hand: for thou knowest not whether shall prosper, either this or that, or whether they both shall be alike good.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v7)</sup> Truly the light is sweet, and a pleasant thing it is for the eyes to behold the sun:

- As you do not know the way of the spirit nor how the bones grow in the womb of her that is with child, so you know not the works of the Most High who makes all things.
- Sow your seed in the morning and in the evening withhold not your hand, for you do not know which shall prosper, this or that, or whether both shall be alike good. The light is sweet, and it is pleasant for the eyes to behold the sun.

**[Ecclesiastes 11:8-10](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v8)</sup> But if a man live many years, and rejoice in them all; yet let him remember the days of darkness; for they shall be many. All that cometh is vanity.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v9)</sup> Rejoice, O young man, in thy youth; and let thy heart cheer thee in the days of thy youth, and walk in the ways of thine heart, and in the sight of thine eyes: but know thou, that for all these things God will bring thee into judgment.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/11#v10)</sup> Therefore remove sorrow from thy heart, and put away evil from thy flesh: for childhood and youth are vanity.

- If a man lives many years, let him rejoice in them all, yet remember the days of darkness, for they shall be many; all that comes is vanity.
- Rejoice, young man, in your youth and walk in the ways of your heart, but know that for all these things the Most High will bring you into judgment. Remove sorrow from your heart and put away evil from your flesh, for childhood and youth are vanity.

# Ecclesiastes 12: Remember Now Thy Creator

Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 12](/bible/ecclesiastes/12)

**[Ecclesiastes 12:1-5](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v1)</sup> Remember now thy Creator in the days of thy youth, while the evil days come not, nor the years draw nigh, when thou shalt say, I have no pleasure in them;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v2)</sup> While the sun, or the light, or the moon, or the stars, be not darkened, nor the clouds return after the rain:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v3)</sup> In the day when the keepers of the house shall tremble, and the strong men shall bow themselves, and the grinders cease because they are few, and those that look out of the windows be darkened,
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v4)</sup> And the doors shall be shut in the streets, when the sound of the grinding is low, and he shall rise up at the voice of the bird, and all the daughters of musick shall be brought low;
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v5)</sup> Also when they shall be afraid of that which is high, and fears shall be in the way, and the almond tree shall flourish, and the grasshopper shall be a burden, and desire shall fail: because man goeth to his long home, and the mourners go about the streets:

- Remember now your Creator in the days of your youth, before the evil days come and the years draw near when you say you have no pleasure in them, before sun, light, moon, and stars are darkened.
- This describes old age and decline: the keepers of the house tremble, the strong men bow, the grinders cease because they are few, and those that look out of the windows are darkened.
- The doors are shut, the sound of grinding is low, a man rises at the voice of a bird, the almond tree flourishes, the grasshopper is a burden, and desire fails; man goes to his long home and the mourners go about the streets.

**[Ecclesiastes 12:6-8](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v6)</sup> Or ever the silver cord be loosed, or the golden bowl be broken, or the pitcher be broken at the fountain, or the wheel broken at the cistern.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v7)</sup> Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was: and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v8)</sup> Vanity of vanities, saith the preacher; all is vanity.

- Before the silver cord is loosed, the golden bowl broken, the pitcher broken at the fountain, or the wheel broken at the cistern, the frame of life gives way.
- Then the dust returns to the earth as it was, and the spirit returns to the Most High who gave it. Vanity of vanities, says the preacher, all is vanity.

**[Ecclesiastes 12:9-14](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v9)</sup> And moreover, because the preacher was wise, he still taught the people knowledge; yea, he gave good heed, and sought out, and set in order many proverbs.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v10)</sup> The preacher sought to find out acceptable words: and that which was written was upright, even words of truth.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v11)</sup> The words of the wise are as goads, and as nails fastened by the masters of assemblies, which are given from one shepherd.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v12)</sup> And further, by these, my son, be admonished: of making many books there is no end; and much study is a weariness of the flesh.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v13)</sup> Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/ecclesiastes/12#v14)</sup> For God shall bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing, whether it be good, or whether it be evil.

- Because the preacher was wise, he still taught the people knowledge, gave good heed, sought out and set in order many proverbs, and sought to find acceptable words, even words of truth.
- The words of the wise are as goads and as nails fastened by the masters of assemblies, given from one shepherd. Of making many books there is no end, and much study is a weariness of the flesh.
- The conclusion of the whole matter: fear the Most High and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man; for he shall bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing, whether it be good or evil.
