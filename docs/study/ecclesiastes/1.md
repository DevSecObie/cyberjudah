Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 1](/bible/ecclesiastes/1)

This is the closing chapter of the feast-day "four chapters a day" reading that began in [Proverbs 29](/bible/proverbs/29). The teacher reads [Ecclesiastes 1](/bible/ecclesiastes/1) straight through as the stopping point for the day, then takes basic questions on note-taking and retention rather than exposition.

**[Ecclesiastes 1:1-3](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v1)</sup> The words of the Preacher, the son of David, king in Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v2)</sup> Vanity of vanities, saith the Preacher, vanity of vanities; all is vanity.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v3)</sup> What profit hath a man of all his labour which he taketh under the sun?

- These are the words of the preacher, the son of David, king in Jerusalem. He declares vanity of vanities, all is vanity, and asks what profit a man has of all his labor under the sun.

**[Ecclesiastes 1:4-7](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v4)</sup> One generation passeth away, and another generation cometh: but the earth abideth for ever.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v5)</sup> The sun also ariseth, and the sun goeth down, and hasteth to his place where he arose.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v6)</sup> The wind goeth toward the south, and turneth about unto the north; it whirleth about continually, and the wind returneth again according to his circuits.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v7)</sup> All the rivers run into the sea; yet the sea is not full; unto the place from whence the rivers come, thither they return again.

- One generation passes and another comes, but the earth abides forever. The sun rises and sets and hastens back to its place.
- The wind circles continually and the rivers all run to the sea, yet the sea is never full; the waters return to run their course again.

**[Ecclesiastes 1:8-11](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v8)</sup> All things are full of labour; man cannot utter it: the eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v9)</sup> The thing that hath been, it is that which shall be; and that which is done is that which shall be done: and there is no new thing under the sun.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v10)</sup> Is there any thing whereof it may be said, See, this is new? it hath been already of old time, which was before us.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v11)</sup> There is no remembrance of former things; neither shall there be any remembrance of things that are to come with those that shall come after.

- All things are full of labor beyond what man can utter; the eye is never satisfied with seeing nor the ear filled with hearing.
- There is no new thing under the sun; what has been is what shall be, and there is no remembrance of former things or of those to come.

**[Ecclesiastes 1:12-15](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v12)</sup> I the Preacher was king over Israel in Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v13)</sup> And I gave my heart to seek and search out by wisdom concerning all things that are done under heaven: this sore travail hath God given to the sons of man to be exercised therewith.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v14)</sup> I have seen all the works that are done under the sun; and, behold, all is vanity and vexation of spirit.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v15)</sup> That which is crooked cannot be made straight: and that which is wanting cannot be numbered.

- The preacher, king over Israel in Jerusalem, gave his heart to seek out by wisdom all things done under heaven, calling it a sore travail given by the Most High.
- He saw all the works done under the sun and found all vanity and vexation of spirit; what is crooked cannot be made straight.

**[Ecclesiastes 1:16-18](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v16)</sup> I communed with mine own heart, saying, Lo, I am come to great estate, and have gotten more wisdom than all they that have been before me in Jerusalem: yea, my heart had great experience of wisdom and knowledge.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v17)</sup> And I gave my heart to know wisdom, and to know madness and folly: I perceived that this also is vexation of spirit.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v18)</sup> For in much wisdom is much grief: and he that increaseth knowledge increaseth sorrow.

- Having gained more wisdom than all before him in Jerusalem, he set his heart to know wisdom, madness, and folly, and found even this to be vexation of spirit.
- In much wisdom is much grief, and he that increases knowledge increases sorrow.
