Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 6](/bible/ecclesiastes/6)

This session of "four chapters a day" moves through [Ecclesiastes 6](/bible/ecclesiastes/6) to 9, written by Solomon. The teaching held this to be a very heavy reading, with a lot of understanding on death, on life, and on much else, and it warned that Ecclesiastes is one of those books that raises questions not all of which can be answered. The framing set at the start contrasted the world's wicked holidays that Esau set up, like Valentine's Day and Christmas, with the Lord's feast days that the righteous are now coming into the truth to keep and enjoy. Chapter 6 opens on an evil common among men: a man given riches, wealth, and honor by the Most High, yet not given the power to enjoy them, so that a stranger eats what he laid up.

**[Ecclesiastes 6:1-2](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v1)</sup> There is an evil which I have seen under the sun, and it is common among men:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v2)</sup> A man to whom God hath given riches, wealth, and honour, so that he wanteth nothing for his soul of all that he desireth, yet God giveth him not power to eat thereof, but a stranger eateth it: this is vanity, and it is an evil disease.

- There is an evil common among men: a man to whom the Most High has given riches, wealth, and honor so that he wants nothing his soul desires, yet is not given the power to eat of it, and a stranger eats it instead.
- This is called vanity and an evil disease, for the wealth profits the man nothing when he has no power to enjoy the labor of his own hands.

**[Ecclesiastes 6:3-6](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v3)</sup> If a man beget an hundred children, and live many years, so that the days of his years be many, and his soul be not filled with good, and also that he have no burial; I say, that an untimely birth is better than he.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v4)</sup> For he cometh in with vanity, and departeth in darkness, and his name shall be covered with darkness.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v5)</sup> Moreover he hath not seen the sun, nor known any thing: this hath more rest than the other.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v6)</sup> Yea, though he live a thousand years twice told, yet hath he seen no good: do not all go to one place?

- Though a man beget a hundred children and live many years, if his soul is not filled with good and he has no burial, an untimely birth is better than he.
- The untimely birth comes in with vanity and departs in darkness, having seen nothing, yet it has more rest than the man who lived and saw no good.
- Though a man live a thousand years twice told and yet see no good, all go to one place, and length of days without the enjoyment of them is empty.

**[Ecclesiastes 6:7-9](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v7)</sup> All the labour of man is for his mouth, and yet the appetite is not filled.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v8)</sup> For what hath the wise more than the fool? what hath the poor, that knoweth to walk before the living?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v9)</sup> Better is the sight of the eyes than the wandering of the desire: this is also vanity and vexation of spirit.

- All the labor of man is for his mouth, and yet the appetite is never filled, so the wise man in this has no advantage over the fool.
- Better is the sight of the eyes, the good a man actually has before him, than the wandering of the desire that is never satisfied; this too is vanity and vexation of spirit.

**[Ecclesiastes 6:10-12](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v10)</sup> That which hath been is named already, and it is known that it is man: neither may he contend with him that is mightier than he.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v11)</sup> Seeing there be many things that increase vanity, what is man the better?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v12)</sup> For who knoweth what is good for man in this life, all the days of his vain life which he spendeth as a shadow? for who can tell a man what shall be after him under the sun?

- That which has been is already named and known to be man, and man may not contend with the Most High, who is mightier than he.
- The more a man multiplies words and things, the more he increases vanity, and no man knows what is truly good for him in the few days of his vain life, nor what shall be after him under the sun.

# Ecclesiastes 7: A Good Name and the House of Mourning

Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 7](/bible/ecclesiastes/7)

Chapter 7 turns to the sober wisdom of the righteous: a good name, the house of mourning, patience, and the fear of the Most High. Solomon sets sorrow above laughter and the rebuke of the wise above the song of fools, and he tells us plainly that he proved all these things by wisdom, seeking out both the wisdom and the wickedness of folly.

**[Ecclesiastes 7:1-4](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v1)</sup> A good name is better than precious ointment; and the day of death than the day of one’s birth.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v2)</sup> It is better to go to the house of mourning, than to go to the house of feasting: for that is the end of all men; and the living will lay it to his heart.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v3)</sup> Sorrow is better than laughter: for by the sadness of the countenance the heart is made better.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v4)</sup> The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning; but the heart of fools is in the house of mirth.

- A good name is better than precious ointment, and the day of death better than the day of one's birth, for the day of death is the end that the living should lay to heart.
- It is better to go to the house of mourning than to the house of feasting, for that is the end of all men, and sorrow is better than laughter because the sadness of the countenance makes the heart better.
- The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning, but the heart of fools is in the house of mirth.

**[Ecclesiastes 7:5-7](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v5)</sup> It is better to hear the rebuke of the wise, than for a man to hear the song of fools.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v6)</sup> For as the crackling of thorns under a pot, so is the laughter of the fool: this also is vanity.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v7)</sup> Surely oppression maketh a wise man mad; and a gift destroyeth the heart.

- It is better to hear the rebuke of the wise than to hear the song of fools, for the laughter of the fool is like the crackling of thorns under a pot, quick and empty.
- Oppression makes a wise man mad, and a gift destroys the heart, warning that even the wise can be turned by pressure and by bribes.

**[Ecclesiastes 7:8-10](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v8)</sup> Better is the end of a thing than the beginning thereof: and the patient in spirit is better than the proud in spirit.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v9)</sup> Be not hasty in thy spirit to be angry: for anger resteth in the bosom of fools.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v10)</sup> Say not thou, What is the cause that the former days were better than these? for thou dost not enquire wisely concerning this.

- Better is the end of a thing than its beginning, and the patient in spirit is better than the proud in spirit.
- Be not hasty in thy spirit to be angry, for anger rests in the bosom of fools, and do not ask why the former days were better than these, for that is not asked wisely.

**[Ecclesiastes 7:11-14](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v11)</sup> Wisdom is good with an inheritance: and by it there is profit to them that see the sun.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v12)</sup> For wisdom is a defence, and money is a defence: but the excellency of knowledge is, that wisdom giveth life to them that have it.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v13)</sup> Consider the work of God: for who can make that straight, which he hath made crooked?
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v14)</sup> In the day of prosperity be joyful, but in the day of adversity consider: God also hath set the one over against the other, to the end that man should find nothing after him.

- Wisdom is good with an inheritance and profits those who see the sun, for wisdom is a defense and money is a defense, but the excellency of knowledge is that wisdom gives life to those who have it.
- Consider the work of the Most High, for no man can make straight what He has made crooked; in the day of prosperity be joyful, but in the day of adversity consider that He has set the one against the other.

**[Ecclesiastes 7:15-18](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v15)</sup> All things have I seen in the days of my vanity: there is a just man that perisheth in his righteousness, and there is a wicked man that prolongeth his life in his wickedness.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v16)</sup> Be not righteous over much; neither make thyself over wise: why shouldest thou destroy thyself?
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v17)</sup> Be not over much wicked, neither be thou foolish: why shouldest thou die before thy time?
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v18)</sup> It is good that thou shouldest take hold of this; yea, also from this withdraw not thine hand: for he that feareth God shall come forth of them all.

- Solomon has seen in his vain days a just man perishing in his righteousness and a wicked man prolonging his life in his wickedness.
- Be not righteous over much nor over wise, and be not over much wicked nor foolish; he that fears the Most High shall come forth of them all.

**[Ecclesiastes 7:19-22](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v19)</sup> Wisdom strengtheneth the wise more than ten mighty men which are in the city.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v20)</sup> For there is not a just man upon earth, that doeth good, and sinneth not.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v21)</sup> Also take no heed unto all words that are spoken; lest thou hear thy servant curse thee:
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v22)</sup> For oftentimes also thine own heart knoweth that thou thyself likewise hast cursed others.

- Wisdom strengthens the wise more than ten mighty men in the city, yet there is not a just man upon the earth that does good and sins not.
- Verse 20 is one of those verses the Christians love to pull out, the same as the New Testament saying we all fall short and Proverbs saying a just man falls seven times; it does not excuse sin but shows that no man is sinless.
- Take no heed to all the words that are spoken, lest thou hear thy servant curse thee, for thine own heart knows that thou hast likewise cursed others.
  - **[Proverbs 24:16](/bible/proverbs/24#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/proverbs/24#v16)</sup> For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.

    A just man falls seven times and rises up again, so a fall does not undo the righteous man.
  - **[Romans 3:23](/bible/romans/3#v23)**
    > <sup>[23](/bible/romans/3#v23)</sup> For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;

    All have sinned and come short of the glory of the Most High, the same point Solomon makes here.

**[Ecclesiastes 7:23-25](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v23)</sup> All this have I proved by wisdom: I said, I will be wise; but it was far from me.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v24)</sup> That which is far off, and exceeding deep, who can find it out?
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v25)</sup> I applied mine heart to know, and to search, and to seek out wisdom, and the reason of things, and to know the wickedness of folly, even of foolishness and madness:

- Solomon proved all this by wisdom and said he would be wise, but it was far from him; that which is far off and exceeding deep, who can find it out.
- He applied his heart to know and search out wisdom and the reason of things, and also to know the wickedness of folly and the foolishness of madness, seeking out both the wicked and the wise.

**[Ecclesiastes 7:26-29](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v26)</sup> And I find more bitter than death the woman, whose heart is snares and nets, and her hands as bands: whoso pleaseth God shall escape from her; but the sinner shall be taken by her.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v27)</sup> Behold, this have I found, saith the preacher, counting one by one, to find out the account:
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v28)</sup> Which yet my soul seeketh, but I find not: one man among a thousand have I found; but a woman among all those have I not found.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v29)</sup> Lo, this only have I found, that God hath made man upright; but they have sought out many inventions.

- Solomon finds more bitter than death the woman whose heart is snares and nets and whose hands are as bands; whoso pleases the Most High escapes her, but the sinner is taken by her.
- Counting one by one, he found one man among a thousand, but not one woman among all those, and concludes that the Most High made man upright, but they have sought out many inventions.

# Ecclesiastes 8: Keep the King's Commandment

Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 8](/bible/ecclesiastes/8)

Chapter 8 counsels obedience to the king, and the teaching made clear that the king spoken of here is the Most High, so keeping the king's commandment means keeping the Lord's commandment. It moves on to the wise man's discernment of time and judgment, and to the difference between the end of the righteous and the wicked.

**[Ecclesiastes 8:1-4](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v1)</sup> Who is as the wise man? and who knoweth the interpretation of a thing? a man’s wisdom maketh his face to shine, and the boldness of his face shall be changed.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v2)</sup> I counsel thee to keep the king’s commandment, and that in regard of the oath of God.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v3)</sup> Be not hasty to go out of his sight: stand not in an evil thing; for he doeth whatsoever pleaseth him.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v4)</sup> Where the word of a king is, there is power: and who may say unto him, What doest thou?

- A man's wisdom makes his face to shine, and the boldness of his face is changed; the counsel is to keep the king's commandment in regard of the oath of the Most High.
- The king spoken of here is the Most High Himself, for verse 3 says He does whatsoever pleases Him; therefore keeping the king's commandment is keeping the Lord's commandment, and where the word of the king is, there is power.

**[Ecclesiastes 8:5-8](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v5)</sup> Whoso keepeth the commandment shall feel no evil thing: and a wise man’s heart discerneth both time and judgment.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v6)</sup> Because to every purpose there is time and judgment, therefore the misery of man is great upon him.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v7)</sup> For he knoweth not that which shall be: for who can tell him when it shall be?
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v8)</sup> There is no man that hath power over the spirit to retain the spirit; neither hath he power in the day of death: and there is no discharge in that war; neither shall wickedness deliver those that are given to it.

- Whoso keeps the commandment shall feel no evil thing, and a wise man's heart discerns both time and judgment, for to every purpose there is a time and judgment.
- No man has power over the spirit to retain the spirit, nor power in the day of death; there is no discharge in that war, and wickedness will not deliver those given to it.

**[Ecclesiastes 8:9-11](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v9)</sup> All this have I seen, and applied my heart unto every work that is done under the sun: there is a time wherein one man ruleth over another to his own hurt.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v10)</sup> And so I saw the wicked buried, who had come and gone from the place of the holy, and they were forgotten in the city where they had so done: this is also vanity.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v11)</sup> Because sentence against an evil work is not executed speedily, therefore the heart of the sons of men is fully set in them to do evil.

- Solomon applied his heart to every work done under the sun and saw one man rule over another to his own hurt.
- He saw the wicked buried and then forgotten in the place where they had done their evil, and because sentence against an evil work is not executed speedily, the hearts of the sons of men are fully set to do evil.

**[Ecclesiastes 8:12-13](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v12)</sup> Though a sinner do evil an hundred times, and his days be prolonged, yet surely I know that it shall be well with them that fear God, which fear before him:
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v13)</sup> But it shall not be well with the wicked, neither shall he prolong his days, which are as a shadow; because he feareth not before God.

- Though a sinner do evil a hundred times and his days be prolonged, Solomon knows it shall be well with those that fear the Most High.
- It shall not be well with the wicked, and he shall not prolong his days, which are as a shadow, because he fears not before the Most High; the meaning is that the wicked do not inherit the kingdom and do not have the everlasting life the righteous receive.

**[Ecclesiastes 8:14-17](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v14)</sup> There is a vanity which is done upon the earth; that there be just men, unto whom it happeneth according to the work of the wicked; again, there be wicked men, to whom it happeneth according to the work of the righteous: I said that this also is vanity.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v15)</sup> Then I commended mirth, because a man hath no better thing under the sun, than to eat, and to drink, and to be merry: for that shall abide with him of his labour the days of his life, which God giveth him under the sun.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v16)</sup> When I applied mine heart to know wisdom, and to see the business that is done upon the earth: (for also there is that neither day nor night seeth sleep with his eyes:)
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v17)</sup> Then I beheld all the work of God, that a man cannot find out the work that is done under the sun: because though a man labour to seek it out, yet he shall not find it; yea further; though a wise man think to know it, yet shall he not be able to find it.

- There is a vanity done upon the earth, that just men are treated according to the work of the wicked and wicked men according to the work of the righteous.
- Solomon commends mirth, for a man has no better thing under the sun than to eat, drink, and be merry in his labor; yet a man cannot find out the work of the Most High done under the sun, and though he labor to seek it out, he shall not find it.

# Ecclesiastes 9: One Event to All, and Wisdom Better Than Strength

Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 9](/bible/ecclesiastes/9)

Chapter 9 declares that the righteous and the wise and their works are in the hand of the Most High, and that one event, death, comes alike to all. Solomon counsels the living to enjoy their labor and to stay in righteousness, and he closes on the theme that wisdom is better than strength, though the poor man's wisdom is despised.

**[Ecclesiastes 9:1-3](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v1)</sup> For all this I considered in my heart even to declare all this, that the righteous, and the wise, and their works, are in the hand of God: no man knoweth either love or hatred by all that is before them.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v2)</sup> All things come alike to all: there is one event to the righteous, and to the wicked; to the good and to the clean, and to the unclean; to him that sacrificeth, and to him that sacrificeth not: as is the good, so is the sinner; and he that sweareth, as he that feareth an oath.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v3)</sup> This is an evil among all things that are done under the sun, that there is one event unto all: yea, also the heart of the sons of men is full of evil, and madness is in their heart while they live, and after that they go to the dead.

- The righteous and the wise and their works are in the hand of the Most High, and no man knows love or hatred by all that is before him.
- All things come alike to all, one event to the righteous and the wicked, the clean and the unclean; this is an evil, that one event comes to all, and the heart of the sons of men is full of evil while they live.

**[Ecclesiastes 9:4-6](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v4)</sup> For to him that is joined to all the living there is hope: for a living dog is better than a dead lion.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v5)</sup> For the living know that they shall die: but the dead know not any thing, neither have they any more a reward; for the memory of them is forgotten.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v6)</sup> Also their love, and their hatred, and their envy, is now perished; neither have they any more a portion for ever in any thing that is done under the sun.

- To him that is joined to the living there is hope, for a living dog is better than a dead lion.
- The living know that they shall die, but the dead know not anything, and their love, hatred, and envy have perished, and they have no more portion in anything done under the sun.

**[Ecclesiastes 9:7-10](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v7)</sup> Go thy way, eat thy bread with joy, and drink thy wine with a merry heart; for God now accepteth thy works.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v8)</sup> Let thy garments be always white; and let thy head lack no ointment.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v9)</sup> Live joyfully with the wife whom thou lovest all the days of the life of thy vanity, which he hath given thee under the sun, all the days of thy vanity: for that is thy portion in this life, and in thy labour which thou takest under the sun.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v10)</sup> Whatsoever thy hand findeth to do, do it with thy might; for there is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave, whither thou goest.

- Eat thy bread with joy and drink thy wine with a merry heart, for the Most High now accepts thy works.
- Let thy garments be always white, which means stay in righteousness, and let thy head lack no ointment, the ointment being the understanding.
- Live joyfully with the wife thou lovest all the days of thy vain life, and whatsoever thy hand finds to do, do it with thy might, for there is no work nor device nor knowledge nor wisdom in the grave.

**[Ecclesiastes 9:11-12](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v11)</sup> I returned, and saw under the sun, that the race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, neither yet bread to the wise, nor yet riches to men of understanding, nor yet favour to men of skill; but time and chance happeneth to them all.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v12)</sup> For man also knoweth not his time: as the fishes that are taken in an evil net, and as the birds that are caught in the snare; so are the sons of men snared in an evil time, when it falleth suddenly upon them.

- The race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, nor bread to the wise, nor riches to men of understanding, but time and chance happen to them all.
- Time and chance here means death; man knows not his time, and like fishes taken in a net and birds caught in a snare, the sons of men are snared in an evil time when it falls suddenly upon them.

**[Ecclesiastes 9:13-16](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v13)</sup> This wisdom have I seen also under the sun, and it seemed great unto me:
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v14)</sup> There was a little city, and few men within it; and there came a great king against it, and besieged it, and built great bulwarks against it:
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v15)</sup> Now there was found in it a poor wise man, and he by his wisdom delivered the city; yet no man remembered that same poor man.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v16)</sup> Then said I, Wisdom is better than strength: nevertheless the poor man’s wisdom is despised, and his words are not heard.

- Solomon saw a wisdom that seemed great to him: a little city with few men, against which a great king came and built great bulwarks, a bulwark being a defensive wall or fortification.
- A poor wise man in the city delivered it by his wisdom, yet no man remembered that same poor man; so wisdom is better than strength, even though the poor man's wisdom is despised and his words are not heard.

**[Ecclesiastes 9:17-18](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v17)</sup> The words of wise men are heard in quiet more than the cry of him that ruleth among fools.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v18)</sup> Wisdom is better than weapons of war: but one sinner destroyeth much good.

- The words of wise men are heard in quiet more than the cry of him that rules among fools.
- Wisdom is better than weapons of war, but one sinner destroys much good, closing the reading on the worth of wisdom and the harm a single sinner can do.
