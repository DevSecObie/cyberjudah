Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 2](/bible/ecclesiastes/2)

This session of "four chapters a day" moves through [Ecclesiastes 2](/bible/ecclesiastes/2) to 5, written by Solomon, the richest and wisest king in history. Ecclesiastes is a book of regret: Solomon writes out the emptiness he found after obtaining everything a man could want, so that we can learn from it and see what really matters in this life, which is to keep the commandments in the faith of Christ.

**[Ecclesiastes 2:1-3](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v1)</sup> I said in mine heart, Go to now, I will prove thee with mirth, therefore enjoy pleasure: and, behold, this also is vanity.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v2)</sup> I said of laughter, It is mad: and of mirth, What doeth it?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v3)</sup> I sought in mine heart to give myself unto wine, yet acquainting mine heart with wisdom; and to lay hold on folly, till I might see what was that good for the sons of men, which they should do under the heaven all the days of their life.

- Solomon proves himself with mirth and pleasure and finds it also vanity; laughter is madness, and mirth accomplishes nothing.
- He gives his heart to wine while still keeping wisdom, and lays hold on folly, trying to see what was good for the sons of men to do under heaven all the days of their life. Solomon is letting us know that he did it all, that he did everything.

**[Ecclesiastes 2:4-8](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v4)</sup> I made me great works; I builded me houses; I planted me vineyards:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v5)</sup> I made me gardens and orchards, and I planted trees in them of all kind of fruits:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v6)</sup> I made me pools of water, to water therewith the wood that bringeth forth trees:
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v7)</sup> I got me servants and maidens, and had servants born in my house; also I had great possessions of great and small cattle above all that were in Jerusalem before me:
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v8)</sup> I gathered me also silver and gold, and the peculiar treasure of kings and of the provinces: I gat me men singers and women singers, and the delights of the sons of men, as musical instruments, and that of all sorts.

- Solomon builds houses, plants vineyards, and makes gardens, orchards, and pools of water for irrigation. His kingdom was not a bunch of hard clay houses; he had irrigation systems and everything that grows on a vine.
- He gathers servants born in his house, great and small cattle, silver and gold, the peculiar treasure of kings, and men singers and women singers. Solomon had bands, instruments with bands, and musical instruments of all sorts.

**[Ecclesiastes 2:9-11](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v9)</sup> So I was great, and increased more than all that were before me in Jerusalem: also my wisdom remained with me.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v10)</sup> And whatsoever mine eyes desired I kept not from them, I withheld not my heart from any joy; for my heart rejoiced in all my labour: and this was my portion of all my labour.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v11)</sup> Then I looked on all the works that my hands had wrought, and on the labour that I had laboured to do: and, behold, all was vanity and vexation of spirit, and there was no profit under the sun.

- Solomon grows greater than all before him in Jerusalem, and out of everything he had, his wisdom still remained with him. He withheld his heart from no joy.
- Yet when he looks on all the works his hands had wrought, all is vanity and vexation of spirit, and there is no profit under the sun. With all he had, he still fell victim to the lust of the flesh, and what he did had no purpose.

**[Ecclesiastes 2:12-17](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v12)</sup> And I turned myself to behold wisdom, and madness, and folly: for what can the man do that cometh after the king? even that which hath been already done.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v13)</sup> Then I saw that wisdom excelleth folly, as far as light excelleth darkness.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v14)</sup> The wise man’s eyes are in his head; but the fool walketh in darkness: and I myself perceived also that one event happeneth to them all.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v15)</sup> Then said I in my heart, As it happeneth to the fool, so it happeneth even to me; and why was I then more wise? Then I said in my heart, that this also is vanity.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v16)</sup> For there is no remembrance of the wise more than of the fool for ever; seeing that which now is in the days to come shall all be forgotten. And how dieth the wise man? as the fool.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v17)</sup> Therefore I hated life; because the work that is wrought under the sun is grievous unto me: for all is vanity and vexation of spirit.

- Solomon turns to behold wisdom, madness, and folly, saying whatever the man coming after the king does has already been done; he already did everything that people want to do.
- He sees wisdom excels folly as light excels darkness, yet one event, death, happens to the wise man and the fool alike. Everything the wise man has and everything the fool has, they all go to the same grave.
- There is no remembrance of the wise more than the fool forever, and all shall be forgotten; therefore he hates life, for the work under the sun is grievous and all is vanity.

**[Ecclesiastes 2:18-23](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v18)</sup> Yea, I hated all my labour which I had taken under the sun: because I should leave it unto the man that shall be after me.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v19)</sup> And who knoweth whether he shall be a wise man or a fool? yet shall he have rule over all my labour wherein I have laboured, and wherein I have shewed myself wise under the sun. This is also vanity.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v20)</sup> Therefore I went about to cause my heart to despair of all the labour which I took under the sun.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v21)</sup> For there is a man whose labour is in wisdom, and in knowledge, and in equity; yet to a man that hath not laboured therein shall he leave it for his portion. This also is vanity and a great evil.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v22)</sup> For what hath man of all his labour, and of the vexation of his heart, wherein he hath laboured under the sun?
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v23)</sup> For all his days are sorrows, and his travail grief; yea, his heart taketh not rest in the night. This is also vanity.

- Solomon hates all his labor because he must leave it to the man that comes after him, and Solomon's son was a very dumb young man. All the vineyards, servants, gold, and silver were lost to a foolish person behind him.
- No one knows whether the heir will be a wise man or a fool, yet he will rule over all the labor. Everything Solomon worked hard for must be left to somebody coming after him who could lose it all.
- This is a great evil: a man labors in wisdom, knowledge, and equity, then leaves it to a man that has not labored for it. His days are sorrows, his travail grief, and his heart takes no rest in the night.

**[Ecclesiastes 2:24-26](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v24)</sup> There is nothing better for a man, than that he should eat and drink, and that he should make his soul enjoy good in his labour. This also I saw, that it was from the hand of God.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v25)</sup> For who can eat, or who else can hasten hereunto, more than I?
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/ecclesiastes/2#v26)</sup> For God giveth to a man that is good in his sight wisdom, and knowledge, and joy: but to the sinner he giveth travail, to gather and to heap up, that he may give to him that is good before God. This also is vanity and vexation of spirit.

- There is nothing better for a man than to eat, drink, and make his soul enjoy good in his labor, for this is from the hand of the Most High. The Most High gives wisdom, knowledge, and joy to the man that is good in his sight.
- Solomon is expressing his state of depression and mental well-being: you build up riches all your life but you still die, and your estate goes to somebody else who may be wise or foolish with it.

# Ecclesiastes 3: A Time to Every Purpose

Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 3](/bible/ecclesiastes/3)

**[Ecclesiastes 3:1-8](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v1)</sup> To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v2)</sup> A time to be born, and a time to die; a time to plant, and a time to pluck up that which is planted;
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v3)</sup> A time to kill, and a time to heal; a time to break down, and a time to build up;
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v4)</sup> A time to weep, and a time to laugh; a time to mourn, and a time to dance;
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v5)</sup> A time to cast away stones, and a time to gather stones together; a time to embrace, and a time to refrain from embracing;
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v6)</sup> A time to get, and a time to lose; a time to keep, and a time to cast away;
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v7)</sup> A time to rend, and a time to sew; a time to keep silence, and a time to speak;
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v8)</sup> A time to love, and a time to hate; a time of war, and a time of peace.

- To everything there is a season and a time to every purpose under heaven: a time to be born and a time to die, a time to plant and a time to pluck up.
- There is a time to kill and a time to heal, a time to weep and a time to laugh, a time to embrace and a time to refrain, a time to keep silence and a time to speak.
- Solomon gives many things with a time for each. People say you are not supposed to hate, but there is a time for hatred, a time for judgment, a time for war, and a time for peace, and the Most High shows that time.

**[Ecclesiastes 3:9-15](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v9)</sup> What profit hath he that worketh in that wherein he laboureth?
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v10)</sup> I have seen the travail, which God hath given to the sons of men to be exercised in it.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v11)</sup> He hath made every thing beautiful in his time: also he hath set the world in their heart, so that no man can find out the work that God maketh from the beginning to the end.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v12)</sup> I know that there is no good in them, but for a man to rejoice, and to do good in his life.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v13)</sup> And also that every man should eat and drink, and enjoy the good of all his labour, it is the gift of God.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v14)</sup> I know that, whatsoever God doeth, it shall be for ever: nothing can be put to it, nor any thing taken from it: and God doeth it, that men should fear before him.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v15)</sup> That which hath been is now; and that which is to be hath already been; and God requireth that which is past.

- The Most High has made everything beautiful in his time and set the world in their heart so that no man can find out his work from beginning to end. Setting the world in our heart means we only know the things we have on earth and have no idea of the works of the Most High.
- This is why a lot of people cannot see something as simple as that the Most High is the only God for Israel, because they have the world in their mind.
- Whatever the Most High does shall be forever; nothing can be added or taken from it, and he does it that men should fear before him. This speaks of the commandments: they were given forever, Israel was chosen forever, and Esau is hated forever.
- That which has been is now, and the Most High requires that which is past. The sins of our past he requires, which is why [Deuteronomy 28](/bible/deuteronomy/28) had to come to pass, given to us in Deuteronomy and fulfilled upon us in the 1600s.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)</sup> But it shall come to pass, if thou wilt not hearken unto the voice of the Lord thy God, to observe to do all his commandments and his statutes which I command thee this day; that all these curses shall come upon thee, and overtake thee:

    The curses for disobedience that Solomon points to as the past the Most High requires, fulfilled on Israel.

**[Ecclesiastes 3:16-22](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v16)</sup> And moreover I saw under the sun the place of judgment, that wickedness was there; and the place of righteousness, that iniquity was there.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v17)</sup> I said in mine heart, God shall judge the righteous and the wicked: for there is a time there for every purpose and for every work.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v18)</sup> I said in mine heart concerning the estate of the sons of men, that God might manifest them, and that they might see that they themselves are beasts.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v19)</sup> For that which befalleth the sons of men befalleth beasts; even one thing befalleth them: as the one dieth, so dieth the other; yea, they have all one breath; so that a man hath no preeminence above a beast: for all is vanity.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v20)</sup> All go unto one place; all are of the dust, and all turn to dust again.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v21)</sup> Who knoweth the spirit of man that goeth upward, and the spirit of the beast that goeth downward to the earth?
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/ecclesiastes/3#v22)</sup> Wherefore I perceive that there is nothing better, than that a man should rejoice in his own works; for that is his portion: for who shall bring him to see what shall be after him?

- Solomon sees wickedness in the place of judgment and iniquity in the place of righteousness, and says in his heart that the Most High shall judge the righteous and the wicked, for there is a time there for every purpose and work.
- He says the Most High tests the sons of men to show that they themselves are beasts, for one thing befalls man and beast: as one dies, so dies the other, and all go to one place, all of the dust and all turning to dust again.
- It is better that a man rejoice in his own works, for that is his portion. Everything happens to the poor and the rich, the wise and the fool, man and beast; we all die, so you might as well rejoice in what you have before you pass it on. This was Solomon's mindset for why he did everything he did.

# Ecclesiastes 4: Oppression, Envy, and Companionship

Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 4](/bible/ecclesiastes/4)

**[Ecclesiastes 4:1-3](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v1)</sup> So I returned, and considered all the oppressions that are done under the sun: and behold the tears of such as were oppressed, and they had no comforter; and on the side of their oppressors there was power; but they had no comforter.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v2)</sup> Wherefore I praised the dead which are already dead more than the living which are yet alive.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v3)</sup> Yea, better is he than both they, which hath not yet been, who hath not seen the evil work that is done under the sun.

- Solomon considers the oppressions done under the sun, the tears of the oppressed who had no comforter, and power on the side of their oppressors.
- He praises the dead already dead more than the living, and better than both is he who has not yet been and has not seen the evil work done under the sun.
- This is the mindset behind why in New Orleans they have second lines, meant as a celebration for people who died and no longer have to deal with the sorrows and pain of this life.

**[Ecclesiastes 4:4-6](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v4)</sup> Again, I considered all travail, and every right work, that for this a man is envied of his neighbour. This is also vanity and vexation of spirit.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v5)</sup> The fool foldeth his hands together, and eateth his own flesh.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v6)</sup> Better is an handful with quietness, than both the hands full with travail and vexation of spirit.

- Solomon sees that for every right work a man is envied by his neighbor, and this too is vanity. Even envy is vanity, because the person you envy is going to die and lose their stuff just like you, so you are envying them for nothing.
- The fool folds his hands and eats his own flesh, and better is a handful with quietness than both hands full with travail and vexation of spirit.

**[Ecclesiastes 4:7-12](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v7)</sup> Then I returned, and I saw vanity under the sun.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v8)</sup> There is one alone, and there is not a second; yea, he hath neither child nor brother: yet is there no end of all his labour; neither is his eye satisfied with riches; neither saith he, For whom do I labour, and bereave my soul of good? This is also vanity, yea, it is a sore travail.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v9)</sup> Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v10)</sup> For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v11)</sup> Again, if two lie together, then they have heat: but how can one be warm alone?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v12)</sup> And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken.

- There is one alone with neither child nor brother, yet no end to his labor, and his eyes are never satisfied with riches; he never asks for whom he labors and bereaves his soul of good. This too is vanity and a sore travail.
- Two are better than one, for they have a good reward for their labor; if they fall, one lifts up his fellow, but woe to him that is alone when he falls with no one to help him up.
- A threefold cord is not quickly broken. This speaks of gathering together; you cannot call yourself an Israelite living alone. If you want the kingdom with everybody else but want to be alone in this life, that does not work, because when someone comes against you, you have no backup and nobody to help you.

**[Ecclesiastes 4:13-16](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v13)</sup> Better is a poor and a wise child than an old and foolish king, who will no more be admonished.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v14)</sup> For out of prison he cometh to reign; whereas also he that is born in his kingdom becometh poor.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v15)</sup> I considered all the living which walk under the sun, with the second child that shall stand up in his stead.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/ecclesiastes/4#v16)</sup> There is no end of all the people, even of all that have been before them: they also that come after shall not rejoice in him. Surely this also is vanity and vexation of spirit.

- Better is a poor and wise child than an old and foolish king who will no more be admonished, for out of prison the poor one comes to reign while the one born in the kingdom becomes poor.
- Solomon considers all the living who walk under the sun and the child that shall stand up in the king's place; there is no end of all the people, yet those who come after shall not rejoice in him, and this too is vanity.
- When it says there is no end of all the people, you cannot use that line to say our people were killed off throughout their day and are no longer around; they are still around.

# Ecclesiastes 5: Vows, Riches, and the Gift of God

Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 5](/bible/ecclesiastes/5)

**[Ecclesiastes 5:1-3](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v1)</sup> Keep thy foot when thou goest to the house of God, and be more ready to hear, than to give the sacrifice of fools: for they consider not that they do evil.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v2)</sup> Be not rash with thy mouth, and let not thine heart be hasty to utter any thing before God: for God is in heaven, and thou upon earth: therefore let thy words be few.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v3)</sup> For a dream cometh through the multitude of business; and a fool’s voice is known by multitude of words.

- Keep your foot when you go to the house of the Most High and be more ready to hear than to give the sacrifice of fools, for they do not consider that they do evil.
- Be not rash with your mouth nor let your heart be hasty to utter anything before the Most High, for he is in heaven and you upon earth; therefore let your words be few. This means watch your mouth, which is why Christ said you will give account of every word.
  - **[Matthew 12:36](/bible/matthew/12#v36)**
    > <sup>[36](/bible/matthew/12#v36)</sup> But I say unto you, That every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment.

    Christ's warning that men give account of every idle word, matching Solomon's charge to let your words be few.
- A dream comes through the multitude of business and a fool's voice through a multitude of words. Dreams come through what you are doing: if you play games all day you may dream of it, and if you teach all the time you may dream you are still teaching.

**[Ecclesiastes 5:4-7](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v4)</sup> When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it; for he hath no pleasure in fools: pay that which thou hast vowed.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v5)</sup> Better is it that thou shouldest not vow, than that thou shouldest vow and not pay.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v6)</sup> Suffer not thy mouth to cause thy flesh to sin; neither say thou before the angel, that it was an error: wherefore should God be angry at thy voice, and destroy the work of thine hands?
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v7)</sup> For in the multitude of dreams and many words there are also divers vanities: but fear thou God.

- When you vow a vow to the Most High, do not defer to pay it, for he has no pleasure in fools; it is better not to vow than to vow and not pay.
- Do not let your mouth cause your flesh to sin, nor say before the angel that it was an error, lest the Most High be angry at your voice and destroy the work of your hands. This is what Christ meant by not swearing by heaven, earth, or your own head.
  - **[Matthew 5:34-36](/bible/matthew/5#v34)**
    > <sup>[34](/bible/matthew/5#v34)</sup> But I say unto you, Swear not at all; neither by heaven; for it is God’s throne:
    >
    > <sup>[35](/bible/matthew/5#v35)</sup> Nor by the earth; for it is his footstool: neither by Jerusalem; for it is the city of the great King.
    >
    > <sup>[36](/bible/matthew/5#v36)</sup> Neither shalt thou swear by thy head, because thou canst not make one hair white or black.

    Christ's command to swear not at all, by heaven, earth, or your head, which Solomon's warning about vows supports.
- In the multitude of dreams and many words there are also diverse vanities, but fear the Most High.

**[Ecclesiastes 5:8-9](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v8)</sup> If thou seest the oppression of the poor, and violent perverting of judgment and justice in a province, marvel not at the matter: for he that is higher than the highest regardeth; and there be higher than they.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v9)</sup> Moreover the profit of the earth is for all: the king himself is served by the field.

- If you see the oppression of the poor and the perverting of judgment and justice in a province, do not marvel, for he that is higher than the highest regards it, and there are higher than they.
- Do not worry about the wicked that are successful and all they are doing, because there is a God and that God will have his vengeance. This is why the scriptures say vengeance is his and he will repay.
  - **[Romans 12:19](/bible/romans/12#v19)**
    > <sup>[19](/bible/romans/12#v19)</sup> Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.

    Vengeance is the Most High's and he will repay, the assurance Solomon gives against successful wicked men.

**[Ecclesiastes 5:10-12](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v10)</sup> He that loveth silver shall not be satisfied with silver; nor he that loveth abundance with increase: this is also vanity.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v11)</sup> When goods increase, they are increased that eat them: and what good is there to the owners thereof, saving the beholding of them with their eyes?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v12)</sup> The sleep of a labouring man is sweet, whether he eat little or much: but the abundance of the rich will not suffer him to sleep.

- He that loves silver shall not be satisfied with silver, nor he that loves abundance with increase; this also is vanity. When goods increase, those that eat them increase.
- As one rapper said, more money more problems: the more money most people get, the bigger their family gets, meaning the more food you can buy, the more mouths there are to feed.
- The sleep of a laboring man is sweet whether he eats little or much, but the abundance of the rich will not let him sleep. The rich lose sleep to gain wealth; ask successful entrepreneurs and businessmen and they work off four or five hours of sleep.

**[Ecclesiastes 5:13-17](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v13)</sup> There is a sore evil which I have seen under the sun, namely, riches kept for the owners thereof to their hurt.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v14)</sup> But those riches perish by evil travail: and he begetteth a son, and there is nothing in his hand.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v15)</sup> As he came forth of his mother’s womb, naked shall he return to go as he came, and shall take nothing of his labour, which he may carry away in his hand.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v16)</sup> And this also is a sore evil, that in all points as he came, so shall he go: and what profit hath he that hath laboured for the wind?
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v17)</sup> All his days also he eateth in darkness, and he hath much sorrow and wrath with his sickness.

- There is a sore evil under the sun: riches kept for the owners to their hurt, and those riches perish by evil travail so that the son is left with nothing in his hand.
- As a man came from his mother's womb naked, so shall he return, and take nothing of his labor to carry away in his hand. In all points as he came, so shall he go, and he has labored for the wind, eating in darkness with much sorrow, wrath, and sickness.

**[Ecclesiastes 5:18-20](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v18)</sup> Behold that which I have seen: it is good and comely for one to eat and to drink, and to enjoy the good of all his labour that he taketh under the sun all the days of his life, which God giveth him: for it is his portion.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v19)</sup> Every man also to whom God hath given riches and wealth, and hath given him power to eat thereof, and to take his portion, and to rejoice in his labour; this is the gift of God.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v20)</sup> For he shall not much remember the days of his life; because God answereth him in the joy of his heart.

- It is good and comely for a man to eat, drink, and enjoy the good of all his labor all the days of his life which the Most High gives him, for it is his portion.
- Every man to whom the Most High gives riches, wealth, and power to enjoy them and rejoice in his labor, this is the gift of the Most High. He shall not much remember the days of his life, because the Most High answers him in the joy of his heart.
- Ecclesiastes is a book of regret, and we can learn from it; in the end you see what really matters in this life. Money is a defense, but keep the commandments in the faith of Christ, which is what Solomon was saying.
