Read the chapter → [Ecclesiastes 6](/bible/ecclesiastes/6)

This session of "four chapters a day" moves through [Ecclesiastes 6](/bible/ecclesiastes/6) to 9, written by Solomon. The teaching held this to be a very heavy reading, with a lot of understanding on death, on life, and on much else, and it warned that Ecclesiastes is one of those books that raises questions not all of which can be answered. The framing set at the start contrasted the world's wicked holidays that Esau set up, like Valentine's Day and Christmas, with the Lord's feast days that the righteous are now coming into the truth to keep and enjoy. Chapter 6 opens on an evil common among men: a man given riches, wealth, and honor by the Most High, yet not given the power to enjoy them, so that a stranger eats what he laid up.

**[Ecclesiastes 6:1-2](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v1)</sup> There is an evil which I have seen under the sun, and it is common among men:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v2)</sup> A man to whom God hath given riches, wealth, and honour, so that he wanteth nothing for his soul of all that he desireth, yet God giveth him not power to eat thereof, but a stranger eateth it: this is vanity, and it is an evil disease.

- There is an evil common among men: a man to whom the Most High has given riches, wealth, and honor so that he wants nothing his soul desires, yet is not given the power to eat of it, and a stranger eats it instead.
- This is called vanity and an evil disease, for the wealth profits the man nothing when he has no power to enjoy the labor of his own hands.

**[Ecclesiastes 6:3-6](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v3)</sup> If a man beget an hundred children, and live many years, so that the days of his years be many, and his soul be not filled with good, and also that he have no burial; I say, that an untimely birth is better than he.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v4)</sup> For he cometh in with vanity, and departeth in darkness, and his name shall be covered with darkness.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v5)</sup> Moreover he hath not seen the sun, nor known any thing: this hath more rest than the other.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v6)</sup> Yea, though he live a thousand years twice told, yet hath he seen no good: do not all go to one place?

- Though a man beget a hundred children and live many years, if his soul is not filled with good and he has no burial, an untimely birth is better than he.
- The untimely birth comes in with vanity and departs in darkness, having seen nothing, yet it has more rest than the man who lived and saw no good.
- Though a man live a thousand years twice told and yet see no good, all go to one place, and length of days without the enjoyment of them is empty.

**[Ecclesiastes 6:7-9](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v7)</sup> All the labour of man is for his mouth, and yet the appetite is not filled.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v8)</sup> For what hath the wise more than the fool? what hath the poor, that knoweth to walk before the living?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v9)</sup> Better is the sight of the eyes than the wandering of the desire: this is also vanity and vexation of spirit.

- All the labor of man is for his mouth, and yet the appetite is never filled, so the wise man in this has no advantage over the fool.
- Better is the sight of the eyes, the good a man actually has before him, than the wandering of the desire that is never satisfied; this too is vanity and vexation of spirit.

**[Ecclesiastes 6:10-12](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v10)</sup> That which hath been is named already, and it is known that it is man: neither may he contend with him that is mightier than he.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v11)</sup> Seeing there be many things that increase vanity, what is man the better?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/6#v12)</sup> For who knoweth what is good for man in this life, all the days of his vain life which he spendeth as a shadow? for who can tell a man what shall be after him under the sun?

- That which has been is already named and known to be man, and man may not contend with the Most High, who is mightier than he.
- The more a man multiplies words and things, the more he increases vanity, and no man knows what is truly good for him in the few days of his vain life, nor what shall be after him under the sun.
