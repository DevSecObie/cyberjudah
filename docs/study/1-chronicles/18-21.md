Read the chapter → [1 Chronicles 18](/bible/1-chronicles/18)

Chronicles compresses a wide span of history that is spread across Samuel and Kings into a tight recap. First Chronicles 18 through 21 moves from David's military success into the events that prepare the ground for the temple. These are short chapters because they are revisiting history already read, so read them alongside Second Samuel to get the full detail. Sometimes we must go over this history again and again, because our minds have been watered down; revisiting keeps the account fresh, and Chronicles is set up to give the compressed history in a few chapters.

**[1 Chronicles 18:1-8](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v1)</sup> Now after this it came to pass, that David smote the Philistines, and subdued them, and took Gath and her towns out of the hand of the Philistines.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v2)</sup> And he smote Moab; and the Moabites became David’s servants, and brought gifts.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v3)</sup> And David smote Hadarezer king of Zobah unto Hamath, as he went to stablish his dominion by the river Euphrates.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v4)</sup> And David took from him a thousand chariots, and seven thousand horsemen, and twenty thousand footmen: David also houghed all the chariot horses, but reserved of them an hundred chariots.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v5)</sup> And when the Syrians of Damascus came to help Hadarezer king of Zobah, David slew of the Syrians two and twenty thousand men.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v6)</sup> Then David put garrisons in Syria–damascus; and the Syrians became David’s servants, and brought gifts. Thus the Lord preserved David whithersoever he went.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v7)</sup> And David took the shields of gold that were on the servants of Hadarezer, and brought them to Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v8)</sup> Likewise from Tibhath, and from Chun, cities of Hadarezer, brought David very much brass, wherewith Solomon made the brasen sea, and the pillars, and the vessels of brass.

- David smote the Philistines and subdued them, took Moab, and struck Hadarezer king of Zobah as he went to establish his dominion by the river Euphrates.
- When the Syrians of Damascus came to help Hadarezer, David slew two and thirty thousand of them and put garrisons in Syria, and the Syrians became his servants and brought gifts.
- These are the wars David won for the children of Israel and for the Most High God.
- The Most High preserved David wherever he went; the victory belonged to the Most High, not to David's own strength.
- David carried the gold and much brass back to Jerusalem, the brass out of which Solomon would later make the brazen sea, the pillars, and the vessels.

**[1 Chronicles 18:9-13](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v9)</sup> Now when Tou king of Hamath heard how David had smitten all the host of Hadarezer king of Zobah;
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v10)</sup> He sent Hadoram his son to king David, to enquire of his welfare, and to congratulate him, because he had fought against Hadarezer, and smitten him; (for Hadarezer had war with Tou;) and with him all manner of vessels of gold and silver and brass.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v11)</sup> Them also king David dedicated unto the Lord, with the silver and the gold that he brought from all these nations; from Edom, and from Moab, and from the children of Ammon, and from the Philistines, and from Amalek.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v12)</sup> Moreover Abishai the son of Zeruiah slew of the Edomites in the valley of salt eighteen thousand.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v13)</sup> And he put garrisons in Edom; and all the Edomites became David’s servants. Thus the Lord preserved David whithersoever he went.

- Tou king of Hamath heard how David had smitten the host of Hadarezer and sent his son to greet David and congratulate him.
- David dedicated the vessels of gold, silver, and brass unto the Most High, along with the spoil taken from Edom, Moab, Ammon, the Philistines, and Amalek.
- Again the account states plainly that the Most High preserved David wherever he went, marking Him as the source of every victory.

**[1 Chronicles 18:14-17](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v14)**
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v14)</sup> So David reigned over all Israel, and executed judgment and justice among all his people.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v15)</sup> And Joab the son of Zeruiah was over the host; and Jehoshaphat the son of Ahilud, recorder.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v16)</sup> And Zadok the son of Ahitub, and Abimelech the son of Abiathar, were the priests; and Shavsha was scribe;
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/1-chronicles/18#v17)</sup> And Benaiah the son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and the Pelethites; and the sons of David were chief about the king.

- David reigned over all Israel and executed judgment and justice among all his people.
- The list of his officers follows: Joab over the host, Jehoshaphat the recorder, Zadok and Abimelech the priests, Shavsha the scribe, and Benaiah over the Cherethites and Pelethites.
- Order in the kingdom mattered; every man had his place and his charge under the king.

# 1 Chronicles 19: War With Ammon and Syria

Read the chapter → [1 Chronicles 19](/bible/1-chronicles/19)

**[1 Chronicles 19:1-5](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v1)</sup> Now it came to pass after this, that Nahash the king of the children of Ammon died, and his son reigned in his stead.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v2)</sup> And David said, I will shew kindness unto Hanun the son of Nahash, because his father shewed kindness to me. And David sent messengers to comfort him concerning his father. So the servants of David came into the land of the children of Ammon to Hanun, to comfort him.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v3)</sup> But the princes of the children of Ammon said to Hanun, Thinkest thou that David doth honour thy father, that he hath sent comforters unto thee? are not his servants come unto thee for to search, and to overthrow, and to spy out the land?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v4)</sup> Wherefore Hanun took David’s servants, and shaved them, and cut off their garments in the midst hard by their buttocks, and sent them away.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v5)</sup> Then there went certain, and told David how the men were served. And he sent to meet them: for the men were greatly ashamed. And the king said, Tarry at Jericho until your beards be grown, and then return.

- When Nahash king of Ammon died, David set out to show kindness to his son Hanun because Nahash had shown kindness to David.
- The princes of Ammon poisoned Hanun's mind, telling him David's comforters were really spies sent to search out and overthrow the land, though that was not the case.
- Hanun shaved David's servants and cut off their garments by their buttocks and sent them away, and the men were greatly ashamed.
- The men were ashamed because they knew how to carry themselves as men of the Most High; our ancestors had far more respect for themselves than to be exposed this way.
- David sent word for them to stay at Jericho until their beards grew again, for it was not the custom of Israel to cut the beard.
  - **[Leviticus 19:27](/bible/leviticus/19#v27)**
    > <sup>[27](/bible/leviticus/19#v27)</sup> Ye shall not round the corners of your heads, neither shalt thou mar the corners of thy beard.

    A commandment not to mar the corners of the beard, which is why the shaving was so shameful.

**[1 Chronicles 19:6-15](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v6)</sup> And when the children of Ammon saw that they had made themselves odious to David, Hanun and the children of Ammon sent a thousand talents of silver to hire them chariots and horsemen out of Mesopotamia, and out of Syria–maachah, and out of Zobah.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v7)</sup> So they hired thirty and two thousand chariots, and the king of Maachah and his people; who came and pitched before Medeba. And the children of Ammon gathered themselves together from their cities, and came to battle.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v8)</sup> And when David heard of it, he sent Joab, and all the host of the mighty men.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v9)</sup> And the children of Ammon came out, and put the battle in array before the gate of the city: and the kings that were come were by themselves in the field.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v10)</sup> Now when Joab saw that the battle was set against him before and behind, he chose out of all the choice of Israel, and put them in array against the Syrians.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v11)</sup> And the rest of the people he delivered unto the hand of Abishai his brother, and they set themselves in array against the children of Ammon.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v12)</sup> And he said, If the Syrians be too strong for me, then thou shalt help me: but if the children of Ammon be too strong for thee, then I will help thee.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v13)</sup> Be of good courage, and let us behave ourselves valiantly for our people, and for the cities of our God: and let the Lord do that which is good in his sight.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v14)</sup> So Joab and the people that were with him drew nigh before the Syrians unto the battle; and they fled before him.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v15)</sup> And when the children of Ammon saw that the Syrians were fled, they likewise fled before Abishai his brother, and entered into the city. Then Joab came to Jerusalem.

- Seeing that they had made themselves odious to David, the Ammonites hired thirty and two thousand chariots along with the king of Maachah and his people.
- David sent Joab and the mighty men; the Ammonites set the battle before the gate of the city while the hired kings stood apart in the field.
- Joab found the battle set against him before and behind, so he divided the army: he took the choice of Israel against the Syrians and gave the rest to his brother Abishai against Ammon.
- Joab set the plan that if the Syrians were too strong for him Abishai would help him, and if Ammon were too strong for Abishai then Joab would come to his aid.
  - **[1 Chronicles 19:13](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v13)</sup> Be of good courage, and let us behave ourselves valiantly for our people, and for the cities of our God: and let the Lord do that which is good in his sight.

    Be of good courage and behave valiantly for the people and the cities of our God, and let the Most High do what is good in His sight.
- The Syrians fled before Joab, and when Ammon saw the Syrians flee they also fled into the city, and Joab returned to Jerusalem.

**[1 Chronicles 19:16-19](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v16)**
> <sup>[16](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v16)</sup> And when the Syrians saw that they were put to the worse before Israel, they sent messengers, and drew forth the Syrians that were beyond the river: and Shophach the captain of the host of Hadarezer went before them.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v17)</sup> And it was told David; and he gathered all Israel, and passed over Jordan, and came upon them, and set the battle in array against them. So when David had put the battle in array against the Syrians, they fought with him.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v18)</sup> But the Syrians fled before Israel; and David slew of the Syrians seven thousand men which fought in chariots, and forty thousand footmen, and killed Shophach the captain of the host.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/1-chronicles/19#v19)</sup> And when the servants of Hadarezer saw that they were put to the worse before Israel, they made peace with David, and became his servants: neither would the Syrians help the children of Ammon any more.

- The beaten Syrians called for reinforcements from beyond the river under Shophach, captain of Hadarezer's host.
- David gathered all Israel, passed over Jordan, set the battle in array, and slew seven thousand chariot men and forty thousand footmen, and killed Shophach the captain.
- The servants of Hadarezer made peace with David and became his servants, and the Syrians would no longer help Ammon; they wanted nothing more to do with David and the mighty men.

# 1 Chronicles 20: The Fall of Rabbah and the Giants

Read the chapter → [1 Chronicles 20](/bible/1-chronicles/20)

**[1 Chronicles 20:1-3](/bible/1-chronicles/20#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/1-chronicles/20#v1)</sup> And it came to pass, that after the year was expired, at the time that kings go out to battle, Joab led forth the power of the army, and wasted the country of the children of Ammon, and came and besieged Rabbah. But David tarried at Jerusalem. And Joab smote Rabbah, and destroyed it.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-chronicles/20#v2)</sup> And David took the crown of their king from off his head, and found it to weigh a talent of gold, and there were precious stones in it; and it was set upon David’s head: and he brought also exceeding much spoil out of the city.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-chronicles/20#v3)</sup> And he brought out the people that were in it, and cut them with saws, and with harrows of iron, and with axes. Even so dealt David with all the cities of the children of Ammon. And David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

- After the year expired, at the time kings go to battle, Joab led the army, wasted the country of Ammon, and besieged Rabbah while David stayed at Jerusalem.
- Joab smote Rabbah and destroyed it; David took the crown of their king, a talent of gold set with precious stones, and it was set upon David's head.
- David brought out much spoil and set the people to labor with saws, iron implements, and axes, then all the people returned to Jerusalem.

**[1 Chronicles 20:4-8](/bible/1-chronicles/20#v4)**
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-chronicles/20#v4)</sup> And it came to pass after this, that there arose war at Gezer with the Philistines; at which time Sibbechai the Hushathite slew Sippai, that was of the children of the giant: and they were subdued.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-chronicles/20#v5)</sup> And there was war again with the Philistines; and Elhanan the son of Jair slew Lahmi the brother of Goliath the Gittite, whose spear staff was like a weaver’s beam.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-chronicles/20#v6)</sup> And yet again there was war at Gath, where was a man of great stature, whose fingers and toes were four and twenty, six on each hand, and six on each foot: and he also was the son of the giant.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-chronicles/20#v7)</sup> But when he defied Israel, Jonathan the son of Shimea David’s brother slew him.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/1-chronicles/20#v8)</sup> These were born unto the giant in Gath; and they fell by the hand of David, and by the hand of his servants.

- War arose again at Gezer with the Philistines, and Sibbechai the Hushathite slew Sippai, one of the children of the giant, and the Philistines were subdued.
- In another battle Elhanan the son of Jair slew Lahmi the brother of Goliath the Gittite, whose spear staff was like a weaver's beam.
- At Gath a man of great stature with six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot defied Israel, and Jonathan the son of Shimea, David's brother, slew him.
- These giants were born to the giant in Gath and fell by the hand of David and by the hand of his servants.

# 1 Chronicles 21: David Numbers Israel

Read the chapter → [1 Chronicles 21](/bible/1-chronicles/21)

**[1 Chronicles 21:1-6](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v1)</sup> And Satan stood up against Israel, and provoked David to number Israel.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v2)</sup> And David said to Joab and to the rulers of the people, Go, number Israel from Beer–sheba even to Dan; and bring the number of them to me, that I may know it.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v3)</sup> And Joab answered, The Lord make his people an hundred times so many more as they be: but, my lord the king, are they not all my lord’s servants? why then doth my lord require this thing? why will he be a cause of trespass to Israel?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v4)</sup> Nevertheless the king’s word prevailed against Joab. Wherefore Joab departed, and went throughout all Israel, and came to Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v5)</sup> And Joab gave the sum of the number of the people unto David. And all they of Israel were a thousand thousand and an hundred thousand men that drew sword: and Judah was four hundred threescore and ten thousand men that drew sword.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v6)</sup> But Levi and Benjamin counted he not among them: for the king’s word was abominable to Joab.

- Satan stood up against Israel and provoked David to number the people; David commanded Joab to number Israel from Beersheba to Dan.
- Joab objected, asking why the king would require this thing and be a cause of trespass to Israel, but the king's word prevailed.
- Joab numbered the people: a thousand thousand and a hundred thousand men that drew sword in Israel, and four hundred threescore and ten thousand in Judah.
- Joab left Levi and Benjamin uncounted because the king's word was abominable to him.

**[1 Chronicles 21:7-13](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v7)</sup> And God was displeased with this thing; therefore he smote Israel.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v8)</sup> And David said unto God, I have sinned greatly, because I have done this thing: but now, I beseech thee, do away the iniquity of thy servant; for I have done very foolishly.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v9)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Gad, David’s seer, saying,
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v10)</sup> Go and tell David, saying, Thus saith the Lord, I offer thee three things: choose thee one of them, that I may do it unto thee.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v11)</sup> So Gad came to David, and said unto him, Thus saith the Lord, Choose thee
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v12)</sup> Either three years’ famine; or three months to be destroyed before thy foes, while that the sword of thine enemies overtaketh thee; or else three days the sword of the Lord, even the pestilence, in the land, and the angel of the Lord destroying throughout all the coasts of Israel. Now therefore advise thyself what word I shall bring again to him that sent me.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v13)</sup> And David said unto Gad, I am in a great strait: let me fall now into the hand of the Lord; for very great are his mercies: but let me not fall into the hand of man.

- The Most High was displeased with the numbering and smote Israel; David confessed he had sinned greatly and done very foolishly and begged that the iniquity be taken away.
- The Most High sent Gad the seer to offer David three choices: three years of famine, three months of destruction before his foes, or three days of pestilence with the destroying angel.
- David chose to fall into the hand of the Most High rather than the hand of man, saying very great are His mercies, for he knew man would not show him the mercy the Most High would.

**[1 Chronicles 21:14-17](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v14)**
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v14)</sup> So the Lord sent pestilence upon Israel: and there fell of Israel seventy thousand men.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v15)</sup> And God sent an angel unto Jerusalem to destroy it: and as he was destroying, the Lord beheld, and he repented him of the evil, and said to the angel that destroyed, It is enough, stay now thine hand. And the angel of the Lord stood by the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v16)</sup> And David lifted up his eyes, and saw the angel of the Lord stand between the earth and the heaven, having a drawn sword in his hand stretched out over Jerusalem. Then David and the elders of Israel, who were clothed in sackcloth, fell upon their faces.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v17)</sup> And David said unto God, Is it not I that commanded the people to be numbered? even I it is that have sinned and done evil indeed; but as for these sheep, what have they done? let thine hand, I pray thee, O Lord my God, be on me, and on my father’s house; but not on thy people, that they should be plagued.

- The Most High sent pestilence, and seventy thousand men of Israel fell; the angel was sent to destroy Jerusalem, but the Most High beheld it, repented of the evil, and told the angel it was enough to stay his hand.
- David lifted up his eyes and saw the angel of the Most High standing between earth and heaven with a drawn sword stretched over Jerusalem, and he and the elders, clothed in sackcloth, fell on their faces.
- David pleaded that he alone had sinned by commanding the numbering, asking that the hand be on him and his father's house but not on the people, who as sheep had done nothing.
- Our forefathers had the pleasure of seeing such acts of the Most High in their midst; in these last days we walk by faith in what the scriptures say, for the war is now of a different kind.

**[1 Chronicles 21:18-27](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v18)**
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v18)</sup> Then the angel of the Lord commanded Gad to say to David, that David should go up, and set up an altar unto the Lord in the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v19)</sup> And David went up at the saying of Gad, which he spake in the name of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v20)</sup> And Ornan turned back, and saw the angel; and his four sons with him hid themselves. Now Ornan was threshing wheat.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v21)</sup> And as David came to Ornan, Ornan looked and saw David, and went out of the threshingfloor, and bowed himself to David with his face to the ground.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v22)</sup> Then David said to Ornan, Grant me the place of this threshingfloor, that I may build an altar therein unto the Lord: thou shalt grant it me for the full price: that the plague may be stayed from the people.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v23)</sup> And Ornan said unto David, Take it to thee, and let my lord the king do that which is good in his eyes: lo, I give thee the oxen also for burnt offerings, and the threshing instruments for wood, and the wheat for the meat offering; I give it all.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v24)</sup> And king David said to Ornan, Nay; but I will verily buy it for the full price: for I will not take that which is thine for the Lord, nor offer burnt offerings without cost.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v25)</sup> So David gave to Ornan for the place six hundred shekels of gold by weight.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v26)</sup> And David built there an altar unto the Lord, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings, and called upon the Lord; and he answered him from heaven by fire upon the altar of burnt offering.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v27)</sup> And the Lord commanded the angel; and he put up his sword again into the sheath thereof.

- The angel commanded Gad to tell David to set up an altar in the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite, and David went up at the word spoken in the name of the Most High.
- Ornan, threshing wheat, turned and saw the angel and hid with his four sons, then saw David and bowed to the ground; David asked to buy the threshingfloor for the full price so the plague might be stayed.
- Ornan offered to give it all, the place, the oxen, the threshing instruments for wood, and the wheat, but David refused to take what was another man's or to offer burnt offerings without cost.
- David paid six hundred shekels of gold, built the altar, offered burnt offerings and peace offerings, and the Most High answered him by fire from heaven and commanded the angel to sheathe his sword.
- David paid the full price rather than take the offering for free, and this is a pattern for Israel today: what is given to the Most High should cost the giver something.
  - **[2 Samuel 24:24](/bible/2-samuel/24#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/2-samuel/24#v24)</sup> And the king said unto Araunah, Nay; but I will surely buy it of thee at a price: neither will I offer burnt offerings unto the Lord my God of that which doth cost me nothing. So David bought the threshingfloor and the oxen for fifty shekels of silver.

    David would not offer to the Most High that which cost him nothing, the same conviction that made him pay the full price.

**[1 Chronicles 21:28-30](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v28)**
> <sup>[28](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v28)</sup> At that time when David saw that the Lord had answered him in the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite, then he sacrificed there.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v29)</sup> For the tabernacle of the Lord, which Moses made in the wilderness, and the altar of the burnt offering, were at that season in the high place at Gibeon.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v30)</sup> But David could not go before it to enquire of God: for he was afraid because of the sword of the angel of the Lord.

- When David saw that the Most High answered him at the threshingfloor of Ornan, he sacrificed there.
- The tabernacle Moses made and the altar of burnt offering were then at the high place in Gibeon.
- David could not go to Gibeon to inquire of the Most High because he was afraid of the sword of the angel, so he worshipped at the new altar instead.
