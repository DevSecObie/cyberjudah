## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Baruch 4 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-6, 10-18, 24-25, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Baruch 4:1](/bible/baruch/4#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/baruch/4#v1)</sup> This is the book of the commandments of God, and the law that endureth for ever: all they that keep it shall come to life; but such as leave it shall die.

- The third biblical fact: the whole Bible is law. Tell your so-called Christian grandma and your auntie and your pastors: the whole Bible is law. “This is the book of the commandments of God, and the law that endureth for ever: all they that keep it shall come to life; but such as leave it shall die.”
- Baruch goes here because Pastor Gino Jennings and his church subscribe to the Apocrypha, which most Christian pastors don’t acknowledge, though the whole King James Bible originally had it. So his own congregation, who come at Israel saying we are wrong, misguided and a cult, can be answered out of a book they believe in.
- Baruch was Jeremiah the prophet’s scribe. Most of the prophets had scribes or recorders: Moses, Samuel, Elijah, Elisha, Joshua. Baruch is one of them, and Baruch is basically Jeremiah’s words that Baruch wrote down.
- “This is the book of the commandments of God, and the law that endureth for ever: all they that keep it shall come to life.” Those that keep the commandments of this book come to life, and that comes from the words of Moses back in Deuteronomy 30 and many other places in Deuteronomy. “But such as leave it shall die”: back to blessing and cursing, life and death.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Esdras 7:57-59](/bible/2-esdras/7#v57)**
    > <sup>[57](/bible/2-esdras/7#v57)</sup> Then answered he me, and said, This is the condition of the battle, which man that is born upon the earth shall fight;
    >
    > <sup>[58](/bible/2-esdras/7#v58)</sup> That, if he be overcome, he shall suffer as thou hast said: but if he get the victory, he shall receive the thing that I say.
    >
    > <sup>[59](/bible/2-esdras/7#v59)</sup> For this is the life whereof Moses spake unto the people while he lived, saying, Choose thee life, that thou mayest live.

    Ezra says the exact same thing. “This is the condition of the battle, which man that is born upon the earth shall fight; That, if he be overcome, he shall suffer as thou hast said: but if he get the victory, he shall receive the thing that I say.”

**[Baruch 4:2](/bible/baruch/4#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/baruch/4#v2)</sup> Turn thee, O Jacob, and take hold of it: walk in the presence of the light thereof, that thou mayest be illuminated.

- “Turn thee, O Jacob, and take hold of it.” Turn thee who? O Jacob. Not all nations, not everybody, not the rainbow coalition. O Jacob. Take hold of the commandment of life, the book of life.
- “Walk in the presence of the light thereof”: apply what is in it, “that thou mayest be illuminated.” A lot of black folks are afraid of the Illuminati, the Masons, skull and crossbones and the secret families. The Bible says keeping his laws you become truly illuminated, truly enlightened. Not Satan’s enlightenment; that’s garbage, that’s temporary. God’s laws are light: the same laws a lot of Christian churches refuse to apply and teach, saying they are done away with.

**[Baruch 4:3](/bible/baruch/4#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/baruch/4#v3)</sup> Give not thine honour to another, nor the things that are profitable unto thee to a strange nation.

- “Give not thine honour to another, nor the things that are profitable unto thee to a strange nation.” Our honour is our laws, our statutes, our commandments, the knowledge we have here: this truth.
- The problem we always had in captivity and exile was sharing our knowledge with the nations, teaching them our laws while in exile, not ruling. We taught the Arabs the Bible, and they conquered us and established Islam. We taught the white man Judaism, and he conquered us and became a Jew. We taught the white man who came over here how to farm and survive the land, and he conquered us and called us illegal aliens.
- Stop teaching the nations while we are in slavery; it is not their time. Now is our time to come together. When our king returns and puts us in authority and rulership, then we teach the nations.
- “Give not thine honour to another, nor the things that are profitable unto thee to a strange nation.” Do not give the nations the understanding God gave you; going back to verse 1, don’t give it to them. Israel tended to take what God gave us, the commandments of God, the Bible, and teach strange nations.

**[Baruch 4:4](/bible/baruch/4#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/baruch/4#v4)</sup> O Israel, happy are we: for things that are pleasing to God are made known unto us.

- “O Israel, happy are we: for things that are pleasing to God are made known unto us.” That is an exclusive statement. Christianity, and the Catholic Church it derives from, has made the Bible catholic, universal. It is originally and still a personal book, given to a particular people by a particular people for a particular people: the personal record of a nation of people.

**[Baruch 4:5-6](/bible/baruch/4#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/baruch/4#v5)</sup> Be of good cheer, my people, the memorial of Israel.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/baruch/4#v6)</sup> Ye were sold to the nations, not for your destruction: but because ye moved God to wrath, ye were delivered unto the enemies.

- “Be of good cheer, my people, the memorial of Israel. Ye were sold to the nations”: keep that in mind, sold to the nations. Baruch is a very small book, but there is a lot in it.
- “Not for your destruction”, not to destroy you entirely, “but because ye moved God to wrath”: because we pissed God off. “Ye were delivered unto the enemies”, enemies plural.

**[Baruch 4:10-12](/bible/baruch/4#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/baruch/4#v10)</sup> For I saw the captivity of my sons and daughters, which the Everlasting brought upon them.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/baruch/4#v11)</sup> With joy did I nourish them; but sent them away with weeping and mourning.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/baruch/4#v12)</sup> Let no man rejoice over me, a widow, and forsaken of many, who for the sins of my children am left desolate; because they departed from the law of God.

- “For I saw the captivity of my sons and daughters, which the Everlasting brought upon them.” This is Jerusalem speaking, our mother, our land, speaking to us: the Most High speaking to us through the spirit in terms of Jerusalem.
- “With joy did I nourish them; but sent them away with weeping and mourning. Let no man rejoice over me, a widow.” The widow is Jerusalem our mother, “forsaken of many, who for the sins of my children am left desolate”: for the sins of my children I, Jerusalem, am left desolate.
- “Because they departed from the law of God.” Their land was laid desolate based upon them departing from the law of God.

**[Baruch 4:13](/bible/baruch/4#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/baruch/4#v13)</sup> They knew not his statutes, nor walked in the ways of his commandments, nor trod in the paths of discipline in his righteousness.

- “They knew not his statutes, nor walked in the ways of his commandments, nor trod in the paths of discipline in his righteousness.” The path of discipline is righteousness, which is the laws and commandments of God.
- So far: in 4:1 they shall come to life; in verse 4 the things pleasing to God; in verse 6 delivered to the enemies; in verse 12 the law of God; in 13 discipline, which is righteousness.

**[Baruch 4:14-16](/bible/baruch/4#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/baruch/4#v14)</sup> Let them that dwell about Sion come, and remember ye the captivity of my sons and daughters, which the Everlasting hath brought upon them.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/baruch/4#v15)</sup> For he hath brought a nation upon them from far, a shameless nation, and of a strange language, who neither reverenced old man, nor pitied child.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/baruch/4#v16)</sup> These have carried away the dear beloved children of the widow, and left her that was alone desolate without daughters.

- “Let them that dwell about Sion come, and remember ye the captivity of my sons and daughters.” “For he hath brought a nation upon them from far, a shameless nation, and of a strange language, who neither reverenced old man, nor pitied child.” That is speaking of Babylon, but it goes even to later on: into Spain, Portugal, the Europeans.
- “These have carried away the dear beloved children of the widow”: the dear beloved children. Keep that in mind. “And left her that was alone desolate without daughters.”

**[Baruch 4:17-18](/bible/baruch/4#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/baruch/4#v17)</sup> But what can I help you?
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/baruch/4#v18)</sup> For he that brought these plagues upon you will deliver you from the hands of your enemies.

- “But what can I help you?” How can I help you, says God. “For he that brought these plagues upon you will deliver you from the hands of your enemies.” He that brought the plagues upon you will be the one to deliver you from the hands of your enemies.

**[Baruch 4:24](/bible/baruch/4#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/baruch/4#v24)</sup> Like as now the neighbours of Sion have seen your captivity: so shall they see shortly your salvation from our God, which shall come upon you with great glory, and brightness of the Everlasting.

- We are going to have redemption in these last days; all we have to do is endure the tribulation. “Like as now the neighbours of Sion have seen your captivity: so shall they see shortly your salvation from our God”. They have seen us in slavery; shortly they are going to see our salvation.

**[Baruch 4:25](/bible/baruch/4#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/baruch/4#v25)</sup> My children, suffer patiently the wrath that is come upon you from God: for thine enemy hath persecuted thee; but shortly thou shalt see his destruction, and shalt tread upon his neck.

- “My children, suffer patiently the wrath that is come upon you from God: for thine enemy hath persecuted thee”. He put us on slave ships and scattered us all over the world. He raped, robbed and murdered, and took our resources from all over the world. He took the lands of our brothers and sisters over here in America, the so-called Native Americans. He has done a lot of evil.
- “But shortly thou shalt see his destruction, and shalt tread upon his neck.” We will tread upon the neck of our enemies.
