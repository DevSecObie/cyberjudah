## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 1 Maccabees 1 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-15, 21-24, 41-52, 54, 56-57, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[1 Maccabees 1:1-9](/bible/1-maccabees/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-maccabees/1#v1)</sup> And it happened, after that Alexander son of Philip, the Macedonian, who came out of the land of Chettiim, had smitten Darius king of the Persians and Medes, that he reigned in his stead, the first over Greece,

> <sup>[3](/bible/1-maccabees/1#v3)</sup> And went through to the ends of the earth, and took spoils of many nations, insomuch that the earth was quiet before him; whereupon he was exalted, and his heart was lifted up.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-maccabees/1#v4)</sup> And he gathered a mighty strong host, and ruled over countries, and nations, and kings, who became tributaries unto him.

- Who were the Greeks? From the time of Philip and Alexander. “Alexander son of Philip, the Macedonian”, who “had smitten Darius king of the Persians and Medes”, “reigned in his stead, the first over Greece”: Alexander the Greek became the first.
- “Alexander son of Philip, the Macedonian, who came out of the land of Chettiim, had smitten Darius king of the Persians and Medes, that he reigned in his stead”: he took over Persia. The Persians were not Israelites, but they were dark-skinned people. How are you going to claim their antiquity when that is not you? That is how sick these people are.
- “The first over Greece”: Alexander was the first king. That is what the brothers showed him in the eighth chapter of Daniel when they met him in the field, and he said, that must be me: I am getting ready to fulfill prophecy.
- The original Greeks were of the seed of Japheth, Noah’s son, but there came a time when Esau overthrew them and took their place. The old books of the scholars, written in 1879 and 1890 when few of us could read, set down the genealogy of Philip of Macedon back through Esau, Isaac and Abraham, and say plainly that King Philip was a descendant of Esau, the son of Isaac. They wrote those books thinking we would never be able to read and reason.

**[1 Maccabees 1:21-23](/bible/1-maccabees/1#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/1-maccabees/1#v21)</sup> And entered proudly into the sanctuary, and took away the golden altar, and the candlestick of light, and all the vessels thereof,
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/1-maccabees/1#v22)</sup> And the table of the shewbread, and the pouring vessels, and the vials, and the censers of gold, and the veil, and the crowns, and the golden ornaments that were before the temple, all which he pulled off.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/1-maccabees/1#v23)</sup> He took also the silver and the gold, and the precious vessels: also he took the hidden treasures which he found.

- The Greeks entered the sanctuary and took the golden altar, candlestick, vessels, shewbread table, veil, crowns, ornaments, silver, gold and hidden treasures. These were sacred to all Israel, but the desecration had particular weight for the Levites, whose life's service was to maintain the things of God's house. Understanding that charge explains why Mattathias, a Levite, was so deeply moved by what he saw.

**[1 Maccabees 1:24](/bible/1-maccabees/1#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/1-maccabees/1#v24)</sup> And when he had taken all away, he went into his own land, having made a great massacre, and spoken very proudly.

- The plunder was accompanied by a great massacre and proud boasting. The account is not simply the loss of possessions: Israel's people were killed and the holy things entrusted to them were defiled. This forms the background to Mattathias' grief and anger.
