So too, men had to guide us through the scriptures to understand who we are, what must be done and what lies ahead; those things had not been made clear to us in the Christian church.
  - **[Romans 3:3-4](/bible/romans/3#v3)**

This is the kind of struggle Mattathias put before his people: obey and remain with the covenant under hardship, or yield to the Greeks and lose identity and history. Abraham's willingness to go to the end in obedience set a measure of faith for them to remember.

**[1 Maccabees 2:53](/bible/1-maccabees/2#v53)**

- Joseph kept the commandment through distress and became lord of Egypt. Those troubles did not prevent God from putting him in position to deliver his people during the famine. Mattathias reminded the men that hardship had already been endured by a forefather for a greater purpose.

**[1 Maccabees 2:59](/bible/1-maccabees/2#v59)**

History preserved in a family became strength for the whole nation.

Their trials show that hardship is not new. The present loss of Israelite identity among families adds another difficulty: many relatives do not even recognize the people to whom this history belongs.

**[1 Maccabees 2:64](/bible/1-maccabees/2#v64)**

> <sup>[64](/bible/1-maccabees/2#v64)</sup> Wherefore, ye my sons, be valiant, and shew yourselves men in the behalf of the law; for by it shall ye obtain glory.

- Mattathias started a rebellion: we are going to fight the Greeks back, defend the temple, and not let the white man destroy our people; win or lose, we die for the laws. The Most High put the Holy Spirit upon Mattathias.
- On his deathbed he says, “ye my sons, be valiant, and shew yourselves men in the behalf of the law; for by it shall ye obtain glory.” This is what the black man has never learned. He shows himself a man for the Hoover Crips, for the Bloods, for MS13, for the local drug dealers. We need men to stand up for God’s laws.

**[1 Maccabees 2:65](/bible/1-maccabees/2#v65)**

> <sup>[65](/bible/1-maccabees/2#v65)</sup> And, behold, I know that your brother Simon is a man of counsel, give ear unto him alway: he shall be a father unto you.

- “And, behold, I know that your brother Simon is a man of counsel”: he was a genius, a tactician. “Give ear unto him alway”: always listen to Simon, he is the brain of you all. “He shall be a father unto you”: listen to him as a father, and let Simon instruct you what to do.
- “I know that your brother Simon is a man of counsel, give ear unto him alway: he shall be a father unto you.” He didn’t say I think; he said I know. Mattathias knew his sons.
- As parents we need to know our children, and see early which one might leave the truth, which one is showing signs of being a hoe, which one wants to be a gang member, and which one believes. Don’t be ignorant. Watch your kids and see whether the spirit is upon them.

**[1 Maccabees 2:66](/bible/1-maccabees/2#v66)**

> <sup>[66](/bible/1-maccabees/2#v66)</sup> As for Judas Maccabeus, he hath been mighty and strong, even from his youth up: let him be your captain, and fight the battle of the people.

- “As for Judas Maccabeus, he hath been mighty and strong, even from his youth up: let him be your captain, and fight the battle of the people.” Your brother had hands from the beginning; he will be your fighter. Judas was the warrior, the hammer, and got the fame; but every hammer needs a hand, and Simon was the mind behind it, telling Judas go here, go there, mobilize over here with this many men. Simon was the brain and Judas the brawn.
- Toussaint Louverture's genius was the same. He combined brilliant military strategy with statecraft and turned a fractured rebellion into the world's most successful slave uprising. By 1800 he controlled all of Hispaniola, Levi and Simeon together; he restored the plantations with paid labor rather than slaves, and his 1801 constitution abolished slavery forever and made all citizens equal regardless of skin color. He defied Napoleon, was captured and died in a French prison in 1803, but his work made Haiti's independence possible in 1804.
- The tactics Toussaint used against the French are the same tactics the Hasmonean family, Judas Maccabeus and Simon, used against the Greeks: a small, under-equipped force against a superpower, using the terrain, strategic retreats, the bait-and-switch ambush. At the pass of Beth-horon, Judas hid his men in the heights and waited until the Greek army was strung out in a long column in the narrow pass, then charged downhill and routed them. It would be no surprise if Toussaint read Maccabees.
