## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Philemon 1 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 10-18, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Philemon 1:10](/bible/philemon/1#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/philemon/1#v10)</sup> I beseech thee for my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my bonds:

- “I beseech thee for my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my bonds.” Paul met him when he was under arrest.

**[Philemon 1:11-12](/bible/philemon/1#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/philemon/1#v11)</sup> Which in time past was to thee unprofitable, but now profitable to thee and to me:
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/philemon/1#v12)</sup> Whom I have sent again: thou therefore receive him, that is, mine own bowels:

- “Which in time past was to thee unprofitable, but now profitable to thee and to me: Whom I have sent again: thou therefore receive him, that is, mine own bowels.” Paul is writing to Philemon: receive him as if you were receiving me.

**[Philemon 1:13-14](/bible/philemon/1#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/philemon/1#v13)</sup> Whom I would have retained with me, that in thy stead he might have ministered unto me in the bonds of the gospel:
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/philemon/1#v14)</sup> But without thy mind would I do nothing; that thy benefit should not be as it were of necessity, but willingly.

- “Whom I would have retained with me, that in thy stead he might have ministered unto me in the bonds of the gospel.” Paul could have kept him to help him with the gospel, but sent him back: “without thy mind would I do nothing”, so that if he stays, it is sent willingly, not of necessity.
- “But without thy mind would I do nothing.” You have to have the mindset of a leader to get anything done. You have to see it mentally and visualize it before it happens. That is how important the mindset of a leader is, “that thy benefit should not be as it were of necessity, but willingly.”

  Precepts:
  - **[Nehemiah 4:6](/bible/nehemiah/4#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/nehemiah/4#v6)</sup> So built we the wall; and all the wall was joined together unto the half thereof: for the people had a mind to work.

    “So built we the wall.” All through our history our forefathers were building, and what we do now is no different. As the Bishop brings out about the little sanctuaries, what we do today is no different from what our forefathers always did. Paul went to Galatia, Colossae, Corinth and Thessalonica: those were sanctuaries, just like the ones you see today, and each of us has put a hand into building every one of them.

**[Philemon 1:15-16](/bible/philemon/1#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/philemon/1#v15)</sup> For perhaps he therefore departed for a season, that thou shouldest receive him for ever;
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/philemon/1#v16)</sup> Not now as a servant, but above a servant, a brother beloved, specially to me, but how much more unto thee, both in the flesh, and in the Lord?

- “For perhaps he therefore departed for a season, that thou shouldest receive him for ever.” He ran away from Philemon. Onesimus was a servant to Philemon. “Not now as a servant, but above a servant, a brother beloved”: deal with him as a brother.

**[Philemon 1:17-18](/bible/philemon/1#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/philemon/1#v17)</sup> If thou count me therefore a partner, receive him as myself.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/philemon/1#v18)</sup> If he hath wronged thee, or oweth thee ought, put that on mine account;

- “If thou count me therefore a partner, receive him as myself. If he hath wronged thee, or oweth thee ought, put that on mine account.” Was he a servant because he was poor, or did he do something bad? He owed him: he stole something or broke something. Paul was dealing with servants who were Hebrews, Israelites in servitude to other Israelites.
