## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Malachi 1 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-4, 6-8, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Malachi 1:1-3](/bible/malachi/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/malachi/1#v1)</sup> The burden of the word of the Lord to Israel by Malachi.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/malachi/1#v2)</sup> I have loved you, saith the Lord. Yet ye say, Wherein hast thou loved us? Was not Esau Jacob’s brother? saith the Lord: yet I loved Jacob,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/malachi/1#v3)</sup> And I hated Esau, and laid his mountains and his heritage waste for the dragons of the wilderness.

- “I have loved you, saith the Lord. Yet ye say, Wherein hast thou loved us?” We ask the Lord that because it seems like the Lord don’t love us: we’re in the ghetto, poverty stricken, incarcerated, on drugs, the women are just hoes, the men are delinquent dads. Where have you loved us?
- But the Lord responds, “Was not Esau Jacob’s brother?” Wasn’t the white man your daddy’s brother? “Yet I loved Jacob”: yet I loved your father Jacob, I loved you.
- “And I hated Esau”: whoa, that’s never been taught in church. “And laid his mountains and his heritage waste”: that was when they lived in the mountains of Georgia, Russia, the Caucasus mountains. That’s when their heritage was laid waste, “for the dragons of the wilderness”: that’s the proof, the dragons and the lizards of the wilderness, that’s what was all out in their wilderness.
- Israel asks the Lord in the spirit, “Wherein hast thou loved us?” You love us when we were constantly in captivity, over and over? The Lord answers with a question: “Was not Esau Jacob’s brother?” “yet I loved Jacob.”

  Precepts:
  - **[Romans 9:13](/bible/romans/9#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/romans/9#v13)</sup> As it is written, Jacob have I loved, but Esau have I hated.

    The New Testament, for you Christians out there: “As it is written, Jacob have I loved, but Esau have I hated.” Paul had to remind us of that, because we were in school with them and hanging out with them, and he had to remind us.
  - **[Romans 9:10-13](/bible/romans/9#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/romans/9#v10)</sup> And not only this; but when Rebecca also had conceived by one, even by our father Isaac;
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/romans/9#v11)</sup> (For the children being not yet born, neither having done any good or evil, that the purpose of God according to election might stand, not of works, but of him that calleth;)
    >
    > <sup>[12](/bible/romans/9#v12)</sup> It was said unto her, The elder shall serve the younger.
    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/romans/9#v13)</sup> As it is written, Jacob have I loved, but Esau have I hated.

    Let's see what he said in Romans, New Testament. “And not only this; but when Rebecca also had conceived by one, even by our father Isaac; (For the children being not yet born, neither having done any good or evil, that the purpose of God according to election might stand, not of works, but of him that calleth.” The purpose of God according to election. So God was the one behind us choosing. He elected us. All praises to the Most High for that. Not of works, but of him that calleth.
  - **[Psalms 50:16](/bible/psalms/50#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/psalms/50#v16)</sup> But unto the wicked God saith, What hast thou to do to declare my statutes, or that thou shouldest take my covenant in thy mouth?

    “But unto the wicked God saith, What hast thou to do to declare my statutes, or that thou shouldest take my covenant in thy mouth?” Esau is the wicked, the same one we just read about in Malachi.

**[Malachi 1:4](/bible/malachi/1#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/malachi/1#v4)</sup> Whereas Edom saith, We are impoverished, but we will return and build the desolate places; thus saith the Lord of hosts, They shall build, but I will throw down; and they shall call them, The border of wickedness, and, The people against whom the Lord hath indignation for ever.

- “Whereas Edom saith, We are impoverished”: we ain’t got nothing, we’re on the bottom, because they were poverty stricken when they lived in those caves. “But we will return and build the desolate places”: write this down, the Renaissance era. They built up their cities.
- “Thus saith the Lord of hosts, They shall build, but I will throw down”: Christ will come and destroy everything they’ve built. They have built all of this glory; people want to come to the United States of America, Britain, London, these glorious cities. God said yeah, they’re going to rebuild, but I’m going to throw it down. “And they shall call them, The border of wickedness.”
- “And, The people against whom the Lord hath indignation for ever”: when you’re teaching you want to stress those two words, the people. The Christian will say God only hates Esau. No: the people. He has righteous anger against them for ever, and there’s nothing you can change. Some of you out there are marrying them, and God don’t give a damn. Some of you women are marrying them and having Edomite kids; God don’t give a damn. They are sealed for destruction for ever. These are them, the red people: your friend Netanyahu in Israel, that’s him too.
- But don’t get it wrong. When a black man has a child with a white woman, sometimes the kids come out looking like that, and those are still Israelites. Who was David’s great grandmother, and what was she? But David was an Israelite. Those whose fathers are our seed are our people, regardless of their complexion. Teachers especially, don’t be offending people.

  Precepts:
  - **[Obadiah 1:1-4](/bible/obadiah/1#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/obadiah/1#v1)</sup> The vision of Obadiah. Thus saith the Lord God concerning Edom; We have heard a rumour from the Lord, and an ambassador is sent among the heathen, Arise ye, and let us rise up against her in battle.
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/obadiah/1#v2)</sup> Behold, I have made thee small among the heathen: thou art greatly despised.
    >
    > <sup>[3](/bible/obadiah/1#v3)</sup> The pride of thine heart hath deceived thee, thou that dwellest in the clefts of the rock, whose habitation is high; that saith in his heart, Who shall bring me down to the ground?
    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/obadiah/1#v4)</sup> Though thou exalt thyself as the eagle, and though thou set thy nest among the stars, thence will I bring thee down, saith the Lord.

    “Thus saith the Lord God concerning Edom”: God says to Edom, “Behold, I have made thee small among the heathen: thou art greatly despised.” They hated it. “The pride of thine heart hath deceived thee, thou that dwellest in the clefts of the rock, whose habitation is high; that saith in his heart, Who shall bring me down to the ground?” Edom gained power on the earth when he rose again, and in his pride he thinks no one can bring him down. He is the one who dwells in the clefts of the rocks, in the mountains: cave dwellers, Caucasians, troglodytes.
  - **[Malachi 3:6](/bible/malachi/3#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/malachi/3#v6)</sup> For I am the Lord, I change not; therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed.

    Christians, and black people in this country, say God can change, you have to evolve. God does not evolve. “For I am the Lord, I change not; therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed.”
  - **[Genesis 36:8](/bible/genesis/36#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/genesis/36#v8)</sup> Thus dwelt Esau in mount Seir: Esau is Edom.

    “Thus dwelt Esau in mount Seir: Esau is Edom.” There is no argument: Esau is Edom. Esau came out red from his mother, hairy like a garment, his name was changed to Edom for the red pottage, and he dwelt in mount Seir. So when Malachi turns from Esau to Edom, it is the same man and the same nation.

**[Malachi 1:6](/bible/malachi/1#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/malachi/1#v6)</sup> A son honoureth his father, and a servant his master: if then I be a father, where is mine honour? and if I be a master, where is my fear? saith the Lord of hosts unto you, O priests, that despise my name. And ye say, Wherein have we despised thy name?

- “Saith the Lord of hosts unto you, O priests, that despise my name.” That is talking about Levi, the so-called Haitian.
- Familiarity is another reason we respect strangers: with strangers there are consequences, and you care how other people see you. At home you feel there is no consequence. Most good women don’t want to embarrass their husbands, so you know you can do what you want. Some have a good woman and treat her in reverse, like the brother singing love songs to a wife who just hit him in the face, only backwards.
- “A son honoureth his father, and a servant his master: if then I be a father, where is mine honour?” A lot of times men only give honour where they think it matters. You honour your boss because it matters, you honour the men in the truth, you give double honour to the elders. But in your household: it don’t matter, she ain’t going to do nothing or say nothing, I can do whatever I want.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Peter 3:7](/bible/1-peter/3#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/1-peter/3#v7)</sup> Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered.

    “Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel.” You are supposed to give honour to your wife as the weaker vessel, and if something is weak, you have to build it up.

**[Malachi 1:7-8](/bible/malachi/1#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/malachi/1#v7)</sup> Ye offer polluted bread upon mine altar; and ye say, Wherein have we polluted thee? In that ye say, The table of the Lord is contemptible.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/malachi/1#v8)</sup> And if ye offer the blind for sacrifice, is it not evil? and if ye offer the lame and sick, is it not evil? offer it now unto thy governor; will he be pleased with thee, or accept thy person? saith the Lord of hosts.

- “Ye offer polluted bread upon mine altar.” That is how you know it is Levi: Levi was the priest, the one who offered the sacrifices. Offering “the blind for sacrifice” and “the lame and sick” shows how Levi got corrupt.
