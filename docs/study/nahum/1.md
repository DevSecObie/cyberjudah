## Introduction

This session reads Micah 5 through Nahum 1; these notes cover Nahum 1, which closes the reading. It is a reading session, so the chapter is read straight through and the notes follow the text. The burden of Nineveh opens with who the Most High is toward his adversaries and toward them that trust in him, and ends with the yoke broken off Judah and the feet upon the mountains that publish peace.

**[Nahum 1:1-6](/bible/nahum/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/nahum/1#v1)</sup> The burden of Nineveh. The book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/nahum/1#v2)</sup> God is jealous, and the Lord revengeth; the Lord revengeth, and is furious; the Lord will take vengeance on his adversaries, and he reserveth wrath for his enemies.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/nahum/1#v3)</sup> The Lord is slow to anger, and great in power, and will not at all acquit the wicked: the Lord hath his way in the whirlwind and in the storm, and the clouds are the dust of his feet.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/nahum/1#v4)</sup> He rebuketh the sea, and maketh it dry, and drieth up all the rivers: Bashan languisheth, and Carmel, and the flower of Lebanon languisheth.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/nahum/1#v5)</sup> The mountains quake at him, and the hills melt, and the earth is burned at his presence, yea, the world, and all that dwell therein.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/nahum/1#v6)</sup> Who can stand before his indignation? and who can abide in the fierceness of his anger? his fury is poured out like fire, and the rocks are thrown down by him.

> <sup>[7](/bible/nahum/1#v7)</sup> The Lord is good, a strong hold in the day of trouble; and he knoweth them that trust in him.
