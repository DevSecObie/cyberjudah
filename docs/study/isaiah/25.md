**[Isaiah 25:1-3](/bible/isaiah/25#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/25#v1)</sup> O Lord, thou art my God; I will exalt thee, I will praise thy name; for thou hast done wonderful things; thy counsels of old are faithfulness and truth.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/25#v2)</sup> For thou hast made of a city an heap; of a defenced city a ruin: a palace of strangers to be no city; it shall never be built.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/25#v3)</sup> Therefore shall the strong people glorify thee, the city of the terrible nations shall fear thee.

- The prophet exalts and praises the Most High for wonderful things and counsels of old that are faithfulness and truth, for He has made of a city a heap and of a defended city a ruin, a palace of strangers to be no city, never to be built again. Therefore the strong people shall glorify Him and the city of the terrible nations shall fear Him.

**[Isaiah 25:4-5](/bible/isaiah/25#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/25#v4)</sup> For thou hast been a strength to the poor, a strength to the needy in his distress, a refuge from the storm, a shadow from the heat, when the blast of the terrible ones is as a storm against the wall.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/25#v5)</sup> Thou shalt bring down the noise of strangers, as the heat in a dry place; even the heat with the shadow of a cloud: the branch of the terrible ones shall be brought low.

- The Most High has been a strength to the poor and to the needy in his distress, a refuge from the storm and a shadow from the heat when the blast of the terrible ones beats like a storm against the wall. He brings down the noise of strangers as the heat in a dry place, and the branch of the terrible ones is brought low.

**[Isaiah 25:6-8](/bible/isaiah/25#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/25#v6)</sup> And in this mountain shall the Lord of hosts make unto all people a feast of fat things, a feast of wines on the lees, of fat things full of marrow, of wines on the lees well refined.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/25#v7)</sup> And he will destroy in this mountain the face of the covering cast over all people, and the vail that is spread over all nations.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/25#v8)</sup> He will swallow up death in victory; and the Lord God will wipe away tears from off all faces; and the rebuke of his people shall he take away from off all the earth: for the Lord hath spoken it.

- In this mountain the Most High of hosts makes for all people a feast of fat things and well refined wines, and destroys the covering cast over all people and the veil spread over all nations. He will swallow up death in victory, wipe away tears from off all faces, and take away the rebuke of His people from all the earth, for the Most High has spoken it.

**[Isaiah 25:9-12](/bible/isaiah/25#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/25#v9)</sup> And it shall be said in that day, Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us: this is the Lord; we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/25#v10)</sup> For in this mountain shall the hand of the Lord rest, and Moab shall be trodden down under him, even as straw is trodden down for the dunghill.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/25#v11)</sup> And he shall spread forth his hands in the midst of them, as he that swimmeth spreadeth forth his hands to swim: and he shall bring down their pride together with the spoils of their hands.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/25#v12)</sup> And the fortress of the high fort of thy walls shall he bring down, lay low, and bring to the ground, even to the dust.

- In that day it shall be said, "Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us," and the people will be glad and rejoice in His salvation, for the hand of the Most High rests in this mountain while Moab is trodden down as straw for the dunghill. The teacher closes by reminding that understanding the word requires keeping the commandments, that the Most High may not give the understanding as soon as it is asked but grants it to those who stay consistent in the readings, that every reading raises new questions because there are deeper levels in books like Isaiah, Ezekiel, and Revelation, and that questions should be taken to the leadership.
