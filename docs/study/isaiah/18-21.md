Read the chapter → [Isaiah 18](/bible/isaiah/18)

This session moves through [Isaiah 18](/bible/isaiah/18) to 21, four burdens laid on the nations around Israel. Most of these chapters go into history, prophecies of what would happen to Babylon, Assyria, Egypt, Judah, and Israel before the time of the Persians and the Medes. Some of it carries a double meaning that the Most High may open more fully one day. Chapter eighteen opens with a woe upon the land shadowing with wings beyond the rivers of Ethiopia, a reference to the land of Assyria, and moves toward the present being brought to the Most High of hosts at mount Zion.

**[Isaiah 18:1-3](/bible/isaiah/18#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/18#v1)</sup> Woe to the land shadowing with wings, which is beyond the rivers of Ethiopia:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/18#v2)</sup> That sendeth ambassadors by the sea, even in vessels of bulrushes upon the waters, saying, Go, ye swift messengers, to a nation scattered and peeled, to a people terrible from their beginning hitherto; a nation meted out and trodden down, whose land the rivers have spoiled!
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/18#v3)</sup> All ye inhabitants of the world, and dwellers on the earth, see ye, when he lifteth up an ensign on the mountains; and when he bloweth a trumpet, hear ye.

- "Woe to the land shadowing with wings which is beyond the rivers of Ethiopia." This is the reference to the land of Assyria, that sends ambassadors by the sea, even in vessels of bulrushes upon the water.
- The message goes to a nation scattered and peeled, a people terrible from their beginning, a nation meted out and trodden down whose land the rivers have spoiled.
- All the inhabitants of the world and dwellers on the earth are called to see when the ensign is lifted up on the mountains and to hear when the trumpet is blown.

**[Isaiah 18:4-7](/bible/isaiah/18#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/18#v4)</sup> For so the Lord said unto me, I will take my rest, and I will consider in my dwelling place like a clear heat upon herbs, and like a cloud of dew in the heat of harvest.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/18#v5)</sup> For afore the harvest, when the bud is perfect, and the sour grape is ripening in the flower, he shall both cut off the sprigs with pruning hooks, and take away and cut down the branches.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/18#v6)</sup> They shall be left together unto the fowls of the mountains, and to the beasts of the earth: and the fowls shall summer upon them, and all the beasts of the earth shall winter upon them.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/18#v7)</sup> In that time shall the present be brought unto the Lord of hosts of a people scattered and peeled, and from a people terrible from their beginning hitherto; a nation meted out and trodden under foot, whose land the rivers have spoiled, to the place of the name of the Lord of hosts, the mount Zion.

- The Most High said He would take His rest and consider upon His dwelling place, like a clear heat upon herbs and like a cloud of dew in the heat of harvest.
- Before the harvest He cuts off the sprigs with pruning hooks and takes away and cuts down the branches, left together to the fowls of the mountains and the beasts of the earth to summer and winter upon.
- In that time the present shall be brought to the Most High of hosts, of a people scattered and peeled, to the place of the name of the Most High of hosts, the mount Zion.

# Isaiah 19: The Burden of Egypt

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 19](/bible/isaiah/19)

**[Isaiah 19:1-4](/bible/isaiah/19#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/19#v1)</sup> The burden of Egypt. Behold, the Lord rideth upon a swift cloud, and shall come into Egypt: and the idols of Egypt shall be moved at his presence, and the heart of Egypt shall melt in the midst of it.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/19#v2)</sup> And I will set the Egyptians against the Egyptians: and they shall fight every one against his brother, and every one against his neighbour; city against city, and kingdom against kingdom.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/19#v3)</sup> And the spirit of Egypt shall fail in the midst thereof; and I will destroy the counsel thereof: and they shall seek to the idols, and to the charmers, and to them that have familiar spirits, and to the wizards.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/19#v4)</sup> And the Egyptians will I give over into the hand of a cruel lord; and a fierce king shall rule over them, saith the Lord, the Lord of hosts.

- "The burden of Egypt." The Most High rides upon a swift cloud and comes to Egypt, and the idols of Egypt are moved at His presence and the heart of Egypt melts in the midst of it.
- He sets the Egyptians against the Egyptians, everyone against his brother and against his neighbor, city against city and kingdom against kingdom.
- The spirit of Egypt fails in the midst of it, and He destroys the counsel thereof; they seek to the idols, the charmers, them that have familiar spirits, and the wizards.
- The Egyptians are given over into the hand of a cruel lord, and a fierce king shall rule over them, saith the Most High of hosts.

**[Isaiah 19:5-10](/bible/isaiah/19#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/19#v5)</sup> And the waters shall fail from the sea, and the river shall be wasted and dried up.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/19#v6)</sup> And they shall turn the rivers far away; and the brooks of defence shall be emptied and dried up: the reeds and flags shall wither.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/19#v7)</sup> The paper reeds by the brooks, by the mouth of the brooks, and every thing sown by the brooks, shall wither, be driven away, and be no more.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/19#v8)</sup> The fishers also shall mourn, and all they that cast angle into the brooks shall lament, and they that spread nets upon the waters shall languish.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/19#v9)</sup> Moreover they that work in fine flax, and they that weave networks, shall be confounded.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/19#v10)</sup> And they shall be broken in the purposes thereof, all that make sluices and ponds for fish.

- The waters fail from the sea, the river is wasted and dried up, and the brooks of defense are emptied and dried up.
- The reeds and flags wither, and everything sown by the brooks withers, is driven away, and is no more.
- The fishers mourn, they that cast angle into the brooks and spread nets upon the waters lament and languish, and they that work in fine flax and weave networks are confounded.

**[Isaiah 19:11-15](/bible/isaiah/19#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/19#v11)</sup> Surely the princes of Zoan are fools, the counsel of the wise counsellors of Pharaoh is become brutish: how say ye unto Pharaoh, I am the son of the wise, the son of ancient kings?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/19#v12)</sup> Where are they? where are thy wise men? and let them tell thee now, and let them know what the Lord of hosts hath purposed upon Egypt.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/19#v13)</sup> The princes of Zoan are become fools, the princes of Noph are deceived; they have also seduced Egypt, even they that are the stay of the tribes thereof.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/19#v14)</sup> The Lord hath mingled a perverse spirit in the midst thereof: and they have caused Egypt to err in every work thereof, as a drunken man staggereth in his vomit.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/19#v15)</sup> Neither shall there be any work for Egypt, which the head or tail, branch or rush, may do.

- The princes of Zoan are fools, and the counsel of the wise counselors of Pharaoh is become brutish.
- They cannot tell Pharaoh what the Most High of hosts has purposed upon Egypt; the princes of Zoan are become fools and the princes of Noph are deceived.
- The Most High has mingled a perverse spirit in the midst of Egypt and caused it to err in every work, as a drunken man staggers in his vomit.
- There shall be no work for Egypt which the head or tail, branch or rush, may do.

**[Isaiah 19:16-22](/bible/isaiah/19#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/19#v16)</sup> In that day shall Egypt be like unto women: and it shall be afraid and fear because of the shaking of the hand of the Lord of hosts, which he shaketh over it.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/19#v17)</sup> And the land of Judah shall be a terror unto Egypt, every one that maketh mention thereof shall be afraid in himself, because of the counsel of the Lord of hosts, which he hath determined against it.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/19#v18)</sup> In that day shall five cities in the land of Egypt speak the language of Canaan, and swear to the Lord of hosts; one shall be called, The city of destruction.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/isaiah/19#v19)</sup> In that day shall there be an altar to the Lord in the midst of the land of Egypt, and a pillar at the border thereof to the Lord.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/19#v20)</sup> And it shall be for a sign and for a witness unto the Lord of hosts in the land of Egypt: for they shall cry unto the Lord because of the oppressors, and he shall send them a saviour, and a great one, and he shall deliver them.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/isaiah/19#v21)</sup> And the Lord shall be known to Egypt, and the Egyptians shall know the Lord in that day, and shall do sacrifice and oblation; yea, they shall vow a vow unto the Lord, and perform it.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/isaiah/19#v22)</sup> And the Lord shall smite Egypt: he shall smite and heal it: and they shall return even to the Lord, and he shall be intreated of them, and shall heal them.

- In that day Egypt shall be like women, afraid because of the shaking of the hand of the Most High of hosts over it, and the land of Judah shall be a terror to Egypt.
- Five cities in the land of Egypt shall speak the language of Canaan and swear to the Most High of hosts, and one shall be called the city of destruction.
- In that day there shall be an altar to the Most High in the midst of the land of Egypt and a pillar at its border, for a sign and a witness to the Most High of hosts.
- They shall cry to the Most High because of oppressors, and He shall send them a savior and a great one to deliver them; He shall smite Egypt and heal it as they return to Him.

**[Isaiah 19:23-25](/bible/isaiah/19#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/isaiah/19#v23)</sup> In that day shall there be a highway out of Egypt to Assyria, and the Assyrian shall come into Egypt, and the Egyptian into Assyria, and the Egyptians shall serve with the Assyrians.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/isaiah/19#v24)</sup> In that day shall Israel be the third with Egypt and with Assyria, even a blessing in the midst of the land:
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/isaiah/19#v25)</sup> Whom the Lord of hosts shall bless, saying, Blessed be Egypt my people, and Assyria the work of my hands, and Israel mine inheritance.

- In that day there shall be a highway out of Egypt to Assyria, and the Egyptians and the Assyrians shall serve together.
- Israel shall be the third with Egypt and with Assyria, even a blessing in the midst of the land.
- All of this is going into the scattered Israelites that are in Egypt and Assyria, whom the Most High of hosts blesses, saying, "Blessed be Egypt my people, and Assyria the work of mine hands, and Israel mine inheritance."

# Isaiah 20: Naked and Barefoot for a Sign

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 20](/bible/isaiah/20)

**[Isaiah 20:1-2](/bible/isaiah/20#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/20#v1)</sup> In the year that Tartan came unto Ashdod, (when Sargon the king of Assyria sent him,) and fought against Ashdod, and took it;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/20#v2)</sup> At the same time spake the Lord by Isaiah the son of Amoz, saying, Go and loose the sackcloth from off thy loins, and put off thy shoe from thy foot. And he did so, walking naked and barefoot.

- In the year that Tartan came to Ashdod, sent by Sargon the king of Assyria, and fought against Ashdod and took it, the Most High spoke by Isaiah the son of Amoz.
- He told Isaiah to loose the sackcloth from his loins and put off his shoe from his foot, and Isaiah did so, walking naked and barefoot.

**[Isaiah 20:3-6](/bible/isaiah/20#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/20#v3)</sup> And the Lord said, Like as my servant Isaiah hath walked naked and barefoot three years for a sign and wonder upon Egypt and upon Ethiopia;
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/20#v4)</sup> So shall the king of Assyria lead away the Egyptians prisoners, and the Ethiopians captives, young and old, naked and barefoot, even with their buttocks uncovered, to the shame of Egypt.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/20#v5)</sup> And they shall be afraid and ashamed of Ethiopia their expectation, and of Egypt their glory.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/20#v6)</sup> And the inhabitant of this isle shall say in that day, Behold, such is our expectation, whither we flee for help to be delivered from the king of Assyria: and how shall we escape?

- As Isaiah walked naked and barefoot three years for a sign and wonder upon Egypt and Ethiopia, so the king of Assyria would lead the Egyptians and Ethiopians away captive, young and old, naked and barefoot, even with their buttocks uncovered to the shame of Egypt.
- Having the buttocks uncovered is a shame; young brothers should stop all that sagging, since the scripture itself marks an uncovered backside as a disgrace.
- The inhabitants of the isle shall say in that day that such is their expectation, ashamed and afraid, and ask how they shall escape the king of Assyria.

# Isaiah 21: The Burden of the Desert of the Sea; Babylon Is Fallen

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 21](/bible/isaiah/21)

**[Isaiah 21:1-5](/bible/isaiah/21#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/21#v1)</sup> The burden of the desert of the sea. As whirlwinds in the south pass through; so it cometh from the desert, from a terrible land.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/21#v2)</sup> A grievous vision is declared unto me; the treacherous dealer dealeth treacherously, and the spoiler spoileth. Go up, O Elam: besiege, O Media; all the sighing thereof have I made to cease.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/21#v3)</sup> Therefore are my loins filled with pain: pangs have taken hold upon me, as the pangs of a woman that travaileth: I was bowed down at the hearing of it; I was dismayed at the seeing of it.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/21#v4)</sup> My heart panted, fearfulness affrighted me: the night of my pleasure hath he turned into fear unto me.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/21#v5)</sup> Prepare the table, watch in the watchtower, eat, drink: arise, ye princes, and anoint the shield.

- "The burden of the desert of the sea." As whirlwinds in the south pass through, so it comes from the desert, from a terrible land; a grievous vision is declared, for the treacherous dealer deals treacherously and the spoiler spoils.
- Elam is called to go up and Media to besiege, and all its sighing the Most High makes to cease.
- The prophet's loins are filled with pain like the pains of a woman that travails; he is bowed down and dismayed, and the night of his pleasure is turned into fear.
- The table is prepared, the watch set in the watchtower, and the princes are called to arise and anoint the shield.

**[Isaiah 21:6-10](/bible/isaiah/21#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/21#v6)</sup> For thus hath the Lord said unto me, Go, set a watchman, let him declare what he seeth.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/21#v7)</sup> And he saw a chariot with a couple of horsemen, a chariot of asses, and a chariot of camels; and he hearkened diligently with much heed:
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/21#v8)</sup> And he cried, A lion: My lord, I stand continually upon the watchtower in the daytime, and I am set in my ward whole nights:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/21#v9)</sup> And, behold, here cometh a chariot of men, with a couple of horsemen. And he answered and said, Babylon is fallen, is fallen; and all the graven images of her gods he hath broken unto the ground.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/21#v10)</sup> O my threshing, and the corn of my floor: that which I have heard of the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, have I declared unto you.

- The Most High tells the prophet to set a watchman to declare what he sees, and the watchman cries that he stands continually upon the watchtower in the daytime and is set in his ward whole nights.
- He sees a chariot with a couple of horsemen and answers, "Babylon is fallen, is fallen," and all the graven images of her gods are broken to the ground.
- This threshing of the floor is the word that the prophet has heard of the Most High of hosts, the God of Israel, and declared.

**[Isaiah 21:11-12](/bible/isaiah/21#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/21#v11)</sup> The burden of Dumah. He calleth to me out of Seir, Watchman, what of the night? Watchman, what of the night?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/21#v12)</sup> The watchman said, The morning cometh, and also the night: if ye will enquire, enquire ye: return, come.

- "The burden of Dumah." One calls to the prophet out of Seir, "Watchman, what of the night? Watchman, what of the night?"
- The watchman answers that the morning comes and also the night; if they will inquire, let them inquire, return, and come.

**[Isaiah 21:13-17](/bible/isaiah/21#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/21#v13)</sup> The burden upon Arabia. In the forest in Arabia shall ye lodge, O ye travelling companies of Dedanim.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/21#v14)</sup> The inhabitants of the land of Tema brought water to him that was thirsty, they prevented with their bread him that fled.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/21#v15)</sup> For they fled from the swords, from the drawn sword, and from the bent bow, and from the grievousness of war.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/21#v16)</sup> For thus hath the Lord said unto me, Within a year, according to the years of an hireling, and all the glory of Kedar shall fail:
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/21#v17)</sup> And the residue of the number of archers, the mighty men of the children of Kedar, shall be diminished: for the Lord God of Israel hath spoken it.

- "The burden upon Arabia." In the forest of Arabia shall the traveling companies of Dedanim lodge.
- The inhabitants of the land of Tema bring water to the thirsty and their bread to those that fled from the sword, the drawn sword, the bent bow, and the grievousness of war.
- Within a year, according to the years of a hireling, all the glory of Kedar shall fail, and the residue of the number of archers, the mighty men of the children of Kedar, shall be diminished, for the Most High God of Israel has spoken it.
- These chapters go largely into history, prophecies of what would happen to Babylon, Assyria, Judah, and Israel before the time of the Persians and the Medes, and some of it carries a double meaning that the Most High may open one day.
