**[Isaiah 20:1-2](/bible/isaiah/20#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/20#v1)</sup> In the year that Tartan came unto Ashdod, (when Sargon the king of Assyria sent him,) and fought against Ashdod, and took it;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/20#v2)</sup> At the same time spake the Lord by Isaiah the son of Amoz, saying, Go and loose the sackcloth from off thy loins, and put off thy shoe from thy foot. And he did so, walking naked and barefoot.

- In the year that Tartan came to Ashdod, sent by Sargon the king of Assyria, and fought against Ashdod and took it, the Most High spoke by Isaiah the son of Amoz.
- He told Isaiah to loose the sackcloth from his loins and put off his shoe from his foot, and Isaiah did so, walking naked and barefoot.

**[Isaiah 20:3-6](/bible/isaiah/20#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/20#v3)</sup> And the Lord said, Like as my servant Isaiah hath walked naked and barefoot three years for a sign and wonder upon Egypt and upon Ethiopia;
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/20#v4)</sup> So shall the king of Assyria lead away the Egyptians prisoners, and the Ethiopians captives, young and old, naked and barefoot, even with their buttocks uncovered, to the shame of Egypt.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/20#v5)</sup> And they shall be afraid and ashamed of Ethiopia their expectation, and of Egypt their glory.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/20#v6)</sup> And the inhabitant of this isle shall say in that day, Behold, such is our expectation, whither we flee for help to be delivered from the king of Assyria: and how shall we escape?

- As Isaiah walked naked and barefoot three years for a sign and wonder upon Egypt and Ethiopia, so the king of Assyria would lead the Egyptians and Ethiopians away captive, young and old, naked and barefoot, even with their buttocks uncovered to the shame of Egypt.
- Having the buttocks uncovered is a shame; young brothers should stop all that sagging, since the scripture itself marks an uncovered backside as a disgrace.
- The inhabitants of the isle shall say in that day that such is their expectation, ashamed and afraid, and ask how they shall escape the king of Assyria.
