Read the chapter → [Isaiah 50](/bible/isaiah/50)

This session reads the four chapters [Isaiah 50](/bible/isaiah/50) through 53. It moves from the charge that Israel sold themselves for their own iniquities, into the prophecy of the Servant who gave his back to the smiters, through the comfort of Zion and the removal of the cup of trembling, and finally into chapter 53, which the teacher says is the whole account of the sacrifice Christ made, why he made it, what he went through, and how he bore our sins.

**[Isaiah 50:1-3](/bible/isaiah/50#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/50#v1)</sup> Thus saith the Lord, Where is the bill of your mother’s divorcement, whom I have put away? or which of my creditors is it to whom I have sold you? Behold, for your iniquities have ye sold yourselves, and for your transgressions is your mother put away.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/50#v2)</sup> Wherefore, when I came, was there no man? when I called, was there none to answer? Is my hand shortened at all, that it cannot redeem? or have I no power to deliver? behold, at my rebuke I dry up the sea, I make the rivers a wilderness: their fish stinketh, because there is no water, and dieth for thirst.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/50#v3)</sup> I clothe the heavens with blackness, and I make sackcloth their covering.

- The Most High asks where is the bill of the mother's divorcement, or which creditor he sold them to. The point of the charge is that for their iniquities they sold themselves, and for their transgressions their mother was put away.
- This is not that the people literally went and sold themselves. They brought this upon themselves by their own sins, and that is what put them into their trouble.
- His hand is not shortened that it cannot redeem, and he has power to deliver. At his rebuke he dries up the sea, makes the rivers a wilderness, and clothes the heavens with blackness.

**[Isaiah 50:4-6](/bible/isaiah/50#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/50#v4)</sup> The Lord God hath given me the tongue of the learned, that I should know how to speak a word in season to him that is weary: he wakeneth morning by morning, he wakeneth mine ear to hear as the learned.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/50#v5)</sup> The Lord God hath opened mine ear, and I was not rebellious, neither turned away back.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/50#v6)</sup> I gave my back to the smiters, and my cheeks to them that plucked off the hair: I hid not my face from shame and spitting.

- The Lord God gave the Servant the tongue of the learned, to know how to speak a word in season to him that is weary, and he wakens the ear morning by morning to hear as the learned.
- The Servant was not rebellious and did not turn away back. He gave his back to the smiters and his cheeks to them that plucked off the hair, and he hid not his face from shame and spitting.
- This goes right into the prophecy of Christ, who was getting beat. The gave-my-back-to-the-smiters is the beating and shame he took.

**[Isaiah 50:7-11](/bible/isaiah/50#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/50#v7)</sup> For the Lord God will help me; therefore shall I not be confounded: therefore have I set my face like a flint, and I know that I shall not be ashamed.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/50#v8)</sup> He is near that justifieth me; who will contend with me? let us stand together: who is mine adversary? let him come near to me.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/50#v9)</sup> Behold, the Lord God will help me; who is he that shall condemn me? lo, they all shall wax old as a garment; the moth shall eat them up.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/50#v10)</sup> Who is among you that feareth the Lord, that obeyeth the voice of his servant, that walketh in darkness, and hath no light? let him trust in the name of the Lord, and stay upon his God.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/50#v11)</sup> Behold, all ye that kindle a fire, that compass yourselves about with sparks: walk in the light of your fire, and in the sparks that ye have kindled. This shall ye have of mine hand; ye shall lie down in sorrow.

- Because the Lord God will help him, the Servant is not confounded; he sets his face like a flint and knows he shall not be ashamed. The one who justifies him is near, so who will contend with him or condemn him.
- His adversaries shall wax old as a garment and the moth shall eat them up. The one who fears the Lord and obeys the voice of his servant, though he walks in darkness and has no light, is told to trust in the name of the Lord and stay upon his God.
- Those who kindle their own fire and compass themselves with sparks, walking in the light of their own fire, shall have this at his hand: they shall lie down in sorrow.
