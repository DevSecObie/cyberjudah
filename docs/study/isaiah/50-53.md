Read the chapter → [Isaiah 50](/bible/isaiah/50)

This session reads the four chapters [Isaiah 50](/bible/isaiah/50) through 53. It moves from the charge that Israel sold themselves for their own iniquities, into the prophecy of the Servant who gave his back to the smiters, through the comfort of Zion and the removal of the cup of trembling, and finally into chapter 53, which the teacher says is the whole account of the sacrifice Christ made, why he made it, what he went through, and how he bore our sins.

**[Isaiah 50:1-3](/bible/isaiah/50#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/50#v1)</sup> Thus saith the Lord, Where is the bill of your mother’s divorcement, whom I have put away? or which of my creditors is it to whom I have sold you? Behold, for your iniquities have ye sold yourselves, and for your transgressions is your mother put away.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/50#v2)</sup> Wherefore, when I came, was there no man? when I called, was there none to answer? Is my hand shortened at all, that it cannot redeem? or have I no power to deliver? behold, at my rebuke I dry up the sea, I make the rivers a wilderness: their fish stinketh, because there is no water, and dieth for thirst.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/50#v3)</sup> I clothe the heavens with blackness, and I make sackcloth their covering.

- The Most High asks where is the bill of the mother's divorcement, or which creditor he sold them to. The point of the charge is that for their iniquities they sold themselves, and for their transgressions their mother was put away.
- This is not that the people literally went and sold themselves. They brought this upon themselves by their own sins, and that is what put them into their trouble.
- His hand is not shortened that it cannot redeem, and he has power to deliver. At his rebuke he dries up the sea, makes the rivers a wilderness, and clothes the heavens with blackness.

**[Isaiah 50:4-6](/bible/isaiah/50#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/50#v4)</sup> The Lord God hath given me the tongue of the learned, that I should know how to speak a word in season to him that is weary: he wakeneth morning by morning, he wakeneth mine ear to hear as the learned.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/50#v5)</sup> The Lord God hath opened mine ear, and I was not rebellious, neither turned away back.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/50#v6)</sup> I gave my back to the smiters, and my cheeks to them that plucked off the hair: I hid not my face from shame and spitting.

- The Lord God gave the Servant the tongue of the learned, to know how to speak a word in season to him that is weary, and he wakens the ear morning by morning to hear as the learned.
- The Servant was not rebellious and did not turn away back. He gave his back to the smiters and his cheeks to them that plucked off the hair, and he hid not his face from shame and spitting.
- This goes right into the prophecy of Christ, who was getting beat. The gave-my-back-to-the-smiters is the beating and shame he took.

**[Isaiah 50:7-11](/bible/isaiah/50#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/50#v7)</sup> For the Lord God will help me; therefore shall I not be confounded: therefore have I set my face like a flint, and I know that I shall not be ashamed.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/50#v8)</sup> He is near that justifieth me; who will contend with me? let us stand together: who is mine adversary? let him come near to me.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/50#v9)</sup> Behold, the Lord God will help me; who is he that shall condemn me? lo, they all shall wax old as a garment; the moth shall eat them up.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/50#v10)</sup> Who is among you that feareth the Lord, that obeyeth the voice of his servant, that walketh in darkness, and hath no light? let him trust in the name of the Lord, and stay upon his God.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/50#v11)</sup> Behold, all ye that kindle a fire, that compass yourselves about with sparks: walk in the light of your fire, and in the sparks that ye have kindled. This shall ye have of mine hand; ye shall lie down in sorrow.

- Because the Lord God will help him, the Servant is not confounded; he sets his face like a flint and knows he shall not be ashamed. The one who justifies him is near, so who will contend with him or condemn him.
- His adversaries shall wax old as a garment and the moth shall eat them up. The one who fears the Lord and obeys the voice of his servant, though he walks in darkness and has no light, is told to trust in the name of the Lord and stay upon his God.
- Those who kindle their own fire and compass themselves with sparks, walking in the light of their own fire, shall have this at his hand: they shall lie down in sorrow.

# Isaiah 51: Comfort for Zion and the Cup of Trembling Removed

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 51](/bible/isaiah/51)

**[Isaiah 51:1-8](/bible/isaiah/51#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/51#v1)</sup> Hearken to me, ye that follow after righteousness, ye that seek the Lord: look unto the rock whence ye are hewn, and to the hole of the pit whence ye are digged.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/51#v2)</sup> Look unto Abraham your father, and unto Sarah that bare you: for I called him alone, and blessed him, and increased him.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/51#v3)</sup> For the Lord shall comfort Zion: he will comfort all her waste places; and he will make her wilderness like Eden, and her desert like the garden of the Lord; joy and gladness shall be found therein, thanksgiving, and the voice of melody.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/51#v4)</sup> Hearken unto me, my people; and give ear unto me, O my nation: for a law shall proceed from me, and I will make my judgment to rest for a light of the people.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/51#v5)</sup> My righteousness is near; my salvation is gone forth, and mine arms shall judge the people; the isles shall wait upon me, and on mine arm shall they trust.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/51#v6)</sup> Lift up your eyes to the heavens, and look upon the earth beneath: for the heavens shall vanish away like smoke, and the earth shall wax old like a garment, and they that dwell therein shall die in like manner: but my salvation shall be for ever, and my righteousness shall not be abolished.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/51#v7)</sup> Hearken unto me, ye that know righteousness, the people in whose heart is my law; fear ye not the reproach of men, neither be ye afraid of their revilings.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/51#v8)</sup> For the moth shall eat them up like a garment, and the worm shall eat them like wool: but my righteousness shall be for ever, and my salvation from generation to generation.

- Those who follow after righteousness and seek the Lord are told to look to the rock they were hewn from and to Abraham their father and Sarah, for he called Abraham alone and blessed him and increased him.
- The Lord shall comfort Zion and all her waste places, making her wilderness like Eden and her desert like a garden of the Lord, with joy, gladness, thanksgiving, and the voice of melody found in her.
- The heavens shall vanish away like smoke and the earth wax old like a garment, and they that dwell therein shall die, but his salvation shall be forever and his righteousness shall not be abolished. Those who know righteousness are told not to fear the reproach of men, for the moth and the worm shall eat their revilers.

**[Isaiah 51:9-16](/bible/isaiah/51#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/51#v9)</sup> Awake, awake, put on strength, O arm of the Lord; awake, as in the ancient days, in the generations of old. Art thou not it that hath cut Rahab, and wounded the dragon?
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/51#v10)</sup> Art thou not it which hath dried the sea, the waters of the great deep; that hath made the depths of the sea a way for the ransomed to pass over?
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/51#v11)</sup> Therefore the redeemed of the Lord shall return, and come with singing unto Zion; and everlasting joy shall be upon their head: they shall obtain gladness and joy; and sorrow and mourning shall flee away.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/51#v12)</sup> I, even I, am he that comforteth you: who art thou, that thou shouldest be afraid of a man that shall die, and of the son of man which shall be made as grass;
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/51#v13)</sup> And forgettest the Lord thy maker, that hath stretched forth the heavens, and laid the foundations of the earth; and hast feared continually every day because of the fury of the oppressor, as if he were ready to destroy? and where is the fury of the oppressor?
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/51#v14)</sup> The captive exile hasteneth that he may be loosed, and that he should not die in the pit, nor that his bread should fail.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/51#v15)</sup> But I am the Lord thy God, that divided the sea, whose waves roared: The Lord of hosts is his name.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/51#v16)</sup> And I have put my words in thy mouth, and I have covered thee in the shadow of mine hand, that I may plant the heavens, and lay the foundations of the earth, and say unto Zion, Thou art my people.

- The arm of the Lord is called to awake and put on strength as in the ancient days, the arm that cut Rahab and wounded the dragon and dried up the sea, making a way for the ransomed to pass over.
- The redeemed of the Lord shall return and come with singing to Zion, with everlasting joy upon their heads; they shall obtain gladness and joy, and sorrow and mourning shall flee away.
- He is the one that comforts them, and asks why they should be afraid of a man that shall die and forget the Lord their maker who stretched forth the heavens and laid the foundations of the earth. He has put his words in their mouth and covered them in the shadow of his hand, and says unto Zion, Thou art my people.

**[Isaiah 51:17-23](/bible/isaiah/51#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/51#v17)</sup> Awake, awake, stand up, O Jerusalem, which hast drunk at the hand of the Lord the cup of his fury; thou hast drunken the dregs of the cup of trembling, and wrung them out.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/51#v18)</sup> There is none to guide her among all the sons whom she hath brought forth; neither is there any that taketh her by the hand of all the sons that she hath brought up.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/isaiah/51#v19)</sup> These two things are come unto thee; who shall be sorry for thee? desolation, and destruction, and the famine, and the sword: by whom shall I comfort thee?
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/51#v20)</sup> Thy sons have fainted, they lie at the head of all the streets, as a wild bull in a net: they are full of the fury of the Lord, the rebuke of thy God.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/isaiah/51#v21)</sup> Therefore hear now this, thou afflicted, and drunken, but not with wine:
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/isaiah/51#v22)</sup> Thus saith thy Lord the Lord, and thy God that pleadeth the cause of his people, Behold, I have taken out of thine hand the cup of trembling, even the dregs of the cup of my fury; thou shalt no more drink it again:
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/isaiah/51#v23)</sup> But I will put it into the hand of them that afflict thee; which have said to thy soul, Bow down, that we may go over: and thou hast laid thy body as the ground, and as the street, to them that went over.

- Jerusalem is called to awake and stand up, having drunk at the hand of the Lord the cup of his fury and the dregs of the cup of trembling. There was none among her sons to guide her or take her by the hand.
- Desolation, destruction, famine, and the sword came upon her, and her sons fainted and lay at the head of the streets, full of the fury of the Lord.
- The Lord who pleads the cause of his people declares he has taken the cup of trembling out of her hand; she shall no more drink it. All the curses and punishments that came upon us we drank every bit of, and now that cup is put into the hand of them that afflicted us and made us bow down so they could go over. So be patient, Israel, be patient.

# Isaiah 52: Awake, Zion, Redeemed Without Money

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 52](/bible/isaiah/52)

**[Isaiah 52:1-6](/bible/isaiah/52#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/52#v1)</sup> Awake, awake; put on thy strength, O Zion; put on thy beautiful garments, O Jerusalem, the holy city: for henceforth there shall no more come into thee the uncircumcised and the unclean.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/52#v2)</sup> Shake thyself from the dust; arise, and sit down, O Jerusalem: loose thyself from the bands of thy neck, O captive daughter of Zion.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/52#v3)</sup> For thus saith the Lord, Ye have sold yourselves for nought; and ye shall be redeemed without money.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/52#v4)</sup> For thus saith the Lord God, My people went down aforetime into Egypt to sojourn there; and the Assyrian oppressed them without cause.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/52#v5)</sup> Now therefore, what have I here, saith the Lord, that my people is taken away for nought? they that rule over them make them to howl, saith the Lord; and my name continually every day is blasphemed.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/52#v6)</sup> Therefore my people shall know my name: therefore they shall know in that day that I am he that doth speak: behold, it is I.

- Zion is called to awake and put on strength, and Jerusalem the holy city to put on her beautiful garments, for henceforth the uncircumcised and the unclean shall no more come into her. She is told to shake off the dust, arise, and loose the bands of her neck.
- They sold themselves for nothing and shall be redeemed without money. His people went down into Egypt to sojourn, and the Assyrian oppressed them without cause.
- Because those that rule over them make them howl and his name is blasphemed continually every day, his people shall therefore know his name, and know in that day that he is the one who speaks.

**[Isaiah 52:7-12](/bible/isaiah/52#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/52#v7)</sup> How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that bringeth good tidings, that publisheth peace; that bringeth good tidings of good, that publisheth salvation; that saith unto Zion, Thy God reigneth!
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/52#v8)</sup> Thy watchmen shall lift up the voice; with the voice together shall they sing: for they shall see eye to eye, when the Lord shall bring again Zion.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/52#v9)</sup> Break forth into joy, sing together, ye waste places of Jerusalem: for the Lord hath comforted his people, he hath redeemed Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/52#v10)</sup> The Lord hath made bare his holy arm in the eyes of all the nations; and all the ends of the earth shall see the salvation of our God.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/52#v11)</sup> Depart ye, depart ye, go ye out from thence, touch no unclean thing; go ye out of the midst of her; be ye clean, that bear the vessels of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/52#v12)</sup> For ye shall not go out with haste, nor go by flight: for the Lord will go before you; and the God of Israel will be your rereward.

- Beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that brings good tidings, that publishes peace and salvation and says to Zion, Thy God reigneth. The watchmen shall lift up the voice and sing together when they see eye to eye and the Lord brings again Zion.
- The waste places of Jerusalem are told to break forth into joy, for the Lord has comforted his people and redeemed Jerusalem, making bare his holy arm in the eyes of all the nations, so all the ends of the earth shall see the salvation of our God.
- They are told to depart and go out from thence, touching no unclean thing and being clean that bear the vessels of the Lord; they shall not go out in haste or by flight, for the Lord will go before them and be their rearward.

**[Isaiah 52:13-15](/bible/isaiah/52#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/52#v13)</sup> Behold, my servant shall deal prudently, he shall be exalted and extolled, and be very high.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/52#v14)</sup> As many were astonied at thee; his visage was so marred more than any man, and his form more than the sons of men:
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/52#v15)</sup> So shall he sprinkle many nations; the kings shall shut their mouths at him: for that which had not been told them shall they see; and that which they had not heard shall they consider.

- The Servant shall deal prudently and be exalted, extolled, and made very high. Many were astonished at him because his visage was marred more than any man and his form more than the sons of men.
- So shall he sprinkle many nations, and kings shall shut their mouths at him, for what had not been told them they shall see, and what they had not heard they shall consider.

# Isaiah 53: The Suffering Servant Bears Our Sins

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 53](/bible/isaiah/53)

**[Isaiah 53:1-6](/bible/isaiah/53#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/53#v1)</sup> Who hath believed our report? and to whom is the arm of the Lord revealed?
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/53#v2)</sup> For he shall grow up before him as a tender plant, and as a root out of a dry ground: he hath no form nor comeliness; and when we shall see him, there is no beauty that we should desire him.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/53#v3)</sup> He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief: and we hid as it were our faces from him; he was despised, and we esteemed him not.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/53#v4)</sup> Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/53#v5)</sup> But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/53#v6)</sup> All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the Lord hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.

- Who has believed the report, and to whom is the arm of the Lord revealed. He grows up before him as a tender plant and a root out of dry ground, with no form nor comeliness and no beauty that we should desire him.
- He is despised and rejected of men, a man of sorrows and acquainted with grief; we hid our faces from him and esteemed him not, though he bore our griefs and carried our sorrows.
- He was wounded for our transgressions and bruised for our iniquities; the chastisement of our peace was upon him, and with his stripes we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray, turned everyone to his own way, and the Lord laid on him the iniquity of us all.

**[Isaiah 53:7-12](/bible/isaiah/53#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/53#v7)</sup> He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth: he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/53#v8)</sup> He was taken from prison and from judgment: and who shall declare his generation? for he was cut off out of the land of the living: for the transgression of my people was he stricken.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/53#v9)</sup> And he made his grave with the wicked, and with the rich in his death; because he had done no violence, neither was any deceit in his mouth.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/53#v10)</sup> Yet it pleased the Lord to bruise him; he hath put him to grief: when thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin, he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days, and the pleasure of the Lord shall prosper in his hand.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/53#v11)</sup> He shall see of the travail of his soul, and shall be satisfied: by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many; for he shall bear their iniquities.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/53#v12)</sup> Therefore will I divide him a portion with the great, and he shall divide the spoil with the strong; because he hath poured out his soul unto death: and he was numbered with the transgressors; and he bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors.

- He was oppressed and afflicted, yet opened not his mouth; he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter and as a sheep dumb before her shearers. He was taken from prison and from judgment and cut off from the land of the living for the transgression of the people.
- He made his grave with the wicked and with the rich in his death, though he had done no violence and no deceit was in his mouth. It pleased the Lord to bruise him and make his soul an offering for sin, and by his knowledge shall his righteous servant justify many, for he shall bear their iniquities.
- This whole chapter is the sacrifice that Christ made, why he made it, what he went through, and the fact that he bore our sins. He poured out his soul unto death, was numbered with the transgressors, bore the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors.
