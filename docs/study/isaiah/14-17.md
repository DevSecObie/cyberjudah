Read the chapter → [Isaiah 14](/bible/isaiah/14)

This session reads [Isaiah 14](/bible/isaiah/14) through 17, a stretch of the prophet's burdens against the nations. It was noted at the outset as one of the deep readings, moving verse by verse with little added exposition, so the notes follow the text closely and gather the questions answered afterward under the verses they belong to. Chapter 14 promises mercy on Jacob and the restoration of Israel to their own land, then takes up a proverb against the king of Babylon, records the fall of Lucifer, declares the breaking of the Assyrian, and closes with a burden upon Palestine. Chapters 15 and 16 carry the burden of Moab, and chapter 17 the burden of Damascus and Ephraim.

**[Isaiah 14:1-2](/bible/isaiah/14#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/14#v1)</sup> For the Lord will have mercy on Jacob, and will yet choose Israel, and set them in their own land: and the strangers shall be joined with them, and they shall cleave to the house of Jacob.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/14#v2)</sup> And the people shall take them, and bring them to their place: and the house of Israel shall possess them in the land of the Lord for servants and handmaids: and they shall take them captives, whose captives they were; and they shall rule over their oppressors.

- The Most High will have mercy on Jacob and will yet choose Israel and set them in their own land, and the stranger shall be joined with them and cleave to the house of Jacob.
- The people that took Israel captive shall be taken by Israel, and the house of Israel shall possess them in the Most High's land for servants and handmaids. Those who once held Israel as captives will become their captives, and Israel shall rule over their oppressors.

**[Isaiah 14:3-8](/bible/isaiah/14#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/14#v3)</sup> And it shall come to pass in the day that the Lord shall give thee rest from thy sorrow, and from thy fear, and from the hard bondage wherein thou wast made to serve,
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/14#v4)</sup> That thou shalt take up this proverb against the king of Babylon, and say, How hath the oppressor ceased! the golden city ceased!
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/14#v5)</sup> The Lord hath broken the staff of the wicked, and the sceptre of the rulers.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/14#v6)</sup> He who smote the people in wrath with a continual stroke, he that ruled the nations in anger, is persecuted, and none hindereth.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/14#v7)</sup> The whole earth is at rest, and is quiet: they break forth into singing.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/14#v8)</sup> Yea, the fir trees rejoice at thee, and the cedars of Lebanon, saying, Since thou art laid down, no feller is come up against us.

- In the day the Most High gives Israel rest from sorrow, fear, and the hard bondage in which they were made to serve, they shall take up this proverb against the king of Babylon: How has the oppressor ceased, and the golden city ceased.
- The Most High has broken the staff of the wicked and the scepter of the rulers, the one who smote the people in wrath with a continual stroke and ruled the nations in anger. Now the whole earth is at rest and quiet and breaks forth into singing.
- Even the fir trees and the cedars of Lebanon rejoice, saying that since the oppressor is laid down, no feller is come up against them.

**[Isaiah 14:9-11](/bible/isaiah/14#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/14#v9)</sup> Hell from beneath is moved for thee to meet thee at thy coming: it stirreth up the dead for thee, even all the chief ones of the earth; it hath raised up from their thrones all the kings of the nations.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/14#v10)</sup> All they shall speak and say unto thee, Art thou also become weak as we? art thou become like unto us?
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/14#v11)</sup> Thy pomp is brought down to the grave, and the noise of thy viols: the worm is spread under thee, and the worms cover thee.

- Hell from beneath is moved to meet the king of Babylon at his coming; it stirs up the dead for him, even the chief ones of the earth, and raises up from their thrones all the kings of the nations.
- They speak and say to him, Are you also become weak as we are, are you become like us. His pomp is brought down to the grave and the noise of his viols, the worm is spread under him, and the worms cover him.

**[Isaiah 14:12-15](/bible/isaiah/14#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/14#v12)</sup> How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! how art thou cut down to the ground, which didst weaken the nations!
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/14#v13)</sup> For thou hast said in thine heart, I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God: I will sit also upon the mount of the congregation, in the sides of the north:
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/14#v14)</sup> I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will be like the most High.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/14#v15)</sup> Yet thou shalt be brought down to hell, to the sides of the pit.

- How is he fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning, cut down to the ground, he that weakened the nations.
- He said in his heart that he would ascend into heaven, exalt his throne above the stars of God, sit upon the mount of the congregation in the sides of the north, ascend above the heights of the clouds, and be like the Most High.
- For all that pride, he shall be brought down to hell, to the sides of the pit.

**[Isaiah 14:16-17](/bible/isaiah/14#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/14#v16)</sup> They that see thee shall narrowly look upon thee, and consider thee, saying, Is this the man that made the earth to tremble, that did shake kingdoms;
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/14#v17)</sup> That made the world as a wilderness, and destroyed the cities thereof; that opened not the house of his prisoners?

- They that see him shall look narrowly upon him and consider him, saying, Is this the man that made the earth to tremble, that did shake kingdoms.
- This is the one that made the world as a wilderness, destroyed the cities thereof, and opened not the house of his prisoners.

**[Isaiah 14:18-20](/bible/isaiah/14#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/14#v18)</sup> All the kings of the nations, even all of them, lie in glory, every one in his own house.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/isaiah/14#v19)</sup> But thou art cast out of thy grave like an abominable branch, and as the raiment of those that are slain, thrust through with a sword, that go down to the stones of the pit; as a carcase trodden under feet.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/14#v20)</sup> Thou shalt not be joined with them in burial, because thou hast destroyed thy land, and slain thy people: the seed of evildoers shall never be renowned.

- All the kings of the nations lie in glory, every one in his own house, but this king is cast out of his grave like an abominable branch, as the raiment of those that are slain, thrust through with a sword, going down to the stones of the pit as a carcass trodden under feet.
- He shall not be joined with them in burial because he destroyed his land and slew his people; the seed of evildoers shall never be renowned.

**[Isaiah 14:21-23](/bible/isaiah/14#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/isaiah/14#v21)</sup> Prepare slaughter for his children for the iniquity of their fathers; that they do not rise, nor possess the land, nor fill the face of the world with cities.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/isaiah/14#v22)</sup> For I will rise up against them, saith the Lord of hosts, and cut off from Babylon the name, and remnant, and son, and nephew, saith the Lord.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/isaiah/14#v23)</sup> I will also make it a possession for the bittern, and pools of water: and I will sweep it with the besom of destruction, saith the Lord of hosts.

- Prepare slaughter for his children for the iniquity of their fathers, that they do not rise, possess the land, nor fill the face of the world with cities.
- The Most High of hosts will rise up against Babylon and cut off the name and remnant, and son and nephew. He will make it a possession for the bittern and pools of water and sweep it with the besom of destruction.
- The besom is a broom made of twigs tied around a stick; sweeping Babylon with the besom of destruction means sweeping it away with the broom of destruction, wiped out completely.

**[Isaiah 14:24-27](/bible/isaiah/14#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/isaiah/14#v24)</sup> The Lord of hosts hath sworn, saying, Surely as I have thought, so shall it come to pass; and as I have purposed, so shall it stand:
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/isaiah/14#v25)</sup> That I will break the Assyrian in my land, and upon my mountains tread him under foot: then shall his yoke depart from off them, and his burden depart from off their shoulders.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/isaiah/14#v26)</sup> This is the purpose that is purposed upon the whole earth: and this is the hand that is stretched out upon all the nations.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/isaiah/14#v27)</sup> For the Lord of hosts hath purposed, and who shall disannul it? and his hand is stretched out, and who shall turn it back?

- The Most High of hosts has sworn that as He has thought, so shall it come to pass, and as He has purposed, so shall it stand: He will break the Assyrian in His land and upon His mountains tread him underfoot.
- Then the Assyrian's yoke shall depart from off Israel and his burden from off their shoulders. This is the purpose purposed upon the whole earth, and the hand stretched out upon all the nations; when the Most High has purposed, none shall disallow it, and when His hand is stretched out, none shall turn it back.

**[Isaiah 14:28-32](/bible/isaiah/14#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/isaiah/14#v28)</sup> In the year that king Ahaz died was this burden.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/isaiah/14#v29)</sup> Rejoice not thou, whole Palestina, because the rod of him that smote thee is broken: for out of the serpent’s root shall come forth a cockatrice, and his fruit shall be a fiery flying serpent.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/isaiah/14#v30)</sup> And the firstborn of the poor shall feed, and the needy shall lie down in safety: and I will kill thy root with famine, and he shall slay thy remnant.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/isaiah/14#v31)</sup> Howl, O gate; cry, O city; thou, whole Palestina, art dissolved: for there shall come from the north a smoke, and none shall be alone in his appointed times.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/isaiah/14#v32)</sup> What shall one then answer the messengers of the nation? That the Lord hath founded Zion, and the poor of his people shall trust in it.

- In the year king Ahaz died came this burden: Palestine is warned not to rejoice because the rod that smote her is broken, for out of the serpent's root shall come a cockatrice, and its fruit a fiery flying serpent.
- The firstborn of the poor shall feed and the needy lie down in safety, but the Most High will kill Palestine's root with famine and slay her remnant. The whole of Palestine is called to howl, for a smoke comes from the north and none shall be alone in his appointed times.
- The answer to the messengers of the nation is that the Most High has founded Zion, and the poor of His people shall trust in it.

# Isaiah 15: The Burden of Moab

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 15](/bible/isaiah/15)

**[Isaiah 15:1-4](/bible/isaiah/15#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/15#v1)</sup> The burden of Moab. Because in the night Ar of Moab is laid waste, and brought to silence; because in the night Kir of Moab is laid waste, and brought to silence;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/15#v2)</sup> He is gone up to Bajith, and to Dibon, the high places, to weep: Moab shall howl over Nebo, and over Medeba: on all their heads shall be baldness, and every beard cut off.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/15#v3)</sup> In their streets they shall gird themselves with sackcloth: on the tops of their houses, and in their streets, every one shall howl, weeping abundantly.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/15#v4)</sup> And Heshbon shall cry, and Elealeh: their voice shall be heard even unto Jahaz: therefore the armed soldiers of Moab shall cry out; his life shall be grievous unto him.

- The burden of Moab: because in the night Ar of Moab is laid waste and brought to silence, and in the night Kir of Moab is laid waste and brought to silence.
- Moab has gone up to Bajith and to Dibon, the high places, to weep; Moab shall howl over Nebo and over Medeba, with baldness on all their heads and every beard cut off.
- In their streets they gird themselves with sackcloth, and on the tops of the houses and in the streets everyone howls, weeping abundantly. The armed soldiers of Moab cry out, and his life is grievous unto him.

**[Isaiah 15:5-9](/bible/isaiah/15#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/15#v5)</sup> My heart shall cry out for Moab; his fugitives shall flee unto Zoar, an heifer of three years old: for by the mounting up of Luhith with weeping shall they go it up; for in the way of Horonaim they shall raise up a cry of destruction.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/15#v6)</sup> For the waters of Nimrim shall be desolate: for the hay is withered away, the grass faileth, there is no green thing.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/15#v7)</sup> Therefore the abundance they have gotten, and that which they have laid up, shall they carry away to the brook of the willows.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/15#v8)</sup> For the cry is gone round about the borders of Moab; the howling thereof unto Eglaim, and the howling thereof unto Beer–elim.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/15#v9)</sup> For the waters of Dimon shall be full of blood: for I will bring more upon Dimon, lions upon him that escapeth of Moab, and upon the remnant of the land.

- The prophet's heart cries out for Moab; his fugitives flee unto Zoar and go up the mounting of Luhith with weeping, and in the way of Horonaim raise up a cry of destruction.
- Throughout these chapters the prophet often names a people by their city, so a name in the burden points to the people who dwell there. Horonaim is a city in Moab, and the whole burden has been speaking of Moab.
- The waters of Nimrim are desolate, the grass fails, and there is no green thing, so the people carry their gathered abundance away to the brook of the willows. The Most High will bring yet more upon Dimon, lions upon those of Moab that escape and upon the remnant of the land.

# Isaiah 16: Moab's Plea and Judgment

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 16](/bible/isaiah/16)

**[Isaiah 16:1-5](/bible/isaiah/16#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/16#v1)</sup> Send ye the lamb to the ruler of the land from Sela to the wilderness, unto the mount of the daughter of Zion.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/16#v2)</sup> For it shall be, that, as a wandering bird cast out of the nest, so the daughters of Moab shall be at the fords of Arnon.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/16#v3)</sup> Take counsel, execute judgment; make thy shadow as the night in the midst of the noonday; hide the outcasts; bewray not him that wandereth.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/16#v4)</sup> Let mine outcasts dwell with thee, Moab; be thou a covert to them from the face of the spoiler: for the extortioner is at an end, the spoiler ceaseth, the oppressors are consumed out of the land.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/16#v5)</sup> And in mercy shall the throne be established: and he shall sit upon it in truth in the tabernacle of David, judging, and seeking judgment, and hasting righteousness.

- Send the lamb to the ruler of the land from Sela to the wilderness, unto the mount of the daughter of Zion, for the daughters of Moab shall be at the fords of Arnon as a wandering bird cast out of the nest.
- Moab is told to take counsel, execute judgment, make her shadow as the night in the midst of the noonday, hide the outcasts, and not betray the one that wanders. She is asked to be a covert to the outcasts from the face of the spoiler.
- When the extortioner and spoiler are consumed out of the land, the throne shall be established in mercy, and one shall sit upon it in truth in the tabernacle of David, judging and seeking judgment and hasting righteousness.

**[Isaiah 16:6-8](/bible/isaiah/16#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/16#v6)</sup> We have heard of the pride of Moab; he is very proud: even of his haughtiness, and his pride, and his wrath: but his lies shall not be so.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/16#v7)</sup> Therefore shall Moab howl for Moab, every one shall howl: for the foundations of Kir–hareseth shall ye mourn; surely they are stricken.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/16#v8)</sup> For the fields of Heshbon languish, and the vine of Sibmah: the lords of the heathen have broken down the principal plants thereof, they are come even unto Jazer, they wandered through the wilderness: her branches are stretched out, they are gone over the sea.

- We have heard of the pride of Moab, that he is very proud, of his haughtiness and pride and wrath, but his lies shall not be so. Therefore Moab shall howl for Moab, everyone shall howl.
- For the foundations of Kirhareseth they shall mourn, surely they are stricken. The fields of Heshbon languish and the vine of Sibmah, whose principal plants the lords of the heathen have broken down; her branches are stretched out, gone over the sea.

**[Isaiah 16:9-12](/bible/isaiah/16#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/16#v9)</sup> Therefore I will bewail with the weeping of Jazer the vine of Sibmah: I will water thee with my tears, O Heshbon, and Elealeh: for the shouting for thy summer fruits and for thy harvest is fallen.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/16#v10)</sup> And gladness is taken away, and joy out of the plentiful field; and in the vineyards there shall be no singing, neither shall there be shouting: the treaders shall tread out no wine in their presses; I have made their vintage shouting to cease.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/16#v11)</sup> Wherefore my bowels shall sound like an harp for Moab, and mine inward parts for Kir–haresh.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/16#v12)</sup> And it shall come to pass, when it is seen that Moab is weary on the high place, that he shall come to his sanctuary to pray; but he shall not prevail.

- The prophet bewails the vine of Sibmah with weeping and waters Heshbon and Elealeh with his tears, for the shouting over the summer fruits and harvest is fallen.
- Gladness and joy are taken away out of the plentiful field; in the vineyards there is no singing nor shouting, and the treaders tread out no wine in their presses, for the Most High has made the vintage shouting to cease.
- The prophet's inward parts sound like a harp for Moab and for Kirharesh, and when Moab wearies himself upon the high place and comes to his sanctuary to pray, he shall not prevail.

**[Isaiah 16:13-14](/bible/isaiah/16#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/16#v13)</sup> This is the word that the Lord hath spoken concerning Moab since that time.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/16#v14)</sup> But now the Lord hath spoken, saying, Within three years, as the years of an hireling, and the glory of Moab shall be contemned, with all that great multitude; and the remnant shall be very small and feeble.

- This is the word the Most High has spoken concerning Moab since that time.
- But now the Most High speaks, saying that within three years, as the years of a hireling, the glory of Moab shall be contemned with all that great multitude, and the remnant shall be very small and feeble.

# Isaiah 17: The Burden of Damascus and Ephraim

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 17](/bible/isaiah/17)

**[Isaiah 17:1-3](/bible/isaiah/17#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/17#v1)</sup> The burden of Damascus. Behold, Damascus is taken away from being a city, and it shall be a ruinous heap.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/17#v2)</sup> The cities of Aroer are forsaken: they shall be for flocks, which shall lie down, and none shall make them afraid.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/17#v3)</sup> The fortress also shall cease from Ephraim, and the kingdom from Damascus, and the remnant of Syria: they shall be as the glory of the children of Israel, saith the Lord of hosts.

- The burden of Damascus: Damascus is taken away from being a city and shall be a ruinous heap. The cities of Aroer are forsaken and left for flocks that lie down with none to make them afraid.
- The fortress ceases from Ephraim and the kingdom from Damascus, and the remnant of Syria shall be as the glory of the children of Israel. This again shows the pattern of naming a people by their city, for Damascus stands for the people of Syria and the fortress of Ephraim for the northern kingdom.

**[Isaiah 17:4-6](/bible/isaiah/17#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/17#v4)</sup> And in that day it shall come to pass, that the glory of Jacob shall be made thin, and the fatness of his flesh shall wax lean.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/17#v5)</sup> And it shall be as when the harvestman gathereth the corn, and reapeth the ears with his arm; and it shall be as he that gathereth ears in the valley of Rephaim.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/17#v6)</sup> Yet gleaning grapes shall be left in it, as the shaking of an olive tree, two or three berries in the top of the uppermost bough, four or five in the outmost fruitful branches thereof, saith the Lord God of Israel.

- In that day the glory of Jacob shall be made thin and the fatness of his flesh shall wax lean, as when the harvestman gathers the corn and reaps the ears in the valley of Rephaim.
- Yet gleaning grapes shall be left in it, as the shaking of an olive tree leaves two or three berries in the top of the uppermost bough and four or five in the outermost fruitful branches. This is the promise of a remnant spared even in the thinning.

**[Isaiah 17:7-8](/bible/isaiah/17#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/17#v7)</sup> At that day shall a man look to his Maker, and his eyes shall have respect to the Holy One of Israel.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/17#v8)</sup> And he shall not look to the altars, the work of his hands, neither shall respect that which his fingers have made, either the groves, or the images.

- In that day a man shall look to his Maker and his eyes shall have respect to the Holy One of Israel.
- He shall not look to the altars, the work of his hands, nor respect that which his fingers have made, neither the groves nor the images. The turning away from idols to the Most High is the mark of that day.

**[Isaiah 17:9-11](/bible/isaiah/17#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/17#v9)</sup> In that day shall his strong cities be as a forsaken bough, and an uppermost branch, which they left because of the children of Israel: and there shall be desolation.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/17#v10)</sup> Because thou hast forgotten the God of thy salvation, and hast not been mindful of the rock of thy strength, therefore shalt thou plant pleasant plants, and shalt set it with strange slips:
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/17#v11)</sup> In the day shalt thou make thy plant to grow, and in the morning shalt thou make thy seed to flourish: but the harvest shall be a heap in the day of grief and of desperate sorrow.

- In that day the strong cities shall be as a forsaken bough and an uppermost branch left because of the children of Israel, and there shall be desolation.
- This desolation comes because they have forgotten the God of their salvation and have not been mindful of the rock of their strength; therefore they plant pleasant plants and set them with strange slips.
- Though in the day they make their plant to grow and in the morning make their seed to flourish, the harvest shall be a heap in the day of grief and of desperate sorrow.

**[Isaiah 17:12-14](/bible/isaiah/17#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/17#v12)</sup> Woe to the multitude of many people, which make a noise like the noise of the seas; and to the rushing of nations, that make a rushing like the rushing of mighty waters!
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/17#v13)</sup> The nations shall rush like the rushing of many waters: but God shall rebuke them, and they shall flee far off, and shall be chased as the chaff of the mountains before the wind, and like a rolling thing before the whirlwind.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/17#v14)</sup> And behold at eveningtide trouble; and before the morning he is not. This is the portion of them that spoil us, and the lot of them that rob us.

- Woe to the multitude of many people that make a noise like the noise of the seas, and to the rushing of nations that rush like the rushing of mighty waters.
- The Most High shall rebuke them, and they shall flee far off and be chased as the chaff of the mountains before the wind and like a rolling thing before the whirlwind.
- At eveningtide there is trouble, and before the morning he is not; this is the portion of them that spoil Israel and the lot of them that rob them.
