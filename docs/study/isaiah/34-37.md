Read the chapter → [Isaiah 34](/bible/isaiah/34)

This session reads [Isaiah 34](/bible/isaiah/34) through 37 in the four chapters a day study. The first two chapters, 34 and 35, are prophecies: chapter 34 declares the Most High's indignation and fury upon all nations and His great slaughter in the land of Idumea, the land of Edom, while chapter 35 is a prophecy of Christ's return and of Israel's return to Zion. Chapters 36 and 37 turn to the history of King Hezekiah and the Assyrian king Sennacherib, whose servant Rabshakeh came up to blaspheme the living God and threaten Jerusalem. The whole reading shows that no nation can stand against Israel unless the Most High Himself has ordained that nation to overthrow His people, and He made it clear that Jerusalem was not going to be touched.

**[Isaiah 34:1-4](/bible/isaiah/34#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/34#v1)</sup> Come near, ye nations, to hear; and hearken, ye people: let the earth hear, and all that is therein; the world, and all things that come forth of it.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/34#v2)</sup> For the indignation of the Lord is upon all nations, and his fury upon all their armies: he hath utterly destroyed them, he hath delivered them to the slaughter.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/34#v3)</sup> Their slain also shall be cast out, and their stink shall come up out of their carcases, and the mountains shall be melted with their blood.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/34#v4)</sup> And all the host of heaven shall be dissolved, and the heavens shall be rolled together as a scroll: and all their host shall fall down, as the leaf falleth off from the vine, and as a falling fig from the fig tree.

- The Most High calls the nations to come near and hear, and the earth and all that is in it to give ear, for His indignation is upon all nations and His fury upon all their armies. He has utterly destroyed them and delivered them to the slaughter.
- The slain are cast out and the stink of their carcasses rises up, and the mountains are melted with their blood. This is the language of the Most High's vengeance poured out on the wicked.
- The hosts of heaven are dissolved and the heavens rolled together as a scroll, and all their host falls down as a leaf falls off the vine and as a falling fig from the fig tree. The judgment reaches from the ground to the sky.

**[Isaiah 34:5-8](/bible/isaiah/34#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/34#v5)</sup> For my sword shall be bathed in heaven: behold, it shall come down upon Idumea, and upon the people of my curse, to judgment.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/34#v6)</sup> The sword of the Lord is filled with blood, it is made fat with fatness, and with the blood of lambs and goats, with the fat of the kidneys of rams: for the Lord hath a sacrifice in Bozrah, and a great slaughter in the land of Idumea.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/34#v7)</sup> And the unicorns shall come down with them, and the bullocks with the bulls; and their land shall be soaked with blood, and their dust made fat with fatness.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/34#v8)</sup> For it is the day of the Lord’s vengeance, and the year of recompences for the controversy of Zion.

- His sword is bathed in heaven and comes down upon Idumea, upon the people of His curse, to judgment. Idumea is the land of Edom, the wicked, marked out here for the day of the Most High's vengeance.
- The sword of the Most High is filled with blood and made fat with the blood of lambs and goats and rams, for He has a sacrifice in Bozrah and a great slaughter in the land of Idumea. The animals named picture the slaughter of the people.
- It is the day of the Most High's vengeance and the year of recompense for the controversy of Zion. The judgment on Edom is the Most High settling the account for what was done to His people.

**[Isaiah 34:9-10](/bible/isaiah/34#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/34#v9)</sup> And the streams thereof shall be turned into pitch, and the dust thereof into brimstone, and the land thereof shall become burning pitch.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/34#v10)</sup> It shall not be quenched night nor day; the smoke thereof shall go up for ever: from generation to generation it shall lie waste; none shall pass through it for ever and ever.

- The streams of Edom are turned into pitch and the dust into brimstone, and the land becomes burning pitch. This is the same imagery of fire and brimstone used against the wicked.
- The burning is not quenched night or day, and the smoke goes up forever from generation to generation. The land lies waste so that none passes through it forever and ever, a permanent desolation.

**[Isaiah 34:11-13](/bible/isaiah/34#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/34#v11)</sup> But the cormorant and the bittern shall possess it; the owl also and the raven shall dwell in it: and he shall stretch out upon it the line of confusion, and the stones of emptiness.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/34#v12)</sup> They shall call the nobles thereof to the kingdom, but none shall be there, and all her princes shall be nothing.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/34#v13)</sup> And thorns shall come up in her palaces, nettles and brambles in the fortresses thereof: and it shall be an habitation of dragons, and a court for owls.

- The cormorant and the bittern possess the land, and the owl and the raven dwell in it. When the Most High is finished, only the unclean birds and beasts inhabit the place.
- He stretches out upon it the line of confusion and the stones of emptiness. The nobles are called to a kingdom that is not there, and all her princes come to nothing.
- Thorns come up in her palaces, and nettles and brambles in her fortresses, and it becomes a habitation of dragons and a court for owls. What was once a proud place is left to ruin and wild things.

**[Isaiah 34:14-17](/bible/isaiah/34#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/34#v14)</sup> The wild beasts of the desert shall also meet with the wild beasts of the island, and the satyr shall cry to his fellow; the screech owl also shall rest there, and find for herself a place of rest.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/34#v15)</sup> There shall the great owl make her nest, and lay, and hatch, and gather under her shadow: there shall the vultures also be gathered, every one with her mate.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/34#v16)</sup> Seek ye out of the book of the Lord, and read: no one of these shall fail, none shall want her mate: for my mouth it hath commanded, and his spirit it hath gathered them.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/34#v17)</sup> And he hath cast the lot for them, and his hand hath divided it unto them by line: they shall possess it for ever, from generation to generation shall they dwell therein.

- The wild beasts of the desert meet with the wild beasts of the island, and the satyr cries to his fellow, and the shrieking owl rests there and finds a place of rest. The desolation is total and lasting.
- Seek out of the book of the Most High and read: none of these shall fail, none shall want her mate, for His mouth has commanded it and His spirit has gathered them. What He has spoken over Edom will surely come to pass.
- He has cast the lot for them and divided the land to them by line, and they shall possess it forever and dwell in it from generation to generation. The judgment is fixed and permanent.

# Isaiah 35: The Ransomed of the Lord Return to Zion

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 35](/bible/isaiah/35)

This chapter is a prophecy of Christ's return and of Israel's return. The wilderness and the desert become glad and blossom, the weak and fearful are strengthened, the Most High comes with vengeance to save, and a highway called the way of holiness is opened for the redeemed alone to walk on.

**[Isaiah 35:1-2](/bible/isaiah/35#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/35#v1)</sup> The wilderness and the solitary place shall be glad for them; and the desert shall rejoice, and blossom as the rose.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/35#v2)</sup> It shall blossom abundantly, and rejoice even with joy and singing: the glory of Lebanon shall be given unto it, the excellency of Carmel and Sharon, they shall see the glory of the Lord, and the excellency of our God.

- The wilderness and the solitary place are glad, and the desert rejoices and blossoms as the rose. The barren places become fruitful when the Most High moves.
- The glory of Lebanon and the excellency of Carmel and Sharon are given to it, and they shall see the glory of the Most High. The prophecy points to the restoration that comes with His return.

**[Isaiah 35:3-4](/bible/isaiah/35#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/35#v3)</sup> Strengthen ye the weak hands, and confirm the feeble knees.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/35#v4)</sup> Say to them that are of a fearful heart, Be strong, fear not: behold, your God will come with vengeance, even God with a recompence; he will come and save you.

- Strengthen the weak hands and confirm the feeble knees, and say to those of a fearful heart, Be strong, fear not. The message to the people is one of encouragement in the day of trouble.
- Behold, your God will come with vengeance, even the Most High with a recompense; He will come and save you. His coming is both vengeance on the wicked and salvation for His people.

**[Isaiah 35:5-7](/bible/isaiah/35#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/35#v5)</sup> Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/35#v6)</sup> Then shall the lame man leap as an hart, and the tongue of the dumb sing: for in the wilderness shall waters break out, and streams in the desert.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/35#v7)</sup> And the parched ground shall become a pool, and the thirsty land springs of water: in the habitation of dragons, where each lay, shall be grass with reeds and rushes.

- Then the eyes of the blind are opened and the ears of the deaf unstopped, the lame man leaps as a hart, and the tongue of the dumb sings. The Most High's coming brings healing and restoration.
- In the wilderness waters break out and streams in the desert, the parched ground becomes a pool, and the thirsty land springs of water. The dry places are made alive again.

**[Isaiah 35:8-10](/bible/isaiah/35#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/35#v8)</sup> And an highway shall be there, and a way, and it shall be called The way of holiness; the unclean shall not pass over it; but it shall be for those: the wayfaring men, though fools, shall not err therein.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/35#v9)</sup> No lion shall be there, nor any ravenous beast shall go up thereon, it shall not be found there; but the redeemed shall walk there:
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/35#v10)</sup> And the ransomed of the Lord shall return, and come to Zion with songs and everlasting joy upon their heads: they shall obtain joy and gladness, and sorrow and sighing shall flee away.

- A highway is there called the way of holiness, and the unclean shall not pass over it. Wayfaring means a traveling man, and though fools travel through many places, they shall not err on this way, because the unclean cannot walk it.
- No lion or ravenous beast goes up on it; it is not found there, but the redeemed walk there. The wayfaring man, the fool, is somebody outside of these people, and only the redeemed are those who can walk on this way.
- The ransomed of the Most High return and come to Zion with songs and everlasting joy upon their heads; they obtain joy and gladness, and sorrow and sighing flee away. This is the return of the people to Zion when the Most High saves them.

# Isaiah 36: Rabshakeh's Blasphemy Against Jerusalem

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 36](/bible/isaiah/36)

This chapter begins the history of King Hezekiah. In the fourteenth year of his reign, Sennacherib king of Assyria came up against the defended cities of Judah and took them, then sent his servant Rabshakeh with a great army to Jerusalem to threaten Hezekiah and mock trust in the Most High.

**[Isaiah 36:1-3](/bible/isaiah/36#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/36#v1)</sup> Now it came to pass in the fourteenth year of king Hezekiah, that Sennacherib king of Assyria came up against all the defenced cities of Judah, and took them.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/36#v2)</sup> And the king of Assyria sent Rabshakeh from Lachish to Jerusalem unto king Hezekiah with a great army. And he stood by the conduit of the upper pool in the highway of the fuller’s field.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/36#v3)</sup> Then came forth unto him Eliakim, Hilkiah’s son, which was over the house, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah, Asaph’s son, the recorder.

- In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah, Sennacherib king of Assyria came up against all the defended cities of Judah and took them. Assyria was the rod the Most High used to press Judah.
- The king of Assyria sent Rabshakeh from Lachish to Jerusalem with a great army, and he stood by the conduit of the upper pool. Eliakim, Shebna the scribe, and Joah the recorder came out to meet him.

**[Isaiah 36:4-10](/bible/isaiah/36#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/36#v4)</sup> And Rabshakeh said unto them, Say ye now to Hezekiah, Thus saith the great king, the king of Assyria, What confidence is this wherein thou trustest?
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/36#v5)</sup> I say, sayest thou, (but they are but vain words) I have counsel and strength for war: now on whom dost thou trust, that thou rebellest against me?
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/36#v6)</sup> Lo, thou trustest in the staff of this broken reed, on Egypt; whereon if a man lean, it will go into his hand, and pierce it: so is Pharaoh king of Egypt to all that trust in him.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/36#v7)</sup> But if thou say to me, We trust in the Lord our God: is it not he, whose high places and whose altars Hezekiah hath taken away, and said to Judah and to Jerusalem, Ye shall worship before this altar?
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/36#v8)</sup> Now therefore give pledges, I pray thee, to my master the king of Assyria, and I will give thee two thousand horses, if thou be able on thy part to set riders upon them.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/36#v9)</sup> How then wilt thou turn away the face of one captain of the least of my master’s servants, and put thy trust on Egypt for chariots and for horsemen?
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/36#v10)</sup> And am I now come up without the Lord against this land to destroy it? the Lord said unto me, Go up against this land, and destroy it.

- Rabshakeh challenges Hezekiah's confidence, asking in whom he trusts, and calls his words but vain words. He mocks any counsel or strength for war.
- He warns against trusting in the staff of the broken reed of Egypt, which pierces the hand of the man who leans on it. Trusting Egypt instead of the Most High is the constant temptation set before Judah.
- He twists Hezekiah's removal of the high places, claiming it offended the Most High, and boasts that even Assyria was sent by the Lord to destroy the land. He speaks as though the Most High is on his side.

**[Isaiah 36:11-12](/bible/isaiah/36#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/36#v11)</sup> Then said Eliakim and Shebna and Joah unto Rabshakeh, Speak, I pray thee, unto thy servants in the Syrian language; for we understand it: and speak not to us in the Jews’ language, in the ears of the people that are on the wall.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/36#v12)</sup> But Rabshakeh said, Hath my master sent me to thy master and to thee to speak these words? hath he not sent me to the men that sit upon the wall, that they may eat their own dung, and drink their own piss with you?

- Eliakim and Shebna and Joah ask Rabshakeh to speak in the Syrian language, which they understand, and not in the Jews' language in the ears of the people on the wall. They do not want the people to hear the threats.
- Rabshakeh refuses, saying his master sent him to the men on the wall, who are the ones set to eat their own dung and drink their own piss with them. He deliberately speaks to frighten the common people.

**[Isaiah 36:13-20](/bible/isaiah/36#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/36#v13)</sup> Then Rabshakeh stood, and cried with a loud voice in the Jews’ language, and said, Hear ye the words of the great king, the king of Assyria.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/36#v14)</sup> Thus saith the king, Let not Hezekiah deceive you: for he shall not be able to deliver you.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/36#v15)</sup> Neither let Hezekiah make you trust in the Lord, saying, The Lord will surely deliver us: this city shall not be delivered into the hand of the king of Assyria.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/36#v16)</sup> Hearken not to Hezekiah: for thus saith the king of Assyria, Make an agreement with me by a present, and come out to me: and eat ye every one of his vine, and every one of his fig tree, and drink ye every one the waters of his own cistern;
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/36#v17)</sup> Until I come and take you away to a land like your own land, a land of corn and wine, a land of bread and vineyards.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/36#v18)</sup> Beware lest Hezekiah persuade you, saying, The Lord will deliver us. Hath any of the gods of the nations delivered his land out of the hand of the king of Assyria?
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/isaiah/36#v19)</sup> Where are the gods of Hamath and Arphad? where are the gods of Sepharvaim? and have they delivered Samaria out of my hand?
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/36#v20)</sup> Who are they among all the gods of these lands, that have delivered their land out of my hand, that the Lord should deliver Jerusalem out of my hand?

- Rabshakeh stands and cries with a loud voice in the Jews' language, telling the people not to let Hezekiah deceive them, for he cannot deliver them. He works to break the people's trust in their king.
- He tells them not to let Hezekiah make them trust in the Most High to deliver the city, but to make an agreement with him and come out, promising each man his own vine and fig tree until he takes them to a land like their own. It is a bribe dressed up as mercy.
- He lists the gods of Hamath, Arphad, and Sepharvaim, and asks whether any of them delivered their land or Samaria out of his hand, so that the Most High should deliver Jerusalem. He counts the living God as no better than the idols of the nations.

**[Isaiah 36:21-22](/bible/isaiah/36#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/isaiah/36#v21)</sup> But they held their peace, and answered him not a word: for the king’s commandment was, saying, Answer him not.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/isaiah/36#v22)</sup> Then came Eliakim, the son of Hilkiah, that was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah, the son of Asaph, the recorder, to Hezekiah with their clothes rent, and told him the words of Rabshakeh.

- The people hold their peace and answer him not a word, for Hezekiah's commandment was to answer him not. They do not argue with the blasphemer.
- Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah come to Hezekiah with their clothes torn and tell him the words of Rabshakeh. They bring the blasphemy before the king in mourning.

# Isaiah 37: Hezekiah's Prayer and the Overthrow of the Assyrians

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 37](/bible/isaiah/37)

Hezekiah rends his clothes, goes into the house of the Most High, and sends to Isaiah the prophet. Isaiah answers with the word that Sennacherib will hear a rumor, return to his own land, and fall by the sword there. Hezekiah prays, the Most High defends Jerusalem, and the angel of the Most High smites the Assyrian camp. This history shows that no nation can stand against Israel unless the Most High has ordained it, and He made clear that Jerusalem would not be touched.

**[Isaiah 37:1-4](/bible/isaiah/37#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/37#v1)</sup> And it came to pass, when king Hezekiah heard it, that he rent his clothes, and covered himself with sackcloth, and went into the house of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/37#v2)</sup> And he sent Eliakim, who was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and the elders of the priests covered with sackcloth, unto Isaiah the prophet the son of Amoz.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/37#v3)</sup> And they said unto him, Thus saith Hezekiah, This day is a day of trouble, and of rebuke, and of blasphemy: for the children are come to the birth, and there is not strength to bring forth.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/37#v4)</sup> It may be the Lord thy God will hear the words of Rabshakeh, whom the king of Assyria his master hath sent to reproach the living God, and will reprove the words which the Lord thy God hath heard: wherefore lift up thy prayer for the remnant that is left.

- When Hezekiah hears the words, he rends his clothes, covers himself with sackcloth, and goes into the house of the Most High. He turns to the Most High rather than to Egypt or to fear.
- He sends Eliakim, Shebna, and the elders of the priests, covered with sackcloth, to Isaiah the prophet. He calls it a day of trouble, rebuke, and blasphemy.
- He asks that Isaiah pray for the remnant that is left, hoping the Most High will hear the words of Rabshakeh, who was sent to reproach the living God. Hezekiah seeks the prophet's intercession.

**[Isaiah 37:5-7](/bible/isaiah/37#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/37#v5)</sup> So the servants of king Hezekiah came to Isaiah.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/37#v6)</sup> And Isaiah said unto them, Thus shall ye say unto your master, Thus saith the Lord, Be not afraid of the words that thou hast heard, wherewith the servants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed me.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/37#v7)</sup> Behold, I will send a blast upon him, and he shall hear a rumour, and return to his own land; and I will cause him to fall by the sword in his own land.

- Isaiah answers the servants: tell your master, Be not afraid of the words with which the servants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed Me. The Most High answers the blasphemy directly.
- Behold, I will send a blast upon him, and he shall hear a rumor and return to his own land, and I will cause him to fall by the sword in his own land. This is the prophecy that Sennacherib would be killed at home.
- This is exactly what happened: Sennacherib was killed in his own land by his own sons, showing the word of the Most High through Isaiah came to pass. No nation can stand against Israel when the Most High has spoken against it.

**[Isaiah 37:8-13](/bible/isaiah/37#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/37#v8)</sup> So Rabshakeh returned, and found the king of Assyria warring against Libnah: for he had heard that he was departed from Lachish.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/37#v9)</sup> And he heard say concerning Tirhakah king of Ethiopia, He is come forth to make war with thee. And when he heard it, he sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying,
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/37#v10)</sup> Thus shall ye speak to Hezekiah king of Judah, saying, Let not thy God, in whom thou trustest, deceive thee, saying, Jerusalem shall not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/37#v11)</sup> Behold, thou hast heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all lands by destroying them utterly; and shalt thou be delivered?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/37#v12)</sup> Have the gods of the nations delivered them which my fathers have destroyed, as Gozan, and Haran, and Rezeph, and the children of Eden which were in Telassar?
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/37#v13)</sup> Where is the king of Hamath, and the king of Arphad, and the king of the city of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivah?

- Rabshakeh returns and finds the king of Assyria warring against Libnah, for he had departed from Lachish. Word comes of Tirhakah king of Ethiopia coming out to make war.
- Sennacherib sends messengers again to Hezekiah, telling him not to let the God in whom he trusts deceive him that Jerusalem will not be given into his hand. He renews the same threat and mockery.
- He points to all the lands and kings that Assyria destroyed, Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and the rest, asking whether they were delivered. He counts Jerusalem as one more city to fall.

**[Isaiah 37:14-20](/bible/isaiah/37#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/37#v14)</sup> And Hezekiah received the letter from the hand of the messengers, and read it: and Hezekiah went up unto the house of the Lord, and spread it before the Lord.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/37#v15)</sup> And Hezekiah prayed unto the Lord, saying,
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/37#v16)</sup> O Lord of hosts, God of Israel, that dwellest between the cherubims, thou art the God, even thou alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth: thou hast made heaven and earth.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/37#v17)</sup> Incline thine ear, O Lord, and hear; open thine eyes, O Lord, and see: and hear all the words of Sennacherib, which hath sent to reproach the living God.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/37#v18)</sup> Of a truth, Lord, the kings of Assyria have laid waste all the nations, and their countries,
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/isaiah/37#v19)</sup> And have cast their gods into the fire: for they were no gods, but the work of men’s hands, wood and stone: therefore they have destroyed them.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/37#v20)</sup> Now therefore, O Lord our God, save us from his hand, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that thou art the Lord, even thou only.

- Hezekiah receives the letter, reads it, goes up to the house of the Most High, and spreads it before Him. He lays the whole threat out before the Most High in prayer.
- He calls on the Lord of hosts, God of Israel, who dwells between the cherubims, as the God alone of all the kingdoms of the earth, who made heaven and earth. He asks Him to incline His ear and hear the words of Sennacherib that reproach the living God.
- He acknowledges that Assyria has laid waste the nations and cast their gods into the fire, but those were no gods, only the work of men's hands, wood and stone. He asks the Most High to save them, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that He alone is the Lord.

**[Isaiah 37:21-29](/bible/isaiah/37#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/isaiah/37#v21)</sup> Then Isaiah the son of Amoz sent unto Hezekiah, saying, Thus saith the Lord God of Israel, Whereas thou hast prayed to me against Sennacherib king of Assyria:
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/isaiah/37#v22)</sup> This is the word which the Lord hath spoken concerning him; The virgin, the daughter of Zion, hath despised thee, and laughed thee to scorn; the daughter of Jerusalem hath shaken her head at thee.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/isaiah/37#v23)</sup> Whom hast thou reproached and blasphemed? and against whom hast thou exalted thy voice, and lifted up thine eyes on high? even against the Holy One of Israel.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/isaiah/37#v24)</sup> By thy servants hast thou reproached the Lord, and hast said, By the multitude of my chariots am I come up to the height of the mountains, to the sides of Lebanon; and I will cut down the tall cedars thereof, and the choice fir trees thereof: and I will enter into the height of his border, and the forest of his Carmel.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/isaiah/37#v25)</sup> I have digged, and drunk water; and with the sole of my feet have I dried up all the rivers of the besieged places.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/isaiah/37#v26)</sup> Hast thou not heard long ago, how I have done it; and of ancient times, that I have formed it? now have I brought it to pass, that thou shouldest be to lay waste defenced cities into ruinous heaps.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/isaiah/37#v27)</sup> Therefore their inhabitants were of small power, they were dismayed and confounded: they were as the grass of the field, and as the green herb, as the grass on the housetops, and as corn blasted before it be grown up.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/isaiah/37#v28)</sup> But I know thy abode, and thy going out, and thy coming in, and thy rage against me.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/isaiah/37#v29)</sup> Because thy rage against me, and thy tumult, is come up into mine ears, therefore will I put my hook in thy nose, and my bridle in thy lips, and I will turn thee back by the way by which thou camest.

- Isaiah sends to Hezekiah that the Most High has heard his prayer against Sennacherib. The virgin daughter of Zion has despised and laughed him to scorn.
- Sennacherib reproached and blasphemed the Holy One of Israel, boasting by his chariots that he climbed the mountains and cut down the cedars and dried up the rivers. His pride is set against the Most High Himself.
- The Most High declares that He formed and brought it to pass of ancient times, that Sennacherib should lay waste the defended cities, and that the inhabitants were weak because the Most High allowed it. Because of his rage against the Most High, He will put His hook in his nose and His bridle in his lips and turn him back the way he came.

**[Isaiah 37:30-32](/bible/isaiah/37#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/isaiah/37#v30)</sup> And this shall be a sign unto thee, Ye shall eat this year such as groweth of itself; and the second year that which springeth of the same: and in the third year sow ye, and reap, and plant vineyards, and eat the fruit thereof.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/isaiah/37#v31)</sup> And the remnant that is escaped of the house of Judah shall again take root downward, and bear fruit upward:
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/isaiah/37#v32)</sup> For out of Jerusalem shall go forth a remnant, and they that escape out of mount Zion: the zeal of the Lord of hosts shall do this.

- The sign to Hezekiah is that this year they eat what grows of itself, the second year what springs of the same, and the third year they sow, reap, plant vineyards, and eat the fruit. The Most High promises restoration and provision.
- The remnant that escapes of the house of Judah takes root downward and bears fruit upward, for out of Jerusalem goes forth a remnant. The zeal of the Lord of hosts performs this.

**[Isaiah 37:33-35](/bible/isaiah/37#v33)**

> <sup>[33](/bible/isaiah/37#v33)</sup> Therefore thus saith the Lord concerning the king of Assyria, He shall not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with shields, nor cast a bank against it.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/isaiah/37#v34)</sup> By the way that he came, by the same shall he return, and shall not come into this city, saith the Lord.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/isaiah/37#v35)</sup> For I will defend this city to save it for mine own sake, and for my servant David’s sake.

- Concerning the king of Assyria, the Most High says he shall not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with shields, nor cast a bank against it. Jerusalem is not going to be touched.
- By the way he came, by the same he shall return, and shall not come into the city. The Most High will defend the city to save it for His own sake and for His servant David's sake.

**[Isaiah 37:36-38](/bible/isaiah/37#v36)**

> <sup>[36](/bible/isaiah/37#v36)</sup> Then the angel of the Lord went forth, and smote in the camp of the Assyrians a hundred and fourscore and five thousand: and when they arose early in the morning, behold, they were all dead corpses.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/isaiah/37#v37)</sup> So Sennacherib king of Assyria departed, and went and returned, and dwelt at Nineveh.
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/isaiah/37#v38)</sup> And it came to pass, as he was worshipping in the house of Nisroch his god, that Adrammelech and Sharezer his sons smote him with the sword; and they escaped into the land of Armenia: and Esar–haddon his son reigned in his stead.

- The angel of the Most High goes forth and smites the camp of the Assyrians, a hundred and eighty-five thousand, and in the morning they are all dead corpses. One angel destroys the whole army, proving no nation stands against Israel when the Most High defends it.
- Sennacherib departs and returns and dwells at Nineveh, and as he worships in the house of Nisroch his god, his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer smite him with the sword and escape into Armenia. The prophecy of [Isaiah 37:7](/bible/isaiah/37#v7) is fulfilled: he falls by the sword in his own land, killed by his own children.
- Esarhaddon his son reigns in his stead. The Most High's word stands from beginning to end.
