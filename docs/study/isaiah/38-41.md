Read the chapter → [Isaiah 38](/bible/isaiah/38)

This session reads the four chapters [Isaiah 38](/bible/isaiah/38) through 41. The teacher opens by tying the history together: [Isaiah 38](/bible/isaiah/38) and 39 are a direct correlation to [2 Kings 20](/bible/2-kings/20), retelling the same events with a little more detail because Isaiah was the prophet in the days of King Hezekiah. From there the reading moves into the comfort of chapter 40 and the declaration in chapter 41 that Israel is the servant and the chosen of the Most High, with destruction coming on the idols and the nations that stand against the prophets.

**[Isaiah 38:1-8](/bible/isaiah/38#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/38#v1)</sup> In those days was Hezekiah sick unto death. And Isaiah the prophet the son of Amoz came unto him, and said unto him, Thus saith the Lord, Set thine house in order: for thou shalt die, and not live.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/38#v2)</sup> Then Hezekiah turned his face toward the wall, and prayed unto the Lord,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/38#v3)</sup> And said, Remember now, O Lord, I beseech thee, how I have walked before thee in truth and with a perfect heart, and have done that which is good in thy sight. And Hezekiah wept sore.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/38#v4)</sup> Then came the word of the Lord to Isaiah, saying,
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/38#v5)</sup> Go, and say to Hezekiah, Thus saith the Lord, the God of David thy father, I have heard thy prayer, I have seen thy tears: behold, I will add unto thy days fifteen years.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/38#v6)</sup> And I will deliver thee and this city out of the hand of the king of Assyria: and I will defend this city.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/38#v7)</sup> And this shall be a sign unto thee from the Lord, that the Lord will do this thing that he hath spoken;
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/38#v8)</sup> Behold, I will bring again the shadow of the degrees, which is gone down in the sun dial of Ahaz, ten degrees backward. So the sun returned ten degrees, by which degrees it was gone down.

- In those days Hezekiah was sick unto death, and Isaiah the son of Amoz came and told him to set his house in order, for he would die and not live. Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and prayed, reminding the Most High how he had walked before him in truth and with a perfect heart, and he wept sore.
- The word of the Lord came again to Isaiah: the Most High had heard the prayer and seen the tears, and would add fifteen years to Hezekiah's days and deliver the city out of the hand of the king of Assyria.
- The sign given was the shadow on the sundial of Ahaz brought back ten degrees. The prophet had told them it was a light thing to make the sundial go forward ten degrees, so instead the sun returned backward ten degrees as the sign to Hezekiah.

    The same account of Hezekiah's sickness and Isaiah's word that he set his house in order.

**[Isaiah 38:9-22](/bible/isaiah/38#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/38#v9)</sup> The writing of Hezekiah king of Judah, when he had been sick, and was recovered of his sickness:
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/38#v10)</sup> I said in the cutting off of my days, I shall go to the gates of the grave: I am deprived of the residue of my years.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/38#v11)</sup> I said, I shall not see the Lord, even the Lord, in the land of the living: I shall behold man no more with the inhabitants of the world.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/38#v12)</sup> Mine age is departed, and is removed from me as a shepherd’s tent: I have cut off like a weaver my life: he will cut me off with pining sickness: from day even to night wilt thou make an end of me.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/38#v13)</sup> I reckoned till morning, that, as a lion, so will he break all my bones: from day even to night wilt thou make an end of me.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/38#v14)</sup> Like a crane or a swallow, so did I chatter: I did mourn as a dove: mine eyes fail with looking upward: O Lord, I am oppressed; undertake for me.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/38#v15)</sup> What shall I say? he hath both spoken unto me, and himself hath done it: I shall go softly all my years in the bitterness of my soul.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/38#v16)</sup> O Lord, by these things men live, and in all these things is the life of my spirit: so wilt thou recover me, and make me to live.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/38#v17)</sup> Behold, for peace I had great bitterness: but thou hast in love to my soul delivered it from the pit of corruption: for thou hast cast all my sins behind thy back.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/38#v18)</sup> For the grave cannot praise thee, death can not celebrate thee: they that go down into the pit cannot hope for thy truth.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/isaiah/38#v19)</sup> The living, the living, he shall praise thee, as I do this day: the father to the children shall make known thy truth.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/38#v20)</sup> The Lord was ready to save me: therefore we will sing my songs to the stringed instruments all the days of our life in the house of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/isaiah/38#v21)</sup> For Isaiah had said, Let them take a lump of figs, and lay it for a plaister upon the boil, and he shall recover.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/isaiah/38#v22)</sup> Hezekiah also had said, What is the sign that I shall go up to the house of the Lord?

- This is the writing of Hezekiah after he had been sick and recovered. He speaks of being deprived of the residue of his years, going to the gates of the grave, his life cut off like a weaver cuts the thread.
- He testifies that the Most High cast all his sins behind his back and delivered his soul from the pit of corruption. The grave cannot praise him and death cannot celebrate him; the living, the living shall praise him, and the father makes his truth known to the children.
- Isaiah had said to take a lump of figs and lay it as a plaster upon the boil that he might recover, and Hezekiah had asked what the sign was that he should go up to the house of the Lord.

# Isaiah 39: The Treasures Shown and Babylon Foretold

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 39](/bible/isaiah/39)

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/39#v1)</sup> At that time Merodach–baladan, the son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a present to Hezekiah: for he had heard that he had been sick, and was recovered.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/39#v2)</sup> And Hezekiah was glad of them, and shewed them the house of his precious things, the silver, and the gold, and the spices, and the precious ointment, and all the house of his armour, and all that was found in his treasures: there was nothing in his house, nor in all his dominion, that Hezekiah shewed them not.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/39#v3)</sup> Then came Isaiah the prophet unto king Hezekiah, and said unto him, What said these men? and from whence came they unto thee? And Hezekiah said, They are come from a far country unto me, even from Babylon.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/39#v4)</sup> Then said he, What have they seen in thine house? And Hezekiah answered, All that is in mine house have they seen: there is nothing among my treasures that I have not shewed them.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/39#v5)</sup> Then said Isaiah to Hezekiah, Hear the word of the Lord of hosts:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/39#v6)</sup> Behold, the days come, that all that is in thine house, and that which thy fathers have laid up in store until this day, shall be carried to Babylon: nothing shall be left, saith the Lord.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/39#v7)</sup> And of thy sons that shall issue from thee, which thou shalt beget, shall they take away; and they shall be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/39#v8)</sup> Then said Hezekiah to Isaiah, Good is the word of the Lord which thou hast spoken. He said moreover, For there shall be peace and truth in my days.

- Merodach-baladan the son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a present to Hezekiah, having heard he had been sick and recovered. Hezekiah was glad of them and showed them all the house of his precious things, the silver, the gold, the spices, the ointment, and all his armor, holding nothing back in all his dominion.
- Isaiah came and asked what the men had said and where they came from. When Hezekiah answered that they came from Babylon and had seen everything among his treasures, Isaiah declared the days would come when all in his house, and what his fathers had stored up, would be carried to Babylon with nothing left.
- The prophecy adds that Hezekiah's own sons that issue from him would be taken and made eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon. Hezekiah called the word of the Lord good because there would be peace and truth in his days.
  - **[Daniel 1:6](/bible/daniel/1#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/daniel/1#v6)</sup> Now among these were of the children of Judah, Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah:

    The word about sons made eunuchs in Babylon points forward to Daniel, a eunuch in the palace of the king of Babylon.

# Isaiah 40: Comfort My People and the Everlasting God

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 40](/bible/isaiah/40)

**[Isaiah 40:1-8](/bible/isaiah/40#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/40#v1)</sup> Comfort ye, comfort ye my people, saith your God.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/40#v2)</sup> Speak ye comfortably to Jerusalem, and cry unto her, that her warfare is accomplished, that her iniquity is pardoned: for she hath received of the Lord’s hand double for all her sins.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/40#v3)</sup> The voice of him that crieth in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make straight in the desert a highway for our God.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/40#v4)</sup> Every valley shall be exalted, and every mountain and hill shall be made low: and the crooked shall be made straight, and the rough places plain:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/40#v5)</sup> And the glory of the Lord shall be revealed, and all flesh shall see it together: for the mouth of the Lord hath spoken it.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/40#v6)</sup> The voice said, Cry. And he said, What shall I cry? All flesh is grass, and all the goodliness thereof is as the flower of the field:
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/40#v7)</sup> The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: because the spirit of the Lord bloweth upon it: surely the people is grass.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/40#v8)</sup> The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever.

- Comfort ye, comfort ye my people, saith your God. The word is to speak comfortably to Jerusalem and cry that her warfare is accomplished and her iniquity pardoned, for she has received of the Lord's hand double for all her sins.
- The voice crying in the wilderness to prepare the way of the Lord goes into the prophecy of John, the spirit of Elijah, with every valley exalted, every mountain and hill made low, and the crooked made straight so the glory of the Lord shall be revealed and all flesh shall see it together.
- All flesh is grass and its goodliness as the flower of the field; the grass withers and the flower fades, but the word of our God stands forever. It compares our lives to the flowers of the field: we are born, we grow, we die, and go back into the ground, but the word of the Most High is forever.

- The Most High measured the waters in the hollow of his hand, meted out heaven with a span, and weighed the mountains in scales. No one directed his Spirit or taught him counsel, judgment, knowledge, or the way of understanding.
- All nations are as a drop of a bucket and counted as the small dust of the balance. Lebanon is not sufficient to burn nor its beasts sufficient for the offering; all nations before him are as nothing, counted less than nothing and vanity.

**[Isaiah 40:18-20](/bible/isaiah/40#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/40#v18)</sup> To whom then will ye liken God? or what likeness will ye compare unto him?

>
> <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/40#v20)</sup> He that is so impoverished that he hath no oblation chooseth a tree that will not rot; he seeketh unto him a cunning workman to prepare a graven image, that shall not be moved.

- To whom then will you liken God, or what likeness compare to him? The workman melts a graven image, the goldsmith spreads it with gold and casts silver chains.
- He that is too poor for such an offering chooses a tree that will not rot and seeks a skilled workman to prepare a graven image that will not be moved. Seeking a tree is the spirit of Nimrod that our people worshipped back then; instead of the tree itself, he made a graven image of the tree.

- Have you not known, have you not heard from the beginning? It is he that sits upon the circle of the earth, and the inhabitants are as grasshoppers; he stretches out the heavens as a curtain and spreads them as a tent to dwell in. The circle of the earth is the two-dimensional shape of a sphere.
- He brings the princes to nothing and makes the judges of the earth as vanity; they are not planted or sown before he blows upon them and the whirlwind takes them away as stubble.
- Lift up your eyes on high and behold who created these things, bringing out their host by number and calling them all by name; by the greatness of his might, not one fails.

>
> <sup>[28](/bible/isaiah/40#v28)</sup> Hast thou not known? hast thou not heard, that the everlasting God, the Lord, the Creator of the ends of the earth, fainteth not, neither is weary? there is no searching of his understanding.

- Why does Jacob say his way is hid from the Lord and his judgment passed over from his God? The everlasting God, the Creator of the ends of the earth, faints not, is not weary, and there is no searching of his understanding.

- This goes into Israel receiving the kingdom and the power of the Lord. Everybody may get weary and faint, but the Lord does not, and those that trust in him mount up with wings as eagles, run and are not weary, walk and do not faint.

# Isaiah 41: Israel My Servant, the Chosen Seed

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 41](/bible/isaiah/41)

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/41#v1)</sup> Keep silence before me, O islands; and let the people renew their strength: let them come near; then let them speak: let us come near together to judgment.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/41#v2)</sup> Who raised up the righteous man from the east, called him to his foot, gave the nations before him, and made him rule over kings? he gave them as the dust to his sword, and as driven stubble to his bow.

>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/41#v4)</sup> Who hath wrought and done it, calling the generations from the beginning? I the Lord, the first, and with the last; I am he.

>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/41#v6)</sup> They helped every one his neighbour; and every one said to his brother, Be of good courage.
>

- Keep silence before me, O islands, and let the people renew their strength and come near to judgment. Who raised up the righteous man from the east, called him to his foot, gave nations before him and made him rule over kings, giving them as dust to his sword and driven stubble to his bow.
- The Lord who wrought and did it, calling the generations from the beginning, is the first and with the last: I am he. This is talking about Christ, the first and the last, whom the isles saw and feared as the ends of the earth drew near.
- The carpenter encouraged the goldsmith, and he that smooths with the hammer encouraged him that smote the anvil, saying it is ready for the soldering, and he fastened the idol with nails so it should not be moved.

- The Most High took Israel from the ends of the earth, called him from the chief men, and said, Thou art my servant, I have chosen thee and not cast thee away.
- Fear thou not, for I am with thee; I will strengthen thee and uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness. All that were incensed against Israel shall be ashamed and confounded, be as nothing, and perish; those that war against Israel shall be as a thing of naught.

He will make Israel a new sharp threshing instrument with teeth to thresh the mountains, beat them small, and make the hills as chaff to be scattered by the whirlwind, that Israel may rejoice and glory in the Holy One of Israel.
- When the poor and needy seek water and their tongue fails for thirst, the Lord will hear them and the God of Israel will not forsake them; he will open rivers in high places and make the wilderness a pool of water.
- He will plant in the wilderness the cedar, the shittah tree, the myrtle, and the oil tree, and set in the desert the fir, the pine, and the box tree together, that all may see and know that the hand of the Lord has done it and the Holy One of Israel created it.

**[Isaiah 41:21-24](/bible/isaiah/41#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/isaiah/41#v21)</sup> Produce your cause, saith the Lord; bring forth your strong reasons, saith the King of Jacob.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/isaiah/41#v22)</sup> Let them bring them forth, and shew us what shall happen: let them shew the former things, what they be, that we may consider them, and know the latter end of them; or declare us things for to come.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/isaiah/41#v23)</sup> Shew the things that are to come hereafter, that we may know that ye are gods: yea, do good, or do evil, that we may be dismayed, and behold it together.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/isaiah/41#v24)</sup> Behold, ye are of nothing, and your work of nought: an abomination is he that chooseth you.

- Produce your cause, saith the Lord; bring forth your strong reasons, saith the King of Jacob. Let the idols show the former things and declare the things to come, that men may know whether they are gods, and do good or evil that all may be dismayed together.
- The idols are of nothing and their work of naught; an abomination is he that chooses them. This goes to the false gods: nowhere in Christianity or any other religion on earth can they tell us why we are in the position we are in, what happened in the past, what is going on now, and what is to come. That is how you know this book is the most powerful book on the face of the earth.

**[Isaiah 41:25-29](/bible/isaiah/41#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/isaiah/41#v25)</sup> I have raised up one from the north, and he shall come: from the rising of the sun shall he call upon my name: and he shall come upon princes as upon morter, and as the potter treadeth clay.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/isaiah/41#v26)</sup> Who hath declared from the beginning, that we may know? and beforetime, that we may say, He is righteous? yea, there is none that sheweth, yea, there is none that declareth, yea, there is none that heareth your words.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/isaiah/41#v27)</sup> The first shall say to Zion, Behold, behold them: and I will give to Jerusalem one that bringeth good tidings.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/isaiah/41#v28)</sup> For I beheld, and there was no man; even among them, and there was no counsellor, that, when I asked of them, could answer a word.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/isaiah/41#v29)</sup> Behold, they are all vanity; their works are nothing: their molten images are wind and confusion.

- The Most High raised one up from the earth who comes with the rising of the sun and calls upon his name, coming upon the princes as upon mortar and as the potter treads clay. None of the idols declared it from the beginning or foretold it so men could call them righteous.
- The first shall say to Zion, Behold them, and give to Jerusalem one that brings good tidings, meaning the gospel, the good news. Among the idols there was no man, no counselor that could answer a word; they are all vanity, their works nothing, their molten images wind and confusion.
- This is a heavy chapter on idolatry and what is to come for Israel. Israel is the chosen, the seed of the Lord, the servants of God, with the kingdom coming to them, while destruction is coming on the idols and the nations that stand against the prophets.
