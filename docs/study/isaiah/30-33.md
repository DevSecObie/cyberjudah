Read the chapter → [Isaiah 30](/bible/isaiah/30)

This session reads four chapters a day through the book of Isaiah during the time of the Assyrian captivity. Before reading, the teacher frames how to study our history: read it over and over, precept what you read back to the laws we broke, and line up the prophets by the kings they served under. [Isaiah 1:1](/bible/isaiah/1#v1) names Isaiah prophesying in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah; [Hosea 1:1](/bible/hosea/1#v1) and [Micah 1:1](/bible/micah/1#v1) name the same kings, showing these prophets stood together in one era. Keep a who's who, a Bible dictionary, and the chart of the captivities close, because four chapters a day is a foundation, not the whole of your history. We came out of Egypt, then suffered the major captivities of Assyria (Isaiah), Babylon (Jeremiah and Ezekiel), the Persians and Medes (Ezra and Nehemiah), Greece (the Maccabees), and Rome (the New Testament). This is spiritual warfare, so gather your weapons of war.

**[Isaiah 30:1-5](/bible/isaiah/30#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/30#v1)</sup> Woe to the rebellious children, saith the Lord, that take counsel, but not of me; and that cover with a covering, but not of my spirit, that they may add sin to sin:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/30#v2)</sup> That walk to go down into Egypt, and have not asked at my mouth; to strengthen themselves in the strength of Pharaoh, and to trust in the shadow of Egypt!
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/30#v3)</sup> Therefore shall the strength of Pharaoh be your shame, and the trust in the shadow of Egypt your confusion.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/30#v4)</sup> For his princes were at Zoan, and his ambassadors came to Hanes.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/30#v5)</sup> They were all ashamed of a people that could not profit them, nor be an help nor profit, but a shame, and also a reproach.

- Woe to the rebellious children who take counsel but not of the Most High, and who go down into Egypt without asking at his mouth. This is the same rebellion named at the start of the book.
- Leaning to Pharaoh's strength instead of the Most High turns their trust into shame and confusion. There were times kings sought the Most High, and times they sought heathen kings to fight their battles for them.
  - **[Isaiah 1:4](/bible/isaiah/1#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/1#v4)</sup> Ah sinful nation, a people laden with iniquity, a seed of evildoers, children that are corrupters: they have forsaken the Lord, they have provoked the Holy One of Israel unto anger, they are gone away backward.

    The seed of evildoers, the same rebellious children being addressed here.

**[Isaiah 30:6-8](/bible/isaiah/30#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/30#v6)</sup> The burden of the beasts of the south: into the land of trouble and anguish, from whence come the young and old lion, the viper and fiery flying serpent, they will carry their riches upon the shoulders of young asses, and their treasures upon the bunches of camels, to a people that shall not profit them.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/30#v7)</sup> For the Egyptians shall help in vain, and to no purpose: therefore have I cried concerning this, Their strength is to sit still.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/30#v8)</sup> Now go, write it before them in a table, and note it in a book, that it may be for the time to come for ever and ever:

- The riches carried down to Egypt on asses and camels buy help that profits nothing, for the Egyptians shall help in vain. The same thing happened in the Babylonian captivity, and the same thing is happening to us today.
- The Most High tells Isaiah to write it in a table and note it in a book for a time to come forever, because these things were written for our learning.
  - **[Lamentations 4:17](/bible/lamentations/4#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/lamentations/4#v17)</sup> As for us, our eyes as yet failed for our vain help: in our watching we have watched for a nation that could not save us.

    In captivity we watched in vain for a nation that could not save us.
  - **[Romans 15:4](/bible/romans/15#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/romans/15#v4)</sup> For whatsoever things were written aforetime were written for our learning, that we through patience and comfort of the scriptures might have hope.

    Things written before were written for our learning; nothing has changed.

**[Isaiah 30:9-14](/bible/isaiah/30#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/30#v9)</sup> That this is a rebellious people, lying children, children that will not hear the law of the Lord:
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/30#v10)</sup> Which say to the seers, See not; and to the prophets, Prophesy not unto us right things, speak unto us smooth things, prophesy deceits:
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/30#v11)</sup> Get you out of the way, turn aside out of the path, cause the Holy One of Israel to cease from before us.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/30#v12)</sup> Wherefore thus saith the Holy One of Israel, Because ye despise this word, and trust in oppression and perverseness, and stay thereon:
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/30#v13)</sup> Therefore this iniquity shall be to you as a breach ready to fall, swelling out in a high wall, whose breaking cometh suddenly at an instant.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/30#v14)</sup> And he shall break it as the breaking of the potters’ vessel that is broken in pieces; he shall not spare: so that there shall not be found in the bursting of it a sherd to take fire from the hearth, or to take water withal out of the pit.

- This is a rebellious, lying people that will not hear the law of the Most High, telling the seers to prophesy smooth things and deceits. Our pastors do the same today, teaching that the laws are done away with.
- They want the Holy One of Israel to cease from before them because they love their oppression and the ways of their slave masters. That iniquity becomes a breach ready to fall, breaking suddenly like a potter's vessel that cannot be spared.

**[Isaiah 30:15-17](/bible/isaiah/30#v15)**
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/30#v15)</sup> For thus saith the Lord God, the Holy One of Israel; In returning and rest shall ye be saved; in quietness and in confidence shall be your strength: and ye would not.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/30#v16)</sup> But ye said, No; for we will flee upon horses; therefore shall ye flee: and, We will ride upon the swift; therefore shall they that pursue you be swift.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/30#v17)</sup> One thousand shall flee at the rebuke of one; at the rebuke of five shall ye flee: till ye be left as a beacon upon the top of a mountain, and as an ensign on an hill.

- In returning and rest, in quietness and confidence, is our salvation and strength, but the people would not have it. Instead they say they will flee upon horses.
- Because they trust their own swiftness, one thousand will flee at the rebuke of one, until they are left as a lonely beacon on a hill.

**[Isaiah 30:18-19](/bible/isaiah/30#v18)**
> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/30#v18)</sup> And therefore will the Lord wait, that he may be gracious unto you, and therefore will he be exalted, that he may have mercy upon you: for the Lord is a God of judgment: blessed are all they that wait for him.

- The Most High waits to be gracious and exalts himself to have mercy, for he is a God of judgment: blessed are all they that wait for him. Waiting means holding patience through the trials and tribulations.
- People will come, even family members, saying no one is coming to save you and you must save yourself with guns. You must stay steadfast and answer the Most High's cry with patience.

**[Isaiah 30:20-22](/bible/isaiah/30#v20)**
> <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/30#v20)</sup> And though the Lord give you the bread of adversity, and the water of affliction, yet shall not thy teachers be removed into a corner any more, but thine eyes shall see thy teachers:

- Then the Most High gives rain for the seed, bread of the increase, fat and plenteous pasture, and rivers on every high hill in the day of the great slaughter when the towers fall.
- The light of the moon shall be as the sun, and the sun sevenfold, in the day he binds up the breach of his people and heals the stroke of their wound.

>

- The name of the Most High comes from far, burning with anger, his tongue a devouring fire, sifting the nations with the sieve of vanity. His people shall have a song and gladness of heart as when one comes to the mountain of the Most High.
- Like King David dancing before the ark until Michal despised him, we will be joyful in that day when we no longer fear being shot in the street, having our doors kicked in, or losing our homes. Through the voice of the Most High the Assyrian is beaten down, for Tophet is ordained of old and its fire kindled by the breath of the Most High.

# Isaiah 31: Egypt Is Flesh, Not Spirit

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 31](/bible/isaiah/31)

**[Isaiah 31:1](/bible/isaiah/31#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/31#v1)</sup> Woe to them that go down to Egypt for help; and stay on horses, and trust in chariots, because they are many; and in horsemen, because they are very strong; but they look not unto the Holy One of Israel, neither seek the Lord!

- Woe to them that go down to Egypt for help, staying on horses and trusting chariots, but not looking to the Holy One of Israel. This makes plain what chapter 30 opened with.
- We honor the Most High with our lips, but when the oppressor offers gifts we turn away and serve our oppressor's will. When Sennacherib's servant Rabshakeh offered Hezekiah two thousand horses, it was the same trick, a gift to pull the heart off the Most High.
  - **[Ecclesiastes 7:7](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v7)</sup> Surely oppression maketh a wise man mad; and a gift destroyeth the heart.

    Oppression makes a wise man mad, and a gift destroyeth the heart.
  - **[Isaiah 36:1,6,8](/bible/isaiah/36#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/36#v1)</sup> Now it came to pass in the fourteenth year of king Hezekiah, that Sennacherib king of Assyria came up against all the defenced cities of Judah, and took them.
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/36#v6)</sup> Lo, thou trustest in the staff of this broken reed, on Egypt; whereon if a man lean, it will go into his hand, and pierce it: so is Pharaoh king of Egypt to all that trust in him.
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/36#v8)</sup> Now therefore give pledges, I pray thee, to my master the king of Assyria, and I will give thee two thousand horses, if thou be able on thy part to set riders upon them.

    Sennacherib mocks trust in Egypt, the broken reed, and dangles two thousand horses to turn the heart.

**[Isaiah 31:2-3](/bible/isaiah/31#v2)**
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/31#v2)</sup> Yet he also is wise, and will bring evil, and will not call back his words: but will arise against the house of the evildoers, and against the help of them that work iniquity.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/31#v3)</sup> Now the Egyptians are men, and not God; and their horses flesh, and not spirit. When the Lord shall stretch out his hand, both he that helpeth shall fall, and he that is holpen shall fall down, and they all shall fail together.

- The Most High is also wise, will bring evil, and will not call back his words, but arises against the evildoers and against those who help them. The Egyptians are men and not God, their horses flesh and not spirit.
- When the Most High stretches out his hand, both the helper and the one helped fall down together. A nation cannot help you when the Most High has decided to punish both.

**[Isaiah 31:4-6](/bible/isaiah/31#v4)**
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/31#v4)</sup> For thus hath the Lord spoken unto me, Like as the lion and the young lion roaring on his prey, when a multitude of shepherds is called forth against him, he will not be afraid of their voice, nor abase himself for the noise of them: so shall the Lord of hosts come down to fight for mount Zion, and for the hill thereof.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/31#v5)</sup> As birds flying, so will the Lord of hosts defend Jerusalem; defending also he will deliver it; and passing over he will preserve it.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/31#v6)</sup> Turn ye unto him from whom the children of Israel have deeply revolted.

- As a lion is not afraid of the shepherds' voices, so the Lord of hosts comes down to fight for Mount Zion, defending Jerusalem as birds flying. He will deliver, preserve, and pass over it.
- Therefore turn to him, from whom the children of Israel have deeply revolted; that revolt is the same rebellion of the rebellious children.

**[Isaiah 31:7-9](/bible/isaiah/31#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/31#v7)</sup> For in that day every man shall cast away his idols of silver, and his idols of gold, which your own hands have made unto you for a sin.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/31#v8)</sup> Then shall the Assyrian fall with the sword, not of a mighty man; and the sword, not of a mean man, shall devour him: but he shall flee from the sword, and his young men shall be discomfited.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/31#v9)</sup> And he shall pass over to his strong hold for fear, and his princes shall be afraid of the ensign, saith the Lord, whose fire is in Zion, and his furnace in Jerusalem.

- In that day every man casts away the idols of silver and gold his own hands made for a sin. Then the Assyrian falls by a sword that is not of man.
- He flees, his young men are discomfited, and his princes are afraid of the ensign, because the Most High's fire is in Zion and his furnace in Jerusalem. When the Most High brings his judgment, the heathen nations can do nothing, so we wait patiently while he waits for us to get ourselves together.

# Isaiah 33: The Lord Our Judge, Lawgiver, and King

**[Isaiah 33:1-2](/bible/isaiah/33#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/33#v1)</sup> Woe to thee that spoilest, and thou wast not spoiled; and dealest treacherously, and they dealt not treacherously with thee! when thou shalt cease to spoil, thou shalt be spoiled; and when thou shalt make an end to deal treacherously, they shall deal treacherously with thee.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/33#v2)</sup> O Lord, be gracious unto us; we have waited for thee: be thou their arm every morning, our salvation also in the time of trouble.

- Woe to the spoiler who was not spoiled and the treacherous who was not dealt with treacherously; when he ceases, he himself will be spoiled and dealt with treacherously.
- "O Lord, be gracious unto us; we have waited for thee" is again the cry of patience. As Passover approaches spirits can get shaky, so the word is brought to heal you and remind you that when you go through trials your faith is being tested, the way [Ecclesiasticus 2](/bible/sirach/2) must be applied.

    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/131#v2)</sup> Surely I have behaved and quieted myself, as a child that is weaned of his mother: my soul is even as a weaned child.
    >
    > <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/131#v3)</sup> Let Israel hope in the Lord from henceforth and for ever.

    King David quiets himself as a weaned child and says, "Let Israel hope in the Lord from henceforth and for ever."

**[Isaiah 33:3-4](/bible/isaiah/33#v3)**
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/33#v3)</sup> At the noise of the tumult the people fled; at the lifting up of thyself the nations were scattered.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/33#v4)</sup> And your spoil shall be gathered like the gathering of the caterpiller: as the running to and fro of locusts shall he run upon them.

- At the noise of the tumult the peoples fled, and at the lifting up of the Most High the nations were scattered. The spoil is gathered like the gathering of the caterpillar and the running of locusts.

**[Isaiah 33:5-6](/bible/isaiah/33#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/33#v5)</sup> The Lord is exalted; for he dwelleth on high: he hath filled Zion with judgment and righteousness.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/33#v6)</sup> And wisdom and knowledge shall be the stability of thy times, and strength of salvation: the fear of the Lord is his treasure.

- The Most High is exalted, dwelling on high, filling Zion with judgment and righteousness. Wisdom and knowledge, which are the commandments, shall be the stability of thy times, and the fear of the Most High is his treasure.
- We are the church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth, and we must learn to behave ourselves and possess our vessels, constrained from sin.

**[Isaiah 33:7-12](/bible/isaiah/33#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/33#v7)</sup> Behold, their valiant ones shall cry without: the ambassadors of peace shall weep bitterly.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/33#v8)</sup> The highways lie waste, the wayfaring man ceaseth: he hath broken the covenant, he hath despised the cities, he regardeth no man.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/33#v9)</sup> The earth mourneth and languisheth: Lebanon is ashamed and hewn down: Sharon is like a wilderness; and Bashan and Carmel shake off their fruits.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/33#v10)</sup> Now will I rise, saith the Lord; now will I be exalted; now will I lift up myself.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/33#v11)</sup> Ye shall conceive chaff, ye shall bring forth stubble: your breath, as fire, shall devour you.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/33#v12)</sup> And the people shall be as the burnings of lime: as thorns cut up shall they be burned in the fire.

- The valiant ones cry outside and the ambassadors of peace weep bitterly; the highways lie waste, the covenant is broken, and the earth mourns while Lebanon, Sharon, Bashan, and Carmel are ashamed. Now the Most High arises, exalts himself, and lifts himself up.
- The people conceive chaff and bring forth stubble, and their own breath as fire devours them; they burn like lime and cut thorns in the fire.

**[Isaiah 33:13-16](/bible/isaiah/33#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/33#v13)</sup> Hear, ye that are far off, what I have done; and, ye that are near, acknowledge my might.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/33#v14)</sup> The sinners in Zion are afraid; fearfulness hath surprised the hypocrites. Who among us shall dwell with the devouring fire? who among us shall dwell with everlasting burnings?
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/33#v15)</sup> He that walketh righteously, and speaketh uprightly; he that despiseth the gain of oppressions, that shaketh his hands from holding of bribes, that stoppeth his ears from hearing of blood, and shutteth his eyes from seeing evil;
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/33#v16)</sup> He shall dwell on high: his place of defence shall be the munitions of rocks: bread shall be given him; his waters shall be sure.

- Those far off and near are told to acknowledge the Most High's might; the sinners in Zion are afraid and fearfulness surprises the hypocrites. That is the breaking that comes in an instant, so prepare yourself.
- Who shall dwell with the devouring fire and everlasting burnings? He that walks righteously, speaks uprightly, despises oppression's gain, shakes his hands from bribes, and stops his ears from blood. He shall dwell on high, his bread and water sure.

**[Isaiah 33:17-19](/bible/isaiah/33#v17)**
> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/33#v17)</sup> Thine eyes shall see the king in his beauty: they shall behold the land that is very far off.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/33#v18)</sup> Thine heart shall meditate terror. Where is the scribe? where is the receiver? where is he that counted the towers?
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/isaiah/33#v19)</sup> Thou shalt not see a fierce people, a people of a deeper speech than thou canst perceive; of a stammering tongue, that thou canst not understand.

- Thine eyes shall see the King in his beauty and behold the land that is very far off, our homeland, far off because we are scattered through the four corners of the earth in Babylon. The heart shall meditate on the terror that is past, asking where is the scribe and the receiver.
- Thou shalt not see the fierce people of a deeper, stammering speech thou canst not understand.

**[Isaiah 33:20-24](/bible/isaiah/33#v20)**
> <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/33#v20)</sup> Look upon Zion, the city of our solemnities: thine eyes shall see Jerusalem a quiet habitation, a tabernacle that shall not be taken down; not one of the stakes thereof shall ever be removed, neither shall any of the cords thereof be broken.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/isaiah/33#v21)</sup> But there the glorious Lord will be unto us a place of broad rivers and streams; wherein shall go no galley with oars, neither shall gallant ship pass thereby.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/isaiah/33#v22)</sup> For the Lord is our judge, the Lord is our lawgiver, the Lord is our king; he will save us.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/isaiah/33#v23)</sup> Thy tacklings are loosed; they could not well strengthen their mast, they could not spread the sail: then is the prey of a great spoil divided; the lame take the prey.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/isaiah/33#v24)</sup> And the inhabitant shall not say, I am sick: the people that dwell therein shall be forgiven their iniquity.

- Look upon Zion, the city of our solemnities; Jerusalem shall be a quiet habitation, a tabernacle not taken down, its stakes never removed and its cords never broken. There the glorious Most High is a place of broad rivers where no hostile galley or gallant ship passes.
- The Most High is our judge, lawgiver, and king, and he will save us; the enemy's tacklings hang loose and cannot spread the sail, so even the lame take the prey, as the feeble among us will be as David in that day. The inhabitant shall not say "I am sick," and the people are forgiven their iniquity, which tells you those in Jerusalem today are not the real Jews.
