Read the chapter → [Isaiah 22](/bible/isaiah/22)

This session reads [Isaiah 22](/bible/isaiah/22) through 25 straight through, as a reading rather than an exposition; the teacher states plainly that these are heavier chapters and that he is only reading today, directing any deeper questions to the leadership. The four chapters move through four burdens and visions: the burden of the valley of vision against Jerusalem and its rulers (22), the burden of Tyre the merchant city (23), the earth made empty and turned upside down in the day of judgment (24), and a closing song of praise for the Most High who is a strength to the poor and who will swallow up death in victory (25).

**[Isaiah 22:1-4](/bible/isaiah/22#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/22#v1)</sup> The burden of the valley of vision. What aileth thee now, that thou art wholly gone up to the housetops?
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/22#v2)</sup> Thou that art full of stirs, a tumultuous city, a joyous city: thy slain men are not slain with the sword, nor dead in battle.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/22#v3)</sup> All thy rulers are fled together, they are bound by the archers: all that are found in thee are bound together, which have fled from far.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/22#v4)</sup> Therefore said I, Look away from me; I will weep bitterly, labour not to comfort me, because of the spoiling of the daughter of my people.

- The burden of the valley of vision falls on a city gone up to the housetops, a tumultuous and once joyous city whose slain men are not slain with the sword nor dead in battle. Its rulers are fled together and bound, and the prophet turns away to weep bitterly and will not be comforted.

**[Isaiah 22:5-8](/bible/isaiah/22#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/22#v5)</sup> For it is a day of trouble, and of treading down, and of perplexity by the Lord God of hosts in the valley of vision, breaking down the walls, and of crying to the mountains.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/22#v6)</sup> And Elam bare the quiver with chariots of men and horsemen, and Kir uncovered the shield.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/22#v7)</sup> And it shall come to pass, that thy choicest valleys shall be full of chariots, and the horsemen shall set themselves in array at the gate.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/22#v8)</sup> And he discovered the covering of Judah, and thou didst look in that day to the armour of the house of the forest.

- It is a day of trouble, of treading down, and of perplexity by the Most High of hosts in the valley of vision, with the walls broken down and a crying to the mountains. Elam bears the quiver with chariots of men and horsemen, and Kir uncovers the shield, so the choicest valleys are full of chariots set in array at the gate.

**[Isaiah 22:9-11](/bible/isaiah/22#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/22#v9)</sup> Ye have seen also the breaches of the city of David, that they are many: and ye gathered together the waters of the lower pool.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/22#v10)</sup> And ye have numbered the houses of Jerusalem, and the houses have ye broken down to fortify the wall.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/22#v11)</sup> Ye made also a ditch between the two walls for the water of the old pool: but ye have not looked unto the maker thereof, neither had respect unto him that fashioned it long ago.

- The people look to their defenses in that day: the breaches of the city of David, the gathering of the waters of the lower pool, the numbering and breaking down of houses to fortify the wall, and a ditch made between the two walls for the water of the old pool. Yet they have not looked unto the Maker thereof nor had respect unto Him that fashioned it long ago.

**[Isaiah 22:12-14](/bible/isaiah/22#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/22#v12)</sup> And in that day did the Lord God of hosts call to weeping, and to mourning, and to baldness, and to girding with sackcloth:
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/22#v13)</sup> And behold joy and gladness, slaying oxen, and killing sheep, eating flesh, and drinking wine: let us eat and drink; for to morrow we shall die.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/22#v14)</sup> And it was revealed in mine ears by the Lord of hosts, Surely this iniquity shall not be purged from you till ye die, saith the Lord God of hosts.

- In that day the Most High of hosts calls to weeping, mourning, baldness, and girding with sackcloth, but instead there is joy and gladness, slaying oxen and eating flesh, saying "let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we shall die." The Most High reveals that this iniquity shall not be purged from them till they die.

**[Isaiah 22:15-19](/bible/isaiah/22#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/22#v15)</sup> Thus saith the Lord God of hosts, Go, get thee unto this treasurer, even unto Shebna, which is over the house, and say,
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/22#v16)</sup> What hast thou here? and whom hast thou here, that thou hast hewed thee out a sepulchre here, as he that heweth him out a sepulchre on high, and that graveth an habitation for himself in a rock?
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/22#v17)</sup> Behold, the Lord will carry thee away with a mighty captivity, and will surely cover thee.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/22#v18)</sup> He will surely violently turn and toss thee like a ball into a large country: there shalt thou die, and there the chariots of thy glory shall be the shame of thy lord’s house.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/isaiah/22#v19)</sup> And I will drive thee from thy station, and from thy state shall he pull thee down.

- The word comes against Shebna, the treasurer over the house, who has hewn himself out a sepulchre on high and graved a habitation for himself in a rock. The Most High will carry him away with a mighty captivity, toss him like a ball into a large country where he shall die, and drive him from his station and pull him down from his state.

**[Isaiah 22:20-25](/bible/isaiah/22#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/22#v20)</sup> And it shall come to pass in that day, that I will call my servant Eliakim the son of Hilkiah:
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/isaiah/22#v21)</sup> And I will clothe him with thy robe, and strengthen him with thy girdle, and I will commit thy government into his hand: and he shall be a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and to the house of Judah.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/isaiah/22#v22)</sup> And the key of the house of David will I lay upon his shoulder; so he shall open, and none shall shut; and he shall shut, and none shall open.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/isaiah/22#v23)</sup> And I will fasten him as a nail in a sure place; and he shall be for a glorious throne to his father’s house.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/isaiah/22#v24)</sup> And they shall hang upon him all the glory of his father’s house, the offspring and the issue, all vessels of small quantity, from the vessels of cups, even to all the vessels of flagons.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/isaiah/22#v25)</sup> In that day, saith the Lord of hosts, shall the nail that is fastened in the sure place be removed, and be cut down, and fall; and the burden that was upon it shall be cut off: for the Lord hath spoken it.

- In Shebna's place the Most High calls His servant Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, clothing him with the robe and girdle and committing the government into his hand, so he is a father to Jerusalem and to Judah with the key of the house of David laid upon his shoulder to open and shut. He is fastened as a nail in a sure place for a glorious throne, yet in that day the nail shall be removed, cut down, and fall, and the burden upon it cut off.

# Isaiah 23: The Burden of Tyre

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 23](/bible/isaiah/23)

**[Isaiah 23:1-5](/bible/isaiah/23#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/23#v1)</sup> The burden of Tyre. Howl, ye ships of Tarshish; for it is laid waste, so that there is no house, no entering in: from the land of Chittim it is revealed to them.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/23#v2)</sup> Be still, ye inhabitants of the isle; thou whom the merchants of Zidon, that pass over the sea, have replenished.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/23#v3)</sup> And by great waters the seed of Sihor, the harvest of the river, is her revenue; and she is a mart of nations.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/23#v4)</sup> Be thou ashamed, O Zidon: for the sea hath spoken, even the strength of the sea, saying, I travail not, nor bring forth children, neither do I nourish up young men, nor bring up virgins.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/23#v5)</sup> As at the report concerning Egypt, so shall they be sorely pained at the report of Tyre.

- The burden of Tyre calls the ships of Tarshish to howl, for the merchant harbor is laid waste with no house and no entering in. The merchants of Zidon who crossed the sea are silenced, the seed of Sihor and the harvest of the river was her revenue, and the report of Tyre brings sorrow as the report concerning Egypt did.

**[Isaiah 23:6-9](/bible/isaiah/23#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/23#v6)</sup> Pass ye over to Tarshish; howl, ye inhabitants of the isle.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/23#v7)</sup> Is this your joyous city, whose antiquity is of ancient days? her own feet shall carry her afar off to sojourn.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/23#v8)</sup> Who hath taken this counsel against Tyre, the crowning city, whose merchants are princes, whose traffickers are the honourable of the earth?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/23#v9)</sup> The Lord of hosts hath purposed it, to stain the pride of all glory, and to bring into contempt all the honourable of the earth.

- The inhabitants of the isle are told to pass over to Tarshish and howl, for this joyous city of ancient days is brought down, the crowning city whose merchants are princes and whose traffickers are the honourable of the earth. The Most High of hosts has purposed it, to stain the pride of all glory and bring into contempt all the honourable of the earth.

**[Isaiah 23:10-14](/bible/isaiah/23#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/23#v10)</sup> Pass through thy land as a river, O daughter of Tarshish: there is no more strength.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/23#v11)</sup> He stretched out his hand over the sea, he shook the kingdoms: the Lord hath given a commandment against the merchant city, to destroy the strong holds thereof.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/23#v12)</sup> And he said, Thou shalt no more rejoice, O thou oppressed virgin, daughter of Zidon: arise, pass over to Chittim; there also shalt thou have no rest.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/23#v13)</sup> Behold the land of the Chaldeans; this people was not, till the Assyrian founded it for them that dwell in the wilderness: they set up the towers thereof, they raised up the palaces thereof; and he brought it to ruin.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/23#v14)</sup> Howl, ye ships of Tarshish: for your strength is laid waste.

- The Most High has stretched out His hand over the sea and shaken the kingdoms, giving a commandment against the merchant city to destroy its strongholds, so the oppressed virgin daughter of Zidon shall no more rejoice. The land of the Chaldeans is set forth as the example, brought to ruin, and again the ships of Tarshish are told to howl, for their strength is laid waste.

**[Isaiah 23:15-18](/bible/isaiah/23#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/23#v15)</sup> And it shall come to pass in that day, that Tyre shall be forgotten seventy years, according to the days of one king: after the end of seventy years shall Tyre sing as an harlot.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/23#v16)</sup> Take an harp, go about the city, thou harlot that hast been forgotten; make sweet melody, sing many songs, that thou mayest be remembered.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/23#v17)</sup> And it shall come to pass after the end of seventy years, that the Lord will visit Tyre, and she shall turn to her hire, and shall commit fornication with all the kingdoms of the world upon the face of the earth.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/23#v18)</sup> And her merchandise and her hire shall be holiness to the Lord: it shall not be treasured nor laid up; for her merchandise shall be for them that dwell before the Lord, to eat sufficiently, and for durable clothing.

- Tyre shall be forgotten seventy years, according to the days of one king, and afterward shall sing as a harlot to be remembered, committing fornication with all the kingdoms of the world. Yet at the end her merchandise and her hire shall be holiness to the Most High, not treasured nor laid up, but for them that dwell before Him to eat sufficiently and for durable clothing.

# Isaiah 24: The Earth Made Empty and Turned Upside Down

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 24](/bible/isaiah/24)

**[Isaiah 24:1-3](/bible/isaiah/24#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/24#v1)</sup> Behold, the Lord maketh the earth empty, and maketh it waste, and turneth it upside down, and scattereth abroad the inhabitants thereof.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/24#v2)</sup> And it shall be, as with the people, so with the priest; as with the servant, so with his master; as with the maid, so with her mistress; as with the buyer, so with the seller; as with the lender, so with the borrower; as with the taker of usury, so with the giver of usury to him.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/24#v3)</sup> The land shall be utterly emptied, and utterly spoiled: for the Lord hath spoken this word.

- The Most High makes the earth empty and waste, turns it upside down, and scatters abroad its inhabitants, and it shall be the same with every class of people: priest and people, servant and master, maid and mistress, buyer and seller, lender and borrower, taker and giver of usury. The land shall be utterly emptied and spoiled, for the Most High has spoken this word.

**[Isaiah 24:4-6](/bible/isaiah/24#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/24#v4)</sup> The earth mourneth and fadeth away, the world languisheth and fadeth away, the haughty people of the earth do languish.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/24#v5)</sup> The earth also is defiled under the inhabitants thereof; because they have transgressed the laws, changed the ordinance, broken the everlasting covenant.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/24#v6)</sup> Therefore hath the curse devoured the earth, and they that dwell therein are desolate: therefore the inhabitants of the earth are burned, and few men left.

- The earth mourns and fades away, the world languishes, and the haughty people of the earth languish, for the earth is defiled under its inhabitants because they have transgressed the laws, changed the ordinance, and broken the everlasting covenant. Therefore the curse has devoured the earth, its inhabitants are desolate and burned, and few men are left.

**[Isaiah 24:7-12](/bible/isaiah/24#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/24#v7)</sup> The new wine mourneth, the vine languisheth, all the merryhearted do sigh.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/24#v8)</sup> The mirth of tabrets ceaseth, the noise of them that rejoice endeth, the joy of the harp ceaseth.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/24#v9)</sup> They shall not drink wine with a song; strong drink shall be bitter to them that drink it.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/24#v10)</sup> The city of confusion is broken down: every house is shut up, that no man may come in.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/24#v11)</sup> There is a crying for wine in the streets; all joy is darkened, the mirth of the land is gone.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/24#v12)</sup> In the city is left desolation, and the gate is smitten with destruction.

- The new wine mourns, the vine languishes, and all the merryhearted sigh as the mirth of the tabrets and the joy of the harp cease. The city of confusion is broken down, every house shut up, and there is a crying for wine in the streets, all joy darkened and the mirth of the land gone, leaving desolation and the gate smitten with destruction.

**[Isaiah 24:13-16](/bible/isaiah/24#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/24#v13)</sup> When thus it shall be in the midst of the land among the people, there shall be as the shaking of an olive tree, and as the gleaning grapes when the vintage is done.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/24#v14)</sup> They shall lift up their voice, they shall sing for the majesty of the Lord, they shall cry aloud from the sea.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/24#v15)</sup> Wherefore glorify ye the Lord in the fires, even the name of the Lord God of Israel in the isles of the sea.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/24#v16)</sup> From the uttermost part of the earth have we heard songs, even glory to the righteous. But I said, My leanness, my leanness, woe unto me! the treacherous dealers have dealt treacherously; yea, the treacherous dealers have dealt very treacherously.

- In the midst of the land among the people it shall be as the shaking of an olive tree and the gleaning of grapes when the vintage is done. A remnant lifts up its voice to sing for the majesty of the Most High and to glorify Him from the sea and the isles, even glory to the righteous, yet the prophet cries of his leanness and of the treacherous dealers who deal very treacherously.

**[Isaiah 24:17-20](/bible/isaiah/24#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/24#v17)</sup> Fear, and the pit, and the snare, are upon thee, O inhabitant of the earth.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/24#v18)</sup> And it shall come to pass, that he who fleeth from the noise of the fear shall fall into the pit; and he that cometh up out of the midst of the pit shall be taken in the snare: for the windows from on high are open, and the foundations of the earth do shake.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/isaiah/24#v19)</sup> The earth is utterly broken down, the earth is clean dissolved, the earth is moved exceedingly.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/24#v20)</sup> The earth shall reel to and fro like a drunkard, and shall be removed like a cottage; and the transgression thereof shall be heavy upon it; and it shall fall, and not rise again.

- Fear, the pit, and the snare are upon the inhabitant of the earth, so that he who flees the noise of the fear falls into the pit, and he who comes up from the pit is taken in the snare, for the windows on high are open and the foundations of the earth shake. The earth is utterly broken down, clean dissolved, moved exceedingly, reeling to and fro like a drunkard, its transgression heavy upon it so that it falls and rises not again.

**[Isaiah 24:21-23](/bible/isaiah/24#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/isaiah/24#v21)</sup> And it shall come to pass in that day, that the Lord shall punish the host of the high ones that are on high, and the kings of the earth upon the earth.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/isaiah/24#v22)</sup> And they shall be gathered together, as prisoners are gathered in the pit, and shall be shut up in the prison, and after many days shall they be visited.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/isaiah/24#v23)</sup> Then the moon shall be confounded, and the sun ashamed, when the Lord of hosts shall reign in mount Zion, and in Jerusalem, and before his ancients gloriously.

- In that day the Most High shall punish the host of the high ones on high and the kings of the earth upon the earth, gathering them together as prisoners in the pit and shutting them up in the prison, to be visited after many days. Then the moon shall be confounded and the sun ashamed when the Most High of hosts reigns in mount Zion and in Jerusalem, before His ancients gloriously.

# Isaiah 25: He Will Swallow Up Death in Victory

Read the chapter → [Isaiah 25](/bible/isaiah/25)

**[Isaiah 25:1-3](/bible/isaiah/25#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/25#v1)</sup> O Lord, thou art my God; I will exalt thee, I will praise thy name; for thou hast done wonderful things; thy counsels of old are faithfulness and truth.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/25#v2)</sup> For thou hast made of a city an heap; of a defenced city a ruin: a palace of strangers to be no city; it shall never be built.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/25#v3)</sup> Therefore shall the strong people glorify thee, the city of the terrible nations shall fear thee.

- The prophet exalts and praises the Most High for wonderful things and counsels of old that are faithfulness and truth, for He has made of a city a heap and of a defended city a ruin, a palace of strangers to be no city, never to be built again. Therefore the strong people shall glorify Him and the city of the terrible nations shall fear Him.

**[Isaiah 25:4-5](/bible/isaiah/25#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/25#v4)</sup> For thou hast been a strength to the poor, a strength to the needy in his distress, a refuge from the storm, a shadow from the heat, when the blast of the terrible ones is as a storm against the wall.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/25#v5)</sup> Thou shalt bring down the noise of strangers, as the heat in a dry place; even the heat with the shadow of a cloud: the branch of the terrible ones shall be brought low.

- The Most High has been a strength to the poor and to the needy in his distress, a refuge from the storm and a shadow from the heat when the blast of the terrible ones beats like a storm against the wall. He brings down the noise of strangers as the heat in a dry place, and the branch of the terrible ones is brought low.

**[Isaiah 25:6-8](/bible/isaiah/25#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/25#v6)</sup> And in this mountain shall the Lord of hosts make unto all people a feast of fat things, a feast of wines on the lees, of fat things full of marrow, of wines on the lees well refined.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/25#v7)</sup> And he will destroy in this mountain the face of the covering cast over all people, and the vail that is spread over all nations.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/25#v8)</sup> He will swallow up death in victory; and the Lord God will wipe away tears from off all faces; and the rebuke of his people shall he take away from off all the earth: for the Lord hath spoken it.

- In this mountain the Most High of hosts makes for all people a feast of fat things and well refined wines, and destroys the covering cast over all people and the veil spread over all nations. He will swallow up death in victory, wipe away tears from off all faces, and take away the rebuke of His people from all the earth, for the Most High has spoken it.

**[Isaiah 25:9-12](/bible/isaiah/25#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/25#v9)</sup> And it shall be said in that day, Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us: this is the Lord; we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/25#v10)</sup> For in this mountain shall the hand of the Lord rest, and Moab shall be trodden down under him, even as straw is trodden down for the dunghill.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/25#v11)</sup> And he shall spread forth his hands in the midst of them, as he that swimmeth spreadeth forth his hands to swim: and he shall bring down their pride together with the spoils of their hands.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/25#v12)</sup> And the fortress of the high fort of thy walls shall he bring down, lay low, and bring to the ground, even to the dust.

- In that day it shall be said, "Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us," and the people will be glad and rejoice in His salvation, for the hand of the Most High rests in this mountain while Moab is trodden down as straw for the dunghill. The teacher closes by reminding that understanding the word requires keeping the commandments, that the Most High may not give the understanding as soon as it is asked but grants it to those who stay consistent in the readings, that every reading raises new questions because there are deeper levels in books like Isaiah, Ezekiel, and Revelation, and that questions should be taken to the leadership.
