Read the chapter → [Isaiah 37](/bible/isaiah/37)

Hezekiah rends his clothes, goes into the house of the Most High, and sends to Isaiah the prophet. Isaiah answers with the word that Sennacherib will hear a rumor, return to his own land, and fall by the sword there. Hezekiah prays, the Most High defends Jerusalem, and the angel of the Most High smites the Assyrian camp. This history shows that no nation can stand against Israel unless the Most High has ordained it, and He made clear that Jerusalem would not be touched.

**[Isaiah 37:1-4](/bible/isaiah/37#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/37#v1)</sup> And it came to pass, when king Hezekiah heard it, that he rent his clothes, and covered himself with sackcloth, and went into the house of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/37#v2)</sup> And he sent Eliakim, who was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and the elders of the priests covered with sackcloth, unto Isaiah the prophet the son of Amoz.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/isaiah/37#v3)</sup> And they said unto him, Thus saith Hezekiah, This day is a day of trouble, and of rebuke, and of blasphemy: for the children are come to the birth, and there is not strength to bring forth.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/37#v4)</sup> It may be the Lord thy God will hear the words of Rabshakeh, whom the king of Assyria his master hath sent to reproach the living God, and will reprove the words which the Lord thy God hath heard: wherefore lift up thy prayer for the remnant that is left.

- When Hezekiah hears the words, he rends his clothes, covers himself with sackcloth, and goes into the house of the Most High. He turns to the Most High rather than to Egypt or to fear.
- He sends Eliakim, Shebna, and the elders of the priests, covered with sackcloth, to Isaiah the prophet. He calls it a day of trouble, rebuke, and blasphemy.
- He asks that Isaiah pray for the remnant that is left, hoping the Most High will hear the words of Rabshakeh, who was sent to reproach the living God. Hezekiah seeks the prophet's intercession.

**[Isaiah 37:5-7](/bible/isaiah/37#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/37#v5)</sup> So the servants of king Hezekiah came to Isaiah.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/37#v6)</sup> And Isaiah said unto them, Thus shall ye say unto your master, Thus saith the Lord, Be not afraid of the words that thou hast heard, wherewith the servants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed me.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/37#v7)</sup> Behold, I will send a blast upon him, and he shall hear a rumour, and return to his own land; and I will cause him to fall by the sword in his own land.

- Isaiah answers the servants: tell your master, Be not afraid of the words with which the servants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed Me. The Most High answers the blasphemy directly.
- Behold, I will send a blast upon him, and he shall hear a rumor and return to his own land, and I will cause him to fall by the sword in his own land. This is the prophecy that Sennacherib would be killed at home.
- This is exactly what happened: Sennacherib was killed in his own land by his own sons, showing the word of the Most High through Isaiah came to pass. No nation can stand against Israel when the Most High has spoken against it.

**[Isaiah 37:8-13](/bible/isaiah/37#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/37#v8)</sup> So Rabshakeh returned, and found the king of Assyria warring against Libnah: for he had heard that he was departed from Lachish.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/37#v9)</sup> And he heard say concerning Tirhakah king of Ethiopia, He is come forth to make war with thee. And when he heard it, he sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying,
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/isaiah/37#v10)</sup> Thus shall ye speak to Hezekiah king of Judah, saying, Let not thy God, in whom thou trustest, deceive thee, saying, Jerusalem shall not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/37#v11)</sup> Behold, thou hast heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all lands by destroying them utterly; and shalt thou be delivered?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/37#v12)</sup> Have the gods of the nations delivered them which my fathers have destroyed, as Gozan, and Haran, and Rezeph, and the children of Eden which were in Telassar?
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/37#v13)</sup> Where is the king of Hamath, and the king of Arphad, and the king of the city of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivah?

- Rabshakeh returns and finds the king of Assyria warring against Libnah, for he had departed from Lachish. Word comes of Tirhakah king of Ethiopia coming out to make war.
- Sennacherib sends messengers again to Hezekiah, telling him not to let the God in whom he trusts deceive him that Jerusalem will not be given into his hand. He renews the same threat and mockery.
- He points to all the lands and kings that Assyria destroyed, Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and the rest, asking whether they were delivered. He counts Jerusalem as one more city to fall.

**[Isaiah 37:14-20](/bible/isaiah/37#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/isaiah/37#v14)</sup> And Hezekiah received the letter from the hand of the messengers, and read it: and Hezekiah went up unto the house of the Lord, and spread it before the Lord.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/isaiah/37#v15)</sup> And Hezekiah prayed unto the Lord, saying,
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/37#v16)</sup> O Lord of hosts, God of Israel, that dwellest between the cherubims, thou art the God, even thou alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth: thou hast made heaven and earth.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/37#v17)</sup> Incline thine ear, O Lord, and hear; open thine eyes, O Lord, and see: and hear all the words of Sennacherib, which hath sent to reproach the living God.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/37#v18)</sup> Of a truth, Lord, the kings of Assyria have laid waste all the nations, and their countries,
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/isaiah/37#v19)</sup> And have cast their gods into the fire: for they were no gods, but the work of men’s hands, wood and stone: therefore they have destroyed them.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/37#v20)</sup> Now therefore, O Lord our God, save us from his hand, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that thou art the Lord, even thou only.

- Hezekiah receives the letter, reads it, goes up to the house of the Most High, and spreads it before Him. He lays the whole threat out before the Most High in prayer.
- He calls on the Lord of hosts, God of Israel, who dwells between the cherubims, as the God alone of all the kingdoms of the earth, who made heaven and earth. He asks Him to incline His ear and hear the words of Sennacherib that reproach the living God.
- He acknowledges that Assyria has laid waste the nations and cast their gods into the fire, but those were no gods, only the work of men's hands, wood and stone. He asks the Most High to save them, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that He alone is the Lord.

**[Isaiah 37:21-29](/bible/isaiah/37#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/isaiah/37#v21)</sup> Then Isaiah the son of Amoz sent unto Hezekiah, saying, Thus saith the Lord God of Israel, Whereas thou hast prayed to me against Sennacherib king of Assyria:
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/isaiah/37#v22)</sup> This is the word which the Lord hath spoken concerning him; The virgin, the daughter of Zion, hath despised thee, and laughed thee to scorn; the daughter of Jerusalem hath shaken her head at thee.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/isaiah/37#v23)</sup> Whom hast thou reproached and blasphemed? and against whom hast thou exalted thy voice, and lifted up thine eyes on high? even against the Holy One of Israel.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/isaiah/37#v24)</sup> By thy servants hast thou reproached the Lord, and hast said, By the multitude of my chariots am I come up to the height of the mountains, to the sides of Lebanon; and I will cut down the tall cedars thereof, and the choice fir trees thereof: and I will enter into the height of his border, and the forest of his Carmel.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/isaiah/37#v25)</sup> I have digged, and drunk water; and with the sole of my feet have I dried up all the rivers of the besieged places.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/isaiah/37#v26)</sup> Hast thou not heard long ago, how I have done it; and of ancient times, that I have formed it? now have I brought it to pass, that thou shouldest be to lay waste defenced cities into ruinous heaps.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/isaiah/37#v27)</sup> Therefore their inhabitants were of small power, they were dismayed and confounded: they were as the grass of the field, and as the green herb, as the grass on the housetops, and as corn blasted before it be grown up.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/isaiah/37#v28)</sup> But I know thy abode, and thy going out, and thy coming in, and thy rage against me.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/isaiah/37#v29)</sup> Because thy rage against me, and thy tumult, is come up into mine ears, therefore will I put my hook in thy nose, and my bridle in thy lips, and I will turn thee back by the way by which thou camest.

- Isaiah sends to Hezekiah that the Most High has heard his prayer against Sennacherib. The virgin daughter of Zion has despised and laughed him to scorn.
- Sennacherib reproached and blasphemed the Holy One of Israel, boasting by his chariots that he climbed the mountains and cut down the cedars and dried up the rivers. His pride is set against the Most High Himself.
- The Most High declares that He formed and brought it to pass of ancient times, that Sennacherib should lay waste the defended cities, and that the inhabitants were weak because the Most High allowed it. Because of his rage against the Most High, He will put His hook in his nose and His bridle in his lips and turn him back the way he came.

**[Isaiah 37:30-32](/bible/isaiah/37#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/isaiah/37#v30)</sup> And this shall be a sign unto thee, Ye shall eat this year such as groweth of itself; and the second year that which springeth of the same: and in the third year sow ye, and reap, and plant vineyards, and eat the fruit thereof.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/isaiah/37#v31)</sup> And the remnant that is escaped of the house of Judah shall again take root downward, and bear fruit upward:
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/isaiah/37#v32)</sup> For out of Jerusalem shall go forth a remnant, and they that escape out of mount Zion: the zeal of the Lord of hosts shall do this.

- The sign to Hezekiah is that this year they eat what grows of itself, the second year what springs of the same, and the third year they sow, reap, plant vineyards, and eat the fruit. The Most High promises restoration and provision.
- The remnant that escapes of the house of Judah takes root downward and bears fruit upward, for out of Jerusalem goes forth a remnant. The zeal of the Lord of hosts performs this.

**[Isaiah 37:33-35](/bible/isaiah/37#v33)**

> <sup>[33](/bible/isaiah/37#v33)</sup> Therefore thus saith the Lord concerning the king of Assyria, He shall not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with shields, nor cast a bank against it.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/isaiah/37#v34)</sup> By the way that he came, by the same shall he return, and shall not come into this city, saith the Lord.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/isaiah/37#v35)</sup> For I will defend this city to save it for mine own sake, and for my servant David’s sake.

- Concerning the king of Assyria, the Most High says he shall not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with shields, nor cast a bank against it. Jerusalem is not going to be touched.
- By the way he came, by the same he shall return, and shall not come into the city. The Most High will defend the city to save it for His own sake and for His servant David's sake.

**[Isaiah 37:36-38](/bible/isaiah/37#v36)**

> <sup>[36](/bible/isaiah/37#v36)</sup> Then the angel of the Lord went forth, and smote in the camp of the Assyrians a hundred and fourscore and five thousand: and when they arose early in the morning, behold, they were all dead corpses.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/isaiah/37#v37)</sup> So Sennacherib king of Assyria departed, and went and returned, and dwelt at Nineveh.
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/isaiah/37#v38)</sup> And it came to pass, as he was worshipping in the house of Nisroch his god, that Adrammelech and Sharezer his sons smote him with the sword; and they escaped into the land of Armenia: and Esar–haddon his son reigned in his stead.

- The angel of the Most High goes forth and smites the camp of the Assyrians, a hundred and eighty-five thousand, and in the morning they are all dead corpses. One angel destroys the whole army, proving no nation stands against Israel when the Most High defends it.
- Sennacherib departs and returns and dwells at Nineveh, and as he worships in the house of Nisroch his god, his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer smite him with the sword and escape into Armenia. The prophecy of [Isaiah 37:7](/bible/isaiah/37#v7) is fulfilled: he falls by the sword in his own land, killed by his own children.
- Esarhaddon his son reigns in his stead. The Most High's word stands from beginning to end.
