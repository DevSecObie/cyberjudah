**[1 Samuel 15:1-3](/bible/1-samuel/15#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-samuel/15#v1)</sup> Samuel also said unto Saul, The Lord sent me to anoint thee to be king over his people, over Israel: now therefore hearken thou unto the voice of the words of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-samuel/15#v2)</sup> Thus saith the Lord of hosts, I remember that which Amalek did to Israel, how he laid wait for him in the way, when he came up from Egypt.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-samuel/15#v3)</sup> Now go and smite Amalek, and utterly destroy all that they have, and spare them not; but slay both man and woman, infant and suckling, ox and sheep, camel and ass.

- Samuel said unto Saul, The Lord sent me to anoint thee to be king over his people; now therefore hearken thou unto the voice of the words of the Lord. Thus saith the Lord of hosts, I remember that which Amalek did to Israel, how he laid wait for him in the way when he came up from Egypt. Generations after the wilderness, the Most High still remembers; the generation of Amalekites had returned, and it was time to fulfil what was written.
- Now go and smite Amalek, and utterly destroy all that they have, and spare them not; but slay both man and woman, infant and suckling, ox and sheep, camel and ass. This is a specific instruction: kill all of them, spare none. Note that the Most High commanded this against a generation that had not personally done the deed in the wilderness; that answers the argument, I never did anything to you, so why hold it against me? The Most High himself commanded recompense for what was done to the forefathers.
- Christianity says forgive and forget, but the Most High God said remember; this is a righteous grudge, and it shows [Leviticus 19:18](/bible/leviticus/19#v18) applies to the children of thy people.

    Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people; the grudge law is toward your own people, not toward the enemies of Israel whom the Most High has sworn against.

- The teaching applies [Exodus 17:16](/bible/exodus/17#v16) to this day: the same war continues from generation to generation, and it is now more spiritual than ever, because the adversary has established the imagination that they are the Jews, and we go out and cast down that imagination every day. Edom cheered Babylon on when Jerusalem burned, and the scripture pronounces the payback.

The day of [Psalms 137](/bible/psalms/137) has not yet come; it belongs to the return of the Lord, so chill out and relax, none of that is for now.

**[1 Samuel 15:7-9](/bible/1-samuel/15#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/1-samuel/15#v7)</sup> And Saul smote the Amalekites from Havilah until thou comest to Shur, that is over against Egypt.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/1-samuel/15#v8)</sup> And he took Agag the king of the Amalekites alive, and utterly destroyed all the people with the edge of the sword.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-samuel/15#v9)</sup> But Saul and the people spared Agag, and the best of the sheep, and of the oxen, and of the fatlings, and the lambs, and all that was good, and would not utterly destroy them: but every thing that was vile and refuse, that they destroyed utterly.

- Saul smote the Amalekites from Havilah until thou comest to Shur, and utterly destroyed the people with the edge of the sword; but he took Agag the king of the Amalekites alive. He halfway fulfilled the order, reasoning like the king might be valuable as a hostage, as if the Lord forgot that detail.
- But Saul and the people spared Agag, and the best of the sheep, and of the oxen, and of the fatlings, and the lambs, and all that was good, and would not utterly destroy them; but every thing that was vile and refuse, that they destroyed utterly. They killed what looked like it deserved killing, so they could say they did some killing, and kept the healthy, good looking animals, telling themselves surely the Lord could not have meant these.

**[1 Samuel 15:10-11](/bible/1-samuel/15#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/1-samuel/15#v10)</sup> Then came the word of the Lord unto Samuel, saying,
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-samuel/15#v11)</sup> It repenteth me that I have set up Saul to be king: for he is turned back from following me, and hath not performed my commandments. And it grieved Samuel; and he cried unto the Lord all night.

- Then came the word of the Lord unto Samuel: It repenteth me that I have set up Saul to be king, for he is turned back from following me, and hath not performed my commandments. Mark the language: Saul did most of what was said, yet the Lord counts it as turning back and not performing his commandments at all.
- For everybody who says, well, I did do this part, understand: whenever there is a but in your obedience, you did not perform the orders given unto you. That is what the Most High is showing us in this story.
- And it grieved Samuel, and he cried unto the Lord all night. Pride had to be in operation for Saul to transgress on that level, and pride is where the throne is lost.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 10:12-14](/bible/sirach/10#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/10#v12)</sup> The beginning of pride is when one departeth from God, and his heart is turned away from his Maker.
    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/10#v13)</sup> For pride is the beginning of sin, and he that hath it shall pour out abomination: and therefore the Lord brought upon them strange calamities, and overthrew them utterly.
    >
    > <sup>[14](/bible/sirach/10#v14)</sup> The Lord hath cast down the thrones of proud princes, and set up the meek in their stead.

    That is exactly what happened: Saul's heart turned from his Maker, he poured out sin upon sin from there, and the Lord cast down the proud prince's throne and set up the meek in his stead; that is the whole story we are about to read.

**[1 Samuel 15:12-15](/bible/1-samuel/15#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/1-samuel/15#v12)</sup> And when Samuel rose early to meet Saul in the morning, it was told Samuel, saying, Saul came to Carmel, and, behold, he set him up a place, and is gone about, and passed on, and gone down to Gilgal.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/1-samuel/15#v13)</sup> And Samuel came to Saul: and Saul said unto him, Blessed be thou of the Lord: I have performed the commandment of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-samuel/15#v14)</sup> And Samuel said, What meaneth then this bleating of the sheep in mine ears, and the lowing of the oxen which I hear?
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-samuel/15#v15)</sup> And Saul said, They have brought them from the Amalekites: for the people spared the best of the sheep and of the oxen, to sacrifice unto the Lord thy God; and the rest we have utterly destroyed.

- Samuel rose early to meet Saul, and it was told him that Saul came to Carmel and set him up a place, a monument to himself, and is gone down to Gilgal. When your transgression becomes the lesson, be thankful when the correction comes quietly and nobody knows your name; do not take the spirit of bitterness, because you deserved a lot more.
- Saul greets Samuel first with a cover: Blessed be thou of the Lord: I have performed the commandment of the Lord. The first thing out of his mouth is a fabrication, knowing full well he transgressed.
- Samuel answers, What meaneth then this bleating of the sheep in mine ears, and the lowing of the oxen which I hear? If you performed the commandment, why do I hear animals?
- Saul deflects again, the same blame game as chapter 13: They have brought them from the Amalekites, for the people spared the best of the sheep and of the oxen, to sacrifice unto the Lord thy God; and the rest we have utterly destroyed. Verse 9 already told us Saul and the people spared them; now suddenly it is only the people, and for a good reason, a sacrifice, as though the Lord ought to be pleased.

  Precepts:
  - **[Deuteronomy 12:32](/bible/deuteronomy/12#v32)**
    > <sup>[32](/bible/deuteronomy/12#v32)</sup> What thing soever I command you, observe to do it: thou shalt not add thereto, nor diminish from it.

    What thing soever I command you, observe to do it: thou shalt not add thereto, nor diminish from it; the Most High made a law for the bright ideas that come along the way of following instructions, and it is plain.

When thou wast little in thine own sight, wast thou not made the head of the tribes of Israel, and the Lord anointed thee king over Israel? Samuel brings Saul back to his remembrance: when you thought nothing of yourself, with no confidence to be a ruler, the Most High still chose you.
- And the Lord sent thee on a journey, and said, Go and utterly destroy the sinners the Amalekites, and fight against them until they be consumed; the errand was the vengeance written in [Deuteronomy 25](/bible/deuteronomy/25). Wherefore then didst thou not obey the voice of the Lord, but didst fly upon the spoil, and didst evil in the sight of the Lord? Keeping the orders partially is evil in the sight of the Lord.

    Saul had answered, Am not I a Benjamite, of the smallest of the tribes of Israel, and my family the least of all the families of the tribe of Benjamin? wherefore then speakest thou so to me? That was the little-in-his-own-sight attitude Saul started with.

**[1 Samuel 15:20-21](/bible/1-samuel/15#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/1-samuel/15#v20)</sup> And Saul said unto Samuel, Yea, I have obeyed the voice of the Lord, and have gone the way which the Lord sent me, and have brought Agag the king of Amalek, and have utterly destroyed the Amalekites.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/1-samuel/15#v21)</sup> But the people took of the spoil, sheep and oxen, the chief of the things which should have been utterly destroyed, to sacrifice unto the Lord thy God in Gilgal.

- Saul is in denial: Yea, I have obeyed the voice of the lord, and have gone the way which the Lord sent me, and have brought Agag the king of Amalek, and have utterly destroyed the Amalekites. Samuel breaks down the rebuke and Saul tells the prophet he is wrong.
- In justifying himself over the animals, Saul volunteers even more of his rebellion, boasting that he spared the king, as if that was the smart move everyone really wanted; then he lays the spoil on the people again: the people took of the spoil, sheep and oxen, the chief of the things which should have been utterly destroyed, to sacrifice unto the Lord thy God in Gilgal.

  Precepts:
  - **[Job 9:20](/bible/job/9#v20)**
    > <sup>[20](/bible/job/9#v20)</sup> If I justify myself, mine own mouth shall condemn me: if I say, I am perfect, it shall also prove me perverse.

    If I justify myself, mine own mouth shall condemn me; when you plead your own righteousness against correction, you end up revealing more of your rebellion out of your own mouth, which is exactly what Saul did.

**[1 Samuel 15:22](/bible/1-samuel/15#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/1-samuel/15#v22)</sup> And Samuel said, Hath the Lord as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the Lord? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.

- Samuel makes it plain: Hath the Lord as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the Lord? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams. Do you think the Lord prefers a sacrifice to atone for broken commandments over you simply keeping the commandments? That is a heavy verse.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 34:18-19](/bible/sirach/34#v18)**
    > <sup>[18](/bible/sirach/34#v18)</sup> He that sacrificeth of a thing wrongfully gotten, his offering is ridiculous; and the gifts of unjust men are not accepted.
    >
    > <sup>[19](/bible/sirach/34#v19)</sup> The most High is not pleased with the offerings of the wicked; neither is he pacified for sin by the multitude of sacrifices.

    The spoil was wrongfully gotten, taken against the will of the Lord, so the offering is ridiculous; all those animals were not going to pacify his anger.
  - **[Proverbs 21:27](/bible/proverbs/21#v27)**
    > <sup>[27](/bible/proverbs/21#v27)</sup> The sacrifice of the wicked is abomination: how much more, when he bringeth it with a wicked mind?

    The sacrifice of the wicked is abomination: how much more, when he bringeth it with a wicked mind? It does not merely do nothing; it makes the Most High more angry, and Saul probably would have kept those animals to eat had it never been pointed out.
  - **[Psalms 51:16-17](/bible/psalms/51#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/psalms/51#v16)</sup> For thou desirest not sacrifice; else would I give it: thou delightest not in burnt offering.
    >
    > <sup>[17](/bible/psalms/51#v17)</sup> The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.

    Thou desirest not sacrifice, else would I give it: the sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise. David wrote this in his repentance: the true sacrifice is being sorry and remorseful for what you did, not pleading why you were not wrong for doing wrong; that is the spirit Saul does not have.

**[1 Samuel 15:23](/bible/1-samuel/15#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/1-samuel/15#v23)</sup> For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and stubbornness is as iniquity and idolatry. Because thou hast rejected the word of the Lord, he hath also rejected thee from being king.

- For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and stubbornness is as iniquity and idolatry. That is heavy. A brother who would never touch voodoo will sit through a whole counsel of do not do this, and call two weeks later saying he did it anyway; according to this verse, that rebellion is the same as witchcraft.
- Stubbornness is idolatry because the instruction does not come from the man; it is the commandment of the Lord that leadership carries. When you are stubborn against it, you are saying what I want to do comes before what God wants me to do, and you have put yourself in the midst of idolatry as surely as circling the stone at Mecca or bowing before a cross.
- Because thou hast rejected the word of the Lord, he hath also rejected thee from being king: you will lose whatever position the Most High put you in.
- This stubborn heart is what fares evil at the last; we will read Saul's end in the last chapter of 1 Samuel, and in chapter 16 the evil spirit enters into him and the Spirit of the Lord departs. The plant of wickedness took root here.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 3:26-27](/bible/sirach/3#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/sirach/3#v26)</sup> A stubborn heart shall fare evil at the last; and he that loveth danger shall perish therein.
    >
    > <sup>[27](/bible/sirach/3#v27)</sup> An obstinate heart shall be laden with sorrows; and the wicked man shall heap sin upon sin.

    Obstinate is another word for stubborn: the mind set on doing what it wants contrary to counsel will be laden with sorrows, and Saul heaped sin upon sin, lying, justifying himself, and blaming the people, which is what carried him into the witchcraft and idolatry of rebellion.

**[1 Samuel 15:24-25](/bible/1-samuel/15#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/1-samuel/15#v24)</sup> And Saul said unto Samuel, I have sinned: for I have transgressed the commandment of the Lord, and thy words: because I feared the people, and obeyed their voice.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/1-samuel/15#v25)</sup> Now therefore, I pray thee, pardon my sin, and turn again with me, that I may worship the Lord.

- Now the confession finally comes, and it is still not sincere: I have sinned, for I have transgressed the commandment of the Lord and thy words; because I feared the people, and obeyed their voice. Even in admitting it he deflects: it is the fault of the wicked people you put me over.
- Then he asks, Pardon my sin, and turn again with me, that I may worship the Lord; take me back to the regular Saul and Samuel connection, as though a fearful excuse settles it.

  Precepts:
  - **[Proverbs 29:25](/bible/proverbs/29#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/proverbs/29#v25)</sup> The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the Lord shall be safe.

    The fear of man bringeth a snare, but whoso putteth his trust in the Lord shall be safe; if you fear what the people will say, you will fall into the trap and transgress, which is why a leader cannot fold when the people become too much to handle; Saul would have been safe had he trusted the Lord.

**[1 Samuel 15:26](/bible/1-samuel/15#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/1-samuel/15#v26)</sup> And Samuel said unto Saul, I will not return with thee: for thou hast rejected the word of the Lord, and the Lord hath rejected thee from being king over Israel.

- Samuel refuses: I will not return with thee; for thou hast rejected the word of the Lord, and the Lord hath rejected thee from being king over Israel. Think it through: Samuel gave Saul the opening at the bleating of the sheep, and Saul would not humble down; he asked why Saul disobeyed the voice of the Lord, and Saul justified himself again. Two admonitions were given and refused before this rejection.

  Precepts:
  - **[Titus 3:10](/bible/titus/3#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/titus/3#v10)</sup> A man that is an heretick after the first and second admonition reject;

    A man that is an heretick after the first and second admonition reject; Saul had two chances to repent and confess and still justified himself, so Samuel did exactly what the scripture says and rejected him.
  - **[Titus 1:16](/bible/titus/1#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/titus/1#v16)</sup> They profess that they know God; but in works they deny him, being abominable, and disobedient, and unto every good work reprobate.

    They profess that they know God, but in works they deny him, being abominable, and disobedient, and unto every good work reprobate; Saul professed, restore me that I may worship the Lord, while every work denied him; this is the starting point of Saul losing the Spirit and going reprobate.

**[1 Samuel 15:27-29](/bible/1-samuel/15#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/1-samuel/15#v27)</sup> And as Samuel turned about to go away, he laid hold upon the skirt of his mantle, and it rent.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/1-samuel/15#v28)</sup> And Samuel said unto him, The Lord hath rent the kingdom of Israel from thee this day, and hath given it to a neighbour of thine, that is better than thou.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/1-samuel/15#v29)</sup> And also the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent: for he is not a man, that he should repent.

- As Samuel turned about to go away, Saul laid hold upon the skirt of his mantle, and it rent. And Samuel said, The Lord hath rent the kingdom of Israel from thee this day, and hath given it to a neighbour of thine, that is better than thou. Just as Saul tore the hem of the garment, so the Lord tore the throne from him; and Samuel says this still before he has ever met David, the same word he gave in chapter 13.
- And also the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent: for he is not a man, that he should repent. The Strength of Israel is the Most High. Understand the spirit of the title: whenever you go off on your own mind, pride has you departing from your strength, and once you have left your strength you are open to Satan; if a brother tells you that you are battling with pride, take heed and examine yourself.

  Precepts:

    God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent: hath he said, and shall he not do it?
  - **[Judith 8:16](/bible/judith/8#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/judith/8#v16)</sup> Do not bind the counsels of the Lord our God: for God is not as man, that he may be threatened; neither is he as the son of man, that he should be wavering.

    The Most High is not as man, that he may be threatened, neither does he waver; the sentence on Saul stood.

**[1 Samuel 15:30-31](/bible/1-samuel/15#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/1-samuel/15#v30)</sup> Then he said, I have sinned: yet honour me now, I pray thee, before the elders of my people, and before Israel, and turn again with me, that I may worship the Lord thy God.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/1-samuel/15#v31)</sup> So Samuel turned again after Saul; and Saul worshipped the Lord.

- Then he said, I have sinned: yet honour me now, I pray thee, before the elders of my people, and before Israel. This spirit is very real: okay, you are right, but do not let the people see the demotion; make sure they still know I am the king.
- That is the spirit of vanity, caught up in a rank and a title while forgetting that the title came for pleasing the Lord; once you discontinue pleasing the Lord, the title is going to leave as well. So Samuel turned again after Saul, and Saul worshipped the Lord.

**[1 Samuel 15:32-33](/bible/1-samuel/15#v32)**

> <sup>[32](/bible/1-samuel/15#v32)</sup> Then said Samuel, Bring ye hither to me Agag the king of the Amalekites. And Agag came unto him delicately. And Agag said, Surely the bitterness of death is past.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/1-samuel/15#v33)</sup> And Samuel said, As thy sword hath made women childless, so shall thy mother be childless among women. And Samuel hewed Agag in pieces before the Lord in Gilgal.

- Then said Samuel, Bring ye hither to me Agag the king of the Amalekites. And Agag came unto him delicately, and said, Surely the bitterness of death is past; with Saul under rebuke, Agag figured nobody was mad at the Amalekites anymore and he was safe.
- And Samuel said, As thy sword hath made women childless, so shall thy mother be childless among women. And Samuel hewed Agag in pieces before the Lord in Gilgal. The old prophet did not play; he finished the commandment himself, measure for measure for what Amalek's sword had done.

**[1 Samuel 15:34-35](/bible/1-samuel/15#v34)**

> <sup>[34](/bible/1-samuel/15#v34)</sup> Then Samuel went to Ramah; and Saul went up to his house to Gibeah of Saul.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/1-samuel/15#v35)</sup> And Samuel came no more to see Saul until the day of his death: nevertheless Samuel mourned for Saul: and the Lord repented that he had made Saul king over Israel.

- Then Samuel went to Ramah, and Saul went up to his house in Gibeah, and Samuel came no more to see Saul until the day of his death: nevertheless Samuel mourned for Saul, and the Lord repented that he had made Saul king over Israel.
- The mourning is what makes it sad: Samuel raised Saul up from nothing, saw his potential, and saw the Spirit come upon him in chapter 11 when he stood up as a savior for the people; he had hope in him, and it came to this. Samuel was grieved, and the Lord repented of the appointment.
