## Introduction

This session covers 1 Samuel chapters 28 through 31, the closing chapters of the book and the end of the history of Saul. The recording rejoins the class at [1 Samuel 28:15](/bible/1-samuel/28#v15), the opening of the chapter having been read before a connection break. Saul, cut off from the Most High for his disobedience, seeks out a woman with a familiar spirit and hears his own death prophesied; the Philistine lords send David home rather than trust an enemy in their ranks; David recovers all that the Amalekites carried away from Ziklag; and Saul and his sons fall on mount Gilboa exactly as Samuel spoke. With chapter 31 the history of Saul comes to an end, and the second book of Samuel takes up the rise and the history of King David.

**[1 Samuel 28:15](/bible/1-samuel/28#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/1-samuel/28#v15)</sup> And Samuel said to Saul, Why hast thou disquieted me, to bring me up? And Saul answered, I am sore distressed; for the Philistines make war against me, and God is departed from me, and answereth me no more, neither by prophets, nor by dreams: therefore I have called thee, that thou mayest make known unto me what I shall do.

- What Saul was doing when he went to the woman with the familiar spirit is called necromancy. Look that word up: it is when you go to somebody that conjures up spirits.
- Saul turned to this because the Lord was departed from him and answered him no more, neither by prophets nor by dreams, so he had Samuel conjured up to tell him what to do.

**[1 Samuel 28:16-18](/bible/1-samuel/28#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/1-samuel/28#v16)</sup> Then said Samuel, Wherefore then dost thou ask of me, seeing the Lord is departed from thee, and is become thine enemy?
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/1-samuel/28#v17)</sup> And the Lord hath done to him, as he spake by me: for the Lord hath rent the kingdom out of thine hand, and given it to thy neighbour, even to David:
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-samuel/28#v18)</sup> Because thou obeyedst not the voice of the Lord, nor executedst his fierce wrath upon Amalek, therefore hath the Lord done this thing unto thee this day.

- Samuel asks Saul why he inquires of him, seeing the Lord is departed from him and is become his enemy. The Lord did to Saul exactly as he spake by Samuel: he rent the kingdom out of Saul's hand and gave it to his neighbour, even to David.
- The reason is stated plainly: because Saul obeyed not the voice of the Lord, nor executed his fierce wrath upon Amalek. The Most High gave a law concerning Amalek, and Saul did not carry it out; that history was read earlier in this book.

  Precepts:
  - **[Deuteronomy 25:17-19](/bible/deuteronomy/25#v17)**
> <sup>[17](/bible/deuteronomy/25#v17)</sup> Remember what Amalek did unto thee by the way, when ye were come forth out of Egypt;
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/deuteronomy/25#v18)</sup> How he met thee by the way, and smote the hindmost of thee, even all that were feeble behind thee, when thou wast faint and weary; and he feared not God.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/deuteronomy/25#v19)</sup> Therefore it shall be, when the Lord thy God hath given thee rest from all thine enemies round about, in the land which the Lord thy God giveth thee for an inheritance to possess it, that thou shalt blot out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven; thou shalt not forget it.

    This is the law concerning Amalek that Samuel was referencing: Israel was commanded to blot out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven, and Saul did not do it.

**[1 Samuel 28:19](/bible/1-samuel/28#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/1-samuel/28#v19)</sup> Moreover the Lord will also deliver Israel with thee into the hand of the Philistines: and to morrow shalt thou and thy sons be with me: the Lord also shall deliver the host of Israel into the hand of the Philistines.

- Tomorrow shalt thou and thy sons be with me means tomorrow you and your sons are going to die; you are going to be with me. Samuel prophesied from his grave, and he also declared that the Lord would deliver the host of Israel into the hand of the Philistines.

**[1 Samuel 28:20-25](/bible/1-samuel/28#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/1-samuel/28#v20)</sup> Then Saul fell straightway all along on the earth, and was sore afraid, because of the words of Samuel: and there was no strength in him; for he had eaten no bread all the day, nor all the night.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/1-samuel/28#v21)</sup> And the woman came unto Saul, and saw that he was sore troubled, and said unto him, Behold, thine handmaid hath obeyed thy voice, and I have put my life in my hand, and have hearkened unto thy words which thou spakest unto me.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/1-samuel/28#v22)</sup> Now therefore, I pray thee, hearken thou also unto the voice of thine handmaid, and let me set a morsel of bread before thee; and eat, that thou mayest have strength, when thou goest on thy way.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/1-samuel/28#v23)</sup> But he refused, and said, I will not eat. But his servants, together with the woman, compelled him; and he hearkened unto their voice. So he arose from the earth, and sat upon the bed.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/1-samuel/28#v24)</sup> And the woman had a fat calf in the house; and she hasted, and killed it, and took flour, and kneaded it, and did bake unleavened bread thereof:
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/1-samuel/28#v25)</sup> And she brought it before Saul, and before his servants; and they did eat. Then they rose up, and went away that night.

- Saul fell straightway all along the earth, sore afraid because of the words of Samuel, and there was no strength in him, for he had eaten no bread all the day nor all the night.
- The woman reminded Saul that she had put her life in her hand and obeyed his voice, and asked him to let her set a morsel of bread before him that he might have strength for his way. He refused at first, but his servants together with the woman compelled him; she killed the fat calf, baked unleavened bread, and they did eat and went away that night.
- The chapter in sum: Saul inquired of the Lord and was not answered, so he turned to a woman with a familiar spirit and conjured up Samuel. Samuel told him that because of his disobedience the Lord was not dealing with him anymore, and prophesied that tomorrow Saul and his sons would be with him.

Nevertheless the lords favour thee not: return, and go in peace. David protested, What have I done, that I may not go fight against the enemies of my lord the king? But Achish answered that though David was good in his sight as an angel of God, the princes had said he shall not go up with us to the battle. So David and his men rose up early and returned into the land of the Philistines, and the Philistines went up to Jezreel.
- One of the things to take from this reading is that they have more sense than we do. The lords of the Philistines told Achish, No, he cannot go to battle with us, because we will always be looking over our shoulders wondering if he is going to change his mind and go back unto his people.
- This is the wisdom we have to apply today. There is always an outsider who dresses like us, talks like us, and gets himself accepted among our people; yet even the Philistines knew: he might be good to you, he might be serving you, but do not trust your enemy.

    The Philistine lords understood this even without the book; it is a lesson for us to apply today.

- Saul said unto his armourbearer, Draw thy sword, and thrust me through therewith; lest these uncircumcised come and thrust me through, and abuse me. What Saul was fearful of is what you see in war, how men will abuse their captives, killing them and abusing their bodies. He was already wounded and could not get away, so instead of being taken captive he told his armourbearer to kill him.
- His armourbearer would not, for he was sore afraid; therefore Saul took a sword and fell upon it, and when his armourbearer saw that Saul was dead, he fell likewise upon his sword and died with him. So Saul died, and his three sons, and his armourbearer, and all his men, that same day together.
- Remember what we read in chapter 28: when Saul went and conjured up the spirit of Samuel, Samuel said to him, To morrow shalt thou and thy sons be with me. He prophesied unto Saul what was going to happen, and now we read the fulfillment of that prophecy.
