**[1 Samuel 19:1-3](/bible/1-samuel/19#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-samuel/19#v1)</sup> And Saul spake to Jonathan his son, and to all his servants, that they should kill David.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-samuel/19#v2)</sup> But Jonathan Saul’s son delighted much in David: and Jonathan told David, saying, Saul my father seeketh to kill thee: now therefore, I pray thee, take heed to thyself until the morning, and abide in a secret place, and hide thyself:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-samuel/19#v3)</sup> And I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where thou art, and I will commune with my father of thee; and what I see, that I will tell thee.

- Saul spake to Jonathan his son and to all his servants that they should kill David. The Philistines could not get him, so now he wants Israelites to kill an Israelite; that is how far down the slope this man has gone.
- But Jonathan delighted much in David and told him: my father seeketh to kill thee; take heed to thyself until the morning, abide in a secret place, and hide thyself. I will commune with my father of thee, and what I see, that I will tell thee.
- Jonathan would have been next in line for the throne; you would think his loyalty lay with his daddy, but he saw the spirit was not on his father, it was on his brother David. His loyalty exceeded his own desires. That is the kind of friend to look for, and the lesson to learn from Jonathan.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 6:14](/bible/sirach/6#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/sirach/6#v14)</sup> A faithfull friend is a strong defence: and he that hath found such an one hath found a treasure.

    A faithful friend is a strong defence, and he that hath found such an one hath found a treasure; that is what Jonathan was to David.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 22:25](/bible/sirach/22#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/sirach/22#v25)</sup> I will not be ashamed to defend a friend; neither will I hide myself from him.

    I will not be ashamed to defend a friend, neither will I hide myself from him; even with his father the king gone crazy, Jonathan would not abandon David.

**[1 Samuel 19:4-7](/bible/1-samuel/19#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/1-samuel/19#v4)</sup> And Jonathan spake good of David unto Saul his father, and said unto him, Let not the king sin against his servant, against David; because he hath not sinned against thee, and because his works have been to thee-ward very good:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-samuel/19#v5)</sup> For he did put his life in his hand, and slew the Philistine, and the Lord wrought a great salvation for all Israel: thou sawest it, and didst rejoice: wherefore then wilt thou sin against innocent blood, to slay David without a cause?
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-samuel/19#v6)</sup> And Saul hearkened unto the voice of Jonathan: and Saul sware, As the Lord liveth, he shall not be slain.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-samuel/19#v7)</sup> And Jonathan called David, and Jonathan shewed him all those things. And Jonathan brought David to Saul, and he was in his presence, as in times past.

- Jonathan spake good of David to Saul: let not the king sin against his servant, because he hath not sinned against thee, and his works have been to thee-ward very good; he put his life in his hand and slew the Philistine, and thou sawest it and didst rejoice. Why wilt thou sin against innocent blood, to slay David without a cause?
- Without a cause: in chapter 18 it was just a feeling, wrath at David for nothing; as Christ explained in the spirit, that anger without a cause leads to actually wanting to murder, which is why it carries the same judgment.
- Saul hearkened unto Jonathan and sware, As the Lord liveth, he shall not be slain; and Jonathan brought David back to Saul, and he was in his presence as in times past.

  Precepts:
  - **[Exodus 23:7](/bible/exodus/23#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/exodus/23#v7)</sup> Keep thee far from a false matter; and the innocent and righteous slay thou not: for I will not justify the wicked.

    The innocent and righteous slay thou not; the law in the book that Saul was set on breaking.
  - **[Proverbs 6:16-17](/bible/proverbs/6#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/proverbs/6#v16)</sup> These six things doth the Lord hate: yea, seven are an abomination unto him:
    >
    > <sup>[17](/bible/proverbs/6#v17)</sup> A proud look, a lying tongue, and hands that shed innocent blood,

    Among the six things the Lord hates, yea seven that are an abomination unto him, are hands that shed innocent blood; that is the boat Saul put himself in.

**[1 Samuel 19:8-10](/bible/1-samuel/19#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-samuel/19#v8)</sup> And there was war again: and David went out, and fought with the Philistines, and slew them with a great slaughter; and they fled from him.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-samuel/19#v9)</sup> And the evil spirit from the Lord was upon Saul, as he sat in his house with his javelin in his hand: and David played with his hand.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-samuel/19#v10)</sup> And Saul sought to smite David even to the wall with the javelin; but he slipped away out of Saul’s presence, and he smote the javelin into the wall: and David fled, and escaped that night.

- There was war again, and David went out and fought with the Philistines and slew them with a great slaughter. Everything was back to normal until David's mighty spirit was displayed on the battlefield again.
- The evil spirit from the Lord was upon Saul as he sat in his house with his javelin in his hand, and he sought to smite David even to the wall while David played with his hand. The evil spirit had leveled up: in times past the harp made it depart, now Saul throws the javelin at the man playing the music.
- David slipped away out of Saul's presence, and the javelin smote into the wall, and David fled and escaped that night, just as in chapter 18.

**[1 Samuel 19:11-17](/bible/1-samuel/19#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/1-samuel/19#v11)</sup> Saul also sent messengers unto David’s house, to watch him, and to slay him in the morning: and Michal David’s wife told him, saying, If thou save not thy life to night, to morrow thou shalt be slain.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-samuel/19#v12)</sup> So Michal let David down through a window: and he went, and fled, and escaped.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/1-samuel/19#v13)</sup> And Michal took an image, and laid it in the bed, and put a pillow of goats’ hair for his bolster, and covered it with a cloth.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-samuel/19#v14)</sup> And when Saul sent messengers to take David, she said, He is sick.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-samuel/19#v15)</sup> And Saul sent the messengers again to see David, saying, Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may slay him.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/1-samuel/19#v16)</sup> And when the messengers were come in, behold, there was an image in the bed, with a pillow of goats’ hair for his bolster.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/1-samuel/19#v17)</sup> And Saul said unto Michal, Why hast thou deceived me so, and sent away mine enemy, that he is escaped? And Michal answered Saul, He said unto me, Let me go; why should I kill thee?

- Saul sent messengers unto David's house to watch him and to slay him in the morning. Having tried to turn his son against David, he now leans on his daughter, David's own wife; but Michal warned David, If thou save not thy life to night, to morrow thou shalt be slain.
- Michal let David down through a window, and he fled and escaped; she laid an image in the bed with a pillow of goats' hair for his bolster and said, He is sick. Saul said, Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may slay him, and found the image; Michal covered herself, saying David threatened her, but she was looking out for her husband.
- At this time Michal was a righteous example, a ride or die; she was a tower against death to her husband. She turns sour some chapters down the line, but here she saved his life.
- Note Saul's words: why hast thou sent away mine enemy? Saul and David were brothers, both Israel, Benjamin and Judah; Saul made his brother his enemy, and David loved him anyway. That is the understanding of Christ's words on loving your enemy: David exemplified it with Saul.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 26:22](/bible/sirach/26#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/sirach/26#v22)</sup> An harlot shall be accounted as spittle; but a married woman is a tower against death to her husband.

    A married woman is a tower against death to her husband; that is what Michal was to David that night.
  - **[Matthew 5:44](/bible/matthew/5#v44)**
    > <sup>[44](/bible/matthew/5#v44)</sup> But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you;

    Love your enemies; Saul was David's enemy, but David loved him, and that is the understanding Christ was going into.

**[1 Samuel 19:18-24](/bible/1-samuel/19#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/1-samuel/19#v18)</sup> So David fled, and escaped, and came to Samuel to Ramah, and told him all that Saul had done to him. And he and Samuel went and dwelt in Naioth.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/1-samuel/19#v19)</sup> And it was told Saul, saying, Behold, David is at Naioth in Ramah.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/1-samuel/19#v20)</sup> And Saul sent messengers to take David: and when they saw the company of the prophets prophesying, and Samuel standing as appointed over them, the Spirit of God was upon the messengers of Saul, and they also prophesied.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/1-samuel/19#v21)</sup> And when it was told Saul, he sent other messengers, and they prophesied likewise. And Saul sent messengers again the third time, and they prophesied also.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/1-samuel/19#v22)</sup> Then went he also to Ramah, and came to a great well that is in Sechu: and he asked and said, Where are Samuel and David? And one said, Behold, they be at Naioth in Ramah.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/1-samuel/19#v23)</sup> And he went thither to Naioth in Ramah: and the Spirit of God was upon him also, and he went on, and prophesied, until he came to Naioth in Ramah.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/1-samuel/19#v24)</sup> And he stripped off his clothes also, and prophesied before Samuel in like manner, and lay down naked all that day and all that night. Wherefore they say, Is Saul also among the prophets?

- David fled and escaped and came to Samuel to Ramah and told him all that Saul had done, and he and Samuel went and dwelt in Naioth. David was smart: he ran to the prophet, into the tower of the Lord.
- Saul sent messengers to take David, and when they saw the company of the prophets prophesying and Samuel standing as appointed over them, the spirit of God was upon the messengers of Saul, and they also prophesied; a second and a third set of messengers were sent, and the same thing happened each time they entered that atmosphere.
- Then Saul went himself, fully set in his mind to take David's life himself, and the spirit of God came upon him also; he prophesied before Samuel, stripped off his clothes, and lay down naked all that day and all that night, wherefore they say, Is Saul also among the prophets?
- That is the close of the session: when you are fighting for the Most High, nothing can be done unto you; if the eyes of the wicked are against you and you are ordained and predestined, his enemies came to take David and the spirit of God shut the evil down every time.
