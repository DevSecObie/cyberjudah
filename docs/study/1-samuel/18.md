**[1 Samuel 18:1-4](/bible/1-samuel/18#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-samuel/18#v1)</sup> And it came to pass, when he had made an end of speaking unto Saul, that the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-samuel/18#v2)</sup> And Saul took him that day, and would let him go no more home to his father’s house.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-samuel/18#v3)</sup> Then Jonathan and David made a covenant, because he loved him as his own soul.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-samuel/18#v4)</sup> And Jonathan stripped himself of the robe that was upon him, and gave it to David, and his garments, even to his sword, and to his bow, and to his girdle.

- The soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul, and they made a covenant; Jonathan stripped himself of his robe and gave it to David, with his garments, his sword, his bow, and his girdle.
- Address the perversion head on, as we did last class: souls being knit is not talking about anything unnatural between the two men. The same word describes the church: hearts knit together in love, as joints and bands in one body. Jonathan and David were on one accord; they had the same spirit.

  Precepts:
  - **[Colossians 2:2](/bible/colossians/2#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/colossians/2#v2)</sup> That their hearts might be comforted, being knit together in love, and unto all riches of the full assurance of understanding, to the acknowledgement of the mystery of God, and of the Father, and of Christ;

    That their hearts might be comforted, being knit together in love; the will of Christ for the church at Colosse, the same knitting of souls.
  - **[Colossians 2:19](/bible/colossians/2#v19)**
    > <sup>[19](/bible/colossians/2#v19)</sup> And not holding the Head, from which all the body by joints and bands having nourishment ministered, and knit together, increaseth with the increase of God.

    The body, by joints and bands knit together, increaseth with the increase of God; as muscles and joints are knit into one body, so were Jonathan and David of one spirit.

**[1 Samuel 18:5-7](/bible/1-samuel/18#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/1-samuel/18#v5)</sup> And David went out whithersoever Saul sent him, and behaved himself wisely: and Saul set him over the men of war, and he was accepted in the sight of all the people, and also in the sight of Saul’s servants.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-samuel/18#v6)</sup> And it came to pass as they came, when David was returned from the slaughter of the Philistine, that the women came out of all cities of Israel, singing and dancing, to meet king Saul, with tabrets, with joy, and with instruments of musick.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-samuel/18#v7)</sup> And the women answered one another as they played, and said, Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands.

- David went whithersoever Saul sent him and behaved himself wisely, and Saul set him over the men of war, and he was accepted in the sight of all the people and of Saul's servants.
- When they returned from the slaughter of the Philistine, the women came out of all cities of Israel singing and dancing to meet king Saul, with tabrets, with joy, and with instruments of musick.
- The women answered one another as they played: Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands. The first line honored Saul, but the song made David exponentially the greater warrior, and brothers catch spirits behind what women say and do.

**[1 Samuel 18:8-9](/bible/1-samuel/18#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-samuel/18#v8)</sup> And Saul was very wroth, and the saying displeased him; and he said, They have ascribed unto David ten thousands, and to me they have ascribed but thousands: and what can he have more but the kingdom?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-samuel/18#v9)</sup> And Saul eyed David from that day and forward.

- Saul was very wroth and the saying displeased him: they have ascribed unto David ten thousands, and to me they have ascribed but thousands; and what can he have more but the kingdom? Wroth is the same word used in [Genesis 4](/bible/genesis/4) of how Cain fell toward Abel, and Abel had done nothing to him but what the Lord required; David likewise did nothing to Saul but what Saul himself asked of him.
- Instead of appreciating the song, give credit where it is due, for the labourer is worthy of his reward, Saul was displeased at his brother's praise; that is the same spirit of envy.
- And Saul eyed David from that day and forward. What was behind that eye? Envy. The envious man is not satisfied with his own portion; Saul had his thousands and still had the kingdom, but his eye was on David's portion, and iniquity dried up his soul from that point forward.

  Precepts:

    > <sup>[5](/bible/genesis/4#v5)</sup> But unto Cain and to his offering he had not respect. And Cain was very wroth, and his countenance fell.

    Cain was very wroth, and his countenance fell; the same word, the same spirit, kindled against a brother who had done nothing wrong.
  - **[Matthew 5:21-22](/bible/matthew/5#v21)**
    > <sup>[21](/bible/matthew/5#v21)</sup> Ye have heard that it was said by them of old time, Thou shalt not kill; and whosoever shall kill shall be in danger of the judgment:
    >
    > <sup>[22](/bible/matthew/5#v22)</sup> But I say unto you, That whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment: and whosoever shall say to his brother, Raca, shall be in danger of the council: but whosoever shall say, Thou fool, shall be in danger of hell fire.

    Whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment, the same judgment as a murderer; anger without a cause is counted as murder in the spirit.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 14:8-9](/bible/sirach/14#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/sirach/14#v8)</sup> The envious man hath a wicked eye; he turneth away his face, and despiseth men.
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/sirach/14#v9)</sup> A covetous man’s eye is not satisfied with his portion; and the iniquity of the wicked drieth up his soul.

    The envious man hath a wicked eye; that is the eye Saul eyed David with, and the iniquity of the wicked dried up his soul.

**[1 Samuel 18:10-12](/bible/1-samuel/18#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/1-samuel/18#v10)</sup> And it came to pass on the morrow, that the evil spirit from God came upon Saul, and he prophesied in the midst of the house: and David played with his hand, as at other times: and there was a javelin in Saul’s hand.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-samuel/18#v11)</sup> And Saul cast the javelin; for he said, I will smite David even to the wall with it. And David avoided out of his presence twice.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-samuel/18#v12)</sup> And Saul was afraid of David, because the Lord was with him, and was departed from Saul.

- On the morrow the evil spirit from God came upon Saul, and this time it came back with more power: there was a javelin in Saul's hand, and he cast it, saying, I will smite David even to the wall with it. David avoided out of his presence twice.
- Saul was afraid of David, because the Lord was with him and was departed from Saul. When Saul saw how quickly David escaped, that spirit on David made the hunter the scared one.

  Precepts:
  - **[James 2:19](/bible/james/2#v19)**
    > <sup>[19](/bible/james/2#v19)</sup> Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.

    The devils also believe, and tremble; when Saul saw the spirit on David, he who sought David's life became the one afraid.

**[1 Samuel 18:13-16](/bible/1-samuel/18#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/1-samuel/18#v13)</sup> Therefore Saul removed him from him, and made him his captain over a thousand; and he went out and came in before the people.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-samuel/18#v14)</sup> And David behaved himself wisely in all his ways; and the Lord was with him.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-samuel/18#v15)</sup> Wherefore when Saul saw that he behaved himself very wisely, he was afraid of him.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/1-samuel/18#v16)</sup> But all Israel and Judah loved David, because he went out and came in before them.

- Saul removed David from him and made him captain over a thousand; he basically demoted the brother, putting his own armourbearer away from his presence for no reason.
- David behaved himself wisely in all his ways, and the Lord was with him, and when Saul saw it he was the more afraid. David's continued righteousness heaped coals of fire on Saul; when your righteousness makes the wicked afraid, that is how you know you are in the spirit.
- But all Israel and Judah loved David, because he went out and came in before them; everybody knew he was the one really putting his life on the line and doing the work, even though he had no throne.

  Precepts:
  - **[Luke 6:22](/bible/luke/6#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/luke/6#v22)</sup> Blessed are ye, when men shall hate you, and when they shall separate you from their company, and shall reproach you, and cast out your name as evil, for the Son of man’s sake.

    Blessed are ye, when men shall hate you, and when they shall separate you from their company; Saul separated David from his company for no reason, a mark of blessing on David.
  - **[Romans 12:20](/bible/romans/12#v20)**
    > <sup>[20](/bible/romans/12#v20)</sup> Therefore if thine enemy hunger, feed him; if he thirst, give him drink: for in so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire on his head.

    In so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire on his head; David's faithful service under a hateful king did exactly that.
  - **[1 Peter 2:15](/bible/1-peter/2#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/1-peter/2#v15)</sup> For so is the will of God, that with well doing ye may put to silence the ignorance of foolish men:

    With well doing ye put to silence the ignorance of foolish men; David kept behaving wisely and left Saul nothing to charge him with.

**[1 Samuel 18:17-19](/bible/1-samuel/18#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/1-samuel/18#v17)</sup> And Saul said to David, Behold my elder daughter Merab, her will I give thee to wife: only be thou valiant for me, and fight the Lord’s battles. For Saul said, Let not mine hand be upon him, but let the hand of the Philistines be upon him.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-samuel/18#v18)</sup> And David said unto Saul, Who am I? and what is my life, or my father’s family in Israel, that I should be son in law to the king?
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/1-samuel/18#v19)</sup> But it came to pass at the time when Merab Saul’s daughter should have been given to David, that she was given unto Adriel the Meholathite to wife.

- Saul offered his elder daughter Merab to wife, only be thou valiant for me and fight the Lord's battles; for Saul said, Let not mine hand be upon him, but let the hand of the Philistines be upon him. Remember, the king's daughter was already the promise attached to killing Goliath; Saul aged him out of that and reattached conditions, thinking the Philistines would finish David.
- David answered humbly: who am I, and what is my life, or my father's family in Israel, that I should be son in law to the king?
- When the time came that Merab should have been given to David, she was given to Adriel the Meholathite; Saul tricked David out of her.

>
> <sup>[22](/bible/1-samuel/18#v22)</sup> And Saul commanded his servants, saying, Commune with David secretly, and say, Behold, the king hath delight in thee, and all his servants love thee: now therefore be the king’s son in law.

>
> <sup>[24](/bible/1-samuel/18#v24)</sup> And the servants of Saul told him, saying, On this manner spake David.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/1-samuel/18#v25)</sup> And Saul said, Thus shall ye say to David, The king desireth not any dowry, but an hundred foreskins of the Philistines, to be avenged of the king’s enemies. But Saul thought to make David fall by the hand of the Philistines.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/1-samuel/18#v26)</sup> And when his servants told David these words, it pleased David well to be the king’s son in law: and the days were not expired.

And Saul gave him Michal his daughter to wife.

- Michal, Saul's daughter, loved David, and the thing pleased Saul: I will give him her, that she may be a snare to him, and that the hand of the Philistines may be against him. He set his own daughter as a trap.
- David again answered humbly, that he was a poor man and lightly esteemed; so Saul waived the dowry money and named his price: an hundred foreskins of the Philistines, thinking to make David fall by the hand of the Philistines, for there was no way he should survive that errand.
- David arose with his men, slew two hundred Philistines, and brought their foreskins in full tale to the king; he doubled the order. That is how devoted a warrior he was for his king and his nation, and Saul gave him Michal his daughter to wife.

**[1 Samuel 18:28-30](/bible/1-samuel/18#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/1-samuel/18#v28)</sup> And Saul saw and knew that the Lord was with David, and that Michal Saul’s daughter loved him.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/1-samuel/18#v29)</sup> And Saul was yet the more afraid of David; and Saul became David’s enemy continually.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/1-samuel/18#v30)</sup> Then the princes of the Philistines went forth: and it came to pass, after they went forth, that David behaved himself more wisely than all the servants of Saul; so that his name was much set by.

- Saul saw and knew that the Lord was with David, and that Michal loved him, and Saul was yet the more afraid of David; and Saul became David's enemy continually. Put the verses together: he knew the Lord was with the man, and he chose to be that man's enemy anyway. If you know the Lord is with somebody and you make yourself their enemy, you have made yourself the adversary.
- David was righteous, obedient, and humble; Saul was envious, wicked, and rebellious, and he could not stand him. As sin is an abomination to the Most High, so godliness is an abomination to a sinner; the sinner cannot bear to look at righteousness.
- The princes of the Philistines went forth, and David behaved himself more wisely than all the servants of Saul, so that his name was much set by; harmless as a dove, wise as a serpent.

  - **[Ecclesiasticus 1:25](/bible/sirach/1#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/sirach/1#v25)</sup> The parables of knowledge are in the treasures of wisdom: but godliness is an abomination to a sinner.

    Godliness is an abomination to a sinner; that is why Saul became David's enemy continually.
  - **[1 John 3:12](/bible/1-john/3#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/1-john/3#v12)</sup> Not as Cain, who was of that wicked one, and slew his brother. And wherefore slew he him? Because his own works were evil, and his brother’s righteous.

    Cain, who was of that wicked one, slew his brother because his own works were evil and his brother's righteous; the same spirit that was on Saul, the root of bitterness that says, I have to take that man's life.
