**[1 Samuel 17:1-3](/bible/1-samuel/17#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-samuel/17#v1)</sup> Now the Philistines gathered together their armies to battle, and were gathered together at Shochoh, which belongeth to Judah, and pitched between Shochoh and Azekah, in Ephes–dammim.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-samuel/17#v2)</sup> And Saul and the men of Israel were gathered together, and pitched by the valley of Elah, and set the battle in array against the Philistines.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-samuel/17#v3)</sup> And the Philistines stood on a mountain on the one side, and Israel stood on a mountain on the other side: and there was a valley between them.

- As we covered before, the Philistines always wanted smoke with the Israelites; they would not leave us alone. They gathered to battle at Shochoh and pitched between Shochoh and Azekah.
- Saul and the men of Israel pitched by the valley of Elah and set the battle in array; the Philistines stood on a mountain on one side, Israel on a mountain on the other side, with a valley between them.

**[1 Samuel 17:4-7](/bible/1-samuel/17#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/1-samuel/17#v4)</sup> And there went out a champion out of the camp of the Philistines, named Goliath, of Gath, whose height was six cubits and a span.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-samuel/17#v5)</sup> And he had an helmet of brass upon his head, and he was armed with a coat of mail; and the weight of the coat was five thousand shekels of brass.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-samuel/17#v6)</sup> And he had greaves of brass upon his legs, and a target of brass between his shoulders.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-samuel/17#v7)</sup> And the staff of his spear was like a weaver’s beam; and his spear’s head weighed six hundred shekels of iron: and one bearing a shield went before him.

- Out of the camp of the Philistines came a champion named Goliath of Gath, whose height was six cubits and a span; the heavyweight champion of the Philistines.
- He wore a helmet of brass and a coat of mail weighing five thousand shekels of brass, greaves of brass on his legs, a target of brass between his shoulders, a spear like a weaver's beam with a head of six hundred shekels of iron; a big man in a mighty costume of armor.
- He did not even carry his own shield; one bearing a shield went before him.

**[1 Samuel 17:8-11](/bible/1-samuel/17#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-samuel/17#v8)</sup> And he stood and cried unto the armies of Israel, and said unto them, Why are ye come out to set your battle in array? am not I a Philistine, and ye servants to Saul? choose you a man for you, and let him come down to me.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-samuel/17#v9)</sup> If he be able to fight with me, and to kill me, then will we be your servants: but if I prevail against him, and kill him, then shall ye be our servants, and serve us.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-samuel/17#v10)</sup> And the Philistine said, I defy the armies of Israel this day; give me a man, that we may fight together.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-samuel/17#v11)</sup> When Saul and all Israel heard those words of the Philistine, they were dismayed, and greatly afraid.

- Goliath cried to the armies of Israel: choose you a man and let him come down to me. If he kills me, we will be your servants; if I prevail and kill him, ye shall be our servants. And he said, I defy the armies of Israel this day; give me a man that we may fight together.
- When Saul and all Israel heard the words of the Philistine, they were dismayed and greatly afraid. They were in a carnal spirit, measuring his height, his armor, and his reach; the spirit had departed, and fear is not the spirit of the Most High.
- The Most High put laws in the book about going to battle, and this fear showed that Saul and the people lacked faith; the same God that destroyed Pharaoh and the Egyptians who pursued Israel was with them, and a trial of faith was set before them in the form of one big intimidating man with a big mouth.

- David, the youngest, went and returned from Saul to feed his father's sheep at Bethlehem.
- The Philistine drew near morning and evening and presented himself forty days; forty days straight of talking his big talk to try to scare Israel.

**[1 Samuel 17:20-24](/bible/1-samuel/17#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/1-samuel/17#v20)</sup> And David rose up early in the morning, and left the sheep with a keeper, and took, and went, as Jesse had commanded him; and he came to the trench, as the host was going forth to the fight, and shouted for the battle.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/1-samuel/17#v21)</sup> For Israel and the Philistines had put the battle in array, army against army.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/1-samuel/17#v22)</sup> And David left his carriage in the hand of the keeper of the carriage, and ran into the army, and came and saluted his brethren.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/1-samuel/17#v23)</sup> And as he talked with them, behold, there came up the champion, the Philistine of Gath, Goliath by name, out of the armies of the Philistines, and spake according to the same words: and David heard them.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/1-samuel/17#v24)</sup> And all the men of Israel, when they saw the man, fled from him, and were sore afraid.

- David rose early, left the sheep with a keeper, and came to the trench as the host was shouting for the battle; while he saluted his brethren, the champion came up and spake according to the same words, the same daily smack talk, and David heard them.
- All the men of Israel, when they saw the man, fled from him and were sore afraid. Think on it: the whole nation's freedom, everything they possessed, was on the line, and they ran scared.
- This is why the Most High said in chapter 16 that he had refused David's brothers: they were there and did not have the spirit to rise up; they were among the ones who fled.

  Precepts:
  - **[Psalms 94:16](/bible/psalms/94#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/psalms/94#v16)</sup> Who will rise up for me against the evildoers? or who will stand up for me against the workers of iniquity?

    Who will rise up for me against the evildoers? That is the spirit the Most High looks for, one who will stand up for his people against the workers of iniquity; he is not worried about outward appearance.

**[1 Samuel 17:25-27](/bible/1-samuel/17#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/1-samuel/17#v25)</sup> And the men of Israel said, Have ye seen this man that is come up? surely to defy Israel is he come up: and it shall be, that the man who killeth him, the king will enrich him with great riches, and will give him his daughter, and make his father’s house free in Israel.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/1-samuel/17#v26)</sup> And David spake to the men that stood by him, saying, What shall be done to the man that killeth this Philistine, and taketh away the reproach from Israel? for who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/1-samuel/17#v27)</sup> And the people answered him after this manner, saying, So shall it be done to the man that killeth him.

- The men of Israel said that the man who killeth Goliath, the king will enrich with great riches, give him his daughter, and make his father's house free in Israel; a prize was on Goliath's head. Compare Caleb, who made a like offer in the book of Joshua.
- David asked, What shall be done to the man that killeth this Philistine and taketh away the reproach from Israel? For who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?
- The soldiers themselves did not understand that they were the armies of the living God; David, a sheep keeper sent on an errand, knew it. Israel is the army of the Most High, and every army requires order; that is why there are captains and officers, and despising that order is despising the army of the living God.

    That I may lay my hand upon Egypt, and bring forth mine armies, and my people the children of Israel; the Most High calls his people, the children of Israel, his armies.
  - **[Joshua 15:16](/bible/joshua/15#v16)**

    Caleb said, He that smiteth Kirjathsepher, and taketh it, to him will I give Achsah my daughter to wife; the same kind of offer the king made over Goliath.

I know thy pride, and the naughtiness of thine heart; for thou art come down that thou mightest see the battle.

I know thy pride and the naughtiness of thine heart; thou art come down that thou mightest see the battle. A big brother talking down to a little brother, accusing him of sneaking out to watch.
- David answered, What have I now done? Is there not a cause? The men are scared, nobody will step up; what is the problem? And he turned to another and spake after the same manner.

**[1 Samuel 17:31-33](/bible/1-samuel/17#v31)**

> <sup>[31](/bible/1-samuel/17#v31)</sup> And when the words were heard which David spake, they rehearsed them before Saul: and he sent for him.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/1-samuel/17#v32)</sup> And David said to Saul, Let no man’s heart fail because of him; thy servant will go and fight with this Philistine.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/1-samuel/17#v33)</sup> And Saul said to David, Thou art not able to go against this Philistine to fight with him: for thou art but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth.

- David's words were rehearsed before Saul, and Saul sent for him. David told Saul, Let no man's heart fail because of him; thy servant will go and fight with this Philistine. Nobody panic; I will handle it.
- Saul answered, Thou art not able to go against this Philistine to fight with him, for thou art but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth.
- Young people coming into this truth: if the Most High is dealing with you in a special way, do not let anybody discourage you or tell you that you are too young to be a mighty man of the Lord. Do not let it deter you.

  Precepts:

    Let no man despise thy youth, but be thou an example of the believers, in word, in conversation, in charity, in spirit, in faith, in purity; the nation had no example of faith that day, so a young youth had to be that example.

>
> <sup>[35](/bible/1-samuel/17#v35)</sup> And I went out after him, and smote him, and delivered it out of his mouth: and when he arose against me, I caught him by his beard, and smote him, and slew him.
>

> <sup>[37](/bible/1-samuel/17#v37)</sup> David said moreover, The Lord that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine. And Saul said unto David, Go, and the Lord be with thee.

- David gave Saul his resume: keeping his father's sheep, when a lion and a bear took a lamb out of the flock, he went out after him, smote him, and delivered it out of his mouth; when he arose against him, David caught him by his beard and slew him. He might have looked small, but he was killing lions and bears behind his sheep.
- David said, The Lord that delivered me out of the paw of the lion and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine. Same God, no difference. And Saul said, Go, and the Lord be with thee.

>
> <sup>[39](/bible/1-samuel/17#v39)</sup> And David girded his sword upon his armour, and he assayed to go; for he had not proved it. And David said unto Saul, I cannot go with these; for I have not proved them. And David put them off him.

- Instead of the helmet and the brass, David took his staff, chose him five smooth stones out of the brook, put them in a shepherd's bag, and took his sling in his hand. What he put on instead was the whole armour of God: the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the spirit.

And the Philistine cursed David by his gods and promised to give his flesh to the fowls of the air and the beasts of the field.
- David answered, Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield: but I come to thee in the name of the Lord of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom thou hast defied. Again the record shows who the army of the Most High is: Israel.
- David understood he did not need the sword, the spear, or the shield; he had the spirit of the living God. It is no amount of carnal firepower that overcomes the enemies of Israel; this is spiritual warfare, and our forefather David understood that.

**[1 Samuel 17:48-51](/bible/1-samuel/17#v48)**

> <sup>[48](/bible/1-samuel/17#v48)</sup> And it came to pass, when the Philistine arose, and came and drew nigh to meet David, that David hasted, and ran toward the army to meet the Philistine.
>
> <sup>[49](/bible/1-samuel/17#v49)</sup> And David put his hand in his bag, and took thence a stone, and slang it, and smote the Philistine in his forehead, that the stone sunk into his forehead; and he fell upon his face to the earth.
>
> <sup>[50](/bible/1-samuel/17#v50)</sup> So David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and with a stone, and smote the Philistine, and slew him; but there was no sword in the hand of David.
>
> <sup>[51](/bible/1-samuel/17#v51)</sup> Therefore David ran, and stood upon the Philistine, and took his sword, and drew it out of the sheath thereof, and slew him, and cut off his head therewith. And when the Philistines saw their champion was dead, they fled.

- David hasted and ran toward the army to meet the Philistine, took a stone from his bag, slang it, and smote the Philistine in his forehead, that the stone sunk into his forehead, and he fell upon his face to the earth. David had precise aim and power; that stone left the sling with the velocity of a bullet, and it took one shot.
- David prevailed with a sling and with a stone, and there was no sword in the hand of David; he ran and stood upon the Philistine, drew Goliath's own sword out of the sheath, slew him, and cut off his head with it.
- When the Philistines saw their champion was dead, they fled. They ran from a young youth because they knew something supernatural was going on; he was in the spirit of the Lord.

- When Saul saw David go forth against the Philistine, he asked Abner, the captain of the host, Whose son is this youth? And Abner could not tell; in the shock of it they forgot whose son the young man was.
- As David returned from the slaughter of the Philistine with the head of the Philistine in his hand, Abner brought him before Saul, and David answered, I am the son of thy servant Jesse the Bethlehemite.
- That is the history of David and Goliath: when the mighty men of Israel ran afraid, a young youth stood up bold in the spirit of the Lord and took the giant down.
