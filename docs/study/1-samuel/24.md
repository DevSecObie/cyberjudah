## Introduction

This session covers 1 Samuel chapters 24 through 27. In the prior chapters Saul was chasing after David, hunting him to kill him; here David twice has Saul delivered into his hand and twice refuses to touch the Lord's anointed. The teaching follows David sparing Saul in the cave at Engedi, the folly of Nabal and the wisdom of Abigail who stayed David from shedding blood, David sparing Saul a second time in the wilderness of Ziph, and David's escape into the land of the Philistines, where Achish gave him Ziklag. The main threads are David's integrity, restraint, and fear of the Most High toward his elder and king, and the lesson of a wife of good understanding who protects her house with her behavior instead of her mouth.

**[1 Samuel 24:1-3](/bible/1-samuel/24#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-samuel/24#v1)</sup> And it came to pass, when Saul was returned from following the Philistines, that it was told him, saying, Behold, David is in the wilderness of En–gedi.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-samuel/24#v2)</sup> Then Saul took three thousand chosen men out of all Israel, and went to seek David and his men upon the rocks of the wild goats.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-samuel/24#v3)</sup> And he came to the sheepcotes by the way, where was a cave; and Saul went in to cover his feet: and David and his men remained in the sides of the cave.

- Coming out of the prior chapters, Saul was hunting after David to kill him. When Saul returned from following the Philistines and was told David was in the wilderness of Engedi, he took three thousand chosen men out of all Israel to seek David and his men upon the rocks of the wild goats.
- Saul went into the cave to cover his feet: that means he was in there relieving himself. See the vulnerable position Saul was in, and yet David spared his life; that is why David could say, thou wast delivered into my hand, but I did not take thy life.

  Precepts:
  - **[Judges 3:24-25](/bible/judges/3#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/judges/3#v24)</sup> When he was gone out, his servants came; and when they saw that, behold, the doors of the parlour were locked, they said, Surely he covereth his feet in his summer chamber.
    >
    > <sup>[25](/bible/judges/3#v25)</sup> And they tarried till they were ashamed: and, behold, he opened not the doors of the parlour; therefore they took a key, and opened them: and, behold, their lord was fallen down dead on the earth.

    Eglon's servants saw the doors of the parlour locked and said, Surely he covereth his feet in his summer chamber, and tarried till they were ashamed; covering the feet is the scripture's phrase for relieving yourself.

**[1 Samuel 24:4-7](/bible/1-samuel/24#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/1-samuel/24#v4)</sup> And the men of David said unto him, Behold the day of which the Lord said unto thee, Behold, I will deliver thine enemy into thine hand, that thou mayest do to him as it shall seem good unto thee. Then David arose, and cut off the skirt of Saul’s robe privily.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-samuel/24#v5)</sup> And it came to pass afterward, that David’s heart smote him, because he had cut off Saul’s skirt.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-samuel/24#v6)</sup> And he said unto his men, The Lord forbid that I should do this thing unto my master, the Lord’s anointed, to stretch forth mine hand against him, seeing he is the anointed of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-samuel/24#v7)</sup> So David stayed his servants with these words, and suffered them not to rise against Saul. But Saul rose up out of the cave, and went on his way.

- Behold, I will deliver thine enemy into thine hand: who is being referred to as the enemy? His own people; Saul was of the tribe of Benjamin, and David was of the tribe of Judah.
- On the skirt: the Zondervan Compact Bible Dictionary (page 136) shows the basic garments in general use among men of biblical times were the inner tunic, the tunic coat, the girdle, and the cloak, with the headdress and sandals. The word skirt in the KJV usually refers to an article of male, not female, clothing, with meanings of extremity and corner. Where we have our fringes attached to the borders of our garments, that is the bottom part of Saul's robe that David cut off privily.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ruth 3:9](/bible/ruth/3#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/ruth/3#v9)</sup> And he said, Who art thou? And she answered, I am Ruth thine handmaid: spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman.

    The dictionary's own example of the skirt: spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; the skirt is the extremity of the garment.
  - **[Exodus 28:33](/bible/exodus/28#v33)**
    > <sup>[33](/bible/exodus/28#v33)</sup> And beneath upon the hem of it thou shalt make pomegranates of blue, and of purple, and of scarlet, round about the hem thereof; and bells of gold between them round about:

    The dictionary's other example: the hem of the priestly robe, the border of a male garment.

- Afterward David's heart smote him because he had cut off Saul's skirt, and he said, The Lord forbid that I should do this thing unto my master, the Lord's anointed. So David stayed his servants with these words and suffered them not to rise against Saul.

**[1 Samuel 24:8-15](/bible/1-samuel/24#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-samuel/24#v8)</sup> David also arose afterward, and went out of the cave, and cried after Saul, saying, My lord the king. And when Saul looked behind him, David stooped with his face to the earth, and bowed himself.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-samuel/24#v9)</sup> And David said to Saul, Wherefore hearest thou men’s words, saying, Behold, David seeketh thy hurt?
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-samuel/24#v10)</sup> Behold, this day thine eyes have seen how that the Lord had delivered thee to day into mine hand in the cave: and some bade me kill thee: but mine eye spared thee; and I said, I will not put forth mine hand against my lord; for he is the Lord’s anointed.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-samuel/24#v11)</sup> Moreover, my father, see, yea, see the skirt of thy robe in my hand: for in that I cut off the skirt of thy robe, and killed thee not, know thou and see that there is neither evil nor transgression in mine hand, and I have not sinned against thee; yet thou huntest my soul to take it.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-samuel/24#v12)</sup> The Lord judge between me and thee, and the Lord avenge me of thee: but mine hand shall not be upon thee.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/1-samuel/24#v13)</sup> As saith the proverb of the ancients, Wickedness proceedeth from the wicked: but mine hand shall not be upon thee.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-samuel/24#v14)</sup> After whom is the king of Israel come out? after whom dost thou pursue? after a dead dog, after a flea.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-samuel/24#v15)</sup> The Lord therefore be judge, and judge between me and thee, and see, and plead my cause, and deliver me out of thine hand.

- When Saul rose out of the cave, David went out after him, cried, My lord the king, and stooped with his face to the earth and bowed himself. He reverenced Saul even though Saul was trying to kill him; that is why the Most High said David is a man after mine own heart. David maintained his integrity.
- David laid the proof before Saul: this day thine eyes have seen how the Lord delivered thee into mine hand in the cave, and some bade me kill thee, but mine eye spared thee, for he is the Lord's anointed. See the skirt of thy robe in my hand: I cut off the skirt and killed thee not; there is neither evil nor transgression in mine hand, yet thou huntest my soul to take it.
- David left the judgment with the Most High: the Lord judge between me and thee, and the Lord avenge me of thee, but mine hand shall not be upon thee; as saith the proverb of the ancients, Wickedness proceedeth from the wicked. After whom is the king of Israel come out? after a dead dog, after a flea.

**[1 Samuel 24:16-20](/bible/1-samuel/24#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/1-samuel/24#v16)</sup> And it came to pass, when David had made an end of speaking these words unto Saul, that Saul said, Is this thy voice, my son David? And Saul lifted up his voice, and wept.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/1-samuel/24#v17)</sup> And he said to David, Thou art more righteous than I: for thou hast rewarded me good, whereas I have rewarded thee evil.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-samuel/24#v18)</sup> And thou hast shewed this day how that thou hast dealt well with me: forasmuch as when the Lord had delivered me into thine hand, thou killedst me not.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/1-samuel/24#v19)</sup> For if a man find his enemy, will he let him go well away? wherefore the Lord reward thee good for that thou hast done unto me this day.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/1-samuel/24#v20)</sup> And now, behold, I know well that thou shalt surely be king, and that the kingdom of Israel shall be established in thine hand.

- Saul lifted up his voice and wept, and said to David, Thou art more righteous than I, for thou hast rewarded me good, whereas I have rewarded thee evil; if a man find his enemy, will he let him go well away?
- Saul made an acknowledgement there because he saw the righteousness of David: I know well that thou shalt surely be king, and that the kingdom of Israel shall be established in thine hand.

  Precepts:
  - **[Isaiah 32:1](/bible/isaiah/32#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/32#v1)</sup> Behold, a king shall reign in righteousness, and princes shall rule in judgment.

    Behold, a king shall reign in righteousness; that is what Saul was acknowledging, that David was operating in righteousness.

**[1 Samuel 24:21-22](/bible/1-samuel/24#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/1-samuel/24#v21)</sup> Swear now therefore unto me by the Lord, that thou wilt not cut off my seed after me, and that thou wilt not destroy my name out of my father’s house.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/1-samuel/24#v22)</sup> And David sware unto Saul. And Saul went home; but David and his men gat them up unto the hold.

- Saul asked David to swear that he would not cut off his seed after him nor destroy his name out of his father's house, and David sware unto Saul; Saul went home, and David and his men gat them up unto the hold.
- David showed so much restraint and respect unto his elder; that is something some of us struggle with today, and we need to use this as an example. The Most High was dealing with David: even though he saw Saul acting wickedly and strangely with him, when he had the opportunity to kill Saul he would not do it.
