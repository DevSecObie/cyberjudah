**[1 Samuel 13:1-4](/bible/1-samuel/13#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-samuel/13#v1)</sup> Saul reigned one year; and when he had reigned two years over Israel,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-samuel/13#v2)</sup> Saul chose him three thousand men of Israel; whereof two thousand were with Saul in Michmash and in mount Beth–el, and a thousand were with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin: and the rest of the people he sent every man to his tent.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-samuel/13#v3)</sup> And Jonathan smote the garrison of the Philistines that was in Geba, and the Philistines heard of it. And Saul blew the trumpet throughout all the land, saying, Let the Hebrews hear.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-samuel/13#v4)</sup> And all Israel heard say that Saul had smitten a garrison of the Philistines, and that Israel also was had in abomination with the Philistines. And the people were called together after Saul to Gilgal.

- Chapter 13 is the beginning of Saul showing his simplicity, the beginning of the proof that he is not fit to be king over Israel.
- Saul chose three thousand men, two thousand with him in Michmash and mount Bethel and a thousand with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin. Jonathan smote the garrison of the Philistines in Geba, and the Philistines heard of it; Saul blew the trumpet throughout all the land, and Israel was had in abomination with the Philistines. His son popped off the battle, so now it is war time.

**[1 Samuel 13:5-7](/bible/1-samuel/13#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/1-samuel/13#v5)</sup> And the Philistines gathered themselves together to fight with Israel, thirty thousand chariots, and six thousand horsemen, and people as the sand which is on the sea shore in multitude: and they came up, and pitched in Michmash, eastward from Beth–aven.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-samuel/13#v6)</sup> When the men of Israel saw that they were in a strait, (for the people were distressed,) then the people did hide themselves in caves, and in thickets, and in rocks, and in high places, and in pits.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-samuel/13#v7)</sup> And some of the Hebrews went over Jordan to the land of Gad and Gilead. As for Saul, he was yet in Gilgal, and all the people followed him trembling.

- The Philistines gathered thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen, and people as the sand on the seashore, far deeper than the three thousand Israel had prepared. When the men of Israel saw they were in a strait, they hid themselves in caves, thickets, rocks, high places, and pits; some Hebrews fled over Jordan, and the people that followed Saul followed him trembling.
- The sons of God, the mighty men of valor, saw the enemy coming and started hiding. As the chosen people, if we really have faith we should be fearless; the Lord had just defeated the Philistines for Israel in chapter 7. To still fear the enemy shows a lack of faith, that you really do not believe in your God.
- Nehemiah shows the right spirit. In the Persian captivity he had liberty from the king to rebuild the walls of Jerusalem, and the enemies Sanballat, Tobiah, and Geshem plotted to scare him off the work. A hired man urged him to hide in the temple because they were coming to slay him, and Nehemiah refused; operating in the spirit of fear is sin according to the scripture.

    Shemaiah counseled Nehemiah to hide in the temple because they will come to slay thee; Nehemiah answered, Should such a man as I flee? He was the governor the Most High set up for everybody to look up to, and he would not go in.
  - **[Nehemiah 6:12-13](/bible/nehemiah/6#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/nehemiah/6#v12)</sup> And, lo, I perceived that God had not sent him; but that he pronounced this prophecy against me: for Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him.

    Nehemiah perceived that God had not sent him, but that Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him, that he should be afraid, and do so, and sin, and that they might have matter for an evil report to reproach him; fear in the leader would let the enemy call him weak and unfit to lead.
  - **[2 Timothy 1:7](/bible/2-timothy/1#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/2-timothy/1#v7)</sup> For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind.

    The Lord did not give us the spirit of fear; for the people of the Most High, fearfulness before the enemy is unbelief.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 10:2](/bible/sirach/10#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/10#v2)</sup> As the judge of the people is himself, so are his officers; and what manner of man the ruler of the city is, such are all they that dwell therein.

    If the ruler is a coward, so goes the rest of the nation; that is why the leader must be fearless, because the hearts of the people depend upon their leadership.
  - **[James 3:1](/bible/james/3#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/james/3#v1)</sup> My brethren, be not many masters, knowing that we shall receive the greater condemnation.

    That is why the scriptures say the greater condemnation: if you aspire to leadership, make sure your spirit is right, because if you fold when you are tried, you are accountable for all the souls that fold right along with you.

**[1 Samuel 13:8-10](/bible/1-samuel/13#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-samuel/13#v8)</sup> And he tarried seven days, according to the set time that Samuel had appointed: but Samuel came not to Gilgal; and the people were scattered from him.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-samuel/13#v9)</sup> And Saul said, Bring hither a burnt offering to me, and peace offerings. And he offered the burnt offering.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-samuel/13#v10)</sup> And it came to pass, that as soon as he had made an end of offering the burnt offering, behold, Samuel came; and Saul went out to meet him, that he might salute him.

- Samuel had instructed Saul in chapter 10: when you get to Gilgal, tarry seven days, make no moves until I arrive. Saul tarried seven days according to the set time, but Samuel came not at the moment expected, and the people were scattered from him and acting frantic.
- Saul said, Bring hither a burnt offering to me, and he offered the burnt offering, and Saul is not the priest; that is Samuel's office. And it came to pass that as soon as he had made an end of offering the burnt offering, behold, Samuel came. He was barely late, and Saul could not hold his patience.
- Brothers do the same thing today: leadership said it would be here at ten, it is five past ten, so we cancel the plan and go home. Be humble and tarry like you were instructed; it may run some minutes over, and the Lord may be testing your patience.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 2:2](/bible/sirach/2#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/2#v2)</sup> Set thy heart aright, and constantly endure, and make not haste in time of trouble.

    Saul made haste in a time of trouble; he panicked and did unadvisedly, and he is going to suffer consequences for it.

**[1 Samuel 13:11-12](/bible/1-samuel/13#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/1-samuel/13#v11)</sup> And Samuel said, What hast thou done? And Saul said, Because I saw that the people were scattered from me, and that thou camest not within the days appointed, and that the Philistines gathered themselves together at Michmash;
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-samuel/13#v12)</sup> Therefore said I, The Philistines will come down now upon me to Gilgal, and I have not made supplication unto the Lord: I forced myself therefore, and offered a burnt offering.

- Samuel asks, What hast thou done? Saul answers with excuses: the people were scattered from me, thou camest not within the days appointed, the Philistines gathered at Michmash, so I forced myself and offered a burnt offering. This is part of Saul's pattern of behavior; he never wants to hold himself accountable, he always deflects onto whoever else.

  Precepts:
  - **[Isaiah 58:9](/bible/isaiah/58#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/58#v9)</sup> Then shalt thou call, and the Lord shall answer; thou shalt cry, and he shall say, Here I am. If thou take away from the midst of thee the yoke, the putting forth of the finger, and speaking vanity;

    Then shalt thou call, and the Lord shall answer, if thou take away from the midst of thee the yoke and the putting forth of the finger; the Most High says stop playing the blame game if you want him to intervene on your behalf, but pointing the finger is exactly what Saul did.

**[1 Samuel 13:13-14](/bible/1-samuel/13#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/1-samuel/13#v13)</sup> And Samuel said to Saul, Thou hast done foolishly: thou hast not kept the commandment of the Lord thy God, which he commanded thee: for now would the Lord have established thy kingdom upon Israel for ever.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-samuel/13#v14)</sup> But now thy kingdom shall not continue: the Lord hath sought him a man after his own heart, and the Lord hath commanded him to be captain over his people, because thou hast not kept that which the Lord commanded thee.

- Samuel does not entertain the excuses: thou hast done foolishly; thou hast not kept the commandment of the Lord thy God. Saul thought he was only bending Samuel's word, but disobeying the instruction was disobeying the commandment of the Lord.
- For now would the Lord have established thy kingdom upon Israel for ever; but now thy kingdom shall not continue. For that one act of foolishness the Lord withdrew what he would have given: Saul's lineage ruling Israel.
- The Lord wants to see whether you will perform what he told you to do even when things do not work out how you planned; that is what proves you faithful in temptation and worthy of being exalted. Saul failed that test in the time of his distress.
- The Lord hath sought him a man after his own heart to be captain over his people. Samuel prophesied this without ever having met the replacement; that man is David, predestined before he is even introduced in the scriptures.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Maccabees 2:52-53](/bible/1-maccabees/2#v52)**
    > <sup>[52](/bible/1-maccabees/2#v52)</sup> Was not Abraham found faithful in temptation, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness?
    >
    > <sup>[53](/bible/1-maccabees/2#v53)</sup> Joseph in the time of his distress kept the commandment and was made lord of Egypt.

    Abraham drew down with the knife even when it made no fleshly sense, and Joseph, betrayed and tempted, kept the commandment in the time of his distress and was exalted to be lord of Egypt; loyalty to the Lord regardless of circumstances is what the trial is for.
  - **[Acts 13:22](/bible/acts/13#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/acts/13#v22)</sup> And when he had removed him, he raised up unto them David to be their king; to whom also he gave testimony, and said, I have found David the son of Jesse, a man after mine own heart, which shall fulfil all my will.

    When he had removed Saul, he raised up unto them David to be their king: I have found David the son of Jesse, a man after mine own heart, which shall fulfil all my will.

The Philistines encamped in Michmash, and the spoilers came out of their camp in three companies, toward Ophrah, toward Bethhoron, and toward the border overlooking the valley of Zeboim; three raiding parties sent out to invade Israel.

**[1 Samuel 13:19-23](/bible/1-samuel/13#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/1-samuel/13#v19)</sup> Now there was no smith found throughout all the land of Israel: for the Philistines said, Lest the Hebrews make them swords or spears:
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/1-samuel/13#v20)</sup> But all the Israelites went down to the Philistines, to sharpen every man his share, and his coulter, and his axe, and his mattock.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/1-samuel/13#v21)</sup> Yet they had a file for the mattocks, and for the coulters, and for the forks, and for the axes, and to sharpen the goads.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/1-samuel/13#v22)</sup> So it came to pass in the day of battle, that there was neither sword nor spear found in the hand of any of the people that were with Saul and Jonathan: but with Saul and with Jonathan his son was there found.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/1-samuel/13#v23)</sup> And the garrison of the Philistines went out to the passage of Michmash.

- There was no smith found throughout all the land of Israel, for the Philistines said, Lest the Hebrews make them swords or spears. The Israelites had to go down to the Philistines to sharpen every man his share, his coulter, his axe, and his mattock, so the Philistines knew exactly how disarmed Israel was; all they had was a file for the tools.
- This is heavy for today. A people that must depend on its adversary for weaponry cannot deliver itself by arms; talk of going to war means nothing when you have to buy your weapons from the very gun store of the ones you say you will fight. Same picture as the scriptures.
- So in the day of battle there was neither sword nor spear in the hand of any of the people, but with Saul and with Jonathan his son was there found; only two men in the army had a blade.
- In chapter 13 Saul blew the kingdom with his instability in a time of distress. The Most High said, You are not fit; I would have established you, but your replacement is already on the way.
