## Introduction

This session covers [1 Samuel 12](/bible/1-samuel/12) through 15, heavy chapters in the rise and fall of king Saul. In chapter 12 Samuel gives his farewell address: he establishes his own blamelessness, rehearses the righteous acts of the Most High from Egypt through the judges, rebukes Israel for demanding a king when the Lord was their king, and calls down thunder and rain in wheat harvest to prove the wickedness of that request. In chapter 13 Saul begins to show he is not fit to be king, making haste in time of trouble by offering the burnt offering instead of waiting for Samuel. In chapter 14 Jonathan and his armor bearer take on the Philistine garrison alone, showing there is no restraint to the Lord to save by many or by few. In chapter 15 the Lord commands Saul to utterly destroy Amalek; Saul obeys partially, and partial obedience is counted as rebellion: to obey is better than sacrifice, and rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft.

**[1 Samuel 12:1](/bible/1-samuel/12#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-samuel/12#v1)</sup> And Samuel said unto all Israel, Behold, I have hearkened unto your voice in all that ye said unto me, and have made a king over you.

- At the end of chapter 11 Israel crowned Saul at Gilgal. Back in chapter 8 the people had pressured Samuel because they wanted to be like the nations round about them, and the Most High led him to choose Saul the son of Kish.
- This is the aftermath speech. Samuel is putting it on record: I hearkened unto your voice in all that ye said unto me and made a king over you; whatever happens from here came from what you asked for.

**[1 Samuel 12:2-5](/bible/1-samuel/12#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/1-samuel/12#v2)</sup> And now, behold, the king walketh before you: and I am old and grayheaded; and, behold, my sons are with you: and I have walked before you from my childhood unto this day.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-samuel/12#v3)</sup> Behold, here I am: witness against me before the Lord, and before his anointed: whose ox have I taken? or whose ass have I taken? or whom have I defrauded? whom have I oppressed? or of whose hand have I received any bribe to blind mine eyes therewith? and I will restore it you.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-samuel/12#v4)</sup> And they said, Thou hast not defrauded us, nor oppressed us, neither hast thou taken ought of any man’s hand.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-samuel/12#v5)</sup> And he said unto them, The Lord is witness against you, and his anointed is witness this day, that ye have not found ought in my hand. And they answered, He is witness.

- Samuel establishes his blamelessness before he reasons with them. He walked before Israel from his childhood unto this day; whose ox or ass has he taken, whom has he defrauded or oppressed, from whose hand has he taken any bribe? Speak up now and he will restore it.
- The people answer that he has not defrauded nor oppressed them nor taken anything from any man's hand, and the Lord and his anointed are witness that day. Samuel was never a bad guy to the nation of Israel, and the whole congregation confirms it.

- It is the Lord that advanced Moses and Aaron and brought your fathers up out of the land of Egypt. Now stand still, that Samuel may reason with them before the Lord of all the righteous acts of the Lord which he did to them and to their fathers.

>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-samuel/12#v10)</sup> And they cried unto the Lord, and said, We have sinned, because we have forsaken the Lord, and have served Baalim and Ashtaroth: but now deliver us out of the hand of our enemies, and we will serve thee.

- When Jacob was come into Egypt and your fathers cried unto the Lord, the Lord sent Moses and Aaron and made Israel dwell in this place. When they forgat the Lord he sold them into the hand of Sisera, captain of the host of Hazor, and of the Philistines, and of the king of Moab; this is the history of the book of Judges, starting in chapter 4.
- They cried unto the Lord and confessed they had sinned in forsaking him and serving Baalim and Ashtaroth. That is the same lesson Samuel taught in chapter 7: when the Philistines beat Israel, they went and got the ark of the covenant, got beat even worse, and lost the ark. It is not just about having the ark of the covenant among you; you have to put away the idols and stop serving Baalim and Ashtaroth.
- So the Lord sent Jerubbaal, which is Gideon, and Bedan, and Jephthah, and Samuel, and delivered Israel out of the hand of their enemies on every side, and they dwelled safe. None of those saviors had to wear a crown.

He knew that if thrones were set up, the people would put their trust and faith in the king instead of the Most High; it was never the Lord's intention to set kings in the flesh over Israel, he was to be our king.

- Now behold the king whom ye have chosen and whom ye have desired. If ye will fear the Lord and serve him and obey his voice and not rebel against the commandment of the Lord, then shall both ye and also the king that reigneth over you continue following the Lord your God. If you and your king do not rebel against the voice of the Lord, the ultimate king, you will be all right.
- Moses, in the spirit of the Lord, knew exactly what Israel would do once they came into the land, and wrote the law of the king ahead of time.

# 1 Samuel 13: Make Not Haste in Time of Trouble

Read the chapter → [1 Samuel 13](/bible/1-samuel/13)

**[1 Samuel 13:1-4](/bible/1-samuel/13#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-samuel/13#v1)</sup> Saul reigned one year; and when he had reigned two years over Israel,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-samuel/13#v2)</sup> Saul chose him three thousand men of Israel; whereof two thousand were with Saul in Michmash and in mount Beth–el, and a thousand were with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin: and the rest of the people he sent every man to his tent.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-samuel/13#v3)</sup> And Jonathan smote the garrison of the Philistines that was in Geba, and the Philistines heard of it. And Saul blew the trumpet throughout all the land, saying, Let the Hebrews hear.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-samuel/13#v4)</sup> And all Israel heard say that Saul had smitten a garrison of the Philistines, and that Israel also was had in abomination with the Philistines. And the people were called together after Saul to Gilgal.

- Chapter 13 is the beginning of Saul showing his simplicity, the beginning of the proof that he is not fit to be king over Israel.
- Saul chose three thousand men, two thousand with him in Michmash and mount Bethel and a thousand with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin. Jonathan smote the garrison of the Philistines in Geba, and the Philistines heard of it; Saul blew the trumpet throughout all the land, and Israel was had in abomination with the Philistines. His son popped off the battle, so now it is war time.

**[1 Samuel 13:5-7](/bible/1-samuel/13#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/1-samuel/13#v5)</sup> And the Philistines gathered themselves together to fight with Israel, thirty thousand chariots, and six thousand horsemen, and people as the sand which is on the sea shore in multitude: and they came up, and pitched in Michmash, eastward from Beth–aven.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-samuel/13#v6)</sup> When the men of Israel saw that they were in a strait, (for the people were distressed,) then the people did hide themselves in caves, and in thickets, and in rocks, and in high places, and in pits.

> <sup>[7](/bible/1-samuel/13#v7)</sup> And some of the Hebrews went over Jordan to the land of Gad and Gilead. As for Saul, he was yet in Gilgal, and all the people followed him trembling.

- The Philistines gathered thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen, and people as the sand on the seashore, far deeper than the three thousand Israel had prepared. When the men of Israel saw they were in a strait, they hid themselves in caves, thickets, rocks, high places, and pits; some Hebrews fled over Jordan, and the people that followed Saul followed him trembling.
- The sons of God, the mighty men of valor, saw the enemy coming and started hiding. As the chosen people, if we really have faith we should be fearless; the Lord had just defeated the Philistines for Israel in chapter 7. To still fear the enemy shows a lack of faith, that you really do not believe in your God.
- Nehemiah shows the right spirit. In the Persian captivity he had liberty from the king to rebuild the walls of Jerusalem, and the enemies Sanballat, Tobiah, and Geshem plotted to scare him off the work. A hired man urged him to hide in the temple because they were coming to slay him, and Nehemiah refused; operating in the spirit of fear is sin according to the scripture.

**[1 Samuel 13:8-10](/bible/1-samuel/13#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-samuel/13#v8)</sup> And he tarried seven days, according to the set time that Samuel had appointed: but Samuel came not to Gilgal; and the people were scattered from him.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-samuel/13#v9)</sup> And Saul said, Bring hither a burnt offering to me, and peace offerings. And he offered the burnt offering.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-samuel/13#v10)</sup> And it came to pass, that as soon as he had made an end of offering the burnt offering, behold, Samuel came; and Saul went out to meet him, that he might salute him.

- Samuel had instructed Saul in chapter 10: when you get to Gilgal, tarry seven days, make no moves until I arrive. Saul tarried seven days according to the set time, but Samuel came not at the moment expected, and the people were scattered from him and acting frantic.
- Saul said, Bring hither a burnt offering to me, and he offered the burnt offering, and Saul is not the priest; that is Samuel's office. And it came to pass that as soon as he had made an end of offering the burnt offering, behold, Samuel came. He was barely late, and Saul could not hold his patience.
- Brothers do the same thing today: leadership said it would be here at ten, it is five past ten, so we cancel the plan and go home. Be humble and tarry like you were instructed; it may run some minutes over, and the Lord may be testing your patience.

  Precepts:

    > <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/2#v2)</sup> Set thy heart aright, and constantly endure, and make not haste in time of trouble.

    Saul made haste in a time of trouble; he panicked and did unadvisedly, and he is going to suffer consequences for it.

**[1 Samuel 13:11-12](/bible/1-samuel/13#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/1-samuel/13#v11)</sup> And Samuel said, What hast thou done? And Saul said, Because I saw that the people were scattered from me, and that thou camest not within the days appointed, and that the Philistines gathered themselves together at Michmash;

- Samuel asks, What hast thou done? Saul answers with excuses: the people were scattered from me, thou camest not within the days appointed, the Philistines gathered at Michmash, so I forced myself and offered a burnt offering. This is part of Saul's pattern of behavior; he never wants to hold himself accountable, he always deflects onto whoever else.

  - **[Judith 8:16](/bible/judith/8#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/judith/8#v16)</sup> Do not bind the counsels of the Lord our God: for God is not as man, that he may be threatened; neither is he as the son of man, that he should be wavering.

    The Most High is not as man, that he may be threatened, neither does he waver; the sentence on Saul stood.

**[1 Samuel 15:30-31](/bible/1-samuel/15#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/1-samuel/15#v30)</sup> Then he said, I have sinned: yet honour me now, I pray thee, before the elders of my people, and before Israel, and turn again with me, that I may worship the Lord thy God.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/1-samuel/15#v31)</sup> So Samuel turned again after Saul; and Saul worshipped the Lord.

- Then he said, I have sinned: yet honour me now, I pray thee, before the elders of my people, and before Israel. This spirit is very real: okay, you are right, but do not let the people see the demotion; make sure they still know I am the king.
- That is the spirit of vanity, caught up in a rank and a title while forgetting that the title came for pleasing the Lord; once you discontinue pleasing the Lord, the title is going to leave as well. So Samuel turned again after Saul, and Saul worshipped the Lord.

**[1 Samuel 15:32-33](/bible/1-samuel/15#v32)**

> <sup>[32](/bible/1-samuel/15#v32)</sup> Then said Samuel, Bring ye hither to me Agag the king of the Amalekites. And Agag came unto him delicately. And Agag said, Surely the bitterness of death is past.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/1-samuel/15#v33)</sup> And Samuel said, As thy sword hath made women childless, so shall thy mother be childless among women. And Samuel hewed Agag in pieces before the Lord in Gilgal.

- Then said Samuel, Bring ye hither to me Agag the king of the Amalekites. And Agag came unto him delicately, and said, Surely the bitterness of death is past; with Saul under rebuke, Agag figured nobody was mad at the Amalekites anymore and he was safe.
- And Samuel said, As thy sword hath made women childless, so shall thy mother be childless among women. And Samuel hewed Agag in pieces before the Lord in Gilgal. The old prophet did not play; he finished the commandment himself, measure for measure for what Amalek's sword had done.

**[1 Samuel 15:34-35](/bible/1-samuel/15#v34)**

> <sup>[34](/bible/1-samuel/15#v34)</sup> Then Samuel went to Ramah; and Saul went up to his house to Gibeah of Saul.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/1-samuel/15#v35)</sup> And Samuel came no more to see Saul until the day of his death: nevertheless Samuel mourned for Saul: and the Lord repented that he had made Saul king over Israel.

- Then Samuel went to Ramah, and Saul went up to his house in Gibeah, and Samuel came no more to see Saul until the day of his death: nevertheless Samuel mourned for Saul, and the Lord repented that he had made Saul king over Israel.
- The mourning is what makes it sad: Samuel raised Saul up from nothing, saw his potential, and saw the Spirit come upon him in chapter 11 when he stood up as a savior for the people; he had hope in him, and it came to this. Samuel was grieved, and the Lord repented of the appointment.
