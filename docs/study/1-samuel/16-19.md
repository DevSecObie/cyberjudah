## Introduction

This session covers 1 Samuel chapters 16 through 19: David selected and anointed as Saul's replacement, his humble beginnings as a keeper of sheep and a player on the harp, his victory over Goliath, Saul's descent into envy and murder once the women sang David's praise, and the faithfulness of Jonathan and Michal in delivering David from Saul's snares. The thread that ties it together is the spirit: the Most High looks not on the outward appearance but on the heart, his spirit came upon David from the day of his anointing, and it departed from Saul, who was given over to his own ruin.

**[1 Samuel 16:1](/bible/1-samuel/16#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-samuel/16#v1)</sup> And the Lord said unto Samuel, How long wilt thou mourn for Saul, seeing I have rejected him from reigning over Israel? fill thine horn with oil, and go, I will send thee to Jesse the Beth–lehemite: for I have provided me a king among his sons.

- Recall the class on chapters 12 through 15: in chapter 15 Saul rebelled against the commandment, and after two admonitions he still would not humble down and repent, so the Most High rejected him from reigning over Israel and told him through Samuel that his kingdom would not continue.
- Samuel had groomed Saul up and saw potential in the brother, so he mourned over him; the Most High asked how long he would keep crying over the man he had rejected.
- Samuel is told to fill his horn with oil and go to Jesse the Bethlehemite, for the Most High had already provided himself a king from among Jesse's sons.

**[1 Samuel 16:2-3](/bible/1-samuel/16#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/1-samuel/16#v2)</sup> And Samuel said, How can I go? if Saul hear it, he will kill me. And the Lord said, Take an heifer with thee, and say, I am come to sacrifice to the Lord.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-samuel/16#v3)</sup> And call Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will shew thee what thou shalt do: and thou shalt anoint unto me him whom I name unto thee.

- Samuel feared that Saul would kill him if he heard of it. Remember the influence and love Samuel had among the people; even so, this shows the power of the king. Saul raised no hand against Samuel yet, but a king had the authority to command a prophet's death.
- The Most High told Samuel to go in with a heifer, as if coming only to sacrifice; he would go incognito, not letting it be known that he was coming to anoint Saul's replacement, and the Most High would show him whom to anoint.

**[1 Samuel 16:6-7](/bible/1-samuel/16#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/1-samuel/16#v6)</sup> And it came to pass, when they were come, that he looked on Eliab, and said, Surely the Lord’s anointed is before him.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-samuel/16#v7)</sup> But the Lord said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the Lord seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the Lord looketh on the heart.

- When Samuel looked on Eliab he judged through his own lens as a man: surely this is the Lord's anointed. The Most High corrected him: look not on his countenance or on the height of his stature, because I have refused him. Looking the mightiest and the tallest does not make a man the one the Most High picked.
- The Lord seeth not as man seeth; man looketh on the outward appearance, but the Lord looketh on the heart. This scripture is heavy: it is showing the spirit of Christ before Christ walked the earth. The Pharisees could not understand why he dealt with the lower class instead of the mighty men; he was looking on the heart, and he knew the Pharisees' hearts were corrupt while the humble were willing to devote their lives to the Most High.
- This still happens today: brothers will look at a group and assume the biggest one must be the leader, not knowing that brother might be the soldier while the leader is the slim one beside him.

**[1 Samuel 16:14](/bible/1-samuel/16#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/1-samuel/16#v14)</sup> But the Spirit of the Lord departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from the Lord troubled him.

- Simultaneously, as the Most High put his spirit on David, the spirit departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from the Lord troubled him. Note it well: the evil spirit came from the Lord; that is a teaching the church world leaves out.
- Chapter 15 said rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and Saul is now headed down that downward spiral; the spirit is leaving him, and he will eventually become a reprobate and dabble in real witchcraft.

- Why did the spirit leave Saul? Wisdom walks with a man by crooked ways at the first and tries him by her laws. Saul was put through a trial, whether he would keep the commandment given through the mouth of the prophet, and he went wrong; so the spirit forsook him and he was given over to his own ruin, that abyss of jealousy, envy, and hatred.

# 1 Samuel 17: Not by Sword and Spear

- Saul sent messengers to take David, and when they saw the company of the prophets prophesying and Samuel standing as appointed over them, the spirit of God was upon the messengers of Saul, and they also prophesied; a second and a third set of messengers were sent, and the same thing happened each time they entered that atmosphere.
- Then Saul went himself, fully set in his mind to take David's life himself, and the spirit of God came upon him also; he prophesied before Samuel, stripped off his clothes, and lay down naked all that day and all that night, wherefore they say, Is Saul also among the prophets?
- That is the close of the session: when you are fighting for the Most High, nothing can be done unto you; if the eyes of the wicked are against you and you are ordained and predestined, his enemies came to take David and the spirit of God shut the evil down every time.
