## Introduction

This session covers [Ruth 4](/bible/ruth/4) together with 1 Samuel 1-3; these notes cover [1 Samuel 1](/bible/1-samuel/1) through 3. With first Samuel the class gets into the history of Israel, our people, as we move through the Bible. The main threads of the teaching: Hannah barren and provoked by her adversary, the vow of the Nazarite she vowed for a man child, the petition granted according to the will of the Most High, the vow paid when Samuel is lent to the Lord all the days of his life, Hannah's prophecy that they that stumbled are girded with strength, the sons of Eli who were sons of Belial and knew not the Lord, the judgment on the house of Eli for honouring his sons above the Most High, and the Lord coming and standing to reveal himself to Samuel, the same way he stands at the door of the spirit of all Israel and knocks.

**[1 Samuel 1:1-3](/bible/1-samuel/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-samuel/1#v1)</sup> Now there was a certain man of Ramathaim–zophim, of mount Ephraim, and his name was Elkanah, the son of Jeroham, the son of Elihu, the son of Tohu, the son of Zuph, an Ephrathite:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-samuel/1#v2)</sup> And he had two wives; the name of the one was Hannah, and the name of the other Peninnah: and Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-samuel/1#v3)</sup> And this man went up out of his city yearly to worship and to sacrifice unto the Lord of hosts in Shiloh. And the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, the priests of the Lord, were there.

- There was a certain man of mount Ephraim named Elkanah, and he had two wives: the name of the one was Hannah, and the name of the other Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children.
- Elkanah went up out of his city yearly to worship and to sacrifice unto the Lord of hosts in Shiloh, where the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, the priests of the Lord, were. Shiloh is another name for Jerusalem.

**[1 Samuel 1:4-7](/bible/1-samuel/1#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/1-samuel/1#v4)</sup> And when the time was that Elkanah offered, he gave to Peninnah his wife, and to all her sons and her daughters, portions:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-samuel/1#v5)</sup> But unto Hannah he gave a worthy portion; for he loved Hannah: but the Lord had shut up her womb.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-samuel/1#v6)</sup> And her adversary also provoked her sore, for to make her fret, because the Lord had shut up her womb.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-samuel/1#v7)</sup> And as he did so year by year, when she went up to the house of the Lord, so she provoked her; therefore she wept, and did not eat.

- When Elkanah offered, he gave to Peninnah his wife and to all her sons and her daughters portions; but unto Hannah he gave a worthy portion, for he loved Hannah: but the Lord had shut up her womb.
- Her adversary provoked her sore, to make her fret, because the Lord had shut up her womb; year by year, when she went up to the house of the Lord, she provoked her, so that Hannah wept and did not eat. The women of Israel have not changed one bit: even back in the times of Samuel there was a sister vexing a barren woman and rubbing it in her face. Sisters of Israel have to get their spirits right; no doubt about it, you are Israel.

**[1 Samuel 1:8](/bible/1-samuel/1#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-samuel/1#v8)</sup> Then said Elkanah her husband to her, Hannah, why weepest thou? and why eatest thou not? and why is thy heart grieved? am not I better to thee than ten sons?

- Elkanah her husband said to her, Hannah, why weepest thou? and why eatest thou not? and why is thy heart grieved? am not I better to thee than ten sons?

**[1 Samuel 1:9-11](/bible/1-samuel/1#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/1-samuel/1#v9)</sup> So Hannah rose up after they had eaten in Shiloh, and after they had drunk. Now Eli the priest sat upon a seat by a post of the temple of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-samuel/1#v10)</sup> And she was in bitterness of soul, and prayed unto the Lord, and wept sore.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-samuel/1#v11)</sup> And she vowed a vow, and said, O Lord of hosts, if thou wilt indeed look on the affliction of thine handmaid, and remember me, and not forget thine handmaid, but wilt give unto thine handmaid a man child, then I will give him unto the Lord all the days of his life, and there shall no razor come upon his head.

- Hannah rose up after they had eaten in Shiloh, and in bitterness of soul she prayed unto the Lord and wept sore, while Eli the priest sat upon a seat by a post of the temple of the Lord.
- She vowed a vow: O Lord of hosts, if thou wilt indeed look on the affliction of thine handmaid, and remember me, and give unto thine handmaid a man child, then I will give him unto the Lord all the days of his life, and there shall no rasor come upon his head. What law is that going back to? The law of the Nazarite. Put some things together as you read through Samuel about Samuel.

  Precepts:
  - **[Numbers 6:5](/bible/numbers/6#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/numbers/6#v5)</sup> All the days of the vow of his separation there shall no razor come upon his head: until the days be fulfilled, in the which he separateth himself unto the Lord, he shall be holy, and shall let the locks of the hair of his head grow.

    All the days of the vow of his separation no rasor comes upon his head; he shall be holy, and shall let the locks of the hair of his head grow.

- The locks of a Nazarite are hair braided so that it locks into place as it grows long; when you braid your hair you are locking it in. You read of the locks of Samson's hair back in the book of Judges; Samson was under that same vow.

  Precepts:
  - **[Judges 16:13](/bible/judges/16#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/judges/16#v13)</sup> And Delilah said unto Samson, Hitherto thou hast mocked me, and told me lies: tell me wherewith thou mightest be bound. And he said unto her, If thou weavest the seven locks of my head with the web.

    Samson spoke of the seven locks of his head; the locks of the Nazarite vow.

**[1 Samuel 1:12-18](/bible/1-samuel/1#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/1-samuel/1#v12)</sup> And it came to pass, as she continued praying before the Lord, that Eli marked her mouth.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/1-samuel/1#v13)</sup> Now Hannah, she spake in her heart; only her lips moved, but her voice was not heard: therefore Eli thought she had been drunken.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-samuel/1#v14)</sup> And Eli said unto her, How long wilt thou be drunken? put away thy wine from thee.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-samuel/1#v15)</sup> And Hannah answered and said, No, my lord, I am a woman of a sorrowful spirit: I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but have poured out my soul before the Lord.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/1-samuel/1#v16)</sup> Count not thine handmaid for a daughter of Belial: for out of the abundance of my complaint and grief have I spoken hitherto.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/1-samuel/1#v17)</sup> Then Eli answered and said, Go in peace: and the God of Israel grant thee thy petition that thou hast asked of him.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-samuel/1#v18)</sup> And she said, Let thine handmaid find grace in thy sight. So the woman went her way, and did eat, and her countenance was no more sad.

- As she continued praying before the Lord, Eli marked her mouth. Hannah spake in her heart; only her lips moved, but her voice was not heard: therefore Eli thought she had been drunken, and said, How long wilt thou be drunken? put away thy wine from thee.
- Hannah answered, No, my lord, I am a woman of a sorrowful spirit: I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but have poured out my soul before the Lord; count not thine handmaid for a daughter of Belial. Then Eli answered, Go in peace: and the God of Israel grant thee thy petition that thou hast asked of him.
- So the woman went her way, and did eat, and her countenance was no more sad. What did she do? She believed when she heard the word of Eli.
- Why did the Lord grant her petition? It was the will of the Most High for Samuel to come through Hannah; it was just in his time. And she was granted it because of what she did before she even asked: she kept his commandments and did those things that are pleasing in his sight.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 John 5:14-15](/bible/1-john/5#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/1-john/5#v14)</sup> And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us:
    >
    > <sup>[15](/bible/1-john/5#v15)</sup> And if we know that he hear us, whatsoever we ask, we know that we have the petitions that we desired of him.

    This is the confidence that we have in him: if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us, and we have the petitions that we desired of him.
  - **[1 John 3:22](/bible/1-john/3#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/1-john/3#v22)</sup> And whatsoever we ask, we receive of him, because we keep his commandments, and do those things that are pleasing in his sight.

    Whatsoever we ask, we receive of him, because we keep his commandments, and do those things that are pleasing in his sight.

- Understand this: a lot of us pray for things that the Lord has not given to us. The Most High has the infinite and ultimate wisdom and knows what is best for us, so some things he keeps back from us; we have to accept that and know that. If we keep the commandments and it be his will, we receive whatsoever we ask, in his time; if it is not his will, we will not receive it.

**[1 Samuel 1:19-20](/bible/1-samuel/1#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/1-samuel/1#v19)</sup> And they rose up in the morning early, and worshipped before the Lord, and returned, and came to their house to Ramah: and Elkanah knew Hannah his wife; and the Lord remembered her.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/1-samuel/1#v20)</sup> Wherefore it came to pass, when the time was come about after Hannah had conceived, that she bare a son, and called his name Samuel, saying, Because I have asked him of the Lord.

- They rose up in the morning early, worshipped before the Lord, and returned to their house to Ramah; they went back up north, for Elkanah came out of mount Ephraim. And Elkanah knew Hannah his wife: knew means he laid with her, they had laid down together; and the Lord remembered her.
- When the time was come about after Hannah had conceived, she bare a son and called his name Samuel, saying, Because I have asked him of the Lord. Samuel means asked of the Lord.

**[1 Samuel 1:21](/bible/1-samuel/1#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/1-samuel/1#v21)</sup> And the man Elkanah, and all his house, went up to offer unto the Lord the yearly sacrifice, and his vow.

- The man Elkanah, and all his house, went up to offer unto the Lord the yearly sacrifice, and his vow. What is that yearly sacrifice going into? [Deuteronomy 16:16](/bible/deuteronomy/16#v16).

  Precepts:
  - **[Deuteronomy 16:16](/bible/deuteronomy/16#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/deuteronomy/16#v16)</sup> Three times in a year shall all thy males appear before the Lord thy God in the place which he shall choose; in the feast of unleavened bread, and in the feast of weeks, and in the feast of tabernacles: and they shall not appear before the Lord empty:

    Three times in a year shall all thy males appear before the Lord: in the feast of unleavened bread, which is known as Passover; in the feast of weeks, which is known as Pentecost; and in the feast of tabernacles, which is also known as the feast of booths. Elkanah, who lived in mount Ephraim, was applying this law over and over again.

**[1 Samuel 1:22-23](/bible/1-samuel/1#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/1-samuel/1#v22)</sup> But Hannah went not up; for she said unto her husband, I will not go up until the child be weaned, and then I will bring him, that he may appear before the Lord, and there abide for ever.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/1-samuel/1#v23)</sup> And Elkanah her husband said unto her, Do what seemeth thee good; tarry until thou have weaned him; only the Lord establish his word. So the woman abode, and gave her son suck until she weaned him.

- But Hannah went not up; she said unto her husband, I will not go up until the child be weaned, and then I will bring him, that he may appear before the Lord, and there abide for ever. A weaned child was three or four years old; that would be the last time he was under her care, so she said, I am not going up until that happens.
- Elkanah said unto her, Do what seemeth thee good; tarry until thou have weaned him; only the Lord establish his word. She did not have to go up: the law said all thy males. So the woman abode, and gave her son suck until she weaned him.

**[1 Samuel 1:24-28](/bible/1-samuel/1#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/1-samuel/1#v24)</sup> And when she had weaned him, she took him up with her, with three bullocks, and one ephah of flour, and a bottle of wine, and brought him unto the house of the Lord in Shiloh: and the child was young.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/1-samuel/1#v25)</sup> And they slew a bullock, and brought the child to Eli.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/1-samuel/1#v26)</sup> And she said, Oh my lord, as thy soul liveth, my lord, I am the woman that stood by thee here, praying unto the Lord.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/1-samuel/1#v27)</sup> For this child I prayed; and the Lord hath given me my petition which I asked of him:
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/1-samuel/1#v28)</sup> Therefore also I have lent him to the Lord; as long as he liveth he shall be lent to the Lord. And he worshipped the Lord there.

- When she had weaned him, she took him up with her, with three bullocks, and one ephah of flour, and a bottle of wine, and brought him unto the house of the Lord in Shiloh: and the child was young.
- She said to Eli, I am the woman that stood by thee here, praying unto the Lord. For this child I prayed; and the Lord hath given me my petition which I asked of him: therefore also I have lent him to the Lord; as long as he liveth he shall be lent to the Lord. That is great faith right there that Hannah had, and she kept her promise: Hannah was an upright and honest woman.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiastes 5:4-6](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v4)</sup> When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it; for he hath no pleasure in fools: pay that which thou hast vowed.
    >
    > <sup>[5](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v5)</sup> Better is it that thou shouldest not vow, than that thou shouldest vow and not pay.
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/ecclesiastes/5#v6)</sup> Suffer not thy mouth to cause thy flesh to sin; neither say thou before the angel, that it was an error: wherefore should God be angry at thy voice, and destroy the work of thine hands?

    When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it; better is it that thou shouldest not vow, than that thou shouldest vow and not pay. The angel before whom you must not say it was an error is your personal angel, your guardian angel for lack of a better word, that watches over you and listens to you; do not say, I did not mean to say that. Yes you did; the angel recorded it and knew exactly what it was.
  - **[Matthew 5:37](/bible/matthew/5#v37)**
    > <sup>[37](/bible/matthew/5#v37)</sup> But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil.

    That is why Christ went on to say, Let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay. Whatever comes out of your mouth, be upright and honest, and do that which comes out of thy mouth.
