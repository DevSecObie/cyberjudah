## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on James 2 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-26, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[James 2:1](/bible/james/2#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/james/2#v1)</sup> My brethren, have not the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory, with respect of persons.

- “My brethren, have not the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory, with respect of persons.” Christians love to run to this chapter too, and it is dismantled here. The context is not to be a respecter of persons. When you are a respecter of persons, you are violating loving your neighbour as yourself.

**[James 2:2-4](/bible/james/2#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/james/2#v2)</sup> For if there come unto your assembly a man with a gold ring, in goodly apparel, and there come in also a poor man in vile raiment;
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/james/2#v3)</sup> And ye have respect to him that weareth the gay clothing, and say unto him, Sit thou here in a good place; and say to the poor, Stand thou there, or sit here under my footstool:
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/james/2#v4)</sup> Are ye not then partial in yourselves, and are become judges of evil thoughts?

- A man comes into the assembly well off, “a man with a gold ring, in goodly apparel”, and also “a poor man in vile raiment”. One is fancy, one not so fancy. “The gay clothing” is not gay as in that: it means happy, dressing in expensive clothes. James is addressing a church being respecters of persons. A brother comes in with Gucci on, Prada on: Hey, sit over here, wow. Then the poor brother comes in: Go sit in the corner over there, man, get out of here. James says that is evil as hell. Don’t do that.
- “Stand thou there”: just like they say of Christ, standing there all the time with no throne. “Are ye not then partial in yourselves, and are become judges of evil thoughts?” By doing these things you are evil as hell, not examining yourself, not seeing how that is wrong.

**[James 2:5](/bible/james/2#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/james/2#v5)</sup> Hearken, my beloved brethren, Hath not God chosen the poor of this world rich in faith, and heirs of the kingdom which he hath promised to them that love him?

- “Hearken, my beloved brethren”: listen to me. “Hath not God chosen the poor of this world rich in faith, and heirs of the kingdom”. This nation of Israel as a whole, even now, is poor: poor in spirit and poor in wealth. Very few of our people are wealthy and well off. We are the poor of this world but rich in the faith in Christ; our riches are right here. He is writing to the heirs, and we are the heirs.

**[James 2:6-7](/bible/james/2#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/james/2#v6)</sup> But ye have despised the poor. Do not rich men oppress you, and draw you before the judgment seats?
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/james/2#v7)</sup> Do not they blaspheme that worthy name by the which ye are called?

- “But ye have despised the poor”, by being respecters of persons. “Do not rich men oppress you”: the Pharisees were oppressing them. The white man is rich and he oppresses you too, yet you care about the rich more than the poor, and the rich treat you like garbage. “And draw you before the judgment seats?” The rich Pharisees who hate your guts. “Do not they blaspheme that worthy name by the which ye are called?” The rich Pharisees speak evil of Christ, your master and your saviour.

**[James 2:8](/bible/james/2#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/james/2#v8)</sup> If ye fulfil the royal law according to the scripture, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself, ye do well:

- “If ye fulfil the royal law according to the scripture, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself, ye do well”. This is what the Christians hold on to: love your neighbour, and you fulfil all the law.
- But when Paul and James wrote these things as letters to the churches and to the brethren that were scattered, for what they said to be substantiated they had to draw from previous writings; otherwise it is just them talking. They needed a reference point, what we call today the Old Testament, to validate their sayings. That is why it says prove all things.
- “If ye fulfil the royal law according to the scripture, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself, ye do well”. Remember the context: respect of persons, mistreating the poor for the rich, being partial. The royal law is love your neighbour as yourself.

  Precepts:
  - **[Galatians 5:14](/bible/galatians/5#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/galatians/5#v14)</sup> For all the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this; Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.

    “For all the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this; Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.” They read this and say: see, you fulfil the law if you love your neighbour; the law is fulfilled now, that’s it.
  - **[Leviticus 19:17-18](/bible/leviticus/19#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/leviticus/19#v17)</sup> Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him.
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/leviticus/19#v18)</sup> Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the Lord.

    “Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him.” Loving your neighbour is teaching him. All scripture is given by inspiration of God and is profitable for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness, so you have to teach him the laws of God: Hey bro, you know you’re breaking the law of God. You can’t do that. The law says this.
  - **[Romans 13:8-10](/bible/romans/13#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/romans/13#v8)</sup> Owe no man any thing, but to love one another: for he that loveth another hath fulfilled the law.
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/romans/13#v9)</sup> For this, Thou shalt not commit adultery, Thou shalt not kill, Thou shalt not steal, Thou shalt not bear false witness, Thou shalt not covet; and if there be any other commandment, it is briefly comprehended in this saying, namely, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/romans/13#v10)</sup> Love worketh no ill to his neighbour: therefore love is the fulfilling of the law.

    What is the royal law? “Owe no man any thing, but to love one another: for he that loveth another hath fulfilled the law”: love your neighbour as yourself. “Thou shalt not commit adultery”: royal law. “Thou shalt not kill”: royal law. “Thou shalt not steal”, “Thou shalt not bear false witness”, “Thou shalt not covet”: royal law. “And if there be any other commandment”, outside of those regarding your neighbour, “it is briefly comprehended in this saying, namely, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.”

**[James 2:9](/bible/james/2#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/james/2#v9)</sup> But if ye have respect to persons, ye commit sin, and are convinced of the law as transgressors.

- “But if ye have respect to persons”, if you violate love your neighbour as yourself, “ye commit sin” against the royal law, “and are convinced of the law as transgressors”: guilty, sinners. The law you are convinced of is the royal law, and you broke it.

**[James 2:10-11](/bible/james/2#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/james/2#v10)</sup> For whosoever shall keep the whole law, and yet offend in one point, he is guilty of all.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/james/2#v11)</sup> For he that said, Do not commit adultery, said also, Do not kill. Now if thou commit no adultery, yet if thou kill, thou art become a transgressor of the law.

- “For whosoever shall keep the whole law, and yet offend in one point, he is guilty of all.” Christians use this to say that if you sin in one law you break them all, so you can’t keep them at all. Stay in context: the whole law here is the whole royal law, love your neighbour as yourself, don’t be a respecter of persons, the rich man and the poor man. The one point is one point of the royal law: thou shalt not bear false witness and the rest, as read in Romans 13.
- “For he that said, Do not commit adultery, said also, Do not kill”: back to the royal law. If you commit no adultery but kill your brother instead, “thou art become a transgressor of the law”: the royal law.

**[James 2:12-13](/bible/james/2#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/james/2#v12)</sup> So speak ye, and so do, as they that shall be judged by the law of liberty.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/james/2#v13)</sup> For he shall have judgment without mercy, that hath shewed no mercy; and mercy rejoiceth against judgment.

- “So speak ye, and so do, as they that shall be judged by the law of liberty.” Under the old covenant, if you violated the moral law, moved your neighbour’s landmark, made the blind wander, slept with your brother’s wife, you got death. Christ redeemed us from that and put us under the law of liberty. So stop doing that.
- “For he shall have judgment without mercy, that hath shewed no mercy”: show no mercy and you shall receive no mercy. “And mercy rejoiceth against judgment”: the Most High chooses to have mercy rather than judge you for violating the royal law.

**[James 2:14-16](/bible/james/2#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/james/2#v14)</sup> What doth it profit, my brethren, though a man say he hath faith, and have not works? can faith save him?
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/james/2#v15)</sup> If a brother or sister be naked, and destitute of daily food,
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/james/2#v16)</sup> And one of you say unto them, Depart in peace, be ye warmed and filled; notwithstanding ye give them not those things which are needful to the body; what doth it profit?

- Part of the elders’ job is to set up organizational foundations: foundations in our knowledge, resting on Christ as the cornerstone, but also literal buildings and institutions where people can see the works of the teaching. Tell someone in the street to repent and he will say, the gospel sounds great, but where is the sanctuary at? Where is your school, your institution? You can’t tell him to go to the internet. Back in 2015, when we dealt with the building in New York, the Bishop said any leader that is not forwardly thinking about building and directing institutions is a waste of time. As a man thinks, you are supposed to see some action from it. Let me see the fruits of your labor, not a whole life of nothing but words. And anything, women, men, friends, family, that threatens that, you separate yourself from it.
- “What doth it profit, my brethren, though a man say he hath faith, and have not works? can faith save him?” Can words save him? Because that is all you are giving them. Faith alone just says, I believe in something. You can have faith in your head all day long, underneath your skull, but I can’t see it. The only way I will know what is in your mind is when I see action as a result of your thinking.
- So if you have faith but no works to validate that faith, how can you say you have faith? There is no such thing as faith without its validation. You have faith in your head and you ain’t making no moves; it might as well not even be there. Faith is only validated by action. As a man thinketh, he becomes what he thinks, and once you see what he becomes you already know what was in his head.
- The churches use faith like it is just a little thing: just believe in white Jesus. That ain’t going to get you nowhere but dead. Can faith alone save him? No.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hebrews 11:1](/bible/hebrews/11#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/hebrews/11#v1)</sup> Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen.

    Some people don’t even understand what faith really is. “Now faith is the substance.” Faith is a what? The substance. So don’t tell me you got faith: faith is the substance, and substance is what you can touch, something you can see, something materialized. Even if he or she has not achieved it yet, you can tell they are on their way by how they carry themselves.

**[James 2:17](/bible/james/2#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/james/2#v17)</sup> Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone.

- “Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone.” That’s crystal clear. Faith does not exist if works are not attached to it. When anybody talks faith, faith, faith with no works behind it, they are not even telling you about faith; they are lying to you, they don’t have faith at all.
- So our faith is demonstrated by maintaining institutions of learning, where our people are fed spiritually and also learn to take care of themselves physically, so that we are not destitute of food or of clothing, but take care of each other the way the elders had the brethren pool what they had extra at the apostles’ feet to take care of the body. Seek the kingdom and all these things will be added. The Lord who clothes the grass of the field and feeds the fowls of the air will take care of us; the real faith, if you believe it, makes stuff happen.
- That is what the works of IUIC show: sisters teaching modesty, self-worth, marriage and broken homes to girls in Africa, and a young girl there fully engaged, listening. If we were just a group that talked about faith and no works, this word would never have got to her. All of the good works done all over are exactly because of the works. The faith we talk about is validated by the works you see in people changing their lives.
- “Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone.” In the same way, faith with no works does not exist. Abraham had faith and kept the commandments of God, and by keeping the commandments of God he proved his faith.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Esdras 9:6-7](/bible/2-esdras/9#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/2-esdras/9#v6)</sup> Even so the times also of the Highest have plain beginnings in wonders and powerful works, and endings in effects and signs.
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/2-esdras/9#v7)</sup> And every one that shall be saved, and shall be able to escape by his works, and by faith, whereby ye have believed,

    People say, the law can’t save you; you’re not justified by the works of the law. Read it: “And every one that shall be saved, and shall be able to escape by his works, and by faith, whereby ye have believed”. Escape by his works, and by faith.
  - **[Revelation 14:12](/bible/revelation/14#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/revelation/14#v12)</sup> Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus.

    “Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus.” Keep the commandments, and the faith of Jesus. The faith is one, and the commandments are the works, as James said and as Esdras said.
  - **[Revelation 22:14](/bible/revelation/22#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/revelation/22#v14)</sup> Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city.

    “Blessed are they that do his commandments”: do them, deeds, actions, works, “that they may have right to the tree of life”. To enter through the gates into the city they must do the commandments and have faith in Christ. They go hand in hand.

**[James 2:18](/bible/james/2#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/james/2#v18)</sup> Yea, a man may say, Thou hast faith, and I have works: shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works.

- “Yea, a man may say, Thou hast faith, and I have works”. In response: “shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works.” You cannot show faith without works. You show your faith through your actions, by keeping the commandments of God, as John says in Revelation 14:12 and 22:14, and as Esdras says in 2 Esdras 9:7.
- “Shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works.” A true leader shows you his faith by his works. As the Bishop said years ago, if your Israelite leader is not about building institutions all over the world, you are following the wrong person, because we understand where this truth is going and what is about to happen in the end times. Make not thyself an underling to a foolish man: if your leader is not about prepping for what is coming, not about prophecy, marriage, order and discipline, you are in the wrong place.
- Our forefathers are the examples: David led by example when he killed Goliath, and Paul led by example through the persecutions he went through.

  Precepts:
  - **[Luke 22:26-27](/bible/luke/22#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/luke/22#v26)</sup> But ye shall not be so: but he that is greatest among you, let him be as the younger; and he that is chief, as he that doth serve.
    >
    > <sup>[27](/bible/luke/22#v27)</sup> For whether is greater, he that sitteth at meat, or he that serveth? is not he that sitteth at meat? but I am among you as he that serveth.

    “He that is greatest among you, let him be as the younger; and he that is chief, as he that doth serve.” “I am among you as he that serveth.” If Christ is serving, what do you have to be on your high horse about?

**[James 2:19-20](/bible/james/2#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/james/2#v19)</sup> Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/james/2#v20)</sup> But wilt thou know, O vain man, that faith without works is dead?

- “The devils also believe, and tremble”: even the devils, unclean spirits, believe in God and are afraid. “But wilt thou know, O vain man, that faith without works is dead?” The vain man is the follower of Christianity, faith without works: Calvinism, Protestantism, Catholicism.

**[James 2:21-23](/bible/james/2#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/james/2#v21)</sup> Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar?
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/james/2#v22)</sup> Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect?
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/james/2#v23)</sup> And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God.

- “Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar?” Those were his actions, and his works proved his faith. “Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect?” Faith worked with his actions, and by his works his faith was perfected: he was proven faithful. “Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness”, as Galatians 3:6 says, “and he was called the Friend of God.”

**[James 2:24](/bible/james/2#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/james/2#v24)</sup> Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only.

- “Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only.” Back to Romans 2:13: the doers of the law shall be justified.

**[James 2:25](/bible/james/2#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/james/2#v25)</sup> Likewise also was not Rahab the harlot justified by works, when she had received the messengers, and had sent them out another way?

- “Was not Rahab the harlot justified by works”: she believed that if she helped the spies escape, they would spare her life. That was her faith, and helping them was her works, so as not to be killed: “when she had received the messengers, and had sent them out another way”. She saved them.

**[James 2:26](/bible/james/2#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/james/2#v26)</sup> For as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also.

- “For as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also.” You have to keep the commandments and the faith of Christ. Each goes with the other.
