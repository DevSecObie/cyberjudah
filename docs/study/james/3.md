## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on James 3 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1, 3-8, 10, 13-16, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[James 3:1](/bible/james/3#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/james/3#v1)</sup> My brethren, be not many masters, knowing that we shall receive the greater condemnation.

- Masters are the teachers, the men entrusted with leading the people. This is more than possessing authority or a title. “knowing that we shall receive the greater condemnation.” The more responsibility and understanding a man professes to have, the more he is accountable for how he uses them. Misusing authority, exploiting the vulnerable, hypocrisy and leading people astray bring a stricter judgment upon those who influence others.
- Leadership carries an unseen weight: sleepless concern, mental strain, decisions and responsibility for other souls. The crown is a trial of the man who wears it. Worldly examples of leadership may illustrate something useful, but they must come under the scriptures; admiration for success, celebrity or power can carry vanity and carnal ambition into the work of God. The burden is not an inconvenience to resent. It is the additional weight a leader must learn to bear.

  Precepts:
  - **[Luke 12:48](/bible/luke/12#v48)**
    > <sup>[48](/bible/luke/12#v48)</sup> But he that knew not, and did commit things worthy of stripes, shall be beaten with few stripes. For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required: and to whom men have committed much, of him they will ask the more.

    “For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required:” Even the man who did not know is accountable and receives stripes, but the one entrusted with more is asked for more. We already bear responsibility for our own sins and the sins of our forefathers. Having understanding shown to us increases the obligation to move rightly, both individually and as the body and nation of Israel in this wilderness.

**[James 3:3](/bible/james/3#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/james/3#v3)</sup> Behold, we put bits in the horses’ mouths, that they may obey us; and we turn about their whole body.

- All of these things could be alleviated if we just learn to control our mouths. “Behold, we put bits in the horses’ mouths, that they may obey us; and we turn about their whole body.” A horse is heavy, all that muscle, and you control the whole horse by a little bit in its mouth. That little thing has so much effect over the whole body. That is the tongue.
- “we turn about their whole body.” The bit directs the horse. In leading our people, the bit is the word of God placed in their mouths so that their whole course can turn away from the carnal self and the old man, toward a refreshed soul and a renewed mind. That is the power carried in counsel and guidance, and it can be wielded responsibly or irresponsibly.
- The Pharisees show the misuse of that authority. In the history of the Maccabees there were leaders ready to make friendship with the Greeks and others who stood boldly for Christ. Following the status quo through the wide gate is easy; leading people through the narrow way is harder.
- Five burdens accompany that work: awareness of things that cannot be unseen; judgment that must be right rather than driven by feeling; empathy for what people endure; accountability for one's own conduct and counsel; and refinement, in which leadership changes the man himself. Giving the wrong judgment can turn a whole body the wrong way. Removing personal emotion does not remove compassion, but it lets the scriptural judgment remain clear.

**[James 3:4](/bible/james/3#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/james/3#v4)</sup> Behold also the ships, which though they be so great, and are driven of fierce winds, yet are they turned about with a very small helm, whithersoever the governor listeth.

- “Yet are they turned about with a very small helm, whithersoever the governor listeth”: a cruise ship or a warship, steered by a little thing in the water and a little wheel. The same with the tongue.

**[James 3:5](/bible/james/3#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/james/3#v5)</sup> Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth!

- “Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth”. James is speaking of the power of the tongue.
- “Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth!” Your tongue can mess up a whole lot. It has that much power, so guard it. Be careful what comes out of it, so you don't tarnish your name or ruin your situation or somebody else's.

  Precepts:
  - **[Proverbs 18:20-21](/bible/proverbs/18#v20)**
    > <sup>[20](/bible/proverbs/18#v20)</sup> A man’s belly shall be satisfied with the fruit of his mouth; and with the increase of his lips shall he be filled.
    >
    > <sup>[21](/bible/proverbs/18#v21)</sup> Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof.

    “A man’s belly shall be satisfied with the fruit of his mouth.” “Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof.” The tongue can get you killed or give life. Be mindful, because your mouth can either get you rewarded or get you into a whole lot of trouble.
  - **[Psalms 34:13-14](/bible/psalms/34#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/psalms/34#v13)</sup> Keep thy tongue from evil, and thy lips from speaking guile.
    >
    > <sup>[14](/bible/psalms/34#v14)</sup> Depart from evil, and do good; seek peace, and pursue it.

    “Keep thy tongue from evil, and thy lips from speaking guile.” Ruling over your tongue takes practice, especially for those who don't know they have no control. They think it's normal to murmur, to complain, to talk about everybody else's problems and business; it has been normalized, they watched their mama do it. It is not normal, not right, not how we are supposed to conduct ourselves. “Depart from evil, and do good; seek peace, and pursue it.”
  - **[Proverbs 21:23-24](/bible/proverbs/21#v23)**
    > <sup>[23](/bible/proverbs/21#v23)</sup> Whoso keepeth his mouth and his tongue keepeth his soul from troubles.
    >
    > <sup>[24](/bible/proverbs/21#v24)</sup> Proud and haughty scorner is his name, who dealeth in proud wrath.

    “Whoso keepeth his mouth and his tongue keepeth his soul from troubles. Proud and haughty scorner is his name, who dealeth in proud wrath.” Learn to control your mouth and you keep yourself from troubles. Some have dodged many bullets because they held their tongue or stayed out of something, never knowing the world of hurt they avoided. Get good at holding your tongue and you will never know what the one who can't control his mouth has to go through.

**[James 3:6](/bible/james/3#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/james/3#v6)</sup> And the tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity: so is the tongue among our members, that it defileth the whole body, and setteth on fire the course of nature; and it is set on fire of hell.

- “And the tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity”: a world of sin. “So is the tongue among our members, that it defileth the whole body”. The thing you say can defile the whole body, with your mouth. It “setteth on fire the course of nature; and it is set on fire of hell.”
- Stand a hundred men here and pass one word around; by the time it reaches the hundredth man it has already changed. That is why the tongue sets on fire. Be careful what comes out of your mouth.

**[James 3:7](/bible/james/3#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/james/3#v7)</sup> For every kind of beasts, and of birds, and of serpents, and of things in the sea, is tamed, and hath been tamed of mankind:

- “For every kind of beasts, and of birds, and of serpents, and of things in the sea, is tamed, and hath been tamed of mankind”. Man tames everything, even the lion in the jungle.

**[James 3:8](/bible/james/3#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/james/3#v8)</sup> But the tongue can no man tame; it is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.

- “But the tongue can no man tame; it is an unruly evil”. You have the power on your tongue to build and the power on your tongue to destroy. What are you going to use it for?
- As a man of God, the same tongue you use to tell brothers and sisters to repent, you find in conversation you are not supposed to be in, and some don’t even catch themselves. Some are very disrespectful to each other. It cannot be so in here. We deal with that disrespect outside; in IUIC there is order, and everybody has to follow that order. We are raising up respectful men, and we must respect each other.

**[James 3:10](/bible/james/3#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/james/3#v10)</sup> Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be.

- Against the camps that put curses on their brethren, the apostle James says: “Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be.” You can’t bless and curse out of the same mouth. Don’t do that. And those of you who follow such men are going to get the same judgment.

**[James 3:13](/bible/james/3#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/james/3#v13)</sup> Who is a wise man and endued with knowledge among you? let him shew out of a good conversation his works with meekness of wisdom.

- “Who is a wise man and endued with knowledge among you? let him shew out of a good conversation his works with meekness of wisdom.” This is exactly the point: works are actions. That is great moral character. Wisdom shows up in your character, your discipline, your honesty, your consistency, and how you move when nobody is watching; a brother's wisdom and discernment show in how he deals with people, with his wife and children, and when he gets corrected.

**[James 3:14](/bible/james/3#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/james/3#v14)</sup> But if ye have bitter envying and strife in your hearts, glory not, and lie not against the truth.

- “But if ye have bitter envying and strife in your hearts.” Not just envy: bitter envy. Bitter means resentful: feeling bitterness or indignation at having been treated unfairly. They feel they are not getting a fair shake, that they were dealt with wrong. That is the mind corrupted, building up resentment out of envy: why does he have her, she should be my wife; why does he have that job, that house; why is he the elder? A man bitter like that is effeminate.
- This class was for edification. We have to make sure we do not roll in these spirits, that we do not have anger and hatred inside our hearts, and that the root of bitterness does not spring up and defile us. And we pray that these brothers from the other congregations repent too. If not, that is your lot, and you are doomed to die in your evil.
- “But if ye have bitter envying and strife in your hearts, glory not, and lie not against the truth.” You see the marvellous works in Israel United in Christ. You see the spirit of the Lord on the bishops to do what they do. You see the fruits of our spirit and of our work. Do not deny the truth because of your hatred, your envy, your malice.
- “But if ye have bitter envying and strife in your hearts, glory not, and lie not against the truth.” The Lord did not put the spirit of strife and envy against one another in us. People pick up these traits and they become their moral character. That is why their name is always in some ish: the sister whose name always comes up, the brother always dropping the ball on his task. If that hits home and some are mad, I don't give a damn. It is what it is. Many answered the questions at the Marriage is Honorable politically correct rather than honestly. The one sister who answered honestly, even though it was not the right answer, can be dealt with and can grow; the politically correct answer hides where you really are.

**[James 3:15-16](/bible/james/3#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/james/3#v15)</sup> This wisdom descendeth not from above, but is earthly, sensual, devilish.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/james/3#v16)</sup> For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.

- “This wisdom descendeth not from above, but is earthly, sensual, devilish. For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.” These are not the characteristics we want associated with us. That wisdom did not come from the Lord; it was picked up through life in the world.
- That is why we have to be around men who have been through these things and can teach us to be better men, better leaders, better husbands and better fathers. If you are not around such people, how can you learn these moral and ethical traits, work in integrity and build your character? Couples who came back from the Marriage is Honorable must keep the same energy and not backtrack to the negative; that would be not upholding their integrity.
- “This wisdom descendeth not from above, but is earthly, sensual, devilish.” It will convince you, it feels good, and it will make you justify your actions. But the base behind it is envy.
- “This wisdom descendeth not from above”: that spirit on you is not from the Lord. It “is earthly, sensual, devilish”: from the damn devil, Satan, your father.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 27:2](/bible/sirach/27#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/27#v2)</sup> As a nail sticketh fast between the joinings of the stones; so doth sin stick close between buying and selling.

    “As a nail sticketh fast between the joinings of the stones; so doth sin stick close between buying and selling.” Sin is never too far from business. If it is not built on the scriptures, it is most likely not of God; there is gain involved.
