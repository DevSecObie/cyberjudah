## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on James 1 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-9, 12-27, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[James 1:1](/bible/james/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/james/1#v1)</sup> James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes which are scattered abroad, greeting.

- “James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes which are scattered abroad, greeting.” The letter is to the twelve tribes that were scattered abroad, the ones that were living as sinners, serving gods of wood and stone. Living that way, we were spotted.
- So count it joy when God starts showing you who you are. To live an unspotted life in this world, we have to learn what we were doing wrong so we can fix it. So why are you mad? That is the whole purpose.
- “James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes which are scattered abroad.” Paul said our twelve tribes, and James writes to the twelve tribes: Paul and James are on one accord. So how is it that they say Paul is teaching don’t keep the commandments, but they don’t accuse James of that?

  Precepts:
  - **[Deuteronomy 32:26](/bible/deuteronomy/32#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/deuteronomy/32#v26)</sup> I said, I would scatter them into corners, I would make the remembrance of them to cease from among men:

    To the twelve tribes scattered abroad: “I said, I would scatter them into corners, I would make the remembrance of them to cease from among men”. God scattered Israel and made our remembrance cease.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:64](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v64)**
    > <sup>[64](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v64)</sup> And the Lord shall scatter thee among all people, from the one end of the earth even unto the other; and there thou shalt serve other gods, which neither thou nor thy fathers have known, even wood and stone.

    Scattered, we were going to serve gods of “wood and stone.” When we were serving gods of wood and stone, we were spotted. So when James writes to the twelve tribes scattered abroad, it is to the ones that were living as sinners, and they have to learn what they were doing wrong to live unspotted.

**[James 1:2-5](/bible/james/1#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/james/1#v2)</sup> My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations;
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/james/1#v3)</sup> Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/james/1#v4)</sup> But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/james/1#v5)</sup> If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.

- “My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing.” Why count it joy to fall into temptations? Because you are learning about yourself, your faults, what you have to fix. Being tried also shows you are a son of God, and the Lord sees you fit to be chastised so that you come out of the trial increased.
- Some people come to this truth and never knew they deal with a spirit of unforgiveness. They can't let somebody off the hook for something that happened ten years ago, five years ago; they still harbor it. Some leave a brother in purgatory, reminding him of something he did years ago that he hasn't done again. The scripture says all manner of sin is forgiven; you have to allow him to grow up out of it, not hold him captive to his past action. That is something you are battling, and the word of God will show you what you have to fix. That is a trying of your faith.
- Let patience have her perfect work: you have to work through it, that you may be perfect and entire, meaning whole, wanting nothing, lacking nothing. You don't want to lack any type of wisdom. When you fall into temptations, it is to build you up.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Corinthians 10:12-15, 21](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v12)</sup> Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.
    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v13)</sup> There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.
    >
    > <sup>[14](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v14)</sup> Wherefore, my dearly beloved, flee from idolatry.
    >
    > <sup>[15](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v15)</sup> I speak as to wise men; judge ye what I say.
    >
    > <sup>[21](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v21)</sup> Ye cannot drink the cup of the Lord, and the cup of devils: ye cannot be partakers of the Lord’s table, and of the table of devils.

    “Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.” A man is wise in his own eyes. Take heed lest you fall: that is walking circumspect.
  - **[2 Corinthians 2:6-11](/bible/2-corinthians/2#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/2-corinthians/2#v6)</sup> Sufficient to such a man is this punishment, which was inflicted of many.
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/2-corinthians/2#v7)</sup> So that contrariwise ye ought rather to forgive him, and comfort him, lest perhaps such a one should be swallowed up with overmuch sorrow.
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/2-corinthians/2#v8)</sup> Wherefore I beseech you that ye would confirm your love toward him.
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/2-corinthians/2#v9)</sup> For to this end also did I write, that I might know the proof of you, whether ye be obedient in all things.
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/2-corinthians/2#v10)</sup> To whom ye forgive any thing, I forgive also: for if I forgave any thing, to whom I forgave it, for your sakes forgave I it in the person of Christ;
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/2-corinthians/2#v11)</sup> Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices.

    Unforgiveness is a trying of your faith. “Sufficient to such a man is this punishment, which was inflicted of many.” He got punished for his action because he was in error. “So that contrariwise ye ought rather to forgive him, and comfort him”: on the contrary side, now that he paid his punishment, you ought to forgive him and comfort him.

**[James 1:6-8](/bible/james/1#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/james/1#v6)</sup> But let him ask in faith, nothing wavering. For he that wavereth is like a wave of the sea driven with the wind and tossed.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/james/1#v7)</sup> For let not that man think that he shall receive any thing of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/james/1#v8)</sup> A double minded man is unstable in all his ways.

- “But let him ask in faith, nothing wavering.” If you are asking for wisdom and knowledge, ask in faith. The one who wavers “is like a wave of the sea driven with the wind and tossed.” “For let not that man think that he shall receive any thing of the Lord.”
- “A double minded man is unstable in all his ways”: not some of his ways, all of his ways. If you only read once a week, there is nothing for the Lord to work with.
- “But let him ask in faith, nothing wavering. For he that wavereth is like a wave of the sea driven with the wind and tossed.” You ask in faith for God to show you about you so you can fix you, knowing that God will direct you on the right path. Then the scripture comes to you correcting you, and now you're wavering. When you waver, you are trying to dodge correction.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 4:26](/bible/sirach/4#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/sirach/4#v26)</sup> Be not ashamed to confess thy sins; and force not the course of the river.

    The wavering man is trying to dodge correction. “Be not ashamed to confess thy sins; and force not the course of the river.” Here's the river; it's flowing. You don't want the river to go that way, so you try to reroute it. Forcing the course of the river is trying to dodge correction and make it seem like it is something that it is not.
  - **[Amos 5:24](/bible/amos/5#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/amos/5#v24)</sup> But let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream.

    “But let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream.” That's a river. Let judgment run down. Let it run. Don't waver and try to get around it; don't try to force the course and divert it. Let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream.

**[James 1:9](/bible/james/1#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/james/1#v9)</sup> Let the brother of low degree rejoice in that he is exalted:

- “Let the brother of low degree rejoice in that he is exalted.” This goes back to verse 5. The brother of low degree is exalted because he is studying and reading. He may not understand, but he is praying, Lord, help my understanding, and one day it opens up: a brother who said little years ago now speaks and explains what the scriptures mean.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 11:12-13](/bible/sirach/11#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/11#v12)</sup> Again, there is another that is slow, and hath need of help, wanting ability, and full of poverty; yet the eye of the Lord looked upon him for good, and set him up from his low estate,
    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/11#v13)</sup> And lifted up his head from misery; so that many that saw it marvelled at him.

    For brothers who say they have a reading impediment and their eyes start hurting: turn on the Bible audio. Listen before you go to sleep, and the first thing you hear when you wake up is the scriptures.

**[James 1:12](/bible/james/1#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/james/1#v12)</sup> Blessed is the man that endureth temptation: for when he is tried, he shall receive the crown of life, which the Lord hath promised to them that love him.

- “Blessed is the man that endureth temptation: for when he is tried, he shall receive the crown of life, which the Lord hath promised to them that love him.” How do you know what you can endure unless you go through it? How do you know you can bench 315 until you put 315 on the rack and see if you can push it? Then you realize you can't push it yet, so you exercise some more. You're asking for muscles; well, you got to lift, you got to go to the gym, and we're going to put weight on there.
- “Blessed is the man that endureth temptation: for when he is tried, he shall receive the crown of life, which the Lord hath promised to them that love him.” Here is one way to endure temptation: turn away. Turn. Don't look.
- “Blessed is the man that endureth temptation”: you need patience, comfort of the scriptures and hope to endure temptation. Endurance means through an extended amount of time, a resistance through a process of time, whether a short sprint or a long marathon, because the demon might leave and come back. “For when he is tried”: it’s not if; the trial is going to come if you profess to be the sons and daughters of the living God. “He shall receive the crown of life”: if you pace yourself with endurance, patience and hope and finish that race, you’ll be crowned with eternal salvation.

  Precepts:
  - **[John 14:15](/bible/john/14#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/john/14#v15)</sup> If ye love me, keep my commandments.

    “Which the Lord hath promised to them that love him”. Many of us have the false imagery of love being a kiss and a hug, from the Christian church, but God tells us plainly what love is. “If ye love me, keep my commandments.” If you love the Most High you’re going to keep his commandments; plain and simple, no precepts needed.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 6:36-37](/bible/sirach/6#v36)**
    > <sup>[36](/bible/sirach/6#v36)</sup> And if thou seest a man of understanding, get thee betimes unto him, and let thy foot wear the steps of his door.
    >
    > <sup>[37](/bible/sirach/6#v37)</sup> Let thy mind be upon the ordinances of the Lord, and meditate continually in his commandments: he shall establish thine heart, and give thee wisdom at thine own desire.

    For brothers who want to be officers and captains: “And if thou seest a man of understanding, get thee betimes unto him”. Get next to a brother of understanding. If you are a soldier, get with an officer of ten or twenty; ask him how he came up the ranks. “And let thy foot wear the steps of his door”: get next to him constantly and ask questions. There are no dumb questions. That is how you learn and build yourself up.

**[James 1:13-14](/bible/james/1#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/james/1#v13)</sup> Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God: for God cannot be tempted with evil, neither tempteth he any man:
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/james/1#v14)</sup> But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed.

- “Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God: for God cannot be tempted with evil, neither tempteth he any man”. Don't blame God. Lord, it ain't me, you put that big thick big booty woman in front of me. God says, stop, bro. I put a 9 millimeter in front of you, a knife in your hand, a crackpipe in the room; I didn't pull the trigger, I didn't stab you, I didn't smoke it. So why are you blaming me?
- God doesn't tempt any man. When you're tempted, you are supposed to resist.
- “Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God.” God does not tempt you. When you are tempted, it is not God tempting you; it is what is in you already. I am not tempted with shooting myself or cutting myself or sticking my hand in fire, and I am not tempted by no dude; that's not my get down. You are only tempted with the things that you like. So if you don't like it, you can change your mind. We can train our minds to change the way we think. That is the renewing of your mind.

  Precepts:
  - **[James 4:7-8](/bible/james/4#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/james/4#v7)</sup> Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/james/4#v8)</sup> Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.

    When you're tempted, you resist. “Submit yourselves therefore to God.” Take the counsel; submit yourself to God. “Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.” How do you resist him? The beginning of verse 7: you submit yourself to God, and then you resist the devil.
  - **[Wisdom of Solomon 9:14-15](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/9#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/9#v14)</sup> For the thoughts of mortal men are miserable, and our devices are but uncertain.
    >
    > <sup>[15](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/9#v15)</sup> For the corruptible body presseth down the soul, and the earthy tabernacle weigheth down the mind that museth upon many things.

    How are we led away from doing right? “For the thoughts of mortal men are miserable, and our devices are but uncertain. For the corruptible body presseth down the soul, and the earthy tabernacle weigheth down the mind that museth upon many things.” That is how we are destroyed.

**[James 1:15](/bible/james/1#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/james/1#v15)</sup> Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.

- “Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin.” When lust has fully conceived, when his eyes and his heart are full of adultery, it brings forth sin: then you act on it. “And sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.” The end result is that you act on it.
- That is how you get men in the body so full of lust that they get down on their knees on a dirty bathroom floor to look under a stall, or glue a mirror to a shoe to look up skirts on the escalator, or walk around trying to take pictures of a woman's panty line. That is a rapist mentality. If you do that, you better stop. And trust me, if you are doing that, you have been doing so much more in your private life. You are fully gone.
- “Then when lust hath conceived”: when you entertain it. “It bringeth forth sin”: sin is action. There are steps to bring that sin to fruition: a phone conversation, something you saw on Instagram, then typing in a site you’re not supposed to be on. “And sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death”: unrepented sin you keep entertaining brings forth death; you will not inherit the kingdom of God.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 9:12](/bible/sirach/9#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/9#v12)</sup> Delight not in the thing that the ungodly have pleasure in; but remember they shall not go unpunished unto their grave.

    “Delight not in the thing that the ungodly have pleasure in.” If you find delight in that, you have to ask yourself, why am I delighted, why do I entertain something that is evil?
  - **[Matthew 5:28](/bible/matthew/5#v28)**
    > <sup>[28](/bible/matthew/5#v28)</sup> But I say unto you, That whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her hath committed adultery with her already in his heart.

    “Whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her hath committed adultery with her already in his heart.” That is how lust is conceived.

**[James 1:16](/bible/james/1#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/james/1#v16)</sup> Do not err, my beloved brethren.

- “Do not err, my beloved brethren.” Do not err. Some brothers hear this word but they are not really doers. They hear it and it sounds good. Sisters too: they hear it, but they don't really want to change.

**[James 1:17](/bible/james/1#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/james/1#v17)</sup> Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.

- “Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.” Every good gift comes down from God, and here is a good gift: wisdom. If you lack it, God will give it to you.
- With God there is no variableness. He is not tossed to and fro; he is not double-minded; it's straight. There is a constancy with God and his wisdom. It is pure, undefiled. You don't have to add to it, take away from it or change it; it is always going to be constant. But with man there is variableness.

**[James 1:18](/bible/james/1#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/james/1#v18)</sup> Of his own will begat he us with the word of truth, that we should be a kind of firstfruits of his creatures.

- “Of his own will begat he us with the word of truth, that we should be a kind of firstfruits of his creatures.” Of God's own will he begat us with the word of truth. We were formed in his image, in his likeness, to be righteous.
- The literal firstfruits given to God had to be perfect. When you brought an animal, it could not be spotted. So we are to keep ourselves unspotted.
- “Of his own will begat he us with the word of truth, that we should be a kind of firstfruits of his creatures.” As you get right you are removing the spots, so that you can be firstfruits to God. That is what we have to become: without spot, without blemish.

  Precepts:
  - **[Wisdom of Solomon 7:25-28, 30](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/7#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/7#v25)</sup> For she is the breath of the power of God, and a pure influence flowing from the glory of the Almighty: therefore can no defiled thing fall into her.
    >
    > <sup>[26](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/7#v26)</sup> For she is the brightness of the everlasting light, the unspotted mirror of the power of God, and the image of his goodness.
    >
    > <sup>[27](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/7#v27)</sup> And being but one, she can do all things: and remaining in herself, she maketh all things new: and in all ages entering into holy souls, she maketh them friends of God, and prophets.
    >
    > <sup>[28](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/7#v28)</sup> For God loveth none but him that dwelleth with wisdom.
    >
    > <sup>[30](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/7#v30)</sup> For after this cometh night: but vice shall not prevail against wisdom.

    It is not impossible to be incorruptible, unspotted and without blemish, but we need the spirit of Christ. “For she is the breath of the power of God”: this is speaking of Christ, the wisdom and the power of God. “And a pure influence flowing from the glory of the Almighty: therefore can no defiled thing fall into her.” When we are following Christ and Christ is working through us, no defiled thing can flow through.

**[James 1:19](/bible/james/1#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/james/1#v19)</sup> Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath:

- “Let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath”: this is how we get unspotted. God sends teachers to show us how to get right, and we get cleansed, we get fixed, because we are swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath.
- “Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath.” If you are listening you are not talking. Be swift to hear and slow to speak, and don't get mad when you hear it. You are not making excuses; you are fixing yourself and getting right.
- Be quick to listen and slow to speak or become angry. Those are practical restraints on the carnal reaction, particularly when pressure rises in the household. An angry man at home carries that same spirit into the congregation.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 32:7-9](/bible/sirach/32#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/sirach/32#v7)</sup> Speak, young man, if there be need of thee: and yet scarcely when thou art twice asked.
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/sirach/32#v8)</sup> Let thy speech be short, comprehending much in few words; be as one that knoweth and yet holdeth his tongue.
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/sirach/32#v9)</sup> If thou be among great men, make not thyself equal with them; and when ancient men are in place, use not many words.

    “Let thy speech be short, comprehending much in few words;” Leadership includes knowing when to speak, what to say and how to say it concisely. The young man is told to speak when needed, and even one who knows must be able to hold his tongue. Do not make yourself equal among great men by finishing their sentences or constantly affirming them; use few words before ancient men.

**[James 1:20-21](/bible/james/1#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/james/1#v20)</sup> For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/james/1#v21)</sup> Wherefore lay apart all filthiness and superfluity of naughtiness, and receive with meekness the engrafted word, which is able to save your souls.

- “Wherefore lay apart all filthiness and superfluity of naughtiness.” Superfluity is an excessive amount, an oversupply, an abundance of something far beyond what is needed. That is people who have no self-control: the man who stands at the escalator in the mall all day trying to look up, the man who walks past women in tight spaces trying to rub against them.
- “And receive with meekness the engrafted word.” To engraft is to join or fix something so deeply and securely that it becomes a permanent, functioning part of a new whole. The word of God is supposed to be engrafted in our minds, permanent, seared into our minds, so that we do the right thing and move right. If your mind is full of adultery and fornication, the word of God is not engrafted in there, because you have been exercising yourself in all the wrong things. Exercise yourself in the word of God so that it becomes one with your thoughts. For the word of God to have power it has to become a verb in your life, an action you are doing, or you are just reading words on a page. Engrafted, it becomes like a knee-jerk reaction.
- To lay apart is to repent and put it away from you: mortify, discipline your members on the earth. Meekness is to humble yourself down. Don't be like a lawyer, making justification in your mind for your actions: I'm going through it, nobody knows, it's hard, my wife makes me mad and I need somebody to talk to. Nobody cares; the scripture says what it says. Either you do it and prove to God you love it, or you don't. Your heart is desperately wicked and there is no justification for this. We must keep the commandments. You can study all the prophecies and all the deep breakdowns, but if you don't have the word of God concerning this engrafted in your mind, you are just making noise.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiastes 7:9](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v9)</sup> Be not hasty in thy spirit to be angry: for anger resteth in the bosom of fools.

    “Be not hasty in thy spirit to be angry: for anger resteth in the bosom of fools.” Anger resting in the bosom means it is always ready to come out as the default response. That is a childish reaction rather than the conduct of a mature man. It reinforces why carnal wrath cannot be the method for ruling either house or congregation.

**[James 1:22](/bible/james/1#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/james/1#v22)</sup> But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.

- “But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.” You can't just hear it; you have to do it. That's the only way it works: you have to apply the word that you've heard. And if you heard it, somebody had to be speaking for you to hear it.
- You deceive yourself if you ain't going to do what you heard. You're lying to yourself, going through the motions like you're in the truth. You're not in the truth.
- The battle begins right here. When you start to hear it, you reason within yourself, and when your mind will not accept what you heard, right is coming in but wrong is there. That's a war.

  Precepts:
  - **[James 4:1](/bible/james/4#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/james/4#v1)</sup> From whence come wars and fightings among you? come they not hence, even of your lusts that war in your members?

    Hearing but not accepting is a battle, a war. “From whence come wars and fightings among you? come they not hence, even of your lusts that war in your members?” That's where the fight comes from. Something is at war inside of you because you're being told something, and something in you is adverse to correction; you don't want to accept it.
  - **[James 3:15-16](/bible/james/3#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/james/3#v15)</sup> This wisdom descendeth not from above, but is earthly, sensual, devilish.
    >
    > <sup>[16](/bible/james/3#v16)</sup> For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.

    “For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.” The word of God is pure; there is no variableness to it, and there should be no confusion in what's being said. If you were really unsure, you would say, what did I lie about? Show me the lie; help me understand. Instead you're confused. That's the war happening inside: right is coming in and you're warring with it because you don't want to accept it.

**[James 1:23-24](/bible/james/1#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/james/1#v23)</sup> For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass:
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/james/1#v24)</sup> For he beholdeth himself, and goeth his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was.

- “For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass: For he beholdeth himself, and goeth his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was.” That glass is the Bible. You look at yourself when you read the book: wow, this is me, I have to fix it. But the forgetful hearer sees himself and forgets what manner of man God was trying to show him he was.
- You look in a mirror and see a big booger in your nose. You don't like how it looks, so you turn your head away. You can't see it no more, but is it still there? You can turn your head, but the booger ain't going nowhere. You can close this book and you're still a sinner; close it and you're still a thief. You don't want to see yourself, because when you see yourself you see how evil we really are, and people don't want to deal with that.
- So leave if you want. Nobody's going to be here holding your hand. You got a better plan; let's see how it works out for you. But if you go, you are drawn away of your own lust.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 42:7](/bible/sirach/42#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/sirach/42#v7)</sup> Deliver all things in number and weight; and put all in writing that thou givest out, or receivest in.

    “Deliver all things in number and weight; and put all in writing”. The verse speaks of something else, but put it in writing. When you receive correction, when you identify a trait that is against what you want to be, write it down.
  - **[Proverbs 14:8](/bible/proverbs/14#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/proverbs/14#v8)</sup> The wisdom of the prudent is to understand his way: but the folly of fools is deceit.

    Brothers point out something you always do. Two brothers kept doing the same thing to me, and I thought, why do they keep messing with me? They had nothing against me: there was a flaw I kept repeating. So I had to write it down, program the outcome I wanted, map out what to correct and how, and put it into practice, until they said, you don't say anything anymore. I trained myself not to react, because I understood what they were trying to teach me about myself. Write it into existence, practice it, and it becomes your growth. That's leveling up.

**[James 1:25](/bible/james/1#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/james/1#v25)</sup> But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed.

- “But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed.” He is not a forgetful hearer of what he was taught. He applies what he was taught, and he gets a blessing.
- “But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed.” He is blessed because he is applying what he learned. Prior to applying these things in our lives, we had a blot, a spot.
- “But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty”: that is what we do every day. We look into the perfect law of liberty, which is the commandments, the mercy of God and the grace of God, given to us to build upon. This is the pattern: we build upon the grace. The liberty we have is that Christ died on a cross and gave us the platform, the grace, to build upon, so we can no longer go backwards; we have to go forward.

    This is the work of Satan, for those who are sleeping: “Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour.” Your adversary doesn't sleep. You think Satan sleeps for a minute? He doesn't.

**[James 1:26](/bible/james/1#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/james/1#v26)</sup> If any man among you seem to be religious, and bridleth not his tongue, but deceiveth his own heart, this man’s religion is vain.

- “If any man among you seem to be religious, and bridleth not his tongue, but deceiveth his own heart, this man’s religion is vain.” A man may seem convicted in what he believes: I believe this Bible, the real Jews are black, Christ is black, we're going to get the kingdom. But he doesn't know when to shut up, and he deceives his own heart with excuse after excuse.
- His religion is vain; it ain't going to get him nothing. You can put on the shirt, put on the fringes, grow a beard, stop eating pork, but if you're not willing to deal with what you've got going on in your head and be honest about it, you're wasting your time. You might as well go back to the world.

**[James 1:27](/bible/james/1#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/james/1#v27)</sup> Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world.

- “Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world.” We all fall short, so to be unspotted we have to learn what we were doing wrong. The world is all messed up, and we have to be unspotted.
- Now that we're unspotted, what are we supposed to do? Teach. We had a guide that directed us in the right way; without that guide we wouldn't have it. The affliction of the fatherless and widows goes back to the temptations. What are they lacking? A guide. They don't have a hedge, they don't have a father, they don't have anybody to lead them. Now that you're unspotted because you learned about yourself, it's time to go out and help people in the same thing so they can become unspotted.
- But how are you going to teach them if they're not swift to hear and slow to speak? How can you help them if they get mad and dodge every time you tell them something?
