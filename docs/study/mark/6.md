## Introduction

This session covers Mark 6 through 9. Christ is rejected in his own country, sends the twelve out two by two, and the chapter turns to the beheading of John the Baptist. Then come the feeding of the five thousand and the four thousand, the walking on the sea, the contention over the tradition of the elders, the confession of Peter, the transfiguration, and the casting out of the dumb spirit. The thread through all four chapters is belief and unbelief, and how plainly Christ dealt with men who would not see.

**[Mark 6:1-4](/bible/mark/6#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/mark/6#v1)</sup> And he went out from thence, and came into his own country; and his disciples follow him.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/mark/6#v2)</sup> And when the sabbath day was come, he began to teach in the synagogue: and many hearing him were astonished, saying, From whence hath this man these things? and what wisdom is this which is given unto him, that even such mighty works are wrought by his hands?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/mark/6#v3)</sup> Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary, the brother of James, and Joses, and of Juda, and Simon? and are not his sisters here with us? And they were offended at him.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/mark/6#v4)</sup> But Jesus said unto them, A prophet is not without honour, but in his own country, and among his own kin, and in his own house.

- In his own country they are astonished at his wisdom and his mighty works, and then are offended at him because they know his family.
- A prophet is not without honour, but in his own country, and among his own kin, and in his own house. What was done to him is done to those teaching today: men who knew you before you repented ask who you think you are.

**[Mark 6:5-6](/bible/mark/6#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/mark/6#v5)</sup> And he could there do no mighty work, save that he laid his hands upon a few sick folk, and healed them.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/mark/6#v6)</sup> And he marvelled because of their unbelief. And he went round about the villages, teaching.

- He could there do no mighty work, save that he laid his hands upon a few sick folk and healed them, and he marvelled because of their unbelief.
- Consider what that means. The unbelief of his own people was such that it made the son of the Most High marvel.

**[Mark 6:7-13](/bible/mark/6#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/mark/6#v7)</sup> And he called unto him the twelve, and began to send them forth by two and two; and gave them power over unclean spirits;
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/mark/6#v8)</sup> And commanded them that they should take nothing for their journey, save a staff only; no scrip, no bread, no money in their purse:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/mark/6#v9)</sup> But be shod with sandals; and not put on two coats.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/mark/6#v10)</sup> And he said unto them, In what place soever ye enter into an house, there abide till ye depart from that place.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/mark/6#v11)</sup> And whosoever shall not receive you, nor hear you, when ye depart thence, shake off the dust under your feet for a testimony against them. Verily I say unto you, It shall be more tolerable for Sodom and Gomorrha in the day of judgment, than for that city.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/mark/6#v12)</sup> And they went out, and preached that men should repent.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/mark/6#v13)</sup> And they cast out many devils, and anointed with oil many that were sick, and healed them.

- He sends them forth by two and two with power over unclean spirits, taking nothing for the journey but a staff.
- Whosoever will not receive you nor hear you, shake off the dust under your feet for a testimony against them. The word goes out whether they hear or whether they forbear, and that shaking of the dust seals the judgment.

**[Mark 6:14-18](/bible/mark/6#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/mark/6#v14)</sup> And king Herod heard of him; (for his name was spread abroad:) and he said, That John the Baptist was risen from the dead, and therefore mighty works do shew forth themselves in him.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/mark/6#v15)</sup> Others said, That it is Elias. And others said, That it is a prophet, or as one of the prophets.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/mark/6#v16)</sup> But when Herod heard thereof, he said, It is John, whom I beheaded: he is risen from the dead.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/mark/6#v17)</sup> For Herod himself had sent forth and laid hold upon John, and bound him in prison for Herodias’ sake, his brother Philip’s wife: for he had married her.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/mark/6#v18)</sup> For John had said unto Herod, It is not lawful for thee to have thy brother’s wife.

- Herod hears of him and says it is John, whom he beheaded, risen from the dead.
- John had told Herod plainly, It is not lawful for thee to have thy brother's wife. Herod knew the law well enough to be accused by it, because those rulers were schooled in the customs of Israel.

- Herodias had a quarrel against John and would have killed him, but could not, for Herod feared him, knowing that he was a just man and an holy.
- When the oath is made, the daughter asks her mother what to ask, and is told, The head of John the Baptist. The king was exceeding sorry, but for his oath's sake he sent the executioner.

**[Mark 6:30-44](/bible/mark/6#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/mark/6#v30)</sup> And the apostles gathered themselves together unto Jesus, and told him all things, both what they had done, and what they had taught.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/mark/6#v31)</sup> And he said unto them, Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while: for there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/mark/6#v32)</sup> And they departed into a desert place by ship privately.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/mark/6#v33)</sup> And the people saw them departing, and many knew him, and ran afoot thither out of all cities, and outwent them, and came together unto him.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/mark/6#v34)</sup> And Jesus, when he came out, saw much people, and was moved with compassion toward them, because they were as sheep not having a shepherd: and he began to teach them many things.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/mark/6#v35)</sup> And when the day was now far spent, his disciples came unto him, and said, This is a desert place, and now the time is far passed:
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/mark/6#v36)</sup> Send them away, that they may go into the country round about, and into the villages, and buy themselves bread: for they have nothing to eat.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/mark/6#v37)</sup> He answered and said unto them, Give ye them to eat. And they say unto him, Shall we go and buy two hundred pennyworth of bread, and give them to eat?
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/mark/6#v38)</sup> He saith unto them, How many loaves have ye? go and see. And when they knew, they say, Five, and two fishes.
>
> <sup>[39](/bible/mark/6#v39)</sup> And he commanded them to make all sit down by companies upon the green grass.
>
> <sup>[40](/bible/mark/6#v40)</sup> And they sat down in ranks, by hundreds, and by fifties.
>
> <sup>[41](/bible/mark/6#v41)</sup> And when he had taken the five loaves and the two fishes, he looked up to heaven, and blessed, and brake the loaves, and gave them to his disciples to set before them; and the two fishes divided he among them all.
>
> <sup>[42](/bible/mark/6#v42)</sup> And they did all eat, and were filled.
>
> <sup>[43](/bible/mark/6#v43)</sup> And they took up twelve baskets full of the fragments, and of the fishes.
>
> <sup>[44](/bible/mark/6#v44)</sup> And they that did eat of the loaves were about five thousand men.

- He was moved with compassion toward them, because they were as sheep not having a shepherd, and he began to teach them many things.
- Five loaves and two fishes feed five thousand men, and twelve baskets of fragments are taken up.
- He made them sit down by companies, in ranks by hundreds and by fifties. No man argued about where he was placed. The Most High is a God of order, and the eating came after the order.

**[Mark 6:45-52](/bible/mark/6#v45)**

> <sup>[45](/bible/mark/6#v45)</sup> And straightway he constrained his disciples to get into the ship, and to go to the other side before unto Bethsaida, while he sent away the people.
>
> <sup>[46](/bible/mark/6#v46)</sup> And when he had sent them away, he departed into a mountain to pray.
>
> <sup>[47](/bible/mark/6#v47)</sup> And when even was come, the ship was in the midst of the sea, and he alone on the land.
>
> <sup>[48](/bible/mark/6#v48)</sup> And he saw them toiling in rowing; for the wind was contrary unto them: and about the fourth watch of the night he cometh unto them, walking upon the sea, and would have passed by them.
>
> <sup>[49](/bible/mark/6#v49)</sup> But when they saw him walking upon the sea, they supposed it had been a spirit, and cried out:
>
> <sup>[50](/bible/mark/6#v50)</sup> For they all saw him, and were troubled. And immediately he talked with them, and saith unto them, Be of good cheer: it is I; be not afraid.
>
> <sup>[51](/bible/mark/6#v51)</sup> And he went up unto them into the ship; and the wind ceased: and they were sore amazed in themselves beyond measure, and wondered.
>
> <sup>[52](/bible/mark/6#v52)</sup> For they considered not the miracle of the loaves: for their heart was hardened.

- He comes to them walking upon the sea, and they suppose it had been a spirit and cry out. These were fishermen; they knew what water does.
- They were sore amazed, for they considered not the miracle of the loaves, for their heart was hardened. Men can watch a thing done in front of them and still not take it to heart.
