**[Mark 7:1-5](/bible/mark/7#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/mark/7#v1)</sup> Then came together unto him the Pharisees, and certain of the scribes, which came from Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/mark/7#v2)</sup> And when they saw some of his disciples eat bread with defiled, that is to say, with unwashen, hands, they found fault.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/mark/7#v3)</sup> For the Pharisees, and all the Jews, except they wash their hands oft, eat not, holding the tradition of the elders.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/mark/7#v4)</sup> And when they come from the market, except they wash, they eat not. And many other things there be, which they have received to hold, as the washing of cups, and pots, brasen vessels, and of tables.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/mark/7#v5)</sup> Then the Pharisees and scribes asked him, Why walk not thy disciples according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with unwashen hands?

- The Pharisees and scribes find fault that the disciples eat bread with unwashen hands, holding the tradition of the elders.
- There is no law in the scriptures that a man must wash his hands before he eats. That was a tradition of the elders, and the whole contention is about tradition, not about law.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 15:2](/bible/matthew/15#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/matthew/15#v2)</sup> Why do thy disciples transgress the tradition of the elders? for they wash not their hands when they eat bread.

    The same account told in Matthew: Why do thy disciples transgress the tradition of the elders?

**[Mark 7:6-9](/bible/mark/7#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/mark/7#v6)</sup> He answered and said unto them, Well hath Esaias prophesied of you hypocrites, as it is written, This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/mark/7#v7)</sup> Howbeit in vain do they worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/mark/7#v8)</sup> For laying aside the commandment of God, ye hold the tradition of men, as the washing of pots and cups: and many other such like things ye do.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/mark/7#v9)</sup> And he said unto them, Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition.

- This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. In vain do they worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.
- Laying aside the commandment of God, ye hold the tradition of men. Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition.

**[Mark 7:10-13](/bible/mark/7#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/mark/7#v10)</sup> For Moses said, Honour thy father and thy mother; and, Whoso curseth father or mother, let him die the death:
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/mark/7#v11)</sup> But ye say, If a man shall say to his father or mother, It is Corban, that is to say, a gift, by whatsoever thou mightest be profited by me; he shall be free.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/mark/7#v12)</sup> And ye suffer him no more to do ought for his father or his mother;
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/mark/7#v13)</sup> Making the word of God of none effect through your tradition, which ye have delivered: and many such like things do ye.

- Moses said, Honour thy father and thy mother, but they had made a way for a man to call what would have profited his parents a gift, and so owe them nothing.
- The elders were allowing children to be released from their parents, and by that tradition they made the word of God of none effect.

**[Mark 7:14-16](/bible/mark/7#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/mark/7#v14)</sup> And when he had called all the people unto him, he said unto them, Hearken unto me every one of you, and understand:
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/mark/7#v15)</sup> There is nothing from without a man, that entering into him can defile him: but the things which come out of him, those are they that defile the man.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/mark/7#v16)</sup> If any man have ears to hear, let him hear.

- There is nothing from without a man, that entering into him can defile him; but the things which come out of him, those are they that defile the man.

**[Mark 7:17-23](/bible/mark/7#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/mark/7#v17)</sup> And when he was entered into the house from the people, his disciples asked him concerning the parable.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/mark/7#v18)</sup> And he saith unto them, Are ye so without understanding also? Do ye not perceive, that whatsoever thing from without entereth into the man, it cannot defile him;
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/mark/7#v19)</sup> Because it entereth not into his heart, but into the belly, and goeth out into the draught, purging all meats?
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/mark/7#v20)</sup> And he said, That which cometh out of the man, that defileth the man.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/mark/7#v21)</sup> For from within, out of the heart of men, proceed evil thoughts, adulteries, fornications, murders,
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/mark/7#v22)</sup> Thefts, covetousness, wickedness, deceit, lasciviousness, an evil eye, blasphemy, pride, foolishness:
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/mark/7#v23)</sup> All these evil things come from within, and defile the man.

- The disciples ask him concerning the parable, which shows that what he said in verse 15 was a parable and not a licence.
- The meats in view are lawful meats. What is unclean was never called meat, and it still does the body harm, which is not what purging means.
- What defiles is named out of his own mouth: evil thoughts, adulteries, fornications, murders, thefts, covetousness, wickedness, deceit, lasciviousness, an evil eye, blasphemy, pride, foolishness.

**[Mark 7:24-30](/bible/mark/7#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/mark/7#v24)</sup> And from thence he arose, and went into the borders of Tyre and Sidon, and entered into an house, and would have no man know it: but he could not be hid.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/mark/7#v25)</sup> For a certain woman, whose young daughter had an unclean spirit, heard of him, and came and fell at his feet:
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/mark/7#v26)</sup> The woman was a Greek, a Syrophenician by nation; and she besought him that he would cast forth the devil out of her daughter.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/mark/7#v27)</sup> But Jesus said unto her, Let the children first be filled: for it is not meet to take the children’s bread, and to cast it unto the dogs.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/mark/7#v28)</sup> And she answered and said unto him, Yes, Lord: yet the dogs under the table eat of the children’s crumbs.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/mark/7#v29)</sup> And he said unto her, For this saying go thy way; the devil is gone out of thy daughter.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/mark/7#v30)</sup> And when she was come to her house, she found the devil gone out, and her daughter laid upon the bed.

- The woman was a Greek, a Syrophenician by nation, and asked him to cast the devil out of her daughter.
- Let the children first be filled: for it is not meet to take the children's bread, and to cast it unto the dogs. The order is stated plainly and he does not soften it.
- She answers, Yea, Lord: yet the dogs under the table eat of the children's crumbs. She took the place the answer gave her, and for that saying her daughter was made whole.

**[Mark 7:31-37](/bible/mark/7#v31)**

> <sup>[31](/bible/mark/7#v31)</sup> And again, departing from the coasts of Tyre and Sidon, he came unto the sea of Galilee, through the midst of the coasts of Decapolis.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/mark/7#v32)</sup> And they bring unto him one that was deaf, and had an impediment in his speech; and they beseech him to put his hand upon him.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/mark/7#v33)</sup> And he took him aside from the multitude, and put his fingers into his ears, and he spit, and touched his tongue;
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/mark/7#v34)</sup> And looking up to heaven, he sighed, and saith unto him, Ephphatha, that is, Be opened.

>
> <sup>[37](/bible/mark/7#v37)</sup> And were beyond measure astonished, saying, He hath done all things well: he maketh both the deaf to hear, and the dumb to speak.

- He takes the deaf man aside from the multitude, puts his fingers into his ears, touches his tongue, and says Ephphatha, that is, Be opened.
- The more he charged them to tell no man, so much the more a great deal they published it, saying, He hath done all things well.
