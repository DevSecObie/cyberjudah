## Introduction

This session covers Mark 6 through 9. Christ is rejected in his own country, sends the twelve out two by two, and the chapter turns to the beheading of John the Baptist. Then come the feeding of the five thousand and the four thousand, the walking on the sea, the contention over the tradition of the elders, the confession of Peter, the transfiguration, and the casting out of the dumb spirit. The thread through all four chapters is belief and unbelief, and how plainly Christ dealt with men who would not see.

**[Mark 6:1-4](/bible/mark/6#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/mark/6#v1)</sup> And he went out from thence, and came into his own country; and his disciples follow him.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/mark/6#v2)</sup> And when the sabbath day was come, he began to teach in the synagogue: and many hearing him were astonished, saying, From whence hath this man these things? and what wisdom is this which is given unto him, that even such mighty works are wrought by his hands?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/mark/6#v3)</sup> Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary, the brother of James, and Joses, and of Juda, and Simon? and are not his sisters here with us? And they were offended at him.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/mark/6#v4)</sup> But Jesus said unto them, A prophet is not without honour, but in his own country, and among his own kin, and in his own house.

- In his own country they are astonished at his wisdom and his mighty works, and then are offended at him because they know his family.
- A prophet is not without honour, but in his own country, and among his own kin, and in his own house. What was done to him is done to those teaching today: men who knew you before you repented ask who you think you are.

**[Mark 6:5-6](/bible/mark/6#v5)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/mark/6#v6)</sup> And he marvelled because of their unbelief. And he went round about the villages, teaching.

- He could there do no mighty work, save that he laid his hands upon a few sick folk and healed them, and he marvelled because of their unbelief.

# Mark 7: The Tradition of the Elders and What Defiles a Man

Read the chapter → [Mark 7](/bible/mark/7)

**[Mark 7:1-5](/bible/mark/7#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/mark/7#v1)</sup> Then came together unto him the Pharisees, and certain of the scribes, which came from Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/mark/7#v2)</sup> And when they saw some of his disciples eat bread with defiled, that is to say, with unwashen, hands, they found fault.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/mark/7#v3)</sup> For the Pharisees, and all the Jews, except they wash their hands oft, eat not, holding the tradition of the elders.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/mark/7#v4)</sup> And when they come from the market, except they wash, they eat not. And many other things there be, which they have received to hold, as the washing of cups, and pots, brasen vessels, and of tables.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/mark/7#v5)</sup> Then the Pharisees and scribes asked him, Why walk not thy disciples according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with unwashen hands?

- The Pharisees and scribes find fault that the disciples eat bread with unwashen hands, holding the tradition of the elders.
- There is no law in the scriptures that a man must wash his hands before he eats. That was a tradition of the elders, and the whole contention is about tradition, not about law.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 15:2](/bible/matthew/15#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/matthew/15#v2)</sup> Why do thy disciples transgress the tradition of the elders? for they wash not their hands when they eat bread.

    The same account told in Matthew: Why do thy disciples transgress the tradition of the elders?

> <sup>[9](/bible/mark/7#v9)</sup> And he said unto them, Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition.

- This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. In vain do they worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.
- Laying aside the commandment of God, ye hold the tradition of men. Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition.

**[Mark 7:10-13](/bible/mark/7#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/mark/7#v10)</sup> For Moses said, Honour thy father and thy mother; and, Whoso curseth father or mother, let him die the death:
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/mark/7#v11)</sup> But ye say, If a man shall say to his father or mother, It is Corban, that is to say, a gift, by whatsoever thou mightest be profited by me; he shall be free.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/mark/7#v12)</sup> And ye suffer him no more to do ought for his father or his mother;
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/mark/7#v13)</sup> Making the word of God of none effect through your tradition, which ye have delivered: and many such like things do ye.

- Moses said, Honour thy father and thy mother, but they had made a way for a man to call what would have profited his parents a gift, and so owe them nothing.
- The elders were allowing children to be released from their parents, and by that tradition they made the word of God of none effect.

- The meats in view are lawful meats. What is unclean was never called meat, and it still does the body harm, which is not what purging means.
- What defiles is named out of his own mouth: evil thoughts, adulteries, fornications, murders, thefts, covetousness, wickedness, deceit, lasciviousness, an evil eye, blasphemy, pride, foolishness.

# Mark 8: The Four Thousand, the Confession of Peter, and the Cross

Read the chapter → [Mark 8](/bible/mark/8)

**[Mark 8:1-9](/bible/mark/8#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/mark/8#v1)</sup> In those days the multitude being very great, and having nothing to eat, Jesus called his disciples unto him, and saith unto them,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/mark/8#v2)</sup> I have compassion on the multitude, because they have now been with me three days, and have nothing to eat:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/mark/8#v3)</sup> And if I send them away fasting to their own houses, they will faint by the way: for divers of them came from far.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/mark/8#v4)</sup> And his disciples answered him, From whence can a man satisfy these men with bread here in the wilderness?
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/mark/8#v5)</sup> And he asked them, How many loaves have ye? And they said, Seven.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/mark/8#v6)</sup> And he commanded the people to sit down on the ground: and he took the seven loaves, and gave thanks, and brake, and gave to his disciples to set before them; and they did set them before the people.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/mark/8#v7)</sup> And they had a few small fishes: and he blessed, and commanded to set them also before them.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/mark/8#v8)</sup> So they did eat, and were filled: and they took up of the broken meat that was left seven baskets.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/mark/8#v9)</sup> And they that had eaten were about four thousand: and he sent them away.

- A second multitude, with him three days and having nothing to eat, is fed with seven loaves and a few small fishes, and seven baskets of the broken meat are taken up. About four thousand were fed.
- The disciples ask whence a man can satisfy these men with bread, having already seen five thousand fed. Their faith had to be built up the same as anyone's.

**[Mark 8:10-13](/bible/mark/8#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/mark/8#v10)</sup> And straightway he entered into a ship with his disciples, and came into the parts of Dalmanutha.

>
> <sup>[12](/bible/mark/8#v12)</sup> And he sighed deeply in his spirit, and saith, Why doth this generation seek after a sign? verily I say unto you, There shall no sign be given unto this generation.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/mark/8#v13)</sup> And he left them, and entering into the ship again departed to the other side.

- The Pharisees come seeking a sign from heaven, tempting him, and he sighs deeply in his spirit and gives them none. They came to him; he did not go to them.

**[Mark 8:14-21](/bible/mark/8#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/mark/8#v14)</sup> Now the disciples had forgotten to take bread, neither had they in the ship with them more than one loaf.

> <sup>[17](/bible/mark/8#v17)</sup> And when Jesus knew it, he saith unto them, Why reason ye, because ye have no bread? perceive ye not yet, neither understand? have ye your heart yet hardened?
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/mark/8#v18)</sup> Having eyes, see ye not? and having ears, hear ye not? and do ye not remember?
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/mark/8#v19)</sup> When I brake the five loaves among five thousand, how many baskets full of fragments took ye up? They say unto him, Twelve.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/mark/8#v20)</sup> And when the seven among four thousand, how many baskets full of fragments took ye up? And they said, Seven.

> <sup>[21](/bible/mark/8#v21)</sup> And he said unto them, How is it that ye do not understand?

- Take heed, beware of the leaven of the Pharisees, and of the leaven of Herod. They reason among themselves that it is because they have no bread.
- Having eyes, see ye not? and having ears, hear ye not? He reminds them of the twelve baskets and the seven, and asks, How is it that ye do not understand?
- He was not soft with them. When he was alone with the twelve he corrected them plainly, and that is why they were often afraid to ask him anything.

**[Mark 8:22-26](/bible/mark/8#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/mark/8#v22)</sup> And he cometh to Bethsaida; and they bring a blind man unto him, and besought him to touch him.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/mark/8#v23)</sup> And he took the blind man by the hand, and led him out of the town; and when he had spit on his eyes, and put his hands upon him, he asked him if he saw ought.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/mark/8#v24)</sup> And he looked up, and said, I see men as trees, walking.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/mark/8#v25)</sup> After that he put his hands again upon his eyes, and made him look up: and he was restored, and saw every man clearly.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/mark/8#v26)</sup> And he sent him away to his house, saying, Neither go into the town, nor tell it to any in the town.

- The blind man of Bethsaida looks up after the first touch and says, I see men as trees, walking; after the second he is restored and sees every man clearly.

**[Mark 8:27-31](/bible/mark/8#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/mark/8#v27)</sup> And Jesus went out, and his disciples, into the towns of Cesarea Philippi: and by the way he asked his disciples, saying unto them, Whom do men say that I am?
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/mark/8#v28)</sup> And they answered, John the Baptist: but some say, Elias; and others, One of the prophets.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/mark/8#v29)</sup> And he saith unto them, But whom say ye that I am? And Peter answereth and saith unto him, Thou art the Christ.

> <sup>[30](/bible/mark/8#v30)</sup> And he charged them that they should tell no man of him.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/mark/8#v31)</sup> And he began to teach them, that the Son of man must suffer many things, and be rejected of the elders, and of the chief priests, and scribes, and be killed, and after three days rise again.

- Whom say ye that I am? Peter answers, Thou art the Christ.
- He begins to teach them that the Son of man must suffer many things, and be rejected of the elders, and of the chief priests, and scribes, and be killed, and after three days rise again.
- Note who rejected him. It was the elders, the chief priests and the scribes. Thousands upon thousands of the people believed on him and followed him.

**[Mark 8:32-33](/bible/mark/8#v32)**

> <sup>[32](/bible/mark/8#v32)</sup> And he spake that saying openly. And Peter took him, and began to rebuke him.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/mark/8#v33)</sup> But when he had turned about and looked on his disciples, he rebuked Peter, saying, Get thee behind me, Satan: for thou savourest not the things that be of God, but the things that be of men.

- Peter takes him and begins to rebuke him, and is answered, Get thee behind me, Satan: for thou savourest not the things that be of God, but the things that be of men.
- The adversary can get on any man. Christ saw the spirit and rebuked it at once, without waiting.

**[Mark 8:34-38](/bible/mark/8#v34)**

> <sup>[34](/bible/mark/8#v34)</sup> And when he had called the people unto him with his disciples also, he said unto them, Whosoever will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/mark/8#v35)</sup> For whosoever will save his life shall lose it; but whosoever shall lose his life for my sake and the gospel’s, the same shall save it.

> <sup>[36](/bible/mark/8#v36)</sup> For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?

> <sup>[37](/bible/mark/8#v37)</sup> Or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul?
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/mark/8#v38)</sup> Whosoever therefore shall be ashamed of me and of my words in this adulterous and sinful generation; of him also shall the Son of man be ashamed, when he cometh in the glory of his Father with the holy angels.

- Whosoever will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me.
- What shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?
- Whosoever shall be ashamed of me and of my words in this adulterous and sinful generation, of him also shall the Son of man be ashamed. The gospel is not to be spoken quietly, nor is who he came for to be left out of it.

# Mark 9: The Transfiguration, the Dumb Spirit, and Who Shall Be Greatest

Read the chapter → [Mark 9](/bible/mark/9)

**[Mark 9:1-8](/bible/mark/9#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/mark/9#v1)</sup> And he said unto them, Verily I say unto you, That there be some of them that stand here, which shall not taste of death, till they have seen the kingdom of God come with power.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/mark/9#v2)</sup> And after six days Jesus taketh with him Peter, and James, and John, and leadeth them up into an high mountain apart by themselves: and he was transfigured before them.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/mark/9#v3)</sup> And his raiment became shining, exceeding white as snow; so as no fuller on earth can white them.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/mark/9#v4)</sup> And there appeared unto them Elias with Moses: and they were talking with Jesus.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/mark/9#v5)</sup> And Peter answered and said to Jesus, Master, it is good for us to be here: and let us make three tabernacles; one for thee, and one for Moses, and one for Elias.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/mark/9#v6)</sup> For he wist not what to say; for they were sore afraid.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/mark/9#v7)</sup> And there was a cloud that overshadowed them: and a voice came out of the cloud, saying, This is my beloved Son: hear him.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/mark/9#v8)</sup> And suddenly, when they had looked round about, they saw no man any more, save Jesus only with themselves.

- He is transfigured before Peter, James and John, and there appear unto them Elias with Moses, talking with him.
- Peter offers to build three tabernacles, not knowing what to say, for they were sore afraid.
- The voice out of the cloud says, This is my beloved Son: hear him. Moses stands for the law and Elias for the prophets, and the word given is to hear the son.

**[Mark 9:9-13](/bible/mark/9#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/mark/9#v9)</sup> And as they came down from the mountain, he charged them that they should tell no man what things they had seen, till the Son of man were risen from the dead.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/mark/9#v10)</sup> And they kept that saying with themselves, questioning one with another what the rising from the dead should mean.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/mark/9#v11)</sup> And they asked him, saying, Why say the scribes that Elias must first come?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/mark/9#v12)</sup> And he answered and told them, Elias verily cometh first, and restoreth all things; and how it is written of the Son of man, that he must suffer many things, and be set at nought.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/mark/9#v13)</sup> But I say unto you, That Elias is indeed come, and they have done unto him whatsoever they listed, as it is written of him.

- Elias verily cometh first, and restoreth all things; and I say unto you, That Elias is indeed come, and they have done unto him whatsoever they listed. Matthew names him as John the Baptist.

**[Mark 9:14-27](/bible/mark/9#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/mark/9#v14)</sup> And when he came to his disciples, he saw a great multitude about them, and the scribes questioning with them.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/mark/9#v15)</sup> And straightway all the people, when they beheld him, were greatly amazed, and running to him saluted him.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/mark/9#v16)</sup> And he asked the scribes, What question ye with them?
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/mark/9#v17)</sup> And one of the multitude answered and said, Master, I have brought unto thee my son, which hath a dumb spirit;
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/mark/9#v18)</sup> And wheresoever he taketh him, he teareth him: and he foameth, and gnasheth with his teeth, and pineth away: and I spake to thy disciples that they should cast him out; and they could not.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/mark/9#v19)</sup> He answereth him, and saith, O faithless generation, how long shall I be with you? how long shall I suffer you? bring him unto me.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/mark/9#v20)</sup> And they brought him unto him: and when he saw him, straightway the spirit tare him; and he fell on the ground, and wallowed foaming.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/mark/9#v21)</sup> And he asked his father, How long is it ago since this came unto him? And he said, Of a child.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/mark/9#v22)</sup> And ofttimes it hath cast him into the fire, and into the waters, to destroy him: but if thou canst do any thing, have compassion on us, and help us.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/mark/9#v23)</sup> Jesus said unto him, If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/mark/9#v24)</sup> And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/mark/9#v25)</sup> When Jesus saw that the people came running together, he rebuked the foul spirit, saying unto him, Thou dumb and deaf spirit, I charge thee, come out of him, and enter no more into him.

> <sup>[26](/bible/mark/9#v26)</sup> And the spirit cried, and rent him sore, and came out of him: and he was as one dead; insomuch that many said, He is dead.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/mark/9#v27)</sup> But Jesus took him by the hand, and lifted him up; and he arose.

- A father brings his son with a dumb spirit which the disciples could not cast out, and is answered, O faithless generation, how long shall I be with you?
- If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth. The father answers with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.
- Those who were healed were healed on their faith. That is why the word to them was, thy faith hath made thee whole.

**[Mark 9:28-29](/bible/mark/9#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/mark/9#v28)</sup> And when he was come into the house, his disciples asked him privately, Why could not we cast him out?

> <sup>[29](/bible/mark/9#v29)</sup> And he said unto them, This kind can come forth by nothing, but by prayer and fasting.

- Asked privately why they could not cast him out, he answers, This kind can come forth by nothing, but by prayer and fasting. Prayer and fasting are not optional for the things that will not move otherwise.

**[Mark 9:30-32](/bible/mark/9#v30)**

> <sup>[32](/bible/mark/9#v32)</sup> But they understood not that saying, and were afraid to ask him.

**[Mark 9:33-37](/bible/mark/9#v33)**

> <sup>[35](/bible/mark/9#v35)</sup> And he sat down, and called the twelve, and saith unto them, If any man desire to be first, the same shall be last of all, and servant of all.

- He asks what they disputed by the way, knowing already that they had disputed who should be the greatest, and they held their peace.
- If any man desire to be first, the same shall be last of all, and servant of all. With rank comes the weight of the people, and every matter comes to the ears of those set over them.
