## Introduction

This session covers [Judges 17](/bible/judges/17) through 20, some of the wildest history in the book: Micah of mount Ephraim and his house of idols, the Levite he hired to be his personal priest, the Danites stealing Micah's gods and conquering Laish, the Levite's concubine and the sons of Belial at Gibeah, and the war of all Israel against Benjamin. The thread that ties these chapters together is stated four times: in those days there was no king in Israel, and every man did that which was right in his own eyes. Israel came out of the wilderness with the commandments, but without a leadership structure they were sheep without a shepherd, and the nation ran wild in all manner of wickedness, primarily idolatry.

**[Judges 17:1-4](/bible/judges/17#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/judges/17#v1)</sup> And there was a man of mount Ephraim, whose name was Micah.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/judges/17#v2)</sup> And he said unto his mother, The eleven hundred shekels of silver that were taken from thee, about which thou cursedst, and spakest of also in mine ears, behold, the silver is with me; I took it. And his mother said, Blessed be thou of the Lord, my son.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/judges/17#v3)</sup> And when he had restored the eleven hundred shekels of silver to his mother, his mother said, I had wholly dedicated the silver unto the Lord from my hand for my son, to make a graven image and a molten image: now therefore I will restore it unto thee.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/judges/17#v4)</sup> Yet he restored the money unto his mother; and his mother took two hundred shekels of silver, and gave them to the founder, who made thereof a graven image and a molten image: and they were in the house of Micah.

- A man of mount Ephraim named Micah stole eleven hundred shekels of silver from his own mother, and when he heard her cursing about the missing silver he confessed, I took it, and restored it to her.
- Instead of rebuking her son, his mother said blessed be thou of the LORD, and told him she had wholly dedicated the silver for him to make a graven image and a molten image. She refused to keep what he returned, took two hundred shekels to the founder, and had idols made to set up in her son's house.
- A mother buying her son idols was nothing new in Israel; there were mothers who steered a son down the wrong path.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Chronicles 22:3](/bible/2-chronicles/22#v3)**
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-chronicles/22#v3)</sup> He also walked in the ways of the house of Ahab: for his mother was his counsellor to do wickedly.

    Later in history king Ahaziah walked in the ways of the house of Ahab, for his mother was his counsellor to do wickedly; the same pattern seen with Micah's mother.

**[Judges 17:5](/bible/judges/17#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/judges/17#v5)</sup> And the man Micah had an house of gods, and made an ephod, and teraphim, and consecrated one of his sons, who became his priest.

- Micah had a whole house of gods. He made an ephod and teraphim and consecrated one of his own sons to be his priest, a counterfeit priesthood serving counterfeit gods.

**[Judges 17:6](/bible/judges/17#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/judges/17#v6)</sup> In those days there was no king in Israel, but every man did that which was right in his own eyes.

- This verse is the key to understanding the whole book of Judges. With no king and no government, Israel was sheep without a shepherd, and when you see how the people carry on in these chapters, that is why: every man did that which was right in his own eyes.
- The same individual spirit is around today in those who say join no camp and let no man teach you. Every time the scripture shows Israel without a leadership structure, all manner of wickedness follows, primarily idolatry.

**[Judges 17:7-10](/bible/judges/17#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/judges/17#v7)</sup> And there was a young man out of Beth–lehem–judah of the family of Judah, who was a Levite, and he sojourned there.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/judges/17#v8)</sup> And the man departed out of the city from Beth–lehem–judah to sojourn where he could find a place: and he came to mount Ephraim to the house of Micah, as he journeyed.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/judges/17#v9)</sup> And Micah said unto him, Whence comest thou? And he said unto him, I am a Levite of Beth–lehem–judah, and I go to sojourn where I may find a place.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/judges/17#v10)</sup> And Micah said unto him, Dwell with me, and be unto me a father and a priest, and I will give thee ten shekels of silver by the year, and a suit of apparel, and thy victuals. So the Levite went in.

- A young Levite of Bethlehemjudah departed to sojourn wherever he could find a place and came to mount Ephraim to the house of Micah.
- When Micah learned the man was a Levite, of the tribe known for the priesthood and the knowledge of the sacrifices, he offered him a deal: dwell with me, be unto me a father and a priest, ten shekels of silver by the year, a suit of apparel, and thy victuals. He hired himself a personal priest for his idols.

**[Judges 17:11-13](/bible/judges/17#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/judges/17#v11)</sup> And the Levite was content to dwell with the man; and the young man was unto him as one of his sons.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/judges/17#v12)</sup> And Micah consecrated the Levite; and the young man became his priest, and was in the house of Micah.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/judges/17#v13)</sup> Then said Micah, Now know I that the Lord will do me good, seeing I have a Levite to my priest.

- The Levite was content to dwell with him and became as one of his sons. Nothing says the Levite was already in idolatry, but when money was offered he went right along with it; being corrupted for reward was always the Levites' weakness.
- Then Micah said, now know I that the LORD will do me good, seeing I have a Levite to my priest. A house full of idols and a hired priest over them, yet he expected the Most High to bless him because a Levite was on the payroll. That is how sick in the mind Israel was without leadership.

- The children of Dan sent five men of valour from Zorah and Eshtaol to spy out land to conquer, and on their way they came to mount Ephraim and lodged at the house of Micah.

**[Judges 18:7-10](/bible/judges/18#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/judges/18#v7)</sup> Then the five men departed, and came to Laish, and saw the people that were therein, how they dwelt careless, after the manner of the Zidonians, quiet and secure; and there was no magistrate in the land, that might put them to shame in any thing; and they were far from the Zidonians, and had no business with any man.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/judges/18#v8)</sup> And they came unto their brethren to Zorah and Eshtaol: and their brethren said unto them, What say ye?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/judges/18#v9)</sup> And they said, Arise, that we may go up against them: for we have seen the land, and, behold, it is very good: and are ye still? be not slothful to go, and to enter to possess the land.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/judges/18#v10)</sup> When ye go, ye shall come unto a people secure, and to a large land: for God hath given it into your hands; a place where there is no want of any thing that is in the earth.

- The five men came to Laish and saw the people that were therein, how they dwelt careless, quiet and secure, after the manner of the Zidonians, the nearby nation whose lifestyle they copied. They were a chill people with no worries, not even watching for anyone to come in and conquer them.
- A magistrate is a judge: there was no judicial system in that land to put a man to shame or convict him for a crime. They were also far from the Zidonians, too far for Zidon to interfere, and had no business with any man, no allies. The spies peeped all of it.
- Their report back at Zorah and Eshtaol: arise, that we may go up against them, for we have seen the land, and behold, it is very good; a place where there is no want of any thing that is in the earth, and God hath given it into your hands. Do not sit still; let us move now.

- The first time Micah let the five spies lodge in his house, they saw his expensive looking gods and got covetous. Now passing back through they tell their brethren, do ye know that there is in these houses an ephod, and teraphim, and a graven image, and a molten image? now therefore consider what ye have to do. On the way to the lick they spotted another lick.
- The Bible addresses everything: do not bring just anybody into your house, even brethren, because the deceitful man has many tricks and approaches to finesse you out of the goods in your house.

Do not snitch; roll with us and be the man over a whole tribe.
- The priest's heart was glad, and he took the ephod, the teraphim, and the graven image and went in the midst of the people. His covetous spirit was plain: already sold out to serve idols for Micah's silver, he dropped the man who treated him as a son the moment a bigger position was offered. Disloyal through and through.

# Judges 19: The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah

Read the chapter → [Judges 19](/bible/judges/19)

**[Judges 19:1-2](/bible/judges/19#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/judges/19#v1)</sup> And it came to pass in those days, when there was no king in Israel, that there was a certain Levite sojourning on the side of mount Ephraim, who took to him a concubine out of Beth–lehem–judah.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/judges/19#v2)</sup> And his concubine played the whore against him, and went away from him unto her father’s house to Beth–lehem–judah, and was there four whole months.

- Once more, there was no king in Israel, no leadership structure, still sheep without a shepherd. A certain Levite sojourning on the side of mount Ephraim took to him a concubine out of Bethlehemjudah; a concubine was a woman dealt with on the level of a servant.
- His concubine played the whore against him, laid with another man, then went away from him unto her father's house to Bethlehemjudah and was there four whole months.

**[Judges 19:3](/bible/judges/19#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/judges/19#v3)</sup> And her husband arose, and went after her, to speak friendly unto her, and to bring her again, having his servant with him, and a couple of asses: and she brought him into her father’s house: and when the father of the damsel saw him, he rejoiced to meet him.

- Instead of letting her go, her husband arose and went after her to speak friendly unto her and to bring her again, with his servant and a couple of asses, and the damsel's father rejoiced to meet him.
- By the law, a woman who is put away and becomes another man's is defiled to her former husband, and taking her back defiles the land. The Most High does not permit it, but with no king in the land and no enforcement of the law, this is what men did.
