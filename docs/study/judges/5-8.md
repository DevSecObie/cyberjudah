## Introduction

This session covers [Judges 5](/bible/judges/5) through 8: the song of Deborah and Barak after the victory over Sisera, the call of Gideon while Israel is wasted under Midian, the three hundred men by whom the Lord saves Israel, and Israel's return to Baal worship as soon as Gideon is dead. The class opens under the order of [1 Corinthians 14:34-35](/bible/1-corinthians/14#v34), that the women keep silence in the assembly, for it is not permitted unto them to speak, but they are commanded to be under obedience, as also saith the law. The running thread is that everything Israel does for the Most High must come from a willing heart, and that the victory of battle stands not in the multitude of an host, but strength comes from heaven.

**[Judges 5:1-2](/bible/judges/5#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/judges/5#v1)</sup> Then sang Deborah and Barak the son of Abinoam on that day, saying,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/judges/5#v2)</sup> Praise ye the Lord for the avenging of Israel, when the people willingly offered themselves.

- Deborah and Barak sing praise to the Lord for the avenging of Israel, when the people willingly offered themselves. That is what the Most High wants from us: a willing heart in all that we do, in the keeping of the commandments, in the keeping of the feast days, and in the praise of him.

  Precepts:
  - **[Exodus 35:22](/bible/exodus/35#v22)**
> <sup>[22](/bible/exodus/35#v22)</sup> And they came, both men and women, as many as were willing hearted, and brought bracelets, and earrings, and rings, and tablets, all jewels of gold: and every man that offered offered an offering of gold unto the Lord.

    At the building of the tabernacle in the wilderness, both men and women as many as were willing hearted brought their gold unto the Lord; we have to have that same willing heart today.

- How do we willingly offer ourselves to the Lord in the twenty-first century? By giving up whatever presents itself as an obstacle between us and the Lord. It may be father, mother, husband, wife, or children; it may be drugs, money, or working on the sabbath. If you willingly offer yourself, you will not let any stumbling block keep you from the Lord.

  Precepts:

**[Judges 5:6-9](/bible/judges/5#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/judges/5#v6)</sup> In the days of Shamgar the son of Anath, in the days of Jael, the highways were unoccupied, and the travellers walked through byways.

>
> <sup>[8](/bible/judges/5#v8)</sup> They chose new gods; then was war in the gates: was there a shield or spear seen among forty thousand in Israel?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/judges/5#v9)</sup> My heart is toward the governors of Israel, that offered themselves willingly among the people. Bless ye the Lord.

- This passage is for all the closet feminists who think Deborah justifies women leading men. She did not lead men into any kind of war; she did not put on body armor and a breastplate, rise up on a horse, and lead the charge. She was the motivating factor to men that were shaky and fearful during that time, a mother in Israel, a guiding light.
- Verse 9 proves it: my heart is toward the governors of Israel, the leaders who offered themselves willingly among the people. Her heart was with the men who led; she was not ruling over them.

# Judges 6: The Call of Gideon: Why Is All This Befallen Us

Read the chapter → [Judges 6](/bible/judges/6)

**[Judges 6:1-2](/bible/judges/6#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/judges/6#v1)</sup> And the children of Israel did evil in the sight of the Lord: and the Lord delivered them into the hand of Midian seven years.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/judges/6#v2)</sup> And the hand of Midian prevailed against Israel: and because of the Midianites the children of Israel made them the dens which are in the mountains, and caves, and strong holds.

- Israel did evil in the sight of the Lord, and the Lord delivered them into the hand of Midian seven years. That is our manner: the Most High delivers us, we get a little bit of freedom, and we go right back to wickedness. The same thing goes on today; we get relaxed, and the Lord has to remind us, putting us right back into hard bondage, that this is not our rest.
- Because of the Midianites, the children of Israel made them the dens in the mountains, and caves, and strongholds; Israel hid in the mountains of their own land.

- Gideon answers, oh my Lord, if the Lord be with us, why then is all this befallen us, and where be all his miracles which our fathers told us of? A lot of us ask that same question today when we watch our people judged in the streets over and over: if we be the chosen people, why does all of this happen to us?

- Gideon says the Lord hath forsaken us, and that is the same thought our people carry today when you hit the streets teaching. But God has not forsaken us; it is actually we that have forsaken God.

- Gideon and his hundred come to the outside of the camp in the beginning of the middle watch, when they had but newly set the watch; in war you always have watchmen taking first, second, and third watch, and Gideon struck right in between the changing of the watchmen.
- The three companies blew the trumpets, brake the pitchers, held the lamps in their left hands and the trumpets in their right, and cried, the sword of the Lord, and of Gideon; and every man stood in his place round about the camp. Three hundred against thousands, you have to do something to mess with their mind: those horns blowing in unison put fear in the nations, and all the host ran, and cried, and fled.
- And the Lord set every man's sword against his fellow, even throughout all the host. The enemy destroyed itself; that is the Lord fighting the battle.

**[Judges 7:23-25](/bible/judges/7#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/judges/7#v23)</sup> And the men of Israel gathered themselves together out of Naphtali, and out of Asher, and out of all Manasseh, and pursued after the Midianites.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/judges/7#v24)</sup> And Gideon sent messengers throughout all mount Ephraim, saying, Come down against the Midianites, and take before them the waters unto Beth–barah and Jordan. Then all the men of Ephraim gathered themselves together, and took the waters unto Beth–barah and Jordan.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/judges/7#v25)</sup> And they took two princes of the Midianites, Oreb and Zeeb; and they slew Oreb upon the rock Oreb, and Zeeb they slew at the winepress of Zeeb, and pursued Midian, and brought the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon on the other side Jordan.

- The men of Naphtali, Asher, and all Manasseh gathered themselves and pursued after the Midianites, and Gideon sent messengers throughout all mount Ephraim to come down and take the waters before them; and Ephraim took two princes of Midian, slaying Oreb upon the rock Oreb and Zeeb at the winepress of Zeeb.
- Ephraim, stand up; get this spirit back and come join your brothers in the war, because Judah is calling you right now. For those that may not know, that is you so-called Hispanics, you Puerto Ricans: you are Ephraim.

# Judges 8: Gideon's Pursuit and Israel's Return to Baal

Read the chapter → [Judges 8](/bible/judges/8)

**[Judges 8:1-3](/bible/judges/8#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/judges/8#v1)</sup> And the men of Ephraim said unto him, Why hast thou served us thus, that thou calledst us not, when thou wentest to fight with the Midianites? And they did chide with him sharply.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/judges/8#v2)</sup> And he said unto them, What have I done now in comparison of you? Is not the gleaning of the grapes of Ephraim better than the vintage of Abi–ezer?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/judges/8#v3)</sup> God hath delivered into your hands the princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb: and what was I able to do in comparison of you? Then their anger was abated toward him, when he had said that.

- The men of Ephraim chode with Gideon sharply: why hast thou served us thus, that thou calledst us not when thou wentest to fight with the Midianites? Ephraim has an attitude because they got called late to the battle and wanted in from the beginning.
- Gideon answers with wisdom: is not the gleaning of the grapes of Ephraim better than the vintage of Abiezer? God delivered into your hands the princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb; what was I able to do in comparison of you? He gave them the best possible situation, the enemy fed right into their hands with nowhere to go, and then their anger was abated toward him.

**[Judges 8:4-9](/bible/judges/8#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/judges/8#v4)</sup> And Gideon came to Jordan, and passed over, he, and the three hundred men that were with him, faint, yet pursuing them.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/judges/8#v5)</sup> And he said unto the men of Succoth, Give, I pray you, loaves of bread unto the people that follow me; for they be faint, and I am pursuing after Zebah and Zalmunna, kings of Midian.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/judges/8#v6)</sup> And the princes of Succoth said, Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna now in thine hand, that we should give bread unto thine army?
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/judges/8#v7)</sup> And Gideon said, Therefore when the Lord hath delivered Zebah and Zalmunna into mine hand, then I will tear your flesh with the thorns of the wilderness and with briers.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/judges/8#v8)</sup> And he went up thence to Penuel, and spake unto them likewise: and the men of Penuel answered him as the men of Succoth had answered him.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/judges/8#v9)</sup> And he spake also unto the men of Penuel, saying, When I come again in peace, I will break down this tower.

- Gideon and the three hundred pass over Jordan, faint, yet pursuing Zebah and Zalmunna the kings of Midian. He asks the men of Succoth for loaves of bread for his men, and the princes of Succoth answer, are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna now in thine hand, that we should give bread unto thine army? They try to play neutral ground for fear the kings might come against them if they helped.
- Gideon promises Succoth that when the Lord has delivered Zebah and Zalmunna into his hand, he will tear their flesh with the thorns of the wilderness and with briers; and when the men of Penuel answer him the same way, he promises to break down their tower when he comes again in peace.

**[Judges 8:10-12](/bible/judges/8#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/judges/8#v10)</sup> Now Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor, and their hosts with them, about fifteen thousand men, all that were left of all the hosts of the children of the east: for there fell an hundred and twenty thousand men that drew sword.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/judges/8#v11)</sup> And Gideon went up by the way of them that dwelt in tents on the east of Nobah and Jogbehah, and smote the host: for the host was secure.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/judges/8#v12)</sup> And when Zebah and Zalmunna fled, he pursued after them, and took the two kings of Midian, Zebah and Zalmunna, and discomfited all the host.

- Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor with about fifteen thousand men, all that were left of the host of the children of the east, for there fell an hundred and twenty thousand men that drew sword. Three hundred against those numbers: you know God was with them.
- Gideon went up by the way of them that dwelt in tents and smote the host while it was secure, and when the two kings fled, he pursued and took them, and discomfited all the host.

**[Judges 8:13-17](/bible/judges/8#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/judges/8#v13)</sup> And Gideon the son of Joash returned from battle before the sun was up,
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/judges/8#v14)</sup> And caught a young man of the men of Succoth, and enquired of him: and he described unto him the princes of Succoth, and the elders thereof, even threescore and seventeen men.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/judges/8#v15)</sup> And he came unto the men of Succoth, and said, Behold Zebah and Zalmunna, with whom ye did upbraid me, saying, Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna now in thine hand, that we should give bread unto thy men that are weary?
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/judges/8#v16)</sup> And he took the elders of the city, and thorns of the wilderness and briers, and with them he taught the men of Succoth.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/judges/8#v17)</sup> And he beat down the tower of Penuel, and slew the men of the city.

- Returning from battle, Gideon caught a young man of Succoth who described unto him the princes and elders of the city, and he came back to Succoth with Zebah and Zalmunna in hand: behold the kings with whom ye did upbraid me. Gideon and his men had finished off Midian on an empty stomach because of them.
- He took the elders of the city, and thorns of the wilderness and briers, and with them he taught the men of Succoth; he taught them a lesson, that when the army of the Lord comes through faint and weary and asks for bread, you do not turn them away. And he beat down the tower of Penuel and slew the men of the city.

**[Judges 8:18-21](/bible/judges/8#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/judges/8#v18)</sup> Then said he unto Zebah and Zalmunna, What manner of men were they whom ye slew at Tabor? And they answered, As thou art, so were they; each one resembled the children of a king.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/judges/8#v19)</sup> And he said, They were my brethren, even the sons of my mother: as the Lord liveth, if ye had saved them alive, I would not slay you.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/judges/8#v20)</sup> And he said unto Jether his firstborn, Up, and slay them. But the youth drew not his sword: for he feared, because he was yet a youth.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/judges/8#v21)</sup> Then Zebah and Zalmunna said, Rise thou, and fall upon us: for as the man is, so is his strength. And Gideon arose, and slew Zebah and Zalmunna, and took away the ornaments that were on their camels’ necks.

- Zebah and Zalmunna describe the men they slew at Tabor: each one resembled the children of a king. Those were powerful men, and they were Gideon's brethren, the sons of his mother; as the Lord liveth, if ye had saved them alive, I would not slay you.
- Gideon tells Jether his firstborn, up, and slay them, but the youth drew not his sword, for he feared, because he was yet a youth. We have all had that moment of getting the nudge from pops and being nervous; the son was young, and that is understandable, but you see Gideon being a father, taking his son to the war and teaching him the ways he must walk.

  Precepts:
  - **[Proverbs 22:6](/bible/proverbs/22#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/proverbs/22#v6)</sup> Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.

    Train up a child in the way he should go: Gideon is doing exactly what we have to do with our young men, teaching them how to walk in the ways of the Lord.

- Zebah and Zalmunna say, rise thou, and fall upon us: for as the man is, so is his strength. They wanted to die quick at the hand of a warrior rather than suffer under the sword of a boy; so Gideon arose and slew them, and took away the ornaments that were on their camels' necks.

**[Judges 8:22-23](/bible/judges/8#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/judges/8#v22)</sup> Then the men of Israel said unto Gideon, Rule thou over us, both thou, and thy son, and thy son’s son also: for thou hast delivered us from the hand of Midian.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/judges/8#v23)</sup> And Gideon said unto them, I will not rule over you, neither shall my son rule over you: the Lord shall rule over you.

- The men of Israel ask Gideon to rule over them, both he, his son, and his son's son, for delivering them from the hand of Midian. Israel has always needed a leader, because we are lost sheep; we need somebody to guide us in the way we must go, or we wander off the path of righteousness. It has always been that way, and you see it playing out right here.
- Gideon refuses: I will not rule over you, neither shall my son rule over you; the Lord shall rule over you.

**[Judges 8:24-27](/bible/judges/8#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/judges/8#v24)</sup> And Gideon said unto them, I would desire a request of you, that ye would give me every man the earrings of his prey. (For they had golden earrings, because they were Ishmaelites.)
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/judges/8#v25)</sup> And they answered, We will willingly give them. And they spread a garment, and did cast therein every man the earrings of his prey.

> <sup>[26](/bible/judges/8#v26)</sup> And the weight of the golden earrings that he requested was a thousand and seven hundred shekels of gold; beside ornaments, and collars, and purple raiment that was on the kings of Midian, and beside the chains that were about their camels’ necks.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/judges/8#v27)</sup> And Gideon made an ephod thereof, and put it in his city, even in Ophrah: and all Israel went thither a whoring after it: which thing became a snare unto Gideon, and to his house.

- Gideon requests of every man the earrings of his prey, for the enemy had golden earrings because they were Ishmaelites; and they willingly gave them, a thousand and seven hundred shekels of gold, beside the ornaments, collars, purple raiment, and camel chains of the kings.
- Gideon made an ephod thereof and put it in his city, even in Ophrah: and all Israel went thither a whoring after it, which thing became a snare unto Gideon, and to his house.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Timothy 6:10](/bible/1-timothy/6#v10)**
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-timothy/6#v10)</sup> For the love of money is the root of all evil: which while some coveted after, they have erred from the faith, and pierced themselves through with many sorrows.

    The love of money is the root of all evil; the gold that Gideon gathered turned into the very snare that trapped his own house.

> <sup>[29](/bible/judges/8#v29)</sup> And Jerubbaal the son of Joash went and dwelt in his own house.

> <sup>[30](/bible/judges/8#v30)</sup> And Gideon had threescore and ten sons of his body begotten: for he had many wives.

- Thus was Midian subdued before the children of Israel, so that they lifted up their heads no more, and the country was in quietness forty years in the days of Gideon. Gideon had threescore and ten sons, for he had many wives, and his concubine in Shechem bare him a son whose name he called Abimelech; and Gideon the son of Joash died in a good old age.
- The sum of the history of Gideon, who is also named Jerubbaal, is this: the victory does not come in the strength of numbers, in how many guns or how many bullets you have. The victory of battle comes from the Lord, and that is why we have to trust in the Lord with all our heart and endure to the end, keeping these commandments until the return of our King of kings.

> <sup>[17](/bible/1-maccabees/3#v17)</sup> Who, when they saw the host coming to meet them, said unto Judas, How shall we be able, being so few, to fight against so great a multitude and so strong, seeing we are ready to faint with fasting all this day?
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-maccabees/3#v18)</sup> Unto whom Judas answered, It is no hard matter for many to be shut up in the hands of a few; and with the God of heaven it is all one, to deliver with a great multitude, or a small company:

- The same cycle turns again the moment the judge is gone. That is the whole lesson of the book: our deliverance is never our own hand, and our downfall is always our own forgetting.
