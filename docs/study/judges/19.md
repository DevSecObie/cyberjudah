**[Judges 19:1-2](/bible/judges/19#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/judges/19#v1)</sup> And it came to pass in those days, when there was no king in Israel, that there was a certain Levite sojourning on the side of mount Ephraim, who took to him a concubine out of Beth–lehem–judah.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/judges/19#v2)</sup> And his concubine played the whore against him, and went away from him unto her father’s house to Beth–lehem–judah, and was there four whole months.

- Once more, there was no king in Israel, no leadership structure, still sheep without a shepherd. A certain Levite sojourning on the side of mount Ephraim took to him a concubine out of Bethlehemjudah; a concubine was a woman dealt with on the level of a servant.
- His concubine played the whore against him, laid with another man, then went away from him unto her father's house to Bethlehemjudah and was there four whole months.

**[Judges 19:3](/bible/judges/19#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/judges/19#v3)</sup> And her husband arose, and went after her, to speak friendly unto her, and to bring her again, having his servant with him, and a couple of asses: and she brought him into her father’s house: and when the father of the damsel saw him, he rejoiced to meet him.

- Instead of letting her go, her husband arose and went after her to speak friendly unto her and to bring her again, with his servant and a couple of asses, and the damsel's father rejoiced to meet him.
- By the law, a woman who is put away and becomes another man's is defiled to her former husband, and taking her back defiles the land. The Most High does not permit it, but with no king in the land and no enforcement of the law, this is what men did.

    Her former husband may not take her again to be his wife after she is defiled, for that is abomination and causes the land to sin.

**[Judges 19:4-9](/bible/judges/19#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/judges/19#v4)</sup> And his father in law, the damsel’s father, retained him; and he abode with him three days: so they did eat and drink, and lodged there.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/judges/19#v5)</sup> And it came to pass on the fourth day, when they arose early in the morning, that he rose up to depart: and the damsel’s father said unto his son in law, Comfort thine heart with a morsel of bread, and afterward go your way.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/judges/19#v6)</sup> And they sat down, and did eat and drink both of them together: for the damsel’s father had said unto the man, Be content, I pray thee, and tarry all night, and let thine heart be merry.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/judges/19#v7)</sup> And when the man rose up to depart, his father in law urged him: therefore he lodged there again.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/judges/19#v8)</sup> And he arose early in the morning on the fifth day to depart: and the damsel’s father said, Comfort thine heart, I pray thee. And they tarried until afternoon, and they did eat both of them.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/judges/19#v9)</sup> And when the man rose up to depart, he, and his concubine, and his servant, his father in law, the damsel’s father, said unto him, Behold, now the day draweth toward evening, I pray you tarry all night: behold, the day groweth to an end, lodge here, that thine heart may be merry; and to morrow get you early on your way, that thou mayest go home.

- The damsel's father retained him: eat, drink, lodge, three days, then a fourth, then a fifth. Like an old man who never has anybody to talk to, he kept finding a reason not to let the Levite go, and a one night plan stretched into five days.
- The father's words on the fifth day cut a popular doctrine. They tarried until afternoon, and he said, behold, the day draweth toward evening, and then, behold, the day groweth to an end. According to the Bible the day ends at evening; and if it ends at evening it begins at evening, so a day runs from evening to evening, including the sabbath. That buries the sunrise to sunrise doctrine, which has no works and no fruits to show it was ever right.

**[Judges 19:10-13](/bible/judges/19#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/judges/19#v10)</sup> But the man would not tarry that night, but he rose up and departed, and came over against Jebus, which is Jerusalem; and there were with him two asses saddled, his concubine also was with him.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/judges/19#v11)</sup> And when they were by Jebus, the day was far spent; and the servant said unto his master, Come, I pray thee, and let us turn in into this city of the Jebusites, and lodge in it.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/judges/19#v12)</sup> And his master said unto him, We will not turn aside hither into the city of a stranger, that is not of the children of Israel; we will pass over to Gibeah.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/judges/19#v13)</sup> And he said unto his servant, Come, and let us draw near to one of these places to lodge all night, in Gibeah, or in Ramah.

- The man would not tarry that night; five days was enough and he had business to tend to. He rose up, departed, and came over against Jebus, which is Jerusalem, with two saddled asses and his concubine.
- Jerusalem is called Jebus here because the Jebusites, the original inhabitants of Jerusalem, still dwelt in it; this is before David rose up and took it from them. When Israel came out of the wilderness, the promised land was inhabited primarily by Canaanites, and the children of Judah could not drive the Jebusites out.

- When the servant suggested turning in to lodge in this city of the Jebusites, his master refused: we will not turn aside hither into the city of a stranger, that is not of the children of Israel; we will pass over to Gibeah. Even in those wild days he would not lodge with heathens, only with his brothers.

- An old man from his work out of the field at even, a man of mount Ephraim sojourning in Gibeah, lifted up his eyes and questioned the wayfaring man: whither goest thou? and whence comest thou? The Levite explained he was passing toward the house of the LORD with straw, provender, bread, and wine of his own, lacking nothing, yet no man would receive him to house.
- The old man said, peace be with thee; howsoever let all thy wants lie upon me; only lodge not in the street. He knew the manner of the men of that city and what the streets of Gibeah held at night. He brought them into his house, and they washed their feet and did eat and drink.

**[Judges 19:22-24](/bible/judges/19#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/judges/19#v22)</sup> Now as they were making their hearts merry, behold, the men of the city, certain sons of Belial, beset the house round about, and beat at the door, and spake to the master of the house, the old man, saying, Bring forth the man that came into thine house, that we may know him.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/judges/19#v23)</sup> And the man, the master of the house, went out unto them, and said unto them, Nay, my brethren, nay, I pray you, do not so wickedly; seeing that this man is come into mine house, do not this folly.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/judges/19#v24)</sup> Behold, here is my daughter a maiden, and his concubine; them I will bring out now, and humble ye them, and do with them what seemeth good unto you: but unto this man do not so vile a thing.

- As they were making their hearts merry, certain sons of Belial, meaning children of the devil, demon seed, beset the house round about and beat at the door in the middle of the night, saying to the master of the house, bring forth the man that came into thine house, that we may know him.
- To know in this context is sexual intercourse; the men of Gibeah were howling for the man himself. This is a rerun of a very familiar story of old, the men of Sodom at Lot's door, and the scripture names exactly what vile thing they were after.

**[Judges 19:25-28](/bible/judges/19#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/judges/19#v25)</sup> But the men would not hearken to him: so the man took his concubine, and brought her forth unto them; and they knew her, and abused her all the night until the morning: and when the day began to spring, they let her go.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/judges/19#v26)</sup> Then came the woman in the dawning of the day, and fell down at the door of the man’s house where her lord was, till it was light.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/judges/19#v27)</sup> And her lord rose up in the morning, and opened the doors of the house, and went out to go his way: and, behold, the woman his concubine was fallen down at the door of the house, and her hands were upon the threshold.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/judges/19#v28)</sup> And he said unto her, Up, and let us be going. But none answered. Then the man took her up upon an ass, and the man rose up, and gat him unto his place.

- The men would not hearken, so the man brought his concubine forth unto them; and they knew her, and abused her all the night until the morning. They is plural: this was a gang rape, forced, nothing voluntary about it, done in the anger of men who could not get the man they wanted.
- When the day began to spring they let her go, and the woman came in the dawning of the day and fell down at the door of the man's house where her lord was, her hands upon the threshold. In the morning he said, up, and let us be going; but none answered. There was no more spirit left in her; she was dead. He laid her upon an ass and got him unto his place.
- What we witnessed is rape, and the scripture is plain that rape is a sin worthy of death; marriage is the only honorable bed. Men out here calling themselves Israelites and teaching otherwise give Israelites a bad name; we preach the laws of the Most High.

    Unto the damsel thou shalt do nothing, for as when a man riseth against his neighbour and slayeth him, even so is this matter; the Most High counts rape the equivalent of murder, and in this gang rape the victim was literally murdered.

> <sup>[29](/bible/judges/19#v29)</sup> And when he was come into his house, he took a knife, and laid hold on his concubine, and divided her, together with her bones, into twelve pieces, and sent her into all the coasts of Israel.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/judges/19#v30)</sup> And it was so, that all that saw it said, There was no such deed done nor seen from the day that the children of Israel came up out of the land of Egypt unto this day: consider of it, take advice, and speak your minds.

- When he was come into his house he took a knife, laid hold on his concubine, and divided her, together with her bones, into twelve pieces, and sent her into all the coasts of Israel, to make the twelve tribes aware of what was done in Gibeah.
- All that saw it said, there was no such deed done nor seen from the day that the children of Israel came up out of the land of Egypt unto this day: consider of it, take advice, and speak your minds. The nation had never seen a matter like this, and it called the whole congregation to counsel.
