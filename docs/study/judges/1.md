## Introduction

This session covers [Judges 1](/bible/judges/1) through 4, reading through the Bible from Genesis through Revelation, four chapters a day, so the whole Bible is read in one year. It is not a class, but highlighted parts of the chapters are touched on with basic precepts to help with the understanding. Much of Judges reads straightforward: after the death of Joshua the tribes go up against the Canaanites, but tribe after tribe does not drive out the inhabitants of the land as the Most High commanded. Chapter 2 shows what dwelling among them cost Israel, chapter 3 opens the cycle of the judges with Othniel, Ehud, and Shamgar, and chapter 4 gives Deborah, Barak, and Jael. As you read, it helps to look at a map of the dividing of the land, such as the dividing-of-the-land map in the rear of the Zondervan's Compact Bible Dictionary, or an ancient twelve tribes chart, to see where these tribes and cities sat.

**[Judges 1:1-3](/bible/judges/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/judges/1#v1)</sup> Now after the death of Joshua it came to pass, that the children of Israel asked the Lord, saying, Who shall go up for us against the Canaanites first, to fight against them?
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/judges/1#v2)</sup> And the Lord said, Judah shall go up: behold, I have delivered the land into his hand.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/judges/1#v3)</sup> And Judah said unto Simeon his brother, Come up with me into my lot, that we may fight against the Canaanites; and I likewise will go with thee into thy lot. So Simeon went with him.

- After the death of Joshua the children of Israel ask the Most High who shall go up first against the Canaanites, and the answer is Judah: behold, I have delivered the land into his hand.
- Judah asks Simeon his brother to come up with him into his lot, and promises to go likewise with Simeon into his lot, so Simeon went with him.

**[Judges 1:4-7](/bible/judges/1#v4)**

>

>

> <sup>[7](/bible/judges/1#v7)</sup> And Adoni–bezek said, Threescore and ten kings, having their thumbs and their great toes cut off, gathered their meat under my table: as I have done, so God hath requited me. And they brought him to Jerusalem, and there he died.

- The Most High delivers the Canaanites and the Perizzites into Judah's hand, and they slay ten thousand men in Bezek.
- Adonibezek flees and is caught, and his thumbs and great toes are cut off. He confesses it himself: threescore and ten kings with their thumbs and great toes cut off gathered their meat under his table, and as he had done, so God requited him.

**[Judges 1:8](/bible/judges/1#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/judges/1#v8)</sup> Now the children of Judah had fought against Jerusalem, and had taken it, and smitten it with the edge of the sword, and set the city on fire.

- The children of Judah fought against Jerusalem, took it, smote it with the edge of the sword, and set the city on fire. This verse ties back into the history we just finished reading in Joshua.
- Judah was supposed to drive the Jebusites, the inhabitants of Jerusalem, out of the land, but could not, and the Jebusites dwelt with the children of Judah at Jerusalem. Jerusalem sat right on Judah's border by the valley of the son of Hinnom.
- The king of Jerusalem was already among the kings of the land which the children of Israel smote and whose land they possessed, so this fight in Judges is Judah taking the city itself.

- This is a review of the history we just read in Joshua; it was not that many days ago in the reading that we covered this, and it repeats again here at the opening of Judges.

**[Judges 1:27-36](/bible/judges/1#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/judges/1#v27)</sup> Neither did Manasseh drive out the inhabitants of Beth–shean and her towns, nor Taanach and her towns, nor the inhabitants of Dor and her towns, nor the inhabitants of Ibleam and her towns, nor the inhabitants of Megiddo and her towns: but the Canaanites would dwell in that land.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/judges/1#v28)</sup> And it came to pass, when Israel was strong, that they put the Canaanites to tribute, and did not utterly drive them out.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/judges/1#v29)</sup> Neither did Ephraim drive out the Canaanites that dwelt in Gezer; but the Canaanites dwelt in Gezer among them.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/judges/1#v30)</sup> Neither did Zebulun drive out the inhabitants of Kitron, nor the inhabitants of Nahalol; but the Canaanites dwelt among them, and became tributaries.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/judges/1#v31)</sup> Neither did Asher drive out the inhabitants of Accho, nor the inhabitants of Zidon, nor of Ahlab, nor of Achzib, nor of Helbah, nor of Aphik, nor of Rehob:
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/judges/1#v32)</sup> But the Asherites dwelt among the Canaanites, the inhabitants of the land: for they did not drive them out.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/judges/1#v33)</sup> Neither did Naphtali drive out the inhabitants of Beth–shemesh, nor the inhabitants of Beth–anath; but he dwelt among the Canaanites, the inhabitants of the land: nevertheless the inhabitants of Beth–shemesh and of Beth–anath became tributaries unto them.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/judges/1#v34)</sup> And the Amorites forced the children of Dan into the mountain: for they would not suffer them to come down to the valley:
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/judges/1#v35)</sup> But the Amorites would dwell in mount Heres in Aijalon, and in Shaalbim: yet the hand of the house of Joseph prevailed, so that they became tributaries.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/judges/1#v36)</sup> And the coast of the Amorites was from the going up to Akrabbim, from the rock, and upward.

- Tribe after tribe repeats the same failure: neither did Manasseh, nor Ephraim, nor Zebulun, nor Asher, nor Naphtali drive out the inhabitants of their cities, and the Amorites forced the children of Dan into the mountain.
- What we are reading is how we did not drive out all the inhabitants of the land, which the Most High had commanded us. Each tribe did not drive them out, and what did we do? We dwelt among them.
- When Israel was strong, we put the Canaanites to tribute, but we still did not utterly drive them out; we still dwelt among them. Chapter 2 gets into what that dwelling among the inhabitants of the land brought upon us.
