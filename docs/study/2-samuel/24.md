## Introduction

This last chapter records the numbering of Israel. The anger of the Most High was kindled against Israel, so he moved David to number the people, a task that belonged to the Levites and not the king. When David's heart smote him he confessed his sin, and the Most High offered him three judgments through the prophet Gad; David chose to fall into the hand of the Lord, and a pestilence killed seventy thousand men. When the angel stretched out his hand over Jerusalem, the Most High said, It is enough. David then bought the threshing floor of Araunah, refusing to offer that which cost him nothing, built an altar, and the plague was stayed. The thread is that all judgment is in the hand of the Most High, and true worship costs us something.

**[2 Samuel 24:1-4](/bible/2-samuel/24#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/24#v1)</sup> And again the anger of the Lord was kindled against Israel, and he moved David against them to say, Go, number Israel and Judah.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/24#v2)</sup> For the king said to Joab the captain of the host, which was with him, Go now through all the tribes of Israel, from Dan even to Beer–sheba, and number ye the people, that I may know the number of the people.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/24#v3)</sup> And Joab said unto the king, Now the Lord thy God add unto the people, how many soever they be, an hundredfold, and that the eyes of my lord the king may see it: but why doth my lord the king delight in this thing?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-samuel/24#v4)</sup> Notwithstanding the king’s word prevailed against Joab, and against the captains of the host. And Joab and the captains of the host went out from the presence of the king, to number the people of Israel.

- The anger of the Lord was kindled against Israel, and he moved David to number Israel and Judah. This was the Most High wanting to judge Israel; he used David and used Satan to provoke him to do it. Joab objected, asking why the king delighted in this thing, for numbering the people was not the king's job but the work of the Levites.
- Notwithstanding, the king's word prevailed against Joab and the captains, and they went out to number the people. Joab did not understand that behind it all, verse one, the anger of the Lord was kindled against Israel; it was of the Most High that this happened.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Chronicles 21:1](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/1-chronicles/21#v1)</sup> And Satan stood up against Israel, and provoked David to number Israel.

    Satan stood up against Israel and provoked David to number Israel; this is the precept showing how the Most High used Satan to accomplish his judgment.

**[2 Samuel 24:5-9](/bible/2-samuel/24#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/2-samuel/24#v5)</sup> And they passed over Jordan, and pitched in Aroer, on the right side of the city that lieth in the midst of the river of Gad, and toward Jazer:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-samuel/24#v6)</sup> Then they came to Gilead, and to the land of Tahtim–hodshi; and they came to Dan–jaan, and about to Zidon,
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-samuel/24#v7)</sup> And came to the strong hold of Tyre, and to all the cities of the Hivites, and of the Canaanites: and they went out to the south of Judah, even to Beer–sheba.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-samuel/24#v8)</sup> So when they had gone through all the land, they came to Jerusalem at the end of nine months and twenty days.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-samuel/24#v9)</sup> And Joab gave up the sum of the number of the people unto the king: and there were in Israel eight hundred thousand valiant men that drew the sword; and the men of Judah were five hundred thousand men.

- Joab and the captains passed over Jordan and went through all the land, from Aroer and Gad and Gilead, to Dan-jaan, to Zidon, to the stronghold of Tyre and the cities of the Hivites and Canaanites, and out to the south of Judah to Beersheba. It took them nine months and twenty days to count all of Israel.
- Joab gave the sum to the king: there were in Israel eight hundred thousand valiant men that drew the sword, and the men of Judah were five hundred thousand men. This did not count the women and the children.

**[2 Samuel 24:10-14](/bible/2-samuel/24#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/2-samuel/24#v10)</sup> And David’s heart smote him after that he had numbered the people. And David said unto the Lord, I have sinned greatly in that I have done: and now, I beseech thee, O Lord, take away the iniquity of thy servant; for I have done very foolishly.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-samuel/24#v11)</sup> For when David was up in the morning, the word of the Lord came unto the prophet Gad, David’s seer, saying,
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-samuel/24#v12)</sup> Go and say unto David, Thus saith the Lord, I offer thee three things; choose thee one of them, that I may do it unto thee.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-samuel/24#v13)</sup> So Gad came to David, and told him, and said unto him, Shall seven years of famine come unto thee in thy land? or wilt thou flee three months before thine enemies, while they pursue thee? or that there be three days’ pestilence in thy land? now advise, and see what answer I shall return to him that sent me.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-samuel/24#v14)</sup> And David said unto Gad, I am in a great strait: let us fall now into the hand of the Lord; for his mercies are great: and let me not fall into the hand of man.

- David's heart smote him after he had numbered the people, and he said unto the Lord, I have sinned greatly; take away the iniquity of thy servant, for I have done very foolishly. As soon as it was done it hit him, because it was not the king's job but the Levites' to number the people.
- In the morning the word of the Lord came to the prophet Gad, David's seer, offering David three things to choose one: seven years of famine, three months fleeing before his enemies, or three days of pestilence. David was in a great strait and said, Let us fall now into the hand of the Lord, for his mercies are great, and let me not fall into the hand of man. He had two prophets, Gad and Nathan, who ministered to him.

**[2 Samuel 24:15-17](/bible/2-samuel/24#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/2-samuel/24#v15)</sup> So the Lord sent a pestilence upon Israel from the morning even to the time appointed: and there died of the people from Dan even to Beer–sheba seventy thousand men.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-samuel/24#v16)</sup> And when the angel stretched out his hand upon Jerusalem to destroy it, the Lord repented him of the evil, and said to the angel that destroyed the people, It is enough: stay now thine hand. And the angel of the Lord was by the threshingplace of Araunah the Jebusite.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-samuel/24#v17)</sup> And David spake unto the Lord when he saw the angel that smote the people, and said, Lo, I have sinned, and I have done wickedly: but these sheep, what have they done? let thine hand, I pray thee, be against me, and against my father’s house.

- The Lord sent the pestilence upon Israel from the morning to the time appointed, and there died seventy thousand men from Dan to Beersheba. When the angel stretched out his hand upon Jerusalem to destroy it, the Lord repented him of the evil and said to the angel, It is enough, stay now thine hand; and the angel was by the threshing place of Araunah the Jebusite.
- David saw the angel that smote the people, something we cannot see today, for the Most High has blinded our eyes to it and this walk is by faith and the keeping of the commandments. David spoke unto the Lord, Lo, I have sinned and done wickedly, but these sheep, what have they done; let thine hand be against me and against my father's house. Remember the reason for the seventy thousand is verse one, the Most High was angry at Israel.

**[2 Samuel 24:18-25](/bible/2-samuel/24#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/2-samuel/24#v18)</sup> And Gad came that day to David, and said unto him, Go up, rear an altar unto the Lord in the threshingfloor of Araunah the Jebusite.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-samuel/24#v19)</sup> And David, according to the saying of Gad, went up as the Lord commanded.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-samuel/24#v20)</sup> And Araunah looked, and saw the king and his servants coming on toward him: and Araunah went out, and bowed himself before the king on his face upon the ground.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-samuel/24#v21)</sup> And Araunah said, Wherefore is my lord the king come to his servant? And David said, To buy the threshingfloor of thee, to build an altar unto the Lord, that the plague may be stayed from the people.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-samuel/24#v22)</sup> And Araunah said unto David, Let my lord the king take and offer up what seemeth good unto him: behold, here be oxen for burnt sacrifice, and threshing instruments and other instruments of the oxen for wood.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-samuel/24#v23)</sup> All these things did Araunah, as a king, give unto the king. And Araunah said unto the king, The Lord thy God accept thee.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-samuel/24#v24)</sup> And the king said unto Araunah, Nay; but I will surely buy it of thee at a price: neither will I offer burnt offerings unto the Lord my God of that which doth cost me nothing. So David bought the threshingfloor and the oxen for fifty shekels of silver.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-samuel/24#v25)</sup> And David built there an altar unto the Lord, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. So the Lord was intreated for the land, and the plague was stayed from Israel.

- Gad told David to rear an altar unto the Lord in the threshing floor of Araunah, and David went up as the Lord commanded. Araunah bowed himself before the king and offered to give the floor, the oxen, and the instruments freely for the burnt sacrifice, saying, The Lord thy God accept thee.
- David refused to take it for nothing, saying he would not offer burnt offerings unto the Lord his God of that which cost him nothing, and bought the threshing floor and the oxen for fifty shekels of silver. He built there an altar and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings, so the Lord was entreated for the land and the plague was stayed from Israel. True worship costs us something.
