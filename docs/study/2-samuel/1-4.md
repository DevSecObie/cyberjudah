## Introduction

This reading covers [2 Samuel 1](/bible/2-samuel/1) through 4, the opening of the second book of Samuel. It moves from the death of Saul and Jonathan to David's coming into the kingdom. The Amalekite who claimed to have slain the Lord's anointed is put to death; David laments Saul and Jonathan in the spirit; David is anointed king over Judah while Ishbosheth is set up over the rest of Israel; and the long war between the two houses runs its course, with David waxing stronger and the house of Saul weaker. Running through it all is one thread: the Most High has His own way of bringing His purpose to pass, and men who take vengeance and shed blood by their own hand, out of order and not ordained of the Lord, answer for it.

**[2 Samuel 1:1-4](/bible/2-samuel/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/1#v1)</sup> Now it came to pass after the death of Saul, when David was returned from the slaughter of the Amalekites, and David had abode two days in Ziklag;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/1#v2)</sup> It came even to pass on the third day, that, behold, a man came out of the camp from Saul with his clothes rent, and earth upon his head: and so it was, when he came to David, that he fell to the earth, and did obeisance.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/1#v3)</sup> And David said unto him, From whence comest thou? And he said unto him, Out of the camp of Israel am I escaped.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-samuel/1#v4)</sup> And David said unto him, How went the matter? I pray thee, tell me. And he answered, That the people are fled from the battle, and many of the people also are fallen and dead; and Saul and Jonathan his son are dead also.

- After the death of Saul, when David had returned from the slaughter of the Amalekites and abode two days in Ziklag, a man came out of the camp of Israel with his clothes rent and earth upon his head, and fell to the earth before David to do him honor.
- David asked him how the matter went, and he answered that the people were fled from the battle, many had fallen and were dead, and Saul and Jonathan his son were dead also.

**[2 Samuel 1:5-10](/bible/2-samuel/1#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/2-samuel/1#v5)</sup> And David said unto the young man that told him, How knowest thou that Saul and Jonathan his son be dead?
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-samuel/1#v6)</sup> And the young man that told him said, As I happened by chance upon mount Gilboa, behold, Saul leaned upon his spear; and, lo, the chariots and horsemen followed hard after him.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-samuel/1#v7)</sup> And when he looked behind him, he saw me, and called unto me. And I answered, Here am I.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-samuel/1#v8)</sup> And he said unto me, Who art thou? And I answered him, I am an Amalekite.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-samuel/1#v9)</sup> He said unto me again, Stand, I pray thee, upon me, and slay me: for anguish is come upon me, because my life is yet whole in me.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-samuel/1#v10)</sup> So I stood upon him, and slew him, because I was sure that he could not live after that he was fallen: and I took the crown that was upon his head, and the bracelet that was on his arm, and have brought them hither unto my lord.

- The young man told David that he had come by chance upon mount Gilboa, and found Saul leaning upon his spear with the chariots and horsemen pressing hard after him. In the earlier account Saul fell upon his sword but did not die at that moment.
- The man said he was an Amalekite, and that Saul asked him to stand upon him and slay him because anguish was come upon him; so he slew him, being sure he could not live, and took the crown from his head and the bracelet from his arm and brought them to David.
- The Amalekites are the seed of Esau, of Eliphaz his son. Their lineage is written in Genesis, and it is a thing to know when reading of Israel's enemies.

>

>

>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-samuel/1#v16)</sup> And David said unto him, Thy blood be upon thy head; for thy mouth hath testified against thee, saying, I have slain the Lord’s anointed.

- David took hold on his clothes and rent them, and all the men with him did likewise, and they mourned and wept and fasted until evening for Saul, for Jonathan, and for the house of Israel that were fallen by the sword.
- The renting of garments was the sign of mourning, but the Most High looks for a rent heart and not rent garments only; true repentance is inward, not just an outward show.
- David asked the man how he was not afraid to stretch forth his hand to destroy the Lord's anointed. Even a man about to die was not to be touched; the judgment of the Lord was to be let play out. David then commanded one of his young men to fall upon the Amalekite, and he died, for his own mouth had testified against him.

    Rend your heart, and not your garments; the Most High looks for true repentance within.

**[2 Samuel 1:17-27](/bible/2-samuel/1#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/2-samuel/1#v17)</sup> And David lamented with this lamentation over Saul and over Jonathan his son:
>

>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-samuel/1#v19)</sup> The beauty of Israel is slain upon thy high places: how are the mighty fallen!
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-samuel/1#v20)</sup> Tell it not in Gath, publish it not in the streets of Askelon; lest the daughters of the Philistines rejoice, lest the daughters of the uncircumcised triumph.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-samuel/1#v21)</sup> Ye mountains of Gilboa, let there be no dew, neither let there be rain, upon you, nor fields of offerings: for there the shield of the mighty is vilely cast away, the shield of Saul, as though he had not been anointed with oil.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-samuel/1#v22)</sup> From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, the bow of Jonathan turned not back, and the sword of Saul returned not empty.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-samuel/1#v23)</sup> Saul and Jonathan were lovely and pleasant in their lives, and in their death they were not divided: they were swifter than eagles, they were stronger than lions.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-samuel/1#v24)</sup> Ye daughters of Israel, weep over Saul, who clothed you in scarlet, with other delights, who put on ornaments of gold upon your apparel.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-samuel/1#v25)</sup> How are the mighty fallen in the midst of the battle! O Jonathan, thou wast slain in thine high places.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-samuel/1#v26)</sup> I am distressed for thee, my brother Jonathan: very pleasant hast thou been unto me: thy love to me was wonderful, passing the love of women.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-samuel/1#v27)</sup> How are the mighty fallen, and the weapons of war perished!

- David lamented over Saul and over Jonathan, and bade them teach the children of Judah the use of the bow, which is written in the book of Jasher. The book of Jasher is not in the canon because the Most High did not will us to have it; He put in the book exactly what He wanted His people to read in the last days.
- This walk in the last days is not by might nor by power but by the Spirit of the Lord. The captivity is not overthrown by rising up with the bow or the gun; this is spiritual war and not physical. There is nothing wrong with knowing how to defend yourself, but the redemption of Israel does not come by force of arms.
- David's lament is a prophecy given in the spirit, almost a eulogy: Saul and Jonathan were lovely and pleasant in their lives, and in their death they were not divided; they were swifter than eagles and stronger than lions. Before the devil jumped on Saul he was good, and the two of them were good together.
- Israel's forefathers died only when the Most High said it was time; that is the only way any of them died. David mourned Jonathan, saying his love to him was wonderful, passing the love of women.

# 2 Samuel 2: David Anointed King Over Judah

- David inquired of the Lord whether he should go up into any of the cities of Judah, and the Lord said, Go up, unto Hebron. David did not move to take the kingdom until he asked whether it was time to be king.
- David went up with his two wives, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess and Abigail the widow of Nabal the Carmelite, and with every man and his household, and they dwelt in the cities of Hebron.
- The men of Judah came and there anointed David king over the house of Judah. He was raised up because the Most High had anointed him to be the next king over Israel.

- Abner the son of Ner, captain of Saul's host, took Ishbosheth the son of Saul and made him king over Gilead and Israel; Ishbosheth was forty years old and reigned two years, while Judah followed David. David reigned in Hebron over Judah seven years and six months.

**[2 Samuel 2:12-23](/bible/2-samuel/2#v12)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/2-samuel/2#v23)</sup> Howbeit he refused to turn aside: wherefore Abner with the hinder end of the spear smote him under the fifth rib, that the spear came out behind him; and he fell down there, and died in the same place: and it came to pass, that as many as came to the place where Asahel fell down and died stood still.

- Abner and the servants of Ishbosheth met Joab and the servants of David at the pool of Gibeon, sitting on opposite sides. Abner said, Let the young men arise and play, meaning let them compete and battle one another; the Lord is a man of war, and such contests were done in that day.
- Twelve of Benjamin and twelve of David's servants caught each his fellow by the head and thrust the sword in his fellow's side, so they fell down together, and that place was called Helkathhazzurim. Abner and the men of Israel were beaten before the servants of David.
- Asahel, one of the three sons of Zeruiah, was as light of foot as a wild roe, meaning he was fast, and he pursued Abner and would not turn aside. Abner warned him again and again to turn off and take another man's armor, not wanting to have to kill him and face Joab his brother.
- When Asahel refused to turn aside, Abner smote him with the hinder end of the spear under the fifth rib, and he died there. A strong thrust is needed to drive a spear through a man, and Abner was strong. Those who came to the place stood still; the lesson is to take heed to the warning and not keep testing a man.

Joab answered that unless Abner had spoken, the people would have gone up in the morning, and he blew the trumpet and the people stood still and fought no more.
- Abner and his men walked all that night and came to Mahanaim. Of David's servants nineteen men and Asahel were missing, but they had smitten of Abner's men three hundred and threescore. They buried Asahel at Bethlehem, and Joab and his men came to Hebron at break of day.

# 2 Samuel 3: The House of David Waxes Stronger

Read the chapter → [2 Samuel 3](/bible/2-samuel/3)

**[2 Samuel 3:1](/bible/2-samuel/3#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/3#v1)</sup> Now there was long war between the house of Saul and the house of David: but David waxed stronger and stronger, and the house of Saul waxed weaker and weaker.

- There was long war between the house of Saul and the house of David, but David waxed stronger and stronger and the house of Saul weaker and weaker. Each had those that followed him, but the Most High increased David because he had been anointed to be the next king over Israel.

- Sons were born to David in Hebron: the firstborn Amnon of Ahinoam, Chileab of Abigail, Absalom the son of Maachah the daughter of the king of Geshur, Adonijah the son of Haggith, Shephatiah of Abital, and Ithream of Eglah his wife.
- Abner made himself strong for the house of Saul. When Ishbosheth charged him over Rizpah, Saul's concubine, Abner was very wroth, saying he had shown kindness to the house of Saul and not delivered them to David, and now was charged with a fault concerning this woman.
- Abner swore to do to himself and more, to translate the kingdom from the house of Saul and set up the throne of David over Israel and Judah, from Dan even to Beersheba. Ishbosheth could not answer him a word, because he feared him. Abner was through with Ishbosheth and turned to go with David and the Lord.

> <sup>[21](/bible/2-samuel/3#v21)</sup> And Abner said unto David, I will arise and go, and will gather all Israel unto my lord the king, that they may make a league with thee, and that thou mayest reign over all that thine heart desireth. And David sent Abner away; and he went in peace.

- Abner sent messengers to David offering to bring all Israel to him. David agreed to make a league, but required that Michal, Saul's daughter, first be brought to him, and he sent to Ishbosheth to deliver his wife whom he had espoused for a hundred foreskins of the Philistines.
- Michal was taken from her husband Phaltiel, who went weeping behind her until Abner told him to return. David was taking back what was his as the king before the Lord.
- Abner spoke with the elders of Israel and with Benjamin, reminding them they had sought David in times past, and that the Lord had said by the hand of David He would save Israel out of the hand of the Philistines and all their enemies. Abner came to David at Hebron with twenty men, David made them a feast, and Abner went in peace to gather all Israel to David.

**[2 Samuel 3:22-30](/bible/2-samuel/3#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/2-samuel/3#v22)</sup> And, behold, the servants of David and Joab came from pursuing a troop, and brought in a great spoil with them: but Abner was not with David in Hebron; for he had sent him away, and he was gone in peace.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-samuel/3#v23)</sup> When Joab and all the host that was with him were come, they told Joab, saying, Abner the son of Ner came to the king, and he hath sent him away, and he is gone in peace.
>

>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-samuel/3#v25)</sup> Thou knowest Abner the son of Ner, that he came to deceive thee, and to know thy going out and thy coming in, and to know all that thou doest.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-samuel/3#v26)</sup> And when Joab was come out from David, he sent messengers after Abner, which brought him again from the well of Sirah: but David knew it not.

> <sup>[27](/bible/2-samuel/3#v27)</sup> And when Abner was returned to Hebron, Joab took him aside in the gate to speak with him quietly, and smote him there under the fifth rib, that he died, for the blood of Asahel his brother.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/2-samuel/3#v28)</sup> And afterward when David heard it, he said, I and my kingdom are guiltless before the Lord for ever from the blood of Abner the son of Ner:
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/2-samuel/3#v29)</sup> Let it rest on the head of Joab, and on all his father’s house; and let there not fail from the house of Joab one that hath an issue, or that is a leper, or that leaneth on a staff, or that falleth on the sword, or that lacketh bread.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/2-samuel/3#v30)</sup> So Joab and Abishai his brother slew Abner, because he had slain their brother Asahel at Gibeon in the battle.

- Joab returned from pursuing a troop and heard that Abner had come and been sent away in peace. Joab came to the king and said Abner had come to deceive him and to know his going out and coming in; he did not trust Abner, and there was already blood between them, for Abner had killed Asahel his brother.
- Joab, without David's knowledge, sent messengers after Abner and brought him back to Hebron, took him aside in the gate to speak quietly, and smote him there under the fifth rib, that he died, for the blood of Asahel his brother.
- It was payback; Joab's blood was burning for revenge. Abner was unwise to return to that place, knowing what Joab would do, but it was of the Lord.

**[2 Samuel 3:31-39](/bible/2-samuel/3#v31)**

> <sup>[31](/bible/2-samuel/3#v31)</sup> And David said to Joab, and to all the people that were with him, Rend your clothes, and gird you with sackcloth, and mourn before Abner. And king David himself followed the bier.

>
> <sup>[34](/bible/2-samuel/3#v34)</sup> Thy hands were not bound, nor thy feet put into fetters: as a man falleth before wicked men, so fellest thou. And all the people wept again over him.

> <sup>[39](/bible/2-samuel/3#v39)</sup> And I am this day weak, though anointed king; and these men the sons of Zeruiah be too hard for me: the Lord shall reward the doer of evil according to his wickedness.

- When David heard it, he declared himself and his kingdom guiltless before the Lord forever from the blood of Abner, and laid it on the head of Joab and all his father's house. He commanded Joab and all the people to rend their clothes and gird with sackcloth and mourn before Abner, and the king himself followed the coffin.
- David wept at the grave and lamented over Abner in the spirit, another eulogy and prophecy: Abner died as a fool dieth, his hands not bound nor his feet in fetters, but he fell as a man falleth before wicked men. All the people wept again and took notice, and David swore not to taste bread till the sun went down, and it pleased the people; whatsoever the king did pleased them, for David was in the right spirit and the Lord was dealing with the nation.
- David said he was weak that day though anointed king, and that the sons of Zeruiah were too hard for him, ruthless men he could not bring under; but the Lord would reward the doer of evil according to his wickedness. That payback came later through Solomon concerning Joab.

# 2 Samuel 4: The Slaying of Ishbosheth

**[2 Samuel 4:1-3](/bible/2-samuel/4#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/4#v1)</sup> And when Saul’s son heard that Abner was dead in Hebron, his hands were feeble, and all the Israelites were troubled.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/4#v2)</sup> And Saul’s son had two men that were captains of bands: the name of the one was Baanah, and the name of the other Rechab, the sons of Rimmon a Beerothite, of the children of Benjamin: (for Beeroth also was reckoned to Benjamin:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/4#v3)</sup> And the Beerothites fled to Gittaim, and were sojourners there until this day.)

- When Saul's son Ishbosheth heard that Abner was dead in Hebron, his hands were feeble and all the Israelites were troubled.
- Ishbosheth had two captains of bands, Baanah and Rechab, sons of Rimmon the Beerothite of the children of Benjamin, for Beeroth also was reckoned to Benjamin; and the Beerothites had fled to Gittaim.

**[2 Samuel 4:4](/bible/2-samuel/4#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/2-samuel/4#v4)</sup> And Jonathan, Saul’s son, had a son that was lame of his feet. He was five years old when the tidings came of Saul and Jonathan out of Jezreel, and his nurse took him up, and fled: and it came to pass, as she made haste to flee, that he fell, and became lame. And his name was Mephibosheth.

- Jonathan's son Mephibosheth was lame of his feet. When the tidings came of Saul and Jonathan out of Jezreel, he was five years old, and his nurse took him up and fled, and in her haste he fell and became lame.
- She fled because a new king taking over would customarily kill all the descendants of the former king to make sure none could overthrow him. That is why the nurse ran with the child.

**[2 Samuel 4:5-8](/bible/2-samuel/4#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/2-samuel/4#v5)</sup> And the sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, Rechab and Baanah, went, and came about the heat of the day to the house of Ish–bosheth, who lay on a bed at noon.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-samuel/4#v6)</sup> And they came thither into the midst of the house, as though they would have fetched wheat; and they smote him under the fifth rib: and Rechab and Baanah his brother escaped.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-samuel/4#v7)</sup> For when they came into the house, he lay on his bed in his bedchamber, and they smote him, and slew him, and beheaded him, and took his head, and gat them away through the plain all night.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-samuel/4#v8)</sup> And they brought the head of Ish–bosheth unto David to Hebron, and said to the king, Behold the head of Ish–bosheth the son of Saul thine enemy, which sought thy life; and the Lord hath avenged my lord the king this day of Saul, and of his seed.

- The sons of Rimmon came about the heat of the day to the house of Ishbosheth, who lay on a bed at noon in the hottest part of the day, and came into the midst of the house as though they would fetch wheat, and smote him under the fifth rib.
- Rechab and Baanah escaped, having smitten and slain him on his bed; and they beheaded him and took his head and got away through the plain all night, and brought the head to David at Hebron, saying, Behold the head of Ishbosheth thine enemy, and claiming the Lord had avenged the king this day of Saul and his seed.
- They did too much; they went overboard. Israel being out of order took the matter upon themselves. David had no quarrel with Saul's sons; it was Saul who sought his life.

**[2 Samuel 4:9-12](/bible/2-samuel/4#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/2-samuel/4#v9)</sup> And David answered Rechab and Baanah his brother, the sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, and said unto them, As the Lord liveth, who hath redeemed my soul out of all adversity,
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-samuel/4#v10)</sup> When one told me, saying, Behold, Saul is dead, thinking to have brought good tidings, I took hold of him, and slew him in Ziklag, who thought that I would have given him a reward for his tidings:
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-samuel/4#v11)</sup> How much more, when wicked men have slain a righteous person in his own house upon his bed? shall I not therefore now require his blood of your hand, and take you away from the earth?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-samuel/4#v12)</sup> And David commanded his young men, and they slew them, and cut off their hands and their feet, and hanged them up over the pool in Hebron. But they took the head of Ish–bosheth, and buried it in the sepulchre of Abner in Hebron.

- David answered Rechab and Baanah that as the Lord liveth, who had redeemed his soul out of all adversity, the man who told him Saul was dead, thinking to bring good tidings, he had taken hold of and slain in Ziklag; that was the reward he gave for such tidings.
- How much more, he said, when wicked men had slain a righteous person in his own house upon his bed, should he not require his blood of their hand and take them away from the earth. The man was at rest, asleep, not even a man-to-man contest for the kingdom; it was not how the Most High brings His purpose to pass.
- David commanded his young men, and they slew Rechab and Baanah, cut off their hands and feet, and hanged them up over the pool in Hebron, but took the head of Ishbosheth and buried it in the sepulchre of Abner. The Most High has His own way of doing things; His people are not to put the cart before the horse.
- This is why David is called a man after the Most High's own heart. He did not accept wickedness done in his name or let the murderers slide; he called them out and they received judgment for taking a man's life not ordained of the Lord nor commanded by the king. Let all things be done decently and in order.
