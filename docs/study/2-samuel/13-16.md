## Introduction

This session covers 2 Samuel 13-16. It is the working out of the judgment the Most High pronounced in the previous chapter, that the sword would never depart from David's house because of what he did with Bathsheba and Uriah. In four chapters David's daughter Tamar is raped by her brother Amnon, one son Absalom murders that brother, Absalom later steals the hearts of Israel and drives his own father out of the kingdom, and a man of the house of Saul curses David as he flees. None of this would have happened if David had not done what he did, but he did do it, and this is the punishment. These are people who know they are Israel, so coming into the truth does not mean everything is sugar cookies and cakes; issues occur in Israel, and how we deal with them is why the Most High gave us charity, [Matthew 18](/bible/matthew/18), and the example of Christ.

# 2 Samuel 14: Joab Brings Absalom Home

Read the chapter → [2 Samuel 14](/bible/2-samuel/14)

## Introduction

Chapter 14 turns on David's longing for Absalom. Joab, perceiving the king's heart, sends a wise woman of Tekoah to speak a parable that traps David into recalling his own banished son. David discerns Joab's hand in it, yet still grants the request and brings Absalom back to Jerusalem, though he refuses to see his face for two full years. The wisdom never departed from David even after his fall; he was still anointed of the Most High, still king over all Israel, and still a very wise man. Absalom's beauty and his weight of hair are noted, and his persistence in setting Joab's field on fire finally gets him back into the king's presence.

>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/14#v2)</sup> And Joab sent to Tekoah, and fetched thence a wise woman, and said unto her, I pray thee, feign thyself to be a mourner, and put on now mourning apparel, and anoint not thyself with oil, but be as a woman that had a long time mourned for the dead:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/14#v3)</sup> And come to the king, and speak on this manner unto him. So Joab put the words in her mouth.

- Joab the son of Zeruiah perceived that the king's heart was toward Absalom, and sent to Tekoah and fetched a wise woman, telling her to feign herself a mourner, put on mourning apparel, and speak to the king in the manner he taught her.
- Joab put the words in her mouth. He set up the whole scene to move the king toward bringing Absalom home.

# 2 Samuel 15: Absalom Steals the Hearts of Israel

## Introduction

Chapter 15 shows Absalom's conspiracy come to a head. David sends the ark back to the city in faith, and sends his friend Hushai back to defeat the counsel of Ahithophel. David treated this as a game of chess, not checkers, strategizing even as he wept, and much of the book of Psalms was written while David was on the run like this.
