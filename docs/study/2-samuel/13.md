## Introduction

This session covers 2 Samuel 13-16. It is the working out of the judgment the Most High pronounced in the previous chapter, that the sword would never depart from David's house because of what he did with Bathsheba and Uriah. In four chapters David's daughter Tamar is raped by her brother Amnon, one son Absalom murders that brother, Absalom later steals the hearts of Israel and drives his own father out of the kingdom, and a man of the house of Saul curses David as he flees. None of this would have happened if David had not done what he did, but he did do it, and this is the punishment. These are people who know they are Israel, so coming into the truth does not mean everything is sugar cookies and cakes; issues occur in Israel, and how we deal with them is why the Most High gave us charity, [Matthew 18](/bible/matthew/18), and the example of Christ.

**[2 Samuel 13:1-3](/bible/2-samuel/13#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/13#v1)</sup> And it came to pass after this, that Absalom the son of David had a fair sister, whose name was Tamar; and Amnon the son of David loved her.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/13#v2)</sup> And Amnon was so vexed, that he fell sick for his sister Tamar; for she was a virgin; and Amnon thought it hard for him to do any thing to her.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/13#v3)</sup> But Amnon had a friend, whose name was Jonadab, the son of Shimeah David’s brother: and Jonadab was a very subtil man.

- Absalom the son of David had a fair sister named Tamar, and Amnon the son of David loved her, and was so vexed for her that he fell sick, for she was a virgin and he thought it hard to do anything to her. Amnon had a friend named Jonadab, the son of Shimeah David's brother, who was a very subtle man.
- Jonadab was sneaky. We all know somebody like this, that grimy dude who knows exactly how to get over on somebody, and that is what Jonadab was.

**[2 Samuel 13:4-7](/bible/2-samuel/13#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/2-samuel/13#v4)</sup> And he said unto him, Why art thou, being the king’s son, lean from day to day? wilt thou not tell me? And Amnon said unto him, I love Tamar, my brother Absalom’s sister.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-samuel/13#v5)</sup> And Jonadab said unto him, Lay thee down on thy bed, and make thyself sick: and when thy father cometh to see thee, say unto him, I pray thee, let my sister Tamar come, and give me meat, and dress the meat in my sight, that I may see it, and eat it at her hand.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-samuel/13#v6)</sup> So Amnon lay down, and made himself sick: and when the king was come to see him, Amnon said unto the king, I pray thee, let Tamar my sister come, and make me a couple of cakes in my sight, that I may eat at her hand.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-samuel/13#v7)</sup> Then David sent home to Tamar, saying, Go now to thy brother Amnon’s house, and dress him meat.

- Jonadab asked Amnon why he, the king's son, was lean from day to day, and Amnon confessed he loved Tamar. Jonadab told him to lie down and make himself sick, and when David came to see him, to ask that Tamar come and dress meat in his sight that he might eat at her hand.
- This was the fastest, sneakiest advice: play sick, and when your father comes to check on you, say you just want to eat cakes from Tamar's hand. David sent home to Tamar and told her to go dress her brother meat.

**[2 Samuel 13:8-11](/bible/2-samuel/13#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/2-samuel/13#v8)</sup> So Tamar went to her brother Amnon’s house; and he was laid down. And she took flour, and kneaded it, and made cakes in his sight, and did bake the cakes.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-samuel/13#v9)</sup> And she took a pan, and poured them out before him; but he refused to eat. And Amnon said, Have out all men from me. And they went out every man from him.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-samuel/13#v10)</sup> And Amnon said unto Tamar, Bring the meat into the chamber, that I may eat of thine hand. And Tamar took the cakes which she had made, and brought them into the chamber to Amnon her brother.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-samuel/13#v11)</sup> And when she had brought them unto him to eat, he took hold of her, and said unto her, Come lie with me, my sister.

- Tamar went to Amnon's house, took flour, kneaded it, made cakes in his sight and baked them, but he refused to eat and commanded every man out from him. Then he told Tamar to bring the meat into the chamber that he might eat of her hand, and when she brought it, he took hold of her and said, Come lie with me, my sister.
- He cleared the room first so there would be no witnesses, then seized his own sister.

**[2 Samuel 13:12-14](/bible/2-samuel/13#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/2-samuel/13#v12)</sup> And she answered him, Nay, my brother, do not force me; for no such thing ought to be done in Israel: do not thou this folly.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-samuel/13#v13)</sup> And I, whither shall I cause my shame to go? and as for thee, thou shalt be as one of the fools in Israel. Now therefore, I pray thee, speak unto the king; for he will not withhold me from thee.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-samuel/13#v14)</sup> Howbeit he would not hearken unto her voice: but, being stronger than she, forced her, and lay with her.

- Tamar answered, Nay, my brother, do not force me, for no such thing ought to be done in Israel, do not thou this folly. But he would not hearken to her voice, and being stronger than she, forced her and lay with her.
- Tamar herself calls this folly, a sin, a thing that ought not to be done in Israel. Any man talking about forcing a woman is wicked; this shows plainly from the scriptures that rape is not good in the Bible. It is the same as murder.

  - **[Deuteronomy 22:25-26](/bible/deuteronomy/22#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/deuteronomy/22#v25)</sup> But if a man find a betrothed damsel in the field, and the man force her, and lie with her: then the man only that lay with her shall die:
    >
    > <sup>[26](/bible/deuteronomy/22#v26)</sup> But unto the damsel thou shalt do nothing; there is in the damsel no sin worthy of death: for as when a man riseth against his neighbour, and slayeth him, even so is this matter:

    He that forces a betrothed damsel is worthy of death, for this is as when a man rises against his neighbour and slays him.

**[2 Samuel 13:15-18](/bible/2-samuel/13#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/2-samuel/13#v15)</sup> Then Amnon hated her exceedingly; so that the hatred wherewith he hated her was greater than the love wherewith he had loved her. And Amnon said unto her, Arise, be gone.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-samuel/13#v16)</sup> And she said unto him, There is no cause: this evil in sending me away is greater than the other that thou didst unto me. But he would not hearken unto her.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-samuel/13#v17)</sup> Then he called his servant that ministered unto him, and said, Put now this woman out from me, and bolt the door after her.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-samuel/13#v18)</sup> And she had a garment of divers colours upon her: for with such robes were the king’s daughters that were virgins apparelled. Then his servant brought her out, and bolted the door after her.

- After he forced her, Amnon hated her exceedingly, so that the hatred was greater than the love he had for her, and he told her to arise and be gone. Tamar said the evil of sending her away was greater than the other he had done, but he would not hearken, and called his servant to put her out and bolt the door after her. She had a garment of diverse colours, for so were the king's daughters that were virgins apparelled.
- The king's daughters that were virgins, meaning those able to be married, dressed a certain way, in a garment of diverse colours, so you could tell them apart from the other women. That was part of the order in the kingdom, and it is why we wear garments today; the men back in the day did the same thing, and that is called rehearsing the righteous acts.

**[2 Samuel 13:19-20](/bible/2-samuel/13#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/2-samuel/13#v19)</sup> And Tamar put ashes on her head, and rent her garment of divers colours that was on her, and laid her hand on her head, and went on crying.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-samuel/13#v20)</sup> And Absalom her brother said unto her, Hath Amnon thy brother been with thee? but hold now thy peace, my sister: he is thy brother; regard not this thing. So Tamar remained desolate in her brother Absalom’s house.

- Tamar put ashes on her head, rent her garment of diverse colours, laid her hand on her head, and went out crying. Absalom her brother asked whether Amnon her brother had been with her, told her to hold her peace for now since he was her brother, and Tamar remained desolate in her brother Absalom's house.
- Absalom, her older brother, told her not to worry about it for now. But Absalom was one of those men who holds a grudge for years just to get someone back for it, and remember these are people who know the law and know they are Israel.

**[2 Samuel 13:21-22](/bible/2-samuel/13#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/2-samuel/13#v21)</sup> But when king David heard of all these things, he was very wroth.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-samuel/13#v22)</sup> And Absalom spake unto his brother Amnon neither good nor bad: for Absalom hated Amnon, because he had forced his sister Tamar.

- When King David heard all these things he was very wroth. And Absalom spake unto his brother Amnon neither good nor bad, for Absalom hated Amnon because he had forced his sister Tamar.
- David was upset at what happened to Tamar but did not do anything about it. Absalom said nothing to Amnon, but he was quietly holding on to the offense, waiting.

**[2 Samuel 13:23-27](/bible/2-samuel/13#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/2-samuel/13#v23)</sup> And it came to pass after two full years, that Absalom had sheepshearers in Baal–hazor, which is beside Ephraim: and Absalom invited all the king’s sons.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-samuel/13#v24)</sup> And Absalom came to the king, and said, Behold now, thy servant hath sheepshearers; let the king, I beseech thee, and his servants go with thy servant.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-samuel/13#v25)</sup> And the king said to Absalom, Nay, my son, let us not all now go, lest we be chargeable unto thee. And he pressed him: howbeit he would not go, but blessed him.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-samuel/13#v26)</sup> Then said Absalom, If not, I pray thee, let my brother Amnon go with us. And the king said unto him, Why should he go with thee?
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-samuel/13#v27)</sup> But Absalom pressed him, that he let Amnon and all the king’s sons go with him.

- After two full years Absalom had sheepshearers in Baalhazor beside Ephraim and invited all the king's sons. He pressed David to come, and when David would not, he pressed that Amnon and all the king's sons go with him, and the king let them go.
- Absalom was setting Amnon up. He specifically maneuvered to get Amnon out of the city and among his own servants.

**[2 Samuel 13:28-29](/bible/2-samuel/13#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/2-samuel/13#v28)</sup> Now Absalom had commanded his servants, saying, Mark ye now when Amnon’s heart is merry with wine, and when I say unto you, Smite Amnon; then kill him, fear not: have not I commanded you? be courageous, and be valiant.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/2-samuel/13#v29)</sup> And the servants of Absalom did unto Amnon as Absalom had commanded. Then all the king’s sons arose, and every man gat him up upon his mule, and fled.

- Absalom commanded his servants to mark when Amnon's heart was merry with wine, and when he gave the word to smite and kill him, to fear not, for he had commanded them. The servants did as Absalom commanded, and all the king's sons arose, every man got upon his mule, and fled.
- Absalom told them to wait until Amnon was drunk and merry with wine, then to strike, and killed him for what he did to Tamar.

**[2 Samuel 13:30-33](/bible/2-samuel/13#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/2-samuel/13#v30)</sup> And it came to pass, while they were in the way, that tidings came to David, saying, Absalom hath slain all the king’s sons, and there is not one of them left.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/2-samuel/13#v31)</sup> Then the king arose, and tare his garments, and lay on the earth; and all his servants stood by with their clothes rent.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/2-samuel/13#v32)</sup> And Jonadab, the son of Shimeah David’s brother, answered and said, Let not my lord suppose that they have slain all the young men the king’s sons; for Amnon only is dead: for by the appointment of Absalom this hath been determined from the day that he forced his sister Tamar.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/2-samuel/13#v33)</sup> Now therefore let not my lord the king take the thing to his heart, to think that all the king’s sons are dead: for Amnon only is dead.

- While they were on the way, tidings came to David that Absalom had slain all the king's sons and not one was left. The king arose, tare his garments, and lay on the earth. But Jonadab said not to suppose all were slain, for Amnon only was dead; by the appointment of Absalom this had been determined from the day Amnon forced his sister Tamar.
- The report was exaggerated, so David thought all his sons were dead. Jonadab corrected him: only Amnon was dead, and it was because of what Amnon had done to Tamar.

**[2 Samuel 13:34-39](/bible/2-samuel/13#v34)**

> <sup>[34](/bible/2-samuel/13#v34)</sup> But Absalom fled. And the young man that kept the watch lifted up his eyes, and looked, and, behold, there came much people by the way of the hill side behind him.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/2-samuel/13#v35)</sup> And Jonadab said unto the king, Behold, the king’s sons come: as thy servant said, so it is.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/2-samuel/13#v36)</sup> And it came to pass, as soon as he had made an end of speaking, that, behold, the king’s sons came, and lifted up their voice and wept: and the king also and all his servants wept very sore.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/2-samuel/13#v37)</sup> But Absalom fled, and went to Talmai, the son of Ammihud, king of Geshur. And David mourned for his son every day.
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/2-samuel/13#v38)</sup> So Absalom fled, and went to Geshur, and was there three years.
>
> <sup>[39](/bible/2-samuel/13#v39)</sup> And the soul of king David longed to go forth unto Absalom: for he was comforted concerning Amnon, seeing he was dead.

- Absalom fled, and the watchman saw much people coming, and Jonadab confirmed the king's sons had come as he said. The king's sons came, lifted up their voice and wept, and the king and all his servants wept very sore. Absalom fled to Talmai the son of Ammihud, king of Geshur, and David mourned for his son every day, and was there three years; and the soul of David longed to go forth to Absalom, being comforted concerning Amnon since he was dead.
- Understand the mind of David: even through what he was going through, and even though many of his sons did wicked things, he still showed compassion. This is why the Most High said David is a man after his own heart; even through all our rebellion, the Most High still shows us mercy.
