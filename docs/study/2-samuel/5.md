## Introduction

This reading covers [2 Samuel 5](/bible/2-samuel/5) through 8. It moves from David being anointed king over all Israel, to his taking of the stronghold of Zion, to his battles with the Philistines, the bringing up of the ark of God, the great covenant the Most High makes with the house of David, and the wars in which David subdues the nations round about. One thread runs through all of it: David knew where his power came from. He inquired of the Lord before he went to war, he feared the Lord after Uzzah was smitten, and he purposed to build a house for the ark rather than sit at ease in his own house of cedar. The covenant of chapter 7 reaches past Solomon to Jesus Christ the black Messiah, whose kingdom is established for ever.

**[2 Samuel 5:1-5](/bible/2-samuel/5#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/5#v1)</sup> Then came all the tribes of Israel to David unto Hebron, and spake, saying, Behold, we are thy bone and thy flesh.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/5#v2)</sup> Also in time past, when Saul was king over us, thou wast he that leddest out and broughtest in Israel: and the Lord said to thee, Thou shalt feed my people Israel, and thou shalt be a captain over Israel.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/5#v3)</sup> So all the elders of Israel came to the king to Hebron; and king David made a league with them in Hebron before the Lord: and they anointed David king over Israel.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-samuel/5#v4)</sup> David was thirty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned forty years.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-samuel/5#v5)</sup> In Hebron he reigned over Judah seven years and six months: and in Jerusalem he reigned thirty and three years over all Israel and Judah.

- All the tribes of Israel came to David in Hebron and said, Behold, we are thy bone and thy flesh, meaning we are kinsmen, related to thee.
- They remembered that even when Saul was king, David was the one that led them out and brought them in as captain, for David fought many wars and had fame among the people. The Lord had said unto David that he should feed His people Israel and be a captain over Israel.
- The elders of Israel came to Hebron, and David made a league with them before the Lord, and they anointed David king over Israel. This is when David was officially anointed king over all the nation.
- David was thirty years old when he began to reign, a young man, and he reigned forty years: seven years over Judah in Hebron, and thirty and three years over all Israel and Judah in Jerusalem.

**[2 Samuel 5:6-10](/bible/2-samuel/5#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/2-samuel/5#v6)</sup> And the king and his men went to Jerusalem unto the Jebusites, the inhabitants of the land: which spake unto David, saying, Except thou take away the blind and the lame, thou shalt not come in hither: thinking, David cannot come in hither.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-samuel/5#v7)</sup> Nevertheless David took the strong hold of Zion: the same is the city of David.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-samuel/5#v8)</sup> And David said on that day, Whosoever getteth up to the gutter, and smiteth the Jebusites, and the lame and the blind, that are hated of David’s soul, he shall be chief and captain. Wherefore they said, The blind and the lame shall not come into the house.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-samuel/5#v9)</sup> So David dwelt in the fort, and called it the city of David. And David built round about from Millo and inward.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-samuel/5#v10)</sup> And David went on, and grew great, and the Lord God of hosts was with him.

- The Jebusites, inhabitants of the land, thought to put a stipulation on David that he could not come in unless he took away the blind and the lame, thinking he could not come in at all. They did not reckon with a man who had already slain his tens of thousands.
- Nevertheless David took the stronghold of Zion, the same is the city of David. When the scripture says nevertheless, it means the thing will be done regardless of what stands against it.
- David said that whosoever smote the Jebusites should be chief and captain. This was the type of warrior David was: a man after God's own heart, who would not let any nation stand in the way of the Lord's people.
- David dwelt in the fort and grew great, and the Lord God of hosts was with him. The Most High was always with David, in the wars he fought and in the ones to come.
- Our forefathers were aggressive toward the nations in that time period, but that is not the time we are in now. The word tells us to be at peace with all men; we do not break the laws of the land, for this is at present a spiritual battle and not a physical one.

>

- Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David with cedar trees, carpenters, and masons, and they built David a house. Hiram was a man of Tyre, and this league would later serve to build the temple in Solomon's day.
- David perceived that the Lord had established him king over Israel and had exalted his kingdom for His people Israel's sake.
- David took more concubines and wives, and more sons and daughters were born to him in Jerusalem. That David had multiple wives does not make it right; the Most High forgave David many of his sins, but the brothers who teach a multiple-wives doctrine are not David and are not men after God's own heart. Not one of our forefathers besides Christ was without sin.

**[2 Samuel 5:17-21](/bible/2-samuel/5#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/2-samuel/5#v17)</sup> But when the Philistines heard that they had anointed David king over Israel, all the Philistines came up to seek David; and David heard of it, and went down to the hold.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-samuel/5#v18)</sup> The Philistines also came and spread themselves in the valley of Rephaim.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-samuel/5#v19)</sup> And David enquired of the Lord, saying, Shall I go up to the Philistines? wilt thou deliver them into mine hand? And the Lord said unto David, Go up: for I will doubtless deliver the Philistines into thine hand.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-samuel/5#v20)</sup> And David came to Baal–perazim, and David smote them there, and said, The Lord hath broken forth upon mine enemies before me, as the breach of waters. Therefore he called the name of that place Baal–perazim.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-samuel/5#v21)</sup> And there they left their images, and David and his men burned them.

- When the Philistines heard David was anointed king, they came up to seek him, spreading themselves in the valley of Rephaim. They had already lost their champion Goliath, yet they came again to challenge this man.
- David inquired of the Lord, Shall I go up to the Philistines? Wilt thou deliver them into mine hand? He did not go to war on his own strength; he sought counsel first, for he knew where his power lay. The Lord answered that He would doubtless deliver them into David's hand.
- David came to Baalperazim and smote them, and said the Lord had broken forth upon his enemies as a breach of waters. The victory was the Lord's, for the Lord is a man of war.
- The Philistines left their images there, and David and his men burned them. David knew the law, that the graven images of the nations were to be burned with fire and their silver and gold not desired, lest a man be snared and cursed by bringing an abomination into his house.

**[2 Samuel 5:22-25](/bible/2-samuel/5#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/2-samuel/5#v22)</sup> And the Philistines came up yet again, and spread themselves in the valley of Rephaim.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-samuel/5#v23)</sup> And when David enquired of the Lord, he said, Thou shalt not go up; but fetch a compass behind them, and come upon them over against the mulberry trees.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-samuel/5#v24)</sup> And let it be, when thou hearest the sound of a going in the tops of the mulberry trees, that then thou shalt bestir thyself: for then shall the Lord go out before thee, to smite the host of the Philistines.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-samuel/5#v25)</sup> And David did so, as the Lord had commanded him; and smote the Philistines from Geba until thou come to Gazer.

- The Philistines came up yet again and spread themselves in the valley of Rephaim. The Most High hardened the hearts of the nations, so it was of God that they came back.
- When David inquired this time, the Lord said, Thou shalt not go up, but fetch a compass behind them and come upon them over against the mulberry trees. This is war strategy given by the Lord: David sought counsel again rather than assume the last answer.
- David was to move when he heard the sound of a going in the tops of the mulberry trees, for then the Lord would go out before him to smite the host of the Philistines. David did as the Lord commanded and smote them from Geba to Gazer.
- Because David meditated in the law, he knew the Lord was his power source. The Lord had promised that five would chase a hundred and a hundred put ten thousand to flight, that He would cause Israel's enemies to be smitten and to flee seven ways. David leaned wholly on that promise.
