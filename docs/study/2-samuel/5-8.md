## Introduction

This reading covers [2 Samuel 5](/bible/2-samuel/5) through 8. It moves from David being anointed king over all Israel, to his taking of the stronghold of Zion, to his battles with the Philistines, the bringing up of the ark of God, the great covenant the Most High makes with the house of David, and the wars in which David subdues the nations round about. One thread runs through all of it: David knew where his power came from. He inquired of the Lord before he went to war, he feared the Lord after Uzzah was smitten, and he purposed to build a house for the ark rather than sit at ease in his own house of cedar. The covenant of chapter 7 reaches past Solomon to Jesus Christ the black Messiah, whose kingdom is established for ever.

> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/5#v2)</sup> Also in time past, when Saul was king over us, thou wast he that leddest out and broughtest in Israel: and the Lord said to thee, Thou shalt feed my people Israel, and thou shalt be a captain over Israel.

> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/5#v3)</sup> So all the elders of Israel came to the king to Hebron; and king David made a league with them in Hebron before the Lord: and they anointed David king over Israel.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-samuel/5#v4)</sup> David was thirty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned forty years.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-samuel/5#v5)</sup> In Hebron he reigned over Judah seven years and six months: and in Jerusalem he reigned thirty and three years over all Israel and Judah.

- All the tribes of Israel came to David in Hebron and said, Behold, we are thy bone and thy flesh, meaning we are kinsmen, related to thee.
- They remembered that even when Saul was king, David was the one that led them out and brought them in as captain, for David fought many wars and had fame among the people. The Lord had said unto David that he should feed His people Israel and be a captain over Israel.
- The elders of Israel came to Hebron, and David made a league with them before the Lord, and they anointed David king over Israel. This is when David was officially anointed king over all the nation.
- David was thirty years old when he began to reign, a young man, and he reigned forty years: seven years over Judah in Hebron, and thirty and three years over all Israel and Judah in Jerusalem.

**[2 Samuel 5:6-10](/bible/2-samuel/5#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/2-samuel/5#v6)</sup> And the king and his men went to Jerusalem unto the Jebusites, the inhabitants of the land: which spake unto David, saying, Except thou take away the blind and the lame, thou shalt not come in hither: thinking, David cannot come in hither.

>

> <sup>[9](/bible/2-samuel/5#v9)</sup> So David dwelt in the fort, and called it the city of David. And David built round about from Millo and inward.
>

- The Jebusites, inhabitants of the land, thought to put a stipulation on David that he could not come in unless he took away the blind and the lame, thinking he could not come in at all. They did not reckon with a man who had already slain his tens of thousands.
- Nevertheless David took the stronghold of Zion, the same is the city of David. When the scripture says nevertheless, it means the thing will be done regardless of what stands against it.
- David said that whosoever smote the Jebusites should be chief and captain. This was the type of warrior David was: a man after God's own heart, who would not let any nation stand in the way of the Lord's people.
- David dwelt in the fort and grew great, and the Lord God of hosts was with him. The Most High was always with David, in the wars he fought and in the ones to come.
- Our forefathers were aggressive toward the nations in that time period, but that is not the time we are in now. The word tells us to be at peace with all men; we do not break the laws of the land, for this is at present a spiritual battle and not a physical one.

- David came to Baalperazim and smote them, and said the Lord had broken forth upon his enemies as a breach of waters. The victory was the Lord's, for the Lord is a man of war.
- The Philistines left their images there, and David and his men burned them. David knew the law, that the graven images of the nations were to be burned with fire and their silver and gold not desired, lest a man be snared and cursed by bringing an abomination into his house.

- When the king sat in his house and the Lord had given him rest from all his enemies round about, David said to Nathan the prophet that he dwelt in a house of cedar while the ark of God dwelt within curtains. David was not seeking his own riches but a place for the ark.
- Nathan told David to do all that was in his heart, for the Lord was with him. But that night the word of the Lord came to Nathan.
- The Lord asked, Shalt thou build me a house to dwell in? He had not dwelt in any house since bringing Israel out of Egypt but had walked in a tent and a tabernacle, and had never asked any tribe why they built Him not a house of cedar.

> <sup>[8](/bible/2-samuel/7#v8)</sup> Now therefore so shalt thou say unto my servant David, Thus saith the Lord of hosts, I took thee from the sheepcote, from following the sheep, to be ruler over my people, over Israel:

- The Lord reminded David that He took him from the sheepcote, from following the sheep, to be ruler over His people Israel, and was with him whithersoever he went, cutting off all his enemies and making him a great name.
- The Most High promised to appoint a place for His people Israel and to plant them that they might dwell in a place of their own and move no more, and that the children of wickedness should afflict them no more.
- The Lord calls the nations the children of wickedness, the same as when it is taught that Esau is of the wicked one. He also promised David rest from all his enemies, and that the Lord Himself would make David a house.

> <sup>[12](/bible/2-samuel/7#v12)</sup> And when thy days be fulfilled, and thou shalt sleep with thy fathers, I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels, and I will establish his kingdom.

> <sup>[16](/bible/2-samuel/7#v16)</sup> And thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever before thee: thy throne shall be established for ever.

- The Lord said that when David's days were fulfilled and he slept with his fathers, He would set up his seed after him, proceeding out of his bowels, and establish his kingdom; that seed would build a house for the Lord's name.
- The word reaches past Solomon. Solomon built the house and sat on the throne, but his kingdom was not established for ever; his son Rehoboam would not hear counsel, and the kingdom split into two nations. The phrase "for ever" points to Jesus Christ the black Messiah, who came through David's lineage.
- The Lord said He would be his father and he His son, and if he committed iniquity He would chasten him with the rod of men. Solomon was not chastened with the rod of men in his lifetime; the one smitten with the rod upon the cheek was Jesus the Christ, who did no sin, though it was Solomon who sinned.
- The Lord promised that His mercy would not depart from him as it did from Saul, and that David's house, kingdom, and throne would be established for ever. The Lord did not make the Bible easy in every place, that He might see who would research and learn precept upon precept.

# 2 Samuel 8: David Subdues the Nations

Read the chapter → [2 Samuel 8](/bible/2-samuel/8)

**[2 Samuel 8:1-8](/bible/2-samuel/8#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/8#v1)</sup> And after this it came to pass, that David smote the Philistines, and subdued them: and David took Metheg–ammah out of the hand of the Philistines.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/8#v2)</sup> And he smote Moab, and measured them with a line, casting them down to the ground; even with two lines measured he to put to death, and with one full line to keep alive. And so the Moabites became David’s servants, and brought gifts.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/8#v3)</sup> David smote also Hadadezer, the son of Rehob, king of Zobah, as he went to recover his border at the river Euphrates.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-samuel/8#v4)</sup> And David took from him a thousand chariots, and seven hundred horsemen, and twenty thousand footmen: and David houghed all the chariot horses, but reserved of them for an hundred chariots.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-samuel/8#v5)</sup> And when the Syrians of Damascus came to succour Hadadezer king of Zobah, David slew of the Syrians two and twenty thousand men.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-samuel/8#v6)</sup> Then David put garrisons in Syria of Damascus: and the Syrians became servants to David, and brought gifts. And the Lord preserved David whithersoever he went.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-samuel/8#v7)</sup> And David took the shields of gold that were on the servants of Hadadezer, and brought them to Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-samuel/8#v8)</sup> And from Betah, and from Berothai, cities of Hadadezer, king David took exceeding much brass.

- David smote the Philistines and subdued them, taking Methegammah out of their hand, and smote Moab, measuring them with a line, casting them to the ground. Our forefather David was a mighty warrior, subduing two different nations, which nations do not usually do at once.
- With two lines he measured Moab to death and with one full line to keep alive; the Moabites became David's servants and brought gifts. War was not about killing everyone, for the living become servants and pay tribute; the fear was put in the nations so they would not fight back.
- David smote Hadadezer the son of Rehob, king of Zobah, as he went to recover his border at the river Euphrates, taking a thousand chariots, seven hundred horsemen, and twenty thousand footmen, houghing the chariot horses but reserving a hundred chariots.
- When the Syrians of Damascus came to help Hadadezer, David slew twenty and two thousand of them and put garrisons in Syria, and the Syrians became servants and brought gifts. The Lord preserved David whithersoever he went. David took the shields of gold and much brass and carried them to Jerusalem.

**[2 Samuel 8:9-14](/bible/2-samuel/8#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/2-samuel/8#v9)</sup> When Toi king of Hamath heard that David had smitten all the host of Hadadezer,
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-samuel/8#v10)</sup> Then Toi sent Joram his son unto king David, to salute him, and to bless him, because he had fought against Hadadezer, and smitten him: for Hadadezer had wars with Toi. And Joram brought with him vessels of silver, and vessels of gold, and vessels of brass:
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-samuel/8#v11)</sup> Which also king David did dedicate unto the Lord, with the silver and gold that he had dedicated of all nations which he subdued;
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-samuel/8#v12)</sup> Of Syria, and of Moab, and of the children of Ammon, and of the Philistines, and of Amalek, and of the spoil of Hadadezer, son of Rehob, king of Zobah.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-samuel/8#v13)</sup> And David gat him a name when he returned from smiting of the Syrians in the valley of salt, being eighteen thousand men.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-samuel/8#v14)</sup> And he put garrisons in Edom; throughout all Edom put he garrisons, and all they of Edom became David’s servants. And the Lord preserved David whithersoever he went.

- When Toi king of Hamath heard that David had smitten all the host of Hadadezer, he sent his son Joram to salute and bless David, because Hadadezer had had wars with Toi. Joram brought vessels of silver, gold, and brass.
- This is the making of allies: when a stronger king removes an enemy you could never beat, the wise thing is to ally with him. David's kingdom made allies, but not by serving their idols or giving sons and daughters in marriage; the alliances served needful things, as Hiram later helped build the temple.
- David dedicated the silver and gold to the Lord, along with that dedicated from all the nations he subdued: Syria, Moab, the children of Ammon, the Philistines, Amalek, and the spoil of Hadadezer.
- David got a name when he returned from smiting the Syrians in the valley of salt, eighteen thousand men. He put garrisons throughout all Edom, and all they of Edom became David's servants, and the Lord preserved David whithersoever he went.

**[2 Samuel 8:15-18](/bible/2-samuel/8#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/2-samuel/8#v15)</sup> And David reigned over all Israel; and David executed judgment and justice unto all his people.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-samuel/8#v16)</sup> And Joab the son of Zeruiah was over the host; and Jehoshaphat the son of Ahilud was recorder;
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-samuel/8#v17)</sup> And Zadok the son of Ahitub, and Ahimelech the son of Abiathar, were the priests; and Seraiah was the scribe;
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-samuel/8#v18)</sup> And Benaiah the son of Jehoiada was over both the Cherethites and the Pelethites; and David’s sons were chief rulers.

- David reigned over all Israel and executed judgment and justice unto all his people. This is the mark of a real king and ruler: to lead the people in true judgment according to the commandments.
- The prayer of a servant is to one day have the spirit of King David, to lead brothers and sisters in justice and truth, and for all the brothers to become such leaders, each praying that those under them be better than themselves.
- David set his officers: Joab over the host; Jehoshaphat the recorder; Zadok and Ahimelech the priests; Seraiah the scribe; Benaiah over the Cherethites and Pelethites; and David's sons were chief rulers.
