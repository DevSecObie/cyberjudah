## Introduction

Chapter 15 shows Absalom's conspiracy come to a head. He prepares chariots and horsemen, stands by the gate, and steals the hearts of the men of Israel with friendish flattery, telling every man his cause is good but there is no man deputed of the king to hear him. Under the pretense of paying a vow in Hebron he has himself proclaimed king, and the conspiracy grows so strong that David flees Jerusalem with his household and his loyal men. David sends the ark back to the city in faith, and sends his friend Hushai back to defeat the counsel of Ahithophel. David treated this as a game of chess, not checkers, strategizing even as he wept, and much of the book of Psalms was written while David was on the run like this.

**[2 Samuel 15:1-4](/bible/2-samuel/15#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/15#v1)</sup> And it came to pass after this, that Absalom prepared him chariots and horses, and fifty men to run before him.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/15#v2)</sup> And Absalom rose up early, and stood beside the way of the gate: and it was so, that when any man that had a controversy came to the king for judgment, then Absalom called unto him, and said, Of what city art thou? And he said, Thy servant is of one of the tribes of Israel.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/15#v3)</sup> And Absalom said unto him, See, thy matters are good and right; but there is no man deputed of the king to hear thee.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-samuel/15#v4)</sup> Absalom said moreover, Oh that I were made judge in the land, that every man which hath any suit or cause might come unto me, and I would do him justice!

- Absalom prepared chariots and horses and fifty men to run before him, rose up early, and stood beside the way of the gate. When any man came to the king for judgment, Absalom called him, said his matters were good and right but there was no man deputed of the king to hear him, and wished that he himself were made judge in the land that he might do every man justice.
- This is the sneakiness Absalom did: he began to underhand the king, winning the love of the people behind the king's back, so that he could later betray him, as the prophecy said the sword would not depart from David's house.

**[2 Samuel 15:5-6](/bible/2-samuel/15#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/2-samuel/15#v5)</sup> And it was so, that when any man came nigh to him to do him obeisance, he put forth his hand, and took him, and kissed him.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-samuel/15#v6)</sup> And on this manner did Absalom to all Israel that came to the king for judgment: so Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel.

- When any man came near to do him obeisance, Absalom put forth his hand, took him, and kissed him, and on this manner he did to all Israel that came to the king for judgment. So Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel.
- Instead of going to the king and resolving things, Absalom got into the king's good graces, then went around patting people on the back and stroking them. This is what the bishop calls friendish, showing people friendship without being a true friend.

**[2 Samuel 15:7-12](/bible/2-samuel/15#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/2-samuel/15#v7)</sup> And it came to pass after forty years, that Absalom said unto the king, I pray thee, let me go and pay my vow, which I have vowed unto the Lord, in Hebron.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-samuel/15#v8)</sup> For thy servant vowed a vow while I abode at Geshur in Syria, saying, If the Lord shall bring me again indeed to Jerusalem, then I will serve the Lord.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-samuel/15#v9)</sup> And the king said unto him, Go in peace. So he arose, and went to Hebron.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-samuel/15#v10)</sup> But Absalom sent spies throughout all the tribes of Israel, saying, As soon as ye hear the sound of the trumpet, then ye shall say, Absalom reigneth in Hebron.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-samuel/15#v11)</sup> And with Absalom went two hundred men out of Jerusalem, that were called; and they went in their simplicity, and they knew not any thing.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-samuel/15#v12)</sup> And Absalom sent for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David’s counsellor, from his city, even from Giloh, while he offered sacrifices. And the conspiracy was strong; for the people increased continually with Absalom.

- After forty years Absalom asked leave to go pay his vow in Hebron, and the king said, Go in peace. But Absalom sent spies through all the tribes to say, at the sound of the trumpet, Absalom reigneth in Hebron. Two hundred men went with him from Jerusalem in their simplicity, knowing nothing. Absalom also sent for Ahithophel, David's counsellor, and the conspiracy was strong, for the people increased continually with Absalom.
- This shows the simplicity of a lot of brothers in Israel: a man who says all the nice things and gains favor in your eyes goes completely against leadership and out of order, and you follow along carnally, not spiritually, because he kissed your hand and told you everything would be all right.

**[2 Samuel 15:13-18](/bible/2-samuel/15#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/2-samuel/15#v13)</sup> And there came a messenger to David, saying, The hearts of the men of Israel are after Absalom.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-samuel/15#v14)</sup> And David said unto all his servants that were with him at Jerusalem, Arise, and let us flee; for we shall not else escape from Absalom: make speed to depart, lest he overtake us suddenly, and bring evil upon us, and smite the city with the edge of the sword.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-samuel/15#v15)</sup> And the king’s servants said unto the king, Behold, thy servants are ready to do whatsoever my lord the king shall appoint.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-samuel/15#v16)</sup> And the king went forth, and all his household after him. And the king left ten women, which were concubines, to keep the house.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-samuel/15#v17)</sup> And the king went forth, and all the people after him, and tarried in a place that was far off.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-samuel/15#v18)</sup> And all his servants passed on beside him; and all the Cherethites, and all the Pelethites, and all the Gittites, six hundred men which came after him from Gath, passed on before the king.

- A messenger told David the hearts of the men of Israel were with Absalom, and David told his servants to arise and flee, lest Absalom overtake them suddenly and smite the city with the sword. The king went forth with all his household, leaving ten women who were concubines to keep the house, and all his servants and the Cherethites, Pelethites, and six hundred Gittites passed on before him.
- David, being the man of war that he is, saw everyone starting to follow Absalom, and instead of sitting there like a dummy, chose to leave and strategize while he was out.

**[2 Samuel 15:19-23](/bible/2-samuel/15#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/2-samuel/15#v19)</sup> Then said the king to Ittai the Gittite, Wherefore goest thou also with us? return to thy place, and abide with the king: for thou art a stranger, and also an exile.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-samuel/15#v20)</sup> Whereas thou camest but yesterday, should I this day make thee go up and down with us? seeing I go whither I may, return thou, and take back thy brethren: mercy and truth be with thee.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-samuel/15#v21)</sup> And Ittai answered the king, and said, As the Lord liveth, and as my lord the king liveth, surely in what place my lord the king shall be, whether in death or life, even there also will thy servant be.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-samuel/15#v22)</sup> And David said to Ittai, Go and pass over. And Ittai the Gittite passed over, and all his men, and all the little ones that were with him.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-samuel/15#v23)</sup> And all the country wept with a loud voice, and all the people passed over: the king also himself passed over the brook Kidron, and all the people passed over, toward the way of the wilderness.

- The king told Ittai the Gittite to return, since he was a stranger and an exile who had only just come, and wished him mercy and truth. But Ittai swore that in whatever place the king was, in death or life, there also would his servant be, so David said, Go and pass over, and Ittai passed over with all his men and little ones. All the country wept as the people passed over the brook Kidron toward the wilderness.
- Ittai showed a level of loyalty that David respected, so David let him come. This loyalty in the face of trouble is the same thing seen throughout Israel's story.

**[2 Samuel 15:24-29](/bible/2-samuel/15#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/2-samuel/15#v24)</sup> And lo Zadok also, and all the Levites were with him, bearing the ark of the covenant of God: and they set down the ark of God; and Abiathar went up, until all the people had done passing out of the city.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-samuel/15#v25)</sup> And the king said unto Zadok, Carry back the ark of God into the city: if I shall find favour in the eyes of the Lord, he will bring me again, and shew me both it, and his habitation:
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-samuel/15#v26)</sup> But if he thus say, I have no delight in thee; behold, here am I, let him do to me as seemeth good unto him.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-samuel/15#v27)</sup> The king said also unto Zadok the priest, Art not thou a seer? return into the city in peace, and your two sons with you, Ahimaaz thy son, and Jonathan the son of Abiathar.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/2-samuel/15#v28)</sup> See, I will tarry in the plain of the wilderness, until there come word from you to certify me.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/2-samuel/15#v29)</sup> Zadok therefore and Abiathar carried the ark of God again to Jerusalem: and they tarried there.

- Zadok and all the Levites bare the ark of the covenant, and David told Zadok to carry it back into the city, saying if he found favour in the eyes of the Most High, he would be brought again to see it and its habitation; but if not, let the Most High do as seemed good. He also told Zadok the seer to return with his son Ahimaaz and Jonathan the son of Abiathar, and to send him word. So they carried the ark again to Jerusalem.
- David sent the ark back in faith: make sure it stays in the city, and if it is the Most High's will, I will return. That is how much faith David had. Many of the songs in the book of Psalms were written while David was on the run like this, and some of it is prophecy of Christ, because the spirit of Christ was in every prophet.

**[2 Samuel 15:30-37](/bible/2-samuel/15#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/2-samuel/15#v30)</sup> And David went up by the ascent of mount Olivet, and wept as he went up, and had his head covered, and he went barefoot: and all the people that was with him covered every man his head, and they went up, weeping as they went up.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/2-samuel/15#v31)</sup> And one told David, saying, Ahithophel is among the conspirators with Absalom. And David said, O Lord, I pray thee, turn the counsel of Ahithophel into foolishness.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/2-samuel/15#v32)</sup> And it came to pass, that when David was come to the top of the mount, where he worshipped God, behold, Hushai the Archite came to meet him with his coat rent, and earth upon his head:
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/2-samuel/15#v33)</sup> Unto whom David said, If thou passest on with me, then thou shalt be a burden unto me:
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/2-samuel/15#v34)</sup> But if thou return to the city, and say unto Absalom, I will be thy servant, O king; as I have been thy father’s servant hitherto, so will I now also be thy servant: then mayest thou for me defeat the counsel of Ahithophel.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/2-samuel/15#v35)</sup> And hast thou not there with thee Zadok and Abiathar the priests? therefore it shall be, that what thing soever thou shalt hear out of the king’s house, thou shalt tell it to Zadok and Abiathar the priests.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/2-samuel/15#v36)</sup> Behold, they have there with them their two sons, Ahimaaz Zadok’s son, and Jonathan Abiathar’s son; and by them ye shall send unto me every thing that ye can hear.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/2-samuel/15#v37)</sup> So Hushai David’s friend came into the city, and Absalom came into Jerusalem.

- David went up the ascent of Mount Olivet weeping, barefoot, his head covered, and all the people with him weeping. When told that Ahithophel was among the conspirators, David prayed that the Most High turn Ahithophel's counsel into foolishness. Then Hushai the Archite met him with his coat rent, and David sent him back to the city to feign service to Absalom and defeat Ahithophel's counsel, reporting all he heard to Zadok and Abiathar by their two sons.
- Ahithophel was such a good counsellor that his counsel was said to be like inquiring at the oracle of God, like talking to an angel of the Most High. So David prayed the Most High would turn that counsel into foolishness, and sent his friend Hushai to act like Absalom's servant so he could listen to Ahithophel's counsel and intervene. This was a game of chess, not checkers, and that is how we have to treat this thing today.
