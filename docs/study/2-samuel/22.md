## Introduction

This chapter is David's song of deliverance, spoken in the day the Most High delivered him out of the hand of all his enemies and out of the hand of Saul. David praises the Most High as his rock, his fortress, his shield and the horn of his salvation, and he confesses that all his strength, his war, and his victories came from God and not from himself. The thread is trust in the Lord, keeping his ways, and giving the glory of every deliverance back to the Most High.

**[2 Samuel 22:1-4](/bible/2-samuel/22#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/22#v1)</sup> And David spake unto the Lord the words of this song in the day that the Lord had delivered him out of the hand of all his enemies, and out of the hand of Saul:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/22#v2)</sup> And he said, The Lord is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer;
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/22#v3)</sup> The God of my rock; in him will I trust: he is my shield, and the horn of my salvation, my high tower, and my refuge, my saviour; thou savest me from violence.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-samuel/22#v4)</sup> I will call on the Lord, who is worthy to be praised: so shall I be saved from mine enemies.

- David spoke unto the Lord the words of this song in the day the Lord had delivered him out of the hand of all his enemies and out of the hand of Saul. He called the Lord his rock, his fortress, and his deliverer, his shield and the horn of his salvation, his high tower and his refuge, his saviour who saved him from violence.
- This is heavy: like Judas Maccabee, Simon, Jonathan and all our forefathers, the first thing David did in trouble was turn to the Most High. They trusted in God and not in man. Today many of our people have it backwards; they trust in a vote and in man and then call on God.

- In his distress he called upon the Lord and cried to his God, and the Most High heard his voice out of his temple and his cry entered into his ears. When we cry to the Most High in truth, he hears.

- The earth shook and trembled, the foundations of heaven moved, smoke went up out of his nostrils and fire out of his mouth devoured; he bowed the heavens and came down with darkness under his feet, rode upon a cherub and did fly, and was seen upon the wings of the wind. That is how the Most High travels, shielded in the darkness of the cloud, the same way he came down and spoke to Moses face to face.
- The Lord thundered from heaven and the Most High uttered his voice. What we hear as thunder is the Most High speaking, but we cannot comprehend it because we no longer have that spirit upon us. Esau says the thunder is only clouds clashing; the scripture says it is the voice of the Lord, and after his voice there is a multitude of waters, meaning the rain that follows.
- The Most High sent out arrows and lightning and scattered his enemies, and the channels of the sea and the foundations of the world were discovered at the rebuking of the Lord, at the blast of the breath of his nostrils. His power over creation is total.

He kept himself from his own iniquity, holding back his flesh; the one time it overtook him he was judged and got right back on track, for a righteous man falls and rises again.

- The afflicted people the Most High saves, but his eyes are upon the haughty to bring them down. The afflicted is Israel. He is David's lamp and lightens his darkness, for by the Most High David ran through a troop, a body of some two hundred men or more.

- As for God, his way is perfect and the word of the Lord is tried; he is a buckler, a shield, to all that trust in him. There is no other God save the Lord, and no rock save our God. This is the answer to the Muslims and Negroes practicing Islam who try to make this rock into a cobblestone; it is talking about the Most High God of Israel, not a stone.
- God is David's strength and power and maketh his way perfect; this is what Israel forgot. We waxed fat and kicked, forgot the Most High and where our strength came from, and began to say we are lords ourselves. But our strength comes from the Most High, who is merciful enough to let us take a breath and blink our eyes every moment.
- The Most High made David's feet like hinds' feet and set him upon high places, and taught his hands to war so that a bow of steel is broken by his arms, not bent but broken. He gave David the shield of his salvation and his gentleness made him great; his repentance enlarged his steps so his feet did not slip. David knew a lion and a bear were nothing to a man trained by the Most High.

- David is prophesying, for this song is twofold; it speaks of David in that moment and of us who are yet to come. The Most High requires of us far less than our iniquity deserves: to repent, keep the commandments, and keep the faith in his son Jesus the Christ. For this David gives thanks among the heathen and sings praises unto the name of the Lord, who shows mercy to his anointed, to David and to his seed for evermore.
