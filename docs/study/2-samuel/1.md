## Introduction

This reading covers [2 Samuel 1](/bible/2-samuel/1) through 4, the opening of the second book of Samuel. It moves from the death of Saul and Jonathan to David's coming into the kingdom. The Amalekite who claimed to have slain the Lord's anointed is put to death; David laments Saul and Jonathan in the spirit; David is anointed king over Judah while Ishbosheth is set up over the rest of Israel; and the long war between the two houses runs its course, with David waxing stronger and the house of Saul weaker. Running through it all is one thread: the Most High has His own way of bringing His purpose to pass, and men who take vengeance and shed blood by their own hand, out of order and not ordained of the Lord, answer for it.

**[2 Samuel 1:1-4](/bible/2-samuel/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/1#v1)</sup> Now it came to pass after the death of Saul, when David was returned from the slaughter of the Amalekites, and David had abode two days in Ziklag;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/1#v2)</sup> It came even to pass on the third day, that, behold, a man came out of the camp from Saul with his clothes rent, and earth upon his head: and so it was, when he came to David, that he fell to the earth, and did obeisance.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/1#v3)</sup> And David said unto him, From whence comest thou? And he said unto him, Out of the camp of Israel am I escaped.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-samuel/1#v4)</sup> And David said unto him, How went the matter? I pray thee, tell me. And he answered, That the people are fled from the battle, and many of the people also are fallen and dead; and Saul and Jonathan his son are dead also.

- After the death of Saul, when David had returned from the slaughter of the Amalekites and abode two days in Ziklag, a man came out of the camp of Israel with his clothes rent and earth upon his head, and fell to the earth before David to do him honor.
- David asked him how the matter went, and he answered that the people were fled from the battle, many had fallen and were dead, and Saul and Jonathan his son were dead also.

**[2 Samuel 1:5-10](/bible/2-samuel/1#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/2-samuel/1#v5)</sup> And David said unto the young man that told him, How knowest thou that Saul and Jonathan his son be dead?
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-samuel/1#v6)</sup> And the young man that told him said, As I happened by chance upon mount Gilboa, behold, Saul leaned upon his spear; and, lo, the chariots and horsemen followed hard after him.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-samuel/1#v7)</sup> And when he looked behind him, he saw me, and called unto me. And I answered, Here am I.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-samuel/1#v8)</sup> And he said unto me, Who art thou? And I answered him, I am an Amalekite.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-samuel/1#v9)</sup> He said unto me again, Stand, I pray thee, upon me, and slay me: for anguish is come upon me, because my life is yet whole in me.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-samuel/1#v10)</sup> So I stood upon him, and slew him, because I was sure that he could not live after that he was fallen: and I took the crown that was upon his head, and the bracelet that was on his arm, and have brought them hither unto my lord.

- The young man told David that he had come by chance upon mount Gilboa, and found Saul leaning upon his spear with the chariots and horsemen pressing hard after him. In the earlier account Saul fell upon his sword but did not die at that moment.
- The man said he was an Amalekite, and that Saul asked him to stand upon him and slay him because anguish was come upon him; so he slew him, being sure he could not live, and took the crown from his head and the bracelet from his arm and brought them to David.
- The Amalekites are the seed of Esau, of Eliphaz his son. Their lineage is written in Genesis, and it is a thing to know when reading of Israel's enemies.

**[2 Samuel 1:11-16](/bible/2-samuel/1#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/2-samuel/1#v11)</sup> Then David took hold on his clothes, and rent them; and likewise all the men that were with him:
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-samuel/1#v12)</sup> And they mourned, and wept, and fasted until even, for Saul, and for Jonathan his son, and for the people of the Lord, and for the house of Israel; because they were fallen by the sword.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-samuel/1#v13)</sup> And David said unto the young man that told him, Whence art thou? And he answered, I am the son of a stranger, an Amalekite.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-samuel/1#v14)</sup> And David said unto him, How wast thou not afraid to stretch forth thine hand to destroy the Lord’s anointed?
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-samuel/1#v15)</sup> And David called one of the young men, and said, Go near, and fall upon him. And he smote him that he died.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-samuel/1#v16)</sup> And David said unto him, Thy blood be upon thy head; for thy mouth hath testified against thee, saying, I have slain the Lord’s anointed.

- David took hold on his clothes and rent them, and all the men with him did likewise, and they mourned and wept and fasted until evening for Saul, for Jonathan, and for the house of Israel that were fallen by the sword.
- The renting of garments was the sign of mourning, but the Most High looks for a rent heart and not rent garments only; true repentance is inward, not just an outward show.
- David asked the man how he was not afraid to stretch forth his hand to destroy the Lord's anointed. Even a man about to die was not to be touched; the judgment of the Lord was to be let play out. David then commanded one of his young men to fall upon the Amalekite, and he died, for his own mouth had testified against him.

    > <sup>[13](/bible/joel/2#v13)</sup> And rend your heart, and not your garments, and turn unto the Lord your God: for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repenteth him of the evil.

    Rend your heart, and not your garments; the Most High looks for true repentance within.

**[2 Samuel 1:17-27](/bible/2-samuel/1#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/2-samuel/1#v17)</sup> And David lamented with this lamentation over Saul and over Jonathan his son:
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-samuel/1#v18)</sup> (Also he bade them teach the children of Judah the use of the bow: behold, it is written in the book of Jasher.)
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-samuel/1#v19)</sup> The beauty of Israel is slain upon thy high places: how are the mighty fallen!
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-samuel/1#v20)</sup> Tell it not in Gath, publish it not in the streets of Askelon; lest the daughters of the Philistines rejoice, lest the daughters of the uncircumcised triumph.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-samuel/1#v21)</sup> Ye mountains of Gilboa, let there be no dew, neither let there be rain, upon you, nor fields of offerings: for there the shield of the mighty is vilely cast away, the shield of Saul, as though he had not been anointed with oil.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-samuel/1#v22)</sup> From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, the bow of Jonathan turned not back, and the sword of Saul returned not empty.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-samuel/1#v23)</sup> Saul and Jonathan were lovely and pleasant in their lives, and in their death they were not divided: they were swifter than eagles, they were stronger than lions.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-samuel/1#v24)</sup> Ye daughters of Israel, weep over Saul, who clothed you in scarlet, with other delights, who put on ornaments of gold upon your apparel.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-samuel/1#v25)</sup> How are the mighty fallen in the midst of the battle! O Jonathan, thou wast slain in thine high places.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-samuel/1#v26)</sup> I am distressed for thee, my brother Jonathan: very pleasant hast thou been unto me: thy love to me was wonderful, passing the love of women.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-samuel/1#v27)</sup> How are the mighty fallen, and the weapons of war perished!

- David lamented over Saul and over Jonathan, and bade them teach the children of Judah the use of the bow, which is written in the book of Jasher. The book of Jasher is not in the canon because the Most High did not will us to have it; He put in the book exactly what He wanted His people to read in the last days.
- This walk in the last days is not by might nor by power but by the Spirit of the Lord. The captivity is not overthrown by rising up with the bow or the gun; this is spiritual war and not physical. There is nothing wrong with knowing how to defend yourself, but the redemption of Israel does not come by force of arms.
- David's lament is a prophecy given in the spirit, almost a eulogy: Saul and Jonathan were lovely and pleasant in their lives, and in their death they were not divided; they were swifter than eagles and stronger than lions. Before the devil jumped on Saul he was good, and the two of them were good together.
- Israel's forefathers died only when the Most High said it was time; that is the only way any of them died. David mourned Jonathan, saying his love to him was wonderful, passing the love of women.
