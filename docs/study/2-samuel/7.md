**[2 Samuel 7:1-7](/bible/2-samuel/7#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/7#v1)</sup> And it came to pass, when the king sat in his house, and the Lord had given him rest round about from all his enemies;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/7#v2)</sup> That the king said unto Nathan the prophet, See now, I dwell in an house of cedar, but the ark of God dwelleth within curtains.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/7#v3)</sup> And Nathan said to the king, Go, do all that is in thine heart; for the Lord is with thee.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-samuel/7#v4)</sup> And it came to pass that night, that the word of the Lord came unto Nathan, saying,
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-samuel/7#v5)</sup> Go and tell my servant David, Thus saith the Lord, Shalt thou build me an house for me to dwell in?
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-samuel/7#v6)</sup> Whereas I have not dwelt in any house since the time that I brought up the children of Israel out of Egypt, even to this day, but have walked in a tent and in a tabernacle.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-samuel/7#v7)</sup> In all the places wherein I have walked with all the children of Israel spake I a word with any of the tribes of Israel, whom I commanded to feed my people Israel, saying, Why build ye not me an house of cedar?

- When the king sat in his house and the Lord had given him rest from all his enemies round about, David said to Nathan the prophet that he dwelt in a house of cedar while the ark of God dwelt within curtains. David was not seeking his own riches but a place for the ark.
- Nathan told David to do all that was in his heart, for the Lord was with him. But that night the word of the Lord came to Nathan.
- The Lord asked, Shalt thou build me a house to dwell in? He had not dwelt in any house since bringing Israel out of Egypt but had walked in a tent and a tabernacle, and had never asked any tribe why they built Him not a house of cedar.

**[2 Samuel 7:8-11](/bible/2-samuel/7#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/2-samuel/7#v8)</sup> Now therefore so shalt thou say unto my servant David, Thus saith the Lord of hosts, I took thee from the sheepcote, from following the sheep, to be ruler over my people, over Israel:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-samuel/7#v9)</sup> And I was with thee whithersoever thou wentest, and have cut off all thine enemies out of thy sight, and have made thee a great name, like unto the name of the great men that are in the earth.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-samuel/7#v10)</sup> Moreover I will appoint a place for my people Israel, and will plant them, that they may dwell in a place of their own, and move no more; neither shall the children of wickedness afflict them any more, as beforetime,
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-samuel/7#v11)</sup> And as since the time that I commanded judges to be over my people Israel, and have caused thee to rest from all thine enemies. Also the Lord telleth thee that he will make thee an house.

- The Lord reminded David that He took him from the sheepcote, from following the sheep, to be ruler over His people Israel, and was with him whithersoever he went, cutting off all his enemies and making him a great name.
- The Most High promised to appoint a place for His people Israel and to plant them that they might dwell in a place of their own and move no more, and that the children of wickedness should afflict them no more.
- The Lord calls the nations the children of wickedness, the same as when it is taught that Esau is of the wicked one. He also promised David rest from all his enemies, and that the Lord Himself would make David a house.

**[2 Samuel 7:12-16](/bible/2-samuel/7#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/2-samuel/7#v12)</sup> And when thy days be fulfilled, and thou shalt sleep with thy fathers, I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels, and I will establish his kingdom.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/2-samuel/7#v13)</sup> He shall build an house for my name, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-samuel/7#v14)</sup> I will be his father, and he shall be my son. If he commit iniquity, I will chasten him with the rod of men, and with the stripes of the children of men:
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-samuel/7#v15)</sup> But my mercy shall not depart away from him, as I took it from Saul, whom I put away before thee.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-samuel/7#v16)</sup> And thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever before thee: thy throne shall be established for ever.

- The Lord said that when David's days were fulfilled and he slept with his fathers, He would set up his seed after him, proceeding out of his bowels, and establish his kingdom; that seed would build a house for the Lord's name.
- The word reaches past Solomon. Solomon built the house and sat on the throne, but his kingdom was not established for ever; his son Rehoboam would not hear counsel, and the kingdom split into two nations. The phrase "for ever" points to Jesus Christ the black Messiah, who came through David's lineage.
- The Lord said He would be his father and he His son, and if he committed iniquity He would chasten him with the rod of men. Solomon was not chastened with the rod of men in his lifetime; the one smitten with the rod upon the cheek was Jesus the Christ, who did no sin, though it was Solomon who sinned.
- The Lord promised that His mercy would not depart from him as it did from Saul, and that David's house, kingdom, and throne would be established for ever. The Lord did not make the Bible easy in every place, that He might see who would research and learn precept upon precept.

**[2 Samuel 7:17-24](/bible/2-samuel/7#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/2-samuel/7#v17)</sup> According to all these words, and according to all this vision, so did Nathan speak unto David.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/2-samuel/7#v18)</sup> Then went king David in, and sat before the Lord, and he said, Who am I, O Lord God? and what is my house, that thou hast brought me hitherto?
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-samuel/7#v19)</sup> And this was yet a small thing in thy sight, O Lord God; but thou hast spoken also of thy servant’s house for a great while to come. And is this the manner of man, O Lord God?
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-samuel/7#v20)</sup> And what can David say more unto thee? for thou, Lord God, knowest thy servant.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-samuel/7#v21)</sup> For thy word’s sake, and according to thine own heart, hast thou done all these great things, to make thy servant know them.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-samuel/7#v22)</sup> Wherefore thou art great, O Lord God: for there is none like thee, neither is there any God beside thee, according to all that we have heard with our ears.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-samuel/7#v23)</sup> And what one nation in the earth is like thy people, even like Israel, whom God went to redeem for a people to himself, and to make him a name, and to do for you great things and terrible, for thy land, before thy people, which thou redeemedst to thee from Egypt, from the nations and their gods?
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-samuel/7#v24)</sup> For thou hast confirmed to thyself thy people Israel to be a people unto thee for ever: and thou, Lord, art become their God.

- Nathan spoke to David according to all these words and this vision. Then David went in and sat before the Lord and said, Who am I, O Lord God, and what is my house, that thou hast brought me hitherto?
- David marveled that the Lord had spoken of his house for a great while to come, for there is none like the Most High, neither is there any God beside Him, according to all Israel had heard with their ears.
- David asked what one nation in the earth is like Israel, whom God went to redeem for a people to Himself, to make Him a name and to do great and terrible things. He did not redeem one man alone but a whole people, and He will bring them out again as a people.
- The teaching applies: though a brother or sister may have a difference, we must forgive one another and come back together, for the Most High chose us as one people. We lost our heritage in captivity, and coming back to it we must let go of grudges.
- The northern and southern kingdoms are to be made one nation again under one king and one shepherd. The one king is Jesus Christ, called the throne of David because David ruled over all twelve tribes; David will be present but is not the king. The chief shepherd, the great shepherd, the good shepherd who lays down his life is Christ, and he gathers the elect from the four winds and brings them up out of the land.

**[2 Samuel 7:25-29](/bible/2-samuel/7#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/2-samuel/7#v25)</sup> And now, O Lord God, the word that thou hast spoken concerning thy servant, and concerning his house, establish it for ever, and do as thou hast said.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-samuel/7#v26)</sup> And let thy name be magnified for ever, saying, The Lord of hosts is the God over Israel: and let the house of thy servant David be established before thee.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-samuel/7#v27)</sup> For thou, O Lord of hosts, God of Israel, hast revealed to thy servant, saying, I will build thee an house: therefore hath thy servant found in his heart to pray this prayer unto thee.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/2-samuel/7#v28)</sup> And now, O Lord God, thou art that God, and thy words be true, and thou hast promised this goodness unto thy servant:
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/2-samuel/7#v29)</sup> Therefore now let it please thee to bless the house of thy servant, that it may continue for ever before thee: for thou, O Lord God, hast spoken it: and with thy blessing let the house of thy servant be blessed for ever.

- David prayed that the Lord would establish for ever the word He had spoken concerning His servant and his house, and do as He had said.
- He asked that the Lord's name be magnified for ever, that men would say, The Lord of hosts is the God of Israel, and that the house of His servant David be established before Him.
- Because the Lord of hosts had revealed to David that He would build him a house, David found in his heart to pray this prayer. He rested on the truth of the Lord's word, that with the Lord's blessing the house of His servant would be blessed for ever.
