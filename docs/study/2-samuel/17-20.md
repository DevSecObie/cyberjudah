## Introduction

This session covers [2 Samuel 17](/bible/2-samuel/17) through 20: Ahithophel's counsel against David and Hushai's counter counsel, the great battle in the wood of Ephraim and the death of Absalom, David's mourning and his return to Jerusalem, and the revolt of Sheba the son of Bichri. The thread running through all four chapters is that the devices in a man's heart are many, but the counsel of the Lord, that shall stand. Absalom moved in the spirit of deceit and betrayal against his own father, and the enemies a man of Israel must forgive are the enemies of his own people, even those risen up out of his own house.

**[2 Samuel 17:1-3](/bible/2-samuel/17#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-samuel/17#v1)</sup> Moreover Ahithophel said unto Absalom, Let me now choose out twelve thousand men, and I will arise and pursue after David this night:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-samuel/17#v2)</sup> And I will come upon him while he is weary and weak handed, and will make him afraid: and all the people that are with him shall flee; and I will smite the king only:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-samuel/17#v3)</sup> And I will bring back all the people unto thee: the man whom thou seekest is as if all returned: so all the people shall be in peace.

- Ahithophel had already given Absalom wicked counsel in the previous chapter, telling him to go in unto his father's concubines in the sight of all Israel so that the hands of all that were with him would be made strong. Ahithophel was David's own counselor turned traitor, being the devil the Bible speaks of, working deceit and betrayal from inside the gates of Israel.
- His counsel in those days was regarded as if a man had inquired at the oracle of God. That tells you Ahithophel was very influential and had the gift of gab, so persuasive that his advice sounded almost biblical.
- Now he asks Absalom to let him choose out twelve thousand men to pursue David that same night while David was weary and weak handed, to smite the king only and bring back all the people. He wanted the victory and the bragging rights of being the one who killed King David.

- Absalom wanted a second opinion, so he called Hushai the Archite to hear likewise what he would say. There is safety in a multitude of counselors, but only where the counsel is according to thus saith the Lord; a counselor who does not go to the scriptures is not one you should want.

- Hushai warned that if Absalom's men were overthrown at the first, even the most valiant warrior whose heart is as the heart of a lion would utterly melt, for all Israel knew David was a mighty man. - Instead Hushai counseled that all Israel be gathered from Dan to Beersheba as the sand by the sea, with Absalom going to battle in his own person. This deliberately inaccurate counsel would waste time gathering troops and give David room to escape; Hushai was a counterintelligence agent, a spy planted in righteousness, always siding with King David.

The Lord had appointed to defeat the good counsel of Ahithophel, to the intent that He might bring evil upon Absalom. The Lord was working with Hushai and David, while Satan was in the spirit of Absalom and Ahithophel.

# 2 Samuel 18: Deal Gently With the Young Man

- David numbered the people and set captains of thousands and hundreds, sending a third part under Joab, a third under Abishai, and a third under Ittai the Gittite. He said he would surely go forth with them himself.
- The people answered that he should not go, for he was worth ten thousand of them and it was better that he support them out of the city. David hearkened to their counsel and said what seemed best to them he would do; he did not run a dictatorship. In a time when he could not trust his own flesh and blood, he trusted the proven men he had warred with for years.
- David charged Joab, Abishai, and Ittai to deal gently for his sake with the young man, even with Absalom, and all the people heard the charge. Even after Absalom defiled his concubines and stole the people's hearts, David still sought to make amends with his son.

- Joab pursued Sheba through all the tribes to Abel of Beth-maachah, and they cast up a bank against the city and battered the wall to throw it down.
- A wise woman of the city called out to Joab, reminding him of the old saying that men would surely ask counsel at Abel, and asked why he would swallow up a city that was a mother in Israel and the inheritance of the Lord. Joab answered that he did not seek to destroy, but only wanted Sheba the son of Bichri, who had lifted up his hand against the king; deliver him and he would depart.
- The woman promised that Sheba's head would be thrown over the wall. She went to all the people in her wisdom, and they cut off Sheba's head and cast it out to Joab, who blew the trumpet and retired from the city, every man to his tent, and returned to Jerusalem unto the king.
