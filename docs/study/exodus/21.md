**[Exodus 21:2-6](/bible/exodus/21#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/21#v2)</sup> If thou buy an Hebrew servant, six years he shall serve: and in the seventh he shall go out free for nothing.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/exodus/21#v3)</sup> If he came in by himself, he shall go out by himself: if he were married, then his wife shall go out with him.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/exodus/21#v4)</sup> If his master have given him a wife, and she have born him sons or daughters; the wife and her children shall be her master’s, and he shall go out by himself.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/exodus/21#v5)</sup> And if the servant shall plainly say, I love my master, my wife, and my children; I will not go out free:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/exodus/21#v6)</sup> Then his master shall bring him unto the judges; he shall also bring him to the door, or unto the door post; and his master shall bore his ear through with an aul; and he shall serve him for ever.

- If thou buy an Hebrew servant, six years he shall serve, and in the seventh he shall go out free for nothing. If he came in by himself he goes out by himself; if he were married when he came in, his wife goes out with him.
- Deuteronomy repeats the same judgment for a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman sold unto thee. [Nehemiah 5](/bible/nehemiah/5) shows how brethren came into servitude: in the dearth some mortgaged lands, vineyards, and houses to buy corn, some borrowed money for the king's tribute, and some brought their sons and daughters into bondage. The brother that waxes poor is not to be compelled to serve as a bondservant, but as an hired servant, and not ruled over harshly.
- If the master gave him a wife and she bare him sons or daughters, the wife and her children are the master's, and he goes out by himself; he did not bring them in. His two choices are to go out alone, or to plainly say, I love my master, my wife, and my children, have his ear bored through with an aul at the door post, and serve his master for ever.
- Zedekiah's covenant in Jeremiah confirms the law in the prophets: every man was to let his Hebrew manservant and maidservant go free at the appointed release.

    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/jeremiah/34#v9)</sup> That every man should let his manservant, and every man his maidservant, being an Hebrew or an Hebrewess, go free; that none should serve himself of them, to wit, of a Jew his brother.

    Zedekiah made a covenant to proclaim liberty, that every man let his Hebrew manservant and maidservant go free; the law of release stood in the prophets.

**[Exodus 21:7-11](/bible/exodus/21#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/exodus/21#v7)</sup> And if a man sell his daughter to be a maidservant, she shall not go out as the menservants do.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/exodus/21#v8)</sup> If she please not her master, who hath betrothed her to himself, then shall he let her be redeemed: to sell her unto a strange nation he shall have no power, seeing he hath dealt deceitfully with her.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/exodus/21#v9)</sup> And if he have betrothed her unto his son, he shall deal with her after the manner of daughters.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/exodus/21#v10)</sup> If he take him another wife; her food, her raiment, and her duty of marriage, shall he not diminish.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/exodus/21#v11)</sup> And if he do not these three unto her, then shall she go out free without money.

- If a man sell his daughter to be a maidservant, she shall not go out as the menservants do. The reason is that her master betrothed her to himself, or unto his son; she is a wife, not a six-year servant, so the release of the menservants does not apply to her.
- If she please not her master who hath betrothed her to himself, he must let her be redeemed; he has no power to sell her unto a strange nation, seeing he hath dealt deceitfully with her. The deceit is that he betrothed her to himself and then would not deal with her, keeping her to himself when she could have been another man's wife.
- If he take him another wife, her food, her raiment, and her duty of marriage shall he not diminish; the duty of marriage is the marriage bed. If he lay with her, she belongs to him and he cannot send her away; and if he do not these three unto her, then shall she go out free without money. He must take care of her.

**[Exodus 21:13-14](/bible/exodus/21#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/exodus/21#v13)</sup> And if a man lie not in wait, but God deliver him into his hand; then I will appoint thee a place whither he shall flee.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/exodus/21#v14)</sup> But if a man come presumptuously upon his neighbour, to slay him with guile; thou shalt take him from mine altar, that he may die.

- If a man lie not in wait, but God deliver him into his hand, a place was appointed whither he shall flee. That is the case of the slayer that kills his neighbour ignorantly, as when a man hews wood with his neighbour and the axe head slips from the helve and lights upon him that he die: he flees to the appointed city and lives, lest the avenger of blood overtake him while his heart is hot, for he was not worthy of death, having hated him not in time past.
- But if a man come presumptuously upon his neighbour to slay him with guile, thou shalt take him from mine altar, that he may die. Men would run and grab the horns of the altar, but wilful murder found no refuge there.

  Precepts:
  - **[Deuteronomy 19:4-6](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v4)</sup> And this is the case of the slayer, which shall flee thither, that he may live: Whoso killeth his neighbour ignorantly, whom he hated not in time past;
    >
    > <sup>[5](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v5)</sup> As when a man goeth into the wood with his neighbour to hew wood, and his hand fetcheth a stroke with the axe to cut down the tree, and the head slippeth from the helve, and lighteth upon his neighbour, that he die; he shall flee unto one of those cities, and live:
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v6)</sup> Lest the avenger of the blood pursue the slayer, while his heart is hot, and overtake him, because the way is long, and slay him; whereas he was not worthy of death, inasmuch as he hated him not in time past.

    The slayer that kills his neighbour ignorantly, as when the axe head slips from the helve, flees to the city of refuge and lives, for he was not worthy of death.

**[Exodus 21:18-19](/bible/exodus/21#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/exodus/21#v18)</sup> And if men strive together, and one smite another with a stone, or with his fist, and he die not, but keepeth his bed:
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/exodus/21#v19)</sup> If he rise again, and walk abroad upon his staff, then shall he that smote him be quit: only he shall pay for the loss of his time, and shall cause him to be thoroughly healed.

- If men strive and one smite another so that he keep his bed, he that smote him shall pay for the loss of his time and shall cause him to be thoroughly healed. That is restitution: paying for the time the man was down and could not work and provide for his family. That is what the court system calls a civil suit today.

**[Exodus 21:20-21](/bible/exodus/21#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/exodus/21#v20)</sup> And if a man smite his servant, or his maid, with a rod, and he die under his hand; he shall be surely punished.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/exodus/21#v21)</sup> Notwithstanding, if he continue a day or two, he shall not be punished: for he is his money.

- If a man smite his servant with a rod and he die under his hand, he shall surely be punished; but if he continue a day or two, he shall not be punished, for he is his money.
- The wisdom writings teach not to be ashamed of much correction of children, nor of making the side of an evil servant to bleed. An evil servant that will not get right could be corrected physically, so long as he was not killed.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 42:1, 5](/bible/sirach/42#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/42#v1)</sup> Of these things be not thou ashamed, and accept no person to sin thereby:

    Be not ashamed of much correction of children, nor to make the side of an evil servant to bleed.

**[Exodus 21:22-25](/bible/exodus/21#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/exodus/21#v22)</sup> If men strive, and hurt a woman with child, so that her fruit depart from her, and yet no mischief follow: he shall be surely punished, according as the woman’s husband will lay upon him; and he shall pay as the judges determine.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/exodus/21#v23)</sup> And if any mischief follow, then thou shalt give life for life,
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/exodus/21#v24)</sup> Eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot,
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/exodus/21#v25)</sup> Burning for burning, wound for wound, stripe for stripe.

- If men strive and hurt a woman with child so that her fruit depart from her, this judgment covers the miscarriage: if no mischief follow, he is punished as the woman's husband lays upon him and the judges determine; and if any mischief follow, then life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, wound for wound.

**[Exodus 21:28-29](/bible/exodus/21#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/exodus/21#v28)</sup> If an ox gore a man or a woman, that they die: then the ox shall be surely stoned, and his flesh shall not be eaten; but the owner of the ox shall be quit.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/exodus/21#v29)</sup> But if the ox were wont to push with his horn in time past, and it hath been testified to his owner, and he hath not kept him in, but that he hath killed a man or a woman; the ox shall be stoned, and his owner also shall be put to death.

- If an ox gore a man or a woman that they die, the ox is stoned and his flesh not eaten, and the owner is quit. But if the ox were wont to push with his horns in time past, and it hath been testified to his owner, and he hath not kept him in, and it kill a man or a woman, the ox is stoned and his owner also put to death.
- Most do not have oxen today, but the same judgment reaches the yard dogs, the pit bulls, German shepherds, and rottweilers. If the dog has bitten before and it was reported to you, and it gets out again through a snapped chain or a breach in the fence and mauls or kills somebody, the scripture holds the owner responsible unto death; that is why animal control writes the ticket and warns you to keep your dogs in.
- The law is slack right now, as Habakkuk says, so many punishments worthy of death in the scriptures, like rape or this judgment, come out today as a fine or a slap on the hand.

  Precepts:
  - **[Habakkuk 1:4](/bible/habakkuk/1#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/habakkuk/1#v4)</sup> Therefore the law is slacked, and judgment doth never go forth: for the wicked doth compass about the righteous; therefore wrong judgment proceedeth.

    The law is slacked, and judgment doth never go forth; that is why death-worthy offences draw light penalties today.
