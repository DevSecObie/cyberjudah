## Introduction

This reading covers [Exodus 14](/bible/exodus/14) through 17: the Red Sea opened and the Egyptians overthrown, the song of Moses, the manna and the sabbath proved in the wilderness, and the first war with Amalek. The session was a straight reading with brief commentary and questions afterward; the answers are folded in under the verses they belong to.

**[Exodus 14:1-5](/bible/exodus/14#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/exodus/14#v1)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Moses, saying,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/14#v2)</sup> Speak unto the children of Israel, that they turn and encamp before Pi–hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, over against Baal–zephon: before it shall ye encamp by the sea.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/exodus/14#v3)</sup> For Pharaoh will say of the children of Israel, They are entangled in the land, the wilderness hath shut them in.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/exodus/14#v4)</sup> And I will harden Pharaoh’s heart, that he shall follow after them; and I will be honoured upon Pharaoh, and upon all his host; that the Egyptians may know that I am the Lord. And they did so.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/exodus/14#v5)</sup> And it was told the king of Egypt that the people fled: and the heart of Pharaoh and of his servants was turned against the people, and they said, Why have we done this, that we have let Israel go from serving us?

- The Most High is not done with the Egyptians. He destroyed their firstborn and brought Israel out, but He kept one more judgment in store for that wicked nation, and He will do the same to the wicked nation that has done the same things to Israel today.
- The heart of Pharaoh and of his servants turned as soon as the people were gone: why have we done this, that we have let Israel go from serving us? The oppressor does not release his slaves gladly.

**[Exodus 14:10-12](/bible/exodus/14#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/exodus/14#v10)</sup> And when Pharaoh drew nigh, the children of Israel lifted up their eyes, and, behold, the Egyptians marched after them; and they were sore afraid: and the children of Israel cried out unto the Lord.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/exodus/14#v11)</sup> And they said unto Moses, Because there were no graves in Egypt, hast thou taken us away to die in the wilderness? wherefore hast thou dealt thus with us, to carry us forth out of Egypt?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/exodus/14#v12)</sup> Is not this the word that we did tell thee in Egypt, saying, Let us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians? For it had been better for us to serve the Egyptians, than that we should die in the wilderness.

- At the first sight of the Egyptians marching, Israel lost all faith. The Lord had done miracle after miracle before their eyes and brought them out with an high hand, yet at the first sign of danger they said He brought them out to die. Our people have always been a rebellious people of little faith: a very froward generation, children in whom is no faith.
- Let us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians: that is the same word our people speak today. Why are you stirring up trouble, why tell us to come out of the ways of Babylon; let us alone to serve the slave master, because serving him feels easier than having faith in the Most High.
- The same spirit was in Israel in the days of Christ: if we let him thus alone, all men will believe on him, and the Romans shall come. The fear of the oppressor outweighed belief in the miracles.
- In captivity the people wax old in mind and will not depart: woe to the bloody city, the prey departed not. Israel was the prey in Egypt and did not want to leave, the same as now.

    A very froward generation, children in whom is no faith; that is the record over Israel.
  - **[John 11:47-48](/bible/john/11#v47)**

**[Exodus 14:13-14](/bible/exodus/14#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/exodus/14#v13)</sup> And Moses said unto the people, Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord, which he will shew to you to day: for the Egyptians whom ye have seen to day, ye shall see them again no more for ever.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/exodus/14#v14)</sup> The Lord shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace.

- Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord. The Egyptians whom ye have seen today, ye shall see them again no more for ever; that sounds like the same word that will stand over Babylon.
- The Lord shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace. The battle was never Israel's to win by strength.

**[Exodus 14:19-22](/bible/exodus/14#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/exodus/14#v19)</sup> And the angel of God, which went before the camp of Israel, removed and went behind them; and the pillar of the cloud went from before their face, and stood behind them:
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/exodus/14#v20)</sup> And it came between the camp of the Egyptians and the camp of Israel; and it was a cloud and darkness to them, but it gave light by night to these: so that the one came not near the other all the night.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/exodus/14#v21)</sup> And Moses stretched out his hand over the sea; and the Lord caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night, and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/exodus/14#v22)</sup> And the children of Israel went into the midst of the sea upon the dry ground: and the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand, and on their left.

- The angel of God that went before the camp, the angel in the chariot whom we know to be Christ, removed and went behind them, standing between the camp of Egypt and the camp of Israel.
- The pillar was a cloud and darkness to the Egyptians but gave light by night to Israel, so that the one came not near the other; Egypt could not see clearly where they were going.
- The Lord caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night; Israel went through on dry ground, and the waters were a wall on the right hand and on the left.

**[Exodus 14:23-28](/bible/exodus/14#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/exodus/14#v23)</sup> And the Egyptians pursued, and went in after them to the midst of the sea, even all Pharaoh’s horses, his chariots, and his horsemen.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/exodus/14#v24)</sup> And it came to pass, that in the morning watch the Lord looked unto the host of the Egyptians through the pillar of fire and of the cloud, and troubled the host of the Egyptians,
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/exodus/14#v25)</sup> And took off their chariot wheels, that they drave them heavily: so that the Egyptians said, Let us flee from the face of Israel; for the Lord fighteth for them against the Egyptians.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/exodus/14#v26)</sup> And the Lord said unto Moses, Stretch out thine hand over the sea, that the waters may come again upon the Egyptians, upon their chariots, and upon their horsemen.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/exodus/14#v27)</sup> And Moses stretched forth his hand over the sea, and the sea returned to his strength when the morning appeared; and the Egyptians fled against it; and the Lord overthrew the Egyptians in the midst of the sea.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/exodus/14#v28)</sup> And the waters returned, and covered the chariots, and the horsemen, and all the host of Pharaoh that came into the sea after them; there remained not so much as one of them.

- In the morning watch the Lord troubled the host of the Egyptians and took off their chariot wheels, so that they said, Let us flee from the face of Israel, for the Lord fighteth for them.
- Moses stretched out his hand and the sea returned in his strength. All that water that had stood up as a wall came down on the Egyptians, their chariots, and their horsemen, and consumed them. All praises to the Most High for loving His children enough to save us by such a mighty salvation.

**[Exodus 14:29-31](/bible/exodus/14#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/exodus/14#v29)</sup> But the children of Israel walked upon dry land in the midst of the sea; and the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand, and on their left.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/exodus/14#v30)</sup> Thus the Lord saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians; and Israel saw the Egyptians dead upon the sea shore.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/exodus/14#v31)</sup> And Israel saw that great work which the Lord did upon the Egyptians: and the people feared the Lord, and believed the Lord, and his servant Moses.

- Israel saw the Egyptians dead upon the sea shore and saw the great work of the Lord, and the people feared the Lord and believed the Lord and His servant Moses.
- The Lord who brought us out the first time with great signs and wonders will save Israel out of this captivity the same way; that is why the brothers and sisters must have faith in these last days, because a trial is the true test of faith.
- We have to accept that our salvation was their destruction. The Red Sea opened for Israel and closed on Egypt in the same hour; whoever hates that the Most High saves one nation by destroying the others will have to come to grips with it.
- Even the ground testified: where water stood before, dry land appeared, and a green field came up out of the violent stream; grass sprang up at the bottom of the sea for the people to pass.
