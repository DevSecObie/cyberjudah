## Introduction

This reading covers [Exodus 18](/bible/exodus/18) through 21: Jethro's counsel to Moses and the structure of righteous leadership, the covenant at Mount Sinai, the Ten Commandments, and the judgments set before Israel. Each chapter is read through, then the highlighted portions are opened with precepts.

**[Exodus 18:1-6](/bible/exodus/18#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/exodus/18#v1)</sup> When Jethro, the priest of Midian, Moses’ father in law, heard of all that God had done for Moses, and for Israel his people, and that the Lord had brought Israel out of Egypt;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/18#v2)</sup> Then Jethro, Moses’ father in law, took Zipporah, Moses’ wife, after he had sent her back,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/exodus/18#v3)</sup> And her two sons; of which the name of the one was Gershom; for he said, I have been an alien in a strange land:
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/exodus/18#v4)</sup> And the name of the other was Eliezer; for the God of my father, said he, was mine help, and delivered me from the sword of Pharaoh:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/exodus/18#v5)</sup> And Jethro, Moses’ father in law, came with his sons and his wife unto Moses into the wilderness, where he encamped at the mount of God:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/exodus/18#v6)</sup> And he said unto Moses, I thy father in law Jethro am come unto thee, and thy wife, and her two sons with her.

- Jethro the priest of Midian, Moses' father in law, heard all that God had done for Moses and for Israel, and came to Moses in the wilderness with Zipporah and her two sons, Gershom and Eliezer.
- Jethro is the same man called Reuel in Exodus 2: the priest of Midian whose seven daughters Moses helped at the well, and who gave Moses Zipporah his daughter. Reuel and Jethro are one person.
- [Exodus 2](/bible/exodus/2) also holds one of the color scriptures: the daughters told their father an Egyptian delivered them out of the hand of the shepherds. Moses, an Israelite, was taken for an Egyptian, because Israel looked like the Egyptians.
- Jethro was not an Israelite; he and his kindred were not of the tribes.

**[Exodus 18:9-11](/bible/exodus/18#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/exodus/18#v9)</sup> And Jethro rejoiced for all the goodness which the Lord had done to Israel, whom he had delivered out of the hand of the Egyptians.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/exodus/18#v10)</sup> And Jethro said, Blessed be the Lord, who hath delivered you out of the hand of the Egyptians, and out of the hand of Pharaoh, who hath delivered the people from under the hand of the Egyptians.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/exodus/18#v11)</sup> Now I know that the Lord is greater than all gods: for in the thing wherein they dealt proudly he was above them.

- Jethro rejoiced for all the goodness which the Lord had done to Israel, and confessed, now I know that the Lord is greater than all gods, for in the thing wherein they dealt proudly he was above them.
- Other non-Israelites came to that same knowledge. All the gods of the nations are idols, but the Lord made the heavens; give unto the Lord the glory due unto his name.
- Naaman the Syrian, healed of his leprosy in Jordan, confessed that there is no God in all the earth but in Israel. His faith was so strong he vowed to offer neither burnt offering nor sacrifice unto other gods, and asked pardon for even bowing in the house of Rimmon with his master.
- Nebuchadnezzar came to praise and extol and honour the King of heaven, for those that walk in pride he is able to abase. The heathen understand that the Most High is greater than all other gods; it is our people that want a god that does not look like them.

**[Exodus 18:15-16](/bible/exodus/18#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/exodus/18#v15)</sup> And Moses said unto his father in law, Because the people come unto me to enquire of God:
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/exodus/18#v16)</sup> When they have a matter, they come unto me; and I judge between one and another, and I do make them know the statutes of God, and his laws.

- The people came to Moses to inquire of God; he judged between one and another and made them know the statutes of God and his laws. That responsibility is Moses' seat.
- The scribes and the Pharisees sat in Moses' seat, which is why their words were to be observed but their works not followed, for they say and do not.

**[Exodus 18:20](/bible/exodus/18#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/exodus/18#v20)</sup> And thou shalt teach them ordinances and laws, and shalt shew them the way wherein they must walk, and the work that they must do.

- Thou shalt teach them ordinances and laws, and shalt shew them the way wherein they must walk and the work that they must do. That is the work done today when the laws of the Most High are taught in the streets and online: shewing the people the ways they must walk and the work they must do.

**[Exodus 18:21-22](/bible/exodus/18#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/exodus/18#v21)</sup> Moreover thou shalt provide out of all the people able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness; and place such over them, to be rulers of thousands, and rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens:
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/exodus/18#v22)</sup> And let them judge the people at all seasons: and it shall be, that every great matter they shall bring unto thee, but every small matter they shall judge: so shall it be easier for thyself, and they shall bear the burden with thee.

- Able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness, were set as rulers of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens. This is the structure of righteous leadership the Most High established: captains and officers over the tribes, offices never heard in the church.
- The judges were charged to hear the causes between brethren and judge righteously, without respect of persons, not afraid of the face of man, for the judgment is God's. The hard causes went up to Moses, as the hard matters today go to the high-ranking officers and ultimately the bishop, while the smaller matters are judged by the lower-ranking officers.
- Judgment did not pass away with the Old Testament. He that is spiritual judgeth all things, and the spiritual man is the man that keeps the commandments. The saints shall judge the world, so brethren should judge the smallest matters among themselves instead of going to law before the unjust; only matters of life and death under today's system go outside.
