## Introduction

This reading covers [Exodus 2](/bible/exodus/2) through 5: the birth of Moses and his flight to Midian, the angel in the burning bush, the three signs given to Moses and the circumcision of his son, and the first appearance before Pharaoh with the answer, Let my people go. The session opened with prayer out of [Psalms 56](/bible/psalms/56), be merciful unto me, O God, for man would swallow me up; in God I have put my trust, I will not fear what flesh can do unto me; and with thanksgiving for the reawakening prophesied in [Ezekiel 37](/bible/ezekiel/37), that the people of the Most High will return unto Him and stand upon their feet, an exceeding great army.

> <sup>[1](/bible/exodus/2#v1)</sup> And there went a man of the house of Levi, and took to wife a daughter of Levi.

- A man of the house of Levi took to wife a daughter of Levi. These are Amram and Jochebed, the father and mother of Moses, Aaron, and Miriam.

**[Exodus 2:3-4](/bible/exodus/2#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/exodus/2#v3)</sup> And when she could not longer hide him, she took for him an ark of bulrushes, and daubed it with slime and with pitch, and put the child therein; and she laid it in the flags by the river’s brink.

- When she could no longer hide him, she took for him an ark of bulrushes, daubed it with slime and with pitch, and laid it in the flags by the river's brink. Bulrushes are the river reeds known as cattails.
- His sister Miriam stood afar off, to wit what would be done to him. The movie tellings of this account do not give the full details; to get a full understanding of what happened in the days of Moses you have to read the scriptures.

> <sup>[6](/bible/exodus/2#v6)</sup> And when she had opened it, she saw the child: and, behold, the babe wept. And she had compassion on him, and said, This is one of the Hebrews’ children.

- The daughter of Pharaoh sent her maid to fetch the ark, and when she had opened it she saw the child, and the babe wept. She had compassion on him and said, This is one of the Hebrews' children.
- She knew the child was a Hebrew and no Egyptian because the Most High put a difference between the Egyptians and Israel; though both peoples were dark of skin, this was a child of the chosen nation.

**[Exodus 2:10](/bible/exodus/2#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/exodus/2#v10)</sup> And the child grew, and she brought him unto Pharaoh’s daughter, and he became her son. And she called his name Moses: and she said, Because I drew him out of the water.

- The child grew, and he became the son of Pharaoh's daughter. She called his name Moses, which means drawn out, because she drew him out of the water.
- So Moses grew up as an Egyptian, although he was a Hebrew.

**[Exodus 2:11-12](/bible/exodus/2#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/exodus/2#v11)</sup> And it came to pass in those days, when Moses was grown, that he went out unto his brethren, and looked on their burdens: and he spied an Egyptian smiting an Hebrew, one of his brethren.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/exodus/2#v12)</sup> And he looked this way and that way, and when he saw that there was no man, he slew the Egyptian, and hid him in the sand.

- When Moses was grown he went out unto his brethren the Israelites and looked on their burdens, and he spied an Egyptian smiting an Hebrew, one of his brethren. Moses knew the Israelites were his brothers; he knew he was not of the Egyptian lineage and that there was something different about him.
- He looked this way and that way, and when he saw that there was no man, he slew the Egyptian and hid him in the sand. He did not just marvel at the oppression; he used his position for his people, unlike many who hold a position of power today and will not risk it for their people. Moses is the ultimate revolutionary, as Christ is.

**[Exodus 2:15](/bible/exodus/2#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/exodus/2#v15)</sup> Now when Pharaoh heard this thing, he sought to slay Moses. But Moses fled from the face of Pharaoh, and dwelt in the land of Midian: and he sat down by a well.

- When Pharaoh heard this thing, he sought to slay Moses; but Moses fled from the face of Pharaoh and dwelt in the land of Midian.
- Moses gave up his whole upbringing and position. By faith he refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter, choosing rather to suffer affliction with the people of God than to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season. In Egypt his head was shaved, his beard cut, his dress and customs Egyptian; he laid all of it down to be what he was called to be, an Israelite.
- The same claim stands now: the remnant does not want the names of the captivity but the natural heritage of Israel, esteeming the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures of this land.

- The precepts identify that angel. Precept upon precept, the angel in the burning bush was Christ.

- [Romans 2](/bible/romans/2) does not do away with circumcision of the flesh. Circumcision verily profiteth if thou keep the law: the Jews who grew up circumcised and keeping the law, yet broke it, made their circumcision uncircumcision; and the uncircumcised of the cast-off northern kingdom who keep the righteousness of the law are counted as circumcised. Circumcision of the heart is putting away the evil of the doings, as the prophets say, take away the foreskins of your heart; the scribes and Pharisees were clean on the outside of the cup but full of iniquity within.

# Exodus 5: Let My People Go

Read the chapter → [Exodus 5](/bible/exodus/5)

**[Exodus 5:1-2](/bible/exodus/5#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/exodus/5#v1)</sup> And afterward Moses and Aaron went in, and told Pharaoh, Thus saith the Lord God of Israel, Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto me in the wilderness.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/5#v2)</sup> And Pharaoh said, Who is the Lord, that I should obey his voice to let Israel go? I know not the Lord, neither will I let Israel go.

- Moses and Aaron went in and told Pharaoh, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto me in the wilderness. That feast was the Passover.
- Pharaoh said, Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice to let Israel go? I know not the LORD, neither will I let Israel go. The Lord had already said He would harden Pharaoh's heart.

**[Exodus 5:3](/bible/exodus/5#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/exodus/5#v3)</sup> And they said, The God of the Hebrews hath met with us: let us go, we pray thee, three days’ journey into the desert, and sacrifice unto the Lord our God; lest he fall upon us with pestilence, or with the sword.

- They said, The God of the Hebrews hath met with us: let us go, we pray thee, three days' journey into the desert, and sacrifice unto the LORD our God; lest he fall upon us with pestilence, or with the sword.

**[Exodus 5:4-5](/bible/exodus/5#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/exodus/5#v4)</sup> And the king of Egypt said unto them, Wherefore do ye, Moses and Aaron, let the people from their works? get you unto your burdens.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/exodus/5#v5)</sup> And Pharaoh said, Behold, the people of the land now are many, and ye make them rest from their burdens.

- The king of Egypt said, Wherefore do ye, Moses and Aaron, let the people from their works? get you unto your burdens. That is the standing complaint of the oppressor: the prophets are pulling the people from their labor and getting their hopes up that their God will deliver them.

**[Exodus 5:6-9](/bible/exodus/5#v6)**

>
> <sup>[8](/bible/exodus/5#v8)</sup> And the tale of the bricks, which they did make heretofore, ye shall lay upon them; ye shall not diminish ought thereof: for they be idle; therefore they cry, saying, Let us go and sacrifice to our God.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/exodus/5#v9)</sup> Let there more work be laid upon the men, that they may labour therein; and let them not regard vain words.

- Pharaoh commanded the taskmasters the same day: ye shall no more give the people straw to make brick; let them go and gather straw for themselves; and the tale of the bricks ye shall lay upon them, ye shall not diminish ought thereof. For they be idle; therefore they cry, saying, Let us go and sacrifice to our God.
- Let there more work be laid upon the men, that they may labour therein; and let them not regard vain words. The same tactic runs in this captivity: burdens are increased and extra wages dangled for working the Sabbath and the feast days, to wear the people down so they will not regard the words of the Most High.
- The work of the Lord is the true calling over any occupation held in Babylon: consider the Apostle and High Priest of our profession, Christ Jesus; fight the good fight of faith, lay hold on eternal life, whereunto thou art also called, and hast professed a good profession before many witnesses.

- The people were scattered abroad throughout all the land of Egypt to gather stubble instead of straw, and the taskmasters hasted them, saying, Fulfil your works, your daily tasks, as when there was straw. Fewer resources, the same production and the same deadline: the pattern of oppression has not changed in this captivity.
- The officers of the children of Israel, which Pharaoh's taskmasters had set over them, were beaten. Israelites were set as officers over their own people, so that the anger of the oppressed lands on their own brother, the supervisor they can see, instead of on the power above him; it has always been an agenda, and it is the same one today.
- This place is the same house of bondage in the spirit: the great city is spiritually called Sodom and Egypt, and the afflictions Egypt laid on Israel are the same tactics laid on Israel here.
