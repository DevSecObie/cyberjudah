**[Exodus 4:1](/bible/exodus/4#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/exodus/4#v1)</sup> And Moses answered and said, But, behold, they will not believe me, nor hearken unto my voice: for they will say, The Lord hath not appeared unto thee.

- Moses answered, But, behold, they will not believe me, nor hearken unto my voice. Moses knew the spirit of his people: Israel is impudent, hardhearted, and stiffnecked, a rebellious house that does not like to listen even when shown precept upon precept.
- The word to Ezekiel is the same commission: Israel is not a people of a strange speech and hard language; had the prophet been sent to such, they would have hearkened; but the house of Israel will not hearken. Go, get thee to them of the captivity, and speak unto them, whether they will hear or whether they will forbear. That stubbornness is itself a mark of who the Israelites are among the nations today.

    >

    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/ezekiel/3#v7)</sup> But the house of Israel will not hearken unto thee; for they will not hearken unto me: for all the house of Israel are impudent and hardhearted.

**[Exodus 4:2-5](/bible/exodus/4#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/4#v2)</sup> And the Lord said unto him, What is that in thine hand? And he said, A rod.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/exodus/4#v3)</sup> And he said, Cast it on the ground. And he cast it on the ground, and it became a serpent; and Moses fled from before it.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/exodus/4#v4)</sup> And the Lord said unto Moses, Put forth thine hand, and take it by the tail. And he put forth his hand, and caught it, and it became a rod in his hand:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/exodus/4#v5)</sup> That they may believe that the Lord God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, hath appeared unto thee.

- The first sign: the rod cast on the ground became a serpent, and Moses fled from before it; he caught it by the tail and it became a rod in his hand.
- The sign was given that they may believe that the LORD God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, hath appeared unto thee. Israel would not believe on a man's word alone; it took a miracle, because we were a people of little faith.

**[Exodus 4:6-8](/bible/exodus/4#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/exodus/4#v6)</sup> And the Lord said furthermore unto him, Put now thine hand into thy bosom. And he put his hand into his bosom: and when he took it out, behold, his hand was leprous as snow.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/exodus/4#v7)</sup> And he said, Put thine hand into thy bosom again. And he put his hand into his bosom again; and plucked it out of his bosom, and, behold, it was turned again as his other flesh.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/exodus/4#v8)</sup> And it shall come to pass, if they will not believe thee, neither hearken to the voice of the first sign, that they will believe the voice of the latter sign.

- The second sign: Moses put his hand into his bosom, and when he took it out, his hand was leprous as snow; he put it in again, and it was turned again as his other flesh.
- This was a miracle precisely because Moses was a very dark-skinned man; the daughters of the priest of Midian had taken him for an Egyptian. The hand came out white as snow and went back to his natural dark skin; had he been white already there would have been no sign in it.
- If they will not believe the first sign, they will believe the voice of the latter sign; the magicians of Egypt could imitate serpents, but not this.

**[Exodus 4:9](/bible/exodus/4#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/exodus/4#v9)</sup> And it shall come to pass, if they will not believe also these two signs, neither hearken unto thy voice, that thou shalt take of the water of the river, and pour it upon the dry land: and the water which thou takest out of the river shall become blood upon the dry land.

- The third sign: if they will not believe the first two, take of the water of the river and pour it upon the dry land, and the water shall become blood upon the dry land.

**[Exodus 4:10-12](/bible/exodus/4#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/exodus/4#v10)</sup> And Moses said unto the Lord, O my Lord, I am not eloquent, neither heretofore, nor since thou hast spoken unto thy servant: but I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/exodus/4#v11)</sup> And the Lord said unto him, Who hath made man’s mouth? or who maketh the dumb, or deaf, or the seeing, or the blind? have not I the Lord?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/exodus/4#v12)</sup> Now therefore go, and I will be with thy mouth, and teach thee what thou shalt say.

- Moses said, O my Lord, I am not eloquent, but I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue. Moses did not stutter, as is commonly taught; he was not fluent in the Hebrew tongue. He was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians and mighty in words and in deeds, but he grew up in Pharaoh's house speaking the Egyptian language and then spent forty years in Midian, and a language moves on in forty years.
- The LORD answered, Who hath made man's mouth? Now therefore go, and I will be with thy mouth, and teach thee what thou shalt say.

  Precepts:

    Moses was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians, and was mighty in words and in deeds; he had no speech impediment.

- Moses pressed, Send, I pray thee, by the hand of him whom thou wilt send; and the anger of the LORD was kindled against Moses. Is not Aaron the Levite thy brother? I know that he can speak well; he spoke the Hebrew of the people fluently.
- Aaron shall be thy spokesman unto the people: he shall be to thee instead of a mouth, and thou shalt be to him instead of God. Moses stood as God in this instance, speaking through Aaron his prophet, who interpreted to the people.
- And thou shalt take this rod in thine hand, wherewith thou shalt do signs.

- Moses returned to Jethro his father in law and asked leave, and Jethro said, Go in peace. The LORD said, Go, return into Egypt: for all the men are dead which sought thy life; the Pharaoh of chapter 2 had died.
- Moses took his wife and his sons and returned to the land of Egypt, and Moses took the rod of God in his hand. The LORD told him, Do all those wonders before Pharaoh which I have put in thine hand: but I will harden his heart, that he shall not let the people go.
- Thou shalt say unto Pharaoh, Thus saith the LORD, Israel is my son, even my firstborn. Israel was not the first nation created; it was among the last. But Israel is the first nation the Most High chose, the apple of His eye from Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob down. If thou refuse to let him go, behold, I will slay thy son, even thy firstborn.

**[Exodus 4:24-26](/bible/exodus/4#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/exodus/4#v24)</sup> And it came to pass by the way in the inn, that the Lord met him, and sought to kill him.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/exodus/4#v25)</sup> Then Zipporah took a sharp stone, and cut off the foreskin of her son, and cast it at his feet, and said, Surely a bloody husband art thou to me.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/exodus/4#v26)</sup> So he let him go: then she said, A bloody husband thou art, because of the circumcision.

- By the way in the inn, the LORD met him, and sought to kill him, because Moses had not circumcised his son. Zipporah took a sharp stone, cut off the foreskin of her son, and said, Surely a bloody husband art thou to me: bloody because of the circumcision she had to perform herself.
- This shows the laws did not begin at Sinai. Circumcision was the covenant given to Abraham for every man child in his generations, on the eighth day, and the uncircumcised man child whose flesh is not circumcised, that soul shall be cut off from his people: he hath broken my covenant. That is why Moses was worthy of death.

- The covenant still stands. A son of Israel must be circumcised; and a man that comes to the truth grown must still be circumcised, even at forty, fifty, or sixty years, as Abraham himself was circumcised at ninety and nine, and as Paul circumcised Timothy, whose father was a Greek. Nothing that defileth enters the kingdom.
- The Egyptians did not practice circumcision; they were a nation of uncleanness, and the covenant was between the Lord and Israel: after the doings of the land of Egypt, wherein ye dwelt, shall ye not do.

- [Romans 2](/bible/romans/2) does not do away with circumcision of the flesh. Circumcision verily profiteth if thou keep the law: the Jews who grew up circumcised and keeping the law, yet broke it, made their circumcision uncircumcision; and the uncircumcised of the cast-off northern kingdom who keep the righteousness of the law are counted as circumcised. Circumcision of the heart is putting away the evil of the doings, as the prophets say, take away the foreskins of your heart; the scribes and Pharisees were clean on the outside of the cup but full of iniquity within.

**[Exodus 4:27-31](/bible/exodus/4#v27)**

- Aaron spake all the words which the LORD had spoken unto Moses and did the signs in the sight of the people, and the people believed: and when they heard that the LORD had visited the children of Israel, and that he had looked upon their affliction, they bowed their heads and worshipped. After a long time in which the Lord had not been dealing with them, He finally heard their prayers.
