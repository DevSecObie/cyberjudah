## Introduction

This reading covers [Exodus 14](/bible/exodus/14) through 17: the Red Sea opened and the Egyptians overthrown, the song of Moses, the manna and the sabbath proved in the wilderness, and the first war with Amalek. The session was a straight reading with brief commentary and questions afterward; the answers are folded in under the verses they belong to.

**[Exodus 14:1-5](/bible/exodus/14#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/exodus/14#v1)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Moses, saying,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/14#v2)</sup> Speak unto the children of Israel, that they turn and encamp before Pi–hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, over against Baal–zephon: before it shall ye encamp by the sea.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/exodus/14#v3)</sup> For Pharaoh will say of the children of Israel, They are entangled in the land, the wilderness hath shut them in.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/exodus/14#v4)</sup> And I will harden Pharaoh’s heart, that he shall follow after them; and I will be honoured upon Pharaoh, and upon all his host; that the Egyptians may know that I am the Lord. And they did so.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/exodus/14#v5)</sup> And it was told the king of Egypt that the people fled: and the heart of Pharaoh and of his servants was turned against the people, and they said, Why have we done this, that we have let Israel go from serving us?

- The Most High is not done with the Egyptians. He destroyed their firstborn and brought Israel out, but He kept one more judgment in store for that wicked nation, and He will do the same to the wicked nation that has done the same things to Israel today.
- The heart of Pharaoh and of his servants turned as soon as the people were gone: why have we done this, that we have let Israel go from serving us? The oppressor does not release his slaves gladly.

**[Exodus 14:10-12](/bible/exodus/14#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/exodus/14#v10)</sup> And when Pharaoh drew nigh, the children of Israel lifted up their eyes, and, behold, the Egyptians marched after them; and they were sore afraid: and the children of Israel cried out unto the Lord.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/exodus/14#v11)</sup> And they said unto Moses, Because there were no graves in Egypt, hast thou taken us away to die in the wilderness? wherefore hast thou dealt thus with us, to carry us forth out of Egypt?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/exodus/14#v12)</sup> Is not this the word that we did tell thee in Egypt, saying, Let us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians? For it had been better for us to serve the Egyptians, than that we should die in the wilderness.

- At the first sight of the Egyptians marching, Israel lost all faith. The Lord had done miracle after miracle before their eyes and brought them out with an high hand, yet at the first sign of danger they said He brought them out to die. Our people have always been a rebellious people of little faith: a very froward generation, children in whom is no faith.
- Let us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians: that is the same word our people speak today. Why are you stirring up trouble, why tell us to come out of the ways of Babylon; let us alone to serve the slave master, because serving him feels easier than having faith in the Most High.
- The same spirit was in Israel in the days of Christ: if we let him thus alone, all men will believe on him, and the Romans shall come. The fear of the oppressor outweighed belief in the miracles.
- In captivity the people wax old in mind and will not depart: woe to the bloody city, the prey departed not. Israel was the prey in Egypt and did not want to leave, the same as now.

**[Exodus 14:13-14](/bible/exodus/14#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/exodus/14#v13)</sup> And Moses said unto the people, Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord, which he will shew to you to day: for the Egyptians whom ye have seen to day, ye shall see them again no more for ever.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/exodus/14#v14)</sup> The Lord shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace.

- Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord. The Egyptians whom ye have seen today, ye shall see them again no more for ever; that sounds like the same word that will stand over Babylon.
- The Lord shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace. The battle was never Israel's to win by strength.

**[Exodus 14:19-22](/bible/exodus/14#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/exodus/14#v19)</sup> And the angel of God, which went before the camp of Israel, removed and went behind them; and the pillar of the cloud went from before their face, and stood behind them:
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/exodus/14#v20)</sup> And it came between the camp of the Egyptians and the camp of Israel; and it was a cloud and darkness to them, but it gave light by night to these: so that the one came not near the other all the night.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/exodus/14#v21)</sup> And Moses stretched out his hand over the sea; and the Lord caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night, and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/exodus/14#v22)</sup> And the children of Israel went into the midst of the sea upon the dry ground: and the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand, and on their left.

- The angel of God that went before the camp, the angel in the chariot whom we know to be Christ, removed and went behind them, standing between the camp of Egypt and the camp of Israel.
- The pillar was a cloud and darkness to the Egyptians but gave light by night to Israel, so that the one came not near the other; Egypt could not see clearly where they were going.
- The Lord caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night; Israel went through on dry ground, and the waters were a wall on the right hand and on the left.

**[Exodus 14:23-28](/bible/exodus/14#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/exodus/14#v23)</sup> And the Egyptians pursued, and went in after them to the midst of the sea, even all Pharaoh’s horses, his chariots, and his horsemen.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/exodus/14#v24)</sup> And it came to pass, that in the morning watch the Lord looked unto the host of the Egyptians through the pillar of fire and of the cloud, and troubled the host of the Egyptians,
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/exodus/14#v25)</sup> And took off their chariot wheels, that they drave them heavily: so that the Egyptians said, Let us flee from the face of Israel; for the Lord fighteth for them against the Egyptians.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/exodus/14#v26)</sup> And the Lord said unto Moses, Stretch out thine hand over the sea, that the waters may come again upon the Egyptians, upon their chariots, and upon their horsemen.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/exodus/14#v27)</sup> And Moses stretched forth his hand over the sea, and the sea returned to his strength when the morning appeared; and the Egyptians fled against it; and the Lord overthrew the Egyptians in the midst of the sea.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/exodus/14#v28)</sup> And the waters returned, and covered the chariots, and the horsemen, and all the host of Pharaoh that came into the sea after them; there remained not so much as one of them.

- In the morning watch the Lord troubled the host of the Egyptians and took off their chariot wheels, so that they said, Let us flee from the face of Israel, for the Lord fighteth for them.
- Moses stretched out his hand and the sea returned in his strength. All that water that had stood up as a wall came down on the Egyptians, their chariots, and their horsemen, and consumed them. All praises to the Most High for loving His children enough to save us by such a mighty salvation.

**[Exodus 14:29-31](/bible/exodus/14#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/exodus/14#v29)</sup> But the children of Israel walked upon dry land in the midst of the sea; and the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand, and on their left.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/exodus/14#v30)</sup> Thus the Lord saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians; and Israel saw the Egyptians dead upon the sea shore.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/exodus/14#v31)</sup> And Israel saw that great work which the Lord did upon the Egyptians: and the people feared the Lord, and believed the Lord, and his servant Moses.

- Israel saw the Egyptians dead upon the sea shore and saw the great work of the Lord, and the people feared the Lord and believed the Lord and His servant Moses.
- The Lord who brought us out the first time with great signs and wonders will save Israel out of this captivity the same way; that is why the brothers and sisters must have faith in these last days, because a trial is the true test of faith.
- We have to accept that our salvation was their destruction. The Red Sea opened for Israel and closed on Egypt in the same hour; whoever hates that the Most High saves one nation by destroying the others will have to come to grips with it.
- Even the ground testified: where water stood before, dry land appeared, and a green field came up out of the violent stream; grass sprang up at the bottom of the sea for the people to pass.

# Exodus 15: The Song of Moses: The Lord Is a Man of War

Read the chapter → [Exodus 15](/bible/exodus/15)

**[Exodus 15:1-3](/bible/exodus/15#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/exodus/15#v1)</sup> Then sang Moses and the children of Israel this song unto the Lord, and spake, saying, I will sing unto the Lord, for he hath triumphed gloriously: the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/15#v2)</sup> The Lord is my strength and song, and he is become my salvation: he is my God, and I will prepare him an habitation; my father’s God, and I will exalt him.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/exodus/15#v3)</sup> The Lord is a man of war: the Lord is his name.

- Then sang Moses and the children of Israel this song unto the Lord. The whole chapter is a song and a prophecy, speaking not only of the coming out of Egypt but in the future tense as well.
- The Lord is a man of war: the Lord is His name. He loves war against His enemies; our carnal minds cannot fathom it, but the word says what it says. He mustereth the host of the battle: He brought the Babylonians, the Persians and Medes, the Greeks, and the Romans against Israel, and He has stood in the midst of the wars of the nations ever since.

  Precepts:
  - **[Isaiah 13:4](/bible/isaiah/13#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/13#v4)</sup> The noise of a multitude in the mountains, like as of a great people; a tumultuous noise of the kingdoms of nations gathered together: the Lord of hosts mustereth the host of the battle.

    The Lord of hosts mustereth the host of the battle; He is a man of war.

**[Exodus 15:14-16](/bible/exodus/15#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/exodus/15#v14)</sup> The people shall hear, and be afraid: sorrow shall take hold on the inhabitants of Palestina.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/exodus/15#v15)</sup> Then the dukes of Edom shall be amazed; the mighty men of Moab, trembling shall take hold upon them; all the inhabitants of Canaan shall melt away.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/exodus/15#v16)</sup> Fear and dread shall fall upon them; by the greatness of thine arm they shall be as still as a stone; till thy people pass over, O Lord, till the people pass over, which thou hast purchased.

- The people shall hear and be afraid; the dukes of Edom shall be amazed, trembling shall take hold of the mighty men of Moab, and the inhabitants of Canaan shall melt away. This goes into the future, when the nations would hear what the Lord had done for Israel.
- Fear and dread fall upon them by the greatness of His arm till the people pass over which the Lord purchased.

**[Exodus 15:20-21](/bible/exodus/15#v20)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/exodus/15#v21)</sup> And Miriam answered them, Sing ye to the Lord, for he hath triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea.

# Exodus 16: Manna From Heaven and the Sabbath Proved

Read the chapter → [Exodus 16](/bible/exodus/16)

**[Exodus 16:1-3](/bible/exodus/16#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/exodus/16#v1)</sup> And they took their journey from Elim, and all the congregation of the children of Israel came unto the wilderness of Sin, which is between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after their departing out of the land of Egypt.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/16#v2)</sup> And the whole congregation of the children of Israel murmured against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/exodus/16#v3)</sup> And the children of Israel said unto them, Would to God we had died by the hand of the Lord in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the flesh pots, and when we did eat bread to the full; for ye have brought us forth into this wilderness, to kill this whole assembly with hunger.

- The whole congregation murmured against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness: would to God we had died by the hand of the Lord in Egypt, when we sat by the flesh pots. The Lord commanded Moses to bring the people out, and when things got tough they made it his fault.
- That is us today. When the deliverance does not come easy, the people turn on the man sent to them instead of trusting the Most High who sent him.

**[Exodus 16:4-5](/bible/exodus/16#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/exodus/16#v4)</sup> Then said the Lord unto Moses, Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you; and the people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/exodus/16#v5)</sup> And it shall come to pass, that on the sixth day they shall prepare that which they bring in; and it shall be twice as much as they gather daily.

- Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you; and the people shall gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no. The Lord proves His people by the daily portion.
- On the sixth day they were to prepare twice as much as they gathered daily; that word becomes important later in the chapter.

**[Exodus 16:6-8](/bible/exodus/16#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/exodus/16#v6)</sup> And Moses and Aaron said unto all the children of Israel, At even, then ye shall know that the Lord hath brought you out from the land of Egypt:
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/exodus/16#v7)</sup> And in the morning, then ye shall see the glory of the Lord; for that he heareth your murmurings against the Lord: and what are we, that ye murmur against us?
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/exodus/16#v8)</sup> And Moses said, This shall be, when the Lord shall give you in the evening flesh to eat, and in the morning bread to the full; for that the Lord heareth your murmurings which ye murmur against him: and what are we? your murmurings are not against us, but against the Lord.

- Your murmurings are not against us, but against the Lord. Moses and Aaron were nobody of themselves; complaining against the leadership the Most High sets is murmuring against the Most High Himself.
- That is a heavy point for today. A man says he has no problem with the Bible, only with leadership, but it was the word coming out that made him mad; the Lord hears every murmur of the complainers and doubters.

**[Exodus 16:13-18](/bible/exodus/16#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/exodus/16#v13)</sup> And it came to pass, that at even the quails came up, and covered the camp: and in the morning the dew lay round about the host.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/exodus/16#v14)</sup> And when the dew that lay was gone up, behold, upon the face of the wilderness there lay a small round thing, as small as the hoar frost on the ground.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/exodus/16#v15)</sup> And when the children of Israel saw it, they said one to another, It is manna: for they wist not what it was. And Moses said unto them, This is the bread which the Lord hath given you to eat.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/exodus/16#v16)</sup> This is the thing which the Lord hath commanded, Gather of it every man according to his eating, an omer for every man, according to the number of your persons; take ye every man for them which are in his tents.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/exodus/16#v17)</sup> And the children of Israel did so, and gathered, some more, some less.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/exodus/16#v18)</sup> And when they did mete it with an omer, he that gathered much had nothing over, and he that gathered little had no lack; they gathered every man according to his eating.

- At even the quails came up and covered the camp, and in the morning the manna lay like a small round thing, small as the hoar frost. They said one to another, It is manna: for they wist not what it was.
- An omer is a measure, a portion, like measuring cups of flour; every man gathered according to his eating, an omer for every man. He that gathered much had nothing over, and he that gathered little had no lack.

**[Exodus 16:19-21](/bible/exodus/16#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/exodus/16#v19)</sup> And Moses said, Let no man leave of it till the morning.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/exodus/16#v20)</sup> Notwithstanding they hearkened not unto Moses; but some of them left of it until the morning, and it bred worms, and stank: and Moses was wroth with them.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/exodus/16#v21)</sup> And they gathered it every morning, every man according to his eating: and when the sun waxed hot, it melted.

- Moses said, Let no man leave of it till the morning; some hearkened not and left it, and it bred worms and stank, and Moses was wroth with them.
- They gathered it every morning, and when the sun waxed hot it melted; the portion was for the day the Lord gave it.

**[Exodus 16:22-30](/bible/exodus/16#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/exodus/16#v22)</sup> And it came to pass, that on the sixth day they gathered twice as much bread, two omers for one man: and all the rulers of the congregation came and told Moses.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/exodus/16#v23)</sup> And he said unto them, This is that which the Lord hath said, To morrow is the rest of the holy sabbath unto the Lord: bake that which ye will bake to day, and seethe that ye will seethe; and that which remaineth over lay up for you to be kept until the morning.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/exodus/16#v24)</sup> And they laid it up till the morning, as Moses bade: and it did not stink, neither was there any worm therein.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/exodus/16#v25)</sup> And Moses said, Eat that to day; for to day is a sabbath unto the Lord: to day ye shall not find it in the field.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/exodus/16#v26)</sup> Six days ye shall gather it; but on the seventh day, which is the sabbath, in it there shall be none.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/exodus/16#v27)</sup> And it came to pass, that there went out some of the people on the seventh day for to gather, and they found none.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/exodus/16#v28)</sup> And the Lord said unto Moses, How long refuse ye to keep my commandments and my laws?
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/exodus/16#v29)</sup> See, for that the Lord hath given you the sabbath, therefore he giveth you on the sixth day the bread of two days; abide ye every man in his place, let no man go out of his place on the seventh day.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/exodus/16#v30)</sup> So the people rested on the seventh day.

- On the sixth day they gathered twice as much, and Moses said, Tomorrow is the rest of the holy sabbath unto the Lord: bake that which ye will bake today. This is the scripture we always use to show that we do not cook on the sabbath; everything is prepared friday before sundown, and the leftovers are eaten the next day.
- What was laid up did not stink, neither was there any worm therein, because the Lord had given the bread of two days on the sixth day; the same manna that bred worms on other nights was preserved for the sabbath.
- Some of the people still went out on the seventh day to gather and found none, and the Lord said, How long refuse ye to keep my commandments and my laws? The sabbath was already binding in the wilderness.
- Abide ye every man in his place on the seventh day is about not going out to gather food on the sabbath. Some foolish Israelites read verse 29 out of context and say you never have to leave your house or join a congregation on the sabbath; that is not what it says. The sabbath is a holy convocation, a large gathering, in all your dwellings, and we are to come together on it.
- For the brothers and sisters far from a congregation: you may have to travel and you may have to be the firstfruits in your area. The first assemblies met in houses, and that is how camps start; a man will drive hours to a club, so drive for the truth, and the Lord honors that faith.

    > <sup>[1](/bible/leviticus/23#v1)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Moses, saying,
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/leviticus/23#v2)</sup> Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, Concerning the feasts of the Lord, which ye shall proclaim to be holy convocations, even these are my feasts.
    >
    > <sup>[3](/bible/leviticus/23#v3)</sup> Six days shall work be done: but the seventh day is the sabbath of rest, an holy convocation; ye shall do no work therein: it is the sabbath of the Lord in all your dwellings.

    The seventh day is a sabbath of rest, an holy convocation in all your dwellings; Exodus 16:29 does not undo the gathering.
  - **[Romans 16:3-5](/bible/romans/16#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/romans/16#v3)</sup> Greet Priscilla and Aquila my helpers in Christ Jesus:
    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/romans/16#v4)</sup> Who have for my life laid down their own necks: unto whom not only I give thanks, but also all the churches of the Gentiles.
    >

# Exodus 17: Water Out of the Rock and War With Amalek

**[Exodus 17:1-4](/bible/exodus/17#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/exodus/17#v1)</sup> And all the congregation of the children of Israel journeyed from the wilderness of Sin, after their journeys, according to the commandment of the Lord, and pitched in Rephidim: and there was no water for the people to drink.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/17#v2)</sup> Wherefore the people did chide with Moses, and said, Give us water that we may drink. And Moses said unto them, Why chide ye with me? wherefore do ye tempt the Lord?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/exodus/17#v3)</sup> And the people thirsted there for water; and the people murmured against Moses, and said, Wherefore is this that thou hast brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our cattle with thirst?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/exodus/17#v4)</sup> And Moses cried unto the Lord, saying, What shall I do unto this people? they be almost ready to stone me.

- At Rephidim there was no water, and the people did chide with Moses: wherefore is this that thou hast brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our cattle with thirst? At the first sign of trial the people jumped off the Lord's bandwagon again, the same as we do today when tribulation comes.
- Moses cried unto the Lord, What shall I do unto this people? they be almost ready to stone me. They were ready to kill the prophet over water, blaming the brother for what only the Most High controls; we have to build our faith up like Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, who trusted the Lord even when trials came.

**[Exodus 17:5-7](/bible/exodus/17#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/exodus/17#v5)</sup> And the Lord said unto Moses, Go on before the people, and take with thee of the elders of Israel; and thy rod, wherewith thou smotest the river, take in thine hand, and go.

> <sup>[6](/bible/exodus/17#v6)</sup> Behold, I will stand before thee there upon the rock in Horeb; and thou shalt smite the rock, and there shall come water out of it, that the people may drink. And Moses did so in the sight of the elders of Israel.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/exodus/17#v7)</sup> And he called the name of the place Massah, and Meribah, because of the chiding of the children of Israel, and because they tempted the Lord, saying, Is the Lord among us, or not?

- The Lord stood before Moses upon the rock in Horeb; Moses smote the rock, and water came out of it in the sight of the elders of Israel.
- He called the place Massah and Meribah, temptation and chiding, because they tempted the Lord, saying, Is the Lord among us, or not? That is wickedness: He had just made Egypt dark, slain their firstborn, and sent His angel in the chariot to guide us, and the people still asked whether He was with them. The mercy of the Most High in bearing that is beyond us.

- Then came Amalek, and fought with Israel in Rephidim. Amalek was the first nation to come against Israel after Egypt; Amalek was Esau's grandson, born to Eliphaz of Timna the concubine, a duke in the land of Edom.
- When Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed; when he let down his hand, Amalek prevailed. Aaron and Hur stayed up his hands until the going down of the sun. Moses was about eighty years old holding a rod above his head all day; the fathers were mighty men, stronger than we are today.
- The sign shewed the people whose hand their life was in: Moses is the man to hearken to, so do not speak against him. And Joshua discomfited Amalek with the edge of the sword; Joshua was a mighty warrior even then.

- The Lord hath sworn that the Lord will have war with Amalek from generation to generation. By the time Moses rehearsed it in Deuteronomy, forty years had passed and the children of Amalek had done nothing themselves; hundreds of years after that the Lord sent Saul by Samuel to smite Amalek for what was done coming out of Egypt. God be true and every man a liar: the nations do not get away with what they did to His people, and the judgment carries to the seed.
