## Introduction

This reading covers [Exodus 6](/bible/exodus/6) through 9: the Most High renews His promise to bring Israel out of Egypt, the genealogy of Reuben, Simeon, and Levi of whom came Moses and Aaron, Aaron's rod turned serpent, and the first seven plagues upon Egypt. The thread of the session is that through each plague the Most High was mocking an idol god of Egypt: Sobek the god of the rivers, Heqet the god of the frogs, Beelzebub the lord of flies and uncleanness, Hathor the goddess of cattle, Sekhmet the goddess of health and beauty, and Nut and Geb the gods of the sky and the earth.

**[Exodus 6:1](/bible/exodus/6#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/exodus/6#v1)</sup> Then the Lord said unto Moses, Now shalt thou see what I will do to Pharaoh: for with a strong hand shall he let them go, and with a strong hand shall he drive them out of his land.

- The Lord renews His promise: Pharaoh will not merely let Israel go, but with a strong hand he shall drive them out of his land.

**[Exodus 6:2-3](/bible/exodus/6#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/6#v2)</sup> And God spake unto Moses, and said unto him, I am the Lord:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/exodus/6#v3)</sup> And I appeared unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob, by the name of God Almighty, but by my name Jehovah was I not known to them.

- The Most High appeared unto Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob by the name of God Almighty, but by His name JEHOVAH was He not known to them. Some say Jehovah and some say Yahawah; you can say whichever name you want to say.

**[Exodus 6:4-5](/bible/exodus/6#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/exodus/6#v4)</sup> And I have also established my covenant with them, to give them the land of Canaan, the land of their pilgrimage, wherein they were strangers.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/exodus/6#v5)</sup> And I have also heard the groaning of the children of Israel, whom the Egyptians keep in bondage; and I have remembered my covenant.

- The covenant stands to give Israel the land of Canaan, the land of their pilgrimage wherein they were strangers; and the Most High heard the groaning of the children of Israel whom the Egyptians kept in bondage.
- The land of Canaan is in the land of Ham. So when people say we are Africans, the answer is that we come from Northeast Africa, from that continent, but we are not Africans; our land sits on the continent, and we are the Israelites.

**[Exodus 6:9-12](/bible/exodus/6#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/exodus/6#v9)</sup> And Moses spake so unto the children of Israel: but they hearkened not unto Moses for anguish of spirit, and for cruel bondage.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/exodus/6#v10)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Moses, saying,
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/exodus/6#v11)</sup> Go in, speak unto Pharaoh king of Egypt, that he let the children of Israel go out of his land.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/exodus/6#v12)</sup> And Moses spake before the Lord, saying, Behold, the children of Israel have not hearkened unto me; how then shall Pharaoh hear me, who am of uncircumcised lips?

- The children of Israel hearkened not unto Moses for anguish of spirit and for cruel bondage.
- Moses answered, Behold, the children of Israel have not hearkened unto me; how then shall Pharaoh hear me, who am of uncircumcised lips? This is why Aaron was given him for a mouthpiece in the next chapter.

**[Exodus 6:16-20](/bible/exodus/6#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/exodus/6#v16)</sup> And these are the names of the sons of Levi according to their generations; Gershon, and Kohath, and Merari: and the years of the life of Levi were an hundred thirty and seven years.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/exodus/6#v17)</sup> The sons of Gershon; Libni, and Shimi, according to their families.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/exodus/6#v18)</sup> And the sons of Kohath; Amram, and Izhar, and Hebron, and Uzziel: and the years of the life of Kohath were an hundred thirty and three years.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/exodus/6#v19)</sup> And the sons of Merari; Mahali and Mushi: these are the families of Levi according to their generations.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/exodus/6#v20)</sup> And Amram took him Jochebed his father’s sister to wife; and she bare him Aaron and Moses: and the years of the life of Amram were an hundred and thirty and seven years.

- Chapter 6 is dealing with the genealogy: the families of Reuben, Simeon, and Levi, and out of Levi came Moses and Aaron. Levi begat Kohath, Kohath begat Amram, and Amram took Jochebed to wife, and she bare him Aaron and Moses.

**[Exodus 6:26-27](/bible/exodus/6#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/exodus/6#v26)</sup> These are that Aaron and Moses, to whom the Lord said, Bring out the children of Israel from the land of Egypt according to their armies.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/exodus/6#v27)</sup> These are they which spake to Pharaoh king of Egypt, to bring out the children of Israel from Egypt: these are that Moses and Aaron.

- These are that Aaron and Moses to whom the Lord said, Bring out the children of Israel from the land of Egypt; these are they which spake to Pharaoh king of Egypt.

# Exodus 7: Aaron's Rod and the River Turned to Blood

Read the chapter → [Exodus 7](/bible/exodus/7)

**[Exodus 7:1-2](/bible/exodus/7#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/exodus/7#v1)</sup> And the Lord said unto Moses, See, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh: and Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/7#v2)</sup> Thou shalt speak all that I command thee: and Aaron thy brother shall speak unto Pharaoh, that he send the children of Israel out of his land.

- The Lord made Moses a god to Pharaoh, and Aaron his brother was his prophet. Moses was as God and Aaron was the mouthpiece, because Moses kept saying he was of uncircumcised lips.
- This is why you do not move in a spirit of speaking against your leadership. Airing your complaints in front of everybody instead of bringing them in order only shows what type of spirit you are moving in; it is completely out of order.

**[Exodus 7:8-12](/bible/exodus/7#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/exodus/7#v8)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Moses and unto Aaron, saying,
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/exodus/7#v9)</sup> When Pharaoh shall speak unto you, saying, Shew a miracle for you: then thou shalt say unto Aaron, Take thy rod, and cast it before Pharaoh, and it shall become a serpent.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/exodus/7#v10)</sup> And Moses and Aaron went in unto Pharaoh, and they did so as the Lord had commanded: and Aaron cast down his rod before Pharaoh, and before his servants, and it became a serpent.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/exodus/7#v11)</sup> Then Pharaoh also called the wise men and the sorcerers: now the magicians of Egypt, they also did in like manner with their enchantments.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/exodus/7#v12)</sup> For they cast down every man his rod, and they became serpents: but Aaron’s rod swallowed up their rods.

- It was Aaron who cast down the rod before Pharaoh, and it became a serpent. A lot of people to this day say it was Moses with the rod, because they do not read the Bible; Moses was telling Aaron what to do, and it was Aaron's rod that turned into the serpent.
- The magicians of Egypt did in like manner with their enchantments, and their rods became serpents; but Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods.

**[Exodus 7:19-22](/bible/exodus/7#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/exodus/7#v19)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Moses, Say unto Aaron, Take thy rod, and stretch out thine hand upon the waters of Egypt, upon their streams, upon their rivers, and upon their ponds, and upon all their pools of water, that they may become blood; and that there may be blood throughout all the land of Egypt, both in vessels of wood, and in vessels of stone.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/exodus/7#v20)</sup> And Moses and Aaron did so, as the Lord commanded; and he lifted up the rod, and smote the waters that were in the river, in the sight of Pharaoh, and in the sight of his servants; and all the waters that were in the river were turned to blood.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/exodus/7#v21)</sup> And the fish that was in the river died; and the river stank, and the Egyptians could not drink of the water of the river; and there was blood throughout all the land of Egypt.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/exodus/7#v22)</sup> And the magicians of Egypt did so with their enchantments: and Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, neither did he hearken unto them; as the Lord had said.

- All the waters of Egypt were turned to blood, in the streams, the rivers, the ponds, the pools, and in vessels of wood and vessels of stone; the fish died and the river stank.
- With this first plague the Most High was mocking Sobek, the Egyptian idol god of the rivers, showing that he could not defend his own waters.
- The magicians did so with their enchantments, but they could only copy the plague; they could not turn the water back clear. Seven days were fulfilled after the Lord had smitten the river.
- Many Egyptologists claim this never happened, because to admit it happened is to admit that the idols they claim to be gods and goddesses were powerless before our God, and that those idols do not exist at all.

# Exodus 8: Frogs, Lice, and Swarms of Flies

Read the chapter → [Exodus 8](/bible/exodus/8)

**[Exodus 8:5-8](/bible/exodus/8#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/exodus/8#v5)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Moses, Say unto Aaron, Stretch forth thine hand with thy rod over the streams, over the rivers, and over the ponds, and cause frogs to come up upon the land of Egypt.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/exodus/8#v6)</sup> And Aaron stretched out his hand over the waters of Egypt; and the frogs came up, and covered the land of Egypt.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/exodus/8#v7)</sup> And the magicians did so with their enchantments, and brought up frogs upon the land of Egypt.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/exodus/8#v8)</sup> Then Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron, and said, Intreat the Lord, that he may take away the frogs from me, and from my people; and I will let the people go, that they may do sacrifice unto the Lord.

- Aaron stretched out his hand over the waters, and the frogs came up and covered the land of Egypt. With this second plague the Most High was mocking Heqet, the Egyptian idol god of the frogs.
- The magicians did so with their enchantments and brought up frogs, but just as with the water they could only add to the plague; they could not take the frogs away.
- So Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron and said, Entreat the Lord that he may take away the frogs. Why did Pharaoh not call on Heqet, the god of frogs? He had to go to the Lord.

**[Exodus 8:16-19](/bible/exodus/8#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/exodus/8#v16)</sup> And the Lord said unto Moses, Say unto Aaron, Stretch out thy rod, and smite the dust of the land, that it may become lice throughout all the land of Egypt.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/exodus/8#v17)</sup> And they did so; for Aaron stretched out his hand with his rod, and smote the dust of the earth, and it became lice in man, and in beast; all the dust of the land became lice throughout all the land of Egypt.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/exodus/8#v18)</sup> And the magicians did so with their enchantments to bring forth lice, but they could not: so there were lice upon man, and upon beast.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/exodus/8#v19)</sup> Then the magicians said unto Pharaoh, This is the finger of God: and Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, and he hearkened not unto them; as the Lord had said.

- All the dust of the land became lice in man and in beast throughout all the land of Egypt. Imagine that: the dust of the whole land of Egypt turned to lice.
- The magicians did so with their enchantments to bring forth lice, but this time they could not; and the magicians said unto Pharaoh, This is the finger of God. Yet Pharaoh's heart was hardened.
- With the third and fourth plagues, the lice and the swarms of flies, the Most High was mocking Beelzebub, the idol lord of flies and uncleanness.

**[Exodus 8:21-24](/bible/exodus/8#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/exodus/8#v21)</sup> Else, if thou wilt not let my people go, behold, I will send swarms of flies upon thee, and upon thy servants, and upon thy people, and into thy houses: and the houses of the Egyptians shall be full of swarms of flies, and also the ground whereon they are.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/exodus/8#v22)</sup> And I will sever in that day the land of Goshen, in which my people dwell, that no swarms of flies shall be there; to the end thou mayest know that I am the Lord in the midst of the earth.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/exodus/8#v23)</sup> And I will put a division between my people and thy people: to morrow shall this sign be.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/exodus/8#v24)</sup> And the Lord did so; and there came a grievous swarm of flies into the house of Pharaoh, and into his servants’ houses, and into all the land of Egypt: the land was corrupted by reason of the swarm of flies.

- These were no ordinary flies. They were biting and killing men and animals; the scripture records that the bitings of flies killed and no remedy was found.
- The Lord severed the land of Goshen, where His people dwelt, so that no swarms of flies were there, and put a division between His people and Pharaoh's people, to the end Pharaoh may know that the Lord is in the midst of the earth.
- Goshen was Israel's own quarter in Egypt, where we had our own houses and our own cattle; it was given to us in the days of Joseph, as we read in the book of Genesis.

  - **[Wisdom of Solomon 16:9](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/16#v9)**

    The bitings of flies killed and no remedy was found; these were no ordinary flies.
  - **[Genesis 47:6](/bible/genesis/47#v6)**

    Goshen, the best of the land, was given to Israel in Joseph's day; that is where the children of Israel dwelt apart from Egypt.

Israel's sacrifices were the very animals Egypt held sacred, so Israel must go three days' journey into the wilderness.

# Exodus 9: Murrain, Boils, and Hail; Goshen Set Apart

Read the chapter → [Exodus 9](/bible/exodus/9)

**[Exodus 9:1-7](/bible/exodus/9#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/exodus/9#v1)</sup> Then the Lord said unto Moses, Go in unto Pharaoh, and tell him, Thus saith the Lord God of the Hebrews, Let my people go, that they may serve me.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/9#v2)</sup> For if thou refuse to let them go, and wilt hold them still,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/exodus/9#v3)</sup> Behold, the hand of the Lord is upon thy cattle which is in the field, upon the horses, upon the asses, upon the camels, upon the oxen, and upon the sheep: there shall be a very grievous murrain.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/exodus/9#v4)</sup> And the Lord shall sever between the cattle of Israel and the cattle of Egypt: and there shall nothing die of all that is the children’s of Israel.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/exodus/9#v5)</sup> And the Lord appointed a set time, saying, To morrow the Lord shall do this thing in the land.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/exodus/9#v6)</sup> And the Lord did that thing on the morrow, and all the cattle of Egypt died: but of the cattle of the children of Israel died not one.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/exodus/9#v7)</sup> And Pharaoh sent, and, behold, there was not one of the cattle of the Israelites dead. And the heart of Pharaoh was hardened, and he did not let the people go.

- A grievous murrain fell upon the cattle of Egypt, upon the horses, asses, camels, oxen, and sheep; and with this fifth plague the Most High was mocking Hathor, the Egyptian idol goddess of cattle.
- The Lord severed between the cattle of Israel and the cattle of Egypt: all the cattle of Egypt died, but of the cattle of the children of Israel died not one. Pharaoh sent, and behold, there was not one of the cattle of the Israelites dead; and his heart was hardened.
- Picture what was taking place in the land: flies all over Egypt, but where Israel dwelt there were no flies; the water was blood, but where Israel dwelt the water was good; the cattle of Egypt died, but Israel's cattle lived. The plagues touched Pharaoh, his servants, and the Egyptians only; they did not touch the Israelites at all, because this was the Lord redeeming the Israelites.
- The Egyptians could not pray to their idols to help them, because the Lord was mocking them, showing them that Hathor does not exist, Sobek does not exist, Heqet does not exist, and Beelzebub does not exist.

**[Exodus 9:8-12](/bible/exodus/9#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/exodus/9#v8)</sup> And the Lord said unto Moses and unto Aaron, Take to you handfuls of ashes of the furnace, and let Moses sprinkle it toward the heaven in the sight of Pharaoh.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/exodus/9#v9)</sup> And it shall become small dust in all the land of Egypt, and shall be a boil breaking forth with blains upon man, and upon beast, throughout all the land of Egypt.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/exodus/9#v10)</sup> And they took ashes of the furnace, and stood before Pharaoh; and Moses sprinkled it up toward heaven; and it became a boil breaking forth with blains upon man, and upon beast.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/exodus/9#v11)</sup> And the magicians could not stand before Moses because of the boils; for the boil was upon the magicians, and upon all the Egyptians.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/exodus/9#v12)</sup> And the Lord hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he hearkened not unto them; as the Lord had spoken unto Moses.

- Moses sprinkled ashes of the furnace toward heaven, and it became a boil breaking forth with blains upon man and upon beast throughout all the land of Egypt.
- The magicians could not stand before Moses because of the boils, for the boils were upon the magicians themselves and upon all the Egyptians. With this sixth plague the Most High was mocking Sekhmet, the Egyptian idol goddess of health and beauty.

**[Exodus 9:13-17](/bible/exodus/9#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/exodus/9#v13)</sup> And the Lord said unto Moses, Rise up early in the morning, and stand before Pharaoh, and say unto him, Thus saith the Lord God of the Hebrews, Let my people go, that they may serve me.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/exodus/9#v14)</sup> For I will at this time send all my plagues upon thine heart, and upon thy servants, and upon thy people; that thou mayest know that there is none like me in all the earth.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/exodus/9#v15)</sup> For now I will stretch out my hand, that I may smite thee and thy people with pestilence; and thou shalt be cut off from the earth.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/exodus/9#v16)</sup> And in very deed for this cause have I raised thee up, for to shew in thee my power; and that my name may be declared throughout all the earth.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/exodus/9#v17)</sup> As yet exaltest thou thyself against my people, that thou wilt not let them go?

- Some mock and ask, What type of God says let my people go and then keeps hardening Pharaoh's heart so he will not let them go? That is exactly the God we serve, and the Lord tells you Himself why He did it: for this cause have I raised thee up, for to shew in thee my power, and that my name may be declared throughout all the earth.
- The same thing is happening now, right here in this captivity. The powers of Babylon know what is being taught, yet the Lord hardens their hearts so that they keep passing laws and pressing against His people, so that the Most High can show His power again, just as He did in Egypt.

**[Exodus 9:18-21](/bible/exodus/9#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/exodus/9#v18)</sup> Behold, to morrow about this time I will cause it to rain a very grievous hail, such as hath not been in Egypt since the foundation thereof even until now.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/exodus/9#v19)</sup> Send therefore now, and gather thy cattle, and all that thou hast in the field; for upon every man and beast which shall be found in the field, and shall not be brought home, the hail shall come down upon them, and they shall die.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/exodus/9#v20)</sup> He that feared the word of the Lord among the servants of Pharaoh made his servants and his cattle flee into the houses:
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/exodus/9#v21)</sup> And he that regarded not the word of the Lord left his servants and his cattle in the field.

- Before the hail the Lord gave warning: gather thy cattle and all that thou hast in the field, for upon every man and beast found in the field the hail shall come down, and they shall die.
- He that feared the word of the Lord among the servants of Pharaoh made his servants and his cattle flee into the houses; they began to recognize the God of heaven and earth, the God of the Israelites.
- He that regarded not the word of the Lord left his servants and his cattle in the field. Many in Egypt held certain idols in more favor than others, and these trusted that Nut, the idol goddess of the sky, would keep them safe.

**[Exodus 9:22-26](/bible/exodus/9#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/exodus/9#v22)</sup> And the Lord said unto Moses, Stretch forth thine hand toward heaven, that there may be hail in all the land of Egypt, upon man, and upon beast, and upon every herb of the field, throughout the land of Egypt.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/exodus/9#v23)</sup> And Moses stretched forth his rod toward heaven: and the Lord sent thunder and hail, and the fire ran along upon the ground; and the Lord rained hail upon the land of Egypt.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/exodus/9#v24)</sup> So there was hail, and fire mingled with the hail, very grievous, such as there was none like it in all the land of Egypt since it became a nation.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/exodus/9#v25)</sup> And the hail smote throughout all the land of Egypt all that was in the field, both man and beast; and the hail smote every herb of the field, and brake every tree of the field.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/exodus/9#v26)</sup> Only in the land of Goshen, where the children of Israel were, was there no hail.

- This was no regular hail. There was hail, and fire mingled with the hail, very grievous, such as there was none like it in all the land of Egypt since it became a nation; the fire ran along upon the ground.
- With this seventh plague the Most High was mocking Nut and Geb, the Egyptian idol gods of the sky and the earth, striking from the sky and burning along the ground they were supposed to rule.
- Only in the land of Goshen, where the children of Israel were, was there no hail. The Lord does not play; our God is a terrible God.

**[Exodus 9:27-30](/bible/exodus/9#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/exodus/9#v27)</sup> And Pharaoh sent, and called for Moses and Aaron, and said unto them, I have sinned this time: the Lord is righteous, and I and my people are wicked.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/exodus/9#v28)</sup> Intreat the Lord (for it is enough) that there be no more mighty thunderings and hail; and I will let you go, and ye shall stay no longer.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/exodus/9#v29)</sup> And Moses said unto him, As soon as I am gone out of the city, I will spread abroad my hands unto the Lord; and the thunder shall cease, neither shall there be any more hail; that thou mayest know how that the earth is the Lord’s.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/exodus/9#v30)</sup> But as for thee and thy servants, I know that ye will not yet fear the Lord God.

- Pharaoh said, I have sinned this time: the Lord is righteous, and I and my people are wicked. This time? So all the other times, none of that was sin? Only now, under the thunder and hail, does he see it.
- Moses answered that the thunder and hail would cease, that thou mayest know how that the earth is the Lord's; but as for thee and thy servants, I know that ye will not yet fear the Lord God.

**[Exodus 9:31-32](/bible/exodus/9#v31)**

> <sup>[31](/bible/exodus/9#v31)</sup> And the flax and the barley was smitten: for the barley was in the ear, and the flax was bolled.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/exodus/9#v32)</sup> But the wheat and the rie were not smitten: for they were not grown up.

- The flax and the barley were smitten, for the barley was in the ear and the flax was bolled; but the wheat and the rie were not smitten, for they were not grown up.

**[Exodus 9:34-35](/bible/exodus/9#v34)**

> <sup>[34](/bible/exodus/9#v34)</sup> And when Pharaoh saw that the rain and the hail and the thunders were ceased, he sinned yet more, and hardened his heart, he and his servants.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/exodus/9#v35)</sup> And the heart of Pharaoh was hardened, neither would he let the children of Israel go; as the Lord had spoken by Moses.

- When Pharaoh saw that the rain and the hail and the thunders were ceased, he sinned yet more and hardened his heart, he and his servants. He still had the wheat and the rie in the ground, because they were not grown up yet, and that is part of why he went right back to hardening himself.
