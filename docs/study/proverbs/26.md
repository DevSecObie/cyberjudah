Read the chapter → [Proverbs 26](/bible/proverbs/26)

This chapter drives home how to deal with fools, sluggards, meddlers, and deceitful flatterers. The teacher walks through each figure Solomon draws, pulling precepts from Ecclesiasticus, Second Peter, Luke, and the law to show the same wisdom taught by the apostles and forefathers, who themselves quoted Solomon.

**[Proverbs 26:1-3](/bible/proverbs/26#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/26#v1)</sup> As snow in summer, and as rain in harvest, so honour is not seemly for a fool.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/26#v2)</sup> As the bird by wandering, as the swallow by flying, so the curse causeless shall not come.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/proverbs/26#v3)</sup> A whip for the horse, a bridle for the ass, and a rod for the fool’s back.

- As snow in summer and rain in harvest, so honor is not fitting for a fool. As the bird wandering and the swallow flying, the causeless curse shall not come.
- A whip for the horse, a bridle for the ass, and a rod for the fool's back. Ecclesiasticus says the same: correction and work are for a servant, and that correction is the rod for the fool's back.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 33:24](/bible/sirach/33#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/sirach/33#v24)</sup> Fodder, a wand, and burdens, are for the ass; and bread, correction, and work, for a servant. .

    Correction and work are appointed for a servant, matching the rod for the fool's back.

**[Proverbs 26:4-6](/bible/proverbs/26#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/proverbs/26#v4)</sup> Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou also be like unto him.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/proverbs/26#v5)</sup> Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/proverbs/26#v6)</sup> He that sendeth a message by the hand of a fool cutteth off the feet, and drinketh damage.

- Do not answer a fool according to his folly, lest you become like him. Many of us have argued with an idiot and been made to look like an idiot; onlookers wonder why you are even arguing with him.
- Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit. He that sends a message by the hand of a fool cuts off his own feet and drinks damage.

**[Proverbs 26:7-9](/bible/proverbs/26#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/proverbs/26#v7)</sup> The legs of the lame are not equal: so is a parable in the mouth of fools.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/proverbs/26#v8)</sup> As he that bindeth a stone in a sling, so is he that giveth honour to a fool.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/proverbs/26#v9)</sup> As a thorn goeth up into the hand of a drunkard, so is a parable in the mouth of fools.

- The legs of the lame are not equal, and so is a parable in the mouth of fools. As one who binds a stone in a sling, so is he that gives honor to a fool.
- As a thorn goes up into the hand of a drunkard, so is a parable in the mouth of fools. Like a splinter driven under the skin, it hurts and can take days to work out.

**[Proverbs 26:10-11](/bible/proverbs/26#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/proverbs/26#v10)</sup> The great God that formed all things both rewardeth the fool, and rewardeth transgressors.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/proverbs/26#v11)</sup> As a dog returneth to his vomit, so a fool returneth to his folly.

- The great God that formed all things both rewards the fool and rewards transgressors.
- As a dog returns to his vomit, so a fool returns to his folly. Second Peter calls this a true proverb, showing the apostles quoted Solomon: those who escape the pollutions of the world through the knowledge of Christ and then are again entangled are worse off than at the beginning.
  - **[2 Peter 2:20-22](/bible/2-peter/2#v20)**
    > <sup>[20](/bible/2-peter/2#v20)</sup> For if after they have escaped the pollutions of the world through the knowledge of the Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ, they are again entangled therein, and overcome, the latter end is worse with them than the beginning.
    >
    > <sup>[21](/bible/2-peter/2#v21)</sup> For it had been better for them not to have known the way of righteousness, than, after they have known it, to turn from the holy commandment delivered unto them.
    >
    > <sup>[22](/bible/2-peter/2#v22)</sup> But it is happened unto them according to the true proverb, The dog is turned to his own vomit again; and the sow that was washed to her wallowing in the mire.

    Those who escape the world through knowing Christ and turn back are worse than before; it is the true proverb of the dog returning to his vomit and the washed sow to the mire.

**[Proverbs 26:12-16](/bible/proverbs/26#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/proverbs/26#v12)</sup> Seest thou a man wise in his own conceit? there is more hope of a fool than of him.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/proverbs/26#v13)</sup> The slothful man saith, There is a lion in the way; a lion is in the streets.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/proverbs/26#v14)</sup> As the door turneth upon his hinges, so doth the slothful upon his bed.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/proverbs/26#v15)</sup> The slothful hideth his hand in his bosom; it grieveth him to bring it again to his mouth.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/proverbs/26#v16)</sup> The sluggard is wiser in his own conceit than seven men that can render a reason.

- Do you see a man wise in his own conceit? There is more hope for a fool than for him.
- The slothful man says there is a lion in the way and in the streets. As the door turns on its hinges, so the slothful turns on his bed; he hides his hand in his bosom and it grieves him to bring it back to his mouth.
- The sluggard is wiser in his own conceit than seven men that can render a reason.

**[Proverbs 26:17-19](/bible/proverbs/26#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/proverbs/26#v17)</sup> He that passeth by, and meddleth with strife belonging not to him, is like one that taketh a dog by the ears.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/proverbs/26#v18)</sup> As a mad man who casteth firebrands, arrows, and death,
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/proverbs/26#v19)</sup> So is the man that deceiveth his neighbour, and saith, Am not I in sport?

- He that passes by and meddles with strife not belonging to him is like one that takes a dog by the ears. Ecclesiasticus warns not to strive in a matter that does not concern you. A young lady who tried to break up a high-school fight was grabbed by police, slammed face-first, and left with brain damage; the Bible is a true book.
- As a madman who casts firebrands, arrows, and death, so is the man that deceives his neighbor and says, "Am I not in sport?"
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 11:9](/bible/sirach/11#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/sirach/11#v9)</sup> Strive not in a matter that concerneth thee not; and sit not in judgment with sinners.

    Do not strive in a matter that does not concern you.

**[Proverbs 26:20-23](/bible/proverbs/26#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/proverbs/26#v20)</sup> Where no wood is, there the fire goeth out: so where there is no talebearer, the strife ceaseth.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/proverbs/26#v21)</sup> As coals are to burning coals, and wood to fire; so is a contentious man to kindle strife.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/proverbs/26#v22)</sup> The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/proverbs/26#v23)</sup> Burning lips and a wicked heart are like a potsherd covered with silver dross.

- Where there is no wood the fire goes out, and where there is no tale-bearer the strife ceases. As coals to burning coals and wood to fire, so is a contentious man to kindle strife.
- The words of a tale-bearer are as wounds that go down into the innermost parts of the belly. Burning lips and a wicked heart are like a potsherd covered with silver dross.

**[Proverbs 26:24-26](/bible/proverbs/26#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/proverbs/26#v24)</sup> He that hateth dissembleth with his lips, and layeth up deceit within him;
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/proverbs/26#v25)</sup> When he speaketh fair, believe him not: for there are seven abominations in his heart.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/proverbs/26#v26)</sup> Whose hatred is covered by deceit, his wickedness shall be shewed before the whole congregation.

- He that hates dissembles with his lips and lays up deceit within, so when he speaks fair, do not believe him, for there are seven abominations in his heart.
- Whose hatred is covered by deceit, his wickedness shall be shown before the whole congregation, put out on display. Luke says nothing is covered that shall not be revealed. Watch for spirits that flatter you with kind words while carrying deceit; that thing will be revealed, so pray to the Most High.
  - **[Luke 12:2](/bible/luke/12#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/luke/12#v2)</sup> For there is nothing covered, that shall not be revealed; neither hid, that shall not be known.

    Nothing is covered that shall not be revealed, nor hid that shall not be known.

**[Proverbs 26:27-28](/bible/proverbs/26#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/proverbs/26#v27)</sup> Whoso diggeth a pit shall fall therein: and he that rolleth a stone, it will return upon him.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/proverbs/26#v28)</sup> A lying tongue hateth those that are afflicted by it; and a flattering mouth worketh ruin.

- Whoever digs a pit shall fall in it, and he that rolls a stone will have it return upon him.
- A lying tongue hates those that are afflicted by it, and a flattering mouth works ruin. That flattery is deceit.
