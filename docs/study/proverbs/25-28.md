Read the chapter → [Proverbs 25](/bible/proverbs/25)

This feast-day "four chapters a day" session covers [Proverbs 25](/bible/proverbs/25) through 28. The teacher notes plainly that Esau is the devil, and that our enemy does not want the black, Hispanic, and Israelite man to have anything, all the more reason to keep the commandments, hold the faith of Christ, and pray for the destruction of our enemies. He begins by reversing to the opening verse to show that this chapter is a separate collection: these are proverbs of Solomon which the men of King Hezekiah of Judah copied out. Those men were studying Solomon's writings; they understood that things written before their time were written for our learning and did not treat them as done away. He assigns homework to go back into the prophets, Chronicles, and Kings and read about King Hezekiah. This is a wisdom book, learned a little at a time and not mastered in a week.

**[Proverbs 25:1](/bible/proverbs/25#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/25#v1)</sup> These are also proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out.

- These are also proverbs of Solomon copied out by the men of Hezekiah king of Judah. Hezekiah and his men studied Solomon's writings because they understood that what was written before their time was written for our learning, to seek out the ways of our fathers and find the right paths.
- Go back and read about King Hezekiah in the prophets, Chronicles, and Kings as a homework assignment.

**[Proverbs 25:2-5](/bible/proverbs/25#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/25#v2)</sup> It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/proverbs/25#v3)</sup> The heaven for height, and the earth for depth, and the heart of kings is unsearchable.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/proverbs/25#v4)</sup> Take away the dross from the silver, and there shall come forth a vessel for the finer.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/proverbs/25#v5)</sup> Take away the wicked from before the king, and his throne shall be established in righteousness.

- It is the glory of the Most High to conceal a thing, but the honor of kings is to search out a matter. As the heavens for height and the earth for depth, the heart of kings is unsearchable.
- Take away the dross from the silver and a vessel comes forth for the finer; take away the wicked from before the king and his throne is established in righteousness.

**[Proverbs 25:6-10](/bible/proverbs/25#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/proverbs/25#v6)</sup> Put not forth thyself in the presence of the king, and stand not in the place of great men:
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/proverbs/25#v7)</sup> For better it is that it be said unto thee, Come up hither; than that thou shouldest be put lower in the presence of the prince whom thine eyes have seen.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/proverbs/25#v8)</sup> Go not forth hastily to strive, lest thou know not what to do in the end thereof, when thy neighbour hath put thee to shame.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/proverbs/25#v9)</sup> Debate thy cause with thy neighbour himself; and discover not a secret to another:
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/proverbs/25#v10)</sup> Lest he that heareth it put thee to shame, and thine infamy turn not away.

- Do not push yourself forward in the presence of the king or stand in the place of great men; better to be told "come up here" than to be put lower before a prince you have seen.
- Do not rush out to strive, or you will not know what to do in the end when your neighbor has shamed you. Debate your cause with your neighbor himself and do not reveal a secret to another.
- Debating your cause with your neighbor himself means going to him directly, as [Matthew 18](/bible/matthew/18) teaches: tell him his fault between you and him alone, and if he hears you, you have gained your brother.
- One who reveals a secret to another is a gossiper, a backbiter, a tale-bearer. You want to be able to trust that what you share is not repeated by others.
- If you reveal the secret, the one who hears it will put you to shame and your bad reputation will not turn away, because now your business is put out there.
  - **[Matthew 18:15](/bible/matthew/18#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/matthew/18#v15)</sup> Moreover if thy brother shall trespass against thee, go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone: if he shall hear thee, thou hast gained thy brother.

    Go and tell your brother his fault between you and him alone; if he hears you, you have gained your brother.
  - **[Proverbs 11:13](/bible/proverbs/11#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/proverbs/11#v13)</sup> A talebearer revealeth secrets: but he that is of a faithful spirit concealeth the matter.

    A tale-bearer reveals secrets, but a faithful spirit conceals the matter.

**[Proverbs 25:16-17](/bible/proverbs/25#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/proverbs/25#v16)</sup> Hast thou found honey? eat so much as is sufficient for thee, lest thou be filled therewith, and vomit it.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/proverbs/25#v17)</sup> Withdraw thy foot from thy neighbour’s house; lest he be weary of thee, and so hate thee.

- Eat only as much honey as is sufficient, lest you be filled and vomit it up.
- Withdraw your foot from your neighbor's house, lest he grow weary of you and hate you. You can wear out your welcome by being there all the time; give somebody a chance to miss you.

> <sup>[18](/bible/proverbs/25#v18)</sup> A man that beareth false witness against his neighbour is a maul, and a sword, and a sharp arrow.

- Like one who takes away a garment in cold weather, or vinegar upon soda, so is he that sings songs to a heavy heart.

**[Proverbs 25:21-22](/bible/proverbs/25#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/proverbs/25#v21)</sup> If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread to eat; and if he be thirsty, give him water to drink:
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/proverbs/25#v22)</sup> For thou shalt heap coals of fire upon his head, and the Lord shall reward thee.

- If your enemy is hungry give him bread, and if he is thirsty give him water, for you will heap coals of fire on his head and the Most High will reward you.
- This is not talking about our natural enemies of the other nations. This is about an enemy among your own people. We read the same in the law and the prophets: even help your enemy's ox or ass, and the commandments given only to Israel are to be taught to our children.
- [Matthew 5](/bible/matthew/5) says the same: love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them that use you and persecute you. Prayers for vengeance in [Luke 18](/bible/luke/18) and [Psalm 149](/bible/psalms/149) are for our natural enemies, but this passage concerns the enemies among your people.
- David showed this with Saul: he cut off the skirt of Saul's robe in the cave but would not kill the Most High's anointed, and Saul confessed David was more righteous, rewarding good for evil. The coals of fire are the shame that comes on such an enemy.

  - **[Matthew 5:43-44](/bible/matthew/5#v43)**

    Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them that despitefully use and persecute you.
  - **[1 Samuel 24:3-4](/bible/1-samuel/24#v3)**

Then David arose, and cut off the skirt of Saul’s robe privily.

# Proverbs 26: Honor Is Not Fitting for a Fool

Read the chapter → [Proverbs 26](/bible/proverbs/26)

This chapter drives home how to deal with fools, sluggards, meddlers, and deceitful flatterers. The teacher walks through each figure Solomon draws, pulling precepts from Ecclesiasticus, Second Peter, Luke, and the law to show the same wisdom taught by the apostles and forefathers, who themselves quoted Solomon.

**[Proverbs 26:1-3](/bible/proverbs/26#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/26#v1)</sup> As snow in summer, and as rain in harvest, so honour is not seemly for a fool.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/26#v2)</sup> As the bird by wandering, as the swallow by flying, so the curse causeless shall not come.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/proverbs/26#v3)</sup> A whip for the horse, a bridle for the ass, and a rod for the fool’s back.

- As snow in summer and rain in harvest, so honor is not fitting for a fool. As the bird wandering and the swallow flying, the causeless curse shall not come.
- A whip for the horse, a bridle for the ass, and a rod for the fool's back. Ecclesiasticus says the same: correction and work are for a servant, and that correction is the rod for the fool's back.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 33:24](/bible/sirach/33#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/sirach/33#v24)</sup> Fodder, a wand, and burdens, are for the ass; and bread, correction, and work, for a servant. .

    Correction and work are appointed for a servant, matching the rod for the fool's back.

**[Proverbs 26:4-6](/bible/proverbs/26#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/proverbs/26#v4)</sup> Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou also be like unto him.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/proverbs/26#v5)</sup> Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/proverbs/26#v6)</sup> He that sendeth a message by the hand of a fool cutteth off the feet, and drinketh damage.

- Do not answer a fool according to his folly, lest you become like him. Many of us have argued with an idiot and been made to look like an idiot; onlookers wonder why you are even arguing with him.
- Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit. He that sends a message by the hand of a fool cuts off his own feet and drinks damage.

**[Proverbs 26:7-9](/bible/proverbs/26#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/proverbs/26#v7)</sup> The legs of the lame are not equal: so is a parable in the mouth of fools.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/proverbs/26#v8)</sup> As he that bindeth a stone in a sling, so is he that giveth honour to a fool.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/proverbs/26#v9)</sup> As a thorn goeth up into the hand of a drunkard, so is a parable in the mouth of fools.

- The legs of the lame are not equal, and so is a parable in the mouth of fools. As one who binds a stone in a sling, so is he that gives honor to a fool.
- As a thorn goes up into the hand of a drunkard, so is a parable in the mouth of fools. Like a splinter driven under the skin, it hurts and can take days to work out.

**[Proverbs 26:10-11](/bible/proverbs/26#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/proverbs/26#v10)</sup> The great God that formed all things both rewardeth the fool, and rewardeth transgressors.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/proverbs/26#v11)</sup> As a dog returneth to his vomit, so a fool returneth to his folly.

- The great God that formed all things both rewards the fool and rewards transgressors.
- As a dog returns to his vomit, so a fool returns to his folly. Second Peter calls this a true proverb, showing the apostles quoted Solomon: those who escape the pollutions of the world through the knowledge of Christ and then are again entangled are worse off than at the beginning.

    Those who escape the world through knowing Christ and turn back are worse than before; it is the true proverb of the dog returning to his vomit and the washed sow to the mire.

# Proverbs 27: Boast Not of Tomorrow

Read the chapter → [Proverbs 27](/bible/proverbs/27)

This chapter opens with the warning not to boast of tomorrow, since none of us know how much time we have, and moves through friendship, open rebuke, contentment, and diligence with what the Most High has given. The teacher ties it to James on the uncertainty of life, to the fixed line the Most High sets on a man's days, and to the crown that has not endured for Israel from one generation to the next.

**[Proverbs 27:1-2](/bible/proverbs/27#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/27#v1)</sup> Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/27#v2)</sup> Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth; a stranger, and not thine own lips.

- Do not boast of tomorrow, for you do not know what a day may bring forth. James says the same: you do not know what will be tomorrow, for your life is a vapor; you ought to say, if the Most High wills, we will do this or that.
- None of us knows how much time we have. The Most High draws the line: Moses spoke unadvisedly at the waters of strife and was told he would not enter the promised land, and though he died in full strength at 120, the Most High set the end of his days.
- Let another man praise you, and not your own mouth; a stranger, and not your own lips. Do not be boastful.
  - **[James 4:13-15](/bible/james/4#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/james/4#v13)</sup> Go to now, ye that say, To day or to morrow we will go into such a city, and continue there a year, and buy and sell, and get gain:
    >
    > <sup>[14](/bible/james/4#v14)</sup> Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapour, that appeareth for a little time, and then vanisheth away.
    >
    > <sup>[15](/bible/james/4#v15)</sup> For that ye ought to say, If the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that.

    You do not know what shall be tomorrow, for life is a vapor; you ought to say, if the Most High wills, we shall do this or that.

**[Proverbs 27:3-6](/bible/proverbs/27#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/proverbs/27#v3)</sup> A stone is heavy, and the sand weighty; but a fool’s wrath is heavier than them both.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/proverbs/27#v4)</sup> Wrath is cruel, and anger is outrageous; but who is able to stand before envy?
>

# Proverbs 28: The Righteous Are Bold as a Lion

Read the chapter → [Proverbs 28](/bible/proverbs/28)

This closing chapter contrasts the wicked and the righteous, the lawkeeper and the lawforsaker, the honest poor and the man of unjust gain. The teacher shows that boldness comes from being with Christ and speaking the words of the Most High, that keeping the commandments prolongs the throne of Israel, and that mercy comes to the one who confesses and forsakes his sins.

**[Proverbs 28:1-2](/bible/proverbs/28#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/28#v1)</sup> The wicked flee when no man pursueth: but the righteous are bold as a lion.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/28#v2)</sup> For the transgression of a land many are the princes thereof: but by a man of understanding and knowledge the state thereof shall be prolonged.

- The wicked flee when no man pursues, but the righteous are bold as a lion. Peter and John were perceived as unlearned men, yet their boldness made men take knowledge that they had been with Christ, who taught as one having authority. We can be bold this same way by using the words of the Most High, which no one can gainsay.
- For the transgression of a land many are its princes, but by a man of understanding and knowledge its state shall be prolonged. That understanding and knowledge is the Most High's commandments; David told Solomon that keeping the commandments keeps a man on the throne of Israel.
  - **[Acts 4:13](/bible/acts/4#v13)**
