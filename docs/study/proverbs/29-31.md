Read the chapter → [Proverbs 29](/bible/proverbs/29)

This session is a "four chapters a day" feast-day reading covering [Proverbs 29](/bible/proverbs/29) through [Ecclesiastes 1](/bible/ecclesiastes/1). The teacher reads through each chapter and marks it as a good chapter full of "good bits," urging everyone to write down their scriptures and any questions as they read. He notes plainly that these are wisdom chapters to take note of, and that the point of the reading is to keep the commandments and retain what you study a little at a time.

**[Proverbs 29:1](/bible/proverbs/29#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/29#v1)</sup> He, that being often reproved hardeneth his neck, shall suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy.

- The man who is often corrected and stiffens his neck against it will be destroyed suddenly and without remedy. Hardening yourself against reproof is a fatal path.

**[Proverbs 29:2](/bible/proverbs/29#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/29#v2)</sup> When the righteous are in authority, the people rejoice: but when the wicked beareth rule, the people mourn.

- When the righteous hold authority the people rejoice, but when the wicked rule the people mourn. Leadership sets the mood and welfare of the whole people.

**[Proverbs 29:3-6](/bible/proverbs/29#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/proverbs/29#v3)</sup> Whoso loveth wisdom rejoiceth his father: but he that keepeth company with harlots spendeth his substance.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/proverbs/29#v4)</sup> The king by judgment establisheth the land: but he that receiveth gifts overthroweth it.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/proverbs/29#v5)</sup> A man that flattereth his neighbour spreadeth a net for his feet.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/proverbs/29#v6)</sup> In the transgression of an evil man there is a snare: but the righteous doth sing and rejoice.

- The one who loves wisdom gladdens his father, while the man who keeps company with harlots wastes his substance.
- A king establishes the land by judgment, but the ruler who takes bribes tears it down. The flatterer sets a net for his neighbor's feet.

**[Proverbs 29:7-8](/bible/proverbs/29#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/proverbs/29#v7)</sup> The righteous considereth the cause of the poor: but the wicked regardeth not to know it.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/proverbs/29#v8)</sup> Scornful men bring a city into a snare: but wise men turn away wrath.

- The righteous man cares about the cause of the poor; the wicked will not even trouble himself to know it.
- Scornful men throw a city into a snare, but wise men turn away wrath.

**[Proverbs 29:9-11](/bible/proverbs/29#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/proverbs/29#v9)</sup> If a wise man contendeth with a foolish man, whether he rage or laugh, there is no rest.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/proverbs/29#v10)</sup> The bloodthirsty hate the upright: but the just seek his soul.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/proverbs/29#v11)</sup> A fool uttereth all his mind: but a wise man keepeth it in till afterwards.

- Contending with a fool brings no rest whether he rages or laughs. The bloodthirsty hate the upright man.
- A fool pours out his whole mind, but a wise man holds it back and keeps it in until afterwards.

**[Proverbs 29:12-14](/bible/proverbs/29#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/proverbs/29#v12)</sup> If a ruler hearken to lies, all his servants are wicked.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/proverbs/29#v13)</sup> The poor and the deceitful man meet together: the Lord lighteneth both their eyes.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/proverbs/29#v14)</sup> The king that faithfully judgeth the poor, his throne shall be established for ever.

- If a ruler listens to lies, all his servants become wicked. The tone of the leader corrupts or steadies those under him.
- The king who faithfully judges the poor will have his throne established forever.

**[Proverbs 29:15-17](/bible/proverbs/29#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/proverbs/29#v15)</sup> The rod and reproof give wisdom: but a child left to himself bringeth his mother to shame.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/proverbs/29#v16)</sup> When the wicked are multiplied, transgression increaseth: but the righteous shall see their fall.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/proverbs/29#v17)</sup> Correct thy son, and he shall give thee rest; yea, he shall give delight unto thy soul.

- The rod and reproof give wisdom, but a child left to himself brings his mother to shame. Correct your son and he will give you rest and delight your soul.

**[Proverbs 29:18](/bible/proverbs/29#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/proverbs/29#v18)</sup> Where there is no vision, the people perish: but he that keepeth the law, happy is he.

- Where there is no vision the people perish, but he that keeps the law is blessed. Without direction and the law a people fall apart.

**[Proverbs 29:19-23](/bible/proverbs/29#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/proverbs/29#v19)</sup> A servant will not be corrected by words: for though he understand he will not answer.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/proverbs/29#v20)</sup> Seest thou a man that is hasty in his words? there is more hope of a fool than of him.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/proverbs/29#v21)</sup> He that delicately bringeth up his servant from a child shall have him become his son at the length.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/proverbs/29#v22)</sup> An angry man stirreth up strife, and a furious man aboundeth in transgression.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/proverbs/29#v23)</sup> A man’s pride shall bring him low: but honour shall uphold the humble in spirit.

- A servant is not corrected by words alone; the hasty man in his words has less hope than a fool.
- An angry man stirs up strife and a furious man abounds in transgression. A man's pride brings him low, but the humble in spirit hold honor.

**[Proverbs 29:24-27](/bible/proverbs/29#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/proverbs/29#v24)</sup> Whoso is partner with a thief hateth his own soul: he heareth cursing, and bewrayeth it not.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/proverbs/29#v25)</sup> The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the Lord shall be safe.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/proverbs/29#v26)</sup> Many seek the ruler’s favour; but every man’s judgment cometh from the Lord.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/proverbs/29#v27)</sup> An unjust man is an abomination to the just: and he that is upright in the way is abomination to the wicked.

- The partner of a thief hates his own soul. The fear of man brings a snare, but whoever trusts in the Most High is safe.
- A man's judgment comes from the Most High, and the unjust and the upright are an abomination to one another.

# Proverbs 30: Agur's Prophecy and the Purity of God's Word

Read the chapter → [Proverbs 30](/bible/proverbs/30)

**[Proverbs 30:1-3](/bible/proverbs/30#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/30#v1)</sup> The words of Agur the son of Jakeh, even the prophecy: the man spake unto Ithiel, even unto Ithiel and Ucal,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/30#v2)</sup> Surely I am more brutish than any man, and have not the understanding of a man.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/proverbs/30#v3)</sup> I neither learned wisdom, nor have the knowledge of the holy.

- These are the words of Agur, who confesses himself more brutish than any man, having neither learned wisdom nor the knowledge of the holy. Wisdom begins with admitting how little one knows on his own.

**[Proverbs 30:4](/bible/proverbs/30#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/proverbs/30#v4)</sup> Who hath ascended up into heaven, or descended? who hath gathered the wind in his fists? who hath bound the waters in a garment? who hath established all the ends of the earth? what is his name, and what is his son’s name, if thou canst tell?

- Who has gone up into heaven or gathered the wind in his fist? The questions point to the Most High and to His Son, whose name the reader is challenged to tell.

**[Proverbs 30:5-6](/bible/proverbs/30#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/proverbs/30#v5)</sup> Every word of God is pure: he is a shield unto them that put their trust in him.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/proverbs/30#v6)</sup> Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar.

- Every word of the Most High is pure, and He is a shield to those who trust in Him. Add nothing to His words, lest He reprove you and you be found a liar.

**[Proverbs 30:7-9](/bible/proverbs/30#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/proverbs/30#v7)</sup> Two things have I required of thee; deny me them not before I die:
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/proverbs/30#v8)</sup> Remove far from me vanity and lies: give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with food convenient for me:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/proverbs/30#v9)</sup> Lest I be full, and deny thee, and say, Who is the Lord? or lest I be poor, and steal, and take the name of my God in vain.

- Agur asks for two things before he dies: to have vanity and lies removed, and to be given neither poverty nor riches but food convenient for him.
- Too much wealth tempts a man to deny the Most High; poverty tempts him to steal and profane the name of his God.

**[Proverbs 30:10-14](/bible/proverbs/30#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/proverbs/30#v10)</sup> Accuse not a servant unto his master, lest he curse thee, and thou be found guilty.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/proverbs/30#v11)</sup> There is a generation that curseth their father, and doth not bless their mother.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/proverbs/30#v12)</sup> There is a generation that are pure in their own eyes, and yet is not washed from their filthiness.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/proverbs/30#v13)</sup> There is a generation, O how lofty are their eyes! and their eyelids are lifted up.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/proverbs/30#v14)</sup> There is a generation, whose teeth are as swords, and their jaw teeth as knives, to devour the poor from off the earth, and the needy from among men.

- Do not accuse a servant to his master. There is a generation that curses its father and does not bless its mother, and one pure in its own eyes yet not washed from its filthiness.
- A generation with lofty eyes devours the poor from off the earth and the needy from among men.

**[Proverbs 30:15-17](/bible/proverbs/30#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/proverbs/30#v15)</sup> The horseleach hath two daughters, crying, Give, give. There are three things that are never satisfied, yea, four things say not, It is enough:
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/proverbs/30#v16)</sup> The grave; and the barren womb; the earth that is not filled with water; and the fire that saith not, It is enough.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/proverbs/30#v17)</sup> The eye that mocketh at his father, and despiseth to obey his mother, the ravens of the valley shall pick it out, and the young eagles shall eat it.

- The grave, the barren womb, the dry earth, and the fire never say "enough." The eye that mocks its father and despises its mother will be picked out by the ravens and eaten by young eagles.

**[Proverbs 30:18-20](/bible/proverbs/30#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/proverbs/30#v18)</sup> There be three things which are too wonderful for me, yea, four which I know not:
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/proverbs/30#v19)</sup> The way of an eagle in the air; the way of a serpent upon a rock; the way of a ship in the midst of the sea; and the way of a man with a maid.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/proverbs/30#v20)</sup> Such is the way of an adulterous woman; she eateth, and wipeth her mouth, and saith, I have done no wickedness.

- Four things are too wonderful to trace: the eagle in the air, the serpent on the rock, the ship in the sea, and the way of a man with a maid.
- The adulterous woman eats, wipes her mouth, and claims she has done no wickedness.

**[Proverbs 30:21-23](/bible/proverbs/30#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/proverbs/30#v21)</sup> For three things the earth is disquieted, and for four which it cannot bear:
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/proverbs/30#v22)</sup> For a servant when he reigneth; and a fool when he is filled with meat;
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/proverbs/30#v23)</sup> For an odious woman when she is married; and an handmaid that is heir to her mistress.

- Four things disquiet the earth: a servant when he reigns, a fool filled with meat, an odious woman when she is married, and a handmaid who displaces her mistress.

**[Proverbs 30:24-28](/bible/proverbs/30#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/proverbs/30#v24)</sup> There be four things which are little upon the earth, but they are exceeding wise:
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/proverbs/30#v25)</sup> The ants are a people not strong, yet they prepare their meat in the summer;
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/proverbs/30#v26)</sup> The conies are but a feeble folk, yet make they their houses in the rocks;
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/proverbs/30#v27)</sup> The locusts have no king, yet go they forth all of them by bands;
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/proverbs/30#v28)</sup> The spider taketh hold with her hands, and is in kings’ palaces.

- Four small creatures are exceeding wise: the ants who store their meat in summer, the feeble conies who house in the rocks, the kingless locusts who march in bands, and the spider found in kings' palaces.

**[Proverbs 30:29-33](/bible/proverbs/30#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/proverbs/30#v29)</sup> There be three things which go well, yea, four are comely in going:
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/proverbs/30#v30)</sup> A lion which is strongest among beasts, and turneth not away for any;
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/proverbs/30#v31)</sup> A greyhound; an he goat also; and a king, against whom there is no rising up.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/proverbs/30#v32)</sup> If thou hast done foolishly in lifting up thyself, or if thou hast thought evil, lay thine hand upon thy mouth.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/proverbs/30#v33)</sup> Surely the churning of milk bringeth forth butter, and the wringing of the nose bringeth forth blood: so the forcing of wrath bringeth forth strife.

- Four things are comely in their going: the lion, the greyhound, the he-goat, and the king against whom none rise up.
- If you have acted foolishly or thought evil, lay your hand on your mouth; for as churning brings butter and wringing the nose brings blood, so forcing wrath brings forth strife.

# Proverbs 31: The Mother's Charge and the Virtuous Woman

Read the chapter → [Proverbs 31](/bible/proverbs/31)

The teacher closes this chapter by calling it a good one for both sisters and brothers to take from: brothers can figure out the type of wife they need to seek and raise toward, and sisters can figure out how they ought to be.

**[Proverbs 31:1-3](/bible/proverbs/31#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/31#v1)</sup> The words of king Lemuel, the prophecy that his mother taught him.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/31#v2)</sup> What, my son? and what, the son of my womb? and what, the son of my vows?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/proverbs/31#v3)</sup> Give not thy strength unto women, nor thy ways to that which destroyeth kings.

- These are the words of King Lemuel, the prophecy his mother taught him. She warns her son not to give his strength to women nor his ways to that which destroys kings.

**[Proverbs 31:4-7](/bible/proverbs/31#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/proverbs/31#v4)</sup> It is not for kings, O Lemuel, it is not for kings to drink wine; nor for princes strong drink:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/proverbs/31#v5)</sup> Lest they drink, and forget the law, and pervert the judgment of any of the afflicted.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/proverbs/31#v6)</sup> Give strong drink unto him that is ready to perish, and wine unto those that be of heavy hearts.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/proverbs/31#v7)</sup> Let him drink, and forget his poverty, and remember his misery no more.

- It is not for kings to drink wine or strong drink, lest they forget the law and pervert the judgment of the afflicted.
- Strong drink is for those ready to perish and heavy of heart, that they may forget their poverty and misery.

**[Proverbs 31:8-9](/bible/proverbs/31#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/proverbs/31#v8)</sup> Open thy mouth for the dumb in the cause of all such as are appointed to destruction.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/proverbs/31#v9)</sup> Open thy mouth, judge righteously, and plead the cause of the poor and needy.

- Open your mouth for the dumb and for all who are appointed to destruction. Judge righteously and plead the cause of the poor and needy.

**[Proverbs 31:10-12](/bible/proverbs/31#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/proverbs/31#v10)</sup> Who can find a virtuous woman? for her price is far above rubies.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/proverbs/31#v11)</sup> The heart of her husband doth safely trust in her, so that he shall have no need of spoil.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/proverbs/31#v12)</sup> She will do him good and not evil all the days of her life.

- Who can find a virtuous woman? Her price is far above rubies. Her husband trusts her safely and she does him good, not evil, all her days.

**[Proverbs 31:13-16](/bible/proverbs/31#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/proverbs/31#v13)</sup> She seeketh wool, and flax, and worketh willingly with her hands.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/proverbs/31#v14)</sup> She is like the merchants’ ships; she bringeth her food from afar.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/proverbs/31#v15)</sup> She riseth also while it is yet night, and giveth meat to her household, and a portion to her maidens.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/proverbs/31#v16)</sup> She considereth a field, and buyeth it: with the fruit of her hands she planteth a vineyard.

- She works willingly with her hands, brings her food from afar like merchant ships, rises while it is still night to feed her household, and considers a field to buy and plant a vineyard.

**[Proverbs 31:17-20](/bible/proverbs/31#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/proverbs/31#v17)</sup> She girdeth her loins with strength, and strengtheneth her arms.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/proverbs/31#v18)</sup> She perceiveth that her merchandise is good: her candle goeth not out by night.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/proverbs/31#v19)</sup> She layeth her hands to the spindle, and her hands hold the distaff.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/proverbs/31#v20)</sup> She stretcheth out her hand to the poor; yea, she reacheth forth her hands to the needy.

- She girds her loins with strength, sees that her merchandise is good, keeps her candle burning by night, and reaches out her hands to the poor and needy.

**[Proverbs 31:21-24](/bible/proverbs/31#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/proverbs/31#v21)</sup> She is not afraid of the snow for her household: for all her household are clothed with scarlet.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/proverbs/31#v22)</sup> She maketh herself coverings of tapestry; her clothing is silk and purple.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/proverbs/31#v23)</sup> Her husband is known in the gates, when he sitteth among the elders of the land.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/proverbs/31#v24)</sup> She maketh fine linen, and selleth it; and delivereth girdles unto the merchant.

- She does not fear the snow, for her household is clothed in scarlet. Her clothing is silk and purple, her husband is known in the gates, and she sells the fine linen and girdles she makes.

**[Proverbs 31:25-27](/bible/proverbs/31#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/proverbs/31#v25)</sup> Strength and honour are her clothing; and she shall rejoice in time to come.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/proverbs/31#v26)</sup> She openeth her mouth with wisdom; and in her tongue is the law of kindness.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/proverbs/31#v27)</sup> She looketh well to the ways of her household, and eateth not the bread of idleness.

- Strength and honor are her clothing and she rejoices in time to come. She opens her mouth with wisdom, her tongue carries the law of kindness, and she does not eat the bread of idleness.

**[Proverbs 31:28-31](/bible/proverbs/31#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/proverbs/31#v28)</sup> Her children arise up, and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praiseth her.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/proverbs/31#v29)</sup> Many daughters have done virtuously, but thou excellest them all.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/proverbs/31#v30)</sup> Favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain: but a woman that feareth the Lord, she shall be praised.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/proverbs/31#v31)</sup> Give her of the fruit of her hands; and let her own works praise her in the gates.

- Her children rise up and call her blessed, and her husband praises her above all. Favor is deceitful and beauty is vain, but a woman who fears the Most High shall be praised and given the fruit of her own hands.
