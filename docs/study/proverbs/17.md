Read the chapter → [Proverbs 17](/bible/proverbs/17)

This session covers [Proverbs 17](/bible/proverbs/17) through 20, wisdom Solomon received directly from the Most High God. Solomon ruled in a peaceable time because his father King David fought the wars and brought nations under subjection, so Solomon had leisure to gain wisdom. In this captivity most of us work long hours by design, because Esau does not want us to return to our rightful places, and that leaves little time to study. But wisdom is required of us, so we must put away distractions like television and apply our time to the word.

  - **[Ecclesiasticus 38:24](/bible/sirach/38#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/sirach/38#v24)</sup> The wisdom of a learned man cometh by opportunity of leisure: and he that hath little business shall become wise.

    Little business makes a man wise, but this captivity steals our hours.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:64](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v64)**
    > <sup>[64](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v64)</sup> And the Lord shall scatter thee among all people, from the one end of the earth even unto the other; and there thou shalt serve other gods, which neither thou nor thy fathers have known, even wood and stone.

    Scattered among the nations, our feet find no rest and we serve, which is why our time is consumed.
  - **[Wisdom of Solomon 7:17](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/7#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/7#v17)</sup> For he hath given me certain knowledge of the things that are, namely, to know how the world was made, and the operation of the elements:

    The Most High gave Solomon knowledge of how everything works.
  - **[1 Kings 4:29](/bible/1-kings/4#v29)**
    > <sup>[29](/bible/1-kings/4#v29)</sup> And God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding exceeding much, and largeness of heart, even as the sand that is on the sea shore.

    God gave Solomon wisdom exceeding all men, and he spake three thousand proverbs, some of which we now read.

**[Proverbs 17:1-6](/bible/proverbs/17#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/17#v1)</sup> Better is a dry morsel, and quietness therewith, than an house full of sacrifices with strife.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/17#v2)</sup> A wise servant shall have rule over a son that causeth shame, and shall have part of the inheritance among the brethren.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/proverbs/17#v3)</sup> The fining pot is for silver, and the furnace for gold: but the Lord trieth the hearts.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/proverbs/17#v4)</sup> A wicked doer giveth heed to false lips; and a liar giveth ear to a naughty tongue.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/proverbs/17#v5)</sup> Whoso mocketh the poor reproacheth his Maker: and he that is glad at calamities shall not be unpunished.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/proverbs/17#v6)</sup> Children’s children are the crown of old men; and the glory of children are their fathers.

- The fining pot and furnace remove impurities from silver and gold; likewise the Most High tries our hearts through trials to rid us of our impurities. We must go through trials and tribulations to gain the kingdom.
- This is why Christianity is false when it teaches you only have to accept Jesus in your heart to be saved forever. That is not what the scriptures say.
- Whoso mocks the poor reproaches his Maker, and gladness at another man's calamity does not go unpunished.
- Children's children are the crown of old men, and the glory of children are their fathers. Many of us grew up in fatherless homes, but we must not glory in a father's dishonor.
- Sisters should not speak evil of the man they chose to lie down with and bring in seed by, no matter how bad he is; children raised to despise their fathers must repent of it in this truth.
- Just as grandchildren crown the old, we glorify our Father in heaven and our forefathers by our repentance and return to righteousness.
  - **[1 Peter 4:12](/bible/1-peter/4#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/1-peter/4#v12)</sup> Beloved, think it not strange concerning the fiery trial which is to try you, as though some strange thing happened unto you:

    Think it not strange concerning the fiery trial; rejoice as partakers of Christ's suffering.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 2:1](/bible/sirach/2#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/2#v1)</sup> My son, if thou come to serve the Lord, prepare thy soul for temptation.

    Set thy heart aright and constantly endure the trials that will come.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 2:5](/bible/sirach/2#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/sirach/2#v5)</sup> For gold is tried in the fire, and acceptable men in the furnace of adversity.

    We must be proven through trials to gain the kingdom.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 3:10](/bible/sirach/3#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/sirach/3#v10)</sup> Glory not in the dishonour of thy father; for thy father’s dishonour is no glory unto thee.

    We must not glory in a father's dishonor.

**[Proverbs 17:7-9](/bible/proverbs/17#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/proverbs/17#v7)</sup> Excellent speech becometh not a fool: much less do lying lips a prince.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/proverbs/17#v8)</sup> A gift is as a precious stone in the eyes of him that hath it: whithersoever it turneth, it prospereth.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/proverbs/17#v9)</sup> He that covereth a transgression seeketh love; but he that repeateth a matter separateth very friends.

- A gift is as a precious stone to him that has it; a well-instructed wife is one of these gifts, silent and loving, priceless and far above rubies.
- A silent and loving woman does not mean one who never talks; it means a shamefaced sister who is not a chest bumper, whose continent mind cannot be valued.
- He that covers a transgression seeks love, but he that repeats a matter separates very friends. Covering sin like Ananias and Sapphira, who conspired to lie to the apostles, is condemned.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 26:14](/bible/sirach/26#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/sirach/26#v14)</sup> A silent and loving woman is a gift of the Lord; and there is nothing so much worth as a mind well instructed.

    Such a wife is priceless, a shamefaced and faithful woman.
  - **[Proverbs 31:10](/bible/proverbs/31#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/proverbs/31#v10)</sup> Who can find a virtuous woman? for her price is far above rubies.

    Who can find a virtuous woman? Her price is far above rubies and her husband safely trusts in her.
  - **[Acts 5:1](/bible/acts/5#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/acts/5#v1)</sup> But a certain man named Ananias, with Sapphira his wife, sold a possession,

    Ananias and Sapphira kept back part of the price and lied to Peter, an example of covering transgression.
  - **[Jeremiah 2:33](/bible/jeremiah/2#v33)**
    > <sup>[33](/bible/jeremiah/2#v33)</sup> Why trimmest thou thy way to seek love? therefore hast thou also taught the wicked ones thy ways.

    Trimming thy way to seek love teaches the wicked one thy ways, the ways of the unrepentant.
  - **[1 John 3:4](/bible/1-john/3#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/1-john/3#v4)</sup> Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law.

    Sin is the transgression of the law.

**[Proverbs 17:10-14](/bible/proverbs/17#v10)**
> <sup>[10](/bible/proverbs/17#v10)</sup> A reproof entereth more into a wise man than an hundred stripes into a fool.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/proverbs/17#v11)</sup> An evil man seeketh only rebellion: therefore a cruel messenger shall be sent against him.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/proverbs/17#v12)</sup> Let a bear robbed of her whelps meet a man, rather than a fool in his folly.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/proverbs/17#v13)</sup> Whoso rewardeth evil for good, evil shall not depart from his house.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/proverbs/17#v14)</sup> The beginning of strife is as when one letteth out water: therefore leave off contention, before it be meddled with.

- A reproof enters more into a wise man than a hundred stripes into a fool; the wise receive correction while the fool must be beaten.
- Whoso rewards evil for good, evil shall not depart from his house. That is everything right there.
- The beginning of strife is like letting out water, so leave off contention before it is meddled with; where envying and strife are, there is confusion and every evil work.
- Evil begins in the thoughts of the mind, so the strife the fool starts flows from an evil heart.
  - **[James 3:16](/bible/james/3#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/james/3#v16)</sup> For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.

    Where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.
  - **[Mark 7:21](/bible/mark/7#v21)**
    > <sup>[21](/bible/mark/7#v21)</sup> For from within, out of the heart of men, proceed evil thoughts, adulteries, fornications, murders,

    Out of the heart of men proceed evil thoughts, where evil begins.

**[Proverbs 17:15-16](/bible/proverbs/17#v15)**
> <sup>[15](/bible/proverbs/17#v15)</sup> He that justifieth the wicked, and he that condemneth the just, even they both are abomination to the Lord.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/proverbs/17#v16)</sup> Wherefore is there a price in the hand of a fool to get wisdom, seeing he hath no heart to it?

- He that justifies the wicked and he that condemns the just are both an abomination to the Most High. To justify the wicked is to wrest judgment and take a bribe.
- A bribe blinds the eyes of the wise and perverts the words of the righteous, and this perverting of judgment is something we suffer with as a people.
- Woe to those who call evil good and good evil, who justify the wicked for reward; the reward usually comes as a financial gift or a place of importance.
- There is no point in a fool holding a price to buy wisdom when he has no heart for it.
  - **[Deuteronomy 16:19](/bible/deuteronomy/16#v19)**
    > <sup>[19](/bible/deuteronomy/16#v19)</sup> Thou shalt not wrest judgment; thou shalt not respect persons, neither take a gift: for a gift doth blind the eyes of the wise, and pervert the words of the righteous.

    Thou shalt not wrest judgment, respect persons, nor take a gift, for a gift blinds the wise.
  - **[Exodus 23:8](/bible/exodus/23#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/exodus/23#v8)</sup> And thou shalt take no gift: for the gift blindeth the wise, and perverteth the words of the righteous.

    Thou shalt take no gift, for the gift blinds the wise and perverts the words of the righteous.
  - **[Isaiah 5:20](/bible/isaiah/5#v20)**
    > <sup>[20](/bible/isaiah/5#v20)</sup> Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness; that put bitter for sweet, and sweet for bitter!

    Woe unto them that call evil good and good evil, that justify the wicked for reward.

**[Proverbs 17:17-20](/bible/proverbs/17#v17)**
> <sup>[17](/bible/proverbs/17#v17)</sup> A friend loveth at all times, and a brother is born for adversity.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/proverbs/17#v18)</sup> A man void of understanding striketh hands, and becometh surety in the presence of his friend.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/proverbs/17#v19)</sup> He loveth transgression that loveth strife: and he that exalteth his gate seeketh destruction.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/proverbs/17#v20)</sup> He that hath a froward heart findeth no good: and he that hath a perverse tongue falleth into mischief.

- A friend loves at all times and a brother is born for adversity; a man void of understanding strikes hands and becomes surety before his friend.
- He that loves transgression loves strife, and he that exalts his gate seeks destruction; a froward heart finds no good and a perverse tongue falls into mischief.

**[Proverbs 17:21-24](/bible/proverbs/17#v21)**
> <sup>[21](/bible/proverbs/17#v21)</sup> He that begetteth a fool doeth it to his sorrow: and the father of a fool hath no joy.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/proverbs/17#v22)</sup> A merry heart doeth good like a medicine: but a broken spirit drieth the bones.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/proverbs/17#v23)</sup> A wicked man taketh a gift out of the bosom to pervert the ways of judgment.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/proverbs/17#v24)</sup> Wisdom is before him that hath understanding; but the eyes of a fool are in the ends of the earth.

- A foolish son is grief to his father; Solomon understood the reasonings of men, which is why a wicked man takes a gift from the bosom to pervert judgment appears again and again in this one chapter.
- A merry heart does good like a medicine, but a broken spirit dries the bones.
- Wisdom is before him that has understanding, but a fool's eyes wander to the ends of the earth. Understanding comes only through keeping the commandments.
- This is why in Ecclesiastes Solomon gives the conclusion of the whole matter: keep the commandments, for that is the whole duty of man.
  - **[Psalm 111:10](/bible/psalms/111#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/psalms/111#v10)</sup> The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom: a good understanding have all they that do his commandments: his praise endureth for ever.

    The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom; good understanding have all they that do his commandments.

**[Proverbs 17:25-28](/bible/proverbs/17#v25)**
> <sup>[25](/bible/proverbs/17#v25)</sup> A foolish son is a grief to his father, and bitterness to her that bare him.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/proverbs/17#v26)</sup> Also to punish the just is not good, nor to strike princes for equity.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/proverbs/17#v27)</sup> He that hath knowledge spareth his words: and a man of understanding is of an excellent spirit.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/proverbs/17#v28)</sup> Even a fool, when he holdeth his peace, is counted wise: and he that shutteth his lips is esteemed a man of understanding.

- A foolish son is a grief to his father and bitterness to his mother; to punish the just and strike princes for equity is not good.
- He that has knowledge spares his words, and even a fool who holds his peace is counted wise. Talking too much, especially without the words of the Most High, marks a fool.
- Let thy speech be short, comprehending much in few words; speak only when there is need of thee and hold thy tongue among great men.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 32:7](/bible/sirach/32#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/sirach/32#v7)</sup> Speak, young man, if there be need of thee: and yet scarcely when thou art twice asked.

    Use few words, especially among great men.
