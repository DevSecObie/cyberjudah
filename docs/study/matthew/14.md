## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Matthew 14 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-4, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Matthew 14:1](/bible/matthew/14#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/matthew/14#v1)</sup> At that time Herod the tetrarch heard of the fame of Jesus,

- “At that time Herod the tetrarch heard of the fame of Jesus”. This is Herod Antipas, a son of Herod the Great. A tetrarch is a governor over the land. His brother Archelaus was over a nation of people. After Herod the Great died the Romans would no longer give his sons the title of king; they had governorship over different parts of their father's kingdom. Even Herod the Great, called a king, was a vassal king: a king in name, answering to Rome.

**[Matthew 14:2-3](/bible/matthew/14#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/matthew/14#v2)</sup> And said unto his servants, This is John the Baptist; he is risen from the dead; and therefore mighty works do shew forth themselves in him.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/matthew/14#v3)</sup> For Herod had laid hold on John, and bound him, and put him in prison for Herodias’ sake, his brother Philip’s wife.

- He said “This is John the Baptist; he is risen from the dead”, for Herod had laid hold on John and put him in prison “for Herodias’ sake, his brother Philip’s wife.” Antipas took his brother's wife, and she was really his niece. The people were ratchet.

**[Matthew 14:4](/bible/matthew/14#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/matthew/14#v4)</sup> For John said unto him, It is not lawful for thee to have her.

- “For John said unto him, It is not lawful for thee to have her.” Why would John tell Herod it was unlawful? Not only because she was near of kin, or because it was adultery. The point is this: Herod was king of the Jews, so he understood the laws.
- The Herods were raised up in the laws. John Hyrcanus conquered the whole of Edom and forced its inhabitants to convert, and from then they became a section of the Jewish people; Josephus gives that history. They were forced to keep the laws of God, and after a time they grew up in them, not that they were righteous, but they understood. Herod the Great built back the temple. So John was telling him: you know the laws, you know it is unlawful to marry your brother's wife.

  Precepts:
  - **[Joshua 8:35](/bible/joshua/8#v35)**
    > <sup>[35](/bible/joshua/8#v35)</sup> There was not a word of all that Moses commanded, which Joshua read not before all the congregation of Israel, with the women, and the little ones, and the strangers that were conversant among them.

    “There was not a word of all that Moses commanded, which Joshua read not before all the congregation of Israel, with the women, and the little ones, and the strangers that were conversant among them.” When we came out of Egypt, the strangers, the other nations living among us, had to be conversant in all the laws.
  - **[Acts 13:1](/bible/acts/13#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/acts/13#v1)</sup> Now there were in the church that was at Antioch certain prophets and teachers; as Barnabas, and Simeon that was called Niger, and Lucius of Cyrene, and Manaen, which had been brought up with Herod the tetrarch, and Saul.

    “Now there were in the church that was at Antioch certain prophets and teachers; as Barnabas”, Simeon, Lucius of Cyrene, “and Manaen, which had been brought up with Herod the tetrarch, and Saul.” Paul and these men came up together with Herod, learning the doctrine of the law. Herod knew the laws.
  - **[Acts 22:3](/bible/acts/22#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/acts/22#v3)</sup> I am verily a man which am a Jew, born in Tarsus, a city in Cilicia, yet brought up in this city at the feet of Gamaliel, and taught according to the perfect manner of the law of the fathers, and was zealous toward God, as ye all are this day.

    Paul says “I am verily a man which am a Jew, born in Tarsus, a city in Cilicia, yet brought up in this city at the feet of Gamaliel”. Saul, who came up with Herod the tetrarch, came up at the feet of Gamaliel.
