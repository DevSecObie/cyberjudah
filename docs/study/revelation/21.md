## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Revelation 21 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-27, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Revelation 21:1](/bible/revelation/21#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/revelation/21#v1)</sup> And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea.

- “And I saw a new heaven and a new earth”: the next Christian thought is that the earth and the heavens will explode and be gone. All it means is a change of worlds, a change of kingdoms. “For the first heaven and the first earth were passed away”: the kingdoms we know today, the United States of America, France, Germany, the UK, are all going to be cast down. “And there was no more sea”: there ain’t going to be no water on the planet? No. The sea is the multitude of nations rebelling against the Most High, and they are all going to be subdued under this new kingdom that’s coming.
- Additional notes on last week’s chapter. “And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away”: based on the scriptures, there is just going to be a change of worlds; the earth abides for ever. “And there was no more sea”: the sea is the riotousness of the nations.
- This is what happens after. Remember chapter 20: what happened to the nations? They are gone, judged by Christ and cast into the lake of fire. Now in chapter 21 John sees “a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea.” No more sea means the nations are gone, for the multitude of many people make a noise like the noise of the seas (Isaiah 17:12).

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiastes 1:4](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/ecclesiastes/1#v4)</sup> One generation passeth away, and another generation cometh: but the earth abideth for ever.

    Regarding the new earth. “One generation passeth away, and another generation cometh”: meaning those souls always come back; that’s for another class, but it’s telling you about regeneration. “But the earth abideth for ever”: the earth does not go away and come back like generations do.
  - **[Daniel 7:9](/bible/daniel/7#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/daniel/7#v9)</sup> I beheld till the thrones were cast down, and the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment was white as snow, and the hair of his head like the pure wool: his throne was like the fiery flame, and his wheels as burning fire.

    A change of worlds. “I beheld till the thrones were cast down”: the kingdoms on the earth cast down, destroyed. “The Ancient of days did sit”: the Most High, having no end of days nor beginning of days. “Whose garment was white as snow”: in order to have a garment you need a body to put a garment on, so God does have a body. “The hair of his head like the pure wool”: he got hair on his head, and it’s not long, stringy hair; it’s like the pure wool.

**[Revelation 21:2](/bible/revelation/21#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/revelation/21#v2)</sup> And I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband.

- “The holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven”: like it said, thy kingdom come, people think it’s going to come out the sky. That’s not what it’s talking about. “Prepared as a bride adorned for her husband”: here’s the key. Jerusalem is a people before it’s a place. The Lord is not marrying a place; he’s marrying a people. So this great city coming from heaven is symbolic of the people first and foremost. For those of you that think the kingdom’s going to come from the sky, you think you’ll be walking down the street one day and see it coming: that’s not happening. This is why the Lord said you must read his word precept upon precept.
- “And I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband”: in 2 Esdras 9 the prophet Ezra saw a vision of a woman who transformed into new Jerusalem, and that is what this verse makes reference to. This city Jerusalem refers to the people, first and foremost.
- Thy kingdom come: we’ve got to understand the secrets of the Lord’s prayer, what he is really talking about. “And I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven”. You’ll read that and go, wait a minute, so the kingdom is coming down? But Isaiah 60 said the nations are going to build it. I’m confused.

- “The nations of them which are saved shall walk in the light of it: and the kings of the earth do bring their glory and honour into it”: Isaiah 60 explains it. Like we always say, the New Testament is an abbreviated version of the Old Testament; the Old goes into a lot more detail.
- Listen: from verse 24 all the way down through chapter 22 is a recap of Revelation chapter 20 and Isaiah 60, giving a summary again. “And the nations of them which are saved shall walk in the light of it: and the kings of the earth do bring their glory and honour into it.” Whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire (Revelation 20:15), so there are no nations when the kingdom of verses 1 to 23 comes from heaven. When nations show up at verse 24, it is recapping.
- Write it down: Revelation 21:24-27, all the way through Revelation 22, is a recap or summary of Revelation 20:1-6. Isaiah 60 is explaining Revelation 20; Micah 4 is explaining Revelation 20; Isaiah 2 is explaining Revelation 20, the reigning with Christ; Zechariah 14, about the nations coming up, and Zechariah 2:1-5 go with it too. Revelation 21:1-23 is after the thousand year reign, after the second death.
