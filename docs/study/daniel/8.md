**[Daniel 8:1-2](/bible/daniel/8#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/daniel/8#v1)</sup> In the third year of the reign of king Belshazzar a vision appeared unto me, even unto me Daniel, after that which appeared unto me at the first.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/daniel/8#v2)</sup> And I saw in a vision; and it came to pass, when I saw, that I was at Shushan in the palace, which is in the province of Elam; and I saw in a vision, and I was by the river of Ulai.

- In the third year of Belshazzar's reign, a vision appeared to Daniel after the first, and he saw himself at Shushan in the palace in the province of Elam, by the river of Ulai.
- This is the same vision as chapter 7, but now the kingdoms are described with different animals.

**[Daniel 8:3-4](/bible/daniel/8#v3)**
> <sup>[3](/bible/daniel/8#v3)</sup> Then I lifted up mine eyes, and saw, and, behold, there stood before the river a ram which had two horns: and the two horns were high; but one was higher than the other, and the higher came up last.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/daniel/8#v4)</sup> I saw the ram pushing westward, and northward, and southward; so that no beasts might stand before him, neither was there any that could deliver out of his hand; but he did according to his will, and became great.

- Daniel saw a ram with two horns, one higher than the other, the higher coming up last, pushing westward, northward, and southward so that no beast could stand before him.
- This ram is the Persians and the Medes again. Before they were a bear; now they are a ram, with the two horns being the two kingdoms.

**[Daniel 8:5-8](/bible/daniel/8#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/daniel/8#v5)</sup> And as I was considering, behold, an he goat came from the west on the face of the whole earth, and touched not the ground: and the goat had a notable horn between his eyes.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/daniel/8#v6)</sup> And he came to the ram that had two horns, which I had seen standing before the river, and ran unto him in the fury of his power.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/daniel/8#v7)</sup> And I saw him come close unto the ram, and he was moved with choler against him, and smote the ram, and brake his two horns: and there was no power in the ram to stand before him, but he cast him down to the ground, and stamped upon him: and there was none that could deliver the ram out of his hand.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/daniel/8#v8)</sup> Therefore the he goat waxed very great: and when he was strong, the great horn was broken; and for it came up four notable ones toward the four winds of heaven.

- A he goat came from the west over the face of the whole earth without touching the ground, with a notable horn between his eyes, and ran unto the ram in the fury of his power.
- This goat is Greece, Alexander the Greek. Moved with hatred, he smote the ram, broke his two horns, cast him down, and stamped upon him, and none could deliver the ram.
- When the goat was strong, the great horn was broken, and four notable horns came up toward the four winds of heaven.

**[Daniel 8:9-12](/bible/daniel/8#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/daniel/8#v9)</sup> And out of one of them came forth a little horn, which waxed exceeding great, toward the south, and toward the east, and toward the pleasant land.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/daniel/8#v10)</sup> And it waxed great, even to the host of heaven; and it cast down some of the host and of the stars to the ground, and stamped upon them.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/daniel/8#v11)</sup> Yea, he magnified himself even to the prince of the host, and by him the daily sacrifice was taken away, and the place of his sanctuary was cast down.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/daniel/8#v12)</sup> And an host was given him against the daily sacrifice by reason of transgression, and it cast down the truth to the ground; and it practised, and prospered.

- Out of one of them came a little horn which waxed exceeding great toward the south, the east, and the pleasant land, magnifying itself even to the host of heaven and casting down some of the host and the stars.
- It magnified itself against the prince of the host, took away the daily sacrifice, cast down the place of the sanctuary, and cast down the truth to the ground, practicing and prospering.

**[Daniel 8:13-14](/bible/daniel/8#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/daniel/8#v13)</sup> Then I heard one saint speaking, and another saint said unto that certain saint which spake, How long shall be the vision concerning the daily sacrifice, and the transgression of desolation, to give both the sanctuary and the host to be trodden under foot?
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/daniel/8#v14)</sup> And he said unto me, Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed.

- A saint asked how long the vision concerning the daily sacrifice and the transgression of desolation should last, and the answer came: unto two thousand and three hundred days, then shall the sanctuary be cleansed.
- The cleansing of the sanctuary goes into Hanukkah, the feast of dedication. That history is recorded in First Maccabees.
  - **[1 Maccabees 4:52-54](/bible/1-maccabees/4#v52)**
    > <sup>[52](/bible/1-maccabees/4#v52)</sup> Now on the five and twentieth day of the ninth month, which is called the month Casleu, in the hundred forty and eighth year, they rose up betimes in the morning,
    >
    > <sup>[53](/bible/1-maccabees/4#v53)</sup> And offered sacrifice according to the law upon the new altar of burnt offerings, which they had made.
    >
    > <sup>[54](/bible/1-maccabees/4#v54)</sup> Look, at what time and what day the heathen had profaned it, even in that was it dedicated with songs, and citherns, and harps, and cymbals.

    The sanctuary is dedicated again on the very day the heathen had profaned it, the dedication Daniel's cleansing points to.

**[Daniel 8:15-19](/bible/daniel/8#v15)**
> <sup>[15](/bible/daniel/8#v15)</sup> And it came to pass, when I, even I Daniel, had seen the vision, and sought for the meaning, then, behold, there stood before me as the appearance of a man.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/daniel/8#v16)</sup> And I heard a man’s voice between the banks of Ulai, which called, and said, Gabriel, make this man to understand the vision.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/daniel/8#v17)</sup> So he came near where I stood: and when he came, I was afraid, and fell upon my face: but he said unto me, Understand, O son of man: for at the time of the end shall be the vision.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/daniel/8#v18)</sup> Now as he was speaking with me, I was in a deep sleep on my face toward the ground: but he touched me, and set me upright.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/daniel/8#v19)</sup> And he said, Behold, I will make thee know what shall be in the last end of the indignation: for at the time appointed the end shall be.

- As Daniel sought the meaning, one with the appearance of a man stood before him, and a voice called Gabriel to make him understand the vision.
- Gabriel came near, and Daniel was afraid and fell on his face, but was told to understand, for at the time of the end shall be the vision. Gabriel set him upright to know what shall be in the last end of the indignation.

**[Daniel 8:20-22](/bible/daniel/8#v20)**
> <sup>[20](/bible/daniel/8#v20)</sup> The ram which thou sawest having two horns are the kings of Media and Persia.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/daniel/8#v21)</sup> And the rough goat is the king of Grecia: and the great horn that is between his eyes is the first king.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/daniel/8#v22)</sup> Now that being broken, whereas four stood up for it, four kingdoms shall stand up out of the nation, but not in his power.

- The ram with two horns are the kings of Media and Persia. The rough goat is the king of Grecia, and the great horn between his eyes is the first king, Alexander the Greek.
- The great horn being broken and four standing up in its place are four kingdoms that stand up out of the nation but not in his power. These four were Alexander's four generals.

**[Daniel 8:23-25](/bible/daniel/8#v23)**
> <sup>[23](/bible/daniel/8#v23)</sup> And in the latter time of their kingdom, when the transgressors are come to the full, a king of fierce countenance, and understanding dark sentences, shall stand up.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/daniel/8#v24)</sup> And his power shall be mighty, but not by his own power: and he shall destroy wonderfully, and shall prosper, and practise, and shall destroy the mighty and the holy people.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/daniel/8#v25)</sup> And through his policy also he shall cause craft to prosper in his hand; and he shall magnify himself in his heart, and by peace shall destroy many: he shall also stand up against the Prince of princes; but he shall be broken without hand.

- In the latter time of their kingdom, when the transgressors are come to the full, a king of fierce countenance understanding dark sentences shall stand up, mighty but not by his own power, destroying wonderfully and prospering, and destroying the mighty and the holy people.
- Through his policy he causes craft to prosper in his hand, magnifies himself, and by peace destroys many, standing up against the Prince of princes, but he shall be broken without hand.
- The nation that stands against the Prince of princes is the United States, Babylon the Great. This is how we know Greece, Rome, and America are an extension of the same beast.

**[Daniel 8:26-27](/bible/daniel/8#v26)**
> <sup>[26](/bible/daniel/8#v26)</sup> And the vision of the evening and the morning which was told is true: wherefore shut thou up the vision; for it shall be for many days.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/daniel/8#v27)</sup> And I Daniel fainted, and was sick certain days; afterward I rose up, and did the king’s business; and I was astonished at the vision, but none understood it.

- The vision of the evening and the morning is true, but Daniel was to shut it up, for it is for many days.
- Daniel fainted and was sick certain days, then rose and did the king's business, astonished at the vision, but none understood it. The Most High gives his prophets to understand dark sayings and visions.
