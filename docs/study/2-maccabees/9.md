## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 2 Maccabees 9 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-28, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[2 Maccabees 9:1-3](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v1)</sup> About that time came Antiochus with dishonour out of the country of Persia
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v2)</sup> For he had entered the city called Persepolis, and went about to rob the temple, and to hold the city; whereupon the multitude running to defend themselves with their weapons put them to flight; and so it happened, that Antiochus being put to flight of the inhabitants returned with shame.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v3)</sup> Now when he came to Ecbatane, news was brought him what had happened unto Nicanor and Timotheus.

- The lesson is Trump and the Epiphanes complex. Epiphanes is Greek for manifest; it is supposed to be God manifest, the Glorious, an epithet borne by several Hellenistic rulers. This is the same Antiochus of 1 Maccabees 1.
- “About that time came Antiochus with dishonour out of the country of Persia”: he went to rob the temple at Persepolis, the people put him to flight, and he “returned with shame.”
- “About that time came Antiochus with dishonour out of the country of Persia”. He went to rob the temple at Persepolis and was put to flight and “returned with shame.” Then at Ecbatane “news was brought him what had happened unto Nicanor and Timotheus”: Antiochus hears that God brought forth the destruction of Nicanor.
- Cyrus the Persian humbled himself: he let all Israel go back to where they came from, paid for everything, let nobody touch them, and told Ezra to let him know if he needed anything, so God let him rule. But for the others that had pride, here come the Greeks. “About that time came Antiochus with dishonour out of the country of Persia”.

**[2 Maccabees 9:4](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v4)</sup> Then swelling with anger, he thought to avenge upon the Jews the disgrace done unto him by those that made him flee. Therefore commanded he his chariot man to drive without ceasing, and to dispatch the journey, the judgment of God now following him. For he had spoken proudly in this sort, That he would come to Jerusalem, and make it a common buryingplace of the Jews.

- “He thought to avenge upon the Jews the disgrace done unto him by those that made him flee.” He had to run from the other nations, so he said I am going to take it out on Israel. “That he would come to Jerusalem, and make it a common buryingplace of the Jews”: this devil swore he would destroy Jerusalem. That is how evil he was.
- “He thought to avenge upon the Jews the disgrace done unto him by those that made him flee.” He was mad at the other country that defeated him, so he said, I’m taking it out on the Jews.
- “For he had spoken proudly in this sort, That he would come to Jerusalem, and make it a common buryingplace of the Jews.” Another one running his mouth: I’m going to turn Jerusalem into a graveyard.
- “The judgment of God now following him. For he had spoken proudly in this sort, That he would come to Jerusalem, and make it a common buryingplace of the Jews.” He had the same thought the king of Assyria did, talking proudly against God’s kingdom.

**[2 Maccabees 9:5-6](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v5)</sup> But the Lord Almighty, the God of Israel, smote him with an incurable and invisible plague: for as soon as he had spoken these words, a pain of the bowels that was remediless came upon him, and sore torments of the inner parts;
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v6)</sup> And that most justly: for he had tormented other men’s bowels with many and strange torments.

- “But the Lord Almighty” struck him “with an incurable and invisible plague”, “a pain of the bowels that was remediless”: inside his guts he was getting jacked up. “And that most justly: for he had tormented other men’s bowels with many and strange torments.” He used to torture Israelites, so God said I am going to torture this devil.
- The Lord Almighty “smote him with an incurable and invisible plague”, a plague inside his body you could not see: “as soon as he had spoken these words, a pain of the bowels that was remediless came upon him, and sore torments of the inner parts”. As soon as he spoke those proud words, straight diarrhoea, instantly. “And that most justly: for he had tormented other men’s bowels with many and strange” torments. You know how those Greeks got down, and the writer is saying it: God said, I have something for you, payback as soon as you speak proudly.
- “But the Lord Almighty”, the God of Israel, “smote him with an incurable and invisible plague”. Here is a man riding in his chariot, I’m going to get them, and all of a sudden, stop the chariot, what is this? It was not something visible he could see; he just started feeling pain. That is why you get nervous when things start hurting out of nowhere.
- “As soon as he had spoken these words”: God was listening to his words. In each of these instances your mouth brought the destruction on you. That is why you have to watch people who don’t know how to shut up; you keep telling them over and over, stop running your mouth, and they can’t listen. There is a God that is listening.

**[2 Maccabees 9:7-8](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v7)</sup> Howbeit he nothing at all ceased from his bragging, but still was filled with pride, breathing out fire in his rage against the Jews, and commanding to haste the journey: but it came to pass that he fell down from his chariot, carried violently; so that having a sore fall, all the members of his body were much pained.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v8)</sup> And thus he that a little afore thought he might command the waves of the sea, (so proud was he beyond the condition of man) and weigh the high mountains in a balance, was now cast on the ground, and carried in an horselitter, shewing forth unto all the manifest power of God.

- “Howbeit he nothing at all ceased from his bragging”: he always bragged, which is why he took the name Epiphanes, God manifest on earth. “But still was filled with pride, breathing out fire in his rage against the Jews.”
- “He that a little afore thought he might command the waves of the sea”: that is how prideful he was, he thought he could command the ocean. Now he was cast on the ground, “shewing forth unto all the manifest power of God.”
- “Howbeit he nothing at all ceased from his bragging”: he kept running his mouth while he was in pain. Some people are just that stupid; everything is going wrong for them and they are still running their mouth and not listening. “But still was filled with pride, breathing out fire in his rage against the Jews”: still saying what he is going to do to God’s chosen people while everything goes wrong around him.
- “And commanding to haste the journey”: move faster. “But it came to pass that he fell down from his chariot, carried violently; so that having a sore fall, all the members of his body were much pained.” Imagine a whole army moving and someone shouting, the king just fell, everybody stop.

**[2 Maccabees 9:9-10](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v9)</sup> So that the worms rose up out of the body of this wicked man, and whiles he lived in sorrow and pain, his flesh fell away, and the filthiness of his smell was noisome to all his army.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v10)</sup> And the man, that thought a little afore he could reach to the stars of heaven, no man could endure to carry for his intolerable stink.

- “So that the worms rose up out of the body of this wicked man”: he had worms coming out of his body, his flesh fell away, and “no man could endure to carry for his intolerable stink.” What kind of disease is that, when you stink so bad people cannot be near you?
- “So that the worms rose up out of the body of this wicked man”: now he had worms coming out of him. “His flesh fell away”: his skin started to rot. “And the filthiness of his smell was noisome to all his army.” Everybody in the army: what is that smell? Can somebody go wash him up?
- “And the man, that thought a little afore he could reach to the stars of heaven”: he had that much pride. “No man could endure to carry for his intolerable stink.” Nobody wanted to be around him. Imagine God doing that to you: nobody can come and help you or bring you food because of how you smell. That is dishonorable.

**[2 Maccabees 9:11-12](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v11)</sup> Here therefore, being plagued, he began to leave off his great pride, and to come to the knowledge of himself by the scourge of God, his pain increasing every moment.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v12)</sup> And when he himself could not abide his own smell, he said these words, It is meet to be subject unto God, and that a man that is mortal should not proudly think of himself, as if he were God.

- “Being plagued, he began to leave off his great pride”: see what judgment will do; the scourge of God puts you back in your right frame of mind. “When he himself could not abide his own smell”: you know you stink when you cannot stand your own smell.
- Then, as evil as he is, this white man says “It is meet to be subject unto God, and that a man that is mortal should not proudly think of himself, as if he were God.” Now he is starting to get it.
- “Here therefore, being plagued, he began to leave off his great pride, and to come to the knowledge of himself by the scourge of God, his pain increasing every moment.” Every moment it got worse and worse. Don’t play with God.
- “And when he himself could not abide his own smell”: it’s you, king, you’re the one that smells. “He said these words, It is meet to be subject unto God”. Now he is humbled. You see how people are? When that pain hits just right, because God knows every person. Some people get touched once and straighten up; they are not doing nothing wrong no more, and they are the first one here every Sabbath, because they got touched once. Some of you, God has to bring this low so you can know where you need to go.

**[2 Maccabees 9:13-17](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v13)</sup> This wicked person vowed also unto the Lord, who now no more would have mercy upon him, saying thus,
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v14)</sup> That the holy city (to the which he was going in haste, to lay it even with the ground, and to make it a common buryingplace,) he would set at liberty:
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v15)</sup> And as touching the Jews, whom he had judged not worthy so much as to be buried, but to be cast out with their children to be devoured of the fowls and wild beasts, he would make them all equals to the citizens of Athens:
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v16)</sup> And the holy temple, which before he had spoiled, he would garnish with goodly gifts, and restore all the holy vessels with many more, and out of his own revenue defray the charges belonging to the sacrifices:
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v17)</sup> Yea, and that also he would become a Jew himself, and go through all the world that was inhabited, and declare the power of God.

- “This wicked person vowed also unto the Lord, who now no more would have mercy upon him.” He vowed that the holy city he was hurrying to lay even with the ground “he would set at liberty.”
- “This wicked person vowed also unto the Lord, who now no more would have mercy upon him”. He was past mercy. He had a chance before, but now God said, no mercy for you, and now he is making vows.
- The holy city “he would set at liberty”: he says, I’ll let all the Israelites go, just take this away from me. “And as touching the Jews, whom he had judged not worthy so much as to be buried, but to be cast out with their children to be devoured of the fowls and wild beasts”: he had said, don’t even bury the Jews, leave them in the street to rot. That is why God is letting him rot and stink the way a dead person does. He wanted the Jews to rot, so God let him rot. He is already dead and doesn’t even know it.
- “Yea, and that also he would become a Jew himself, and go through all the world that was inhabited”. I don’t want to be a white man no more, I’m ready to join the Israelites and tell them about you. That is when the torment touched him: all of a sudden you want to be born again and be God’s friend.

**[2 Maccabees 9:18](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v18)</sup> But for all this his pains would not cease: for the just judgment of God was come upon him: therefore despairing of his health, he wrote unto the Jews the letter underwritten, containing the form of a supplication, after this manner:

- “But for all this his pains would not cease: for the just judgment of God was come upon him.” For all that he promised the Lord, his pain would not go away.
- “But for all this his pains would not cease: for the just judgment of God was come upon him”. God said, no, you are going to pay for this one. You were running your mouth; now you are going to learn what God is about. “He wrote unto the Jews the letter underwritten, containing the form of a supplication”: now he is asking the Jews for help.

    >
    > <sup>[17](/bible/hebrews/12#v17)</sup> For ye know how that afterward, when he would have inherited the blessing, he was rejected: for he found no place of repentance, though he sought it carefully with tears.

    What Antiochus did, their forefather Esau did. This is the Epiphanes complex. “Lest there be any fornicator, or profane person, as Esau, who for one morsel of meat sold his birthright”: he did not give a damn about his birthright; it was garbage to him.

**[2 Maccabees 9:19-27](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v19)</sup> Antiochus, king and governor, to the good Jews his citizens wisheth much joy, health, and prosperity:
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v20)</sup> If ye and your children fare well, and your affairs be to your contentment, I give very great thanks to God, having my hope in heaven.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v21)</sup> As for me, I was weak, or else I would have remembered kindly your honour and good will. Returning out of Persia, and being taken with a grievous disease, I thought it necessary to care for the common safety of all:
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v22)</sup> Not distrusting mine health, but having great hope to escape this sickness.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v23)</sup> But considering that even my father, at what time he led an army into the high countries, appointed a successor,
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v24)</sup> To the end that, if any thing fell out contrary to expectation, or if any tidings were brought that were grievous, they of the land, knowing to whom the state was left, might not be troubled:
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v25)</sup> Again, considering how that the princes that are borderers and neighbours unto my kingdom wait for opportunities, and expect what shall be the event, I have appointed my son Antiochus king, whom I often committed and commended unto many of you, when I went up into the high provinces; to whom I have written as followeth:
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v26)</sup> Therefore I pray and request you to remember the benefits that I have done unto you generally, and in special, and that every man will be still faithful to me and my son.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v27)</sup> For I am persuaded that he understanding my mind will favourably and graciously yield to your desires.

- He wrote “to the good Jews his citizens”, wishing them all the blessings in the world, saying he had his hope in heaven, and asking them to be faithful to him and to his son Antiochus, whom he appointed king. If the daddy is a devil, the son is a devil too.
- “Antiochus, king and governor, to the good Jews his citizens wisheth much joy, health, and prosperity”. Now you are good Jews. Look how his voice just changed; it is all sweet talk now. “As for me, I was weak, or else I would have remembered kindly your honour and good will”: if God hadn’t done this to me, I would have been nice to you; I didn’t want to hurt you. “And being taken with a grievous disease, I thought it necessary to care for the common safety of all”: I care about you guys.
- “But considering that even my father, at what time he led an army into the high countries”, appointed a successor, “I have appointed my son Antiochus king”. He is blaming his father for everything: look, I didn’t want to do this.
- “Therefore I pray and request you to remember the benefits that I have done unto you”: now he says, remember I was nice to you. The man was just coming to kill them, and he is blaming his daddy. “And that every man will be still faithful to me and my son.”

**[2 Maccabees 9:28](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/2-maccabees/9#v28)</sup> Thus the murderer and blasphemer having suffered most grievously, as he entreated other men, so died he a miserable death in a strange country in the mountains.

- “Thus the murderer and blasphemer having suffered most grievously, as he entreated other men, so died he a miserable death in a strange country in the mountains.”
- Antiochus Epiphanes was such an evil man that he waited until God plagued him at the end of his life to say I want to convert and preach the gospel, and the Lord said no. That is the Epiphanes complex the white man has. Black people have been trying to change this white man for centuries, all the way back to Rome and Greece, and he has never changed. He has never dealt favorably toward us, ever. Every few years a Negro says I can change him. God said no: God is not going to change the white man; he is going to kill him.
- “Thus the murderer and blasphemer having suffered most grievously, as he entreated other men, so died he a miserable death in a strange country in the mountains.” That was beautiful. He died a miserable death in a strange country while he was begging God. All praises to the Most High God of Israel.
