## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 2 Maccabees 3 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-22, 24-34, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[2 Maccabees 3:1](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v1)</sup> Now when the holy city was inhabited with all peace, and the laws were kept very well, because of the godliness of Onias the high priest, and his hatred of wickedness,

- Jerusalem was “inhabited with all peace, and the laws were kept very well, because of the godliness of Onias the high priest, and his hatred of wickedness”. This is the same Onias read about at the beginning.
- A man’s faith helps other people’s faith. Because of Onias’s faith, his strength and his love for God’s laws, everybody else was inspired, and the laws were kept very well. And his hatred of wickedness is the mentality we all must have.

  Precepts:
  - **[Psalms 97:10](/bible/psalms/97#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/psalms/97#v10)</sup> Ye that love the Lord, hate evil: he preserveth the souls of his saints; he delivereth them out of the hand of the wicked.

    “Ye that love the Lord, hate evil: he preserveth the souls of his saints; he delivereth them out of the hand of the wicked.” Onias hated wickedness, and that is the mentality we all must have. For the Lord to deliver us out of the hand of the wicked, we must begin to train ourselves to hate evil.
  - **[Psalms 101:3](/bible/psalms/101#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/101#v3)</sup> I will set no wicked thing before mine eyes: I hate the work of them that turn aside; it shall not cleave to me.

    “I will set no wicked thing before mine eyes: I hate the work of them that turn aside; it shall not cleave to me.”
  - **[Luke 9:62](/bible/luke/9#v62)**
    > <sup>[62](/bible/luke/9#v62)</sup> And Jesus said unto him, No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God.

    Christ said: “No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God.” You put your hand to the plough to do the work. Then you get so frustrated with brothers always correcting you, because you’re doing stupid stuff, that you separate yourself to do your own thing.

**[2 Maccabees 3:2-3](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v2)</sup> It came to pass that even the kings themselves did honour the place, and magnify the temple with their best gifts;
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v3)</sup> Insomuch that Seleucus of Asia of his own revenues bare all the costs belonging to the service of the sacrifices.

- Even the kings honoured the place and gave the temple their best gifts because of this man: “Seleucus of Asia of his own revenues bare all the costs belonging to the service of the sacrifices.”
- Alexander had four generals: Lysimachus over Thrace and Asia Minor, Cassander over Macedonia and Greece, Ptolemy over Egypt, and Seleucus over Babylon and Syria. This king came out of Seleucus’s line.

**[2 Maccabees 3:4](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v4)</sup> But one Simon of the tribe of Benjamin, who was made governor of the temple, fell out with the high priest about disorder in the city.

- “One Simon of the tribe of Benjamin, who was made governor of the temple, fell out with the high priest about disorder in the city.” When he could not overcome Onias, who had a good name, he went to the enemy and snitched, telling him about the money in the treasury, just because he couldn’t have his way. That is an effeminate man. He didn’t even go at Onias one-on-one; he did something to the detriment of the whole nation: if I can’t have it, I’ll burn it all down. We live these scriptures: men who are mad will rip the whole thing apart and not care about anybody else’s children or family.
- “But one Simon of the tribe of Benjamin, who was made governor of the temple, fell out with the high priest about disorder in the city.” Highlight this man’s name and his tribe.
- The governor of the temple managed the temple’s resources, making sure the offerings and donations were accounted for and used for their intended purposes; he saw to the upkeep of the temple, helped order the worship and the scheduling of the sacrifices, handled disputes among the priests and worshippers, worked alongside the high priest, and kept the temple grounds secure. But the high priest was over him regarding the temple. Onias and Simon were working together over the temple, and they had a falling out.

**[2 Maccabees 3:5](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v5)</sup> And when he could not overcome Onias, he gat him to Apollonius the son of Thraseas, who then was governor of Celosyria and Phenice,

- “And when he could not overcome Onias”: whatever Onias said as high priest, it stood, and Simon did not agree with it. So what did he do? He “gat him to Apollonius the son of Thraseas, who then was governor of Celosyria and Phenice”. He ran to the other nations against his own high priest. You can’t make this stuff up.
- Jerusalem is down in Palestine; Celosyria is up by Damascus. That is where Simon of the tribe of Benjamin went, to deal with the other nations dwelling there.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 37:1-4](/bible/sirach/37#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/37#v1)</sup> Every friend saith, I am his friend also: but there is a friend, which is only a friend in name.
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/37#v2)</sup> Is it not a grief unto death, when a companion and friend is turned to an enemy?
    >
    > <sup>[3](/bible/sirach/37#v3)</sup> O wicked imagination, whence camest thou in to cover the earth with deceit?
    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/sirach/37#v4)</sup> There is a companion, which rejoiceth in the prosperity of a friend, but in the time of trouble will be against him.

    “Every friend saith, I am his friend also: but there is a friend, which is only a friend in name. Is it not a grief unto death, when a companion and friend is turned to an enemy? O wicked imagination, whence camest thou in to cover the earth with deceit? There is a companion, which rejoiceth in the prosperity of a friend, but in the time of trouble will be against him.”

**[2 Maccabees 3:6](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v6)</sup> And told him that the treasury in Jerusalem was full of infinite sums of money, so that the multitude of their riches, which did not pertain to the account of the sacrifices, was innumerable, and that it was possible to bring all into the king’s hand.

- He was so mad at Onias that he went and told the other nations “that the treasury in Jerusalem was full of infinite sums of money, so that the multitude of their riches, which did not pertain to the account of the sacrifices, was innumerable, and that it was possible to bring all into the king’s hand.” You don’t want to do what I say? I’m going to tell the other nations about all the money in the temple.
- The riches that did not pertain to the sacrifices were the tithes: the first tithe for the Levites, because the Levites had no inheritance in the land and served full time in the temple; a tithe of that tithe for the priests; and every third year a store laid up to feed the Levites, the widows, the orphans and the strangers. Those strangers were first and foremost Israelites of another tribe, a man of Judah or of Issachar who was travelling. In certain times money was also set aside to repair and maintain the temple. That is the money Simon told the nations about.

  Precepts:
  - **[Deuteronomy 14:28-29](/bible/deuteronomy/14#v28)**
    > <sup>[28](/bible/deuteronomy/14#v28)</sup> At the end of three years thou shalt bring forth all the tithe of thine increase the same year, and shalt lay it up within thy gates:
    >
    > <sup>[29](/bible/deuteronomy/14#v29)</sup> And the Levite, (because he hath no part nor inheritance with thee,) and the stranger, and the fatherless, and the widow, which are within thy gates, shall come, and shall eat and be satisfied; that the Lord thy God may bless thee in all the work of thine hand which thou doest.

    This is what the money in the temple was for: “At the end of three years thou shalt bring forth all the tithe of thine increase the same year, and shalt lay it up within thy gates: And the Levite, (because he hath no part nor inheritance with thee,) and the stranger, and the fatherless, and the widow, which are within thy gates, shall come, and shall eat and be satisfied; that the Lord thy God may bless thee in all the work of thine hand which thou doest.”
  - **[Tobit 1:3-8](/bible/tobit/1#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/tobit/1#v3)</sup> I Tobit have walked all the days of my life in the way of truth and justice, and I did many almsdeeds to my brethren, and my nation, who came with me to Nineve, into the land of the Assyrians.
    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/tobit/1#v4)</sup> And when I was in mine own country, in the land of Israel, being but young, all the tribe of Nephthali my father fell from the house of Jerusalem, which was chosen out of all the tribes of Israel, that all the tribes should sacrifice there, where the temple of the habitation of the most High was consecrated and built for all ages.
    >
    > <sup>[5](/bible/tobit/1#v5)</sup> Now all the tribes which together revolted, and the house of my father Nephthali, sacrificed unto the heifer Baal.
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/tobit/1#v6)</sup> But I alone went often to Jerusalem at the feasts, as it was ordained unto all the people of Israel by an everlasting decree, having the firstfruits and tenths of increase, with that which was first shorn; and them gave I at the altar to the priests the children of Aaron.
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/tobit/1#v7)</sup> The first tenth part of all increase I gave to the sons of Aaron, who ministered at Jerusalem: another tenth part I sold away, and went, and spent it every year at Jerusalem:
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/tobit/1#v8)</sup> And the third I gave unto them to whom it was meet, as Debora my father’s mother had commanded me, because I was left an orphan by my father.

    Tobit says, “I did many almsdeeds to my brethren”. He was of the northern kingdom, carried into the Assyrian captivity at Nineve. “All the tribe of Nephthali my father fell from the house of Jerusalem”: they went against God’s laws, and “sacrificed unto the heifer Baal.”
  - **[Baruch 3:8](/bible/baruch/3#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/baruch/3#v8)</sup> Behold, we are yet this day in our captivity, where thou hast scattered us, for a reproach and a curse, and to be subject to payments, according to all the iniquities of our fathers, which departed from the Lord our God.

    “Behold, we are yet this day in our captivity, where thou hast scattered us, for a reproach and a curse, and to be subject to payments, according to all the iniquities of our fathers, which departed from the Lord our God.”

**[2 Maccabees 3:7](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v7)</sup> Now when Apollonius came to the king, and had shewed him of the money whereof he was told, the king chose out Heliodorus his treasurer, and sent him with a commandment to bring him the foresaid money.

- Simon told this Edomite, Apollonius, and Apollonius went to the king. The king sent “Heliodorus his treasurer” with “a commandment to bring him the foresaid money.” Bring me all that money.

**[2 Maccabees 3:8](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v8)</sup> So forthwith Heliodorus took his journey, under a colour of visiting the cities of Celosyria and Phenice, but indeed to fulfil the king’s purpose.

- Heliodorus went “under a colour of visiting the cities of Celosyria and Phenice, but indeed to fulfil the king’s purpose.” That is what he let everybody think he was doing; it was really to take all the money from Jerusalem.

**[2 Maccabees 3:9](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v9)</sup> And when he was come to Jerusalem, and had been courteously received of the high priest of the city, he told him what intelligence was given of the money, and declared wherefore he came, and asked if these things were so indeed.

- He was “courteously received of the high priest”, told him what he had heard, “and asked if these things were so indeed.” Do you have money laid up for the widows, the orphans, the fatherless, the strangers and the Levites?

**[2 Maccabees 3:10](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v10)</sup> Then the high priest told him that there was such money laid up for the relief of widows and fatherless children:

- “Then the high priest told him that there was such money laid up for the relief of widows and fatherless children”. The high priest is Onias III, and he says yes, there is money for the widows and the fatherless. The temple had money in it for them.

**[2 Maccabees 3:11](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v11)</sup> And that some of it belonged to Hircanus son of Tobias, a man of great dignity, and not as that wicked Simon had misinformed: the sum whereof in all was four hundred talents of silver, and two hundred of gold:

- Onias told Heliodorus that some of the money belonged to Hircanus, “and not as that wicked Simon had misinformed”: that wicked Simon of the tribe of Benjamin. “The sum whereof in all was four hundred talents of silver, and two hundred of gold”.

**[2 Maccabees 3:12](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v12)</sup> And that it was altogether impossible that such wrongs should be done unto them, that had committed it to the holiness of the place, and to the majesty and inviolable sanctity of the temple, honoured over all the world.

- The class read these precepts with it.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 34:21-22](/bible/sirach/34#v21)**
    > <sup>[21](/bible/sirach/34#v21)</sup> The bread of the needy is their life: he that defraudeth him thereof is a man of blood.
    >
    > <sup>[22](/bible/sirach/34#v22)</sup> He that taketh away his neighbour’s living slayeth him; and he that defraudeth the labourer of his hire is a bloodshedder.

    The scripture that says keeping a man’s living from him is murder: “The bread of the needy is their life: he that defraudeth him thereof is a man of blood. He that taketh away his neighbour’s living slayeth him; and he that defraudeth the labourer of his hire is a bloodshedder.”

**[2 Maccabees 3:13-14](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v13)</sup> But Heliodorus, because of the king’s commandment given him, said, That in any wise it must be brought into the king’s treasury.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v14)</sup> So at the day which he appointed he entered in to order this matter: wherefore there was no small agony throughout the whole city.

- But Heliodorus, “because of the king’s commandment”, said it must be brought into the king’s treasury, and “there was no small agony throughout the whole city.”

**[2 Maccabees 3:15](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v15)</sup> But the priests, prostrating themselves before the altar in their priests’ vestments, called unto heaven upon him that made a law concerning things given to be kept, that they should safely be preserved for such as had committed them to be kept.

- “But the priests, prostrating themselves before the altar in their priests’ vestments, called unto heaven”. Imagine the scene: the priests laid flat with their faces to the ground.

**[2 Maccabees 3:16-17](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v16)</sup> Then whoso had looked the high priest in the face, it would have wounded his heart: for his countenance and the changing of his colour declared the inward agony of his mind.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v17)</sup> For the man was so compassed with fear and horror of the body, that it was manifest to them that looked upon him, what sorrow he had now in his heart.

- Whoever looked the high priest in the face saw “the inward agony of his mind”: Onias III was in agony. Why? Because he knew that their taking that money would affect a lot of our people in Jerusalem, a lot of the widows and the orphans.

**[2 Maccabees 3:18-22](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v18)</sup> Others ran flocking out of their houses to the general supplication, because the place was like to come into contempt.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v19)</sup> And the women, girt with sackcloth under their breasts, abounded in the streets, and the virgins that were kept in ran, some to the gates, and some to the walls, and others looked out of the windows.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v20)</sup> And all, holding their hands toward heaven, made supplication.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v21)</sup> Then it would have pitied a man to see the falling down of the multitude of all sorts, and the fear of the high priest, being in such an agony.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v22)</sup> They then called upon the Almighty Lord to keep the things committed of trust safe and sure for those that had committed them.

- “Others ran flocking out of their houses to the general supplication”; the women “girt with sackcloth”; the virgins ran to the gates, the walls and the windows; “And all, holding their hands toward heaven, made supplication.” They all started praying, and “called upon the Almighty Lord to keep the things committed of trust safe and sure”.

**[2 Maccabees 3:24-26](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v24)</sup> Now as he was there present himself with his guard about the treasury, the Lord of spirits, and the Prince of all power, caused a great apparition, so that all that presumed to come in with him were astonished at the power of God, and fainted, and were sore afraid.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v25)</sup> For there appeared unto them an horse with a terrible rider upon him, and adorned with a very fair covering, and he ran fiercely, and smote at Heliodorus with his forefeet, and it seemed that he that sat upon the horse had complete harness of gold.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v26)</sup> Moreover two other young men appeared before him, notable in strength, excellent in beauty, and comely in apparel, who stood by him on either side, and scourged him continually, and gave him many sore stripes.

- “The Lord of spirits, and the Prince of all power, caused a great apparition”. There appeared “an horse with a terrible rider upon him”, and “he that sat upon the horse had complete harness of gold.” That angel had a complete harness of gold.
- Two other young men “scourged him continually, and gave him many sore stripes.” They were whipping the hell out of this Edomite.
- Two young men “scourged him continually, and gave him many sore stripes”: the angels whipped Heliodorus because he tried to take the funds.

**[2 Maccabees 3:27-30](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v27)</sup> And Heliodorus fell suddenly unto the ground, and was compassed with great darkness: but they that were with him took him up, and put him into a litter.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v28)</sup> Thus him, that lately came with a great train and with all his guard into the said treasury, they carried out, being unable to help himself with his weapons: and manifestly they acknowledged the power of God:
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v29)</sup> For he by the hand of God was cast down, and lay speechless without all hope of life.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v30)</sup> But they praised the Lord, that had miraculously honoured his own place: for the temple, which a little afore was full of fear and trouble, when the Almighty Lord appeared, was filled with joy and gladness.

- Heliodorus “fell suddenly unto the ground, and was compassed with great darkness”, and was carried out, “unable to help himself with his weapons”, so that “manifestly they acknowledged the power of God.” “But they praised the Lord, that had miraculously honoured his own place”.
- “They that were with him took him up, and put him into a litter.”

**[2 Maccabees 3:31](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v31)**

> <sup>[31](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v31)</sup> Then straightways certain of Heliodorus’ friends prayed Onias, that he would call upon the most High to grant him his life, who lay ready to give up the ghost.

- Heliodorus’ friends “prayed Onias”, they begged him, to call upon the most High for the life of the man “who lay ready to give up the ghost.” He was about to die.

**[2 Maccabees 3:32](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v32)**

> <sup>[32](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v32)</sup> So the high priest, suspecting lest the king should misconceive that some treachery had been done to Heliodorus by the Jews, offered a sacrifice for the health of the man.

- The high priest “offered a sacrifice for the health of the man”, praying for him so that the king would not think the Jews had done treachery.
- “So the high priest, suspecting lest the king should misconceive that some treachery had been done to Heliodorus by the Jews, offered a sacrifice for the health of the man.” Onias III thought it through: if the angels kill him, the angels will leave after a while, and the Greeks will come after us.

**[2 Maccabees 3:33-34](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v33)**

> <sup>[33](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v33)</sup> Now as the high priest was making an atonement, the same young men in the same clothing appeared and stood beside Heliodorus, saying, Give Onias the high priest great thanks, insomuch as for his sake the Lord hath granted thee life:
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/2-maccabees/3#v34)</sup> And seeing that thou hast been scourged from heaven, declare unto all men the mighty power of God. And when they had spoken these words, they appeared no more.

- The same young men appeared and said, “Give Onias the high priest great thanks, insomuch as for his sake the Lord hath granted thee life”, and “declare unto all men the mighty power of God.”
