## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 2 Maccabees 7 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-29, 36-37, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[2 Maccabees 7:1-2](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v1)</sup> It came to pass also, that seven brethren with their mother were taken, and compelled by the king against the law to taste swine’s flesh, and were tormented with scourges and whips.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v2)</sup> But one of them that spake first said thus, What wouldest thou ask or learn of us? we are ready to die, rather than to transgress the laws of our fathers.

- These are those that stand stiffly. Seven brethren and their mother were “compelled by the king against the law to taste swine’s flesh.” Now why is it that swine’s flesh is all throughout the black church? And they run to Colossians for it, but that is not what Colossians is talking about.
- “We are ready to die, rather than to transgress the laws of our fathers.” That is standing stiffly, that is getting your crown. Do you know what level of faith that is? You truly cannot desire anything left in this world. We are already captives: bills, working fourteen hours a day. What are you leaving? There has got to be something better.
- Here is some resolve as we lay these things to heart and search out our transgressions. “It came to pass also, that seven brethren with their mother were taken, and compelled by the king against the law to taste swine’s flesh, and were tormented with scourges and whips.” They were tormented by the same cruel, wicked, unforgiving people we have been looking at all class.

**[2 Maccabees 7:3-7](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v3)</sup> Then the king, being in a rage, commanded pans and caldrons to be made hot:
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v4)</sup> Which forthwith being heated, he commanded to cut out the tongue of him that spake first, and to cut off the utmost parts of his body, the rest of his brethren and his mother looking on.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v5)</sup> Now when he was thus maimed in all his members, he commanded him being yet alive to be brought to the fire, and to be fried in the pan: and as the vapour of the pan was for a good space dispersed, they exhorted one another with the mother to die manfully, saying thus,
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v6)</sup> The Lord God looketh upon us, and in truth hath comfort in us, as Moses in his song, which witnessed to their faces, declared, saying, And he shall be comforted in his servants.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v7)</sup> So when the first was dead after this manner, they brought the second to make him a mocking stock: and when they had pulled off the skin of his head with the hair, they asked him, Wilt thou eat, before thou be punished throughout every member of thy body?

- The king in a rage cut out the tongue of the first, cut off the utmost parts of his body, and had him “fried in the pan”, his brethren and mother looking on. They exhorted one another “to die manfully”, saying, “The Lord God looketh upon us, and in truth hath comfort in us.” Then they asked the second, “Wilt thou eat, before thou be punished throughout every member of thy body?” That is the arrogance of the wicked when the earth is given into their hand and they have the power to speak proudly.

**[2 Maccabees 7:8-9](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v8)</sup> But he answered in his own language, and said, No. Wherefore he also received the next torment in order, as the former did.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v9)</sup> And when he was at the last gasp, he said, Thou like a fury takest us out of this present life, but the King of the world shall raise us up, who have died for his laws, unto everlasting life.

- “But he answered in his own language, and said, No.” At the last gasp he said, “the King of the world shall raise us up, who have died for his laws, unto everlasting life.”

**[2 Maccabees 7:10-11](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v10)</sup> After him was the third made a mocking stock: and when he was required, he put out his tongue, and that right soon, holding forth his hands manfully,
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v11)</sup> And said courageously, These I had from heaven; and for his laws I despise them; and from him I hope to receive them again.

- The third “put out his tongue, and that right soon, holding forth his hands manfully.” He just put his hands out: let’s get this over with. “These I had from heaven; and for his laws I despise them; and from him I hope to receive them again.”

**[2 Maccabees 7:12](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v12)</sup> Insomuch that the king, and they that were with him, marvelled at the young man’s courage, for that he nothing regarded the pains.

- “Insomuch that the king, and they that were with him, marvelled at the young man’s courage, for that he nothing regarded the pains.” They marvelled because there was a certain spirit in them. There was something they knew that the heathen did not: that there is only one living God.

  Precepts:
  - **[Psalms 96:5](/bible/psalms/96#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/96#v5)</sup> For all the gods of the nations are idols: but the Lord made the heavens.

    Why did they marvel? “For all the gods of the nations are idols: but the Lord made the heavens.” All the gods of the nations are idols, so the nations have no living God. That is why the young man’s courage was a marvel to them.
  - **[1 Corinthians 8:5-6](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v5)</sup> For though there be that are called gods, whether in heaven or in earth, (as there be gods many, and lords many,)
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/1-corinthians/8#v6)</sup> But to us there is but one God, the Father, of whom are all things, and we in him; and one Lord Jesus Christ, by whom are all things, and we by him.

    “For though there be that are called gods, whether in heaven or in earth, (as there be gods many, and lords many,) But to us there is but one God, the Father, of whom are all things, and we in him; and one Lord Jesus Christ, by whom are all things, and we by him.”

**[2 Maccabees 7:13-14](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v13)</sup> Now when this man was dead also, they tormented and mangled the fourth in like manner.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v14)</sup> So when he was ready to die he said thus, It is good, being put to death by men, to look for hope from God to be raised up again by him: as for thee, thou shalt have no resurrection to life.

- The fourth said, “It is good, being put to death by men, to look for hope from God to be raised up again by him: as for thee, thou shalt have no resurrection to life.” Imagine the look on his face. Before they tortured him he had a message: there is no resurrection for you, no hope for you.
- There is a difference. The Most High deals with the twelve tribes of Israel; he made a covenant with our forefathers Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and we must understand this in these last days.
- “It is good, being put to death by men, to look for hope from God to be raised up again by him.” It is good that this mortal body is slain; we look for a resurrection from the Almighty, to be raised up again.
- “As for thee, thou shalt have no resurrection to life.” As for the heathen, the fake Jews, the Iranians, all these other nations: there is no resurrection for them. That is the difference between the twelve tribes of Israel and the other nations.

  Precepts:
  - **[Wisdom of Solomon 18:7](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/18#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/18#v7)</sup> So of thy people was accepted both the salvation of the righteous, and destruction of the enemies.

    This has to be our mindset concerning salvation and gathering with our people. “So of thy people was accepted both the salvation of the righteous, and destruction of the enemies”: the salvation of the righteous, and the destruction of those that are against us.

**[2 Maccabees 7:15-17](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v15)</sup> Afterward they brought the fifth also, and mangled him.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v16)</sup> Then looked he unto the king, and said, Thou hast power over men, thou art corruptible, thou doest what thou wilt; yet think not that our nation is forsaken of God;
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v17)</sup> But abide a while, and behold his great power, how he will torment thee and thy seed.

- The fifth said, “Thou hast power over men, thou art corruptible, thou doest what thou wilt; yet think not that our nation is forsaken of God.” Because he said, I will never leave thee nor forsake thee. “But abide a while, and behold his great power, how he will torment thee and thy seed.”
- “But abide a while, and behold his great power, how he will torment thee and thy seed.” Wait a little while and you will see God’s great power: he will torment Antiochus, the Greeks, and his seed. The seed is his descendants.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 23:29-32](/bible/matthew/23#v29)**
    > <sup>[29](/bible/matthew/23#v29)</sup> Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! because ye build the tombs of the prophets, and garnish the sepulchres of the righteous,
    >
    > <sup>[30](/bible/matthew/23#v30)</sup> And say, If we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partakers with them in the blood of the prophets.
    >
    > <sup>[31](/bible/matthew/23#v31)</sup> Wherefore ye be witnesses unto yourselves, that ye are the children of them which killed the prophets.
    >
    > <sup>[32](/bible/matthew/23#v32)</sup> Fill ye up then the measure of your fathers.

    “Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! because ye build the tombs of the prophets, and garnish the sepulchres of the righteous, And say, If we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partakers with them in the blood of the prophets.” That is what the Pharisees said, and it is the same thing white people say today: if we had been around back then, we would not have taken part in slavery.

**[2 Maccabees 7:18-19](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v18)</sup> After him also they brought the sixth, who being ready to die said, Be not deceived without cause: for we suffer these things for ourselves, having sinned against our God: therefore marvellous things are done unto us.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v19)</sup> But think not thou, that takest in hand to strive against God, that thou shalt escape unpunished.

- The sixth said, “we suffer these things for ourselves, having sinned against our God.” So we understand why we are going through this. “But think not thou, that takest in hand to strive against God, that thou shalt escape unpunished.” They will not escape unpunished.
- “Be not deceived without cause: for we suffer these things for ourselves, having sinned against our God: therefore marvellous things are done unto us.” The white man was not able to enslave us and bring us here because he was so powerful; it was because we sinned against God. That is why all these things were done unto us.
- “But think not thou, that takest in hand to strive against God, that thou shalt escape unpunished.” Don’t think the white man is going unpunished. Many say forget about slavery, forget the colonization, the rape and the murder; God says you will not go unpunished.
- White people turn around and say, that was my forefathers, I wasn’t there, I didn’t do that to you; some with white guilt, some arrogant: that was the past, get over it. But the judgment is on the seed too.

**[2 Maccabees 7:20-23](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v20)</sup> But the mother was marvellous above all, and worthy of honourable memory: for when she saw her seven sons slain within the space of one day, she bare it with a good courage, because of the hope that she had in the Lord.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v21)</sup> Yea, she exhorted every one of them in her own language, filled with courageous spirits; and stirring up her womanish thoughts with a manly stomach, she said unto them,
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v22)</sup> I cannot tell how ye came into my womb; for I neither gave you breath nor life, neither was it I that formed the members of every one of you;
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v23)</sup> But doubtless the Creator of the world, who formed the generation of man, and found out the beginning of all things, will also of his own mercy give you breath and life again, as ye now regard not your own selves for his laws’ sake.

- The mother saw her seven sons slain in one day and “bare it with a good courage, because of the hope that she had in the Lord.” She said, “I cannot tell how ye came into my womb”, but “the Creator of the world, who formed the generation of man, and found out the beginning of all things, will also of his own mercy give you breath and life again, as ye now regard not your own selves for his laws’ sake.”
- What kind of mercy would it be for the same people who torment us like this to get an equal reward in the kingdom? That is not a merciful God, to allow them to do that to us and then give them the same portion. Somebody needed to hear this, to build up their spirit and their strength.
- “But the mother was marvellous above all, and worthy of honourable memory: for when she saw her seven sons slain within the space of one day, she bare it with a good courage, because of the hope that she had in the Lord.”
- As Jeremiah 15 said, some are for death unto death. That is out there now, and later it will be in here: they are going to want you to transgress the laws of God. Our people transgress willingly because they don’t know any better, hence our fervency to go out and wake them up to the fury of God. Where did they get, God is good all the time? He is good to Israel. Ask the Egyptians in the Red Sea, ask the inhabitants of the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah. We have to endure the punishments, but he promises us mercy. He is not good to these nations, and if he hasn’t judged them yet, know for certain it is coming.

**[2 Maccabees 7:24-25](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v24)</sup> Now Antiochus, thinking himself despised, and suspecting it to be a reproachful speech, whilst the youngest was yet alive, did not only exhort him by words, but also assured him with oaths, that he would make him both a rich and a happy man, if he would turn from the laws of his fathers; and that also he would take him for his friend, and trust him with affairs.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v25)</sup> But when the young man would in no case hearken unto him, the king called his mother, and exhorted her that she would counsel the young man to save his life.

- Antiochus “assured him with oaths, that he would make him both a rich and a happy man, if he would turn from the laws of his fathers; and that also he would take him for his friend, and trust him with affairs.” He tried to bribe him: riches, position, a political seat.
- “But when the young man would in no case hearken unto him, the king called his mother, and exhorted her that she would counsel the young man to save his life.” They have done the same: they reached out to Nolan Wells’ mother and father, and to Trayvon Martin’s mother, and told her, if you don’t tell these people to calm down, they are going to be locked up. Tell them what needs to be said to save them. It is done out there, and it will be done in here, where they will force you to break the commandments of God.

**[2 Maccabees 7:26-27](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v26)</sup> And when he had exhorted her with many words, she promised him that she would counsel her son.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v27)</sup> But she bowing herself toward him, laughing the cruel tyrant to scorn, spake in her country language on this manner; O my son, have pity upon me that bare thee nine months in my womb, and gave thee suck three years, and nourished thee, and brought thee up unto this age, and endured the troubles of education.

- “She promised him that she would counsel her son. But she bowing herself toward him, laughing the cruel tyrant to scorn, spake in her country language on this manner; O my son, have pity upon me that bare thee nine months in my womb”.
- Sisters, that may be your son in that day; brothers, it may be your son. You don’t wake up one day thinking your son is never coming home. When that young man drove to that city for his friend’s party, he didn’t think he wasn’t going to make it home.
- Our foremothers were always about raising their children, being keepers at home and teachers of good things. Here is a married woman who taught her children.
- “But she bowing herself toward him, laughing the cruel tyrant to scorn, spake in her country language on this manner; O my son, have pity upon me that bare thee nine months in my womb”. She “endured the troubles of education.” We think programs are supposed to teach our children and prevent bad behavior. No, it is the morals of God, what we read in Titus 2: teaching the young women to be discreet, chaste, keepers at home, and the young men to be sober minded and like their father. She is instructing these young men to be like their father.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Maccabees 7:10-12](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v10)</sup> After him was the third made a mocking stock: and when he was required, he put out his tongue, and that right soon, holding forth his hands manfully,
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v11)</sup> And said courageously, These I had from heaven; and for his laws I despise them; and from him I hope to receive them again.
    >
    > <sup>[12](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v12)</sup> Insomuch that the king, and they that were with him, marvelled at the young man’s courage, for that he nothing regarded the pains.

    Another example of her children. “After him was the third made a mocking stock: and when he was required, he put out his tongue, and that right soon, holding forth his hands manfully.” He had just seen his brothers killed.
  - **[2 Maccabees 7:20](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v20)**
    > <sup>[20](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v20)</sup> But the mother was marvellous above all, and worthy of honourable memory: for when she saw her seven sons slain within the space of one day, she bare it with a good courage, because of the hope that she had in the Lord.

    “But the mother was marvellous above all, and worthy of honourable memory: for when she saw her seven sons slain within the space of one day, she bare it with a good courage”. Where was that instilled in her? From her husband. And she bare it “because of the hope that she had in the Lord.”

**[2 Maccabees 7:28](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v28)</sup> I beseech thee, my son, look upon the heaven and the earth, and all that is therein, and consider that God made them of things that were not; and so was mankind made likewise.

- “I beseech thee, my son, look upon the heaven and the earth, and all that is therein, and consider that God made them of things that were not; and so was mankind made likewise.”

**[2 Maccabees 7:29](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v29)</sup> Fear not this tormentor, but, being worthy of thy brethren, take thy death, that I may receive thee again in mercy with thy brethren.

- “Fear not this tormentor”: do not fear your enemy, give glory to God. This is how you train up your children. Our people had a sense of that before integration: they knew who the enemy was and were taught to love themselves and their nation.
- “But, being worthy of thy brethren, take thy death”. She told her son to take his death. How many women can say that to their child?

**[2 Maccabees 7:36-37](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v36)**

> <sup>[36](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v36)</sup> For our brethren, who now have suffered a short pain, are dead under God’s covenant of everlasting life: but thou, through the judgment of God, shalt receive just punishment for thy pride.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/2-maccabees/7#v37)</sup> But I, as my brethren, offer up my body and life for the laws of our fathers, beseeching God that he would speedily be merciful unto our nation; and that thou by torments and plagues mayest confess, that he alone is God;

- “For our brethren, who now have suffered a short pain, are dead under God’s covenant of everlasting life: but thou, through the judgment of God, shalt receive just punishment for thy pride.” We have to be so sound in that.
- “But I, as my brethren, offer up my body and life for the laws of our fathers, beseeching God that he would speedily be merciful unto our nation; and that thou by torments and plagues mayest confess, that he alone is God”. That is what we ask: that the Lord be merciful unto our nation.
