## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 2 Maccabees 13 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 3-8, 13-14, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[2 Maccabees 13:3](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v3)</sup> Menelaus also joined himself with them, and with great dissimulation encouraged Antiochus, not for the safeguard of the country, but because he thought to have been made governor.

- What happened to Menelaus, who was wicked as hell, is not found until chapter 13. He “joined himself with them, and with great dissimulation encouraged Antiochus, not for the safeguard of the country, but because he thought to have been made governor.” If I can’t be high priest again, I’ll be governor.

**[2 Maccabees 13:4](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v4)</sup> But the King of kings moved Antiochus’ mind against this wicked wretch, and Lysias informed the king that this man was the cause of all mischief, so that the king commanded to bring him unto Berea, and to put him to death, as the manner is in that place.

- “But the King of kings moved Antiochus’ mind against this wicked wretch”. The Lord moved Antiochus’s mind: one day you’re friends, and the Most High says, snap, that’s not your friend, I want you to hate him. “And Lysias informed the king that this man was the cause of all mischief”. It is all coming out now.

**[2 Maccabees 13:5-8](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v5)</sup> Now there was in that place a tower of fifty cubits high, full of ashes, and it had a round instrument, which on every side hanged down into the ashes.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v6)</sup> And whosoever was condemned of sacrilege, or had committed any other grievous crime, there did all men thrust him unto death.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v7)</sup> Such a death it happened that wicked man to die, not having so much as burial in the earth; and that most justly:
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v8)</sup> For inasmuch as he had committed many sins about the altar, whose fire and ashes were holy, he received his death in ashes.

- At Berea was “a tower of fifty cubits high, full of ashes”, where whoever “was condemned of sacrilege, or had committed any other grievous crime” was thrust in to his death. “Such a death it happened that wicked man to die, not having so much as burial in the earth; and that most justly: For inasmuch as he had committed many sins about the altar, whose fire and ashes were holy, he received his death in ashes.” He got his just reward.

**[2 Maccabees 13:13](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v13)</sup> And Judas, being apart with the elders, determined, before the king’s host should enter into Judea, and get the city, to go forth and try the matter in fight by the help of the Lord.

- If you are not a nationalist you cannot be a revolutionist: you cannot start a revolution if you do not love your nation or do not want a nation. When the Greeks tried to rule over us, Mattathias and his sons rose up and started a revolution. Judas determined “to go forth and try the matter in fight by the help of the Lord”: he was ready to shed blood, and in every revolution there is bloodshed.

**[2 Maccabees 13:14](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/2-maccabees/13#v14)</sup> So when he had committed all to the Creator of the world, and exhorted his soldiers to fight manfully, even unto death, for the laws, the temple, the city, the country, and the commonwealth, he camped by Modin:

- He “exhorted his soldiers to fight manfully, even unto death, for the laws, the temple, the city, the country”. All revolutionaries understand they can die in what they do. Judas Maccabeus and his men were not only revolutionaries but nationalists: they loved their nation and their people. A revolutionary is a nationalist ready to die for his country and his people.
