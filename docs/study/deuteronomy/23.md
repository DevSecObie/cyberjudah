## Introduction

This session covers [Deuteronomy 23](/bible/deuteronomy/23) through 26. Many of these laws should sound familiar, because we have already been through Exodus, Leviticus, and Numbers, and some of these laws are simply being repeated in Deuteronomy. Chapter 23 sets who may enter the congregation of the Lord and requires the camp to be holy because the Most High walks in the midst of it; chapter 24 covers the bill of divorcement, pledges, and mercy toward the poor; chapter 25 covers just judgment, the duty toward a dead brother's wife, and the memorial against Amalek; chapter 26 covers the firstfruits confession and the Most High avouching Israel to be his peculiar people.

**[Deuteronomy 23:1](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v1)</sup> He that is wounded in the stones, or hath his privy member cut off, shall not enter into the congregation of the Lord.

- He that is wounded in the stones or hath his privy member cut off shall not enter into the congregation of the Lord.
- This law repeats what was already given: the priests' law in Leviticus lists the same blemishes that barred a man from approaching to offer the bread of his God, including him that hath his stones broken.

  Precepts:
  - **[Leviticus 21:17-20](/bible/leviticus/21#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/leviticus/21#v17)</sup> Speak unto Aaron, saying, Whosoever he be of thy seed in their generations that hath any blemish, let him not approach to offer the bread of his God.
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/leviticus/21#v18)</sup> For whatsoever man he be that hath a blemish, he shall not approach: a blind man, or a lame, or he that hath a flat nose, or any thing superfluous,
    >
    > <sup>[19](/bible/leviticus/21#v19)</sup> Or a man that is brokenfooted, or brokenhanded,
    >
    > <sup>[20](/bible/leviticus/21#v20)</sup> Or crookbackt, or a dwarf, or that hath a blemish in his eye, or be scurvy, or scabbed, or hath his stones broken;

    Whosoever of Aaron's seed hath any blemish, including him that hath his stones broken, shall not approach to offer the bread of his God.

**[Deuteronomy 23:2](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v2)</sup> A bastard shall not enter into the congregation of the Lord; even to his tenth generation shall he not enter into the congregation of the Lord.

- A bastard shall not enter into the congregation of the Lord, even to his tenth generation: the children begotten of forbidden unions with the nations were shut out of the congregation.
- The prophets tie the bastard to Ashdod: a bastard shall dwell in Ashdod. Ashdod was one of the cities given to the tribe of Judah in the inheritance, but Israel did not drive out all the inhabitants and lost it.
- In the days of Nehemiah, Jews had married wives of Ashdod, of Ammon, and of Moab, and their children spake half in the speech of Ashdod and could not speak in the Jews' language. Why is that? Because the mothers do the raising of the children.

**[Deuteronomy 23:7-8](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v7)</sup> Thou shalt not abhor an Edomite; for he is thy brother: thou shalt not abhor an Egyptian; because thou wast a stranger in his land.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v8)</sup> The children that are begotten of them shall enter into the congregation of the Lord in their third generation.

- Thou shalt not abhor an Edomite, for he is thy brother: this was a temporary law. Edom broke the brotherly covenant; he did pursue his brother with the sword, did cast off all pity, his anger did tear perpetually, and he kept his wrath for ever.
- Edom's hatred is a perpetual hatred: he shed the blood of the children of Israel by the force of the sword in the time of their calamity, and as we see our people being slaughtered by Esau, that anger continues even to this very day.
- Thou shalt not abhor an Egyptian, because thou wast a stranger in his land: many brothers and sisters do not want to deal with that part and only want to deal with Edom, but Egypt likewise came under judgment for the violence against the children of Judah, because they shed innocent blood in their land.

**[Deuteronomy 23:9](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v9)</sup> When the host goeth forth against thine enemies, then keep thee from every wicked thing.

- The example is Achan: Israel was warned in any wise to keep from the accursed thing, lest they make the camp of Israel a curse and trouble it, and Achan the son of Carmi of the tribe of Judah took of the accursed thing, the Babylonish garment, and the anger of the Lord was kindled against the children of Israel.
- Because of that trespass Israel could not stand before their enemies but turned their backs, for they were accursed; the Lord would not be with them any more except they destroyed the accursed from among them, and Achan and his family and all that belonged to him were destroyed and burned, and a heap set upon them, because he broke the covenant.

>

- There was a place without the camp to go forth abroad, and a paddle upon the weapon to dig and cover that which cometh from thee: to make it plain, if you had to ease yourself, you went outside the camp to do it.
- Verse 14 explains why: the Lord thy God walketh in the midst of thy camp, to deliver thee and to give up thine enemies before thee; therefore shall thy camp be holy, that he see no unclean thing in thee and turn away from thee.
- That is what we just read happened with Achan the son of Carmi: he brought an unclean thing into the camp, and the Most High turned from Israel in battle.
- God says he is in the midst of Israel and none else; the Lord would go before us and fight our battles, and that is why he wanted no unclean thing in the camp, for we are holy and the camp was to be holy.

**[Deuteronomy 23:19-20](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/deuteronomy/23#v19)</sup> Thou shalt not lend upon usury to thy brother; usury of money, usury of victuals, usury of any thing that is lent upon usury:
>

- Thou shalt not lend upon usury to thy brother, usury of money, of victuals, of any thing: when you read the definition of usury, that is interest. We are not to charge interest to each other when we loan each other anything.
- Unto a stranger thou mayest lend upon usury: the blessing is that Israel shall lend unto many nations and shall not borrow, the head and not the tail. That is our rightful place on the earth when we keep these commandments: we should lend unto them and may take usury upon what we lend to them, but we should not do that to our own people.
- The same law was already read in Exodus: if thou lend money to any of my people that is poor by thee, thou shalt not be to him as an usurer.
