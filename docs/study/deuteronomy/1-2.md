## Introduction

This session covered Numbers 35-36 together with Deuteronomy 1-2; the Numbers chapters sit in their own chapter set. Deuteronomy means the second giving of the law: the book is a great recapping and reiterating of what already took place, primarily in the book of Numbers, and the audience of the whole book is the Israelites. Chapter 1 retells the order set among the judges and the rebellion at Kadesh-barnea; chapter 2 retells the passage by Esau, Moab, and Ammon, the giants that fell before them, and the destruction of Sihon king of the Amorites.

> <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v1)</sup> These be the words which Moses spake unto all Israel on this side Jordan in the wilderness, in the plain over against the Red sea, between Paran, and Tophel, and Laban, and Hazeroth, and Dizahab.

>
> <sup>[3](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v3)</sup> And it came to pass in the fortieth year, in the eleventh month, on the first day of the month, that Moses spake unto the children of Israel, according unto all that the Lord had given him in commandment unto them;

- These be the words which Moses spake unto all Israel on this side Jordan in the wilderness: the audience of the entire book of Deuteronomy is the Israelites.
- Moses spake in the fortieth year, in the eleventh month, on the first day of the month, according unto all that the Lord had given him in commandment unto them.

**[Deuteronomy 1:4](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v4)</sup> After he had slain Sihon the king of the Amorites, which dwelt in Heshbon, and Og the king of Bashan, which dwelt at Astaroth in Edrei:

- Moses declares this law after he had slain Sihon the king of the Amorites. When the Lord told Abraham his seed would serve four hundred years, he set the time of the return by the Amorites: the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.
- By this point their iniquity was fulfilled, like Babylon whose sins reached unto heaven, so the Lord loosed Israel to come through the land of the Amorites and slay Sihon their king.

  Precepts:
  - **[Genesis 15:16](/bible/genesis/15#v16)**

    The fourth generation would come hither again, for the iniquity of the Amorites was not yet full; Sihon fell when it was full.

**[Deuteronomy 1:5-8](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v5)</sup> On this side Jordan, in the land of Moab, began Moses to declare this law, saying,
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v6)</sup> The Lord our God spake unto us in Horeb, saying, Ye have dwelt long enough in this mount:
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v7)</sup> Turn you, and take your journey, and go to the mount of the Amorites, and unto all the places nigh thereunto, in the plain, in the hills, and in the vale, and in the south, and by the sea side, to the land of the Canaanites, and unto Lebanon, unto the great river, the river Euphrates.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v8)</sup> Behold, I have set the land before you: go in and possess the land which the Lord sware unto your fathers, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, to give unto them and to their seed after them.

- Ye have dwelt long enough in this mount: the Lord set the land before them, from the mount of the Amorites to Lebanon and the great river Euphrates, to go in and possess it.
- The land was sworn unto Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob and to their seed after them; the Most High reiterated that covenant to the fathers over and over through Genesis, reassuring them that their seed would possess the land of Canaan.
- Israel entered not for their own righteousness but for the sake of the promise made to the fathers; the rebellious generation was cut off, yet their children were still brought in because the Most High sware it.

**[Deuteronomy 1:9-12](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v9)</sup> And I spake unto you at that time, saying, I am not able to bear you myself alone:
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v10)</sup> The Lord your God hath multiplied you, and, behold, ye are this day as the stars of heaven for multitude.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v11)</sup> (The Lord God of your fathers make you a thousand times so many more as ye are, and bless you, as he hath promised you!)
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v12)</sup> How can I myself alone bear your cumbrance, and your burden, and your strife?

- I am not able to bear you myself alone: the people were multiplied as the stars of heaven, and every one of them came with cumbrance, burden, and strife.
- Moses refers back to what his father in law saw in Exodus 18: Moses sat judging the people alone from morning until evening, the people waiting all day in line, because they came to him to inquire of God and be shown the statutes and laws.
- His father in law told him the thing was not good: judging alone would wear away both Moses and the people. The counsel was to provide able men that fear God and set them in ranks over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens.

    The thing that thou doest is not good; thou wilt surely wear away, both thou and this people, for the thing is too heavy for one man.

    Provide able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness, and place them as rulers of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens.

**[Deuteronomy 1:13-15](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v13)</sup> Take you wise men, and understanding, and known among your tribes, and I will make them rulers over you.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v14)</sup> And ye answered me, and said, The thing which thou hast spoken is good for us to do.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v15)</sup> So I took the chief of your tribes, wise men, and known, and made them heads over you, captains over thousands, and captains over hundreds, and captains over fifties, and captains over tens, and officers among your tribes.

- The people answered that the thing was good to do, and Moses took the chief of the tribes, wise men and known, and made them captains over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens, and officers among the tribes.
- Rank in a congregation is biblical. With schools worldwide, every issue cannot come to one man; soldiers, officers, captains, deacons, and bishop follow the same order the fathers ordained here, and the terminology of this verse is the terminology used in the body today.

**[Deuteronomy 1:16-18](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v16)</sup> And I charged your judges at that time, saying, Hear the causes between your brethren, and judge righteously between every man and his brother, and the stranger that is with him.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v17)</sup> Ye shall not respect persons in judgment; but ye shall hear the small as well as the great; ye shall not be afraid of the face of man; for the judgment is God’s: and the cause that is too hard for you, bring it unto me, and I will hear it.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v18)</sup> And I commanded you at that time all the things which ye should do.

- The charge to the judges: hear the causes between brethren and judge righteously between every man and his brother and the stranger with him, and hear the small as well as the great.
- Ye shall not respect persons in judgment: a poor man gets no pass for being poor, and a mighty man with money and possessions is not above the laws of the Most High; no one can buy himself out of judgment.
- Ye shall not be afraid of the face of man, for the judgment is God's: no tough face may intimidate or sway a verdict, because it is the Most High passing the judgment through the judge. The cause too hard goes up the chain to Moses, and that chain itself was instituted here.

  Precepts:
  - **[Leviticus 19:15](/bible/leviticus/19#v15)**

    Do no unrighteousness in judgment; respect not the person of the poor, nor honour the person of the mighty.

- The spies brought of the fruit of the land in their hands and brought word again: it is a good land which the Lord our God doth give us.

> <sup>[27](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v27)</sup> And ye murmured in your tents, and said, Because the Lord hated us, he hath brought us forth out of the land of Egypt, to deliver us into the hand of the Amorites, to destroy us.

- Notwithstanding ye would not go up, but rebelled against the commandment of the Lord: they acknowledged the land was good, yet all refused except Caleb and Joshua, who had faith to go take it.
- They murmured in their tents that the Lord hated them and brought them out of Egypt to deliver them into the hand of the Amorites to destroy them.
- The spies had discouraged their hearts with the report: the people greater and taller than we, cities great and walled up to heaven, and the sons of the Anakims, the giants, in the land.

**[Deuteronomy 1:29-31](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v29)</sup> Then I said unto you, Dread not, neither be afraid of them.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v30)</sup> The Lord your God which goeth before you, he shall fight for you, according to all that he did for you in Egypt before your eyes;
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v31)</sup> And in the wilderness, where thou hast seen how that the Lord thy God bare thee, as a man doth bear his son, in all the way that ye went, until ye came into this place.

- Dread not, neither be afraid of them: the Lord your God which goeth before you, he shall fight for you, according to all that he did in Egypt before your eyes.
- The Amorites were no mightier than Egypt, and these same Israelites witnessed how the Lord broke Egypt to bring them out; where was their faith?
- The principle still applies: the body still has adversaries that rise against it, and the command remains to be in nothing terrified by them. Fear of the adversary is a spirit the Most High never gave his people; whoever is terrified of the enemy is out of the Spirit.

**[Deuteronomy 1:32-33](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v32)**

> <sup>[32](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v32)</sup> Yet in this thing ye did not believe the Lord your God,
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/deuteronomy/1#v33)</sup> Who went in the way before you, to search you out a place to pitch your tents in, in fire by night, to shew you by what way ye should go, and in a cloud by day.

- The Lord bare Israel in the wilderness as a man doth bear his son, went before them in fire by night and in a cloud by day to search out a place to pitch their tents; yet in this thing ye did not believe the Lord your God.
- That is the amazing part of this history: they saw all the miracles and were carried like a son, and still believed the Lord was setting them up to be killed by giants, when a giant has nothing on the Most High.
- The fathers in the wilderness had the gospel preached to them as we have: deliverance from captivity into the promised kingdom. But the word did not profit them, not being mixed with faith; they had the laws without the faith, and it is impossible to have one without the other.

- Also the Lord was angry with me for your sakes: at the waters the people fell into the same disbelief they had shown over and over, and Moses in his anger called them rebels and smote the rock twice when the Lord had said to speak to the rock.
- Anger lets the commandments slip: be angry and sin not, but when anger takes hold, a man fails to apply what he knows, and Moses failed to sanctify the Most High in the eyes of the children of Israel. His judgment was to be kept out of the land.
- Moses is still good; he stood with Elias before Christ on the mount. He missed the promised land at that time, not the kingdom. But leadership carries the greater condemnation: the more given unto you, the more stripes when you fail.

# Deuteronomy 2: Meddle Not With Them, and the Giants That Perished

Read the chapter → [Deuteronomy 2](/bible/deuteronomy/2)

**[Deuteronomy 2:1-7](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v1)</sup> Then we turned, and took our journey into the wilderness by the way of the Red sea, as the Lord spake unto me: and we compassed mount Seir many days.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v2)</sup> And the Lord spake unto me, saying,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v3)</sup> Ye have compassed this mountain long enough: turn you northward.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v4)</sup> And command thou the people, saying, Ye are to pass through the coast of your brethren the children of Esau, which dwell in Seir; and they shall be afraid of you: take ye good heed unto yourselves therefore:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v5)</sup> Meddle not with them; for I will not give you of their land, no, not so much as a foot breadth; because I have given mount Seir unto Esau for a possession.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v6)</sup> Ye shall buy meat of them for money, that ye may eat; and ye shall also buy water of them for money, that ye may drink.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v7)</sup> For the Lord thy God hath blessed thee in all the works of thy hand: he knoweth thy walking through this great wilderness: these forty years the Lord thy God hath been with thee; thou hast lacked nothing.

- Israel compassed mount Seir many days, and the Lord said, ye have compassed this mountain long enough; turn you northward and pass through the coast of your brethren the children of Esau, which dwell in Seir, the people called the white man today.
- They shall be afraid of you: the Most High put fear upon Esau so they would not trouble Israel, because he did not want a war; take ye good heed unto yourselves therefore, and meddle not with them.
- Mount Seir was given unto Esau for a possession, not so much as a foot breadth of it for Israel; the promise to Israel was the land of Canaan. Israel could buy meat and water of them for money, but take nothing.
- The Lord thy God hath blessed thee; these forty years he hath been with thee, and thou hast lacked nothing.

**[Deuteronomy 2:8-9](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v8)</sup> And when we passed by from our brethren the children of Esau, which dwelt in Seir, through the way of the plain from Elath, and from Ezion–gaber, we turned and passed by the way of the wilderness of Moab.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v9)</sup> And the Lord said unto me, Distress not the Moabites, neither contend with them in battle: for I will not give thee of their land for a possession; because I have given Ar unto the children of Lot for a possession.

- Passing from the children of Esau, the same order came concerning Moab: distress not the Moabites, neither contend with them in battle, for Ar was given unto the children of Lot for a possession.
- For Lot's sake the Most High secured that land to his children; it was not Israel's to conquer.

**[Deuteronomy 2:10-12](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v10)</sup> The Emims dwelt therein in times past, a people great, and many, and tall, as the Anakims;
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v11)</sup> Which also were accounted giants, as the Anakims; but the Moabites call them Emims.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v12)</sup> The Horims also dwelt in Seir beforetime; but the children of Esau succeeded them, when they had destroyed them from before them, and dwelt in their stead; as Israel did unto the land of his possession, which the Lord gave unto them.

- The Emims dwelt in that land in times past, a people great and many and tall as the Anakims, accounted giants; and the Horims dwelt in Seir beforetime, and the children of Esau succeeded them and dwelt in their stead.
- That is heavy: the Moabites are not the chosen people, yet they went into a land of giants and took it. Israel had the Most High on their side, the pillar, the power, the angels, and still found a way to be scared of giants.

**[Deuteronomy 2:13-16](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v13)</sup> Now rise up, said I, and get you over the brook Zered. And we went over the brook Zered.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v14)</sup> And the space in which we came from Kadesh–barnea, until we were come over the brook Zered, was thirty and eight years; until all the generation of the men of war were wasted out from among the host, as the Lord sware unto them.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v15)</sup> For indeed the hand of the Lord was against them, to destroy them from among the host, until they were consumed.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v16)</sup> So it came to pass, when all the men of war were consumed and dead from among the people,

- From Kadesh-barnea to the crossing of the brook Zered was thirty and eight years, until all the generation of the men of war were wasted out from among the host, as the Lord sware unto them.
- The hand of the Lord was against them to destroy them from among the host until they were consumed; only when all the men of war were dead did the word come to pass over.

**[Deuteronomy 2:17-19](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v17)</sup> That the Lord spake unto me, saying,
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v18)</sup> Thou art to pass over through Ar, the coast of Moab, this day:
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v19)</sup> And when thou comest nigh over against the children of Ammon, distress them not, nor meddle with them: for I will not give thee of the land of the children of Ammon any possession; because I have given it unto the children of Lot for a possession.

- Coming over against the children of Ammon, the same charge stood: distress them not, nor meddle with them, for that land was given unto the children of Lot for a possession.
- Esau, Moab, and Ammon were all off limits; the Most High appointed their possessions, and Israel was to pick no fight with them.

**[Deuteronomy 2:20-21](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v20)</sup> (That also was accounted a land of giants: giants dwelt therein in old time; and the Ammonites call them Zamzummims;
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v21)</sup> A people great, and many, and tall, as the Anakims; but the Lord destroyed them before them; and they succeeded them, and dwelt in their stead:

- Ammon's land was also accounted a land of giants: the Zamzummims, a people great and many and tall as the Anakims; and the Lord destroyed them, and the Ammonites succeeded them and dwelt in their stead.
- The giants were famous from the beginning, of great stature and expert in war, yet the Lord chose them not and gave them no knowledge; they were destroyed because they had no wisdom and perished through their own foolishness. There are no giants today; stature and skill in war saved none of them.

  Precepts:
  - **[Baruch 3:26-28](/bible/baruch/3#v26)**
> <sup>[26](/bible/baruch/3#v26)</sup> There were the giants famous from the beginning, that were of so great stature, and so expert in war.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/baruch/3#v27)</sup> Those did not the Lord choose, neither gave he the way of knowledge unto them:
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/baruch/3#v28)</sup> But they were destroyed, because they had no wisdom, and perished through their own foolishness.

    The giants the people feared perished through their own foolishness, for the Lord chose them not.

**[Deuteronomy 2:22-23](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v22)</sup> As he did to the children of Esau, which dwelt in Seir, when he destroyed the Horims from before them; and they succeeded them, and dwelt in their stead even unto this day:
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v23)</sup> And the Avims which dwelt in Hazerim, even unto Azzah, the Caphtorims, which came forth out of Caphtor, destroyed them, and dwelt in their stead.)

- As he did to the children of Esau when he destroyed the Horims from before them, so these nations succeeded the giants and dwelt in their stead even unto this day.
- The Avims which dwelt in Hazerim, even unto Azzah, the Caphtorims destroyed them and dwelt in their stead; the Most High moves nations in and out of lands as he wills.

**[Deuteronomy 2:24-25](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v24)</sup> Rise ye up, take your journey, and pass over the river Arnon: behold, I have given into thine hand Sihon the Amorite, king of Heshbon, and his land: begin to possess it, and contend with him in battle.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v25)</sup> This day will I begin to put the dread of thee and the fear of thee upon the nations that are under the whole heaven, who shall hear report of thee, and shall tremble, and be in anguish because of thee.

- Rise up, pass over the river Arnon: behold, I have given into thine hand Sihon the Amorite, king of Heshbon, and his land; begin to possess it, and contend with him in battle. This recaps [Numbers 21](/bible/numbers/21), when it was time to destroy the king of the Amorites.
- This day will I begin to put the dread of thee and the fear of thee upon the nations under the whole heaven. The Most High never gave Israel the spirit of fear, but when it was time to conquer he put the fear of Israel on the nations, so they trembled and were in anguish.

**[Deuteronomy 2:26-30](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v26)</sup> And I sent messengers out of the wilderness of Kedemoth unto Sihon king of Heshbon with words of peace, saying,
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v27)</sup> Let me pass through thy land: I will go along by the high way, I will neither turn unto the right hand nor to the left.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v28)</sup> Thou shalt sell me meat for money, that I may eat; and give me water for money, that I may drink: only I will pass through on my feet;
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v29)</sup> (As the children of Esau which dwell in Seir, and the Moabites which dwell in Ar, did unto me;) until I shall pass over Jordan into the land which the Lord our God giveth us.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v30)</sup> But Sihon king of Heshbon would not let us pass by him: for the Lord thy God hardened his spirit, and made his heart obstinate, that he might deliver him into thy hand, as appeareth this day.

- Moses sent messengers unto Sihon with words of peace: let me pass through thy land by the highway, buying meat and water of thee for money, only passing through on my feet, as was done passing Esau in Seir and Moab in Ar.
- But Sihon would not let Israel pass, for the Lord thy God hardened his spirit and made his heart obstinate, that he might deliver him into thy hand.
- It is the same dealing as with Pharaoh: the Lord hardened his heart so the judgment could land, for the iniquity of the Amorites was full and their land was appointed to Israel.

**[Deuteronomy 2:31-37](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v31)**

> <sup>[31](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v31)</sup> And the Lord said unto me, Behold, I have begun to give Sihon and his land before thee: begin to possess, that thou mayest inherit his land.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v32)</sup> Then Sihon came out against us, he and all his people, to fight at Jahaz.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v33)</sup> And the Lord our God delivered him before us; and we smote him, and his sons, and all his people.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v34)</sup> And we took all his cities at that time, and utterly destroyed the men, and the women, and the little ones, of every city, we left none to remain:
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v35)</sup> Only the cattle we took for a prey unto ourselves, and the spoil of the cities which we took.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v36)</sup> From Aroer, which is by the brink of the river of Arnon, and from the city that is by the river, even unto Gilead, there was not one city too strong for us: the Lord our God delivered all unto us:
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/deuteronomy/2#v37)</sup> Only unto the land of the children of Ammon thou camest not, nor unto any place of the river Jabbok, nor unto the cities in the mountains, nor unto whatsoever the Lord our God forbad us.

- Sihon came out against Israel to fight at Jahaz, and the Lord delivered him: they smote him and his sons and all his people, took all his cities, and utterly destroyed the men, women, and little ones of every city; none was left to remain.
- That is how thorough the judgment of the Most High was for Israel's sake; that is how specially privileged Israel was, and still they struggled to believe and obey him.
- Only the cattle and the spoil of the cities were taken for a prey. From Aroer by the brink of the river of Arnon even unto Gilead, there was not one city too strong: the Lord our God delivered all unto us.
- Only the land of the children of Ammon they came not unto, nor unto whatsoever the Lord our God forbad: Israel possessed every place the Lord led them and touched nothing he withheld.
