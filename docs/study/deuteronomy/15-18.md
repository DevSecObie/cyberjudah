## Introduction

This session read [Deuteronomy 15](/bible/deuteronomy/15) through 18 with brief exposition. Chapter 15 covers the seventh year of release for the poor, the law of lending and giving to the poor brother, the Hebrew servant who must go out free and well furnished in the seventh year, and the firstling males of the herd and flock sanctified unto the Most High. Chapter 16 covers the feast of the passover, the feast of weeks, and the feast of tabernacles, the three times in the year all males appear before the Most High, judges and just judgment, and the groves and images that are forbidden. Chapter 17 covers the sound sacrifice, the judgment of idolaters, hard controversies determined by the priests and judges, and the election and duty of a king. Chapter 18 covers the priests' and Levites' inheritance and their due, the abominations of the nations to be avoided, the Prophet like unto Moses who must be heard, and the presumptuous prophets who are to die.

- Of a foreigner thou mayest exact it again, but that which is thine with thy brother thine hand shall release. The seven year release stands on the sabbath of the land already given in [Leviticus 25](/bible/leviticus/25).

- Beware that there be not a thought in thy wicked heart saying, The seventh year, the year of release, is at hand, and thine eye be evil against thy poor brother and thou givest him nought; if he cry unto the Lord against thee, it is sin unto thee.
- Give him surely, and let not thine heart be grieved when thou givest, for because of this thing the Most High shall bless thee in all thy works.
- Notice that Esau had this Bible, these commandments and these laws, yet worked us to death for over four hundred years, and when they released us we did not go out with anything. They had these laws written before them and did not do them.
- The poor shall never cease out of the land; Christ said the same, that the poor are always going to be among us. That is why no campaign can fight poverty out of existence: Christ said it will always be among us.

- When people say slavery was taking place in Africa among us, it was not slavery like we suffered: it was people among us that fell poor and sold themselves to their brothers, the very case this law and [Leviticus 25](/bible/leviticus/25) govern.
- Thou shalt furnish him liberally out of thy flock, out of thy floor, and out of thy winepress; he must leave blessed, with some of the flock, the corn, and the wine. He cannot go out poor the way he came in; that shows you the Most High is a good God.
- The reason given is memory: remember that thou wast a bondman in the land of Egypt, and the Lord thy God redeemed thee.

- Thou shalt eat no leavened bread with it; seven days shalt thou eat unleavened bread, even the bread of affliction, because thou camest forth out of the land of Egypt in haste, that thou mayest remember the day all the days of thy life.
- Nothing of the flesh sacrificed the first day at even, the lamb, may remain all night until the morning. The passover may not be sacrificed within any of thy gates, but only at the place which the Lord thy God shall choose to place his name there, at even, at the going down of the sun.
- Six days thou shalt eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day is a solemn assembly and no work: the first day of the passover is a sabbath, and the seventh day of the passover is a sabbath.

# Deuteronomy 17: Idolatry Judged and the Duty of a King

Read the chapter → [Deuteronomy 17](/bible/deuteronomy/17)

**[Deuteronomy 17:1](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v1)</sup> Thou shalt not sacrifice unto the Lord thy God any bullock, or sheep, wherein is blemish, or any evilfavouredness: for that is an abomination unto the Lord thy God.

- Thou shalt not sacrifice unto the Most High any bullock or sheep wherein is blemish, or any evilfavouredness, for that is an abomination unto the Lord thy God.
- Back then you could be put to death for sacrificing a bullock or sheep with a blemish, because doing an abominable thing carried death.

**[Deuteronomy 17:2-5](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v2)</sup> If there be found among you, within any of thy gates which the Lord thy God giveth thee, man or woman, that hath wrought wickedness in the sight of the Lord thy God, in transgressing his covenant,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v3)</sup> And hath gone and served other gods, and worshipped them, either the sun, or moon, or any of the host of heaven, which I have not commanded;
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v4)</sup> And it be told thee, and thou hast heard of it, and enquired diligently, and, behold, it be true, and the thing certain, that such abomination is wrought in Israel:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v5)</sup> Then shalt thou bring forth that man or that woman, which have committed that wicked thing, unto thy gates, even that man or that woman, and shalt stone them with stones, till they die.

- If there be found among you man or woman that hath wrought wickedness, transgressed the covenant, and gone and served other gods, the sun or moon or any of the host of heaven, which the Most High commanded not, the matter had to be established first.
- A lot of people do not know how to apply [Leviticus 5:1](/bible/leviticus/5#v1); the precept for [Leviticus 5:1](/bible/leviticus/5#v1) is [Deuteronomy 17:4](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v4). Whether it be told thee or thou hast heard of it, thou shalt enquire diligently, and behold, if it be true and the thing certain that such abomination is wrought in Israel, then judgment proceeds.
- Make sure you are certain of a thing before you bring somebody to judgment; only after diligent enquiry was the man or woman brought to the gates and stoned with stones till they die.

  Precepts:
  - **[Leviticus 5:1](/bible/leviticus/5#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/leviticus/5#v1)</sup> And if a soul sin, and hear the voice of swearing, and is a witness, whether he hath seen or known of it; if he do not utter it, then he shall bear his iniquity.

    If a soul hear the voice of swearing, and is a witness, and do not utter it, he shall bear his iniquity; the witness law is applied through the diligent enquiry of Deuteronomy 17:4.

**[Deuteronomy 17:6-7](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v6)</sup> At the mouth of two witnesses, or three witnesses, shall he that is worthy of death be put to death; but at the mouth of one witness he shall not be put to death.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v7)</sup> The hands of the witnesses shall be first upon him to put him to death, and afterward the hands of all the people. So thou shalt put the evil away from among you.

- At the mouth of two witnesses or three witnesses shall he that is worthy of death be put to death; at the mouth of one witness he shall not be put to death.
- The hands of the witnesses shall be first upon him to put him to death, and afterward the hands of all the people. Wait till we go back to this: the ones who witnessed against you were the first to lay hands on you, so a false witness could not hide behind the crowd.
- So thou shalt put the evil away from among you.

- The man that will do presumptuously and will not hearken unto the priest that standeth to minister before the Most High, or unto the judge, even that man shall die; and all the people shall hear, and fear, and do no more presumptuously.

**[Deuteronomy 17:14-17](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v14)**
