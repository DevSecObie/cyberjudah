## Introduction

This session covers [Deuteronomy 31](/bible/deuteronomy/31) through 34, the close of the Torah. These are some of the most monumental chapters for identifying who we are today: we have already been through [Genesis 49](/bible/genesis/49), [Deuteronomy 28](/bible/deuteronomy/28), and [Leviticus 26](/bible/leviticus/26), and today we cover [Deuteronomy 33](/bible/deuteronomy/33), the blessing that shows where the tribes of Israel are geographically in these last days. Moses, barred from the promised land, spends his final days giving the children of Israel prophecy: chapter 31 is his charge and the warning of rebellion to come, chapter 32 is the song of Moses, chapter 33 is the blessing of the tribes, and chapter 34 is his death in the mount.

**[Deuteronomy 31:1-2](/bible/deuteronomy/31#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/31#v1)</sup> And Moses went and spake these words unto all Israel.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/31#v2)</sup> And he said unto them, I am an hundred and twenty years old this day; I can no more go out and come in: also the Lord hath said unto me, Thou shalt not go over this Jordan.

- Moses is one hundred and twenty years old this day, and the Most High has told him he shall not go over this Jordan. After he was provoked by the people at the waters of strife, he did not sanctify the Most High before the children of Israel, so he was barred from entering the promised land.
- The Most High lets him go up on the mountain to take a look at the land, but he will not enter into it. Before his farewell, chapters 31 through 33 give the children of Israel prophecy of what would happen to them.

- Israel became a burden to Moses to the point he asked the Most High to kill him rather than let him see his own wretchedness. While I am yet alive with you this day, ye have been rebellious against the Lord; and how much more after my death?

> <sup>[12](/bible/deuteronomy/34#v12)</sup> And in all that mighty hand, and in all the great terror which Moses shewed in the sight of all Israel.

- And there arose not a prophet since in Israel like unto Moses, whom the Lord knew face to face. This verse is heavy because of a doctrine: some say Joshua was the prophet of [Deuteronomy 18:18](/bible/deuteronomy/18#v18), the prophet raised up like unto Moses, and therefore there is no Christ.
- But the scripture that acknowledges Joshua rising up with the spirit upon him says in the same breath that there arose not a prophet since in Israel like unto Moses. Joshua was a mighty prophet, but he did not fit that word; Christ did.
- None matched all the signs and the wonders which the Lord sent Moses to do in the land of Egypt, and all that mighty hand, and all the great terror which Moses shewed in the sight of all Israel. With this the Torah, the books of Moses, is finished.

  Precepts:
  - **[Deuteronomy 18:18](/bible/deuteronomy/18#v18)**
> <sup>[18](/bible/deuteronomy/18#v18)</sup> I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto thee, and will put my words in his mouth; and he shall speak unto them all that I shall command him.

    I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto thee: Christ, not Joshua, fulfilled this word.
