## Introduction

This session covered Deuteronomy 3-6. As Israel nears the end of the first five books, the thread of each is worth holding: Genesis is the beginning, the book of your genes and genealogy; Exodus is the grand exit from slavery in Egypt; Leviticus carries the levitical laws given through the Levites; Numbers is the numbering of the families and armies of Israel; and Deuteronomy is a reiteration, a repetition of the laws of the Most High and of everything that happened in the previous books. Chapters 1 and 2 retold how the Most High helped Moses by appointing leaders, so that one man would not carry the whole burden of a stiffnecked and rebellious people alone, and gave Israel instruction on which wars to take on, sparing Edom and the children of Moab. Chapter 3 opens with the war against Og king of Bashan.

**[Deuteronomy 3:1-3](/bible/deuteronomy/3#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/3#v1)</sup> Then we turned, and went up the way to Bashan: and Og the king of Bashan came out against us, he and all his people, to battle at Edrei.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/3#v2)</sup> And the Lord said unto me, Fear him not: for I will deliver him, and all his people, and his land, into thy hand; and thou shalt do unto him as thou didst unto Sihon king of the Amorites, which dwelt at Heshbon.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/deuteronomy/3#v3)</sup> So the Lord our God delivered into our hands Og also, the king of Bashan, and all his people: and we smote him until none was left to him remaining.

- Bashan is about to be conquered, and Og the king of Bashan came out with all his people to battle at Edrei. The Most High said fear him not, for I will deliver him and all his people and his land into thy hand, as was done to Sihon king of the Amorites at Heshbon.
- Always remember, the battle is the Lord's; this fight is spiritual. Israel does not need weapons of mass destruction: the Bible is the weapon, and when Israel keeps the commandments the Most High fights for his people.
- The Lord our God delivered into our hands also the king of Bashan: they were out there doing physical war, but they gave the praise, the glory, and the victory to the Most High. They did not boast in their own swords; that is how you sanctify the Most High.
- The same order stands today: when you wake up you thank the Most High, and you thank him for the food even though you cooked it, because he is the one who allowed you to have it. You always put the Most High first.

- Israel took the land from the two kings of the Amorites on this side Jordan, from the river of Arnon unto mount Hermon.
- Only Og king of Bashan remained of the remnant of giants. His bedstead was a bedstead of iron, nine cubits long and four cubits broad: about thirteen feet long and six feet wide, near the size of two king beds, showing how tall and big this man was.
- Og was an Amorite, and the Amorites descend from Ham; [Genesis 10](/bible/genesis/10) proves the lineage. The Hamitic nations were known for great stature, as Goliath the Philistine stood nine feet nine inches, and to this day the tallest peoples on earth, the Watusi, the Dinka, and the Nilotic tribes of Sudan, Ethiopia, and Kenya, are Hamites.
- Ancient Palestine was first the land of the Philistines, Hamitic tribes, before the Arabs and later others took hold of it; the name goes back to those ancient Philistines.

# Deuteronomy 4: Neither Add nor Diminish

Read the chapter → [Deuteronomy 4](/bible/deuteronomy/4)

**[Deuteronomy 4:1](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v1)</sup> Now therefore hearken, O Israel, unto the statutes and unto the judgments, which I teach you, for to do them, that ye may live, and go in and possess the land which the Lord God of your fathers giveth you.

- Hearken, O Israel: hearken means listen, pay attention, and there is a difference between hearing and listening.
- The statutes and judgments are taught for to do them; knowing you are an Israelite is only half the battle. The main thing is applying the scriptures, doing what the Most High is telling you to do, that ye may live.
- The promise is to go in and possess the land, and possessing land is true wealth: your milk and honey, your cattle, your agriculture, where you cultivate and take care of your family. It is not the diamonds, the cars, the sneakers, or the newest phone; a big house with no land is not wealth, and that is why the heathen boxes our people into apartments.

**[Deuteronomy 4:2](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v2)</sup> Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it, that ye may keep the commandments of the Lord your God which I command you.

- Ye shall not add unto the word, neither diminish ought from it. This was spoken around 1500 BC, and on the last page of the Bible the same charge closes the book: the prophets across millenniums and captivities all speak the same thing, Moses, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Christ, and John, and that consistency proves the Bible is a true book, because twenty people cannot keep one story straight for five minutes.
- The watered down versions such as the NIV and ESV show what taking away looks like: many of them remove [Matthew 18:11](/bible/matthew/18#v11) and other scriptures, and they have taken out the Apocrypha.

- Which shall hear all these statutes and say, surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people: they may not say it out loud today, but when they see Israel united in Christ and in order, that is what rises in their minds.

Solomon shows how the name is taken in vain: lest I be poor, and steal, and take the name of my God in vain. Full riches make a man deny the Most High and forget the congregation and the work, and money has pulled people out of this walk; poverty drives a man to steal. The chief things for life are simple, water, bread, clothing, and an house to cover shame, and the Most High blessed Solomon with riches because riches were not his god.

- These are the commandments, the statutes, and the judgments, commanded to be taught, that thou mightest fear the Lord thy God: the fear always comes up first, thou, and thy son, and thy son's son, all the days of thy life.
- Hear therefore, O Israel, and observe to do it, that ye may increase mightily. Increase and power are the desire of the people, and keeping the commandments is the one thing our people have not tried; assimilation, the gods of the nations, and politics, liberal or conservative, have all been tried and none of it has worked.

- Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God is one Lord: and thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.
- Thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children: diligently means you do not miss a beat. The children are not to be taught the dance challenges, halloween, and the satanic birthdays, and remember that you teach by your actions, so you fix yourself first.
- Talk of them when thou sittest in thine house: go through the scriptures at home, the histories of Samson, Joseph, Solomon, and David. When thou walkest by the way: when you run into each other, ask, are you keeping the laws, what four chapters did you read today?
- When thou liest down, and when thou risest up: the bedtime stories are biblical, the ten plagues of Egypt rather than the fairy tales, prayer before they sleep and when they rise, and even the coloring books painting the images brown, bringing it back to its true course.

**[Deuteronomy 6:17-19](/bible/deuteronomy/6#v17)**

>
> <sup>[18](/bible/deuteronomy/6#v18)</sup> And thou shalt do that which is right and good in the sight of the Lord: that it may be well with thee, and that thou mayest go in and possess the good land which the Lord sware unto thy fathers,

> <sup>[19](/bible/deuteronomy/6#v19)</sup> To cast out all thine enemies from before thee, as the Lord hath spoken.

- Ye shall diligently keep the commandments of the Lord your God, and his testimonies, and his statutes. Testimony is one of the trigger words that confuses our people: the laws are what Moses gave, and the testimonies are the history of the forefathers and foremothers, what they went through and how it was handled according to the laws.
- Everything correlates: when king David had Uriah killed, that goes back to thou shalt not kill, thou shalt not covet, and bearing false witness; he was dealing with much sin in that one matter. That is how you go precept upon precept, line upon line, to the law and to the testimony.
- A personal testimony that cannot come back to the Bible is not biblical; if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light, no truth, in them.
- Thou shalt do that which is right and good in the sight of the Lord: not by personal standards of what feels good in the heart, but by the standard set before us, for the scripture says plainly that the law is good. The reward is to go in and possess the good land, the real wealth, not the bags and rings and sneakers, which are vanity.
