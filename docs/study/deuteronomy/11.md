## Introduction

This session was a reading of the four chapters with basic precepts supplied along the way to help the understanding, not a full class; questions were submitted to the captains. Chapter 11 recounts the great acts of the Most High that Israel's own eyes saw and sets before Israel a blessing and a curse. Chapter 12 commands the destruction of the idolatrous places and fixes worship at the place where the Lord puts his name. Chapter 13 gives the judgment on prophets, family, and cities that entice Israel to other gods. Chapter 14 declares Israel a peculiar people, holy unto the Lord, and lays out the law of clean and unclean foods and the tithe.

**[Deuteronomy 11:4](/bible/deuteronomy/11#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/deuteronomy/11#v4)</sup> And what he did unto the army of Egypt, unto their horses, and to their chariots; how he made the water of the Red sea to overflow them as they pursued after you, and how the Lord hath destroyed them unto this day;

- Moses recounts to the generation whose own eyes saw it what the Most High did unto the army of Egypt, unto their horses and chariots: he made the water of the Red sea to overflow them as they pursued after Israel.
- This is part of the history Moses was recounting: remember what the Lord did for your fathers.

**[Deuteronomy 11:13-15](/bible/deuteronomy/11#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/deuteronomy/11#v13)</sup> And it shall come to pass, if ye shall hearken diligently unto my commandments which I command you this day, to love the Lord your God, and to serve him with all your heart and with all your soul,

>
> <sup>[15](/bible/deuteronomy/11#v15)</sup> And I will send grass in thy fields for thy cattle, that thou mayest eat and be full.

- Hearken diligently unto the commandments, to love the Lord your God and serve him with all your heart and with all your soul, and he gives the rain of the land in his due season, the first and the latter rain, that thou mayest gather thy corn, thy wine, and thine oil, with grass in the fields for the cattle.
- This is the blessing side that [Deuteronomy 28](/bible/deuteronomy/28) spells out: when Israel kept the commandments, the Lord gave rain in due season in Israel's own land. Chapter 28 comes up in the reading in a few days; these are the basic precepts for it.

**[Deuteronomy 11:16-17](/bible/deuteronomy/11#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/deuteronomy/11#v16)</sup> Take heed to yourselves, that your heart be not deceived, and ye turn aside, and serve other gods, and worship them;
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/deuteronomy/11#v17)</sup> And then the Lord’s wrath be kindled against you, and he shut up the heaven, that there be no rain, and that the land yield not her fruit; and lest ye perish quickly from off the good land which the Lord giveth you.

- Take heed that your heart be not deceived to turn aside and serve other gods: the second part is the curse. The Lord's wrath is kindled, he shuts up the heaven that there be no rain, the land yields not her fruit, and ye perish quickly from off the good land.
- No rain means drought: the crops cannot grow, and with no grass the animals have nothing to feed on, so the cattle go lean and there is no meat to eat.
- The things written aforetime were written for our learning: Jeremiah shows this curse landing in Israel's history, the ground chapt because there was no rain, and the confession that our backslidings are many.

- Behold, I set before you this day a blessing and a curse: the blessing if ye obey the commandments of the Lord your God, the curse if ye will not obey but turn aside after other gods which ye have not known.
- The blessing is what [Deuteronomy 28:1-14](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v1) describes; the curse is what came upon Israel, running through [Deuteronomy 28](/bible/deuteronomy/28) to verse 68.
