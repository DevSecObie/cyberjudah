## Introduction

This session covered [Deuteronomy 19](/bible/deuteronomy/19) through 22, laws given as Israel prepared to come into the land of Canaan. Chapter 19 sets apart cities of refuge for the accidental slayer, protects the landmark, and establishes the matter at the mouth of two or three witnesses. Chapter 20 gives the laws of warfare and the answer to fear in battle. Chapter 21 handles the unknown slain man, the captive wife, the right of the firstborn, and the stubborn and rebellious son. Chapter 22 gathers the laws of love toward a brother, the garments of men and women, the fringes, and the laws of virginity and marriage.

**[Deuteronomy 19:1-3](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v1)</sup> When the Lord thy God hath cut off the nations, whose land the Lord thy God giveth thee, and thou succeedest them, and dwellest in their cities, and in their houses;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v2)</sup> Thou shalt separate three cities for thee in the midst of thy land, which the Lord thy God giveth thee to possess it.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v3)</sup> Thou shalt prepare thee a way, and divide the coasts of thy land, which the Lord thy God giveth thee to inherit, into three parts, that every slayer may flee thither.

- When the Most High cut off the nations of the land of Canaan, the Canaanites, the Jebusites, the Perizzites, and the Girgashites, and Israel succeeded them and dwelt in their cities and houses, Israel was to separate three cities in the midst of the land and prepare the way to them.
- This law goes into accidental death, what the Bible sometimes calls manslaughter. An Israelite who accidentally killed another could flee to one of these three cities of refuge, where the avenger of blood could not seek him out and kill him there to revenge the blood of his nearest of kin.

**[Deuteronomy 19:4-6](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v4)</sup> And this is the case of the slayer, which shall flee thither, that he may live: Whoso killeth his neighbour ignorantly, whom he hated not in time past;
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v5)</sup> As when a man goeth into the wood with his neighbour to hew wood, and his hand fetcheth a stroke with the axe to cut down the tree, and the head slippeth from the helve, and lighteth upon his neighbour, that he die; he shall flee unto one of those cities, and live:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v6)</sup> Lest the avenger of the blood pursue the slayer, while his heart is hot, and overtake him, because the way is long, and slay him; whereas he was not worthy of death, inasmuch as he hated him not in time past.

- Whoso killeth his neighbour ignorantly, whom he hated not in time past, means he did not mean to do it; it was an accident. The example given is two men hewing wood, and the axe head slips from the helve and strikes the neighbour that he die: he shall flee to one of those cities and live.
- The avenger of blood is the nearest of kin, and while his heart is hot he may pursue and overtake the slayer because the way is long. But the slayer was not worthy of death, because he hated him not in time past; there was no premeditation, no intention to kill.
- Accidents happen, and the Most High put laws in place for when these things happened. If it was for sure an accident, the man is not worthy of death, as the scripture says.

**[Deuteronomy 19:7-10](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v7)</sup> Wherefore I command thee, saying, Thou shalt separate three cities for thee.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v8)</sup> And if the Lord thy God enlarge thy coast, as he hath sworn unto thy fathers, and give thee all the land which he promised to give unto thy fathers;
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v9)</sup> If thou shalt keep all these commandments to do them, which I command thee this day, to love the Lord thy God, and to walk ever in his ways; then shalt thou add three cities more for thee, beside these three:
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v10)</sup> That innocent blood be not shed in thy land, which the Lord thy God giveth thee for an inheritance, and so blood be upon thee.

- If the Lord enlarge thy coast as he sware unto the fathers, then three more cities were to be added. As Israel grew and possessed more of the land of Canaan, more cities of refuge were established where a slayer could flee from the avenger of blood.
- The purpose is stated plainly: that innocent blood be not shed in the land which the Lord giveth thee for an inheritance, and so blood be upon thee.

**[Deuteronomy 19:11-13](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v11)</sup> But if any man hate his neighbour, and lie in wait for him, and rise up against him, and smite him mortally that he die, and fleeth into one of these cities:
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v12)</sup> Then the elders of his city shall send and fetch him thence, and deliver him into the hand of the avenger of blood, that he may die.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v13)</sup> Thine eye shall not pity him, but thou shalt put away the guilt of innocent blood from Israel, that it may go well with thee.

- This gives the distinction between an accident and intentional killing; this is describing murder. He hated his neighbour and lay in wait for him: he devised a scheme, waited for the right opportunity and the right time, rose up against him, and smote him mortally that he died.
- The murderer cannot then run to a city of refuge to lay low. Those cities were not established for him; they were established so retaliation did not come on those who accidentally brought harm to somebody. The elders of his own city shall fetch him from there and deliver him into the hand of the avenger of blood, that he may die.
- Thine eye shall not pity him: putting away the guilt of innocent blood from Israel is what makes it go well with the nation.

**[Deuteronomy 19:14](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v14)</sup> Thou shalt not remove thy neighbour’s landmark, which they of old time have set in thine inheritance, which thou shalt inherit in the land that the Lord thy God giveth thee to possess it.

- What is a landmark? It pays to use tools outside the scriptures, because what good is reading the Bible without understanding of the words written in it. The Zondervan Bible Dictionary defines a landmark as an object, stone, or post used to mark the boundary of a field, and notes that removal of landmarks was prohibited by the Mosaic law.
- The landmark establishes the boundaries of a man's property, where it begins and where it ends. A man who keeps nudging his neighbour's landmark over little by little until he has taken an acre is stealing land; the dictionary notes it was considered equal to theft.

**[Deuteronomy 19:15](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v15)</sup> One witness shall not rise up against a man for any iniquity, or for any sin, in any sin that he sinneth: at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established.

- One witness shall not rise up against a man for any iniquity or any sin. One man alone saying he saw a theft is not proof; anybody can come and lie and get people put to death, imprisoned, or beaten.
- At the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established. It has to be clear-cut; two or three solid witnesses, not one solid witness and a shaky witness. We deal with facts.

**[Deuteronomy 19:16-19](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v16)</sup> If a false witness rise up against any man to testify against him that which is wrong;
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v17)</sup> Then both the men, between whom the controversy is, shall stand before the Lord, before the priests and the judges, which shall be in those days;
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v18)</sup> And the judges shall make diligent inquisition: and, behold, if the witness be a false witness, and hath testified falsely against his brother;
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v19)</sup> Then shall ye do unto him, as he had thought to have done unto his brother: so shalt thou put the evil away from among you.

- When a false witness rises up, both men in the controversy stand before the Lord, before the priests and the judges of those days. False accusation is real: people will lie on a man to cover themselves, and false witnesses arise among your own people as well, which is part of why the nation fell into captivity.
- The judges shall make diligent inquisition: they get to the bottom of the matter and find out exactly who is telling the truth, who is lying, and what the facts are. A judge cannot judge a matter off what one person says; each side tells the story in his own favour, so you must examine the whole matter, question both sides against each other, and decipher the truth. Diligent inquisition is what separates a good judge from a bad judge; a lazy or partial judge makes false judgment.
- If the witness is found false, then shall ye do unto him as he had thought to have done unto his brother. Accuse a man falsely of stealing, and the judgment for stealing comes on you; accuse him falsely of adultery or another sin punishable by death, and you are put to death, because that is what you intended for your neighbour. So is evil put away from Israel.

**[Deuteronomy 19:20](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v20)</sup> And those which remain shall hear, and fear, and shall henceforth commit no more any such evil among you.

- The judgment is public and it teaches: those which remain shall hear and fear, and shall henceforth commit no more any such evil among you.

**[Deuteronomy 19:21](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/deuteronomy/19#v21)</sup> And thine eye shall not pity; but life shall go for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot.

- Thine eye shall not pity: in this time under Moses it was life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot. If a man knocked out your tooth, the recompense was exact, and it went through the judges one way or another; thus saith the law.
- Christ spoke on this saying, because exact retaliation between men creates a never-ending cycle of vengeance and hatred. Turning the other cheek does not mean letting somebody beat you down into the dirt; it means when somebody does something to you, do not come back later seeking revenge.
- Seeking revenge only brings more judgment on your own head; the wise course is to let it go and let the Most High repay, for vengeance belongs to him.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 5:38-39](/bible/matthew/5#v38)**
    > <sup>[38](/bible/matthew/5#v38)</sup> Ye have heard that it hath been said, An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth:
    >
    > <sup>[39](/bible/matthew/5#v39)</sup> But I say unto you, That ye resist not evil: but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also.

    Ye have heard that it hath been said, an eye for an eye; but Christ says resist not evil, meaning do not seek revenge, not that you allow yourself to be beaten down.
  - **[Romans 12:19](/bible/romans/12#v19)**
    > <sup>[19](/bible/romans/12#v19)</sup> Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.

    Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord: do not avenge yourself, for revenge only brings judgment on you.

# Deuteronomy 20: Laws of Warfare and the Spirit of Fear

Read the chapter → [Deuteronomy 20](/bible/deuteronomy/20)

**[Deuteronomy 20:1](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v1)</sup> When thou goest out to battle against thine enemies, and seest horses, and chariots, and a people more than thou, be not afraid of them: for the Lord thy God is with thee, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.

- When thou goest out to battle and seest horses and chariots and a people more than thou, be not afraid, for the Lord thy God is with thee, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.
- That goes for us now, because we are still in a battle. We are not physically out battling the nations as our forefathers were when they took the land; we are in a spiritual battle, for the nations are confederate against Israel being a nation any more and against our remembrance of Israel and our laws. This whole world is set up for our people to go into sin and iniquity.
- Fear makes people trim their ways: afraid of family at the pagan holiday table, tucking their fringes in certain places, scared to be seen in the truth. Stand boldly; the Lord has not given us the spirit of fear, for that spirit came from Satan himself.

  Precepts:
  - **[Psalms 83:4](/bible/psalms/83#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/83#v4)</sup> They have said, Come, and let us cut them off from being a nation; that the name of Israel may be no more in remembrance.

    The nations have said, come, and let us cut them off from being a nation, that the name of Israel may be no more in remembrance; this is the battle Israel is in now.
  - **[2 Corinthians 10:3-4](/bible/2-corinthians/10#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/2-corinthians/10#v3)</sup> For though we walk in the flesh, we do not war after the flesh:
    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/2-corinthians/10#v4)</sup> (For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds;)

    Though we walk in the flesh, we do not war after the flesh; the weapons of our warfare are not carnal but mighty through God to the pulling down of strongholds, the doctrines, lusts, and wicked thoughts in the minds of our people, fought with the scriptures, the sword of truth.
  - **[2 Timothy 1:7](/bible/2-timothy/1#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/2-timothy/1#v7)</sup> For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind.

    God hath not given us the spirit of fear; do not be afraid in this battle.

**[Deuteronomy 20:2-4](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v2)</sup> And it shall be, when ye are come nigh unto the battle, that the priest shall approach and speak unto the people,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v3)</sup> And shall say unto them, Hear, O Israel, ye approach this day unto battle against your enemies: let not your hearts faint, fear not, and do not tremble, neither be ye terrified because of them;
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v4)</sup> For the Lord your God is he that goeth with you, to fight for you against your enemies, to save you.

- When Israel came nigh unto the battle, the priest approached and spoke: let not your hearts faint, fear not, do not tremble, neither be ye terrified, for the Lord your God is he that goeth with you to fight for you against your enemies to save you.
- A lot of times you do not see that the Lord is fighting for you in the spirit; that is where faith comes in, for the Bible is a faith-based book. You have to have faith that the Most High is fighting for you, with angels encamped round about the righteous, as David told us.

  Precepts:
  - **[Psalms 34:7](/bible/psalms/34#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/34#v7)</sup> The angel of the Lord encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them.

    The angel of the Lord encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them.

**[Deuteronomy 20:5-7](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v5)</sup> And the officers shall speak unto the people, saying, What man is there that hath built a new house, and hath not dedicated it? let him go and return to his house, lest he die in the battle, and another man dedicate it.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v6)</sup> And what man is he that hath planted a vineyard, and hath not yet eaten of it? let him also go and return unto his house, lest he die in the battle, and another man eat of it.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v7)</sup> And what man is there that hath betrothed a wife, and hath not taken her? let him go and return unto his house, lest he die in the battle, and another man take her.

- The officers sent home the man that had built a new house and not dedicated it, the man that had planted a vineyard and not eaten of it, and the man that had betrothed a wife and not taken her, lest he die in the battle and another man take his portion.
- You could very well die fighting for your people, so go enjoy these things first: dedicate your new house unto the Lord, eat the fruits of your labour from your vineyard, take your wife, then come to war for your people.

**[Deuteronomy 20:8](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v8)</sup> And the officers shall speak further unto the people, and they shall say, What man is there that is fearful and fainthearted? let him go and return unto his house, lest his brethren’s heart faint as well as his heart.

- The fearful and fainthearted man was also sent home, lest his brethren's heart faint as well as his heart. The spirit of fear spreads: one man's fear makes the next man wonder what he knows, and soon the whole company questions the battle.

**[Deuteronomy 20:9](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v9)</sup> And it shall be, when the officers have made an end of speaking unto the people, that they shall make captains of the armies to lead the people.

- When the officers finished speaking, they made captains of the armies to lead the people, because you cannot have order without a ranking structure. Everybody cannot be calling the shots; there is one shot caller and the people follow, and that same order set in Moses' time is why a body under rank is proficient in everything it does.

**[Deuteronomy 20:10-11](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v10)</sup> When thou comest nigh unto a city to fight against it, then proclaim peace unto it.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v11)</sup> And it shall be, if it make thee answer of peace, and open unto thee, then it shall be, that all the people that is found therein shall be tributaries unto thee, and they shall serve thee.

- When thou comest nigh unto a city to fight against it, first proclaim peace unto it. If it answered peace and opened, all the people found in it became tributaries unto Israel and served them.
- In this time Israel had the power to make nations tributaries. Right now we are not even a nation, just a people with Bibles and nothing established; but wait until Christ comes, for Israel will be established as a nation again and rule the earth, and the nations will be tributaries.

**[Deuteronomy 20:12-14](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v12)</sup> And if it will make no peace with thee, but will make war against thee, then thou shalt besiege it:
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v13)</sup> And when the Lord thy God hath delivered it into thine hands, thou shalt smite every male thereof with the edge of the sword:
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v14)</sup> But the women, and the little ones, and the cattle, and all that is in the city, even all the spoil thereof, shalt thou take unto thyself; and thou shalt eat the spoil of thine enemies, which the Lord thy God hath given thee.

- Israel came to a land with terms: agree, pay the tribute, and there is peace. If the city would make no peace but made war, Israel besieged it, and when the Lord delivered it into their hands, every male was smitten with the edge of the sword.
- The women, the little ones, the cattle, and all the spoil of the city Israel took unto themselves and ate the spoil of their enemies which the Lord their God had given them.

**[Deuteronomy 20:15-18](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v15)</sup> Thus shalt thou do unto all the cities which are very far off from thee, which are not of the cities of these nations.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v16)</sup> But of the cities of these people, which the Lord thy God doth give thee for an inheritance, thou shalt save alive nothing that breatheth:
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v17)</sup> But thou shalt utterly destroy them; namely, the Hittites, and the Amorites, the Canaanites, and the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites; as the Lord thy God hath commanded thee:
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v18)</sup> That they teach you not to do after all their abominations, which they have done unto their gods; so should ye sin against the Lord your God.

- That was the rule for the cities very far off. But of the cities of the nations given Israel for an inheritance, thou shalt save alive nothing that breatheth; everything had to die, from humans to animals.
- The Hittites, the Amorites, the Canaanites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites were to be utterly destroyed for a stated purpose: that they teach you not to do after all their abominations which they have done unto their gods, their whoredoms, their idolatry, and their wicked ways of living, so that Israel sin not against the Lord.

**[Deuteronomy 20:19-20](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v19)</sup> When thou shalt besiege a city a long time, in making war against it to take it, thou shalt not destroy the trees thereof by forcing an axe against them: for thou mayest eat of them, and thou shalt not cut them down (for the tree of the field is man’s life) to employ them in the siege:
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/deuteronomy/20#v20)</sup> Only the trees which thou knowest that they be not trees for meat, thou shalt destroy and cut them down; and thou shalt build bulwarks against the city that maketh war with thee, until it be subdued.

- In a long siege the trees for meat were not to be destroyed by forcing an axe against them, for thou mayest eat of them; the tree of the field is man's life.
- Only the trees known not to be trees for meat could be cut down to build bulwarks against the city that maketh war with thee, until it be subdued.

# Deuteronomy 21: The Slain Man, the Firstborn, and the Rebellious Son

Read the chapter → [Deuteronomy 21](/bible/deuteronomy/21)

**[Deuteronomy 21:1-9](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v1)</sup> If one be found slain in the land which the Lord thy God giveth thee to possess it, lying in the field, and it be not known who hath slain him:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v2)</sup> Then thy elders and thy judges shall come forth, and they shall measure unto the cities which are round about him that is slain:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v3)</sup> And it shall be, that the city which is next unto the slain man, even the elders of that city shall take an heifer, which hath not been wrought with, and which hath not drawn in the yoke;
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v4)</sup> And the elders of that city shall bring down the heifer unto a rough valley, which is neither eared nor sown, and shall strike off the heifer’s neck there in the valley:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v5)</sup> And the priests the sons of Levi shall come near; for them the Lord thy God hath chosen to minister unto him, and to bless in the name of the Lord; and by their word shall every controversy and every stroke be tried:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v6)</sup> And all the elders of that city, that are next unto the slain man, shall wash their hands over the heifer that is beheaded in the valley:
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v7)</sup> And they shall answer and say, Our hands have not shed this blood, neither have our eyes seen it.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v8)</sup> Be merciful, O Lord, unto thy people Israel, whom thou hast redeemed, and lay not innocent blood unto thy people of Israel’s charge. And the blood shall be forgiven them.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v9)</sup> So shalt thou put away the guilt of innocent blood from among you, when thou shalt do that which is right in the sight of the Lord.

- When a man was found slain in the field and it was not known who slew him, the elders and judges measured unto the cities round about the slain man, and the elders of the nearest city took a heifer which had not been wrought with nor drawn in the yoke down to a rough valley, neither eared nor sown, and struck off the heifer's neck there.
- The priests the sons of Levi came near, for by their word shall every controversy and every stroke be tried, and the elders of that city washed their hands over the beheaded heifer, answering, our hands have not shed this blood, neither have our eyes seen it.
- The prayer was, be merciful, O Lord, unto thy people whom thou hast redeemed, and lay not innocent blood unto thy people of Israel's charge; so the guilt of innocent blood was put away from among them when they did that which is right in the sight of the Lord.

**[Deuteronomy 21:10-14](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v10)</sup> When thou goest forth to war against thine enemies, and the Lord thy God hath delivered them into thine hands, and thou hast taken them captive,
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v11)</sup> And seest among the captives a beautiful woman, and hast a desire unto her, that thou wouldest have her to thy wife;
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v12)</sup> Then thou shalt bring her home to thine house; and she shall shave her head, and pare her nails;
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v13)</sup> And she shall put the raiment of her captivity from off her, and shall remain in thine house, and bewail her father and her mother a full month: and after that thou shalt go in unto her, and be her husband, and she shall be thy wife.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v14)</sup> And it shall be, if thou have no delight in her, then thou shalt let her go whither she will; but thou shalt not sell her at all for money, thou shalt not make merchandise of her, because thou hast humbled her.

- When Israel conquered a city and a man saw among the captives a beautiful woman and desired her to wife, these are the things that had to be done at that time: bring her home, and she shall shave her head, pare her nails, and put the raiment of her captivity from off her; she was humbled.
- She was to remain in the house and bewail her father and her mother a full month, because her father and mother were put to death; you had to give her time to mourn her family. After that full month he could go in unto her and be her husband.
- If he had no delight in her, he had to let her go whither she would; he could not sell her at all for money nor make merchandise of her, because he had humbled her.
- This is the law for when Israel came out of Egypt and had the power to subdue the nations; it is not talking about now, when we are the conquered nation. Confused Israelites read scriptures like this and formulate strange doctrines, and it is nonsense; we do not have that power now.

**[Deuteronomy 21:15-17](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v15)</sup> If a man have two wives, one beloved, and another hated, and they have born him children, both the beloved and the hated; and if the firstborn son be hers that was hated:
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v16)</sup> Then it shall be, when he maketh his sons to inherit that which he hath, that he may not make the son of the beloved firstborn before the son of the hated, which is indeed the firstborn:
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v17)</sup> But he shall acknowledge the son of the hated for the firstborn, by giving him a double portion of all that he hath: for he is the beginning of his strength; the right of the firstborn is his.

- This deals with a man with sons from two wives, one beloved and one hated. When he makes his sons inherit, he may not make the son of the beloved firstborn before the son of the hated, which is indeed the firstborn.
- One son may be more beloved than the other, but at the end of the day the firstborn is still the firstborn. The father must acknowledge the son of the hated for the firstborn by giving him a double portion of all that he hath, for he is the beginning of his strength; the right of the firstborn is his.

**[Deuteronomy 21:18-21](/bible/deuteronomy/21#v18)**

If your brother is away and his gate is broken and his herd is going out, you cannot say that is not my business; you get up and bring them again unto your brother, even if you have to mend his gate until he gets back.

Thou mayest not hide thyself, like the man who puts his foot over a dropped twenty and helps look for it; keeping what you saw your brother lose is hiding thyself from him.
