## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Colossians 2 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-23, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Colossians 2:1](/bible/colossians/2#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/colossians/2#v1)</sup> For I would that ye knew what great conflict I have for you, and for them at Laodicea, and for as many as have not seen my face in the flesh;

- Laodicea is in Asia Minor. The Jews of that western diaspora were Greek-speaking, not Hebrew-speaking. Paul himself was born in Tarsus, and his father sent him to Jerusalem to learn Hebrew and become a scholar in the scriptures.
- “And for as many as have not seen my face in the flesh.” The book of Acts shows Paul in Asia Minor, but it never says specifically that he went to Laodicea. On the map of Asia Minor lie Pergamos, Thyatira, Sardis, Smyrna, Philadelphia, Ephesus and Laodicea, and it was one of Paul’s companions who dealt with Laodicea directly. Paul had a group of fellow soldiers who were about the work, not pressured into it, about God’s business.

> <sup>[2](/bible/colossians/2#v2)</sup> That their hearts might be comforted, being knit together in love, and unto all riches of the full assurance of understanding, to the acknowledgement of the mystery of God, and of the Father, and of Christ;

All nations do it; it is mainly black people who always have an issue, always have a problem. Only the word of God can knit us together as one man.

- “To the acknowledgement of the mystery of God, and of the Father, and of Christ.” That mystery goes back to the chapter before, Colossians 1:24-29, where Paul explains it. And the Godhead of verse 9 goes back to the bottom of this verse: the Father and Christ.

**[Colossians 2:3](/bible/colossians/2#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/colossians/2#v3)</sup> In whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.

- In the Father and in Christ “are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.” That hidden wisdom is what Paul speaks of in 1 Corinthians 2: not the wisdom of men, not the wisdom of this world, but the wisdom of God in a mystery.
- The Pharisees taught that the treasures of wisdom were in animal sacrifice. Paul is saying no: the treasures of wisdom and understanding are through Christ.

**[Colossians 2:4](/bible/colossians/2#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/colossians/2#v4)</sup> And this I say, lest any man should beguile you with enticing words.

- “Lest any man should beguile you.” To beguile is to charm, enchant or attract someone, sometimes in a deceptive or cunning way. The word bridges a line between friendly charm and sneaky manipulation. Paul is warning lest any man should beguile you, deceive you, trick you.
- “With enticing words”: those enticing words go back to the wisdom of this world in 1 Corinthians 2, man’s wisdom, which can beguile and deceive you.
- The initial context Paul was dealing with is the scribes and Pharisees, who were using the same scriptures to manipulate the followers of Christ into going back to animal sacrifice. They said salvation comes through sacrifice. That is how they beguiled the people back then.

**[Colossians 2:11](/bible/colossians/2#v11)**

- “In putting off the body of the sins of the flesh”: Paul explains it himself. “By the circumcision of Christ”: let Christ circumcise your mind. His words must circumcise our minds.
- “In whom also ye are circumcised with the circumcision made without hands”: this is talking about the new bodies. Circumcision is the clipping of the foreskin; the skin of the child’s penis is removed, and that removal makes him clean. Circumcision with hands is taking a sharp object and cutting your foreskin off. This circumcision is without hands: a spiritual circumcision.

> <sup>[17](/bible/colossians/2#v17)</sup> Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.

**[Colossians 2:23](/bible/colossians/2#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/colossians/2#v23)</sup> Which things have indeed a shew of wisdom in will worship, and humility, and neglecting of the body; not in any honour to the satisfying of the flesh.

- “And neglecting of the body”: that deals with fasting.
- “Not in any honour to the satisfying of the flesh.” That is hard wording. Its meaning is that those things were of no value against the indulgence of the flesh. These men were fasting, praying and sacrificing, yet they were still sexually indulgent all over the place, still dealing with all the other temptations. When it came to satisfying the flesh, they had no honour. It was sex, sex and more sex.
