## Introduction

This session covers 1 Kings chapters 17 through 20: Elijah the Tishbite pronouncing the drought upon Ahab, the ravens feeding him at the brook, the widow of Zarephath whose barrel of meal and cruise of oil did not fail, the raising of her dead son, the showdown with the prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel, the fire that fell from heaven, the return of the rain, Elijah's flight from Jezebel and the still small voice at Horeb, the calling of Elisha, and the two victories over Ben-hadad of Syria that Ahab squandered by his own wickedness. The thread that ties it together is the power of the Most High: he commands the animals, the rain, life and death, and fire from heaven, and he tries a man by his laws, giving him time to repent, but gives the rebellious over to their own ruin.

**[1 Kings 17:1](/bible/1-kings/17#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-kings/17#v1)</sup> And Elijah the Tishbite, who was of the inhabitants of Gilead, said unto Ahab, As the Lord God of Israel liveth, before whom I stand, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, but according to my word.

- Elijah the Tishbite, of the inhabitants of Gilead, said unto Ahab that as the Lord God of Israel liveth, before whom he stands, there shall not be dew nor rain these years but according to his word. Elijah prayed and it was no rain.
- The teaching opens by framing the whole session on the power of the Most High: he has power over everything, the animals and all creation, and he set that power on display through his prophet.

- The brook dried up for want of rain, and the Lord sent Elijah all the way across the land to Zarephath, which belongeth to Zidon on the coast, where he had commanded a widow woman to sustain him. From the east side by Jordan to the west by Sidon was a hard walk of a Sabbath day's journey or more, with no water anywhere.
- Coming to the gate, Elijah asked the widow for water and a morsel of bread, and she answered that she had but a handful of meal in a barrel and a little oil in a cruise, gathering two sticks to dress a last meal for herself and her son that they might eat it and die.
- Elijah told her to make him a little cake first and after that for herself and her son, for the Lord God of Israel said the barrel of meal would not waste nor the cruise of oil fail until the day the Lord sent rain. She obeyed, and she and Elijah and her house did eat many days. For trusting in the Lord and obeying, she was sustained.
- The instruction to feed the man of God first is a precept: the man eats first. Sisters must trust in the Lord when he tells them to reverence their husbands and be submissive in all things, and not lean to their own understanding.

- Elijah took the child up to the loft, laid him on his own bed, and cried unto the Lord, stretching himself upon the child three times and praying that the child's soul come into him again. The Lord heard the voice of Elijah, and the soul of the child came into him again and he revived.
- Elijah delivered the living child to his mother, and she said, Now by this I know that thou art a man of God and that the word of the Lord in thy mouth is truth.
- All praises that Elijah was there to call upon the Lord, for it was not the young man's time to go. A man's days are determined and appointed, and he cannot pass the bound the Most High has set; because it was not the child's time, the Lord let his soul return.

Elijah swore by the Lord of hosts that he would surely show himself to Ahab that day, and so Obadiah went to Ahab, and Ahab came to meet Elijah.

- When Ahab saw Elijah he said, Art thou he that troubleth Israel? That is the mind of a wicked person: he blamed the trouble on the ordained prophet of God rather than on his own wickedness. When the spirit is gone, wisdom cannot enter a malicious soul, so Ahab had thoughts without understanding.
- Elijah answered, I have not troubled Israel, but thou and thy father's house, in that ye have forsaken the commandments of the Lord and thou hast followed Baalim. It was Ahab following the sin of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, worshipping the golden calves, that brought the trouble.
- Elijah commanded that all Israel be gathered to Mount Carmel with the four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal and the four hundred prophets of the groves that eat at Jezebel's table. Jezebel was the one feeding these eight hundred and fifty false prophets and encouraging them, and Ahab never checked her.

Picture the scene: eight hundred and fifty men doing this, running wild, while Elijah sat back watching the madness.

They cried aloud and cut themselves with knives and lancets till the blood gushed out, for cutting themselves was part of their false worship whenever they called on Baal.
- From midday past the time of the evening sacrifice there was neither voice nor any to answer nor any that regarded. No one answered because the Most High will not give his glory to another, and the gods of the nations are idols that are not real; they are made up by people who have no God, so when they call on them the idols cannot answer.

> <sup>[39](/bible/1-kings/20#v39)</sup> And as the king passed by, he cried unto the king: and he said, Thy servant went out into the midst of the battle; and, behold, a man turned aside, and brought a man unto me, and said, Keep this man: if by any means he be missing, then shall thy life be for his life, or else thou shalt pay a talent of silver.
>
> <sup>[40](/bible/1-kings/20#v40)</sup> And as thy servant was busy here and there, he was gone. And the king of Israel said unto him, So shall thy judgment be; thyself hast decided it.
>
> <sup>[41](/bible/1-kings/20#v41)</sup> And he hasted, and took the ashes away from his face; and the king of Israel discerned him that he was of the prophets.
>
> <sup>[42](/bible/1-kings/20#v42)</sup> And he said unto him, Thus saith the Lord, Because thou hast let go out of thy hand a man whom I appointed to utter destruction, therefore thy life shall go for his life, and thy people for his people.
>
> <sup>[43](/bible/1-kings/20#v43)</sup> And the king of Israel went to his house heavy and displeased, and came to Samaria.

- A son of the prophets told his neighbor to smite him in the word of the Lord; the man refused, and the prophet said a lion would slay him for not obeying the voice of the Lord, and a lion found him and slew him. Another man smote and wounded him, and the prophet disguised himself with ashes on his face and waited for the king, hiding the certain look that prophets carry, the same way we carry a certain look today.
- The prophet cried to the king a parable of a soldier charged to keep a prisoner on pain of his own life, who let the man escape; the king of Israel judged, So shall thy judgment be, thou thyself hast decided it. Then the prophet took the ashes away and Ahab discerned he was one of the prophets.
- The prophet declared, Thus saith the Lord, because thou hast let go a man whom I appointed to utter destruction, thy life shall go for his life and thy people for his people. Ben-hadad was supposed to die at war with Ahab, tied to the word that Hazael would move into the kingship of Syria, but Ahab made a covenant with him instead of killing him.
- The king of Israel went to his house heavy and displeased and came to Samaria, crying again over a just judgment because he would not obey the voice of the Lord. The lesson for us is the same: as it is written, so shall we do, that we may be justified and our life not be taken from us for rebellion against the Lord.
