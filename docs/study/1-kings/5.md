Read the chapter → [1 Kings 5](/bible/1-kings/5)

This session moves through 1 Kings five through eight, the preparation and building of the temple of the Most High. Chapter five shows Solomon reaching out to Hiram king of Tyre for cedar out of Lebanon, a league made possible because David his father had already made a name and a friendship there. Chapter six lays out the building of the house itself, its dimensions, chambers, and gold overlay. Chapter seven turns to Solomon's own house and the brass work of the temple, the pillars, the molten sea, and the bases. Chapter eight brings the ark into the most holy place and records Solomon's long prayer of dedication before the whole congregation of Israel. The teaching stays close to the reading and draws in Ecclesiasticus, Chronicles, Leviticus, Hebrews, and Zechariah to open up what is being built and why.

**[1 Kings 5:1](/bible/1-kings/5#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/1-kings/5#v1)</sup> And Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants unto Solomon; for he had heard that they had anointed him king in the room of his father: for Hiram was ever a lover of David.

- Hiram king of Tyre sends his servants to Solomon because he had heard that Solomon was anointed king in the room of his father. Hiram was ever a lover of David, so the friendship carries over to the son.
- Hiram was king of Tyre, a Zidonian by his father and connected by lineage to the tribe of Naphtali on his mother's side, which is drawn out later in the reading.

**[1 Kings 5:2-6](/bible/1-kings/5#v2)**
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-kings/5#v2)</sup> And Solomon sent to Hiram, saying,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-kings/5#v3)</sup> Thou knowest how that David my father could not build an house unto the name of the Lord his God for the wars which were about him on every side, until the Lord put them under the soles of his feet.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-kings/5#v4)</sup> But now the Lord my God hath given me rest on every side, so that there is neither adversary nor evil occurrent.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-kings/5#v5)</sup> And, behold, I purpose to build an house unto the name of the Lord my God, as the Lord spake unto David my father, saying, Thy son, whom I will set upon thy throne in thy room, he shall build an house unto my name.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-kings/5#v6)</sup> Now therefore command thou that they hew me cedar trees out of Lebanon; and my servants shall be with thy servants: and unto thee will I give hire for thy servants according to all that thou shalt appoint: for thou knowest that there is not among us any that can skill to hew timber like unto the Sidonians.

- Solomon sends word to Hiram that David his father could not build a house unto the name of the Lord because of the wars about him on every side, until the Most High put his enemies under the soles of his feet. Now the Most High has given rest, with neither adversary nor evil occurrence, so Solomon purposes to build the house as the Lord had spoken concerning David's son.
- Solomon asks that cedar trees be hewn out of Lebanon, for the Zidonians had a gift of skill in timber above any other nation, and the best cedar came from Lebanon. Lebanon lay to the northwest on the coast, where the best trees grew, and Solomon knew his own people had none who could hew timber like the Zidonians.

**[1 Kings 5:7-9](/bible/1-kings/5#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-kings/5#v7)</sup> And it came to pass, when Hiram heard the words of Solomon, that he rejoiced greatly, and said, Blessed be the Lord this day, which hath given unto David a wise son over this great people.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/1-kings/5#v8)</sup> And Hiram sent to Solomon, saying, I have considered the things which thou sentest to me for: and I will do all thy desire concerning timber of cedar, and concerning timber of fir.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-kings/5#v9)</sup> My servants shall bring them down from Lebanon unto the sea: and I will convey them by sea in floats unto the place that thou shalt appoint me, and will cause them to be discharged there, and thou shalt receive them: and thou shalt accomplish my desire, in giving food for my household.

- When Hiram hears the words of Solomon he rejoices greatly and blesses the Lord who has given David a wise son over this great people. He agrees to supply timber of cedar and fir according to all Solomon's desire.
- Hiram's servants would bring the timber down from Lebanon to the sea and float it by sea to the place Solomon appointed, and there it would be discharged and received. This was a trade, a league between two peoples.

**[1 Kings 5:10-12](/bible/1-kings/5#v10)**
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-kings/5#v10)</sup> So Hiram gave Solomon cedar trees and fir trees according to all his desire.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-kings/5#v11)</sup> And Solomon gave Hiram twenty thousand measures of wheat for food to his household, and twenty measures of pure oil: thus gave Solomon to Hiram year by year.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-kings/5#v12)</sup> And the Lord gave Solomon wisdom, as he promised him: and there was peace between Hiram and Solomon; and they two made a league together.

- Hiram gives Solomon cedar and fir according to all his desire, and Solomon gives Hiram twenty thousand measures of wheat for food to his household and twenty measures of pure oil, year by year. The Most High gives Solomon wisdom as he promised, and there is peace between Hiram and Solomon, and they two make a league together.
- This is why a good name and inheritance matter. Solomon was able to say deal with me as you dealt with David my father, and slide into the benefit of the friendship his father had built. It is wise to be at peace with the nations where we can, dealing righteously, being wise as serpents and harmless as doves.

**[1 Kings 5:13-18](/bible/1-kings/5#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/1-kings/5#v13)</sup> And king Solomon raised a levy out of all Israel; and the levy was thirty thousand men.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-kings/5#v14)</sup> And he sent them to Lebanon, ten thousand a month by courses: a month they were in Lebanon, and two months at home: and Adoniram was over the levy.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-kings/5#v15)</sup> And Solomon had threescore and ten thousand that bare burdens, and fourscore thousand hewers in the mountains;
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/1-kings/5#v16)</sup> Beside the chief of Solomon’s officers which were over the work, three thousand and three hundred, which ruled over the people that wrought in the work.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/1-kings/5#v17)</sup> And the king commanded, and they brought great stones, costly stones, and hewed stones, to lay the foundation of the house.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-kings/5#v18)</sup> And Solomon’s builders and Hiram’s builders did hew them, and the stonesquarers: so they prepared timber and stones to build the house.

- Solomon raises a levy out of all Israel of thirty thousand men and sends them to Lebanon ten thousand a month by course, a month in Lebanon and two months at home. A levy here is an enlistment for service, the men gathered to bring the wood back to Jerusalem, and Adoniram was over the levy.
- Solomon had order in the work, with bearers of burdens, hewers in the mountains, and above them the chief of his officers, thirty-three hundred who ruled over the people that wrought. The temple was built successfully because our forefathers had a spirit to work together under rank and structure without murmuring over who did which task.
