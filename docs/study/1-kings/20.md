**[1 Kings 20:1-6](/bible/1-kings/20#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-kings/20#v1)</sup> And Ben–hadad the king of Syria gathered all his host together: and there were thirty and two kings with him, and horses, and chariots: and he went up and besieged Samaria, and warred against it.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-kings/20#v2)</sup> And he sent messengers to Ahab king of Israel into the city, and said unto him, Thus saith Ben–hadad,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-kings/20#v3)</sup> Thy silver and thy gold is mine; thy wives also and thy children, even the goodliest, are mine.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-kings/20#v4)</sup> And the king of Israel answered and said, My lord, O king, according to thy saying, I am thine, and all that I have.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-kings/20#v5)</sup> And the messengers came again, and said, Thus speaketh Ben–hadad, saying, Although I have sent unto thee, saying, Thou shalt deliver me thy silver, and thy gold, and thy wives, and thy children;
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-kings/20#v6)</sup> Yet I will send my servants unto thee to morrow about this time, and they shall search thine house, and the houses of thy servants; and it shall be, that whatsoever is pleasant in thine eyes, they shall put it in their hand, and take it away.

- Ben-hadad the king of Syria gathered all his hosts, with thirty-two kings, horses, and chariots, and besieged Samaria. He sent to Ahab demanding his silver, gold, wives, and children, even the goodliest, and Ahab answered, My lord, O king, I am thine, and all that I have.
- Ben-hadad sent again, saying that his servants would come and search Ahab's house and take away whatsoever was pleasant in his eyes. This siege was Ahab's judgment from the Lord for trying to kill Elijah, for serving Baal, and for letting Jezebel run him and kill the prophets.

**[1 Kings 20:7-12](/bible/1-kings/20#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/1-kings/20#v7)</sup> Then the king of Israel called all the elders of the land, and said, Mark, I pray you, and see how this man seeketh mischief: for he sent unto me for my wives, and for my children, and for my silver, and for my gold; and I denied him not.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/1-kings/20#v8)</sup> And all the elders and all the people said unto him, Hearken not unto him, nor consent.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-kings/20#v9)</sup> Wherefore he said unto the messengers of Ben–hadad, Tell my lord the king, All that thou didst send for to thy servant at the first I will do: but this thing I may not do. And the messengers departed, and brought him word again.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-kings/20#v10)</sup> And Ben–hadad sent unto him, and said, The gods do so unto me, and more also, if the dust of Samaria shall suffice for handfuls for all the people that follow me.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-kings/20#v11)</sup> And the king of Israel answered and said, Tell him, Let not him that girdeth on his harness boast himself as he that putteth it off.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-kings/20#v12)</sup> And it came to pass, when Ben–hadad heard this message, as he was drinking, he and the kings in the pavilions, that he said unto his servants, Set yourselves in array. And they set themselves in array against the city.

- Ahab called the elders of the land, who told him not to hearken nor consent to Ben-hadad's demand, so Ahab sent word that he would grant the first demand but not the second. Ben-hadad swore by his gods to reduce Samaria to dust for handfuls among his people.
- Ahab answered, Let not him that girdeth on his harness boast himself as he that putteth it off. When Ben-hadad heard this while drinking with the kings in the pavilions, he commanded his servants to set themselves in array against the city; Ahab's boast had provoked him to prepare for battle.

**[1 Kings 20:13-21](/bible/1-kings/20#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/1-kings/20#v13)</sup> And, behold, there came a prophet unto Ahab king of Israel, saying, Thus saith the Lord, Hast thou seen all this great multitude? behold, I will deliver it into thine hand this day; and thou shalt know that I am the Lord.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-kings/20#v14)</sup> And Ahab said, By whom? And he said, Thus saith the Lord, Even by the young men of the princes of the provinces. Then he said, Who shall order the battle? And he answered, Thou.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-kings/20#v15)</sup> Then he numbered the young men of the princes of the provinces, and they were two hundred and thirty two: and after them he numbered all the people, even all the children of Israel, being seven thousand.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/1-kings/20#v16)</sup> And they went out at noon. But Ben–hadad was drinking himself drunk in the pavilions, he and the kings, the thirty and two kings that helped him.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/1-kings/20#v17)</sup> And the young men of the princes of the provinces went out first; and Ben–hadad sent out, and they told him, saying, There are men come out of Samaria.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-kings/20#v18)</sup> And he said, Whether they be come out for peace, take them alive; or whether they be come out for war, take them alive.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/1-kings/20#v19)</sup> So these young men of the princes of the provinces came out of the city, and the army which followed them.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/1-kings/20#v20)</sup> And they slew every one his man: and the Syrians fled; and Israel pursued them: and Ben–hadad the king of Syria escaped on an horse with the horsemen.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/1-kings/20#v21)</sup> And the king of Israel went out, and smote the horses and chariots, and slew the Syrians with a great slaughter.

- A prophet came unto Ahab saying, Thus saith the Lord, Hast thou seen all this great multitude? I will deliver it into thine hand this day, and thou shalt know that I am the Lord. Asked by whom, the prophet answered, By the young men of the princes of the provinces, and that Ahab himself should order the battle.
- Two hundred and thirty-two young men of the princes of the provinces went out first, followed by the children of Israel, being seven thousand; they went out at noon while Ben-hadad was drinking himself drunk with the thirty-two kings, taking Israel lightly.
- The young men slew every one his man and the Syrians fled, and Israel pursued them; Ben-hadad escaped on a horse, and the king of Israel smote the horses and chariots and slew the Syrians with a great slaughter. The Most High gave the victory though Israel's numbers were small.

**[1 Kings 20:22-30](/bible/1-kings/20#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/1-kings/20#v22)</sup> And the prophet came to the king of Israel, and said unto him, Go, strengthen thyself, and mark, and see what thou doest: for at the return of the year the king of Syria will come up against thee.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/1-kings/20#v23)</sup> And the servants of the king of Syria said unto him, Their gods are gods of the hills; therefore they were stronger than we; but let us fight against them in the plain, and surely we shall be stronger than they.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/1-kings/20#v24)</sup> And do this thing, Take the kings away, every man out of his place, and put captains in their rooms:
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/1-kings/20#v25)</sup> And number thee an army, like the army that thou hast lost, horse for horse, and chariot for chariot: and we will fight against them in the plain, and surely we shall be stronger than they. And he hearkened unto their voice, and did so.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/1-kings/20#v26)</sup> And it came to pass at the return of the year, that Ben–hadad numbered the Syrians, and went up to Aphek, to fight against Israel.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/1-kings/20#v27)</sup> And the children of Israel were numbered, and were all present, and went against them: and the children of Israel pitched before them like two little flocks of kids; but the Syrians filled the country.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/1-kings/20#v28)</sup> And there came a man of God, and spake unto the king of Israel, and said, Thus saith the Lord, Because the Syrians have said, The Lord is God of the hills, but he is not God of the valleys, therefore will I deliver all this great multitude into thine hand, and ye shall know that I am the Lord.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/1-kings/20#v29)</sup> And they pitched one over against the other seven days. And so it was, that in the seventh day the battle was joined: and the children of Israel slew of the Syrians an hundred thousand footmen in one day.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/1-kings/20#v30)</sup> But the rest fled to Aphek, into the city; and there a wall fell upon twenty and seven thousand of the men that were left. And Ben–hadad fled, and came into the city, into an inner chamber.

- The prophet warned Ahab to strengthen himself and mark, for at the return of the year the king of Syria would come up against him again. The servants of Ben-hadad counseled that Israel's gods were gods of the hills, so they should fight in the plain, replace the kings with captains, and number a fresh army horse for horse and chariot for chariot; Ben-hadad hearkened and did so.
- At the return of the year Ben-hadad came up to fight, and the children of Israel pitched before them like two little flocks of kids while the Syrians filled the country; Israel's army was small compared to the great Syrian host.
- A man of God told Ahab, Because the Syrians have said the Lord is God of the hills but not of the valleys, I will deliver all this great multitude into thine hand, and ye shall know that I am the Lord. Here the Most High was giving Ahab a chance to repent, redeeming him by showing his power with fewer numbers; yet Ahab still did not repent, did not check Jezebel, and did not destroy the idols of Baal, for his sin had blinded him.
- Israel and Syria pitched one against the other seven days, and on the seventh day the battle was joined, and the children of Israel slew of the Syrians a hundred thousand footmen in one day. The rest fled to Aphek, where a wall fell upon twenty-seven thousand of the men left. Seven thousand against a hundred thousand, and the numbers did not matter because the Lord was with Israel; but Ahab still did not repent.

**[1 Kings 20:31-34](/bible/1-kings/20#v31)**

> <sup>[31](/bible/1-kings/20#v31)</sup> And his servants said unto him, Behold now, we have heard that the kings of the house of Israel are merciful kings: let us, I pray thee, put sackcloth on our loins, and ropes upon our heads, and go out to the king of Israel: peradventure he will save thy life.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/1-kings/20#v32)</sup> So they girded sackcloth on their loins, and put ropes on their heads, and came to the king of Israel, and said, Thy servant Ben–hadad saith, I pray thee, let me live. And he said, Is he yet alive? he is my brother.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/1-kings/20#v33)</sup> Now the men did diligently observe whether any thing would come from him, and did hastily catch it: and they said, Thy brother Ben–hadad. Then he said, Go ye, bring him. Then Ben–hadad came forth to him; and he caused him to come up into the chariot.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/1-kings/20#v34)</sup> And Ben–hadad said unto him, The cities, which my father took from thy father, I will restore; and thou shalt make streets for thee in Damascus, as my father made in Samaria. Then said Ahab, I will send thee away with this covenant. So he made a covenant with him, and sent him away.

- Ben-hadad fled into an inner chamber, and his servants, having heard that the kings of the house of Israel are merciful, put sackcloth on their loins and ropes on their heads and went to plead for his life. Ahab, hearing that Ben-hadad lived, called him his brother and had him brought up into his chariot.
- Look at Ahab's wickedness: this was a Syrian who was just trying to kill him last year, and he brought him up into the king's chariot. Ben-hadad offered to restore the cities his father had taken and give Ahab streets in Damascus, and Ahab said, I will send thee away with this covenant, and he made a covenant with the heathen and sent him away.

**[1 Kings 20:35-43](/bible/1-kings/20#v35)**

> <sup>[35](/bible/1-kings/20#v35)</sup> And a certain man of the sons of the prophets said unto his neighbour in the word of the Lord, Smite me, I pray thee. And the man refused to smite him.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/1-kings/20#v36)</sup> Then said he unto him, Because thou hast not obeyed the voice of the Lord, behold, as soon as thou art departed from me, a lion shall slay thee. And as soon as he was departed from him, a lion found him, and slew him.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/1-kings/20#v37)</sup> Then he found another man, and said, Smite me, I pray thee. And the man smote him, so that in smiting he wounded him.
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/1-kings/20#v38)</sup> So the prophet departed, and waited for the king by the way, and disguised himself with ashes upon his face.
>
> <sup>[39](/bible/1-kings/20#v39)</sup> And as the king passed by, he cried unto the king: and he said, Thy servant went out into the midst of the battle; and, behold, a man turned aside, and brought a man unto me, and said, Keep this man: if by any means he be missing, then shall thy life be for his life, or else thou shalt pay a talent of silver.
>
> <sup>[40](/bible/1-kings/20#v40)</sup> And as thy servant was busy here and there, he was gone. And the king of Israel said unto him, So shall thy judgment be; thyself hast decided it.
>
> <sup>[41](/bible/1-kings/20#v41)</sup> And he hasted, and took the ashes away from his face; and the king of Israel discerned him that he was of the prophets.
>
> <sup>[42](/bible/1-kings/20#v42)</sup> And he said unto him, Thus saith the Lord, Because thou hast let go out of thy hand a man whom I appointed to utter destruction, therefore thy life shall go for his life, and thy people for his people.
>
> <sup>[43](/bible/1-kings/20#v43)</sup> And the king of Israel went to his house heavy and displeased, and came to Samaria.

- A son of the prophets told his neighbor to smite him in the word of the Lord; the man refused, and the prophet said a lion would slay him for not obeying the voice of the Lord, and a lion found him and slew him. Another man smote and wounded him, and the prophet disguised himself with ashes on his face and waited for the king, hiding the certain look that prophets carry, the same way we carry a certain look today.
- The prophet cried to the king a parable of a soldier charged to keep a prisoner on pain of his own life, who let the man escape; the king of Israel judged, So shall thy judgment be, thou thyself hast decided it. Then the prophet took the ashes away and Ahab discerned he was one of the prophets.
- The prophet declared, Thus saith the Lord, because thou hast let go a man whom I appointed to utter destruction, thy life shall go for his life and thy people for his people. Ben-hadad was supposed to die at war with Ahab, tied to the word that Hazael would move into the kingship of Syria, but Ahab made a covenant with him instead of killing him.
- The king of Israel went to his house heavy and displeased and came to Samaria, crying again over a just judgment because he would not obey the voice of the Lord. The lesson for us is the same: as it is written, so shall we do, that we may be justified and our life not be taken from us for rebellion against the Lord.
