## Introduction

This session covers 1 Kings 9-12. Solomon has finished the house of the Most High and his own house, and the Most High appears to him a second time to renew the promise and to warn him plainly that if Israel turns to other gods they will be cut off from the land and become a proverb and a byword among all people. From there the reading moves through the greatness of Solomon's kingdom, his gold, his navy, and the visit of the queen of Sheba, and then into his fall, for Solomon loved many strange women and they turned his heart after their gods. Because of that one sin the kingdom is torn, ten tribes given to Jeroboam and two kept for the house of David for David's sake. The reading closes with Rehoboam rejecting the counsel of the elders and Jeroboam setting up golden calves, so that Israel is divided to this day. Solomon built the first temple in Jerusalem, and Solomon was a black man, which the scripture itself declares.

- When Solomon had finished the building of the house of the Most High and the king's house and all he desired to do, the Most High appeared to him the second time, as he had appeared at Gibeon.
- The so-called Jews boast of the temple and their heritage in a land that does not belong to them, but the man who built the first temple was a black man, and the scripture says so plainly.

This shows the Most High is not all love, for it says therefore he brought upon them all this evil.

Solomon bought the best of everything, but there comes a point where riches and power can pervert the mind if a man is not grounded, and that is what begins to happen.
