Read the chapter → [1 Kings 5](/bible/1-kings/5)

This session moves through 1 Kings five through eight, the preparation and building of the temple of the Most High. Chapter five shows Solomon reaching out to Hiram king of Tyre for cedar out of Lebanon, a league made possible because David his father had already made a name and a friendship there. Chapter six lays out the building of the house itself, its dimensions, chambers, and gold overlay. Chapter seven turns to Solomon's own house and the brass work of the temple, the pillars, the molten sea, and the bases. Chapter eight brings the ark into the most holy place and records Solomon's long prayer of dedication before the whole congregation of Israel. The teaching stays close to the reading and draws in Ecclesiasticus, Chronicles, Leviticus, Hebrews, and Zechariah to open up what is being built and why.

**[1 Kings 5:1](/bible/1-kings/5#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/1-kings/5#v1)</sup> And Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants unto Solomon; for he had heard that they had anointed him king in the room of his father: for Hiram was ever a lover of David.

- Hiram king of Tyre sends his servants to Solomon because he had heard that Solomon was anointed king in the room of his father. Hiram was ever a lover of David, so the friendship carries over to the son.
- Hiram was king of Tyre, a Zidonian by his father and connected by lineage to the tribe of Naphtali on his mother's side, which is drawn out later in the reading.

- Solomon sends word to Hiram that David his father could not build a house unto the name of the Lord because of the wars about him on every side, until the Most High put his enemies under the soles of his feet. Now the Most High has given rest, with neither adversary nor evil occurrence, so Solomon purposes to build the house as the Lord had spoken concerning David's son.
- Solomon asks that cedar trees be hewn out of Lebanon, for the Zidonians had a gift of skill in timber above any other nation, and the best cedar came from Lebanon. Lebanon lay to the northwest on the coast, where the best trees grew, and Solomon knew his own people had none who could hew timber like the Zidonians.

- When Hiram hears the words of Solomon he rejoices greatly and blesses the Lord who has given David a wise son over this great people. He agrees to supply timber of cedar and fir according to all Solomon's desire.
- Hiram's servants would bring the timber down from Lebanon to the sea and float it by sea to the place Solomon appointed, and there it would be discharged and received. This was a trade, a league between two peoples.

It is wise to be at peace with the nations where we can, dealing righteously, being wise as serpents and harmless as doves.

    > <sup>[23](/bible/zechariah/8#v23)</sup> Thus saith the Lord of hosts; In those days it shall come to pass, that ten men shall take hold out of all languages of the nations, even shall take hold of the skirt of him that is a Jew, saying, We will go with you: for we have heard that God is with you.

    In those days ten men out of all languages of the nations shall take hold of the skirt of him that is a Jew, saying, We will go with you, for we have heard that God is with you, the same joining of the nations to the house of Jacob that Solomon foresaw.

**[1 Kings 8:46-53](/bible/1-kings/8#v46)**
> <sup>[46](/bible/1-kings/8#v46)</sup> If they sin against thee, (for there is no man that sinneth not,) and thou be angry with them, and deliver them to the enemy, so that they carry them away captives unto the land of the enemy, far or near;
>
> <sup>[47](/bible/1-kings/8#v47)</sup> Yet if they shall bethink themselves in the land whither they were carried captives, and repent, and make supplication unto thee in the land of them that carried them captives, saying, We have sinned, and have done perversely, we have committed wickedness;
>
> <sup>[48](/bible/1-kings/8#v48)</sup> And so return unto thee with all their heart, and with all their soul, in the land of their enemies, which led them away captive, and pray unto thee toward their land, which thou gavest unto their fathers, the city which thou hast chosen, and the house which I have built for thy name:
>
> <sup>[49](/bible/1-kings/8#v49)</sup> Then hear thou their prayer and their supplication in heaven thy dwelling place, and maintain their cause,
>
> <sup>[50](/bible/1-kings/8#v50)</sup> And forgive thy people that have sinned against thee, and all their transgressions wherein they have transgressed against thee, and give them compassion before them who carried them captive, that they may have compassion on them:
>
> <sup>[51](/bible/1-kings/8#v51)</sup> For they be thy people, and thine inheritance, which thou broughtest forth out of Egypt, from the midst of the furnace of iron:
>
> <sup>[52](/bible/1-kings/8#v52)</sup> That thine eyes may be open unto the supplication of thy servant, and unto the supplication of thy people Israel, to hearken unto them in all that they call for unto thee.
>
> <sup>[53](/bible/1-kings/8#v53)</sup> For thou didst separate them from among all the people of the earth, to be thine inheritance, as thou spakest by the hand of Moses thy servant, when thou broughtest our fathers out of Egypt, O Lord God.

- Solomon prays for the day Israel sins and is carried captive to the land of the enemy, far or near, that if they bethink themselves, repent, and turn again with all their heart, confessing plainly the wickedness they have done, the Lord hear their prayer, maintain their cause, and forgive. He asks that the Most High give them compassion before those who carried them captive.
- No other nation on the earth has this way out, that when it sins it can always turn to the Most High, face the east, and pray for forgiveness, because he separated Israel from among all people of the earth to be his inheritance, brought forth out of the furnace of iron of Egypt. The luxuries Israel is afforded even in captivity are because the Lord is on their side.

He asks that the Lord be with them as with their fathers, incline their hearts to keep his commandments, statutes, and judgments.
- He asks that his words of supplication be near the Lord day and night, that the Most High maintain the cause of his people at all times, that all the people of the earth may know the Lord is God and there is none else. Therefore let the heart be perfect with the Lord, to walk in his statutes and keep his commandments.
