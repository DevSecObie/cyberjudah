Read the chapter → [1 Kings 22](/bible/1-kings/22)

This session covers [1 Kings 22](/bible/1-kings/22), the last chapter of Ahab's reign. Ahab and Jehoshaphat join to take Ramoth-gilead, and four hundred prophets speak with one mouth to send the king up to battle. The teaching follows how Jehoshaphat presses for a true prophet of the Most High, how Micaiah reveals the lying spirit the Most High put in the mouth of the prophets, and how the judgment already pronounced on Ahab is fulfilled when the dogs lick up his blood. The chapter closes with the reign of Jehoshaphat over Judah and the beginning of Ahaziah over Israel.

**[1 Kings 22:1-6](/bible/1-kings/22#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/1-kings/22#v1)</sup> And they continued three years without war between Syria and Israel.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-kings/22#v2)</sup> And it came to pass in the third year, that Jehoshaphat the king of Judah came down to the king of Israel.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-kings/22#v3)</sup> And the king of Israel said unto his servants, Know ye that Ramoth in Gilead is ours, and we be still, and take it not out of the hand of the king of Syria?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-kings/22#v4)</sup> And he said unto Jehoshaphat, Wilt thou go with me to battle to Ramoth–gilead? And Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, I am as thou art, my people as thy people, my horses as thy horses.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-kings/22#v5)</sup> And Jehoshaphat said unto the king of Israel, Enquire, I pray thee, at the word of the Lord to day.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-kings/22#v6)</sup> Then the king of Israel gathered the prophets together, about four hundred men, and said unto them, Shall I go against Ramoth–gilead to battle, or shall I forbear? And they said, Go up; for the Lord shall deliver it into the hand of the king.

- After three years without war between Syria and Israel, Jehoshaphat king of Judah comes down to the king of Israel. Ahab reminds his servants that Ramoth in Gilead belongs to Israel, yet they sit still and do not take it out of the hand of the king of Syria.
- Ahab asks Jehoshaphat to go with him to battle, and Jehoshaphat answers that he is as Ahab is, his people and horses as Ahab's, but first asks that they inquire at the word of the Lord. Ahab gathers about four hundred prophets, who all say with one mouth, go up, for the Lord shall deliver it into the hand of the king.
- This is the setting for a division that ran between the northern kingdom and the southern kingdom. After the split, the northern kingdom fell into idolatry and forsook the Levites, so a prophet of the Most High would want nothing to do with their manner of worship.
  - **[2 Kings 3:13](/bible/2-kings/3#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/2-kings/3#v13)</sup> And Elisha said unto the king of Israel, What have I to do with thee? get thee to the prophets of thy father, and to the prophets of thy mother. And the king of Israel said unto him, Nay: for the Lord hath called these three kings together, to deliver them into the hand of Moab.

    Elisha tells a later king of Israel to go to the prophets of his father and his mother, showing how a true prophet of the Most High kept his distance from the corrupted worship of the northern kingdom.
  - **[2 Chronicles 13:9](/bible/2-chronicles/13#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/2-chronicles/13#v9)</sup> Have ye not cast out the priests of the Lord, the sons of Aaron, and the Levites, and have made you priests after the manner of the nations of other lands? so that whosoever cometh to consecrate himself with a young bullock and seven rams, the same may be a priest of them that are no gods.

    Jeroboam cast out the priests of the Lord, the sons of Aaron, and made priests after the manner of the nations, so that any man could consecrate himself to serve gods that are no gods.
  - **[Amos 7:10-13](/bible/amos/7#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/amos/7#v10)</sup> Then Amaziah the priest of Beth–el sent to Jeroboam king of Israel, saying, Amos hath conspired against thee in the midst of the house of Israel: the land is not able to bear all his words.
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/amos/7#v11)</sup> For thus Amos saith, Jeroboam shall die by the sword, and Israel shall surely be led away captive out of their own land.
    >
    > <sup>[12](/bible/amos/7#v12)</sup> Also Amaziah said unto Amos, O thou seer, go, flee thee away into the land of Judah, and there eat bread, and prophesy there:
    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/amos/7#v13)</sup> But prophesy not again any more at Beth–el: for it is the king’s chapel, and it is the king’s court.

    Amaziah the priest of Bethel tries to drive the prophet Amos out of the king's chapel, telling him to prophesy in Judah instead, which shows how the northern kingdom rejected the true word.

**[1 Kings 22:7-8](/bible/1-kings/22#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-kings/22#v7)</sup> And Jehoshaphat said, Is there not here a prophet of the Lord besides, that we might enquire of him?
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/1-kings/22#v8)</sup> And the king of Israel said unto Jehoshaphat, There is yet one man, Micaiah the son of Imlah, by whom we may enquire of the Lord: but I hate him; for he doth not prophesy good concerning me, but evil. And Jehoshaphat said, Let not the king say so.

- Jehoshaphat is not moved by four hundred prophets speaking the same thing, and asks whether there is not yet a prophet of the Lord that they may inquire of him. He wants to hear from a true prophet of the Most High, not from prophets who serve the king.
- Ahab answers that there is one man, Micaiah the son of Imlah, but he hates him because Micaiah does not prophesy good concerning him, but evil. Jehoshaphat rebukes him, saying, let not the king say so.

**[1 Kings 22:9-12](/bible/1-kings/22#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-kings/22#v9)</sup> Then the king of Israel called an officer, and said, Hasten hither Micaiah the son of Imlah.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-kings/22#v10)</sup> And the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat the king of Judah sat each on his throne, having put on their robes, in a void place in the entrance of the gate of Samaria; and all the prophets prophesied before them.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-kings/22#v11)</sup> And Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah made him horns of iron: and he said, Thus saith the Lord, With these shalt thou push the Syrians, until thou have consumed them.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-kings/22#v12)</sup> And all the prophets prophesied so, saying, Go up to Ramoth–gilead, and prosper: for the Lord shall deliver it into the king’s hand.

- The king of Israel calls an officer to hasten Micaiah, while Ahab and Jehoshaphat sit each on his throne in their robes at the entrance of the gate of Samaria, and all the prophets prophesy before them.
- Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah makes horns of iron and says, thus saith the Lord, with these shalt thou push the Syrians until thou have consumed them. Here again a man claims to speak for the Most High while the whole company presses Ahab to go up and prosper.

**[1 Kings 22:13-23](/bible/1-kings/22#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/1-kings/22#v13)</sup> And the messenger that was gone to call Micaiah spake unto him, saying, Behold now, the words of the prophets declare good unto the king with one mouth: let thy word, I pray thee, be like the word of one of them, and speak that which is good.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-kings/22#v14)</sup> And Micaiah said, As the Lord liveth, what the Lord saith unto me, that will I speak.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-kings/22#v15)</sup> So he came to the king. And the king said unto him, Micaiah, shall we go against Ramoth–gilead to battle, or shall we forbear? And he answered him, Go, and prosper: for the Lord shall deliver it into the hand of the king.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/1-kings/22#v16)</sup> And the king said unto him, How many times shall I adjure thee that thou tell me nothing but that which is true in the name of the Lord?
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/1-kings/22#v17)</sup> And he said, I saw all Israel scattered upon the hills, as sheep that have not a shepherd: and the Lord said, These have no master: let them return every man to his house in peace.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-kings/22#v18)</sup> And the king of Israel said unto Jehoshaphat, Did I not tell thee that he would prophesy no good concerning me, but evil?
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/1-kings/22#v19)</sup> And he said, Hear thou therefore the word of the Lord: I saw the Lord sitting on his throne, and all the host of heaven standing by him on his right hand and on his left.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/1-kings/22#v20)</sup> And the Lord said, Who shall persuade Ahab, that he may go up and fall at Ramoth–gilead? And one said on this manner, and another said on that manner.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/1-kings/22#v21)</sup> And there came forth a spirit, and stood before the Lord, and said, I will persuade him.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/1-kings/22#v22)</sup> And the Lord said unto him, Wherewith? And he said, I will go forth, and I will be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets. And he said, Thou shalt persuade him, and prevail also: go forth, and do so.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/1-kings/22#v23)</sup> Now therefore, behold, the Lord hath put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these thy prophets, and the Lord hath spoken evil concerning thee.

- The messenger tells Micaiah that all the prophets declare good to the king with one mouth, and urges him to speak likewise. Micaiah answers that whatever the Lord saith unto him, that will he speak, and at first he echoes the others, prompting the king to adjure him to tell nothing but the truth.
- Micaiah then reveals what he saw, all Israel scattered on the hills as sheep having no shepherd, and the Lord saying these have no master. He saw the Lord on his throne with all the host of heaven, and a spirit that offered to be a lying spirit in the mouth of all Ahab's prophets to persuade him to fall at Ramoth-gilead.
- The Most High put a lying spirit in the mouth of these prophets and spoke evil concerning Ahab. The deceiver as well as the deceived are under the hand of the Most High, who uses the lie to bring the judgment he has already declared.
  - **[Job 12:16](/bible/job/12#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/job/12#v16)</sup> With him is strength and wisdom: the deceived and the deceiver are his.

    With the Most High is strength and wisdom, and the deceived and the deceiver are his, for he leadeth counsellors away spoiled and maketh the judges fools.

**[1 Kings 22:24-28](/bible/1-kings/22#v24)**
> <sup>[24](/bible/1-kings/22#v24)</sup> But Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah went near, and smote Micaiah on the cheek, and said, Which way went the Spirit of the Lord from me to speak unto thee?
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/1-kings/22#v25)</sup> And Micaiah said, Behold, thou shalt see in that day, when thou shalt go into an inner chamber to hide thyself.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/1-kings/22#v26)</sup> And the king of Israel said, Take Micaiah, and carry him back unto Amon the governor of the city, and to Joash the king’s son;
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/1-kings/22#v27)</sup> And say, Thus saith the king, Put this fellow in the prison, and feed him with bread of affliction and with water of affliction, until I come in peace.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/1-kings/22#v28)</sup> And Micaiah said, If thou return at all in peace, the Lord hath not spoken by me. And he said, Hearken, O people, every one of you.

- Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah smites Micaiah on the cheek and asks which way the spirit of the Lord went from him to speak to Micaiah, defending his own false prophecy. The king of Israel orders Micaiah taken back and put in prison, fed with bread and water of affliction until Ahab returns in peace.
- Micaiah answers that if Ahab returns at all in peace, the Lord has not spoken by him, and calls all the people to hearken. His word stands as the test of who truly spoke for the Most High.

**[1 Kings 22:29-38](/bible/1-kings/22#v29)**
> <sup>[29](/bible/1-kings/22#v29)</sup> So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat the king of Judah went up to Ramoth–gilead.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/1-kings/22#v30)</sup> And the king of Israel said unto Jehoshaphat, I will disguise myself, and enter into the battle; but put thou on thy robes. And the king of Israel disguised himself, and went into the battle.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/1-kings/22#v31)</sup> But the king of Syria commanded his thirty and two captains that had rule over his chariots, saying, Fight neither with small nor great, save only with the king of Israel.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/1-kings/22#v32)</sup> And it came to pass, when the captains of the chariots saw Jehoshaphat, that they said, Surely it is the king of Israel. And they turned aside to fight against him: and Jehoshaphat cried out.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/1-kings/22#v33)</sup> And it came to pass, when the captains of the chariots perceived that it was not the king of Israel, that they turned back from pursuing him.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/1-kings/22#v34)</sup> And a certain man drew a bow at a venture, and smote the king of Israel between the joints of the harness: wherefore he said unto the driver of his chariot, Turn thine hand, and carry me out of the host; for I am wounded.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/1-kings/22#v35)</sup> And the battle increased that day: and the king was stayed up in his chariot against the Syrians, and died at even: and the blood ran out of the wound into the midst of the chariot.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/1-kings/22#v36)</sup> And there went a proclamation throughout the host about the going down of the sun, saying, Every man to his city, and every man to his own country.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/1-kings/22#v37)</sup> So the king died, and was brought to Samaria; and they buried the king in Samaria.
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/1-kings/22#v38)</sup> And one washed the chariot in the pool of Samaria; and the dogs licked up his blood; and they washed his armour; according unto the word of the Lord which he spake.

- The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat go up to Ramoth-gilead. Ahab disguises himself and tells Jehoshaphat to wear his robes, hoping to escape the word spoken against him, while the king of Syria commands his captains to fight only against the king of Israel.
- The captains pursue Jehoshaphat until they perceive he is not the king of Israel and turn back. A certain man draws a bow at a venture and smites Ahab between the joints of the harness, and the king is held up in his chariot until he dies at even, the blood running into the chariot.
- When they wash the chariot in the pool of Samaria, the dogs lick up his blood according to the word of the Lord. The judgment pronounced earlier against Ahab and his house begins to be fulfilled exactly as spoken.

**[1 Kings 22:39-40](/bible/1-kings/22#v39)**
> <sup>[39](/bible/1-kings/22#v39)</sup> Now the rest of the acts of Ahab, and all that he did, and the ivory house which he made, and all the cities that he built, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
>
> <sup>[40](/bible/1-kings/22#v40)</sup> So Ahab slept with his fathers; and Ahaziah his son reigned in his stead.

- The rest of the acts of Ahab, the ivory house he made and the cities he built, are written in the book of the Chronicles of the kings of Israel. Ahab sleeps with his fathers, and Ahaziah his son reigns in his stead.

**[1 Kings 22:41-50](/bible/1-kings/22#v41)**
> <sup>[41](/bible/1-kings/22#v41)</sup> And Jehoshaphat the son of Asa began to reign over Judah in the fourth year of Ahab king of Israel.
>
> <sup>[42](/bible/1-kings/22#v42)</sup> Jehoshaphat was thirty and five years old when he began to reign; and he reigned twenty and five years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Azubah the daughter of Shilhi.
>
> <sup>[43](/bible/1-kings/22#v43)</sup> And he walked in all the ways of Asa his father; he turned not aside from it, doing that which was right in the eyes of the Lord: nevertheless the high places were not taken away; for the people offered and burnt incense yet in the high places.
>
> <sup>[44](/bible/1-kings/22#v44)</sup> And Jehoshaphat made peace with the king of Israel.
>
> <sup>[45](/bible/1-kings/22#v45)</sup> Now the rest of the acts of Jehoshaphat, and his might that he shewed, and how he warred, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
>
> <sup>[46](/bible/1-kings/22#v46)</sup> And the remnant of the sodomites, which remained in the days of his father Asa, he took out of the land.
>
> <sup>[47](/bible/1-kings/22#v47)</sup> There was then no king in Edom: a deputy was king.
>
> <sup>[48](/bible/1-kings/22#v48)</sup> Jehoshaphat made ships of Tharshish to go to Ophir for gold: but they went not; for the ships were broken at Ezion–geber.
>
> <sup>[49](/bible/1-kings/22#v49)</sup> Then said Ahaziah the son of Ahab unto Jehoshaphat, Let my servants go with thy servants in the ships. But Jehoshaphat would not.
>
> <sup>[50](/bible/1-kings/22#v50)</sup> And Jehoshaphat slept with his fathers, and was buried with his fathers in the city of David his father: and Jehoram his son reigned in his stead.

- Jehoshaphat the son of Asa begins to reign over Judah in the fourth year of Ahab, at thirty-five years old, and reigns twenty-five years in Jerusalem. He walks in the ways of Asa his father, doing right in the eyes of the Lord, though the high places are not taken away and the people still burn incense there.
- Jehoshaphat makes peace with the king of Israel and puts the remnant of the sodomites out of the land. There is then no king in Edom but a deputy, because Edom had been subdued and had not yet revolted.
  - **[1 Chronicles 22:17-18](/bible/1-chronicles/22#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/1-chronicles/22#v17)</sup> David also commanded all the princes of Israel to help Solomon his son, saying,
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/1-chronicles/22#v18)</sup> Is not the Lord your God with you? and hath he not given you rest on every side? for he hath given the inhabitants of the land into mine hand; and the land is subdued before the Lord, and before his people.

    David charges the princes of Israel, saying the Lord has given the inhabitants of the land into his hand and the land is subdued before the Lord, which is why Edom had no king of its own in Jehoshaphat's day.

**[1 Kings 22:51-53](/bible/1-kings/22#v51)**
> <sup>[51](/bible/1-kings/22#v51)</sup> Ahaziah the son of Ahab began to reign over Israel in Samaria the seventeenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, and reigned two years over Israel.
>
> <sup>[52](/bible/1-kings/22#v52)</sup> And he did evil in the sight of the Lord, and walked in the way of his father, and in the way of his mother, and in the way of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin:
>
> <sup>[53](/bible/1-kings/22#v53)</sup> For he served Baal, and worshipped him, and provoked to anger the Lord God of Israel, according to all that his father had done.

- Ahaziah the son of Ahab begins to reign over Israel in Samaria in the seventeenth year of Jehoshaphat and reigns two years. He does evil in the sight of the Lord and walks in the way of his father and his mother and in the way of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin.
- He serves Baal and worships him and provokes the Lord God of Israel to anger, according to all that his father had done. The sin of the house of Ahab continues into the next generation.
