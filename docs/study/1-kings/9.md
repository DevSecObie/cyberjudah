## Introduction

This session covers 1 Kings 9-12. Solomon has finished the house of the Most High and his own house, and the Most High appears to him a second time to renew the promise and to warn him plainly that if Israel turns to other gods they will be cut off from the land and become a proverb and a byword among all people. From there the reading moves through the greatness of Solomon's kingdom, his gold, his navy, and the visit of the queen of Sheba, and then into his fall, for Solomon loved many strange women and they turned his heart after their gods. Because of that one sin the kingdom is torn, ten tribes given to Jeroboam and two kept for the house of David for David's sake. The reading closes with Rehoboam rejecting the counsel of the elders and Jeroboam setting up golden calves, so that Israel is divided to this day. Solomon built the first temple in Jerusalem, and Solomon was a black man, which the scripture itself declares.

**[1 Kings 9:1-2](/bible/1-kings/9#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-kings/9#v1)</sup> And it came to pass, when Solomon had finished the building of the house of the Lord, and the king’s house, and all Solomon’s desire which he was pleased to do,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-kings/9#v2)</sup> That the Lord appeared to Solomon the second time, as he had appeared unto him at Gibeon.

- When Solomon had finished the building of the house of the Most High and the king's house and all he desired to do, the Most High appeared to him the second time, as he had appeared at Gibeon.
- The so-called Jews boast of the temple and their heritage in a land that does not belong to them, but the man who built the first temple was a black man, and the scripture says so plainly.

  Precepts:
  - **[Song of Solomon 1:5](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/song-of-solomon/1#v5)</sup> I am black, but comely, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, as the tents of Kedar, as the curtains of Solomon.

    Solomon by his own word is black, so the builder of the first temple was a black man.

**[1 Kings 9:3-5](/bible/1-kings/9#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/1-kings/9#v3)</sup> And the Lord said unto him, I have heard thy prayer and thy supplication, that thou hast made before me: I have hallowed this house, which thou hast built, to put my name there for ever; and mine eyes and mine heart shall be there perpetually.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-kings/9#v4)</sup> And if thou wilt walk before me, as David thy father walked, in integrity of heart, and in uprightness, to do according to all that I have commanded thee, and wilt keep my statutes and my judgments:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-kings/9#v5)</sup> Then I will establish the throne of thy kingdom upon Israel for ever, as I promised to David thy father, saying, There shall not fail thee a man upon the throne of Israel.

- The Most High told Solomon he had heard his prayer, had hallowed the house to put his name there forever, and that his eyes and heart would be there perpetually.
- If Solomon walked before him in integrity of heart and uprightness as David his father walked, keeping his statutes and judgments, then he would establish the throne of the kingdom over Israel forever.
- People think David had no integrity because of the census, Bathsheba, and Uriah the Hittite, yet the Most High still says David walked in integrity of heart, because when David sinned he repented and turned back.

**[1 Kings 9:6-9](/bible/1-kings/9#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/1-kings/9#v6)</sup> But if ye shall at all turn from following me, ye or your children, and will not keep my commandments and my statutes which I have set before you, but go and serve other gods, and worship them:
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-kings/9#v7)</sup> Then will I cut off Israel out of the land which I have given them; and this house, which I have hallowed for my name, will I cast out of my sight; and Israel shall be a proverb and a byword among all people:
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/1-kings/9#v8)</sup> And at this house, which is high, every one that passeth by it shall be astonished, and shall hiss; and they shall say, Why hath the Lord done thus unto this land, and to this house?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-kings/9#v9)</sup> And they shall answer, Because they forsook the Lord their God, who brought forth their fathers out of the land of Egypt, and have taken hold upon other gods, and have worshipped them, and served them: therefore hath the Lord brought upon them all this evil.

- If they or their children turned from following him and served other gods, he would cut off Israel out of the land, cast the house out of his sight, and Israel would become a proverb and a byword among all people.
- This is the same warning Moses gave in the law, that disobedience brings the curse of scattering, and it reaches not only that generation but the seed after them.
- The house that was high and mighty would astonish everyone who passed by, and they would ask why the Most High had done this, and the answer is because Israel forsook the Most High who brought their fathers out of Egypt and took hold of other gods. This shows the Most High is not all love, for it says therefore he brought upon them all this evil.

  Precepts:
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:15](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v15)**

    If Israel will not hearken to do all his commandments, all these curses shall come and overtake them.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:37](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v37)**
    > <sup>[37](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v37)</sup> And thou shalt become an astonishment, a proverb, and a byword, among all nations whither the Lord shall lead thee.

    Israel shall become an astonishment, a proverb, and a byword among all nations where the Most High shall lead them.

**[1 Kings 9:10-14](/bible/1-kings/9#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/1-kings/9#v10)</sup> And it came to pass at the end of twenty years, when Solomon had built the two houses, the house of the Lord, and the king’s house,
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-kings/9#v11)</sup> (Now Hiram the king of Tyre had furnished Solomon with cedar trees and fir trees, and with gold, according to all his desire,) that then king Solomon gave Hiram twenty cities in the land of Galilee.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-kings/9#v12)</sup> And Hiram came out from Tyre to see the cities which Solomon had given him; and they pleased him not.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/1-kings/9#v13)</sup> And he said, What cities are these which thou hast given me, my brother? And he called them the land of Cabul unto this day.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-kings/9#v14)</sup> And Hiram sent to the king sixscore talents of gold.

- After twenty years, when Solomon had built the two houses, Hiram king of Tyre had furnished him with cedar, fir, and gold, and Solomon gave Hiram twenty cities in Galilee, but they pleased Hiram not.
- Solomon gave Hiram the worst of the land, the poor cities, and Hiram was not pleased, calling them the land of Cabul to this day. Hiram had given the best cedar and fir, and in return Solomon paid him with the least, which was wisdom on Solomon's part.

> <sup>[15](/bible/1-kings/9#v15)</sup> And this is the reason of the levy which king Solomon raised; for to build the house of the Lord, and his own house, and Millo, and the wall of Jerusalem, and Hazor, and Megiddo, and Gezer.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/1-kings/9#v16)</sup> For Pharaoh king of Egypt had gone up, and taken Gezer, and burnt it with fire, and slain the Canaanites that dwelt in the city, and given it for a present unto his daughter, Solomon’s wife.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/1-kings/9#v17)</sup> And Solomon built Gezer, and Beth–horon the nether,
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-kings/9#v18)</sup> And Baalath, and Tadmor in the wilderness, in the land,
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/1-kings/9#v19)</sup> And all the cities of store that Solomon had, and cities for his chariots, and cities for his horsemen, and that which Solomon desired to build in Jerusalem, and in Lebanon, and in all the land of his dominion.

- This is the reason of the levy Solomon raised to build the house of the Most High, his own house, Millo, the wall of Jerusalem, and the cities of Hazor, Megiddo, and Gezer, which Pharaoh had taken and given to his daughter, Solomon's wife.
- Solomon already had the daughter of Pharaoh to wife, which was breaking a commandment, and the sin began to snowball. Yet he built cities for his chariots and horsemen and all he desired, so that he took care of his people; the Most High had given him wisdom that no man on earth had except Christ.

>
> <sup>[23](/bible/1-kings/9#v23)</sup> These were the chief of the officers that were over Solomon’s work, five hundred and fifty, which bare rule over the people that wrought in the work.

- All the people left of the Amorites, Hittites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites, who were not of Israel, their children that were left Solomon made a tribute of bondservice to this day, which is lawful by the law.
- Of the children of Israel Solomon made no bondmen, but they were men of war, his servants, princes, captains, and rulers of his chariots and horsemen; instead of enslaving his own people he built them up and gave them positions.

**[1 Kings 9:24-28](/bible/1-kings/9#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/1-kings/9#v24)</sup> But Pharaoh’s daughter came up out of the city of David unto her house which Solomon had built for her: then did he build Millo.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/1-kings/9#v25)</sup> And three times in a year did Solomon offer burnt offerings and peace offerings upon the altar which he built unto the Lord, and he burnt incense upon the altar that was before the Lord. So he finished the house.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/1-kings/9#v26)</sup> And king Solomon made a navy of ships in Ezion–geber, which is beside Eloth, on the shore of the Red sea, in the land of Edom.

>
> <sup>[28](/bible/1-kings/9#v28)</sup> And they came to Ophir, and fetched from thence gold, four hundred and twenty talents, and brought it to king Solomon.

- Pharaoh's daughter came up to her house that Solomon built, and Solomon offered burnt offerings and peace offerings three times a year, and burnt incense before the Most High, and so finished the house.
- Solomon made a navy of ships at Eziongeber on the shore of the Red Sea, and Hiram sent shipmen that had knowledge of the sea with Solomon's servants, and they fetched from Ophir gold, four hundred and twenty talents.
- Ophir was famous for its gold from very early days; Solomon had the wisdom to know the routes and the times of year to sail, and no one could undermine him, for he had the power, the wisdom, the money, and the knowledge.
