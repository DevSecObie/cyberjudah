## Introduction

This session covers 1 Kings chapters 17 through 20: Elijah the Tishbite pronouncing the drought upon Ahab, the ravens feeding him at the brook, the widow of Zarephath whose barrel of meal and cruise of oil did not fail, the raising of her dead son, the showdown with the prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel, the fire that fell from heaven, the return of the rain, Elijah's flight from Jezebel and the still small voice at Horeb, the calling of Elisha, and the two victories over Ben-hadad of Syria that Ahab squandered by his own wickedness. The thread that ties it together is the power of the Most High: he commands the animals, the rain, life and death, and fire from heaven, and he tries a man by his laws, giving him time to repent, but gives the rebellious over to their own ruin.

**[1 Kings 17:1](/bible/1-kings/17#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-kings/17#v1)</sup> And Elijah the Tishbite, who was of the inhabitants of Gilead, said unto Ahab, As the Lord God of Israel liveth, before whom I stand, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, but according to my word.

- Elijah the Tishbite, of the inhabitants of Gilead, said unto Ahab that as the Lord God of Israel liveth, before whom he stands, there shall not be dew nor rain these years but according to his word. Elijah prayed and it was no rain.
- The teaching opens by framing the whole session on the power of the Most High: he has power over everything, the animals and all creation, and he set that power on display through his prophet.

>

> <sup>[4](/bible/1-kings/17#v4)</sup> And it shall be, that thou shalt drink of the brook; and I have commanded the ravens to feed thee there.

>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-kings/17#v6)</sup> And the ravens brought him bread and flesh in the morning, and bread and flesh in the evening; and he drank of the brook.

- The word of the Lord sent Elijah eastward to hide by the brook Cherith before Jordan, and there he drank of the brook and the ravens brought him bread and flesh in the morning and in the evening.
- This shows the power of the Most High over the animals: he can command the ravens to bring a man food if it be his will, just as he did Elijah.

**[1 Kings 17:7-16](/bible/1-kings/17#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/1-kings/17#v7)</sup> And it came to pass after a while, that the brook dried up, because there had been no rain in the land.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/1-kings/17#v8)</sup> And the word of the Lord came unto him, saying,
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/1-kings/17#v9)</sup> Arise, get thee to Zarephath, which belongeth to Zidon, and dwell there: behold, I have commanded a widow woman there to sustain thee.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-kings/17#v10)</sup> So he arose and went to Zarephath. And when he came to the gate of the city, behold, the widow woman was there gathering of sticks: and he called to her, and said, Fetch me, I pray thee, a little water in a vessel, that I may drink.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-kings/17#v11)</sup> And as she was going to fetch it, he called to her, and said, Bring me, I pray thee, a morsel of bread in thine hand.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-kings/17#v12)</sup> And she said, As the Lord thy God liveth, I have not a cake, but an handful of meal in a barrel, and a little oil in a cruse: and, behold, I am gathering two sticks, that I may go in and dress it for me and my son, that we may eat it, and die.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/1-kings/17#v13)</sup> And Elijah said unto her, Fear not; go and do as thou hast said: but make me thereof a little cake first, and bring it unto me, and after make for thee and for thy son.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-kings/17#v14)</sup> For thus saith the Lord God of Israel, The barrel of meal shall not waste, neither shall the cruse of oil fail, until the day that the Lord sendeth rain upon the earth.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/1-kings/17#v15)</sup> And she went and did according to the saying of Elijah: and she, and he, and her house, did eat many days.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/1-kings/17#v16)</sup> And the barrel of meal wasted not, neither did the cruse of oil fail, according to the word of the Lord, which he spake by Elijah.

- The brook dried up for want of rain, and the Lord sent Elijah all the way across the land to Zarephath, which belongeth to Zidon on the coast, where he had commanded a widow woman to sustain him. From the east side by Jordan to the west by Sidon was a hard walk of a Sabbath day's journey or more, with no water anywhere.
- Coming to the gate, Elijah asked the widow for water and a morsel of bread, and she answered that she had but a handful of meal in a barrel and a little oil in a cruise, gathering two sticks to dress a last meal for herself and her son that they might eat it and die.
- Elijah told her to make him a little cake first and after that for herself and her son, for the Lord God of Israel said the barrel of meal would not waste nor the cruise of oil fail until the day the Lord sent rain. She obeyed, and she and Elijah and her house did eat many days. For trusting in the Lord and obeying, she was sustained.
- The instruction to feed the man of God first is a precept: the man eats first. Sisters must trust in the Lord when he tells them to reverence their husbands and be submissive in all things, and not lean to their own understanding.

  Precepts:

    > <sup>[33](/bible/ephesians/5#v33)</sup> Nevertheless let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband.

    Let every one so love his wife even as himself, and the wife see that she reverence her husband; that reverence goes into the honor of feeding the man first, just as the widow did.

**[1 Kings 17:17-24](/bible/1-kings/17#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/1-kings/17#v17)</sup> And it came to pass after these things, that the son of the woman, the mistress of the house, fell sick; and his sickness was so sore, that there was no breath left in him.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-kings/17#v18)</sup> And she said unto Elijah, What have I to do with thee, O thou man of God? art thou come unto me to call my sin to remembrance, and to slay my son?
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/1-kings/17#v19)</sup> And he said unto her, Give me thy son. And he took him out of her bosom, and carried him up into a loft, where he abode, and laid him upon his own bed.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/1-kings/17#v20)</sup> And he cried unto the Lord, and said, O Lord my God, hast thou also brought evil upon the widow with whom I sojourn, by slaying her son?
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/1-kings/17#v21)</sup> And he stretched himself upon the child three times, and cried unto the Lord, and said, O Lord my God, I pray thee, let this child’s soul come into him again.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/1-kings/17#v22)</sup> And the Lord heard the voice of Elijah; and the soul of the child came into him again, and he revived.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/1-kings/17#v23)</sup> And Elijah took the child, and brought him down out of the chamber into the house, and delivered him unto his mother: and Elijah said, See, thy son liveth.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/1-kings/17#v24)</sup> And the woman said to Elijah, Now by this I know that thou art a man of God, and that the word of the Lord in thy mouth is truth.

- After these things the widow's son fell sick, and his sickness was so sore that there was no breath left in him. She said unto Elijah, Art thou come to call my sin to remembrance and to slay my son? Her thought was that the child was paying for the parent's sins.
- Elijah took the child up to the loft, laid him on his own bed, and cried unto the Lord, stretching himself upon the child three times and praying that the child's soul come into him again. The Lord heard the voice of Elijah, and the soul of the child came into him again and he revived.
- Elijah delivered the living child to his mother, and she said, Now by this I know that thou art a man of God and that the word of the Lord in thy mouth is truth.
- All praises that Elijah was there to call upon the Lord, for it was not the young man's time to go. A man's days are determined and appointed, and he cannot pass the bound the Most High has set; because it was not the child's time, the Lord let his soul return.
