**[1 Kings 19:1-3](/bible/1-kings/19#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-kings/19#v1)</sup> And Ahab told Jezebel all that Elijah had done, and withal how he had slain all the prophets with the sword.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/1-kings/19#v2)</sup> Then Jezebel sent a messenger unto Elijah, saying, So let the gods do to me, and more also, if I make not thy life as the life of one of them by to morrow about this time.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/1-kings/19#v3)</sup> And when he saw that, he arose, and went for his life, and came to Beer–sheba, which belongeth to Judah, and left his servant there.

- Ahab told Jezebel all that Elijah had done and how he had slain all the prophets with the sword. This shows the devil on Ahab: he ran back running his mouth about the slain false prophets and never once mentioned that Elijah had brought rain after a three-year drought.
- Jezebel sent a messenger swearing that she would make Elijah's life as the life of one of the slain prophets by the next day. She wanted him dead because he had taken away her power and the name she had gotten in Israel through her idolatry. This is the same wicked spirit named in Revelation, the top-notch wicked woman who feared no God at all.
- When Elijah saw it, he arose and went for his life and left his servant behind. He was an old man, not young like Elisha, and he knew Jezebel would truly try to kill him, so he fled.

**[1 Kings 19:4-8](/bible/1-kings/19#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/1-kings/19#v4)</sup> But he himself went a day’s journey into the wilderness, and came and sat down under a juniper tree: and he requested for himself that he might die; and said, It is enough; now, O Lord, take away my life; for I am not better than my fathers.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-kings/19#v5)</sup> And as he lay and slept under a juniper tree, behold, then an angel touched him, and said unto him, Arise and eat.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-kings/19#v6)</sup> And he looked, and, behold, there was a cake baken on the coals, and a cruse of water at his head. And he did eat and drink, and laid him down again.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-kings/19#v7)</sup> And the angel of the Lord came again the second time, and touched him, and said, Arise and eat; because the journey is too great for thee.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/1-kings/19#v8)</sup> And he arose, and did eat and drink, and went in the strength of that meat forty days and forty nights unto Horeb the mount of God.

- Elijah went a day's journey into the wilderness, sat down under a juniper tree, and requested that he might die, saying, It is enough; now, O Lord, take away my life, for I am not better than my fathers.
- Our forefathers had ultimate faith and understood regeneration: they could ask the Most High to take their life and let them rest in the grave, trusting he would bring them back whenever he chose. They did not have the lust of the eyes and of this world as their main thought. Elijah counted himself no better than his fathers even though he was great; Christ later called John the Baptist, who came in the spirit of Elijah, the greatest of the prophets.
- As Elijah slept, an angel touched him and said, Arise and eat, and there was a cake baking on the coals and a cruise of water at his head. He ate and lay down again; a second time the angel touched him and said the journey was too great for him. He arose and ate, and went in the strength of that meat forty days and forty nights unto Horeb, the mount of God. The Most High made food through the angel that sustained Elijah for forty days; that was good spiritual food.

**[1 Kings 19:9-14](/bible/1-kings/19#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/1-kings/19#v9)</sup> And he came thither unto a cave, and lodged there; and, behold, the word of the Lord came to him, and he said unto him, What doest thou here, Elijah?
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-kings/19#v10)</sup> And he said, I have been very jealous for the Lord God of hosts: for the children of Israel have forsaken thy covenant, thrown down thine altars, and slain thy prophets with the sword; and I, even I only, am left; and they seek my life, to take it away.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-kings/19#v11)</sup> And he said, Go forth, and stand upon the mount before the Lord. And, behold, the Lord passed by, and a great and strong wind rent the mountains, and brake in pieces the rocks before the Lord; but the Lord was not in the wind: and after the wind an earthquake; but the Lord was not in the earthquake:
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-kings/19#v12)</sup> And after the earthquake a fire; but the Lord was not in the fire: and after the fire a still small voice.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/1-kings/19#v13)</sup> And it was so, when Elijah heard it, that he wrapped his face in his mantle, and went out, and stood in the entering in of the cave. And, behold, there came a voice unto him, and said, What doest thou here, Elijah?
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-kings/19#v14)</sup> And he said, I have been very jealous for the Lord God of hosts: because the children of Israel have forsaken thy covenant, thrown down thine altars, and slain thy prophets with the sword; and I, even I only, am left; and they seek my life, to take it away.

- Elijah came to a cave in Horeb and lodged there, and the word of the Lord asked, What doest thou here, Elijah? He answered that he had been very zealous for the Lord God of hosts, for the children of Israel had forsaken the covenant, thrown down the altars, and slain the prophets, and he only was left and they sought his life.
- The Lord told him to stand upon the mount, and the Lord passed by: a great and strong wind rent the mountains, but the Lord was not in the wind; then an earthquake, but the Lord was not in the earthquake; then a fire, but the Lord was not in the fire; and after the fire a still small voice.
- That is the power of the Most High and of Christ: wind of extreme speed, an earthquake to catch up with it, then fire, and then he spoke in a still small voice. We do not know who we are dealing with; this is the same Most High who destroyed all but eight in the flood and rained fire on the cities of Sodom. Fear him who has the power to destroy the body.
- When Elijah heard it he wrapped his face in his mantle and stood in the cave entrance, and the voice asked again, What doest thou here, Elijah? He answered the same, that he was zealous for the Lord and that he alone was left and they sought his life.

**[1 Kings 19:15-18](/bible/1-kings/19#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/1-kings/19#v15)</sup> And the Lord said unto him, Go, return on thy way to the wilderness of Damascus: and when thou comest, anoint Hazael to be king over Syria:
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/1-kings/19#v16)</sup> And Jehu the son of Nimshi shalt thou anoint to be king over Israel: and Elisha the son of Shaphat of Abel–meholah shalt thou anoint to be prophet in thy room.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/1-kings/19#v17)</sup> And it shall come to pass, that him that escapeth the sword of Hazael shall Jehu slay: and him that escapeth from the sword of Jehu shall Elisha slay.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/1-kings/19#v18)</sup> Yet I have left me seven thousand in Israel, all the knees which have not bowed unto Baal, and every mouth which hath not kissed him.

- The Lord sent Elijah back to the wilderness of Damascus to anoint Hazael to be king over Syria, Jehu the son of Nimshi to be king over Israel, and Elisha the son of Shaphat of Abel-meholah to be prophet in his room. Him that escaped the sword of Hazael would Jehu slay, and him that escaped Jehu would Elisha slay.
- The Most High was telling Elijah not to worry, for he had things set up: Syria would come against the northern kingdom, Jehu would rise up to slay Ahab and all his house, and Elisha would pick up where Elijah left off when it was his time to come home.
- The Lord said he had left himself seven thousand in Israel, all the knees which had not bowed unto Baal. Elijah was not by himself; the Lord had people reserved who had not yet awakened, ordained to come in at a certain time for a certain purpose. It is the same today: our job is to preach the word, for we never know what video or flyer will wake up the ones the Lord has reserved.

**[1 Kings 19:19-21](/bible/1-kings/19#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/1-kings/19#v19)</sup> So he departed thence, and found Elisha the son of Shaphat, who was plowing with twelve yoke of oxen before him, and he with the twelfth: and Elijah passed by him, and cast his mantle upon him.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/1-kings/19#v20)</sup> And he left the oxen, and ran after Elijah, and said, Let me, I pray thee, kiss my father and my mother, and then I will follow thee. And he said unto him, Go back again: for what have I done to thee?
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/1-kings/19#v21)</sup> And he returned back from him, and took a yoke of oxen, and slew them, and boiled their flesh with the instruments of the oxen, and gave unto the people, and they did eat. Then he arose, and went after Elijah, and ministered unto him.

- Elijah found Elisha plowing with twelve yoke of oxen and cast his mantle, like a cloak, upon him. Elisha left the oxen and ran after Elijah, asking to kiss his father and mother first, then he would follow.
- Elisha returned, took a yoke of oxen and slew them, boiled their flesh with the instruments of the oxen, and gave to the people, and they did eat; then he arose and followed Elijah and ministered unto him. He fed the people before he left and then he was gone on his mission, not turning back.
