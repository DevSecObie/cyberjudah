## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 26 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 5-6, 8, 10-26, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:5](/bible/sirach/26#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/sirach/26#v5)</sup> There be three things that mine heart feareth; and for the fourth I was sore afraid: the slander of a city, the gathering together of an unruly multitude, and a false accusation: all these are worse than death.

- Some of us men are narcissists, and some still live in the past. We get on the sisters about how they won’t forgive and won’t forget what happened in the world, and this side of the room is the exact same. We hold on to old arguments, old relationships, being cheated on. Instead of a bag lady you are a bag brother. You don’t trust her, not for something she did, but for what somebody else did to you. You get defensive and you compare.
- The worst thing a woman can do is compare her husband to a man from her past or to her dad, my dad used to wash the cars and cut the grass. You don’t want to hear that: you should have married your dad, you should have stayed with that negro. But that works both ways. A woman doesn’t want to be compared to another woman either.
- “There be three things that mine heart feareth; and for the fourth I was sore afraid: the slander of a city, the gathering together of an unruly multitude, and a false accusation: all these are worse than death.” Three things make the heart fail, but the fourth he was sore afraid of.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:6](/bible/sirach/26#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/sirach/26#v6)</sup> But a grief of heart and sorrow is a woman that is jealous over another woman, and a scourge of the tongue which communicateth with all.

- “But a grief of heart and sorrow is a woman that is jealous over another woman.” This is wisdom: those three are worse than death, and this fourth one is even worse than that. Think of Lorena Bobbitt, or Al Green and the hot grits. You don’t want a wife that is jealous over another woman, so let that stuff go: no more here we go again, you just like my mom, my ex used to do this. Women are different than men.
- “And a scourge of the tongue which communicateth with all.” You are going to have a woman full of mouth. The scripture speaks of a silent and loving woman, and you won’t have one, because she won’t be silent and loving if she is jealous of another woman.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:8](/bible/sirach/26#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/sirach/26#v8)</sup> A drunken woman and a gadder abroad causeth great anger, and she will not cover her own shame.

- “A drunken woman and a gadder abroad causeth great anger, and she will not cover her own shame.” Brothers and sisters blame it on the alcohol. Sisters, there is nothing uglier, nastier or more disgusting than a drunken woman. This is not talking about a glass of wine or a drink; it is talking about a drunken woman. When she is drunk she gets nasty, mean-spirited and belligerent, wants to fight and starts cursing people out loud. It has happened here: a sister snuck a big bottle of liquor in between her legs to Passover.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:10](/bible/sirach/26#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/sirach/26#v10)</sup> If thy daughter be shameless, keep her in straitly, lest she abuse herself through overmuch liberty.

- “If thy daughter be shameless, keep her in straitly, lest she abuse herself through overmuch liberty.” Whoever has daughters can already identify from a child if the daughter is shameless, and if the boy is going to be a thief or a homo. From around the age of five you can see their tendencies, their habits, their likes and their dislikes: the boy always putting on mama’s shoes, one hand on the hip; the little girl always in the face of a boy who comes by the house, trying to sit on the uncle’s or the cousin’s lap. These are little things parents must see.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:11](/bible/sirach/26#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/sirach/26#v11)</sup> Watch over an impudent eye: and marvel not if she trespass against thee.

- “Watch over an impudent eye: and marvel not if she trespass against thee.” Don’t be shocked when she does it. You saw those tendencies when she was young.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:12](/bible/sirach/26#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/26#v12)</sup> She will open her mouth, as a thirsty traveller when he hath found a fountain, and drink of every water near her: by every hedge will she sit down, and open her quiver against every arrow.

- “She will open her mouth, as a thirsty traveller when he hath found a fountain, and drink of every water near her.” It’s a sausage fest. “By every hedge will she sit down, and open her quiver against every arrow.” Her quiver is the vagina and the arrow is the penis: God is saying she will open herself to every man. She doesn’t give a damn. They say numbers don’t matter; numbers do matter.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:13](/bible/sirach/26#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/26#v13)</sup> The grace of a wife delighteth her husband, and her discretion will fatten his bones.

- “The grace of a wife delighteth her husband, and her discretion will fatten his bones.” The grace of a wife, it delight her husband. You sisters that have grace -- grace, mercy -- it delights your husband.
- “and her discretion will fatten his bones.” Your discretion will fatten his bones.
- Submission is really obedience, and certain spirits accompany it. “The grace of a wife delighteth her husband, and her discretion will fatten his bones.”
- Grace here is not mercy or pardon. It is favour, good will, kindness; a manifestation of favour, especially by a superior; and graciousness, being pleasantly kind, benevolent and courteous. It brings delight rather than heaviness. The grace of a wife is the pleasingness a woman can have on her husband, and it is not just looks and appearance; Peter said let it not be the outward adorning. If she wants to get into her man’s heart and show him she loves him, it is the meek and quiet spirit.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:14](/bible/sirach/26#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/sirach/26#v14)</sup> A silent and loving woman is a gift of the Lord; and there is nothing so much worth as a mind well instructed.

- “A silent and loving woman is a gift of the Lord”. That's a gift from God. That's a gift, brothers, to have a woman that's silent.
- And when it says silent, it ain't talking about her walking around the house with tape over her mouth when her husband come home, like those videos going around. It ain't talking about that. It's talking about you are not argumentative. You listen. You serve your husband, you serve your Lord, you listen, you're not argumentative, you're not being evil. That's a blessing from God: you helping your man, your man serving God, and you are helping your man in serving God, making sure he good, he all right.
- “and there is nothing so much worth as a mind well instructed.” A mind well instructed, meaning the sister is instructed by her husband and she listened to her husband. The Lord love to see that.
- “A silent and loving woman is a gift of the Lord; and there is nothing so much worth as a mind well instructed.” Silence doesn’t only mean be quiet: it speaks of a mind well instructed, so her behaviours are what matter behind it. Silent is quiet and peaceable: not always arguing, not wanting to quarrel, not ruled by the need to say something. It doesn’t mean mute, though if her husband likes mute, she adapts to him.

  Precepts:
  - **[Proverbs 31:26](/bible/proverbs/31#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/proverbs/31#v26)</sup> She openeth her mouth with wisdom; and in her tongue is the law of kindness.

    “She openeth her mouth with wisdom; and in her tongue is the law of kindness.” It doesn’t say kindness; it says the law of kindness. His commandments are what come out of her mouth. That is her chaste conversation coupled with fear, and it is her requirement as a wife to her husband.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:15](/bible/sirach/26#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/sirach/26#v15)</sup> A shamefaced and faithful woman is a double grace, and her continent mind cannot be valued.

- “A shamefaced and faithful woman is a double grace, and her continent mind cannot be valued.” A shamefaced woman: you ain't up in everybody's face. And faithful: you faithful to your man, you know you faithful to your man.
- “is a double grace.” That's double grace from God. The Lord show you mercy, brother. And her continent mind cannot be valued.
- If a sister tries to apply Proverbs 31 without genuine submission, it is not Proverbs 31. It’s not a wise woman building her house but one tearing it down, decorations for a house with a weak foundation. Soft, respectful and quiet: write that down, especially you loudmouth, disrespectful ones. Saying I’m not the only one with issues is deflection, not addressing your responsibilities. If submission depends on how he treats you, it’s not submission, it’s manipulation. It’s not your job to fix the man’s issues; stay in your lane and pray, because Christ is the head of your husband, not you. If you fear God you do what he says, and if you can’t get that part right, the fear is feigned.
- Proverbs 31 shows a woman industrious and wise, a steward of the household and a support to her husband, but it is not about tasks and servitude; the submission has to come first. “A shamefaced and faithful woman is a double grace”: shamefaced and faithful, she submits regardless of anything, not conditional, not manipulation, and meditates on what submission means. “And her continent mind cannot be valued”: a mind that is not swayed, without the mood swings and all the other BS brought into the situation.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 26:26](/bible/sirach/26#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/sirach/26#v26)</sup> A woman that honoureth her husband shall be judged wise of all; but she that dishonoureth him in her pride shall be counted ungodly of all.

    Read again: “A woman that honoureth her husband shall be judged wise of all; but she that dishonoureth him in her pride shall be counted ungodly of all.” The shamefaced and faithful woman, the continent mind, the ordering of her house and the constant heart are some of the ways she honours him.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:16](/bible/sirach/26#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/sirach/26#v16)</sup> As the sun when it ariseth in the high heaven; so is the beauty of a good wife in the ordering of her house.

- “As the sun when it ariseth in the high heaven; so is the beauty of a good wife in the ordering of her house.” When the sun is shining in the high heaven, twelve o'clock, the sun bright in the sky -- so is the beauty of a good wife in the ordering of her house.
- To order her house is what? If she's ordering her house, she's serving her husband, she's serving her Lord, she order her house and serve her man.
- For you wicked black woman that say, I ain't serving no man, I ain't cooking, I ain't cleaning -- this is what God says.
- “As the sun when it ariseth in the high heaven; so is the beauty of a good wife in the ordering of her house.” Proverbs 31 falls under this, but it starts with the faithful, continent mind and the shamefacedness of submission. Order equals beauty, sisters. Wives who neglect their homes for other things are out of order and out of alignment with God’s will. The home is the first priority above all things, beginning with the husband and then the children, because of the responsibility given to the woman.

  Precepts:
  - **[Titus 2:4-5](/bible/titus/2#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/titus/2#v4)</sup> That they may teach the young women to be sober, to love their husbands, to love their children,
    >
    > <sup>[5](/bible/titus/2#v5)</sup> To be discreet, chaste, keepers at home, good, obedient to their own husbands, that the word of God be not blasphemed.

    “That they may teach the young women to be sober, to love their husbands, to love their children, to be discreet, chaste, keepers at home”: keepers at home is the ordering of her house. “good, obedient to their own husbands, that the word of God be not blasphemed.” It says teach them to love their husbands, and sums it up as obedient to their own husbands.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:17](/bible/sirach/26#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/sirach/26#v17)</sup> As the clear light is upon the holy candlestick; so is the beauty of the face in ripe age.

- “As the clear light is upon the holy candlestick; so is the beauty of the face in ripe age.” The clear light upon the holy candlestick is the menorah.
- So is the beauty of her face in ripe age -- her beauty in old age. Because as she get old, she like fine wine. Why? Because the Most High bless her. Some of you sisters is blessed: when you get old you are like fine wine, your age is beauty, through your wisdom and all of that that you have learned and your guiding of your house. Because it's showing your continence.
- Then you got someone that's thirty years old and they look like sixty, busted, and you're like, what's going on? You done get ran out and ran through and been to the club all your life.
- “So is the beauty of the face in ripe age.” Men age gracefully, women just age. If you want your husband to see you beautiful the rest of his days, you have to apply this.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:18](/bible/sirach/26#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/sirach/26#v18)</sup> As the golden pillars are upon the sockets of silver; so are the fair feet with a constant heart.

- “As the golden pillars are upon the sockets of silver; so are the fair feet with a constant heart.” That is the beauty he will see in you, past the age and past the gray hairs. None of it is physical beauty. The fair feet go into Proverbs 31, and the constant heart is not letting things pull you away from the Lord and your lord. Beauty is functional, not sexualized.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:19](/bible/sirach/26#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/sirach/26#v19)</sup> My son, keep the flower of thine age sound; and give not thy strength to strangers.

- Beware these foreign relationships. The scripture says, know what a man has need of before you counsel with him: if someone is in a broke, impoverished situation and needs a green card to come to America, why marry them? You don’t know if they really like you or are just trying to get over here.
- And brothers, if you move to another country, single, and meet a sister and marry her, do not ever bring her back here, if you want to keep her.
- “My son, keep the flower of thine age sound”: keep the flower of your age of sound mind. Watch over her, take care of her. “And give not thy strength to strangers”: because interracial mixing causes you spiritual dilapidation.
- “My son, keep the flower of thine age sound”: keep it strong. The flower of your age is your virginity. “And give not thy strength to strangers”: do not give yourself over to heathen women. That is what he’s talking about.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:20-21](/bible/sirach/26#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/sirach/26#v20)</sup> When thou hast gotten a fruitful possession through all the field, sow it with thine own seed, trusting in the goodness of thy stock.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/sirach/26#v21)</sup> So thy race which thou leavest shall be magnified, having the confidence of their good descent.

- “When thou hast gotten a fruitful possession through all the field, sow it with thine own seed.” The word seed there translates to sperm. “Trusting in the goodness of thy stock”: you better trust in the goodness of your family stock.
- “So thy race which thou leavest”: thy race goes back to your stock. It “shall be magnified, having the confidence of their good descent.”
- “When thou hast gotten a fruitful possession through all the field, sow it with thine own seed, trusting in the goodness of thy stock.” “So thy race which thou leavest shall be magnified.” If you want your race to be magnified, you have to sow your own seed in your own land. The Chinese want their race magnified, the white man wants his race magnified. What about you, black man? Or do you say, it’s not about race, it’s about Jesus? That is what you hear from dumb Christians.
- “When thou hast gotten a fruitful possession”: a fruitful possession is a woman, “through all the field”, throughout the land, “sow it with thine own seed”. Your own seed. “Trusting in the goodness of thy stock”: trusting in the goodness of your stock. That word stock was found in genealogy.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:22](/bible/sirach/26#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/sirach/26#v22)</sup> An harlot shall be accounted as spittle; but a married woman is a tower against death to her husband.

- “An harlot shall be accounted as spittle; but a married woman is a tower against death to her husband.” How is she a tower against death? Your wife knows you better than anybody on this planet. She knows the real you. We go out saying Shalom Israel, Christ bless, but she knows you are lazy, she knows you procrastinate, she knows you really don’t believe, that you don’t really watch classes. Some wives watch more classes and study more than their husbands.
- And what does she do? Some go to leadership about their husbands, but the majority of good sisters sit back and hide all that. She holds it all in, and as men we 100% take that for granted, until you are faced with what the brother in the song is faced with.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:23](/bible/sirach/26#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/sirach/26#v23)</sup> A wicked woman is given as a portion to a wicked man: but a godly woman is given to him that feareth the Lord.

- “A wicked woman is given as a portion to a wicked man.” Ask yourself why. It is to show you a reflection of yourself. God orchestrated that woman to come into your life to show you exactly who you are: your woman is your reflection. The Lord gives you that to show you what you are really dealing with inside. Forget the bombs; let the bomb go off in your head.
- “But a godly woman is given to him that feareth the Lord”: a godly man will have a woman that reflects his godliness. To the wicked one the Lord sends the wicked woman. Who gave it? The Most High. It is that woman you gave me, Adam said; that was to bring out an impediment in his obedience.
- So a man complains of a wicked woman, yet claims he is not wicked in his words and in his compliant deeds of following her. If that is true, he will escape from her. This is about women in the world and about backdoor marriages: when you don’t start it off right, it ends up wrong.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiastes 7:26](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v26)</sup> And I find more bitter than death the woman, whose heart is snares and nets, and her hands as bands: whoso pleaseth God shall escape from her; but the sinner shall be taken by her.

    “And I find more bitter than death the woman”: not all women. The kind of woman worse than death is the one who does all that the rest of the verse says. What could be worse than death? Some brothers are living in it: driving home from a hard day’s work, sitting in the driveway with a hot beer, afraid to touch the doorknob because of what is behind that door, going through eight scenarios in five steps.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 25:26](/bible/sirach/25#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/sirach/25#v26)</sup> If she go not as thou wouldest have her, cut her off from thy flesh, and give her a bill of divorce, and let her go.

    Here is the man in the world who is trying to repent, claiming he is not wicked, with a wicked woman on him; and it goes both ways, men too. She keeps up the snares and the bands of Ecclesiastes 7:26, and whoso pleaseth God shall escape from that kind of woman, while the sinner is taken by her.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:24](/bible/sirach/26#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/sirach/26#v24)</sup> A dishonest woman contemneth shame: but an honest woman will reverence her husband.

- Verses 13 to 16 gave the righteous woman: gracious, discreet, silent, loving, well instructed, shamefaced, faithful, continent and orderly. Now the contrast, because sometimes we understand better by knowing what not to be.
- “A dishonest woman contemneth shame: but an honest woman will reverence her husband.” Dishonest is lying and dealing falsely, but it is also not being honest with herself in the manipulation she brings to her marriage by trying to be the head; she ends up deceiving herself. And if she doesn’t reverence him, she is dishonest and disloyal.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:25](/bible/sirach/26#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/sirach/26#v25)</sup> A shameless woman shall be counted as a dog; but she that is shamefaced will fear the Lord.

- “A shameless woman shall be counted as a dog; but she that is shamefaced will fear the Lord.” Christ told the Syrophenician woman he would not give the children’s bread to the dogs: it is not for you. Ephesians is about unity and how it is kept in ourselves, the congregation, the marriage and with the children. A wife who lets her house be torn down instead of building it up is counted as a dog. She can’t think she will ride into the kingdom of heaven on her husband’s righteousness or his position; once she becomes a wife in this truth she is accountable for her own actions before the Lord, her own members of sin, and her accountability to her husband and her children.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:26](/bible/sirach/26#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/sirach/26#v26)</sup> A woman that honoureth her husband shall be judged wise of all; but she that dishonoureth him in her pride shall be counted ungodly of all.

- “A woman that honoureth her husband shall be judged wise of all”: she will be able to get into the kingdom of heaven. “But she that dishonoureth him in her pride shall be counted ungodly of all,” and nothing ungodly enters the gates of the kingdom of heaven. Some Christian woman will clip this and ask whether she really can’t inherit the kingdom for being like that to her husband. Yes: a woman who marries and doesn’t do what is required of her as a wife is just biding her time.
- Notice it calls the lack of submission pride; not applying these things is pride.
- The godly wife shows grace, discretion, is silent and loving, well instructed, shamefaced and faithful, honours her husband and orders her house (some sisters have a lazy spirit and the house is always a mess, and that is part of it too). The contrary spirit is pride, dishonour, shamelessness, loudness, scolding, contention and undisciplined speech; some sisters think they get by because they don’t do the rest, but they have undisciplined speech. So she should ask: which woman am I becoming when conflict enters my marriage? She meditates on these chapters and deals with it, and it is not taken out on her husband, nor made a qualifier for how she treats him.
