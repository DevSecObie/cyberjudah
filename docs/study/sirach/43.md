## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 43 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 6-9, 27-28, 30-31, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 43:6](/bible/sirach/43#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/sirach/43#v6)</sup> He made the moon also to serve in her season for a declaration of times, and a sign of the world.

- Many times we get emails asking why the Passover dates are different amongst the Israelite camps. Here’s the main reason why many camps have different Passover dates than us: “He made the moon also to serve in her season for a declaration of times, and a sign of the world.”
- We are in some dark times, and the Lord has been showing us, but we don’t catch it. “He made the moon also to serve in her season for a declaration of times”: the moon serves in a season for a declaration of time.
- “And a sign of the world”: that’s the part right there. The moon symbolizes a sign of the world we live in. Put up the phases of the moon: what they call a full moon is actually the new moon. It goes from bright, and phases all the way to dark, decreasing; then it begins to increase again.
- “He made the moon also to serve in her season”: the moon serves the seasons, and the feasts of the Lord are seasonal. “For a declaration of times”: the high holy days and things of that nature.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Esdras 14:10-12](/bible/2-esdras/14#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/2-esdras/14#v10)</sup> For the world hath lost his youth, and the times begin to wax old.
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/2-esdras/14#v11)</sup> For the world is divided into twelve parts, and the ten parts of it are gone already, and half of a tenth part:
    >
    > <sup>[12](/bible/2-esdras/14#v12)</sup> And there remaineth that which is after the half of the tenth part.

    “For the world hath lost his youth, and the times begin to wax old.” “For the world is divided into twelve parts”: the angel is telling Ezra to look at the earth like a clock. Twelve parts on the clock, “and the ten parts of it are gone already”: ten o’clock, gone already, “and half of a tenth part”: that’s the 10:30 mark.
  - **[2 Esdras 6:6-10](/bible/2-esdras/6#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/2-esdras/6#v6)</sup> Then did I consider these things, and they all were made through me alone, and through none other: by me also they shall be ended, and by none other.
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/2-esdras/6#v7)</sup> Then answered I and said, What shall be the parting asunder of the times? or when shall be the end of the first, and the beginning of it that followeth?
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/2-esdras/6#v8)</sup> And he said unto me, From Abraham unto Isaac, when Jacob and Esau were born of him, Jacob’s hand held first the heel of Esau.
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/2-esdras/6#v9)</sup> For Esau is the end of the world, and Jacob is the beginning of it that followeth.
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/2-esdras/6#v10)</sup> The hand of man is betwixt the heel and the hand: other question, Esdras, ask thou not.

    “Then did I consider these things, and they all were made through me alone, and through none other”: that’s Christ speaking. “By me also they shall be ended, and by none other.” Ezra asks, “What shall be the parting asunder of the times? or when shall be the end of the first”: when shall be the end of the first world, “and the beginning of it that followeth?”, the beginning of the world that follows.
  - **[2 Esdras 11:1-2](/bible/2-esdras/11#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/2-esdras/11#v1)</sup> Then saw I a dream, and, behold, there came up from the sea an eagle, which had twelve feathered wings, and three heads.
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/2-esdras/11#v2)</sup> And I saw, and, behold, she spread her wings over all the earth, and all the winds of the air blew on her, and were gathered together.

    “Then saw I a dream, and, behold, there came up from the sea an eagle, which had twelve feathered wings, and three heads.” The question is, who was this? Obadiah answers it: Edom, whose symbol is the eagle. Then back here: “And I saw, and, behold, she spread her wings over all the earth, and all the winds of the air blew on her”. He’s talking about the eagle in all her forms: Esau, the end of the world.

**[Ecclesiasticus 43:7](/bible/sirach/43#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/sirach/43#v7)</sup> From the moon is the sign of feasts, a light that decreaseth in her perfection.

- “From the moon is the sign of feasts”: see that, the feasts are set by the moon. Then “a light that decreaseth in her perfection”. A light: that’s the key term. The moon is a light that decreases from her perfection.
- “A light that decreaseth in her perfection.” The new moon is bright, in her perfection. When was the earth in its perfection? During the time of Adam. Then it begins to decrease, and darkness begins to cover it till it gets black.
- When the moon is dark, that represents sin and Satan and all the wickedness, Edom and all the wickedness that we see taking place.
- So what have we learned? The moon is a declaration of times, a sign of the world; it’s symbolic of the world. It started out in its perfection and it decreased until it gets dark, as we read in Isaiah 60:2. Then after Esau, who comes? Brothers, Jacob, like we read, and it begins to go right back. That’s what the moon symbolizes.

    “For look how much the world shall be weaker through age, so much the more shall evils increase upon them that dwell therein.” Weaker through age means time: the more time goes by on this earth, the more evils will increase. That is the light decreasing from her perfection.

**[Ecclesiasticus 43:8](/bible/sirach/43#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/sirach/43#v8)</sup> The month is called after her name, increasing wonderfully in her changing, being an instrument of the armies above, shining in the firmament of heaven;

- “The month is called after her name”: that’s where the word month comes from; month means moon. “Increasing wonderfully in her changing, being an instrument of the armies above, shining in the firmament of heaven”.
- When you look at the moon phases, the new moon is what society calls the full moon. It decreases wonderfully in her changing till it’s dark, then it increases in her changing, which goes back to the full.
- “The month is called after her name”: the word month comes from the word moon. “Increasing wonderfully in her changing”: the phases of the moon.
- “Being an instrument of the armies above, shining in the firmament of heaven”: the moon is an instrument of the armies of heaven that follow Christ, like a base for them.

**[Ecclesiasticus 43:9](/bible/sirach/43#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/sirach/43#v9)</sup> The beauty of heaven, the glory of the stars, an ornament giving light in the highest places of the Lord.

- “The beauty of heaven, the glory of the stars, an ornament giving light in the highest places of the Lord.” Giving light: not dark and unseen, not a sliver of light. Giving light is her perfection.
- Read Ecclesiasticus 43 from verse 1 on down and it speaks of the rainbow, the sun and the moon, all the ornaments of heaven when they are bright and at their highest, at their peak: not at dusk, not at sundown. Every one of them is spoken of in its glory, in its perfection. So the moon in her glory, which is full, is the sign of the feast. Not the moon unseen, when no one knows where it is.

**[Ecclesiasticus 43:27](/bible/sirach/43#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/sirach/43#v27)</sup> We may speak much, and yet come short: wherefore in sum, he is all.

- “We may speak much, and yet come short.” We can’t pray to him enough for everything he created, everything he gives us. We could pray a million times a day and it would not be enough for all he does for us.

**[Ecclesiasticus 43:28](/bible/sirach/43#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/sirach/43#v28)</sup> How shall we be able to magnify him? for he is great above all his works.

- “How shall we be able to magnify him? for he is great above all his works.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 43:30](/bible/sirach/43#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/sirach/43#v30)</sup> When ye glorify the Lord, exalt him as much as ye can; for even yet will he far exceed: and when ye exalt him, put forth all your strength, and be not weary; for ye can never go far enough.

- “When ye glorify the Lord, exalt him as much as ye can.” When we pray, we have to exalt him as much as we can. “And when ye exalt him, put forth all your strength.” We have to put forth all our strength when we pray, and be sincere. Cry to him and tell him the truth. Not ‘Oh God, can you help me get a car,’ ‘Oh God, just protect me’: have serious, real prayers. God, I’m struggling. I’m fighting, Lord, to stand in this truth, to keep my head up, to do what’s right. I want to please you, Lord. I’m falling weak. I need wisdom, knowledge and understanding. I need faith; my faith is running low. “And be not weary; for ye can never go far enough.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 43:31](/bible/sirach/43#v31)**

> <sup>[31](/bible/sirach/43#v31)</sup> Who hath seen him, that he might tell us? and who can magnify him as he is?

- “Who hath seen him, that he might tell us? and who can magnify him as he is?” Nobody can magnify him as he is.
