## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 19 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 13-17, 24-26, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 19:13](/bible/sirach/19#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/19#v13)</sup> Admonish a friend, it may be he hath not done it: and if he have done it, that he do it no more.

- “Admonish a friend, it may be he hath not done it.” You hear somebody said something about you: go and admonish that person, correct and warn them, because sometimes the person never said it. It could be Satan messing with you, or somebody slandering or trying to cause confusion. “And if he have done it, that he do it no more”: if he did you wrong, tell him, don’t do that again.

**[Ecclesiasticus 19:14](/bible/sirach/19#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/sirach/19#v14)</sup> Admonish thy friend, it may be he hath not said it: and if he have, that he speak it not again.

- “Admonish thy friend, it may be he hath not said it: and if he have, that he speak it not again.” That is how to get rid of the root of bitterness: you hear something, or think somebody did something to you, go and admonish that person. Don’t hold on to it inside, because Satan can play with that and it can turn into the root of bitterness. If they did say something evil about you, tell them not to say it again; if they say they are sorry, forgive them, forget about it, and don’t hold on to it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 19:15](/bible/sirach/19#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/sirach/19#v15)</sup> Admonish a friend: for many times it is a slander, and believe not every tale.

- “Admonish a friend: for many times it is a slander, and believe not every tale.” A lot of times when somebody comes and says, this person said this and that, that person doesn’t like you, it is slander. It is Satan. Don’t believe everything you hear, because Satan is real.

**[Ecclesiasticus 19:16](/bible/sirach/19#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/sirach/19#v16)</sup> There is one that slippeth in his speech, but not from his heart; and who is he that hath not offended with his tongue?

- “There is one that slippeth in his speech, but not from his heart.” Brothers and sisters slip in their speech and say something offensive, and you think, why did that person say that to me? But they did not mean to hurt or offend you; they are just offensive when they speak. If you take it inside, you become bitter toward a person who meant nothing by it. God read their mind, but your mind took it as evil.
- “And who is he that hath not offended with his tongue?” Every one of us is going to offend from time to time with our tongue, saying something someone does not like even when we did not mean it in a bad way.

**[Ecclesiasticus 19:17](/bible/sirach/19#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/sirach/19#v17)</sup> Admonish thy neighbour before thou threaten him; and not being angry, give place to the law of the most High.

- “Admonish thy neighbour before thou threaten him”: warn your neighbour before you go threatening him, instead of wanting to fight somebody. “And not being angry, give place to the law of the most High”: you cannot be angry; always give place to the law of the Most High, meaning you go to that person and try to fix it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 19:24](/bible/sirach/19#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/sirach/19#v24)</sup> He that hath small understanding, and feareth God, is better than one that hath much wisdom, and transgresseth the law of the most High.

- The laws of God are extremely important. When we stand before Christ he’s not going to ask us to break down Daniel the 2nd chapter or the 7th chapter; he’s going to have the book open and compare what you have been doing in your life with the laws of God. “He that hath small understanding, and feareth God”: small understanding pertaining to the scriptures, not the applications of God’s laws but the prophecies and the different breakdowns. “Is better than one that hath much wisdom”: better than a brother who can go into those deep mysteries and break down, “and transgresseth the law of the most High.”
- The times of temptation are not only the trials coming from outside, from Trump, Netanyahu and the politics of the last days. Now that things are being set in motion that will try our faith, we have to look out for each other, and before we can do that we have to look out for ourselves: the trials and tribulations within ourselves.
- “He that hath small understanding, and feareth God, is better than one that hath much wisdom, and transgresseth the law of the most High.” Small understanding is the brother who does not know a lot of the scriptures, who is not really a precept guy, but he fears God. Much wisdom is the one who knows the scriptures, understands the Bible and the commandments, and breaks the laws of God anyway. The first is better.
- It is good to know about Trump, the last days, the prophecies and the current events of the world. But the most important thing to truly know is how to apply and keep God’s laws, and to know what is going on in here, in the heart and the mind.

  Precepts:
  - **[Exodus 20:14](/bible/exodus/20#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/exodus/20#v14)</sup> Thou shalt not commit adultery.

    The law on adultery. Brothers and sisters, because a lot of times we get on the men, but the sisters too have been getting caught up in adultery. “Thou shalt not commit adultery.” That’s a simple law. It’s written plainly; there’s no ifs and buts, no precepts needed. You don’t need precepts for that; it’s very plain to understand.
  - **[Deuteronomy 24:1-4](/bible/deuteronomy/24#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/24#v1)</sup> When a man hath taken a wife, and married her, and it come to pass that she find no favour in his eyes, because he hath found some uncleanness in her: then let him write her a bill of divorcement, and give it in her hand, and send her out of his house.
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/24#v2)</sup> And when she is departed out of his house, she may go and be another man’s wife.
    >
    > <sup>[3](/bible/deuteronomy/24#v3)</sup> And if the latter husband hate her, and write her a bill of divorcement, and giveth it in her hand, and sendeth her out of his house; or if the latter husband die, which took her to be his wife;
    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/deuteronomy/24#v4)</sup> Her former husband, which sent her away, may not take her again to be his wife, after that she is defiled; for that is abomination before the Lord: and thou shalt not cause the land to sin, which the Lord thy God giveth thee for an inheritance.

    “Her former husband, which sent her away, may not take her again to be his wife, after that she is defiled”: these are plain words, English words. You don’t have to go into Aramaic or Hebrew; you don’t need Bible Hub or Blue Letter Bible. “For that is abomination before the Lord”: we all know what an abomination is, a putrid, detestable thing that God hates. “And thou shalt not cause the land to sin”: when you do that you cause the land to sin, because it’s a domino effect; one person does it, the next person does it, when it goes unchecked and unpunished.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 19:2](/bible/sirach/19#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/19#v2)</sup> Wine and women will make men of understanding to fall away: and he that cleaveth to harlots will become impudent.

    “Wine and women will make men of understanding to fall away.” This is how the man of much wisdom comes to transgress the law. The wine here goes two ways. It is literally liquor: you go to the club, they serve the liquor, everyone gets drunk and loose, and things take place that should not. And it is doctrines. So the man of small understanding who fears God is better than the man of much wisdom who falls for women and wine, women and doctrines, which make men of understanding to fall away.

**[Ecclesiasticus 19:25](/bible/sirach/19#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/sirach/19#v25)</sup> There is an exquisite subtilty, and the same is unjust; and there is one that turneth aside to make judgment appear; and there is a wise man that justifieth in judgment.

- “There is an exquisite subtilty”: someone that’s very subtle, slick, cunning, hunting, like how Satan was with Eve, subtle. “And the same is unjust”: that person who is exquisitely subtle is unjust.
- “And there is one that turneth aside to make judgment appear”: one that deals with things righteously, to reveal judgment. One is slick and cunning and subtle; the other exposes things. “And there is a wise man that justifieth in judgment.”
- “There is an exquisite subtilty, and the same is unjust.” It is speaking of a person: there is a person of exquisite subtlety.
- Exquisite is something of flawless craftsmanship, delicate beauty, or rare and consummate excellence: carefully crafted, very detailed, very intricate, extremely pleasing to the senses, of special beauty or charm, of rare and appealing excellence, like a face, a flower, music or poetry. Add one more thing to that list: a doctrine. A doctrine can be taught in a way where you can’t catch on. It is very deceptive. It is not beautiful, it is charming, appealing, alluring, seductive.

**[Ecclesiasticus 19:26](/bible/sirach/19#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/sirach/19#v26)</sup> There is a wicked man that hangeth down his head sadly; but inwardly he is full of deceit,

- Verse 26 elaborates on that exquisite subtlety: a wicked man that hangs down his head sadly, but is “full of deceit”.
