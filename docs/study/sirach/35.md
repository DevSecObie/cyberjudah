## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 35 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1, 3, 8-9, 12, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 35:1](/bible/sirach/35#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/35#v1)</sup> He that keepeth the law bringeth offerings enough: he that taketh heed to the commandment offereth a peace offering.

- “He that keepeth the law bringeth offerings enough: he that taketh heed to the commandment offereth a peace offering.” When you keep the commandments of God, that is sacrifice enough. Sacrificing was a temporary thing.

**[Ecclesiasticus 35:3](/bible/sirach/35#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/sirach/35#v3)</sup> To depart from wickedness is a thing pleasing to the Lord; and to forsake unrighteousness is a propitiation.

- “To depart from wickedness is a thing pleasing to the Lord”: to forsake sin, turn your back on sin, is pleasing to God. God loves that. Remove yourself from your sin.
- “And to forsake unrighteousness is a propitiation.” The unrighteous are not going to get the kingdom. Propitiation is like atonement: the action of propitiating or appeasing a god. Forsaking unrighteousness is pleasing and appeasing God. No unrighteous man is going to get the kingdom, brothers.
- “To depart from wickedness is a thing pleasing to the Lord.” To depart from evil, to depart from your sin, is a beautiful thing; that thing pleases the Lord. “And to forsake unrighteousness is a propitiation.” To forsake sin. Propitiation is like an atonement, a payment, appeasing God: forsaking your sin is an atonement that pleases God.
- “To depart from wickedness is a thing pleasing to the Lord; and to forsake unrighteousness is a propitiation.” Propitiation goes back to atonement: conciliation to the Lord, bringing you close to the Lord.

**[Ecclesiasticus 35:8-9](/bible/sirach/35#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/sirach/35#v8)</sup> Give the Lord his honour with a good eye, and diminish not the firstfruits of thine hands.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/sirach/35#v9)</sup> In all thy gifts shew a cheerful countenance, and dedicate thy tithes with gladness.

- “Diminish not the firstfruits of thine hands”: sacrifice. “In all thy gifts shew a cheerful countenance, and dedicate thy tithes with gladness.”
- Do not walk around depressed and angry and sad. That spirit is contagious. Some walk around with a miserable countenance, sad all the time, woe is me. Some sit in the audience and look up on purpose to get attention: look at me, look at my tears. We ignore that.

**[Ecclesiasticus 35:12](/bible/sirach/35#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/35#v12)</sup> Do not think to corrupt with gifts; for such he will not receive: and trust not to unrighteous sacrifices; for the Lord is judge, and with him is no respect of persons.

- “Do not think to corrupt with gifts; for such he will not receive”. That goes into bribery: having hatred for your brother, but offering the best sacrifice so the Most High will overlook it. I will offer the best of my flock, but I can’t stand that brother across the street. The Most High does not care about that: it is a corrupt gift, and he will not receive it.
- “And trust not to unrighteous sacrifices; for the Lord is judge, and with him is no respect of persons.” He does not care about your best ox or your best sheep. If you have hatred for your people or your brother, it is a waste of a sacrifice and a waste of time.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 5:22-24](/bible/matthew/5#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/matthew/5#v22)</sup> But I say unto you, That whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment: and whosoever shall say to his brother, Raca, shall be in danger of the council: but whosoever shall say, Thou fool, shall be in danger of hell fire.
    >
    > <sup>[23](/bible/matthew/5#v23)</sup> Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee;
    >
    > <sup>[24](/bible/matthew/5#v24)</sup> Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.

    Christ says the same thing. “But I say unto you, That whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment”. Without a cause: sometimes a brother will make you angry, but this is without a cause. “Whosoever shall say to his brother, Raca”, you are a fool, “shall be in danger of the council: but whosoever shall say, Thou fool, shall be in danger of hell fire.” Because he will maintain that anger, that rage, that wrath against you forever.
