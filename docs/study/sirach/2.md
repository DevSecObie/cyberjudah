## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 2 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-5, 7-17, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 2:1](/bible/sirach/2#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/2#v1)</sup> My son, if thou come to serve the Lord, prepare thy soul for temptation.

- “My son, if thou come to serve the Lord, prepare thy soul for temptation.” So you men and women, when you come to serve the Lord, prepare your soul for temptation. That temptation in verse 1 is the adversity in verse 5.
- “My son, if thou come to serve the Lord, prepare thy soul for temptation.” This is where we take full swipes at Christianity, the religion, or better said the witchcraft of Christianity, because it does not teach you how to prepare yourself for temptation. It does not teach the laws of God. It just says believe in Jesus and you will be saved. That is absurd.
- You can believe that the man Jesus Christ came on the earth and died, but believing is an action word. It is not just saying I believe. A man can believe that if he stops eating carbs he will lose weight; believe it all you want, unless you stop eating carbs you are not losing any weight.
- The laws of God are not grievous. There is no law in the Bible impossible for us to keep: do not murder, do not commit adultery, do not steal. Other beliefs demand far harder things, like strapping a bomb to yourself or beating your own back with a chain. The biggest thing we have to do is fast for twenty-four hours. What is hard, women not wearing pants? The law is only hard to one who does not want to change. We are a rebellious people that will not hear the law of the Lord, and then we get mad and hate.

So when we go out there we are not confused that the people will turn against us, that the government will be against us, that we will be hated. We understand that.

**[Ecclesiasticus 2:17](/bible/sirach/2#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/sirach/2#v17)</sup> They that fear the Lord will prepare their hearts, and humble their souls in his sight,

- “They that fear the Lord will prepare their hearts”: that is the fresh water. We prepare our heart for death and for the unjust thoughts that will trouble us in this life. Shake off the sabbath sickness and the feast day flu.
- “And humble their souls in his sight”. When you fear the Lord, you prepare your heart to humble your soul in the presence of God, and you will not disobey his words. Fasting is a form of humbleness; praying is a form of humbleness; taking heed to what the leaders said and perfecting it is a form of humbleness.
- Ask yourself: are you in this truth to perfect yourself, or to play games? If you are here to perfect it, you will meditate on these things day and night; if not, you will have an excuse for everything. Nine or ten years in the truth and still a soldier or an officer of ten: nobody holds you back but your sin, the gossip, the complaining, the bitterness. God does the promoting. A brother once wondered why he was not raised up to captain, and it came out that he was fighting homosexuality within him, in the mix of his sin; asked which one he was, he said, I'm the receiver. You want to be a captain and you never killed that demon? This is a spiritual war, and we can see beyond and read your mind. You cannot be a leader over men while you never fight what is inside you.
