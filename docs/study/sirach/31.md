## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 31 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 13, 16-20, 27-30, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 31:13](/bible/sirach/31#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/31#v13)</sup> Remember that a wicked eye is an evil thing: and what is created more wicked than an eye? therefore it weepeth upon every occasion.

- “Remember that a wicked eye is an evil thing”. It starts with your eyes and your mind: you see something, it hits your mind, and then you have to decide whether you will give in to that lust and feed it. “And what is created more wicked than an eye?” Desire starts first with what you see.

**[Ecclesiasticus 31:16-17](/bible/sirach/31#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/sirach/31#v16)</sup> Eat, as it becometh a man, those things which are set before thee; and devour not, lest thou be hated.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/sirach/31#v17)</sup> Leave off first for manners’ sake; and be not unsatiable, lest thou offend.

- “Eat, as it becometh a man, those things which are set before thee”, “lest thou be hated. Leave off first for manners’ sake; and be not unsatiable, lest thou offend.” It goes into how we should be when it comes to food, as well as table manners. All these things strengthen us.
- You got to teach your family how to eat. It is your job, brothers, to teach your family how to eat, so you can fulfill your purpose. “Eat, as it becometh a man, those things which are set before thee”: eat the way a man ought to eat.
- “Eat, as it becometh a man, those things which are set before thee”. Don’t devour, gorging food down all day every day. Eat the way you are supposed to eat, to live. The undisciplined man overeats, oversleeps, and is always tired and sluggish.
- “And be not unsatiable, lest thou offend.” Some people want to grab everything; their plate looks like a mountain, and after that they eat again. A lot of brothers living near Golden Corrals are in there all day every day. That is not helping you, it is destroying you.

**[Ecclesiasticus 31:18-19](/bible/sirach/31#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/sirach/31#v18)</sup> When thou sittest among many, reach not thine hand out first of all.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/sirach/31#v19)</sup> A very little is sufficient for a man well nurtured, and he fetcheth not his wind short upon his bed.

- “When thou sittest among many, reach not thine hand out first of all. A very little is sufficient for a man well nurtured”: you just eat healthy foods; that’s what well nurtured is, in the proper moderation.
- “A very little is sufficient for a man well nurtured.” If you eat right, you don’t have to eat a lot. If you want to burn fat you have to eat protein; some people do the keto diet; you eat less, you move more, you exercise. Those are the ways to burn fat, to shed fat, and you can do it.
- But you have to do it first, brothers, and then the sisters will get in line when they see your example. When you ain’t doing nothing and they see you ain’t doing nothing, they just fall right into that same rut. Every time in my life I’ve gained weight, my wife gained weight; every time in my life I’ve lost weight, she’s lost weight. We one flesh. She can’t sit down while you going to the gym every day; she’ll say, ‘I’m looking bad, let me go with you.’ That’s what ends up happening. They follow our lead.
- “A very little is sufficient for a man well nurtured”. Very little, because you want to live a long life and be healthy. “And he fetcheth not his wind short upon his bed.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 31:20](/bible/sirach/31#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/sirach/31#v20)</sup> Sound sleep cometh of moderate eating: he riseth early, and his wits are with him: but the pain of watching, and choler, and pangs of the belly, are with an unsatiable man.

- “Sound sleep cometh of moderate eating: he riseth early, and his wits are with him: but the pain of watching, and choler, and pangs of the belly, are with an unsatiable man.”
- You would think, damn, why jump into diet after all this wisdom stuff in the head? Remember: your thoughts become words, words become actions, then habits, and then it becomes your character. Notice it didn’t say unsatiable appetite; it said unsatiable man. If you can’t display your discipline in your fatness and in your drunkenness, you might be an alcoholic, one of those high-functioning ones, always drinking on the weekends or at home, thinking you don’t have an alcohol problem because you’re not sloppy drunk all the time. You’re unsatiable.
- There’s a connection between the stomach and the character. They call your digestive system the second brain now, and they say the way you care for it affects everything else; it’s the key to a lot of diseases and longevity. If you’re an unsatiable person in your eating, in your alcohol, whatever it is that you do, you will have that as your actions.
- “Sound sleep cometh of moderate eating”. With too much sugar the brain is overactive and you can’t sleep; sugar keeps you up at night, spiking and crashing your blood sugar, making for restless, lighter sleep and more awakenings, then more sugar cravings the next day. It is a never-ending cycle, and over the years it brings mental problems and mood swings because you are never well rested.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 18:30](/bible/sirach/18#v30)**
    > <sup>[30](/bible/sirach/18#v30)</sup> Go not after thy lusts, but refrain thyself from thine appetites.

    This is why it says, “Go not after thy lusts, but refrain thyself from thine appetites.” Don’t be unsatiable. It calls them appetites; it likens lust to food.

**[Ecclesiasticus 31:27](/bible/sirach/31#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/sirach/31#v27)</sup> Wine is as good as life to a man, if it be drunk moderately: what life is then to a man that is without wine? for it was made to make men glad.

- Brothers, every one of you knows your limit: two and you are done, three and you are done. Get caught drunk on the road and you bring shame to the gospel, stumbling out of the car in your purple garments or feast day garments, maybe after hurting somebody. Those are the little things that will save your life.
- “Wine is as good as life to a man, if it be drunk moderately.” If you drink enough to get drunk, that is not moderate. Some argue with and curse their wives when drunk, some get violent; you destroy your marriage and your family because you cannot control your liquor, and most of all you lose the kingdom. For some it is weed, a sister getting high before she walked in to the Sabbath.
- “What life is then to a man that is without wine?” Everybody wants to sip once in a while, but you must know how to control yourself. “For it was made to make men glad.” That is why they call it spirit: there is a spirit in it, and some people cannot handle even a glass. It was made to make men glad, not violent, not argumentative, not belligerent, not verbally abusive. If you find yourself in any of those spirits after drinking, you need to stop. A brother choking his wife and blaming the drinking cannot control himself, has no discipline, and is not a leader.
- “Wine is as good as life to a man, if it be drunk moderately: what life is then to a man that is without wine? for it was made to make men glad.” The permission is joined to moderation. Drinking is not itself being called a sin, but the word moderately cannot be reduced to a feeling that one is not yet drunk. Moderation includes avoiding extremes, observing reasonable limits and remaining temperate.

**[Ecclesiasticus 31:28-29](/bible/sirach/31#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/sirach/31#v28)</sup> Wine measurably drunk and in season bringeth gladness of the heart, and cheerfulness of the mind:
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/sirach/31#v29)</sup> But wine drunken with excess maketh bitterness of the mind, with brawling and quarrelling.

- “Wine measurably drunk and in season bringeth gladness of the heart, and cheerfulness of the mind.” There is nothing wrong with having a drink, but know your measure, know your limits. Don’t pick up the bottle before Esau’s workplace where they know you are an Israelite; you bring shame to the gospel. How do you expect your family to repent when you show up drunk? Your sons and daughters take it in, and if you are abusing your wife when drunk you are abusing the kids too, if not physically then verbally.
- “Wine measurably drunk and in season bringeth gladness of the heart, and cheerfulness of the mind:” Both measure and season matter. Drinking every day is challenged as not being in season. The pattern is visible to the people closest to a man even when he denies it. A wife may see the daily drinking or find bottles in the car long before he admits what is happening.
- Defensiveness does not refute what she sees. Examine the evidence honestly instead of silencing her or pretending the pattern is invisible. That self-examination belongs to leaders too: receive Deacon Yosua's instruction, take a step back and reevaluate the habit instead of treating correction as something for everyone else. Mutual accountability is proposed so the brothers can address dependency and remain blameless.
- “Wine measurably drunk and in season bringeth gladness of the heart, and cheerfulness of the mind.” Measurably drunk, and in season.

    A history of one of our forefathers and drunkenness. Simon was set up: “So when Simon and his sons had drunk largely, Ptolemee and his men rose up, and took their weapons, and came upon Simon into the banqueting place, and slew him, and his two sons.” Notice it says he had drunk largely.

**[Ecclesiasticus 31:30](/bible/sirach/31#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/sirach/31#v30)</sup> Drunkenness increaseth the rage of a fool till he offend: it diminisheth strength, and maketh wounds.

- “Drunkenness increaseth the rage of a fool till he offend.” The Bible calls you a fool. We don’t want to hear that you were drunk, officer of ten or of fifty. There is no excuse.
- Drunkenness increases a fool's rage until he offends. Words that would not be spoken sober can become insults and disrespect. The drunk declaration, I always hated your ass, nigga, exposes a hostility that has been kept hidden. It wounds relationships as well as putting the body in danger.
- “it diminisheth strength, and maketh wounds.” Waking with injuries or evidence of events one cannot remember is no sign of strength. The comic extremes in The Hangover illustrate the loss of control, but the underlying issue is the real damage caused when a person has surrendered judgment and restraint.
- “Drunkenness increaseth the rage of a fool till he offend.” You are a fool now, ready to fight everybody. “It diminisheth strength, and maketh wounds.” Your discernment leaves you and your wisdom is gone; you can't make good choices. That is why brothers get DUIs, hitting the white line twenty times and running the stop sign.
