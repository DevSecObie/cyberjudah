## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 21 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 2, 4-7, 14-15, 19, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 21:2](/bible/sirach/21#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/21#v2)</sup> Flee from sin as from the face of a serpent: for if thou comest too near it, it will bite thee: the teeth thereof are as the teeth of a lion, slaying the souls of men.

- “Flee from sin as from the face of a serpent”. Don't play with it, don't caress it, don't charm it. When you see sin, flee from it.
- “For if thou comest too near it, it will bite thee”. Some of you play with sin so much that you're within striking distance. Because the snake is coiled up you think you're safe at a foot away, but its striking distance could be two feet, and by the time you take two steps you're done. Joseph put his Nikes on and was out: he had never been with a woman, and when she took hold of him he knew if he didn't run, he was done. Many of us like to entertain sin a little more, and end up in the deep miry clay, in a dunghill.
- “The teeth thereof are as the teeth of a lion, slaying the souls of men.” The devil is out there like a raging lion, roaming about seeking whom he may devour, smelling everybody, coming back tomorrow, and then heads off. Why? Because you entertained your sins. Keep yourself in the laws.
- “Flee from sin as from the face of a serpent”. We use the word flee for lust, and the same applies to food: you cannot flee if it is in your refrigerator. Flee from that refrigerator, flee from the unhealthy foods. “For if thou comest too near it, it will bite thee”, it will bite you and destroy you. That is why many brothers have health issues, cannot make it to the Sabbath, and are in the hospital every week. You need a healthy vessel to do the work productively.

**[Ecclesiasticus 21:4](/bible/sirach/21#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/sirach/21#v4)</sup> To terrify and do wrong will waste riches: thus the house of proud men shall be made desolate.

- “To terrify and do wrong will waste riches: thus the house of proud men shall be made desolate.” A lot of brothers out there who are well off, and these celebrities, waste their riches, their blessings, because they are all in sin.

**[Ecclesiasticus 21:5](/bible/sirach/21#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/sirach/21#v5)</sup> A prayer out of a poor man’s mouth reacheth to the ears of God, and his judgment cometh speedily.

- “A prayer out of a poor man’s mouth reacheth to the ears of God, and his judgment cometh speedily.” A poor man’s mouth is a poor spirit, a contrite and humble heart: Lord, I’m battling, have mercy upon me, help me battle this. God sees that you are sincere.

**[Ecclesiasticus 21:6](/bible/sirach/21#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/sirach/21#v6)</sup> He that hateth to be reproved is in the way of sinners: but he that feareth the Lord will repent from his heart.

- “He that hateth to be reproved is in the way of sinners.” When you hate correction and find an excuse, you are in the way of sinners. “But he that feareth the Lord will repent from his heart”: sincerely, not out of a grudge or anger or because someone said so, but because you know so and you acknowledge it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 21:7](/bible/sirach/21#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/sirach/21#v7)</sup> An eloquent man is known far and near; but a man of understanding knoweth when he slippeth.

- “An eloquent man is known far and near”: by how he carries himself, how he speaks, how he teaches and how he judges a matter. “But a man of understanding knoweth when he slippeth.” Whether you are messing up, when you are going through things, only you know.

**[Ecclesiasticus 21:14](/bible/sirach/21#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/sirach/21#v14)</sup> The inner parts of a fool are like a broken vessel, and he will hold no knowledge as long as he liveth.

- “The inner parts of a fool are like a broken vessel, and he will hold no knowledge as long as he liveth.” These brothers have lost all wisdom out of their vessel, if they ever obtained it at all. That is why foolishness spews out of their mouths. Israel must understand that this line will not go throughout the earth. The blueprint is simple: the secret sauce is keeping the commandments.

**[Ecclesiasticus 21:15](/bible/sirach/21#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/sirach/21#v15)</sup> If a skilful man hear a wise word, he will commend it, and add unto it: but as soon as one of no understanding heareth it, it displeaseth him, and he casteth it behind his back.

- “If a skilful man hear a wise word, he will commend it, and add unto it: but as soon as one of no understanding heareth it, it displeaseth him, and he casteth it behind his back.” When a man of no understanding hears the commandments and that he must keep them, it displeaseth him, and he casts it behind his back. That is what we are looking at: every manner of foolishness coming out of these brothers' mouths, down to an elder saying he will hire himself out as the Hebrew Hammer if the people don't pay their tithes.

**[Ecclesiasticus 21:19](/bible/sirach/21#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/sirach/21#v19)</sup> Doctrine unto fools is as fetters on the feet, and like manacles on the right hand.

- These are the thoughts of the wicked. “Doctrine unto fools is as fetters on the feet”: the doctrine, the laws of God, to a fool is like being handcuffed or being in prison. They can’t stand it. “And like manacles on the right hand”: handcuffs on the hands.
- I can’t do nothing, man. I ain’t joining that group. I agree with the history but not the theology. Because the laws, doctrines and commandments of God are fetters on their feet and manacles on their hands, they have no freedom in their mind when keeping God’s laws. It is prison to them. So they stay to themselves and stay separate.
