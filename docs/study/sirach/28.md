## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 28 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-8, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 28:1](/bible/sirach/28#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/28#v1)</sup> He that revengeth shall find vengeance from the Lord, and he will surely keep his sins in remembrance.

- Some, instead of repenting when the Lord shames them, set their mind on revenge. “He that revengeth shall find vengeance from the Lord.” Man did not write that scripture; God did. You seek to take vengeance when you did not see that the Lord brought you to your shame so you would repent, and God’s spirit is going to judge your simple behind. Vengeance belongs to him, so when you think about vengeance, God already has your number. God does not play with you. Look at those men in Austin who were always aiming for judgment: God Almighty pulled the cards on each one of them. If you don’t want the vengeance of the Lord coming to you, apply Matthew 18; it is going to help you.
- “And he will surely keep his sins in remembrance.” When you aim for vengeance, your sins still remain. You have a covetous spirit in you, and your brother has a covetous spirit; worry about your own. He is trying to fix himself while you are aiming to destroy him. You have a lust: fix you. The Lord says your sin still remains; that is not my word, that is what God said.
- “He that revengeth shall find vengeance from the Lord”. As we read, thou shalt not avenge nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people. If your brother did something evil to you and you go back and do evil to him, God will bring vengeance on you. “And he will surely keep his sins in remembrance.” God will remember your sin any time you take vengeance.

**[Ecclesiasticus 28:2](/bible/sirach/28#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/28#v2)</sup> Forgive thy neighbour the hurt that he hath done unto thee, so shall thy sins also be forgiven when thou prayest.

- “Forgive thy neighbour the hurt that he hath done unto thee”. Put emphasis on the hurt. People are going to hurt you. Wives might hurt their husbands, husbands their wives. A brother might step out and commit fornication on his wife, and she is going to feel hurt. A brother might carry a false report to leadership and lie on you, and it hurts. But God says forgive thy neighbour the hurt. Your neighbour first and foremost is your spouse; your neighbour is your brother, your race, your people. We have to learn to forgive each other.
- “So shall thy sins also be forgiven when thou prayest.” That is the only way God will forgive you when you pray. When you sin against your God, you hurt him; he is a jealous God. Yet your brother did ought against you and you can’t forgive him, but you want God to forgive you?
- In this truth there has been much hurt from people, enough to say, I ain’t messing with none of these dudes no more. But you have to forgive. The Bishop taught years ago the three trials of faith: personal, marital and congregational. Congregational is when someone among you offends you; marital is when your husband says something hurtful. Will you forgive, or shut yourself down and carry secret hatred? Each of us has to decide whether we will apply what this Bible says.

  Precepts:
  - **[Luke 17:3](/bible/luke/17#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/luke/17#v3)</sup> Take heed to yourselves: If thy brother trespass against thee, rebuke him; and if he repent, forgive him.

    This is the key thing with repentance. “Take heed to yourselves: If thy brother trespass against thee, rebuke him”. If your brother did evil against you, rebuke him: bro, you were wrong for what you did, don’t do that again.
  - **[Matthew 18:21-22](/bible/matthew/18#v21)**
    > <sup>[21](/bible/matthew/18#v21)</sup> Then came Peter to him, and said, Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times?
    >
    > <sup>[22](/bible/matthew/18#v22)</sup> Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven.

    “Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times?” “I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven.” That is four hundred and ninety times. If he comes and acknowledges what he did wrong and says he is sorry, you have to forgive him. You cannot say, I don’t think he really repented, so I’m not forgiving him. You want a good conscience before God.
  - **[Matthew 18:35](/bible/matthew/18#v35)**
    > <sup>[35](/bible/matthew/18#v35)</sup> So likewise shall my heavenly Father do also unto you, if ye from your hearts forgive not every one his brother their trespasses.

    “So likewise shall my heavenly Father do also unto you, if ye from your hearts forgive not every one his brother their trespasses.” From your hearts, not from your mouth. You can say I forgive you while deep down you are still carrying secret hatred, a grudge for what that person did, and you don’t want to let it go even though they said they were sorry.

**[Ecclesiasticus 28:3](/bible/sirach/28#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/sirach/28#v3)</sup> One man beareth hatred against another, and doth he seek pardon from the Lord?

- “One man beareth hatred against another, and doth he seek pardon from the Lord?” You carry secret hatred against somebody, and you want God to forgive you?

**[Ecclesiasticus 28:4](/bible/sirach/28#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/sirach/28#v4)</sup> He sheweth no mercy to a man, which is like himself: and doth he ask forgiveness of his own sins?

- “He sheweth no mercy to a man, which is like himself: and doth he ask forgiveness of his own sins?” You don’t even show mercy to a man who is just like you, and you want forgiveness for the evil you did.

**[Ecclesiasticus 28:5](/bible/sirach/28#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/sirach/28#v5)</sup> If he that is but flesh nourish hatred, who will intreat for pardon of his sins?

- “If he that is but flesh nourish hatred, who will intreat for pardon of his sins?” To nourish hatred is to feed it secretly: you keep talking about it, I can’t stand that, he did this to me again. If you hold on to it and say, I will never forgive you, not until I die, the Lord will say, then you will die with that hatred.

**[Ecclesiasticus 28:6](/bible/sirach/28#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/sirach/28#v6)</sup> Remember thy end, and let enmity cease; remember corruption and death, and abide in the commandments.

- “Remember thy end, and let enmity cease”. Let hatred cease. We all have to die eventually. What sense is there in living on this earth hating everybody all the time, when eventually I die like everybody else and have to go before my maker? Let hatred go. “Remember corruption and death, and abide in the commandments.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 28:7](/bible/sirach/28#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/sirach/28#v7)</sup> Remember the commandments, and bear no malice to thy neighbour: remember the covenant of the Highest, and wink at ignorance.

- “Remember the commandments, and bear no malice to thy neighbour”: don’t bear secret hatred towards your neighbour. “Remember the covenant of the Highest, and wink at ignorance.” Ignorance is when your brother is ignorant, he doesn’t understand. When somebody does something hurtful or stupid to you, you have to wink at it.
- “Remember the commandments, and bear no malice to thy neighbour: remember the covenant of the Highest, and wink at ignorance.” To wink at ignorance means sometimes people say dumb stupid stuff. Sometimes it is immaturity, the person has not grown, and they probably didn’t mean it that way. For the root of bitterness not to build up in you, sometimes you just have to wink at it: that person just doesn’t know. Don’t take it seriously.
- When people do evil things to you, wink at it; don’t let bitterness rise up inside you. Everybody here is not perfect and people will get on your nerves. We are trying to become perfect, with people from different walks of life, so it is all about applying the law of loving each other as we love ourselves and getting rid of bitterness.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 5:38-39](/bible/matthew/5#v38)**
    > <sup>[38](/bible/matthew/5#v38)</sup> Ye have heard that it hath been said, An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth:
    >
    > <sup>[39](/bible/matthew/5#v39)</sup> But I say unto you, That ye resist not evil: but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also.

    This explains winking at ignorance. “Ye have heard that it hath been said, An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth”. You poke my eye, I poke out yours; you knock out my tooth, I knock out yours. That was the law.

**[Ecclesiasticus 28:8](/bible/sirach/28#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/sirach/28#v8)</sup> Abstain from strife, and thou shalt diminish thy sins: for a furious man will kindle strife.

- “Abstain from strife, and thou shalt diminish thy sins: for a furious man will kindle strife.” Strife is arguing: the arguing and the bitterness, going back and forth. Brothers and sisters who keep arguing and striving all the damn time have something evil in them. Abstaining from strife is how you diminish your sins.
