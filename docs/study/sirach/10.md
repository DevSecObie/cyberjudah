## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 10 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-3, 7-9, 12-13, 18, 20-21, 24-25, 27, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 10:1](/bible/sirach/10#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/10#v1)</sup> A wise judge will instruct his people; and the government of a prudent man is well ordered.

- “A wise judge will instruct his people”: instruct them in the laws of God. “And the government of a prudent man is well ordered”: ordered as it is written.
- “A wise judge will instruct his people; and the government of a prudent man is well ordered.” The work establishes government. Its order includes many smaller responsibilities that make up the whole. Being a powerful street teacher does not excuse holes in the building, neglected operations or unresolved problems in the congregation. A one-sided leader can focus on the principal mission and overlook the conditions needed to carry it out.
- Do not blame everything on the men you have. A leader must find and develop the diamonds in the rough, ask for help where he lacks understanding and inspect whether expectations are being met. Overconfidence, pride and willful ignorance all undermine order. Accepting blame with an apology is only a beginning; repeated apologies without correction leave the problem standing.
- “A wise judge will instruct his people; and the government of a prudent man is well ordered.”

  Precepts:
  - **[Titus 1:5](/bible/titus/1#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/titus/1#v5)</sup> For this cause left I thee in Crete, that thou shouldest set in order the things that are wanting, and ordain elders in every city, as I had appointed thee:

    Titus was left in Crete to “set in order the things that are wanting, and ordain elders in every city, as I had appointed thee:” This is the government of the prudent man in Ecclesiasticus. Elders, rank, structure and processes exist so the work is ordered and carried out properly. Appointment is accompanied by responsibility to identify and correct what is lacking, not merely to occupy an office.
