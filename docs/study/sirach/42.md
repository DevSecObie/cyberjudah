## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 42 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 12-14, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 42:12-13](/bible/sirach/42#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/42#v12)</sup> Behold not every body’s beauty, and sit not in the midst of women.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/42#v13)</sup> For from garments cometh a moth, and from women wickedness.

- How do you deal with not fitting in with the sisters, even when you try and haven’t offended anyone? It’s a big problem with a lot of you women. I’m not saying you, because I don’t know you, but I’m telling you what the Bible says. “Behold not every body’s beauty, and sit not in the midst of women.”
- Who does that, who looks at everybody? Women. They size you up: your shoes, your hair, your nails, your dress. They’re looking for things, and if you don’t meet those standards they treat you funny: you don’t have makeup, you don’t have earrings, look at you, you’re fat, you’re skinny.
- So a woman comes to this truth and tells me, I don’t know what’s wrong with the women in this school, they’re not nice to me. I tell them sit your ass down, that’s not what you’re here for, to be everybody’s friend. When the time comes for you to help the body and to let your light shine, you do it, but other than that don’t come here trying to be everybody’s friend. “Sit not in the midst of women.”
- I will never tell you how to befriend women, because I don’t know how, and the Bible doesn’t say. It just warns you: don’t try to be that sister that always wants to be in the midst. Somehow the other sisters are going to have your name. They’re going to be looking at how you did this, how you did that, how you was with your kids, how you was over here. They’re going to watch you, you’re going to feel uncomfortable, and then you’re going to blame the church. You know how many women left IUIC blaming me for stuff that I don’t know what’s going on? So I stay out of women’s business, because they’re not here to learn; they’re doing exactly what the scripture says, and it’s something I cannot fix.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Corinthians 14:33-34](/bible/1-corinthians/14#v33)**
    > <sup>[33](/bible/1-corinthians/14#v33)</sup> For God is not the author of confusion, but of peace, as in all churches of the saints.
    >
    > <sup>[34](/bible/1-corinthians/14#v34)</sup> Let your women keep silence in the churches: for it is not permitted unto them to speak; but they are commanded to be under obedience, as also saith the law.

    That’s why Paul had this written. “For God is not the author of confusion, but of peace, as in all churches of the saints”: God wants peace in all the churches, and this is the solution. “Let your women keep silence in the churches”: the best way to keep peace, Paul said, is for them to just be quiet. “For it is not permitted unto them.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 42:14](/bible/sirach/42#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/sirach/42#v14)</sup> Better is the churlishness of a man than a courteous woman, a woman, I say, which bringeth shame and reproach.

- “Better is the churlishness of a man than a courteous woman”: a churlish man is a mean man, and God said that’s better than a courteous woman. “A woman, I say, which bringeth shame and reproach.” She’s courteous, ‘Shalom, Most High in Christ bless,’ but she’s going to bring shame and reproach. Some women know how to work their way into your heart, into that circle, but when you get to meet them you’re like, yo, I didn’t know she was so obnoxious, so arrogant, so dismissive, so mean. The way you overlooked that is because she’s courteous. After you get to meet her you’re like, yo, I hate this girl. You give her a Shalom and you’re out, because you know some drama is attached to her.
