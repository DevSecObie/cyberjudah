## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 41 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 3-4, 6-7, 17, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 41:3](/bible/sirach/41#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/sirach/41#v3)</sup> Fear not the sentence of death, remember them that have been before thee, and that come after; for this is the sentence of the Lord over all flesh.

- “Fear not the sentence of death, remember them that have been before thee, and that come after; for this is the sentence of the Lord over all flesh.” Death is the sentence from the Lord over all flesh. Everybody is destined to die; this physical body is destined to perish.

**[Ecclesiasticus 41:4](/bible/sirach/41#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/sirach/41#v4)</sup> And why art thou against the pleasure of the most High? there is no inquisition in the grave, whether thou have lived ten, or an hundred, or a thousand years.

- “And why art thou against the pleasure of the most High?” That is heavy. “There is no inquisition in the grave”: when you die there is no inquisition, no questioning, “whether thou have lived ten, or an hundred, or a thousand years.” When we die, we are at rest.

  Precepts:
  - **[Job 3:11-19](/bible/job/3#v11)**
    > <sup>[11](/bible/job/3#v11)</sup> Why died I not from the womb? why did I not give up the ghost when I came out of the belly?
    >
    > <sup>[12](/bible/job/3#v12)</sup> Why did the knees prevent me? or why the breasts that I should suck?
    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/job/3#v13)</sup> For now should I have lain still and been quiet, I should have slept: then had I been at rest,
    >
    > <sup>[14](/bible/job/3#v14)</sup> With kings and counsellors of the earth, which built desolate places for themselves;
    >
    > <sup>[15](/bible/job/3#v15)</sup> Or with princes that had gold, who filled their houses with silver:
    >
    > <sup>[16](/bible/job/3#v16)</sup> Or as an hidden untimely birth I had not been; as infants which never saw light.
    >
    > <sup>[17](/bible/job/3#v17)</sup> There the wicked cease from troubling; and there the weary be at rest.
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/job/3#v18)</sup> There the prisoners rest together; they hear not the voice of the oppressor.
    >
    > <sup>[19](/bible/job/3#v19)</sup> The small and great are there; and the servant is free from his master.

    Job is the precept that there is no inquisition in the grave. Job was catching so much hell that he said “Why died I not from the womb? why did I not give up the ghost when I came out of the belly?” Why didn’t I die as a baby? His kids got put to death, he lost all his businesses, he got sick, all kinds of plagues were on his flesh, and his wife was a nag getting on his nerves.
  - **[Isaiah 57:1-2](/bible/isaiah/57#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/57#v1)</sup> The righteous perisheth, and no man layeth it to heart: and merciful men are taken away, none considering that the righteous is taken away from the evil to come.
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/isaiah/57#v2)</sup> He shall enter into peace: they shall rest in their beds, each one walking in his uprightness.

    “The righteous perisheth, and no man layeth it to heart”, and Isaiah says “the righteous is taken away from the evil to come.” When he dies “He shall enter into peace: they shall rest in their beds”: the chambers of rest.
  - **[Revelation 6:9-10](/bible/revelation/6#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/revelation/6#v9)</sup> And when he had opened the fifth seal, I saw under the altar the souls of them that were slain for the word of God, and for the testimony which they held:
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/revelation/6#v10)</sup> And they cried with a loud voice, saying, How long, O Lord, holy and true, dost thou not judge and avenge our blood on them that dwell on the earth?

    This shows the righteous more. At the fifth seal John saw “under the altar the souls of them that were slain for the word of God, and for the testimony which they held.” Remember, they are dead, yet “they cried with a loud voice, saying, How long, O Lord, holy and true, dost thou not judge and avenge our blood on them that dwell on the earth?”

**[Ecclesiasticus 41:6-7](/bible/sirach/41#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/sirach/41#v6)</sup> The inheritance of sinners’ children shall perish, and their posterity shall have a perpetual reproach.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/sirach/41#v7)</sup> The children will complain of an ungodly father, because they shall be reproached for his sake.

- They reap the benefits of 400 years of free slave labor, and say they can't feel guilty for it. “The inheritance of sinners’ children shall perish, and their posterity shall have a perpetual reproach. The children will complain of an ungodly father, because they shall be reproached for his sake.” Because, yeah, we always bring out what your forefathers did. The reproach falls on the descendants, on the children, and they are direct beneficiaries of the evil that their forefathers did. That's fact. Isaiah 14:21: prepare slaughter for his children for the iniquity of their fathers.

  Precepts:
  - **[Isaiah 14:21](/bible/isaiah/14#v21)**
    > <sup>[21](/bible/isaiah/14#v21)</sup> Prepare slaughter for his children for the iniquity of their fathers; that they do not rise, nor possess the land, nor fill the face of the world with cities.

    If the reproach falls on the descendants, on the children, and they are direct beneficiaries of the evil that their forefathers did: “Prepare slaughter for his children for the iniquity of their fathers;” That's what God said. “that they do not rise, nor possess the land, nor fill the face of the world with cities.” That's thus saith the Lord. That's not what we said. That's what God said.

**[Ecclesiasticus 41:17](/bible/sirach/41#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/sirach/41#v17)</sup> Be ashamed of whoredom before father and mother: and of a lie before a prince and a mighty man;

- “Be ashamed of whoredom before father and mother: and of a lie before a prince and a mighty man”: the Bible says to be ashamed of that. In the Apocrypha, where it lists the things to be ashamed of. In some things you can be shamefaced, but in these things you must be ashamed.
- “Of an offence before a judge and ruler; of iniquity before a congregation and people; of unjust dealing before thy partner and friend”: and of theft in regard of the place where thou sojournest, and in regard of the truth of God and his covenant. And to lean with thine elbow upon the meat, and of scorning to give and take, and of silence before them that salute thee.
