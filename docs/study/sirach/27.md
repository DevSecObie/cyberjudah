## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 27 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-3, 12-13, 16-21, 26-29, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:1](/bible/sirach/27#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/27#v1)</sup> Many have sinned for a small matter; and he that seeketh for abundance will turn his eyes away.

- “Many have sinned for a small matter”. They sinned because they are not walking in the spirit; that little situation should not have caused anyone to sin. A brother asks, can you do this job? That's nothing. Ten grand, done in two months. Two years later, no job and the money is gone. That is a small matter: you gave your word, and you must be a man of your word.
- “And he that seeketh for abundance will turn his eyes away.” He wants it all, so he turns his eyes away from the truth and the laws of God. He knows what the Bible says, but the money is green, he's in love with Benjamin and Grant and Jackson and says the hell with Washington. He does it once, twice, three times, and before he knows it he's buried. Always put the Lord first.
- Time shows your character. How you deal with a situation and how you bounce back from it is how we know what you are really about. Some fight in the truth the same spirit they had in the world, some find a spirit they never knew was there, and some always did shady business and hid it. But nothing can be hidden in here, because darkness and light can't dwell together: give it time and all your sins will be exposed. That is a good thing, because you want the Lord to show you your sins so you can repent and fix them. The word is sharper than any two-edged sword and cuts both ways: we all have something to pick up from this. Covet good gifts: covet a brother's skill and become his understudy, but don't hate him because he has it and you don't.

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:2](/bible/sirach/27#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/27#v2)</sup> As a nail sticketh fast between the joinings of the stones; so doth sin stick close between buying and selling.

- “As a nail sticketh fast between the joinings of the stones; so doth sin stick close between buying and selling.” It is very hard to pull a nail out of a stone, and sin sticks that close to buying and selling. The moment you talk business, sin says: I can help you make more money; they trust you; tell them the wood cost five thousand when you got it for three with your discount card, charge three for the job, and you come out with six. That is the devil playing tricks on you. That's a setup.
- This is why business between brothers in the truth so often never ends well, and why you had better be well studied if you are going to do business. The moment you say you want to do business, you are entering a realm where demons are all around you, tempting you to see how righteous you will be.

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:3](/bible/sirach/27#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/sirach/27#v3)</sup> Unless a man hold himself diligently in the fear of the Lord, his house shall soon be overthrown.

- “Unless a man hold himself diligently in the fear of the Lord, his house shall soon be overthrown.” Diligent: characterized by steady, earnest, and energetic effort; painstaking. You must be doing everything you can to hold yourself in the fear of the Lord. If you are not giving it all you've got, you're done.
- Don't be here carelessly, just keeping the Sabbath. When you find yourself idle, doing nothing, you are chilling with the devil, because idleness teaches much evil. The number one way to hold yourself diligently is to read this Bible continuously and know its stories, because in them you learn the do's and don'ts. When you are in a situation, you remember Joseph: how did he get away? He ran.
- So in business, if you don't hold yourself diligently you will do shady business, and shady business doesn't last. You thought you were going to be a big-time tycoon, and you end up garbage.

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:12](/bible/sirach/27#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/27#v12)</sup> If thou be among the indiscreet, observe the time; but be continually among men of understanding.

- Trauma researchers found that the nervous system stores unresolved traumatic experiences and reactivates them in response to cues in the environment. That is why the Bible says: “If thou be among the indiscreet, observe the time; but be continually among men of understanding.”

  Precepts:
  - **[Proverbs 12:26](/bible/proverbs/12#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/proverbs/12#v26)</sup> The righteous is more excellent than his neighbour: but the way of the wicked seduceth them.

    “The righteous is more excellent than his neighbour: but the way of the wicked seduceth them.” You cannot sit among the people who put you through all these traumatic experiences, or who remind you of the time you turned to drugs, sex and alcohol. Separate yourself from them, and be around like-minded people on the same healing journey.

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:13](/bible/sirach/27#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/27#v13)</sup> The discourse of fools is irksome, and their sport is in the wantonness of sin.

- “The discourse of fools is irksome”: the conversation of fools is annoying. Listen to one plus one plus one is one for three hours and you will lose your mind. You’re a dumbass; why am I here?
- “And their sport is in the wantonness of sin.” Here is the whole premise. By making the Messiah fully God and fully man, you make it so only Christ can keep God’s laws. We are only human; we have one essence and he has two, so only a two-essence being can keep the commandments, and we don’t have to. When you peel back the layers of confusion, it is all about removing the humanity of Christ, or keeping some of it while making him a God too, so that man can’t keep the commandments: Christ kept the laws so we don’t have to do anything. That is what all those great swelling words come back to.
- Their sport, their exercise, is the desire not to keep God’s laws, the lust of sin. That is why the question was asked at the start: what is the essence of Christmas, of New Year’s, Halloween, Carnival, Labor Day, the Fourth of July or birthdays? Where is that in the Bible? What does your three-in-one God say about that? Nothing.
- This is something that does not even require you to watch it, and it has taken the place of television for many: the podcast. “The discourse of fools is irksome.” Discourse is conversation, and the conversation of fools is supposed to be irksome to your spirit. “And their sport is in the wantonness of sin”: that is what they do for fun.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 5:48](/bible/matthew/5#v48)**
    > <sup>[48](/bible/matthew/5#v48)</sup> Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect.

    Christ said: “Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect.” The Bible says you can do it: keep his commandments. They say no, only God can do that, so Christ cannot be fully a man, because a full man cannot keep those laws, but a full man and a full God can. The insidiousness behind the doctrine is to make it impossible for man to keep God’s commandments.
  - **[Luke 1:6](/bible/luke/1#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/luke/1#v6)</sup> And they were both righteous before God, walking in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless.

    John the Baptist’s parents: “And they were both righteous before God, walking in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless.” Blameless: that is perfect. So human beings can keep God’s commandments. It can be done, and the doctrine that says only a God-man could do it is the wantonness of sin.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 21:18](/bible/sirach/21#v18)**
    > <sup>[18](/bible/sirach/21#v18)</sup> As is a house that is destroyed, so is wisdom to a fool: and the knowledge of the unwise is as talk without sense.

    “As is a house that is destroyed, so is wisdom to a fool: and the knowledge of the unwise is as talk without sense.” The discourse of fools, talk without sense: that is the podcast, listening to fools and the knowledge of the unwise.

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:16](/bible/sirach/27#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/sirach/27#v16)</sup> Whoso discovereth secrets loseth his credit; and shall never find friend to his mind.

- They can't keep confidential things confidential; they can't hold water. Tell them something and you might as well have said it on the loud speaker. “Whoso discovereth secrets loseth his credit”: instantly, because now everybody knows he is a blabbermouth. “And shall never find friend to his mind”: that is why it is hard for you to make friends. Watch the one who can't control their mouth; they typically have trouble making friends, because they lack self-control.

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:17](/bible/sirach/27#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/sirach/27#v17)</sup> Love thy friend, and be faithful unto him: but if thou bewrayest his secrets, follow no more after him.

- “Love thy friend, and be faithful unto him”: faithful when he is with you and when he is not. Reveal his secret and “follow no more after him”: you might as well let that person go. To win them back as your friend is damn near impossible.

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:18-20](/bible/sirach/27#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/sirach/27#v18)</sup> For as a man hath destroyed his enemy; so hast thou lost the love of thy neighbour.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/sirach/27#v19)</sup> As one that letteth a bird go out of his hand, so hast thou let thy neighbour go, and shalt not get him again.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/sirach/27#v20)</sup> Follow after him no more, for he is too far off; he is as a roe escaped out of the snare.

- “For as a man hath destroyed his enemy; so hast thou lost the love of thy neighbour.” “As one that letteth a bird go out of his hand, so hast thou let thy neighbour go”: the moment you open the window or open your hand, the bird is gone. So with the friend whose secrets you ran your mouth about, blabbering on in Israel to everybody, or to one person. “Follow after him no more, for he is too far off.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:21](/bible/sirach/27#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/sirach/27#v21)</sup> As for a wound, it may be bound up; and after reviling there may be reconcilement: but he that bewrayeth secrets is without hope.

- “As for a wound, it may be bound up; and after reviling there may be reconcilement”: you can wound your friend by accident and heal. How many fought their friends when they were young, black eye and all, and were cool after? “But he that bewrayeth secrets is without hope.” Betray secrets and see what happens; the scripture says let him go.

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:26](/bible/sirach/27#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/sirach/27#v26)</sup> Whoso diggeth a pit shall fall therein: and he that setteth a trap shall be taken therein.

- “Whoso diggeth a pit shall fall therein.” They thought evil against us, but the Lord meant it for good. Jacob is going to win in the end. “And he that setteth a trap shall be taken therein.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:27](/bible/sirach/27#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/sirach/27#v27)</sup> He that worketh mischief, it shall fall upon him, and he shall not know whence it cometh.

- “He that worketh mischief, it shall fall upon him, and he shall not know whence it cometh.” He will not know, because his pride has deceived him.

  Precepts:
  - **[Psalms 94:20](/bible/psalms/94#v20)**
    > <sup>[20](/bible/psalms/94#v20)</sup> Shall the throne of iniquity have fellowship with thee, which frameth mischief by a law?

    That is what they do: they work mischief and then call it law. “Shall the throne of iniquity have fellowship with thee, which frameth mischief by a law?” Two men getting married, two women getting married, all these laws; everything done since Trump came into office, the firing and the DEI hires, all of it against us. They frame mischief and call it law.

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:28](/bible/sirach/27#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/sirach/27#v28)</sup> Mockery and reproach are from the proud; but vengeance, as a lion, shall lie in wait for them.

- “Mockery and reproach are from the proud.” That is all they do: they falsely accuse us and mock us. “But vengeance, as a lion, shall lie in wait for them.”

  Precepts:
  - **[Jeremiah 4:7](/bible/jeremiah/4#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/jeremiah/4#v7)</sup> The lion is come up from his thicket, and the destroyer of the Gentiles is on his way; he is gone forth from his place to make thy land desolate; and thy cities shall be laid waste, without an inhabitant.

    Vengeance as a lion: “The lion is come up from his thicket, and the destroyer of the Gentiles is on his way; he is gone forth from his place to make thy land desolate; and thy cities shall be laid waste, without an inhabitant.” That is twofold: it is talking about Nebuchadnezzar, and it is talking about Christ.

**[Ecclesiasticus 27:29](/bible/sirach/27#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/sirach/27#v29)</sup> They that rejoice at the fall of the righteous shall be taken in the snare; and anguish shall consume them before they die.

- “They that rejoice at the fall of the righteous shall be taken in the snare.” Remember when they said, raze it, raze it, to the ground. The Lord has not forgotten. “And anguish shall consume them before they die.” It does not pay to be wicked, and it does not pay to follow after your oppressor, because in the end they are going to be destroyed.
