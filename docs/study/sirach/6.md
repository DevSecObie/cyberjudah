## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 6 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 2-13, 17-18, 20, 23-37, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:2](/bible/sirach/6#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/6#v2)</sup> Extol not thyself in the counsel of thine own heart; that thy soul be not torn in pieces as a bull straying alone.

- “Extol not thyself in the counsel of thine own heart”: extol, or exalt, not yourself in the counsel of your own mind, “that thy soul be not torn in pieces as a bull straying alone.” A bull alone has no protection, no one to help him. In a herd, the one that veers off is not in communication with everybody else; he’s on his own, in his own head, drinking water over there by himself, and the lion looks at him and says look at this stupid one, I’m gonna eat him alive. That’s Satan.
- When you stay alone in your own head for a long period of time, Satan starts to play with you. You hear a thought in your mind saying they hate you, they don’t like you, they’re being too rough, they didn’t hear your side. Meanwhile you hear the scriptures being read and you get it, but a voice says that’s not what that really means, that’s a misinterpretation. That’s Satan playing with you. Satan tries to convince you that those who love you and care for you are the ones against you, trying to destroy you. If it’s not in your head, it comes out of your spouse’s mouth.
- Why is your soul torn in pieces? Because after a while the understanding that you have begins to be ripped away, torn away in pieces over time.
- When you’re going through it and you’re dead wrong, there’s nothing to say. There will be times you may have a valid point, but at that moment when you’re wrong, nothing you say matters. Fix yourself, and after the smoke has settled and they see the brother or sister is trying to recover, then you can say what you meant and be listened to. When you go home and tell yourself that ain’t fair, they didn’t let me talk, that’s Satan talking to you: see, they don’t want to hear the scriptures, they wrong. That’s a very scary place to be, because you all alone. Satan seems to be in full effect late at night, around 2, 3, 4 in the morning, when you’re to yourself and everyone is asleep; brothers who fall into their lust often fall around that time.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Peter 5:8](/bible/1-peter/5#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/1-peter/5#v8)</sup> Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour:

    Where are you straying at? In your own head, in your own mind, all over the place. “Be sober, be vigilant”: how are you sober and mindful? When you’re around other vigilant and mindful brothers that you’re in communication with. “Because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour”. Who does the lion seek to devour? The bulls that stray alone, that do their own thing.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 5:10-11](/bible/sirach/5#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/sirach/5#v10)</sup> Be stedfast in thy understanding; and let thy word be the same.
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/sirach/5#v11)</sup> Be swift to hear; and let thy life be sincere; and with patience give answer.

    “Be stedfast in thy understanding”: be steadfast in what you’ve learned. “And let thy word be the same”: let your words be the same, coming out of the Bible. “Be swift to hear”: in your own head you ain’t listening; you’re listening to somebody, but it ain’t coming from here. “And let thy life be sincere”: be honest with yourself. Am I right in this? All the witnesses say no; three, four counsellor brothers are saying bro, you’re going off, and you’re like nah, you guys are wrong. You’re straying alone.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:3](/bible/sirach/6#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/sirach/6#v3)</sup> Thou shalt eat up thy leaves, and lose thy fruit, and leave thyself as a dry tree.

- “Thou shalt eat up thy leaves”: your leaves is your understanding. You turn on what you’ve learned. You start to say maybe nations can’t be saved, them guys are a cult, they’re trying to control me, all nations can be saved, I got good white friends at work, I can have more than one wife, I make six figures. You start hearing stupid stuff in your head. It sounds like you, but it’s not you. “And lose thy fruit”: you lose your understanding because you’re alone in here. “And leave thyself as a dry tree”: all the water, the understanding, is gone.
- “Thou shalt eat up thy leaves, and lose thy fruit, and leave thyself as a dry tree.” So many have fallen victim to their own counsel and their own minds, thinking they have it all figured out. You don't have the answers. You need to seek counsel.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:4](/bible/sirach/6#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/sirach/6#v4)</sup> A wicked soul shall destroy him that hath it, and shall make him to be laughed to scorn of his enemies.

- “A wicked soul shall destroy him that hath it”: him that strays alone, eats his own leaves up, loses his fruit and becomes a dry tree becomes a wicked soul. If your soul is torn in pieces, you become a wicked soul. “And shall make him to be laughed to scorn of his enemies.” The enemies are the brothers we once called brothers. Now you doing videos, why I left, I left the cult, and we laugh at you. They all do the same thing: those who leave and become a bull straying alone always find each other, and over time they turn on each other, because they all got that alone spirit. They all say I don’t believe in elders, I don’t believe in rank, we’re all equal in God. Meanwhile Paul said it clear: God the Father, the Son, man, woman and child, order. When they leave, God don’t care about order, we’re all elders. Your soul is ripped to pieces, now you’re a dry tree with a video that got two views, one view, his grandma watching. That is the aftermath of staying alone and exalting your own self in your mind.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:5](/bible/sirach/6#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/sirach/6#v5)</sup> Sweet language will multiply friends: and a fair-speaking tongue will increase kind greetings.

- “Sweet language will multiply friends”. Sweet language goes back to Malachi 3:16: communication. It multiplies friends, because brother, if you don’t talk, nobody knows you; if you just sit there quiet looking around, you look like a damn agent. “And a fair-speaking tongue will increase kind greetings”: you end up respected and reputable. Communication is key.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:6](/bible/sirach/6#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/sirach/6#v6)</sup> Be in peace with many: nevertheless have but one counsellor of a thousand.

- “Be in peace with many: nevertheless have but one counsellor of a thousand.” It goes into counsel because if you don’t do these things it leads back to verse two: exalt yourself in your own counsel, stray alone, eat your leaves, lose your fruit, become a dry tree, become a wicked soul, laughed to scorn by enemies. That is the end result of not fulfilling verses 5 and 6. If you’re not fulfilling 5 and 6, Satan becomes your counsellor, and you become 2, 3 and 4.
- “Be in peace with many: nevertheless have but one counsellor of a thousand.” The problem with a lot of you is that you don’t have a counsellor. Don’t do it first and then ask for counsel; before you even say hi to a sister, ask for counsel first. And counsel up: you cannot take counsel from somebody who came in two months ago like you. Ask a captain what he knows of that sister. If she has only been here a year, a good counsellor says give her another year: the first year is the best behaviour, and in the second you start to see how that spirit works, around other sisters and in the office. You got all the time in the world; if she is for you she isn’t going nowhere. What God put together let no man put asunder, but some of you God did not put together: you went through the back door, and when you did your back door business you took no counsel, so don’t come looking for leadership now.
- “Be in peace with many: nevertheless have but one counsellor of a thousand.” There is nothing wrong with having a counsellor, but be careful who you are counselling. This is for the brothers and the sisters both.
- Married brothers should not be counselling sisters, and neither should single brothers. We have sisters here for that. Not at the table after class, but during the week, on the phone, talking about counselling her: no. You are setting yourself up. Everybody who has a friendly face on is not what you think they are.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Esdras 10:15](/bible/2-esdras/10#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/2-esdras/10#v15)</sup> Now therefore keep thy sorrow to thyself, and bear with a good courage that which hath befallen thee.

    For the one who would not take counsel and comes running to leadership afterward: “Now therefore keep thy sorrow to thyself, and bear with a good courage that which hath befallen thee.” Keep your sorrow to yourself; these things have nothing to do with leadership.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:7](/bible/sirach/6#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/sirach/6#v7)</sup> If thou wouldest get a friend, prove him first, and be not hasty to credit him.

- These are generic scriptures, gone through again to show how the Lord uses the terminology of Satan, the devil, spirits and angels. “If thou wouldest get a friend, prove him first”: men and women, if you want to get a friend, you got to prove that friend. “And be not hasty to credit him”: don’t be quick to credit him.
- “If thou wouldest get a friend, prove him first.” A brother from the world invited into your house cannot go in your fridge and start drinking your things, and cannot walk into your bedroom and sit on your bed; there are places he cannot go. It is the same when you are proving for marriage. The person you decide to marry is one of the biggest decisions in this truth. Mighty brothers who know the scriptures left and right, great teachers, make horrible decisions with women, and the person you marry can drain the spirit out of you. That person is who you will spend the rest of your life with.
- Nearly everybody you start talking to tells you what you want to hear; give them six months or a year and ask the same question again, and you will see a difference. Show me who your friends are and I’ll show you who you are: a sister who only keeps company with single sisters, none of them married, gets nothing that builds her mind for marriage, while the married sister would tell her the reality. Some want to be married but not in a marriage, a husband but not a lord. There are no white horses and no fairy tale in marriage. Take your time. Brothers, say it: I’m the prize.
- “And be not hasty to credit him.” When a brother or sister walks in from the street they are broken, and our job is to rebuild them, but nobody builds a house in one day; it is built brick by brick. Some come from the homosexual lifestyle, some from the lesbian lifestyle, and those spirits take years to rebuild. A married sister divorced and went back to that life, and brothers have gone back too, because nobody gave them time to heal. They come from a filthy, disgusting lifestyle, so do not be hasty to credit them.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 11:2](/bible/sirach/11#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/11#v2)</sup> Commend not a man for his beauty; neither abhor a man for his outward appearance.

    “Commend not a man for his beauty”. That’s what women do: they commend a man for his beauty. Oh look at him, his shoulders are so wide, he got size 14 shoes, daddy got hazel eyes. “Neither abhor a man for his outward appearance”: look at him, short, fat and black; look at him, skinny, buck teeth, one eye crooked; look at this … That’s when they look at the outward appearance. Brothers do that too, but this is talking about women.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 18:30](/bible/sirach/18#v30)**
    > <sup>[30](/bible/sirach/18#v30)</sup> Go not after thy lusts, but refrain thyself from thine appetites.

    “Go not after thy lusts, but refrain thyself from thine appetites.” In the whole process of proving a friend, you have to know what lust is within you. Often you rush the process and get caught up in all kinds of jams because you went after your lust, with no plan of growing old with that sister, or that brother.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 19:2](/bible/sirach/19#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/19#v2)</sup> Wine and women will make men of understanding to fall away: and he that cleaveth to harlots will become impudent.

    “Wine and women will make men of understanding to fall away.” Go not after your lust, because wine and women make a man of understanding lose all sense of reality and common sense; he may know the scripture, but applying it goes out the window. “And he that cleaveth to harlots will become impudent”: if your appetite is harlots you become rude and hard-minded.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:8-13](/bible/sirach/6#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/sirach/6#v8)</sup> For some man is a friend for his own occasion, and will not abide in the day of thy trouble.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/sirach/6#v9)</sup> And there is a friend, who being turned to enmity and strife will discover thy reproach.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/sirach/6#v10)</sup> Again, some friend is a companion at the table, and will not continue in the day of thy affliction.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/sirach/6#v11)</sup> But in thy prosperity he will be as thyself, and will be bold over thy servants.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/6#v12)</sup> If thou be brought low, he will be against thee, and will hide himself from thy face.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/6#v13)</sup> Separate thyself from thine enemies, and take heed of thy friends.

- “For some man is a friend for his own occasion”. Sisters, you better peep that: some brothers got game. “And will not abide in the day of thy trouble.” That goes for you brothers too: you think the brother next to you is your friend? Don’t be telling people what you got. Like one brother who came up and told a sister, I make x amount of money. Why you tell her that? Now you never know if she is really with you for you, or because of what you make. Brothers are the same way.
- “There is a friend, who being turned to enmity and strife will discover thy reproach”: turned to hatred and strife, he’ll tell all your business.
- “Again, some friend is a companion at the table”. That’s why, sister, the people on Facebook making plates of food showing how good they cook: he’s only talking to you because he knows you cook good. “And will not continue in the day of thy affliction”: when you’re going through hard times, he’s out the door.
- “But in thy prosperity he will be as thyself, and will be bold over thy servants.” He’s telling your kids, shut up, sit down, go over there; telling your friends, don’t do that; like that’s his house.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:17](/bible/sirach/6#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/sirach/6#v17)</sup> Whoso feareth the Lord shall direct his friendship aright: for as he is, so shall his neighbour be also.

- We never grew up around order, structure and unity. So when we come around it, it makes us uncomfortable, because sometimes we realize we are the problem: we are the reason an environment becomes toxic and people bicker and fight, because we have an instigating spirit, the thing we grew up seeing. In a body and a truth where the light shines on that, you are forced to change, and many brothers and sisters do not want to.
- “Whoso feareth the Lord shall direct his friendship aright: for as he is, so shall his neighbour be also.” If you fear the Lord you will direct your friendships aright, because as you are, so shall your neighbour be. If you are constantly having issues and problems, who is the one causing the trouble? With the laws of God and all the examples in the scriptures, you should be able to direct your friendships aright. So why is your name always in something?

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:18](/bible/sirach/6#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/sirach/6#v18)</sup> My son, gather instruction from thy youth up: so shalt thou find wisdom till thine old age.

- “My son, gather instruction from thy youth up”. We did not have that. We did not have the opportunity to gather wisdom from our youth up, so we have to be born again coming into this truth and begin to do it now. You cannot drag in the way your grandmama and your auntie used to deal with each other. It is a new day. “So shalt thou find wisdom till thine old age.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:20](/bible/sirach/6#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/sirach/6#v20)</sup> She is very unpleasant to the unlearned: he that is without understanding will not remain with her.

- This is for the toxic people. Wisdom “is very unpleasant to the unlearned.” You never learned about yourself. All you ever did was point out other people’s problems; you never examined yourself or looked in the mirror. So when wisdom finally reveals who you are, it is very unpleasant.
- “He that is without understanding will not remain with her.” That is why people find a reason to leave. They get offended, they find a reason to be mad, and they go. The root of it is that they do not want to change once that wisdom is revealed.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:23](/bible/sirach/6#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/sirach/6#v23)</sup> Give ear, my son, receive my advice, and refuse not my counsel,

- Until we receive our homeland, we are all we have. We have to endeavor to keep the unity, desire one another, show ourselves friendly, exhort each other, show charity and mercy one to another, as we keep these commandments. I have no favorite place until I get my homeland back; my favorite people are the faithful brothers and sisters that keep the commandments, and wherever they are is my favorite place. We are pilgrims and nomads on the earth.
- “Give ear, my son, receive my advice”: give ear, twelve tribes. The Lord is giving us advice. “And refuse not my counsel”: do not turn away from what you are hearing.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:24-26](/bible/sirach/6#v24)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/sirach/6#v26)</sup> Come unto her with thy whole heart, and keep her ways with all thy power.

- “And put thy feet into her fetters, and thy neck into her chain.” That is wisdom: the laws, statutes and commandments, the restraint. “Come unto her with thy whole heart”: come in sincerity to serve the Lord.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:27-28](/bible/sirach/6#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/sirach/6#v27)</sup> Search, and seek, and she shall be made known unto thee: and when thou hast got hold of her, let her not go.

> <sup>[28](/bible/sirach/6#v28)</sup> For at the last thou shalt find her rest, and that shall be turned to thy joy.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:29-31](/bible/sirach/6#v29)**

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:32-36](/bible/sirach/6#v32)**

> <sup>[32](/bible/sirach/6#v32)</sup> My son, if thou wilt, thou shalt be taught: and if thou wilt apply thy mind, thou shalt be prudent.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/sirach/6#v33)</sup> If thou love to hear, thou shalt receive understanding: and if thou bow thine ear, thou shalt be wise,
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/sirach/6#v34)</sup> Stand in the multitude of the elders; and cleave unto him that is wise.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/sirach/6#v35)</sup> Be willing to hear every godly discourse; and let not the parables of understanding escape thee.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/sirach/6#v36)</sup> And if thou seest a man of understanding, get thee betimes unto him, and let thy foot wear the steps of his door.

- A lot of brothers want the position of a leader but lack the experience of being a follower. That’s not how God works: you always start by following, then you work into your leadership. Joshua didn’t just pop up and become a leader, he followed Moses. Peter didn’t just pop up and become a leader, he followed Christ. There are levels of discipleship, and good leadership first begins as a good student. We always read this, but it can never be read enough.
- “My son, if thou wilt, thou shalt be taught”: you have to have a willing mind; you have to want to be taught. So why travel all this way and linger in the hall, in security or with the medics, and not be in here learning?
- “And if thou wilt apply thy mind, thou shalt be prudent”: this is why some men excel others, even though they may have less time than you. A brother can be around five years and we barely know his name or see him, while a brother who came in one or two years ago is always around asking what this scripture means, what that means. That brother will zip by you, and then you’re mad at us because we exalted him; but bro, we don’t really know you, you stay way back there. At the campfire at Tabernacles, with all the seats empty up front, one brother still sits way back in the nosebleed section, and he has been with us almost eight years.
- That is why it behooves all of us in leadership positions to pray for laborers. Everybody in here is not a laborer; you might take up a seat. You might be very busy and still not be productive. There is a difference between being busy and being productive.

**[Ecclesiasticus 6:37](/bible/sirach/6#v37)**

> <sup>[37](/bible/sirach/6#v37)</sup> Let thy mind be upon the ordinances of the Lord, and meditate continually in his commandments: he shall establish thine heart, and give thee wisdom at thine own desire.

- “Let thy mind be upon the ordinances of the Lord, and meditate continually in his commandments: he shall establish thine heart.” The last part, the wisdom given at thine own desire, comes with studying, praying and applying. That’s getting wisdom at your own desire.
- “Let thy mind be upon the ordinances of the Lord, and meditate continually in his commandments: he shall establish thine heart, and give thee wisdom at thine own desire.”
- So when you pray for wisdom, brothers and sisters, God will only give it at your own desire. If you only look in the Bible today, on the Sabbath, once a week, your desire is, Lord, I really don't want it that much. You are not occupying your time with his word. God will only increase your understanding at your own desire.
- “Let thy mind be upon the ordinances of the Lord”: the law written in your fleshy heart. “And meditate continually in his commandments”: give yourself wholly. “He shall establish thine heart”: he will set your purpose and light that fire. “And give thee wisdom at thine own desire.” If you are not growing or being promoted, it is because none of this is being received and applied.

Since you do not want your daily bread, God says, all right, now I won't give it. “God gave them over to a reprobate mind.” That is what you want, so that is what you get, and now everything is contrary to God and to the commandments.
