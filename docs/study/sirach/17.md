## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 17 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-7, 11-12, 16-28, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 17:1-4](/bible/sirach/17#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/17#v1)</sup> The Lord created man of the earth, and turned him into it again.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/17#v2)</sup> He gave them few days, and a short time, and power also over the things therein.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/sirach/17#v3)</sup> He endued them with strength by themselves, and made them according to his image,
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/sirach/17#v4)</sup> And put the fear of man upon all flesh, and gave him dominion over beasts and fowls.

- Now to the senses. “The Lord created man of the earth, and turned him into it again. He gave them few days, and a short time, and power also over the things therein. He endued them with strength by themselves, and made them according to his image, And put the fear of man upon all flesh, and gave him dominion over beasts and fowls.”
- “The Lord created man of the earth, and turned him into it again. He gave them few days, and a short time, and power also over the things therein. He endued them with strength by themselves, and made them according to his image.”
- God gave us, his creation, the sons of God, the ability to think on our own, to create, build, engineer, help and heal, on this earthly level, as a shadow of him.
- And because he gave that to us, the nations fear us right now. God’s wisdom is on us. We don’t have military power. The power we have is this book, and it is the only power we need. This is what has the nations trembling, and the reason they don’t want to talk to us: we could use this book and confound the world. Our strength is the word of God.

**[Ecclesiasticus 17:5](/bible/sirach/17#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/sirach/17#v5)</sup> They received the use of the five operations of the Lord, and in the sixth place he imparted them understanding, and in the seventh speech, an interpreter of the cogitations thereof.

- “They received the use of the five operations of the Lord, and in the sixth place he imparted them understanding, and in the seventh speech, an interpreter of the cogitations thereof.” Cogitation is the act of thinking very carefully and deeply about a subject, a carefully considered thought, meditation: the capacity to think or reflect.
- The sixth is understanding and the seventh is speech. Speech is the interpreter of what your mind meditates on; out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.
- Thinking and reflecting is one of the senses we have, but it does not work unless it is funneled through the word of God. That is how your mind has to work. If you think on something while you are not moving right, the thought that comes out is going to be off.
- “They received the use of the five operations of the Lord”: God’s creation, mankind, received the five uses, the five senses: see, taste, smell, hear, and feel or touch. Those are the five.

**[Ecclesiasticus 17:6](/bible/sirach/17#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/sirach/17#v6)</sup> Counsel, and a tongue, and eyes, ears, and a heart, gave he them to understand.

- “Counsel, and a tongue, and eyes, ears, and a heart, gave he them to understand.” Counsel is one, a tongue another, eyes another, ears another, and a heart another. Not in any order, the seven are counsel, tongue, eyes, ears, heart, understanding and speech.
- Counsel is when a man weighs options and determines what should be done, but he must be a godly man and surround himself with godly people. The tongue is the instrument of the body we use to form our words.
- The eye is for observation, to examine what is happening. Just by looking you can know a situation isn’t right. Two brothers were working together; one stepped to the back, and when he came back he saw the other hand his phone to a girl and walk away from her. Without hearing anything he knew something was wrong. The brother said it was nothing, then said it was business, then kept repeating himself: a multitude of words. That is your senses being exercised, and why it is good to look at a man’s face when he speaks, because his face tells what is really going on.
- A man’s ears hear words, instruction, warning and counsel, and your ears have to be tuned to the subtleties in speech. The heart takes what is seen and heard, processes it and comes to a conclusion; you hear, you see and you process, but to discern between good and evil you have to be spiritual. Understanding is a man comprehending the meaning of a matter and distinguishing between right and wrong. And speech reveals the thoughts that were formed in the heart.

**[Ecclesiasticus 17:11](/bible/sirach/17#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/sirach/17#v11)</sup> Beside this he gave them knowledge, and the law of life for an heritage.

- This is our true culture. “Beside this he gave them knowledge, and the law of life for an heritage.” The law of life is our heritage, and our heritage is our culture.
- When you read about the customs and laws the children of Israel kept, that is what God gave us: the new moon celebrations, the sabbath day, the feast of tabernacles. That is our heritage and our custom.
- What our people in the Caribbean islands celebrate, what the West Indians in Brooklyn celebrate every Labor Day, even what black Americans follow here, is not our culture. Those so-called cultures were made up in slavery and after slavery, and most of it we learned from the white man. Black culture, West Indian culture: Negro, you got no culture. You were a slave. You were destroyed. All that stuff you follow, you made it up or you learned it from the white man.
- “Beside this he gave them knowledge, and the law of life”: we have the law of life, which is in the book of life, and we have to keep those laws because it is our heritage. We are ingrained with them; we are one and the same with the laws of God. He did not make us to live or conduct our lives without the law.

**[Ecclesiasticus 17:26](/bible/sirach/17#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/sirach/17#v26)</sup> Turn again to the most High, and turn away from iniquity: for he will lead thee out of darkness into the light of health, and hate thou abomination vehemently.

- “Turn again to the most High, and turn away from iniquity: for he will lead thee out of darkness into the light of health”.
- “And hate thou abomination vehemently.” Vehemently is aggressively, forcefully, passionately, in an intense manner with great feeling. To hate evil vehemently is something you have to train yourself to do, because sin is pleasurable, but the Lord says the sin you are committing is abomination.
- For example, the Bible teaches that a woman should not wear what pertains unto a man: pants. At work you see females in pants showing their shape and figure, and you don’t hate it; you’re like, damn. Should we be like that? No. It should not entice you. The society we grew up in is all we see, and it corrupts our mind and our way of thinking, so we have to train ourselves over time to hate these abominations vehemently. As Lot was vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked.
- “Turn again to the most High, and turn away from iniquity: for he will lead thee out of darkness”: he will bring you out of darkness, from sin, from the devil, “into the light of health”: into Christ, because Christ is health. “And hate thou abomination vehemently”: hate it aggressively.

**[Ecclesiasticus 17:27-28](/bible/sirach/17#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/sirach/17#v27)</sup> Who shall praise the most High in the grave, instead of them which live and give thanks?

Thanksgiving perisheth from the dead, as from one that is not.” You can’t give thanks, you can’t pray, if you are dead in the midst of your sin, as one that has no life in them anymore. “The living and sound in heart shall praise the Lord”: keep the law and commandments right now, praise his name and glorify him. Pray three times a day, anoint yourself, fast; do the things you need to do to overcome your sins, or you won’t make it. We won’t make it if we don’t continue.
- “Who shall praise the most High in the grave, instead of them which live and give thanks?” Some will keep that hatred until they die. You cannot repent in the grave. Once you are dead and gone, there is no, I’m sorry, I forgive you now. That is over.
- You have time in your life to fix things. If you have a problem with a brother or a sister, fix it now while you are still here; no one can determine what tomorrow will bring. Bring it to their attention. Some choose the Day of Atonement to do that, but you should have done it way before.
- “Thanksgiving perisheth from the dead, as from one that is not: the living and sound in heart shall praise the Lord.”
