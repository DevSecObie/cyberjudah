## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 37 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 7-10, 16, 19, 27-31, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 37:7-8](/bible/sirach/37#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/sirach/37#v7)</sup> Every counsellor extolleth counsel; but there is some that counselleth for himself.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/sirach/37#v8)</sup> Beware of a counsellor, and know before what need he hath; for he will counsel for himself; lest he cast the lot upon thee,

- “but there is some that counselleth for himself.” Be careful. Some people act like they're doing a good deed by talking and counselling with you, but they're counselling for themselves. “Beware of a counsellor”. Be cautious. You're supposed to come to the table, to the leaders of the church. Instead, you're talking to a brother who's been here damn eight months, or you've been here a year and are giving somebody counsel on the low. You don't fully understand judgment yet. You're supposed to send him up to the leaders of the school.
- Well, I was just talking to him. No, you was counselling him. What the hell are you talking about, just talking to him? Good intentions is what they're using. “and know before what need he hath”. Know what he needs; some men will counsel for themselves.

**[Ecclesiasticus 37:9](/bible/sirach/37#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/sirach/37#v9)</sup> And say unto thee, Thy way is good: and afterward he stand on the other side, to see what shall befall thee.

- “Thy way is good”. He'll tell you what you're talking about is good. “and afterward he stand on the other side, to see what shall befall thee.” When you get in trouble, he steps aside like he never said anything. Let me see if they're going to blast him. If they don't, it's all right; if they do, he won't step up and say he told you or spoke to you. That good deed, that good intention to tell you something, leaves you alone when you get in trouble. Be cautious about who you talk to and whose advice you take.

**[Ecclesiasticus 37:10](/bible/sirach/37#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/sirach/37#v10)</sup> Consult not with one that suspecteth thee: and hide thy counsel from such as envy thee.

- “Consult not with one that suspecteth thee: and hide thy counsel from such as envy thee.” Some people counsel for themselves. They act like they're doing right, but when you get in trouble they'll back up off you.

**[Ecclesiasticus 37:16](/bible/sirach/37#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/sirach/37#v16)</sup> Let reason go before every enterprize, and counsel before every action.

- Imagine driving on the wrong side of the highway with an officer telling you to turn around, and you say, you don't know what you're talking about. Boom, you're dead.
- “Let reason go before every enterprize.” Before every enterprise, make sure it is sound and makes sense, that thought has been put into it. Never do anything on a whim, piecing things together with half-assed research. “And counsel before every action.” Before you make a move, before you seal the deal, get counsel and find out if this is what you should do, and you will save yourself a whole lot of heartache.

**[Ecclesiasticus 37:19](/bible/sirach/37#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/sirach/37#v19)</sup> There is one that is wise and teacheth many, and yet is unprofitable to himself.

- “There is one that is wise and teacheth many, and yet is unprofitable to himself.” King David a prophet of God, King Saul chosen of God; yet at those moments, until the repentance came in, they were unprofitable to themselves, because they committed the transgressions. That is like all of us. Family and friends now ask, are you among the prophets? Your neighbour sees you walk to your car in your purple and gold: you with those guys? I saw them here, I saw them there.
- You don't want to be the one God called with his wisdom who, through lack of application and of examining himself, is unprofitable to himself: beneficial to everyone who came his way, answering questions, casting down imaginations and everything that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, but when it is time for him to apply it, there is an issue.
- Men in the ranks: your peer tells you something and you have a gripe with him, but when a captain says the same thing, right away you take it. Some call it respect of persons, respecting one rank or one leader over another, and it shows the bitterness is in a lot more of us than we want to admit. Satan is right at your door. If those other building blocks, which may seem like nothing to you, are already in place, all he needs is for the bitterness to become full-fledged and overflow. An order or a directive comes on your phone and you think, here we go, and Satan is right there: I bet he ain't asked nobody else to do it. Why they always asking you?
- We stay in denial until the breach in the wall breaks and it overflows like Hurricane Katrina, and you ask, how did I end up here? You did not deal with it when it was little building blocks and nuances. You said nothing and acted like everything was normal.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Corinthians 9:27](/bible/1-corinthians/9#v27)**
    > <sup>[27](/bible/1-corinthians/9#v27)</sup> But I keep under my body, and bring it into subjection: lest that by any means, when I have preached to others, I myself should be a castaway.

    “But I keep under my body, and bring it into subjection: lest that by any means, when I have preached to others, I myself should be a castaway.” You can be a teacher, a preacher to others, an instructor in wisdom to others, and it be unprofitable to yourself. Paul, dealing with the Corinthians, did not want to be a castaway: not only did he have to teach it to them, he had to live it as well, or he would be, as Ecclesiasticus 37 says, unprofitable to himself. And we cannot have that.

**[Ecclesiasticus 37:27](/bible/sirach/37#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/sirach/37#v27)</sup> My son, prove thy soul in thy life, and see what is evil for it, and give not that unto it.

- “My son, prove thy soul in thy life, and see what is evil for it, and give not that unto it.” Prove what your soul wants and what is evil for it, the things you should not be eating or consuming.
- “My son, prove thy soul in thy life, and see what is evil for it, and give not that unto it.” It is your body, your temple, your choice, but make sure you make the right choice. Based on what you choose to eat, you are choosing death. There are things a man gives to himself that are evil for him; if you feed yourself evil, if you feed yourself poison, you hate yourself. Don’t give that thing unto it.
- “My son, prove thy soul in thy life, and see what is evil for it, and give not that unto it.” See what is evil for your soul, and do not give it that. From verse 27 down to 30 the whole analogy is literally food, but it is spiritual too. Just as you see which foods are evil for you, which make you sick and unhealthy, see what things are evil for your spirit and will make you mentally unhealthy. Just as you say, I cannot eat these foods on my diet, you say, I cannot watch this show; Spartacus may be a good show, but it is full of sex scenes.
- “My son, prove thy soul in thy life, and see what is evil for it, and give not that unto it.” This part of the chapter goes into things a man may count good for himself that are not good for him, the meats and the dainties and the overeating. But that short part of the verse covers everything: you have to examine what is evil for your life and abstain from it, and not give yourself over to it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 37:28](/bible/sirach/37#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/sirach/37#v28)</sup> For all things are not profitable for all men, neither hath every soul pleasure in every thing.

- “For all things are not profitable for all men.” Everybody can’t eat everything: some people eat chocolate and break out. “neither hath every soul pleasure in every thing”: everybody does not like the same things.
- “For all things are not profitable for all men”. Be careful what you choose to put in your body; find out what is good for you. And whatever kind of man you are, sugar can never be good for anyone. Overconsumption of sugar will put you in a bad situation.
- “For all things are not profitable for all men, neither hath every soul pleasure in every thing.” A fat brother’s diet is different from the diet of a brother who is already lean; it is a totally different structure. Not everything is profitable for everyone.
- “For all things are not profitable for all men.” What may be good for one brother is definitely not good for another, and what is good for one sister is not good for another sister. You have to know yourself, to see what is beneficial or detrimental to your own life.

**[Ecclesiasticus 37:29](/bible/sirach/37#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/sirach/37#v29)</sup> Be not unsatiable in any dainty thing, nor too greedy upon meats:

- Read for the trauma of food: children left without nutritious food, or let to eat whatever they want. “Be not unsatiable in any dainty thing”: the word dainty means sweets. “Nor too greedy upon meats”: the constant eating of meats.
- Some mothers let the child dictate what he eats: no collard greens, no chicken, give me pizza, French fries and Snickers bars. Then the child gets diabetes and she comes up for prayer and won’t take accountability, and God hears the lies coming out of her mouth. Mothers who never taught their daughters to cook raise whole families on processed food that leads to death. Letting your children eat like that is a type of abuse.
- “Be not unsatiable in any dainty thing.” Do not be unsatiable in dainty things like sweets. Now Esau is coming out saying too much sugar gives you cancer; God told us not to eat too much sugar because he knows what it does to the body.
- “nor too greedy upon meats.” Don’t eat too much meat. We are not supposed to be eating meat every day.

  Precepts:
  - **[Proverbs 25:27](/bible/proverbs/25#v27)**
    > <sup>[27](/bible/proverbs/25#v27)</sup> It is not good to eat much honey: so for men to search their own glory is not glory.

    Too much of anything is no good for you. “It is not good to eat much honey.” It ain’t good to eat too much honey, too much sugar. It is okay to have a little cheat day, but do it all the time and you are going to get sick. That’s what God says.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 31:19](/bible/sirach/31#v19)**
    > <sup>[19](/bible/sirach/31#v19)</sup> A very little is sufficient for a man well nurtured, and he fetcheth not his wind short upon his bed.

    “A very little is sufficient for a man well nurtured, and he fetcheth not his wind short upon his bed.” A little is enough for a man who is well nurtured. Eat a little and you won’t be farting all the time lying in the bed next to your wife.

**[Ecclesiasticus 37:30](/bible/sirach/37#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/sirach/37#v30)</sup> For excess of meats bringeth sickness, and surfeiting will turn into choler.

- “For excess of meats bringeth sickness, and surfeiting will turn into choler.” Surfeiting means excess. A lot of medical conditions go back to processed foods, and to bad sleep.
- “For excess of meats bringeth sickness.” You are going to get sick if you keep eating beef and chicken every day. That’s what God says; take it or leave it. “and surfeiting will turn into choler”: excess will turn into sickness.
- “For excess of meats bringeth sickness, and surfeiting will turn into choler.” When you overeat, you are going to get sick; it is as simple as that. We can’t change it to please you. That man is too big, and very simple things are coming: high cholesterol, high blood pressure, heart attack, stroke or diabetes. Any doctor will tell you the same, and nobody calls the doctor a bully.
- The Florida Department of Health found arsenic at elevated levels in 26 of 33 well-known candies; for a child, more than 96 Nerds or eight Swedish Fish in a year is past the safe amount. You hear it straight from Esau, and still say we are bullying. Surfeiting is that late-night bellyache, the belly pain that makes you sick.

**[Ecclesiasticus 37:31](/bible/sirach/37#v31)**

> <sup>[31](/bible/sirach/37#v31)</sup> By surfeiting have many perished; but he that taketh heed prolongeth his life.

- “By surfeiting have many perished; but he that taketh heed prolongeth his life.” By excess eating many have perished. Feeding your children whatever they want is another form of trauma: the parents charged with murder in the death of their seven-year-old son, four feet two and a half inches tall and 255 pounds, is the parents’ fault, because they listened to the child.
- “By surfeiting have many perished.” By excess many people perish: too much sweets, too much meat, then you get sick and die. The Lord tells us how to deal even with food: not to have it in excess.
- “but he that taketh heed prolongeth his life.” Take heed and you will live longer; you won’t die of colon cancer or get diabetes from too much sugar. Our forefathers understood these things and lived long and healthy.
- “By surfeiting have many perished”. If you keep overeating you are going to die. So is the Bible a bully’s book? No, you just hate God, and you hate yourself, because loving God is the only way to love yourself, and if you can’t love yourself you cannot love me.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 38:15](/bible/sirach/38#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/sirach/38#v15)</sup> He that sinneth before his Maker, let him fall into the hand of the physician.

    “He that sinneth before his Maker, let him fall into the hand of the physician.” What kind of sin puts you in the physician’s hands? Overeating. The Bible says if you want to live long, don’t do it. But do we take heed? No. It is the man who does not take heed of verse 31 who falls into the hand of the physician.
