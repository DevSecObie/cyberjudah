## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 11 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-2, 6-8, 12-15, 18-19, 25, 29, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 11:1](/bible/sirach/11#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/11#v1)</sup> Wisdom lifteth up the head of him that is of low degree, and maketh him to sit among great men.

- “Wisdom lifteth up the head of him that is of low degree, and maketh him to sit among great men.” No matter what financial position the brother is in, or if he is just starting a job and working from the bottom, none of that matters: wisdom lifts up his head and will cause him to sit amongst great men.

**[Ecclesiasticus 11:2](/bible/sirach/11#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/11#v2)</sup> Commend not a man for his beauty; neither abhor a man for his outward appearance.

- “Commend not a man for his beauty”. Sisters, the Bible says do not commend a man for his outward appearance. How many of you were deceived by a good-looking man before you came into this truth?
- Every woman wants a man; it is natural, and a woman who says she does not want or need a man is going against nature. The woman was taken out of the man, bone of his bone and flesh of his flesh; her body reacts to her husband, and when she is with him all her fear goes out the window and she can rest. We are made in the image of God and she is made in the image of us; she is our glory, which is why the little girls look like their daddy. The woman was made for the man and she cannot do nothing without us. So when a woman says she don't need a man, that's BS, and some of you women in the truth walk around like you do not need your husband. That's BS. You had better release that feminism.
- “neither abhor a man for his outward appearance.” A sister would not look at a brother who had his own business, did not work for the white man, spoke several languages, was in the green and booked up through March, because she could not see herself with him, while she could not see herself forty-something and unmarried either. Sit your ass over there in the back then and wait for Prince Charming. These women want a fairy tale, Love & Hip Hop, Disney, and they are missing out on good husbands. Leadership is trying to tell you what good men are: before a brother is ever linked with a sister, we have already grilled him and seen his bank account, because a father would be pissed off to give his daughter to a man with $300 who cannot buy her shoes or pay the light bill.
- Every brother is not Boris Kodjoe or Idris Elba. A grandmother who praised how handsome Shemar Moore was in front of the husband who had taken care of her for over forty years was being disrespectful, and that small thing is how women fall into lust and adultery or leave a husband because he does not do it for them anymore.

**[Ecclesiasticus 11:6-7](/bible/sirach/11#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/sirach/11#v6)</sup> Many mighty men have been greatly disgraced; and the honourable delivered into other men’s hands.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/sirach/11#v7)</sup> Blame not before thou hast examined the truth: understand first, and then rebuke.

- “Many mighty men have been greatly disgraced; and the honourable delivered into other men’s hands.” But “Blame not before thou hast examined the truth: understand first, and then rebuke.” That is why leadership didn't move right away: they needed time to process what was going on and let it play itself out.
- But it gets to a point where there is so much evidence that something is being done wrong. Brothers were spoken to a year before it all transpired and told it would be dealt with; they were being pricked. Then a trip was made across the country and brothers didn't show up. There is no way brothers travel to the other side of the country and the men don't show up. Once leaders get around the men and start talking, and things don't come out right, they dig until it makes sense. What they thought would happen was that it would brush by. That's not how it works; those are small signs, and it comes out in the wash.

**[Ecclesiasticus 11:8](/bible/sirach/11#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/sirach/11#v8)</sup> Answer not before thou hast heard the cause: neither interrupt men in the midst of their talk.

- “Answer not before thou hast heard the cause: neither interrupt men in the midst of their talk.” Sometimes you don't interrupt men in the midst of their talk, because they will put their foot in their mouth. They are their own worst enemy, thinking they are going to explain themselves out of the situation. Let your yea be yea and your nay be nay.
- A brother somewhere else was asked, did you steal? He said, no, I borrowed some money, but I put it back. Did you ask? No, but I put it back. If you went to the bank, took money without asking, and came back a week later to put it back, you'd still get arrested. You're a thief: you took without asking.

**[Ecclesiasticus 11:12](/bible/sirach/11#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/11#v12)</sup> Again, there is another that is slow, and hath need of help, wanting ability, and full of poverty; yet the eye of the Lord looked upon him for good, and set him up from his low estate,

- “Again, there is another that is slow”: that’s us, brothers and sisters, we’re slow. We don’t catch things right away. You’ll be at camp, and there’ll be an Edomite and an Israelite listening, and you say Jesus Christ is black. The black man will say it don’t matter. The white man will say, I’m going into slavery: he’s already added one plus one equals two. The black man and the black woman is slow as hell: it doesn’t matter what he looks like. The white guy figured it out already.
- “And hath need of help”: we’re the ones that always need help. “Wanting ability”: we lack ability. “And full of poverty”: our people is full of poverty. Now watch: “yet the eye of the Lord looked upon him for good, and set him up from his low estate”.

**[Ecclesiasticus 11:13](/bible/sirach/11#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/11#v13)</sup> And lifted up his head from misery; so that many that saw it marvelled at him.

- “And lifted up his head from misery”: he said, my people is miserable, let me lift up their head. “So that many that saw it marvelled at him”: everybody that sees us rise is gonna marvel. This is that low-life, no-good people, and God’s raising them up. Everybody that sees this shall marvel. It’s the prophecy.

**[Ecclesiasticus 11:14-15](/bible/sirach/11#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/sirach/11#v14)</sup> Prosperity and adversity, life and death, poverty and riches, come of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/sirach/11#v15)</sup> Wisdom, knowledge, and understanding of the law, are of the Lord: love, and the way of good works, are from him.

- “Prosperity and adversity, life and death, poverty and riches, come of the Lord.” “Wisdom, knowledge, and understanding of the law, are of the Lord”: understanding of what? Of the law. “Love, and the way of good works, are from him.”
- The fire is not there to burn you; the fire is there to purify you, to take out the things you have in you. A good life is not an easy life. A lot of people fear these things, but these elements work together, so there is no need to fear them when they are happening.
- “Prosperity and adversity”: you will always find those two together. In prosperity you would not know who is a friend; in adversity you would know that he was never a friend. “Life and death”: where you find life, you are going to find death. “Poverty and riches”: where you find poverty, you will find people that have the money. They all “come of the Lord.”
- So why do you want to balance your life? Nobody's life is balanced. Your profession is this work; put that work in. You sit there doing nothing and say, Lord, I want that blessing; Lord, I need this new job. What are you doing? Nothing. So where is the blessing going to come from? We need you in the field, out there doing your thing, building yourself up.

**[Ecclesiasticus 11:18](/bible/sirach/11#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/sirach/11#v18)</sup> There is that waxeth rich by his wariness and pinching, and this is the portion of his reward:

- Some of us in this country have more than we need. Black people are the biggest consumers in America, the biggest consumer on earth; we have things in our houses we never wear, still in the package. We have been spoiled by the system, and that is why some cannot see themselves outside America. Some have good jobs, the white man has paid you well, and you never think: what if God took everything away tomorrow? Could I survive? It can happen.
- “There is that waxeth rich by his wariness and pinching.” Here is a man who worked hard and became rich: he got paid well, a nice house, nice cars, top notch, he and his wife living well.

**[Ecclesiasticus 11:19](/bible/sirach/11#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/sirach/11#v19)</sup> Whereas he saith, I have found rest, and now will eat continually of my goods; and yet he knoweth not what time shall come upon him, and that he must leave those things to others, and die.

- “Whereas he saith, I have found rest, and now will eat continually of my goods.” He is going to keep on eating, and he doesn’t give a damn about his brothers and his sisters.
- “And yet he knoweth not what time shall come upon him.” He has no idea what is about to happen tomorrow; he thinks he is always going to be good. What if tomorrow he has a heart attack and drops dead? “And that he must leave those things to others, and die.” You work all your life and build wealth, and then you leave it to somebody who never worked for it. Leave it to your children, and if they turn out knuckleheads addicted to drugs, forty or fifty years of building is gone in less than five.
- Some are so ignorant they say, hell with you poor people; I don’t want my children in a black school, I want to live next to white folks and eat at a white restaurant. The system co-opted you. You do not know what is going to happen tomorrow.

**[Ecclesiasticus 11:25](/bible/sirach/11#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/sirach/11#v25)</sup> In the day of prosperity there is a forgetfulness of affliction: and in the day of affliction there is no more remembrance of prosperity.

- You're going through stuff, and now you want to quit, and you ain't relying on the brotherhood. “In the day of prosperity”: when things are going well. “There is a forgetfulness of affliction”: you forget what it's like to be in a low estate, so you have to take heed lest you fall. “And in the day of affliction”: when the fire comes up and the heat gets turned up. “There is no more remembrance of prosperity.”
- Remember, the trying of your faith worketh patience, and Ecclesiasticus 2 warns those who have lost patience. In adversity you forget the last end, the increase the Lord is going to give you, and the things he has in store. If you just purge your spirit you would be able to receive everything the Lord has promised in the future. But you quit because you forgot what the goal was, forgot the mission, and want to abandon ship. When you are going through adversity you have to be patient in your lowest state and not forget the prosperity to come. That is what strengthens your faith.

**[Ecclesiasticus 11:29](/bible/sirach/11#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/sirach/11#v29)</sup> Bring not every man into thine house: for the deceitful man hath many trains.

- “Bring not every man into thine house: for the deceitful man hath many trains.” The things our forefathers did in the 1940s were scripture, and they did it off instinct and morality without even knowing it.
- From Sirach 6 all the way to Sirach 15 the chapters connect, from proving a friend to not letting just anyone stroll up into your house. The whole thing is saying you have to prove a person. You cannot know somebody in three months, or even in a year. If they are 27 or 30, that is 26 years of them you do not know: their family history, whether mental issues or diabetes run in their family, whether they have an STD.
- Yet people jump the gun, do not listen to leadership, and when it does not work they call leadership the devil. The instructions come from the Bible and have a proven track record with hundreds of couples. You would listen to a coach with a winning record; IUIC's leadership has that record and that experience, so why not listen? So for those thinking of leaving Ladies in Waiting or Men of Valor, or of jumping the gun in proving: don't do it. Reconsider.
