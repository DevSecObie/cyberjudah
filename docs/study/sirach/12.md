## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 12 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-6, 10-12, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 12:1](/bible/sirach/12#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/12#v1)</sup> When thou wilt do good, know to whom thou doest it; so shalt thou be thanked for thy benefits.

- Pam preaches that when the spirit moves on you, you do good to everybody and help everybody. Where was that when we were in chains, when they were burning crosses in our yards, when we were being hanged across the country, as we still are? But when we say the Lord is only for the Israelites, now all of a sudden the doctrine is false. We have to go by what the scripture says.
- “When thou wilt do good, know to whom thou doest it; so shalt thou be thanked for thy benefits.” Know to whom you are doing it: you have to have a relationship with these people and know them. Christianity has you thinking that because you gave $5 to a bum on the street you are doing God's work. No, you just bought him a beer or a bottle of liquor and continued the nonsense. We used to do it too: drive off feeling great for the day, while celebrating Christmas, birthdays and Thanksgiving.

**[Ecclesiasticus 12:2](/bible/sirach/12#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/12#v2)</sup> Do good to the godly man, and thou shalt find a recompence; and if not from him, yet from the most High.

- “Do good to the godly man, and thou shalt find a recompence; and if not from him, yet from the most High.” If that righteous brother cannot pay you back, the Lord will pay you back.
- Is the bum on the street a godly man? If he is of our people he can repent, but at that moment, half drunk or high and asking for money, a lot of the time that is judgment, and you are jumping in the way of the judgment. They have to repent. A man is given $3 "to get something to eat", and he goes straight to buy crack. You are hindering the affliction, the judgment that is going on.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hosea 5:15](/bible/hosea/5#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/hosea/5#v15)</sup> I will go and return to my place, till they acknowledge their offence, and seek my face: in their affliction they will seek me early.

    Why can the man on the street be under judgment that we must not jump in front of? “I will go and return to my place, till they acknowledge their offence, and seek my face: in their affliction they will seek me early.”
  - **[Ezra 7:10](/bible/ezra/7#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/ezra/7#v10)</sup> For Ezra had prepared his heart to seek the law of the Lord, and to do it, and to teach in Israel statutes and judgments.

    How do you seek the face of God? “For Ezra had prepared his heart to seek the law of the Lord, and to do it, and to teach in Israel statutes and judgments.” That is how you seek the face of God: you keep the commandments.

**[Ecclesiasticus 12:3-4](/bible/sirach/12#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/sirach/12#v3)</sup> There can no good come to him that is always occupied in evil, nor to him that giveth no alms.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/sirach/12#v4)</sup> Give to the godly man, and help not a sinner.

- “There can no good come to him that is always occupied in evil, nor to him that giveth no alms.” And: “Give to the godly man, and help not a sinner.” Pam said that when you get the spirit you help everybody; the Bible says help not a sinner, that is, one who breaks God's commandments. It is not your job to give your $3 to the crackhead. That is not what the Lord wants you to do.

**[Ecclesiasticus 12:5](/bible/sirach/12#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/sirach/12#v5)</sup> Do well unto him that is lowly, but give not to the ungodly: hold back thy bread, and give it not unto him, lest he overmaster thee thereby: for else thou shalt receive twice as much evil for all the good thou shalt have done unto him.

- “Do well unto him that is lowly, but give not to the ungodly: hold back thy bread, and give it not unto him”. It says it again. Hold back your bread means do not give them a damn thing. You think you are a great, helpful person, but you are going against God when you give it to the one who has the devil on him.
- “For else thou shalt receive twice as much evil for all the good thou shalt have done unto him.” You get twice as much evil for helping the wicked person who refuses to repent and keep the commandments. And if you give it to another nation, you are really tripping: this is Esau's kingdom. When an Edomite asks for money, all praises to the Father; he is dying slowly.

**[Ecclesiasticus 12:6](/bible/sirach/12#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/sirach/12#v6)</sup> For the most High hateth sinners, and will repay vengeance unto the ungodly, and keepeth them against the mighty day of their punishment.

- “For the most High hateth sinners, and will repay vengeance unto the ungodly, and keepeth them against the mighty day of their punishment.” This is the mystery in Christianity. They say he is the God of love who hates the sin but loves the sinner; the Bible says he hates sinners and keeps them for the day of their punishment. He is going to judge.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Chronicles 19:2](/bible/2-chronicles/19#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/2-chronicles/19#v2)</sup> And Jehu the son of Hanani the seer went out to meet him, and said to king Jehoshaphat, Shouldest thou help the ungodly, and love them that hate the Lord? therefore is wrath upon thee from before the Lord.

    The Most High hates sinners; then should we help them? Jehu the seer said to king Jehoshaphat, “Shouldest thou help the ungodly, and love them that hate the Lord? therefore is wrath upon thee from before the Lord.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 12:10](/bible/sirach/12#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/sirach/12#v10)</sup> Never trust thine enemy: for like as iron rusteth, so is his wickedness.

- “Never trust thine enemy”. Not sometimes or on Sunday—never. “for like as iron rusteth, so is his wickedness.” Polish him up and he'll revert to who he is. You might not find every Edomite or other nation like this, but all benefit from the oppression of God's children. Why would they stand against that system? Our liberation means their demise. I wouldn't do it if I were them. My responsibility is to ignite your minds so you don't see yourselves as less and stand against the system godly. Stay in scripture; no stupid stuff. Sisters are part of this mission too. They can't stop a godly nation. This mission is spiritual, changing our people's minds.
- With natural chemistry, iron exposed to the right elements rusts. Evil is that characteristic: when you have something he wants, or don't obey how he thinks you should, the right exposure shows what he's made of.
- Their religion is contaminated. It is not for you. Don't let anybody make you feel bad for who you are. You're made in God's image, special to him and us. When we get older you must sit here and keep this work going until Christ returns. Death or imprisonment shouldn't stop it; when Christ returns the righteous rise again.
- “Never trust thine enemy”. During the civil rights movement many white people, especially Jewish white people, came into the movement acting like they were helping Martin Luther King, but they were working for the FBI, undermining him and feeding him things, because the plan was to make America the nation it is today. Stanley Levison, who helped write the I Have a Dream speech, was one of them, and the so-called Jews brought the money into the movement.

    “Separate thyself from thine enemies, and take heed of thy friends.” Not keep your enemies closer: separate yourself from them.
  - **[Hebrews 12:16](/bible/hebrews/12#v16)**

    This is why we bring these examples out: our people keep trying to convert and change the enemy, thinking there are good ones, good heathen out there. The scripture says never trust thine enemy.

**[Ecclesiasticus 12:11](/bible/sirach/12#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/sirach/12#v11)</sup> Though he humble himself, and go crouching, yet take good heed and beware of him, and thou shalt be unto him as if thou hadst wiped a lookingglass, and thou shalt know that his rust hath not been altogether wiped away.

- “Though he humble himself, and go crouching, yet take good heed and beware of him”. He makes concessions, acts nice, lets you sit in the front of the bus and drink from the fountain, then cracks your forehead when he's ready. “his rust hath not been altogether wiped away.” Wipe the glass; the rust isn't all gone. Meanwhile they rob your resources and say they want to bring you Jesus Christ. The devil. You'd have to be out of your mind.
- “Though he humble himself, and go crouching, yet take good heed and beware of him”. He comes saying he wants to help with the black struggle, but he has an ulterior motive. “Thou shalt be unto him as if thou hadst wiped a lookingglass”: you will see him for what he is. “Thou shalt know that his rust hath not been altogether wiped away”: his rust is his evil, and it is still there. Never trust your enemies.
- Martin Luther King understood it, but too late. He told a friend five days before he was murdered that he was afraid he was integrating his people into a burning house; he saw that America had lost its moral vision and that black people were struggling to get into a system that is a Sodom and Gomorrah. When he said he had been to the mountaintop and seen the promised land, he was not talking about America; he was speaking of the land promised to us, the kingdom. He knew they were about to kill him. Do not sleep on Martin Luther King: he loved his people, and everything he did was for their betterment, but his movement was hijacked by liberals and by Jewish people.
- “Though he humble himself, and go crouching, yet take good heed and beware of him.” He was cool back then; now he is out trying to provoke black people to anger. Thou shalt know “that his rust hath not been altogether wiped away.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 12:12](/bible/sirach/12#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/12#v12)</sup> Set him not by thee, lest, when he hath overthrown thee, he stand up in thy place; neither let him sit at thy right hand, lest he seek to take thy seat, and thou at the last remember my words, and be pricked therewith.

- “Set him not by thee”. The saying, keep your friends close and your enemies closer, is not the Bible. Whoever came up with it didn’t have enemies. When you have an enemy, stay far away from him.
- Antipater had a position in our court, but he was secretly speaking with the Romans, currying favor with them. He was playing chess: positioning himself as loyal to the Maccabean family while he talked with Rome. When Hyrcanus II and his brother Aristobulus II were fighting, Antipater, a seditious and troublemaking man, exploited the weak-willed Hyrcanus, persuaded him to contend against his brother, and convinced him his brother meant to kill him. Aristobulus was a black man, a Maccabean. The diplomacy and artful politics of Antipater, a crafty snake and a devil, produced the Herodian dynasty, and his son Herod married the Hasmonean princess and usurped the throne of Judea under Rome.
- That is what this verse warns of: “lest, when he hath overthrown thee, he stand up in thy place; neither let him sit at thy right hand, lest he seek to take thy seat, and thou at the last remember my words, and be pricked therewith.” Hyrcanus set Antipater by him, and this is what took place. Because of Simon Maccabeus we were allowed to govern ourselves under the Greeks, but later the descendants of the Maccabees promoted Edomites to the throne.
