## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 33 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 10-15, 17, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 33:10-11](/bible/sirach/33#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/sirach/33#v10)</sup> And all men are from the ground, and Adam was created of earth.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/sirach/33#v11)</sup> In much knowledge the Lord hath divided them, and made their ways diverse.

- “And all men are from the ground, and Adam was created of earth. In much knowledge the Lord hath divided them, and made their ways diverse.” In much knowledge God divided all the men he created, and made everybody’s ways different.

**[Ecclesiasticus 33:12](/bible/sirach/33#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/33#v12)</sup> Some of them hath he blessed and exalted, and some of them hath he sanctified, and set near himself: but some of them hath he cursed and brought low, and turned out of their places.

- “Some of them hath he blessed and exalted”: that is Jacob, the twelve tribes of Israel, the so-called blacks and Hispanics. “and some of them hath he sanctified, and set near himself”.
- “but some of them hath he cursed and brought low, and turned out of their places.” God cursed the Caucasian race; they are a cursed people. That goes back to Genesis 4. They are called Edom, and that is why there is no pigment in their skin: that is a curse.

**[Ecclesiasticus 33:13](/bible/sirach/33#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/33#v13)</sup> As the clay is in the potter’s hand, to fashion it at his pleasure: so man is in the hand of him that made him, to render to them as liketh him best.

- God created this earth for righteousness’ sake. He chose a righteous man, Adam, to establish his earth in righteousness, and Eve was to be his helper, his stay and his comfort, establishing righteousness by following the commandments God taught Adam and that Adam was to teach his wife; but she did not want to do that. God also put two boys in the Bible for a reason, Adam had Cain and Abel, to show the difference between righteousness and wickedness. It is time to clear up the vision and see what God is really showing us. What are we doing here in America? Our land is in Jerusalem. That means something happened, and we made somebody mad to cause it. That is what the people in the churches need to understand about why we are catching all this hell, brutality and murder.
- We had to learn the negative before we can set up the positive, because you need contrast: good is set against evil. You cannot determine what is good if good is not set in contrast to evil. You don’t know what cold water is without getting burned by hot water, and you cannot tell what daylight is if you have never experienced night. That is why God put those two boys on this earth, and that is why his continuation has a period on it: once the fulfillment of his evil has reached its pinnacle, God has no more use for them. Round them all up, as in the book of Obadiah, burn them up and get rid of them. That is his fate, not ours, and you can’t do nothing to change it. The sick negro trying to pull him out the fire might burn up with him.
- Contrast is the state of being strikingly different from something else, juxtaposed, the other end of it. Black people and white people are contrast to each other, and God set it up that way.
- “As the clay is in the potter’s hand, to fashion it at his pleasure: so man is in the hand of him that made him”: man is in the hand of God that made him. That is contrast, good versus evil. The man he is fashioning is in the hand of the maker to do his purpose, “to render to them as liketh him best”: as he likes best, you are going to fulfill my will. He made the so-called white man to be the devil; he made him that way. He made you black men and women, Hispanic men and women, Native American Indian men and women, the jewels of God.

**[Ecclesiasticus 33:14](/bible/sirach/33#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/sirach/33#v14)</sup> Good is set against evil, and life against death: so is the godly against the sinner, and the sinner against the godly.

- “Good is set against evil”: this is the point made in the beginning. We would not be able to tell what good is if we did not have to go through the evil. So this wicked man has a purpose. His job was to show us what it is like being on this earth when God’s laws are not enforced, and you see what it has done: it has destroyed everything, including the minds of the people here. You got a man born with testicles and he says he wants to be a woman, and they say that’s okay. Here’s a woman born with breasts, vagina and every damn thing else, and she told my son she wants to be a man. That’s not God’s laws, and that kind of sick thinking will destroy everything.
- “And life against death”: you cannot appreciate life unless you know what death is.
- “So is the godly against the sinner”: how could we determine what godliness is without the understanding of sin? That’s the reason why we had to go through all this evil, to learn. “And the sinner against the godly”: so the Lord set up these trials.
- The understanding behind all of this is to illuminate the benefits of good against the detriment of evil, like the advantages of the day, where you can see clearly, against the perils of the night, where you’re most likely to get mugged and robbed. You learn to appreciate the daytime when you’ve experienced what happens at night. That’s what we are experiencing, living on this earth with this vile beast.

**[Ecclesiasticus 33:15](/bible/sirach/33#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/sirach/33#v15)</sup> So look upon all the works of the most High; and there are two and two, one against another.

- “So look upon all the works of the most High; and there are two and two, one against another.” We are warring against each other. That is why people cannot stand us, and why the righteous of us cannot stand the evil that they do. God set good against evil, so if we are against the so-called white man, it is because of evil.

**[Ecclesiasticus 33:17](/bible/sirach/33#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/sirach/33#v17)</sup> Consider that I laboured not for myself only, but for all them that seek learning.

- “Consider that I laboured not for myself only, but for all them that seek learning.” In this truth we are not doing it for ourselves. Captains left at four in the morning to be here to help teach; Captain Shamaya drove down and arrived at five to check the congregation and the saints of the Most High. The Bishops and Deacons travel all over the world. It is for the nation of Israel, because we understand the kingdom at hand.
- You must be nation-minded. Too often it is me, me, me. All through the Bible the greatest leaders were focused on the nation of Israel and on how to move the nation further ahead.
