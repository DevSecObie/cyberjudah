## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 48 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1, 8-13, 16, 22-25, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 48:1](/bible/sirach/48#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/48#v1)</sup> Then stood up Elias the prophet as fire, and his word burned like a lamp.

- “Then stood up Elias the prophet as fire, and his word burned like a lamp.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 48:8](/bible/sirach/48#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/sirach/48#v8)</sup> Who anointedst kings to take revenge, and prophets to succeed after him:

- What Ecclesiasticus said about Elijah: “Who anointedst kings to take revenge”: Elijah anointed kings to take revenge, “and prophets to succeed after him.” He was over schools. Elisha was the most prominent one, but there were others; he had set up schools.
- That is the difference between us and everybody else: Bishop is raising up men to succeed him. Bishop sees the picture; he already knows the leaders, the government, are going to kill him eventually, and we understand eventually they are going to kill us. So every brother, we are raising you up to succeed us. They cannot kill all of us: the spirit is going to jump from this brother to another brother, and the Most High is going to keep raising up the prophets. That’s why the Lord says he’s raising up a kingdom in the midst of this kingdom that will never be destroyed.
- A lot of leaders, even Israelite leaders, are the main teacher, the only one always teaching; they are not raising up men to succeed them, so when they fall the whole thing falls apart. Same thing happened with Marcus Garvey: when he fell that movement fell; Martin Luther King, Malcolm X, because they did not set up succession. Even these holidays, the Bishop is the one who usually did them, but a couple years ago he said you all deal with that, and now the deacons take it over. This truth is not about one man; it’s about all of us working together as a team. The Bible says they are going to make war and kill us: smite the shepherd and the sheep are going to scatter. You think the government is going to sit back and see us teach his gospel?

**[Ecclesiasticus 48:9](/bible/sirach/48#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/sirach/48#v9)</sup> Who was taken up in a whirlwind of fire, and in a chariot of fiery horses:

- “Who was taken up in a whirlwind of fire, and in a chariot of fiery horses”: we know that is Elijah.

**[Ecclesiasticus 48:10](/bible/sirach/48#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/sirach/48#v10)</sup> Who wast ordained for reproofs in their times, to pacify the wrath of the Lord’s judgment, before it brake forth into fury, and to turn the heart of the father unto the son, and to restore the tribes of Jacob.

- “Who wast ordained for reproofs in their times, to pacify the wrath of the Lord’s judgment, before it brake forth into fury”. That is our job, before the decree brings forth the fierce anger of God, as in Zephaniah 3. “And to turn the heart of the father unto the son, and to restore the tribes of Jacob.” Our job is to turn our people back to God. What is well pleasing to God is for us to keep his laws and then become the prophets who lead the people back to them. Much is given, much is required of us in our walk as leaders, so we have to be honest.
- “Who wast ordained for reproofs in their times”: not one time. He is ordained for reproofs in multiple times; that’s why it is said he shall first come. “To pacify the wrath of the Lord’s judgment, before it brake forth into fury”: he had to come so Christ doesn’t have to come in and destroy everything; he’s got to raise this up again.
- “And to restore the tribes of Jacob”: that’s his job. That means he would come teaching both kingdoms: this is Judah, this is Ephraim. Not ‘this is blacks only’; if it’s blacks only, you ain’t the guys. Abba was the guy.

**[Ecclesiasticus 48:11](/bible/sirach/48#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/sirach/48#v11)</sup> Blessed are they that saw thee, and slept in love; for we shall surely live.

- “Blessed are they that saw thee, and slept in love”: blessed are those that saw him, whoever he was, whether in that time of John or in our time. “For we shall surely live”: we shall surely live as an exceeding great army.
- And it’s not the end of it. Bivens died, but he’s back: he might be in his 20s, he might be in his 30s. He keeps coming, and won’t stop coming until Christ returns.

**[Ecclesiasticus 48:12](/bible/sirach/48#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/48#v12)</sup> Elias it was, who was covered with a whirlwind: and Eliseus was filled with his spirit: whilst he lived, he was not moved with the presence of any prince, neither could any bring him into subjection.

- Here is the example of Elijah: the scripture says nothing moved him, not words, nothing. “Elias it was, who was covered with a whirlwind”: Elias is Elijah, and Eliseus, “filled with his spirit”, is Elisha.
- “Whilst he lived, he was not moved with the presence of any prince”: it don’t care if you were royalty, he was not moved. “Neither could any bring him into subjection”: nobody. That means not his sins, not his lust, not his desires; he was not going to be subject to them. Now those situations control people instead of them being in control. Those things move you, they become the stuff that motivates you and moves you, and it should not be that way. That’s wrong; that’s not the mind of Christ.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 11:7-11](/bible/matthew/11#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/matthew/11#v7)</sup> And as they departed, Jesus began to say unto the multitudes concerning John, What went ye out into the wilderness to see? A reed shaken with the wind?
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/matthew/11#v8)</sup> But what went ye out for to see? A man clothed in soft raiment? behold, they that wear soft clothing are in kings’ houses.
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/matthew/11#v9)</sup> But what went ye out for to see? A prophet? yea, I say unto you, and more than a prophet.
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/matthew/11#v10)</sup> For this is he, of whom it is written, Behold, I send my messenger before thy face, which shall prepare thy way before thee.
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/matthew/11#v11)</sup> Verily I say unto you, Among them that are born of women there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist: notwithstanding he that is least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he.

    Christ speaks “unto the multitudes concerning John”, John the Baptist, and when we get further we know who he was speaking about: Elijah. “What went ye out into the wilderness to see? A reed shaken with the wind?” No. Not a man moved by every wind, and not “a man clothed in soft raiment”, for “they that wear soft clothing are in kings’ houses.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 48:13](/bible/sirach/48#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/48#v13)</sup> No word could overcome him; and after his death his body prophesied.

- “No word could overcome him”: no matter what you said. Sticks and stones may break, and nothing overcame him.
- “And after his death his body prophesied”: that’s literal. First there was a memorial of him, and then the times that he’s come back, he is always unmovable and always unshakable. Think of his mission when he came back in these recent times: he had to come to bring understanding back to us after we were in darkness, during the rise of all these masturbatory substitutes, with the people laughing him to shame and trying to scorn him. If he was movable, this thing wouldn’t be where we are now. He wasn’t moved. You got to build your walls up.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 11:13-14](/bible/matthew/11#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/matthew/11#v13)</sup> For all the prophets and the law prophesied until John.
    >
    > <sup>[14](/bible/matthew/11#v14)</sup> And if ye will receive it, this is Elias, which was for to come.

    “For all the prophets and the law prophesied until John. And if ye will receive it, this is Elias, which was for to come.” This is Elias, like we just read in Ecclesiasticus.

**[Ecclesiasticus 48:16](/bible/sirach/48#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/sirach/48#v16)</sup> Of whom some did that which was pleasing to God, and some multiplied sins.

- “Of whom some did that which was pleasing to God, and some multiplied sins.” Some did the laws of God, and some multiplied sins.

  Precepts:
  - **[Baruch 4:1, 4](/bible/baruch/4#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/baruch/4#v1)</sup> This is the book of the commandments of God, and the law that endureth for ever: all they that keep it shall come to life; but such as leave it shall die.
    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/baruch/4#v4)</sup> O Israel, happy are we: for things that are pleasing to God are made known unto us.

    “This is the book of the commandments of God, and the law that endureth for ever: all they that keep it shall come to life; but such as leave it shall die.” “O Israel, happy are we: for things that are pleasing to God are made known unto us.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 48:22-23](/bible/sirach/48#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/sirach/48#v22)</sup> For Ezekias had done the thing that pleased the Lord, and was strong in the ways of David his father, as Esay the prophet, who was great and faithful in his vision, had commanded him.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/sirach/48#v23)</sup> In his time the sun went backward, and he lengthened the king’s life.

- Hezekiah as king “had done the thing that pleased the Lord”, and Isaiah the prophet was with him, “great and faithful in his vision.” In his time the sun went backward and the king’s life was lengthened.

**[Ecclesiasticus 48:24-25](/bible/sirach/48#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/sirach/48#v24)</sup> He saw by an excellent spirit what should come to pass at the last, and he comforted them that mourned in Sion.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/sirach/48#v25)</sup> He shewed what should come to pass for ever, and secret things or ever they came.

- “He saw by an excellent spirit what should come to pass at the last, and he comforted them that mourned in Sion. He shewed what should come to pass for ever, and secret things or ever they came.” That is what a true prophet does: comfort the people that mourn and explain to them the secret things. No ministry is doing what we do, going into the Bible and showing secret things while the world is falling apart.
