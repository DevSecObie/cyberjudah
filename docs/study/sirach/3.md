## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Ecclesiasticus 3 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-16, 19-31, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:1-2](/bible/sirach/3#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/3#v1)</sup> Hear me your father, O children, and do thereafter, that ye may be safe.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/3#v2)</sup> For the Lord hath given the father honour over the children, and hath confirmed the authority of the mother over the sons.

- When we talk to our people in the community, some parents say their kids are too young to understand, too young to learn this information. But they weren't too young for a PlayStation and Grand Theft Auto, Roblox and Minecraft, and there are toddlers trained up in sports and entertainment, doing everything the father did and told them, getting better until it was natural habit. Grown men have to be that same example biblically and spiritually, and the sisters have to understand that the reflection of the children is cast from the man, because what they see us do, they do. Right now a child is waiting for class to be over so he can do the chant. We have to be what these scriptures are and live it.
- “Hear me your father, O children, and do thereafter, that ye may be safe. For the Lord hath given the father honour over the children, and hath confirmed the authority of the mother over the sons.” This is the order of the household. Around nine or ten a son starts to feel himself, and the whoopings and fussing from mom wear off. That's when the father steps in: you don't talk to your mama like that; you don't deal with my wife like that. It teaches him the commandments and how to discipline himself when the father is out of town: you have to be who I am when I'm not in the house. How can she trust you to take care of her when you deal with her wrongfully and don't honor her?
- No matter how old the son gets, he will never be older than his mama. Though he be a ruler in Israel, he will never reach the age or position to disrespect her or treat her as less; she is always the authority over him. We have to keep instilling that, because sons will overtake our women, and the women get tired and make too much of their sons, letting them do whatever because they can't control them.
- If we can’t get this basic law of honoring father and mother together, we are messed up and ain’t getting the kingdom. “Hear me your father, O children, and do thereafter, that ye may be safe.” We all want our children to be safe: follow the guidelines God has laid out.

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:3-4](/bible/sirach/3#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/sirach/3#v3)</sup> Whoso honoureth his father maketh an atonement for his sins:
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/sirach/3#v4)</sup> And he that honoureth his mother is as one that layeth up treasure.

- “Whoso honoureth his father maketh an atonement for his sins”. You thought only giving alms made an atonement for sins; honoring your father makes an atonement for sins. “And he that honoureth his mother is as one that layeth up treasure.” All of it goes back to Exodus 20:12.
- “Whoso honoureth his father maketh an atonement for his sins: And he that honoureth his mother is as one that layeth up treasure.” The Lord sees how you treat your parents and the authority over you, and that authority isn't only your own mother and father. It is your elders, and the respect for our forefathers in the scriptures, because they are who we're ultimately listening to: showing respect to what Moses wrote and his sacrifices, to what Abraham, Isaac and Jacob instilled in us, and taking the words of King Solomon and King David and saying, yes, sir, I'm going to do what you told me to do. That goes to the ears and eyes of the Lord: we listen to the Father who created us by listening to our parents.

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:5-6](/bible/sirach/3#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/sirach/3#v5)</sup> Whoso honoureth his father shall have joy of his own children; and when he maketh his prayer, he shall be heard.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/sirach/3#v6)</sup> He that honoureth his father shall have a long life; and he that is obedient unto the Lord shall be a comfort to his mother.

- “Whoso honoureth his father shall have joy of his own children; and when he maketh his prayer, he shall be heard.” God will hear your prayers. “He that honoureth his father shall have a long life”.
- “Whoso honoureth his father shall have joy of his own children”. You want that respect and reverence from your own children, and they see how you talk to your parents and how you do or don't deal with them. “And when he maketh his prayer, he shall be heard.” “He that honoureth his father shall have a long life; and he that is obedient unto the Lord shall be a comfort to his mother.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:7](/bible/sirach/3#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/sirach/3#v7)</sup> He that feareth the Lord will honour his father, and will do service unto his parents, as to his masters.

- “He that feareth the Lord will honour his father, and will do service unto his parents, as to his masters.” Doing service means obeying: yes, sir, I'm on it. It teaches a child to govern his own life, because he will always be at somebody's service. Employed, or even an entrepreneur with clients and customers, there is always somebody ruling over you. Being a child is being a servant to your parents, and they are teaching you to be a servant in life, because you will be responsible for being a servant to God. It is all training to serve God. Serve your parents at a child's level and you can serve men at an adult level, and serve God as an adult and bear the Most High's chastening and the consequences of your actions, good or bad.

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:8-9](/bible/sirach/3#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/sirach/3#v8)</sup> Honour thy father and mother both in word and deed, that a blessing may come upon thee from them.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/sirach/3#v9)</sup> For the blessing of the father establisheth the houses of children; but the curse of the mother rooteth out foundations.

- “Honour thy father and mother both in word and deed”. You can't be a respectful kid, yes sir, yes ma'am, and not do what they say; that throws out the politeness. The deed is what matters. When the trash can is full and dirty, you take the trash out, clean the can, and put it out for the truck, consistently, because it's your duty. If you can't listen to those words and do that deed, how will you be an acceptable citizen, or an acceptable servant to God?
- “That a blessing may come upon thee from them. For the blessing of the father establisheth the houses of children”. Like the children trained up from toddlers, we have to be the same in word and deed to our children: give them the words, show them how, put them to the test, and navigate them through the scriptures as we do. They should be the mirror of what we are righteously, because our mirror is our Father. “But the curse of the mother rooteth out foundations.”
- “Honour thy father and mother both in word and deed”: don’t say you’re going to do it and then not do it. “That a blessing may come upon thee from them”: from your parents.
- “For the blessing of the father establisheth the houses of children; but the curse of the mother rooteth out foundations.” So don’t disrespect your mother.

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:10-11](/bible/sirach/3#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/sirach/3#v10)</sup> Glory not in the dishonour of thy father; for thy father’s dishonour is no glory unto thee.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/sirach/3#v11)</sup> For the glory of a man is from the honour of his father; and a mother in dishonour is a reproach to the children.

- “Glory not in the dishonour of thy father”. Some get happy at their father being dishonored, whether he was a crackhead or got locked up. “For thy father’s dishonour is no glory unto thee”: nine times out of ten you are your father, and his dishonor is your dishonor.
- “For the glory of a man is from the honour of his father; and a mother in dishonour is a reproach to the children.” Whatever happens with the parents reflects on the children. That is why in Tobit, Tobit asked the angel what family he was of: he wanted to know his family stock, his seed. If his parents had a reputation as thieves and cutthroats, he would not let him go with his son. The reputation of the parents often comes down upon the children.
- “Glory not in the dishonour of thy father”. Even if your parent in your eyes isn't much, don't utter it; keep it within yourself, deal with the parents you have, and don't repeat their steps. What you put in your mind stays with you: you rehearse it and act out how you feel about your parents, and that acting out spreads to disrespecting other elders, until the child is headstrong and can't be told what to do. “For thy father’s dishonour is no glory unto thee. For the glory of a man is from the honour of his father; and a mother in dishonour is a reproach to the children.”

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:12-13](/bible/sirach/3#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/3#v12)</sup> My son, help thy father in his age, and grieve him not as long as he liveth.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/3#v13)</sup> And if his understanding fail, have patience with him; and despise him not when thou art in thy full strength.

- “My son, help thy father in his age, and grieve him not as long as he liveth. And if his understanding fail”, if he gets Alzheimer’s or dementia, “have patience with him; and despise him not when thou art in thy full strength.”
- “My son, help thy father in his age”. Everything you establish in a child comes back to you. This is the joy: your son can take on the tasks you can't do anymore and relieve your stress, and the older he gets, the more you put on him. The same with the daughters. Mothers complain about cooking and cleaning, but they should have been training their daughters to cook at least one meal a day and serve in the household, learning daily life skills beyond cleaning themselves and doing schoolwork, so they can repay it. Otherwise, who takes care of you when you're sick and down? It goes into how you lead and treat other brothers in this body too.
- “And grieve him not as long as he liveth. And if his understanding fail, have patience with him”. That is the key: repetition. Learning requires repetition, structure, practice, accountability and consistency, not just volume. Saying something is not the same as teaching it.

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:14-15](/bible/sirach/3#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/sirach/3#v14)</sup> For the relieving of thy father shall not be forgotten: and instead of sins it shall be added to build thee up.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/sirach/3#v15)</sup> In the day of thine affliction it shall be remembered; thy sins also shall melt away, as the ice in the fair warm weather.

- “For the relieving of thy father shall not be forgotten”: God will not forget that you relieved your father. “In the day of thine affliction it shall be remembered”: affliction comes to all of us, and in that day what you did for your father will be remembered. “Thy sins also shall melt away, as the ice in the fair warm weather”: your sins will be forgiven.

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:16](/bible/sirach/3#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/sirach/3#v16)</sup> He that forsaketh his father is as a blasphemer; and he that angereth his mother is cursed of God.

- “He that forsaketh his father is as a blasphemer; and he that angereth his mother is cursed of God.” Reflect back on the videos: the girl beating the crap out of her mother is going to pay for that. The Lord will make that daughter pay.

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:19](/bible/sirach/3#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/sirach/3#v19)</sup> Many are in high place, and of renown: but mysteries are revealed unto the meek.

- “Many are in high place, and of renown”: people in powerful positions, high government, celebrities, athletes. “But mysteries are revealed unto the meek.” The meek of the earth are the Israelites; the mysteries of God have been revealed unto us.

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:20-22](/bible/sirach/3#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/sirach/3#v20)</sup> For the power of the Lord is great, and he is honoured of the lowly.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/sirach/3#v21)</sup> Seek not out the things that are too hard for thee, neither search the things that are above thy strength.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/sirach/3#v22)</sup> But what is commanded thee, think thereupon with reverence; for it is not needful for thee to see with thine eyes the things that are in secret.

- “Seek not out the things that are too hard for thee”, then “But what is commanded thee, think thereupon with reverence.” He commanded us to keep the commandments and keep his covenant, and he will give us everything else and put us high above all nations of the earth. “It is not needful for thee to see with thine eyes the things that are in secret”: some things God keeps to himself. That is why some of you are into tarot cards, brujería, Changó, voodoo, wanting to see secret spiritual things. All you have to do is keep the commandments.

This is why the media does not understand what is going on: the scripture tells us something totally different from what they are saying. That is why the Illuminati and secret societies don’t matter; they will burn when we get our turn, and the Bible is the only thing we are supposed to learn.

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:24-25](/bible/sirach/3#v24)**

All she had to do was obey.

**[Ecclesiasticus 3:30-31](/bible/sirach/3#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/sirach/3#v30)</sup> Water will quench a flaming fire; and alms maketh an atonement for sins.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/sirach/3#v31)</sup> And he that requiteth good turns is mindful of that which may come hereafter; and when he falleth, he shall find a stay.

- “Water will quench a flaming fire; and alms maketh an atonement for sins. And he that requiteth good turns is mindful of that which may come hereafter; and when he falleth, he shall find a stay.” Because of your behavior, if you have a right heart with God, when you fall you will find a stay. We are all going to make mistakes and things will go bad for us, but there is a way so that when things go bad the Lord will sustain you.
- Never get to that place of pride where God has to hide his hope from you and say, you are on your own. The same way a parent has to tell his child, you are on your own; the same way a spouse has to tell someone they love, you need to get out; the same way the world leaders are telling Donald Trump, you are on your own, you are not getting my country bombed up. Pride is deceitful, and most people in it don’t understand that they are in pride. When you correct them over and over and they don’t listen, you have to separate yourself from them.
- “Water will quench a flaming fire; and alms maketh an atonement for sins.” Alms is assistance for your people, for the poor; when Israel collected alms it was for the healing and the building of the nation, not for a pastor’s Bentley, mansion and jet-set life. As water puts out a fire, alms given to the right people, people not in evil, make an atonement for sin; God sees you have mercy in your heart. That is a mystery the church doesn’t know. If you miss a month of alms, double up the next.
- “He that requiteth good turns is mindful of that which may come hereafter”: there is cause and effect in giving. “When he falleth, he shall find a stay”: like Manasses, who did much evil but still helped his people, and the Lord delivered him.
