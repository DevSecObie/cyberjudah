Read the chapter → [Psalms 39](/bible/psalms/39)

This session covers [Psalms 39](/bible/psalms/39) through 42. It picks up from [Psalm 38](/bible/psalms/38), where King David asks the Most High for mercy over the sin he committed: he murdered Uriah the Hittite and committed adultery. These four chapters are almost a continuation of that, David going through affliction because of his sin and the opposition that came after it: Absalom trying to overthrow him, Absalom lying with his wives, the death of his child, and a loathsome disease in his flesh. The thread throughout is repentance. David sinned a sin most of us have not committed, yet he turned back to the Most High and asked forgiveness, and that has to be our mindset too. Righteousness is not never sinning; it is getting yourself back up and pressing forward, and David is the perfect example.

**[Psalm 39:1-3](/bible/psalms/39#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/39#v1)</sup> I said, I will take heed to my ways, that I sin not with my tongue: I will keep my mouth with a bridle, while the wicked is before me.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/39#v2)</sup> I was dumb with silence, I held my peace, even from good; and my sorrow was stirred.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/39#v3)</sup> My heart was hot within me, while I was musing the fire burned: then spake I with my tongue,

- David says he will keep his mouth with a bridle. A bridle is the bit you see in a horse's mouth that you use to move it whichever way you want; it is a restraint. So he restrains his mouth when the wicked is before him.
- "I was dumb with silence" means he was lost for words. This ties back to [Psalm 38](/bible/psalms/38), where those who saw his affliction mocked him and he had nothing to say in that moment.
- He held his tongue among the wicked, and as he thought, the fire burned. He became more and more frustrated, and then he spoke with his tongue.

**[Psalm 39:4-6](/bible/psalms/39#v4)**
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/39#v4)</sup> Lord, make me to know mine end, and the measure of my days, what it is; that I may know how frail I am.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/39#v5)</sup> Behold, thou hast made my days as an handbreadth; and mine age is as nothing before thee: verily every man at his best state is altogether vanity. Selah.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/39#v6)</sup> Surely every man walketh in a vain shew: surely they are disquieted in vain: he heapeth up riches, and knoweth not who shall gather them.

- David asks the Lord to make him know his end and the measure of his days, so he may know how frail he is. This is him going through his affliction and thinking on his own death.
- Every man at his best state is altogether vanity. At your prime you are nothing; you cannot measure up to the Most High. Men's lives are short, and the sentence for all flesh is death.
- When you die you take no wisdom and no riches with you. A man heaps up riches and does not even know who shall gather them.
  - **[Psalm 62:9](/bible/psalms/62#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/62#v9)</sup> Surely men of low degree are vanity, and men of high degree are a lie: to be laid in the balance, they are altogether lighter than vanity.

    Men of low degree are vanity and men of high degree are a lie; laid in the balance they are altogether lighter than vanity, so the life we have is vain and short-lived.

**[Psalm 39:5](/bible/psalms/39#v5) (continued)**
*5 Behold, thou hast made my days as an handbreadth; and mine age is as nothing before thee: verily every man at his best state is altogether vanity. Selah.*
- Look at how the Most High cut down our days, from the hundreds of years he originally gave to 120, then to 70. You rarely see people pass 100 years. Our age is as nothing before him.

**[Psalm 39:7-9](/bible/psalms/39#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/39#v7)</sup> And now, Lord, what wait I for? my hope is in thee.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/39#v8)</sup> Deliver me from all my transgressions: make me not the reproach of the foolish.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/39#v9)</sup> I was dumb, I opened not my mouth; because thou didst it.

- David asks the Lord to deliver him from all his transgressions and not to make him the reproach of the foolish. This is the same request as [Psalm 51](/bible/psalms/51), where he asks for a clean heart and for his iniquities to be taken away.
- This is the note we must take from David: he committed murder and adultery, sins many of us have not committed, yet he still turned back to the Lord and asked forgiveness. That has to be our mindset.
- "I was dumb, I opened not my mouth" because the Lord did it. He is consumed by the blow of the Lord's hand, meaning he is consumed with the afflictions the Most High gave him.

**[Psalm 39:11](/bible/psalms/39#v11)**
> <sup>[11](/bible/psalms/39#v11)</sup> When thou with rebukes dost correct man for iniquity, thou makest his beauty to consume away like a moth: surely every man is vanity. Selah.

- When the Most High rebukes a man for iniquity, he makes his beauty consume away like a moth. When man commits iniquity the Lord corrects, and many times people end up dying when they commit iniquity.
  - **[Job 24:19](/bible/job/24#v19)**
    > <sup>[19](/bible/job/24#v19)</sup> Drought and heat consume the snow waters: so doth the grave those which have sinned.

    Drought and heat consume the snow waters; so does the grave those which have sinned. The grave is what waits for those who commit sin, and that is what David is referring to.
- This is what we must keep in mind as we walk: there is really one thing waiting for us if we are not keeping the commandments, and that is the grave. For some who keep the commandments the grave may still come, but eternal life waits after it, and David understood this, which is why he repented.

**[Psalm 39:12-13](/bible/psalms/39#v12)**
> <sup>[12](/bible/psalms/39#v12)</sup> Hear my prayer, O Lord, and give ear unto my cry; hold not thy peace at my tears: for I am a stranger with thee, and a sojourner, as all my fathers were.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/psalms/39#v13)</sup> O spare me, that I may recover strength, before I go hence, and be no more.

- David asks the Lord to hear his prayer and hold not his peace at his tears, for he is a stranger and a sojourner as all his fathers were. He asks to be spared that he may recover strength before he goes hence and is no more.
- "Spare me that I may recover strength" means forgive my sins so I may bounce back before I die. That should be our thought too. Nobody knows when they will die; you could die tomorrow or in two seconds from a bullet, so your main focus must be to recover your strength before you go to the grave.
- Everybody who comes into the truth is going to mess up and commit transgression, and everybody ends up where David ended up, having to ask forgiveness and be pardoned. The Lord continually afflicted David for his iniquity, and a lot of us are being afflicted for our iniquities to this very day, so we must come to the Lord with this mindset.
  - **[Baruch 4:28](/bible/baruch/4#v28)**
    > <sup>[28](/bible/baruch/4#v28)</sup> For as it was your mind to go astray from God: so, being returned, seek him ten times more.

    When our mind goes astray and we end up in sin, we return and seek him ten times more; committing iniquity does not mean you cannot recover strength.

# Psalm 40: Patient Waiting, Obedience Over Sacrifice, and the Great Congregation

Read the chapter → [Psalms 40](/bible/psalms/40)

**[Psalm 40:1-2](/bible/psalms/40#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/40#v1)</sup> I waited patiently for the Lord; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/40#v2)</sup> He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings.

- David waited patiently for the Lord, and the Lord inclined to him and heard his cry. The Lord only hears the cry of those keeping the commandments, so even while afflicted David kept pushing and kept the commandments rather than saying "I already sinned, I might as well keep sinning."
- The Lord brought him up out of a horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set his feet upon a rock. The Lord lifted up his spirit, gave him the pardon, and established his goings.
  - **[Proverbs 3:1-2](/bible/proverbs/3#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/3#v1)</sup> My son, forget not my law; but let thine heart keep my commandments:
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/proverbs/3#v2)</sup> For length of days, and long life, and peace, shall they add to thee.

    Forget not the law, but let thine heart keep the commandments, for length of days and long life and peace shall they add to thee.
  - **[Proverbs 3:5-6](/bible/proverbs/3#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/proverbs/3#v5)</sup> Trust in the Lord with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/proverbs/3#v6)</sup> In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.

    Trust in the Lord with all thine heart and lean not to thine own understanding; in all thy ways acknowledge him and he shall direct thy paths. David leaned on his own understanding, went off for a minute, then returned and applied this.

**[Psalm 40:3-5](/bible/psalms/40#v3)**
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/40#v3)</sup> And he hath put a new song in my mouth, even praise unto our God: many shall see it, and fear, and shall trust in the Lord.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/40#v4)</sup> Blessed is that man that maketh the Lord his trust, and respecteth not the proud, nor such as turn aside to lies.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/40#v5)</sup> Many, O Lord my God, are thy wonderful works which thou hast done, and thy thoughts which are to us-ward: they cannot be reckoned up in order unto thee: if I would declare and speak of them, they are more than can be numbered.

- The Lord put a new song in David's mouth, praise to God, so that many shall see it and fear and trust in the Lord. Blessed is the man who makes the Lord his trust.
- The Most High's wonderful works and his thoughts toward us cannot be reckoned up in order; if David would declare them, they are more than can be numbered.

**[Psalm 40:6](/bible/psalms/40#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/40#v6)</sup> Sacrifice and offering thou didst not desire; mine ears hast thou opened: burnt offering and sin offering hast thou not required.

- Even in David's time he understood that sacrifice and offering were not what the Lord desired. There are multiple places where the Lord was not looking for the sacrifices.
  - **[1 Samuel 15:22](/bible/1-samuel/15#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/1-samuel/15#v22)</sup> And Samuel said, Hath the Lord as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the Lord? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.

    The Lord delights in obeying his voice more than in burnt offerings and sacrifices; to obey is better than sacrifice and to hearken than the fat of rams.
  - **[Hebrews 10:1-3](/bible/hebrews/10#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/hebrews/10#v1)</sup> For the law having a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with those sacrifices which they offered year by year continually make the comers thereunto perfect.
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/hebrews/10#v2)</sup> For then would they not have ceased to be offered? because that the worshippers once purged should have had no more conscience of sins.
    >
    > <sup>[3](/bible/hebrews/10#v3)</sup> But in those sacrifices there is a remembrance again made of sins every year.

    The law had a shadow of good things; those sacrifices offered year by year could never make the comers perfect, and in them is a remembrance again made of sins every year. The Lord wants obedience over sacrifice.
- Sacrifice is really just a show of your obedience. The Pharisees liked to say you could not get the kingdom without sacrifice, but they did not keep the commandments; they were all show and all talk, so they were the ones who could not make it to the kingdom.

**[Psalm 40:7-8](/bible/psalms/40#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/40#v7)</sup> Then said I, Lo, I come: in the volume of the book it is written of me,
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/40#v8)</sup> I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart.

- "Lo, I come: in the volume of the book it is written of me" is a hint at Christ. "I delight to do thy will, O my God" and "thy law is within mine heart" show that the will of God is keeping the laws of God. That is what he wants from you.

**[Psalm 40:9-10](/bible/psalms/40#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/40#v9)</sup> I have preached righteousness in the great congregation: lo, I have not refrained my lips, O Lord, thou knowest.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/psalms/40#v10)</sup> I have not hid thy righteousness within my heart; I have declared thy faithfulness and thy salvation: I have not concealed thy lovingkindness and thy truth from the great congregation.

- David preached righteousness in the great congregation and did not refrain his lips. The great congregation is Israel, Zion, not any other nation or people.
  - **[Psalm 22:22-23](/bible/psalms/22#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/psalms/22#v22)</sup> I will declare thy name unto my brethren: in the midst of the congregation will I praise thee.
    >
    > <sup>[23](/bible/psalms/22#v23)</sup> Ye that fear the Lord, praise him; all ye the seed of Jacob, glorify him; and fear him, all ye the seed of Israel.

    "I will declare thy name unto my brethren; in the midst of the congregation will I praise thee." The congregation is the seed of Jacob, the seed of Israel, the Israelites, not any other nation. If you are thinking of Esau or Ishmael or any other nation, it is not referring to them.
- David has not hidden the Lord's righteousness within his heart; he has declared his faithfulness, salvation, lovingkindness, and truth from the great congregation, the Israelites.

**[Psalm 40:11-12](/bible/psalms/40#v11)**
> <sup>[11](/bible/psalms/40#v11)</sup> Withhold not thou thy tender mercies from me, O Lord: let thy lovingkindness and thy truth continually preserve me.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/psalms/40#v12)</sup> For innumerable evils have compassed me about: mine iniquities have taken hold upon me, so that I am not able to look up; they are more than the hairs of mine head: therefore my heart faileth me.

- David asks the Lord not to withhold his tender mercies, for innumerable evils have compassed him about and his iniquities have taken hold of him, more than the hairs of his head, so that his heart fails him.
- This is the shame that came upon him for the things he did. It was not a hidden sin; many people were aware of the sin David committed. When we commit wickedness and then get around the brothers keeping the commandments, sorrow comes upon us because we know we were not supposed to do it and now feel we do not fit in. Everybody flips up, but the example is David getting himself together.

**[Psalm 40:13-15](/bible/psalms/40#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/psalms/40#v13)</sup> Be pleased, O Lord, to deliver me: O Lord, make haste to help me.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/psalms/40#v14)</sup> Let them be ashamed and confounded together that seek after my soul to destroy it; let them be driven backward and put to shame that wish me evil.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/psalms/40#v15)</sup> Let them be desolate for a reward of their shame that say unto me, Aha, aha.

- David asks the Lord to make haste to help him and to put to shame those who seek after his soul to destroy it, and those who say "Aha" to mock him for his transgressions.
  - **[Psalm 38:11-12](/bible/psalms/38#v11)**
    > <sup>[11](/bible/psalms/38#v11)</sup> My lovers and my friends stand aloof from my sore; and my kinsmen stand afar off.
    >
    > <sup>[12](/bible/psalms/38#v12)</sup> They also that seek after my life lay snares for me: and they that seek my hurt speak mischievous things, and imagine deceits all the day long.

    His lovers and friends stood aloof from his sore, his kinsmen stood afar off, and those who sought his life laid snares and spoke mischievous things; but he was as a deaf and dumb man who opened not his mouth, having no room to speak for what he did.
  - **[Psalm 35:3-6](/bible/psalms/35#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/35#v3)</sup> Draw out also the spear, and stop the way against them that persecute me: say unto my soul, I am thy salvation.
    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/35#v4)</sup> Let them be confounded and put to shame that seek after my soul: let them be turned back and brought to confusion that devise my hurt.
    >
    > <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/35#v5)</sup> Let them be as chaff before the wind: and let the angel of the Lord chase them.
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/35#v6)</sup> Let their way be dark and slippery: and let the angel of the Lord persecute them.

    "Say unto my soul, I am thy salvation." Let them be confounded and put to shame that seek his soul; let them be as chaff before the wind and let the angel of the Lord chase them. This applies to our enemies to this day: look at the education systems and the conditions of our people; every nation outside the Israelites is trying to hide a net for our soul.
- Every nation outside the Israelites falls in this category, seeking the heart of the Israelites without cause.
  - **[Proverbs 24:17-18](/bible/proverbs/24#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/proverbs/24#v17)</sup> Rejoice not when thine enemy falleth, and let not thine heart be glad when he stumbleth:
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/proverbs/24#v18)</sup> Lest the Lord see it, and it displease him, and he turn away his wrath from him.

    Rejoice not when thine enemy falleth and let not thine heart be glad when he stumbleth, lest the Lord see it and turn his wrath away from him. That wrath then turns onto you, even if the enemy is one of your own people you have issues with.

**[Psalm 40:16-17](/bible/psalms/40#v16)**
> <sup>[16](/bible/psalms/40#v16)</sup> Let all those that seek thee rejoice and be glad in thee: let such as love thy salvation say continually, The Lord be magnified.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/psalms/40#v17)</sup> But I am poor and needy; yet the Lord thinketh upon me: thou art my help and my deliverer; make no tarrying, O my God.

- Let those who seek the Lord rejoice and be glad, and let those who love his salvation say continually, the Lord be magnified. Though David is poor and needy, the Lord thinks upon him; the Lord is his help and his deliverer.

# Psalm 41: Blessed Is He That Considers the Poor

Read the chapter → [Psalms 41](/bible/psalms/41)

**[Psalm 41:1-4](/bible/psalms/41#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/41#v1)</sup> Blessed is he that considereth the poor: the Lord will deliver him in time of trouble.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/41#v2)</sup> The Lord will preserve him, and keep him alive; and he shall be blessed upon the earth: and thou wilt not deliver him unto the will of his enemies.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/41#v3)</sup> The Lord will strengthen him upon the bed of languishing: thou wilt make all his bed in his sickness.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/41#v4)</sup> I said, Lord, be merciful unto me: heal my soul; for I have sinned against thee.

- Blessed is he that considers the poor; the Lord will deliver him in time of trouble. This applies to the whole nation, because we are the poor.
  - **[Isaiah 14:32](/bible/isaiah/14#v32)**
    > <sup>[32](/bible/isaiah/14#v32)</sup> What shall one then answer the messengers of the nation? That the Lord hath founded Zion, and the poor of his people shall trust in it.

    The Lord has founded Zion, and the poor of his people shall trust in it. Zion is the Israelites, so the poor the Lord speaks of are the Israelites.
  - **[Matthew 5:3](/bible/matthew/5#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/matthew/5#v3)</sup> Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.

    Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven; the same thing, the poor are the Israelites.
- The Lord will preserve the one who considers the poor, keep him alive, bless him on the earth, and not deliver him to the will of his enemies. David asks the Lord to be merciful and heal his soul, for he has sinned.

**[Psalm 41:5-9](/bible/psalms/41#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/41#v5)</sup> Mine enemies speak evil of me, When shall he die, and his name perish?
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/41#v6)</sup> And if he come to see me, he speaketh vanity: his heart gathereth iniquity to itself; when he goeth abroad, he telleth it.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/41#v7)</sup> All that hate me whisper together against me: against me do they devise my hurt.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/41#v8)</sup> An evil disease, say they, cleaveth fast unto him: and now that he lieth he shall rise up no more.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/41#v9)</sup> Yea, mine own familiar friend, in whom I trusted, which did eat of my bread, hath lifted up his heel against me.

- David's enemies speak evil of him, saying "when shall he die, and his name perish," meaning when will this man be buried and just go. This is a high level of hatred. From other nations he is not surprised, but if you are in the truth and have this hatred toward your own people, that is wicked.
- When his enemy comes to see him he speaks vanity, flattering words, while his heart gathers iniquity to itself, and when he goes abroad he tells it and slanders him. All that hate David whisper together against him and devise his hurt.
- They say an evil disease cleaves fast to him and that now he lies down he shall rise up no more; they rejoice in the affliction and disease David acquired through his transgressions. When you fall in this truth, some spirits are built to mock and bring hurt to the spirit of the one who slipped.
- Even his own familiar friend, in whom he trusted, who ate of his bread, has lifted up his heel against him. You have no clue who your real friends are until you fall.
  - **[Micah 7:8-10](/bible/micah/7#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/micah/7#v8)</sup> Rejoice not against me, O mine enemy: when I fall, I shall arise; when I sit in darkness, the Lord shall be a light unto me.
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/micah/7#v9)</sup> I will bear the indignation of the Lord, because I have sinned against him, until he plead my cause, and execute judgment for me: he will bring me forth to the light, and I shall behold his righteousness.
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/micah/7#v10)</sup> Then she that is mine enemy shall see it, and shame shall cover her which said unto me, Where is the Lord thy God? mine eyes shall behold her: now shall she be trodden down as the mire of the streets.

    Rejoice not against me, O mine enemy; when I fall I shall arise, and when I sit in darkness the Lord shall be a light unto me. I will bear the indignation of the Lord because I have sinned, until he plead my cause; then she that is mine enemy shall see it and shame shall cover her, and she shall be trodden down as the mire of the streets. The Lord will fight on your behalf as long as you are doing what you must do.

**[Psalm 41:10-12](/bible/psalms/41#v10)**
> <sup>[10](/bible/psalms/41#v10)</sup> But thou, O Lord, be merciful unto me, and raise me up, that I may requite them.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/psalms/41#v11)</sup> By this I know that thou favourest me, because mine enemy doth not triumph over me.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/psalms/41#v12)</sup> And as for me, thou upholdest me in mine integrity, and settest me before thy face for ever.

- David asks the Lord to be merciful and raise him up that he may requite, or repay, them. He knows the Lord favors him because his enemy does not triumph over him. David went through much, including his son trying to kill him and take the kingdom, but the way you know the Lord showed favor is that Absalom was not able to take the kingdom from him.
- The Lord upholds David in his integrity and sets him before his face forever. The Lord kept him strong and moving in the right spirit.
  - **[Job 36:7-11](/bible/job/36#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/job/36#v7)</sup> He withdraweth not his eyes from the righteous: but with kings are they on the throne; yea, he doth establish them for ever, and they are exalted.
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/job/36#v8)</sup> And if they be bound in fetters, and be holden in cords of affliction;
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/job/36#v9)</sup> Then he sheweth them their work, and their transgressions that they have exceeded.
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/job/36#v10)</sup> He openeth also their ear to discipline, and commandeth that they return from iniquity.
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/job/36#v11)</sup> If they obey and serve him, they shall spend their days in prosperity, and their years in pleasures.

    The Lord withdraws not his eyes from the righteous; if they be bound in cords of affliction, then he shows them their transgressions, opens their ear to discipline, and commands that they return from iniquity. If they obey they spend their days in prosperity; if not, they perish by the sword. Righteousness is not never sinning; it is being able to get back up, and if the Lord wants you, he will get you back right.

**[Psalm 41:13](/bible/psalms/41#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/psalms/41#v13)</sup> Blessed be the Lord God of Israel from everlasting, and to everlasting. Amen, and Amen.

- Blessed be the Lord God of Israel from everlasting to everlasting. Amen and amen.

# Psalm 42: Panting After God in the Midst of Affliction

Read the chapter → [Psalms 42](/bible/psalms/42)

**[Psalm 42:1-3](/bible/psalms/42#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/42#v1)</sup> As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth my soul after thee, O God.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/42#v2)</sup> My soul thirsteth for God, for the living God: when shall I come and appear before God?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/42#v3)</sup> My tears have been my meat day and night, while they continually say unto me, Where is thy God?

- As the hart pants after the water brooks, so David's soul pants after God. A hart is a deer; the panting is drinking up water. His soul thirsts for the living God, and he asks when he shall come and appear before God.
- His tears have been his meat day and night, while his enemies continually say to him, "Where is thy God?" This is the same mockery as in Micah 7: because he is afflicted, they ask where his God is.

- "Why art thou cast down, O my soul, and why art thou disquieted in me? Hope thou in God, for I shall yet praise him." He preaches to his own downcast soul to hope in God. He remembers the Lord from the land of Jordan, the Hermonites, and the hill Mizar.

**[Psalm 42:7-9](/bible/psalms/42#v7)**

- Yet the Lord will command his lovingkindness in the daytime, and in the night his song shall be with David. He says to God his rock, "Why hast thou forgotten me? Why go I mourning because of the oppression of the enemy?"

    This was the issue with the Israelites coming against David and speaking evil of him: he turned away from sin, yet they reproached him for it, so he asked the Lord to requite them.

**[Psalm 42:11](/bible/psalms/42#v11) (continued)**
*11 Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God.*
- Punishment for sins drives you back to the Most High. This is [Hosea 5:15](/bible/hosea/5#v15); when affliction comes we go back to the Lord, and that is how the Lord gets us back. Even people in the world call on the Lord when things go wrong, though he does not always hear them.

    > <sup>[15](/bible/hosea/5#v15)</sup> I will go and return to my place, till they acknowledge their offence, and seek my face: in their affliction they will seek me early.

    The Most High will go and return to his place until they acknowledge their offence and seek his face; in their affliction they will seek him early.

The Lord is the one who allowed the cursing, and through the affliction received from man, David would be repaid good. Being cursed and afflicted, we receive a reward for overcoming it.
