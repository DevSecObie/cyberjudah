Read the chapter → [Psalms 59](/bible/psalms/59)

This session reads through [Psalms 59](/bible/psalms/59) through 62, four chapters a day for the nation. The teacher notes up front that the chapters are extremely short, so the purpose is mostly to read and give as much explanation as needed without doing too much. [Psalm 59](/bible/psalms/59) is set during the time when Saul sent men to watch David's house to kill him. The teaching holds this up as the mind we should have when we are going through affliction: with people watching us, nations watching us, the heathen raging, and our own people hating us, these are the prayers that need to come out of our spirit. Stay in the spirit.

**[Psalms 59:1-2](/bible/psalms/59#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/59#v1)</sup> Deliver me from mine enemies, O my God: defend me from them that rise up against me.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/59#v2)</sup> Deliver me from the workers of iniquity, and save me from bloody men.

- The superscription places this at the time Saul sent men who watched the house to kill David. Saul and his men were watching David, his ins and outs, to try to kill him, and this is what David was going through.
- David cries for the Most High to deliver him from his enemies and defend him from those that rise up against him, and to save him from the workers of iniquity and the bloody men.

**[Psalms 59:3-5](/bible/psalms/59#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/59#v3)</sup> For, lo, they lie in wait for my soul: the mighty are gathered against me; not for my transgression, nor for my sin, O Lord.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/59#v4)</sup> They run and prepare themselves without my fault: awake to help me, and behold.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/59#v5)</sup> Thou therefore, O Lord God of hosts, the God of Israel, awake to visit all the heathen: be not merciful to any wicked transgressors. Selah.

- The mighty are gathered against David, lying in wait for his soul, not for his transgression nor his sin. They run and prepare themselves without any fault of his.
- He calls on the Lord God of hosts, the God of Israel, to awake to visit all the heathen and to be not merciful to any wicked transgressors.

**[Psalms 59:6-8](/bible/psalms/59#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/59#v6)</sup> They return at evening: they make a noise like a dog, and go round about the city.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/59#v7)</sup> Behold, they belch out with their mouth: swords are in their lips: for who, say they, doth hear?
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/59#v8)</sup> But thou, O Lord, shalt laugh at them; thou shalt have all the heathen in derision.

- The enemies return at evening, make a noise like a dog, and go around about the city; they belch out with their mouth, and swords are in their lips, thinking no one hears.
- But the Most High laughs at them and holds all the heathen in derision.

**[Psalms 59:9-11](/bible/psalms/59#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/59#v9)</sup> Because of his strength will I wait upon thee: for God is my defence.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/psalms/59#v10)</sup> The God of my mercy shall prevent me: God shall let me see my desire upon mine enemies.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/psalms/59#v11)</sup> Slay them not, lest my people forget: scatter them by thy power; and bring them down, O Lord our shield.

- Because of the enemy's strength, David waits upon the Most High, who is his defense and the God of his mercy.
- He asks that they not be slain outright lest his people forget, but scattered by the power of the Most High and brought down.

**[Psalms 59:12-13](/bible/psalms/59#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/psalms/59#v12)</sup> For the sin of their mouth and the words of their lips let them even be taken in their pride: and for cursing and lying which they speak.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/psalms/59#v13)</sup> Consume them in wrath, consume them, that they may not be: and let them know that God ruleth in Jacob unto the ends of the earth. Selah.

- For the sin of their mouth and the words of their lips, let them be taken in their pride, in their cursing and lying.
- The heavy point: let them know that God ruleth in Jacob unto the ends of the earth. That is a heavy point.

**[Psalms 59:14-17](/bible/psalms/59#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/psalms/59#v14)</sup> And at evening let them return; and let them make a noise like a dog, and go round about the city.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/psalms/59#v15)</sup> Let them wander up and down for meat, and grudge if they be not satisfied.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/psalms/59#v16)</sup> But I will sing of thy power; yea, I will sing aloud of thy mercy in the morning: for thou hast been my defence and refuge in the day of my trouble.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/psalms/59#v17)</sup> Unto thee, O my strength, will I sing: for God is my defence, and the God of my mercy.

- Again the enemies return at evening making a noise like a dog, wandering up and down for meat, grudging if they are not satisfied.
- But David will sing of the power of the Most High and sing aloud of His mercy in the morning, for He has been his defense and refuge in the day of trouble.
- This is the mind of King David, and the type of mind we should have when going through affliction. With people watching us, nations watching us, the heathen raging, and our own people hating us, these are the prayers that need to come out of our spirit. Stay in the spirit.

# Psalm 60: Through God We Shall Do Valiantly

Read the chapter → [Psalms 60](/bible/psalms/60)

[Psalm 60](/bible/psalms/60) is a psalm of David written when he strove with Aram-naharaim and Aram-zobah, when Joab returned and smote twelve thousand of Edom in the valley of salt. The teacher points out that the brief synopsis at the top of the psalm in the King James gives you the understanding of why David is saying what he is saying, and during what time he is saying it; make sure you are reading those. The psalm moves from a nation cast off and scattered to the confidence that vain is the help of man, but through God we shall do valiantly.

**[Psalms 60:1-3](/bible/psalms/60#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/60#v1)</sup> O God, thou hast cast us off, thou hast scattered us, thou hast been displeased; O turn thyself to us again.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/60#v2)</sup> Thou hast made the earth to tremble; thou hast broken it: heal the breaches thereof; for it shaketh.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/60#v3)</sup> Thou hast shewed thy people hard things: thou hast made us to drink the wine of astonishment.

- David says the Most High has cast us off and scattered us, has been displeased, and asks Him to turn to us again.
- The Most High made the earth to tremble and broke it; David asks Him to heal the breaches, for it is shaken, and He has showed His people hard things and made them drink the wine of astonishment.

**[Psalms 60:4-6](/bible/psalms/60#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/60#v4)</sup> Thou hast given a banner to them that fear thee, that it may be displayed because of the truth. Selah.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/60#v5)</sup> That thy beloved may be delivered; save with thy right hand, and hear me.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/60#v6)</sup> God hath spoken in his holiness; I will rejoice, I will divide Shechem, and mete out the valley of Succoth.

- The Most High has given a banner to them that fear Him, that it may be displayed because of the truth, so that His beloved may be delivered by His right hand.
- God has spoken in His holiness that He will rejoice, divide Shechem, and mete out the valley of Succoth.

**[Psalms 60:7-9](/bible/psalms/60#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/60#v7)</sup> Gilead is mine, and Manasseh is mine; Ephraim also is the strength of mine head; Judah is my lawgiver;
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/60#v8)</sup> Moab is my washpot; over Edom will I cast out my shoe: Philistia, triumph thou because of me.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/60#v9)</sup> Who will bring me into the strong city? who will lead me into Edom?

- Gilead is His, Manasseh is His, Ephraim is the strength of His hand, and Judah is His lawgiver; Moab is His washpot, over Edom He casts out His shoe.
- He asks who will bring him into the strong city and who will lead him into Edom.

**[Psalms 60:10-12](/bible/psalms/60#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/psalms/60#v10)</sup> Wilt not thou, O God, which hadst cast us off? and thou, O God, which didst not go out with our armies?
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/psalms/60#v11)</sup> Give us help from trouble: for vain is the help of man.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/psalms/60#v12)</sup> Through God we shall do valiantly: for he it is that shall tread down our enemies.

- David asks whether it is not the Most High, who had cast them off and did not go out with their armies, who will give help from trouble, for vain is the help of man.
- Through God we shall do valiantly, for it is He that shall tread down our enemies.

# Psalm 61: Lead Me to the Rock That Is Higher Than I

Read the chapter → [Psalms 61](/bible/psalms/61)

[Psalm 61](/bible/psalms/61) is a psalm of David for the chief musician. It is the cry of a man whose heart is overwhelmed, asking to be led to the rock that is higher than himself, to abide in the tabernacle of the Most High and trust in the covert of His wings. The teaching draws out what it means that David was a man after the Most High's own heart: he constantly prayed, constantly sought out the doings of the Most High, and kept repenting from the wrong that he did.

**[Psalms 61:1-4](/bible/psalms/61#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/61#v1)</sup> Hear my cry, O God; attend unto my prayer.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/61#v2)</sup> From the end of the earth will I cry unto thee, when my heart is overwhelmed: lead me to the rock that is higher than I.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/61#v3)</sup> For thou hast been a shelter for me, and a strong tower from the enemy.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/61#v4)</sup> I will abide in thy tabernacle for ever: I will trust in the covert of thy wings. Selah.

- David asks the Most High to hear his cry and attend to his prayer; from the end of the earth he will cry when his heart is overwhelmed, asking to be led to the rock that is higher than himself.
- The Most High has been a shelter for him and a strong tower from the enemy; he will abide in the tabernacle forever and trust in the covert of His wings.

**[Psalms 61:5-8](/bible/psalms/61#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/61#v5)</sup> For thou, O God, hast heard my vows: thou hast given me the heritage of those that fear thy name.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/61#v6)</sup> Thou wilt prolong the king’s life: and his years as many generations.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/61#v7)</sup> He shall abide before God for ever: O prepare mercy and truth, which may preserve him.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/61#v8)</sup> So will I sing praise unto thy name for ever, that I may daily perform my vows.

- The Most High has heard David's vows and given him the heritage of those that fear His name, and will prolong the king's life as many generations.
- The king shall abide before God forever; so David will sing praise unto His name forever and daily perform his vows.
- This is what it means that David was a man after the Most High's own heart. He constantly prayed and constantly sought out the doings of the Most High. Was David free from any guilt? No. But he kept getting himself together, kept praying, and kept repenting from the wrong that he did. That is one thing that made him perfect.

# Psalm 62: Let Your Expectation Be of God

Read the chapter → [Psalms 62](/bible/psalms/62)

[Psalm 62](/bible/psalms/62) is the last chapter of the session, a psalm of David to Jeduthun. The soul waits only upon God, who alone is the rock, the salvation, and the defense. The teacher lifts up verse 5 as the heavy point: a lot of us expect things from men and expect people not to let us down, but let your expectations be of God as David's expectations were of God. You cannot change anybody, but God can. Expect great things from the Most High and you do great things yourself.

**[Psalms 62:1-4](/bible/psalms/62#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/62#v1)</sup> Truly my soul waiteth upon God: from him cometh my salvation.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/62#v2)</sup> He only is my rock and my salvation; he is my defence; I shall not be greatly moved.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/62#v3)</sup> How long will ye imagine mischief against a man? ye shall be slain all of you: as a bowing wall shall ye be, and as a tottering fence.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/62#v4)</sup> They only consult to cast him down from his excellency: they delight in lies: they bless with their mouth, but they curse inwardly. Selah.

- Truly David's soul waits upon God, from whom comes his salvation; He only is his rock and salvation and defense, so he shall not be greatly moved.
- He rebukes those who imagine mischief against a man, who shall be slain like a bowing wall and a tottering fence; they consult to cast him down from his excellency, delight in lies, and bless with their mouth but curse inwardly.

**[Psalms 62:5-8](/bible/psalms/62#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/62#v5)</sup> My soul, wait thou only upon God; for my expectation is from him.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/62#v6)</sup> He only is my rock and my salvation: he is my defence; I shall not be moved.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/62#v7)</sup> In God is my salvation and my glory: the rock of my strength, and my refuge, is in God.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/62#v8)</sup> Trust in him at all times; ye people, pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us. Selah.

- David tells his own soul to wait only upon God, for his expectation is from Him; He only is his rock, salvation, defense, glory, and refuge.
- A lot of us expect things from men and expect people not to let us down. "I expected more of you," "I expected you to be this way," and that is your problem. Let your expectations be of God, as David's expectations were of God. You cannot change anybody, but God can.
- Trust in Him at all times, pour out your heart before Him, for God is a refuge for us.

**[Psalms 62:9-12](/bible/psalms/62#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/62#v9)</sup> Surely men of low degree are vanity, and men of high degree are a lie: to be laid in the balance, they are altogether lighter than vanity.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/psalms/62#v10)</sup> Trust not in oppression, and become not vain in robbery: if riches increase, set not your heart upon them.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/psalms/62#v11)</sup> God hath spoken once; twice have I heard this; that power belongeth unto God.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/psalms/62#v12)</sup> Also unto thee, O Lord, belongeth mercy: for thou renderest to every man according to his work.

- Men of low degree are vanity and men of high degree are a lie; laid in the balance they are altogether lighter than vanity. Trust not in oppression, become not vain in robbery, and if riches increase, set not your heart upon them.
- God has spoken once, twice have we heard this: that power belongs to God, and to Him belongs mercy, for He renders to every man according to his work.
- Expect great things from the Most High and you do great things yourself.
