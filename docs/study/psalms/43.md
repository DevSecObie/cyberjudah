Read the chapter → [Psalms 43](/bible/psalms/43)

This session moves through [Psalms 43](/bible/psalms/43) through 46, read for the nation four chapters a day. [Psalm 43](/bible/psalms/43) is the plea of a man cast down, asking the Most High to judge his cause against an ungodly nation and to send out His light and truth to lead him back to the holy hill. The light is defined by the scriptures themselves: the commandment is a lamp and the law is light. The teaching threads through the countenance of the soul, the strength that comes from keeping the commandments, and the health that a merry heart gives even in oppression.

**[Psalms 43:1-2](/bible/psalms/43#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/43#v1)</sup> Judge me, O God, and plead my cause against an ungodly nation: O deliver me from the deceitful and unjust man.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/43#v2)</sup> For thou art the God of my strength: why dost thou cast me off? why go I mourning because of the oppression of the enemy?

- David asks the Most High to judge him and plead his cause against an ungodly nation, and to deliver him from the deceitful and unjust man.
- He calls the Most High the God of his strength, then asks why he is cast off and why he goes mourning because of the oppression of the enemy.

**[Psalms 43:3](/bible/psalms/43#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/43#v3)</sup> O send out thy light and thy truth: let them lead me; let them bring me unto thy holy hill, and to thy tabernacles.

- The plea is for the Most High to send out His light and His truth so they can lead him to the holy hill and the tabernacles.
- The light is not a mystery: it is the commandment and the law. That is what leads a man to the altar of the Most High.
  - **[Proverbs 6:23](/bible/proverbs/6#v23)**
    > <sup>[23](/bible/proverbs/6#v23)</sup> For the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light; and reproofs of instruction are the way of life:

    The commandment is a lamp and the law is light, and reproofs of instruction are the way of life.

**[Psalms 43:4-5](/bible/psalms/43#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/43#v4)</sup> Then will I go unto the altar of God, unto God my exceeding joy: yea, upon the harp will I praise thee, O God my God.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/43#v5)</sup> Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me? hope in God: for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God.

- The altar of the Most High is called his exceeding joy, and he purposes to praise Him upon the harp.
- He questions his own soul, asking why it is cast down and disquieted, and answers himself: hope in God, for He is the health of my countenance.
- The health of the countenance ties to a merry heart. You can always tell when someone carries something heavy; a broken spirit shows on the face. If you truly pay attention to somebody, you can tell when things are not going well with them.
  - **[Psalms 4:5-8](/bible/psalms/4#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/4#v5)</sup> Offer the sacrifices of righteousness, and put your trust in the Lord.
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/4#v6)</sup> There be many that say, Who will shew us any good? Lord, lift thou up the light of thy countenance upon us.
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/4#v7)</sup> Thou hast put gladness in my heart, more than in the time that their corn and their wine increased.
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/4#v8)</sup> I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, Lord, only makest me dwell in safety.

    Offer the sacrifices of righteousness and trust in the Most High; the light of His countenance puts gladness in the heart more than corn and wine increase.
  - **[Proverbs 15:13](/bible/proverbs/15#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/proverbs/15#v13)</sup> A merry heart maketh a cheerful countenance: but by sorrow of the heart the spirit is broken.

    A merry heart makes a cheerful countenance, but by sorrow of the heart the spirit is broken.
