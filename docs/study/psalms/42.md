**[Psalm 42:1-3](/bible/psalms/42#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/42#v1)</sup> As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth my soul after thee, O God.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/42#v2)</sup> My soul thirsteth for God, for the living God: when shall I come and appear before God?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/42#v3)</sup> My tears have been my meat day and night, while they continually say unto me, Where is thy God?

- As the hart pants after the water brooks, so David's soul pants after God. A hart is a deer; the panting is drinking up water. His soul thirsts for the living God, and he asks when he shall come and appear before God.
- His tears have been his meat day and night, while his enemies continually say to him, "Where is thy God?" This is the same mockery as in Micah 7: because he is afflicted, they ask where his God is.

**[Psalm 42:4-6](/bible/psalms/42#v4)**
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/42#v4)</sup> When I remember these things, I pour out my soul in me: for I had gone with the multitude, I went with them to the house of God, with the voice of joy and praise, with a multitude that kept holyday.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/42#v5)</sup> Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted in me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him for the help of his countenance.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/42#v6)</sup> O my God, my soul is cast down within me: therefore will I remember thee from the land of Jordan, and of the Hermonites, from the hill Mizar.

- When David remembers these things he pours out his soul, remembering how he went with the multitude to the house of God with the voice of joy and praise, with a multitude that kept holy day.
- "Why art thou cast down, O my soul, and why art thou disquieted in me? Hope thou in God, for I shall yet praise him." He preaches to his own downcast soul to hope in God. He remembers the Lord from the land of Jordan, the Hermonites, and the hill Mizar.

**[Psalm 42:7-9](/bible/psalms/42#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/42#v7)</sup> Deep calleth unto deep at the noise of thy waterspouts: all thy waves and thy billows are gone over me.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/42#v8)</sup> Yet the Lord will command his lovingkindness in the daytime, and in the night his song shall be with me, and my prayer unto the God of my life.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/42#v9)</sup> I will say unto God my rock, Why hast thou forgotten me? why go I mourning because of the oppression of the enemy?

- "Deep calleth unto deep at the noise of thy waterspouts; all thy waves and thy billows are gone over me." The waves are the affliction; all the Lord's afflictions have gone over him.
- Yet the Lord will command his lovingkindness in the daytime, and in the night his song shall be with David. He says to God his rock, "Why hast thou forgotten me? Why go I mourning because of the oppression of the enemy?"

**[Psalm 42:10-11](/bible/psalms/42#v10)**
> <sup>[10](/bible/psalms/42#v10)</sup> As with a sword in my bones, mine enemies reproach me; while they say daily unto me, Where is thy God?
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/psalms/42#v11)</sup> Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God.

- As with a sword in his bones, his enemies reproach him while they say daily, "Where is thy God?" Again he preaches to his soul: "Why art thou cast down? Hope thou in God, for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God."
  - **[Job 30:16-23](/bible/job/30#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/job/30#v16)</sup> And now my soul is poured out upon me; the days of affliction have taken hold upon me.
    >
    > <sup>[17](/bible/job/30#v17)</sup> My bones are pierced in me in the night season: and my sinews take no rest.
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/job/30#v18)</sup> By the great force of my disease is my garment changed: it bindeth me about as the collar of my coat.
    >
    > <sup>[19](/bible/job/30#v19)</sup> He hath cast me into the mire, and I am become like dust and ashes.
    >
    > <sup>[20](/bible/job/30#v20)</sup> I cry unto thee, and thou dost not hear me: I stand up, and thou regardest me not.
    >
    > <sup>[21](/bible/job/30#v21)</sup> Thou art become cruel to me: with thy strong hand thou opposest thyself against me.
    >
    > <sup>[22](/bible/job/30#v22)</sup> Thou liftest me up to the wind; thou causest me to ride upon it, and dissolvest my substance.
    >
    > <sup>[23](/bible/job/30#v23)</sup> For I know that thou wilt bring me to death, and to the house appointed for all living.

    Job's soul is poured out; the days of affliction take hold of him, his bones are pierced in the night, and God has become cruel to him and opposes himself against him, bringing him to the grave, the house appointed for all living. This is the same situation David was in: the Lord was opposed to him for his sin and did not listen at some point, but after David turned around, the Lord began to listen.
- This is the situation a lot of us end up in if we lose sight of what we are supposed to be doing, which is keeping the commandments.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 8:5](/bible/sirach/8#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/sirach/8#v5)</sup> Reproach not a man that turneth from sin, but remember that we are all worthy of punishment.

    This was the issue with the Israelites coming against David and speaking evil of him: he turned away from sin, yet they reproached him for it, so he asked the Lord to requite them.

**[Psalm 42:11](/bible/psalms/42#v11) (continued)**
*11 Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God.*
- Punishment for sins drives you back to the Most High. This is [Hosea 5:15](/bible/hosea/5#v15); when affliction comes we go back to the Lord, and that is how the Lord gets us back. Even people in the world call on the Lord when things go wrong, though he does not always hear them.
  - **[Hosea 5:15](/bible/hosea/5#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/hosea/5#v15)</sup> I will go and return to my place, till they acknowledge their offence, and seek my face: in their affliction they will seek me early.

    The Most High will go and return to his place until they acknowledge their offence and seek his face; in their affliction they will seek him early.
  - **[2 Samuel 16:10-12](/bible/2-samuel/16#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/2-samuel/16#v10)</sup> And the king said, What have I to do with you, ye sons of Zeruiah? so let him curse, because the Lord hath said unto him, Curse David. Who shall then say, Wherefore hast thou done so?
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/2-samuel/16#v11)</sup> And David said to Abishai, and to all his servants, Behold, my son, which came forth of my bowels, seeketh my life: how much more now may this Benjamite do it? let him alone, and let him curse; for the Lord hath bidden him.
    >
    > <sup>[12](/bible/2-samuel/16#v12)</sup> It may be that the Lord will look on mine affliction, and that the Lord will requite me good for his cursing this day.

    When Absalom sought the kingdom, David let Shimei curse him, saying the Lord had bidden him to curse, and "it may be that the Lord will look on mine affliction, and that the Lord will requite me good for his cursing this day." The Lord is the one who allowed the cursing, and through the affliction received from man, David would be repaid good. Being cursed and afflicted, we receive a reward for overcoming it.
