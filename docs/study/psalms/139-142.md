This session covers the four chapters read for the day, [Psalms 139](/bible/psalms/139) through 142. Across these psalms David describes a God who sees and knows all things, who delivers His people from the evil man, and who is the refuge of those who cry out to Him. The reading is done with the congregation as part of reading four chapters a day, and the aim is not just to read but to understand exactly what the scripture is talking about and how the examples of our forefathers and foremothers can be used for us.

**[Psalms 139:1-11](/bible/psalms/139#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/139#v1)</sup> O Lord, thou hast searched me, and known me.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/139#v2)</sup> Thou knowest my downsitting and mine uprising, thou understandest my thought afar off.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/139#v3)</sup> Thou compassest my path and my lying down, and art acquainted with all my ways.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/139#v4)</sup> For there is not a word in my tongue, but, lo, O Lord, thou knowest it altogether.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/139#v5)</sup> Thou hast beset me behind and before, and laid thine hand upon me.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/139#v6)</sup> Such knowledge is too wonderful for me; it is high, I cannot attain unto it.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/139#v7)</sup> Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy presence?
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/139#v8)</sup> If I ascend up into heaven, thou art there: if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/139#v9)</sup> If I take the wings of the morning, and dwell in the uttermost parts of the sea;
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/psalms/139#v10)</sup> Even there shall thy hand lead me, and thy right hand shall hold me.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/psalms/139#v11)</sup> If I say, Surely the darkness shall cover me; even the night shall be light about me.

- David is describing the Lord and His ability to see and know all things. From the very beginning he says the Lord knows his downsitting and his uprising.
- No matter what you do, the Lord sees it. You cannot hide from the Lord in the dark; it does not work.
- Whether he ascends up into heaven or makes his bed in hell, the Lord is there. There is nowhere a man can flee from His presence.

**[Psalms 139:12](/bible/psalms/139#v12)**
> <sup>[12](/bible/psalms/139#v12)</sup> Yea, the darkness hideth not from thee; but the night shineth as the day: the darkness and the light are both alike to thee.

- The darkness and the night are the same thing to the Lord. When the sun is out you see everything clearly, and the Lord says He sees the same thing even in the dark.

**[Psalms 139:13-18](/bible/psalms/139#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/psalms/139#v13)</sup> For thou hast possessed my reins: thou hast covered me in my mother’s womb.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/psalms/139#v14)</sup> I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my soul knoweth right well.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/psalms/139#v15)</sup> My substance was not hid from thee, when I was made in secret, and curiously wrought in the lowest parts of the earth.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/psalms/139#v16)</sup> Thine eyes did see my substance, yet being unperfect; and in thy book all my members were written, which in continuance were fashioned, when as yet there was none of them.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/psalms/139#v17)</sup> How precious also are thy thoughts unto me, O God! how great is the sum of them!
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/psalms/139#v18)</sup> If I should count them, they are more in number than the sand: when I awake, I am still with thee.

- His reins are his life, his soul. When he says the Lord has possessed his reins, he means his life, and that the Lord covered him in his mother's womb.
- The Lord's knowledge of David reaches back to when his substance was made in secret, before any of his members existed. The Lord is aware of all things at all times.

**[Psalms 139:19-24](/bible/psalms/139#v19)**
> <sup>[19](/bible/psalms/139#v19)</sup> Surely thou wilt slay the wicked, O God: depart from me therefore, ye bloody men.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/psalms/139#v20)</sup> For they speak against thee wickedly, and thine enemies take thy name in vain.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/psalms/139#v21)</sup> Do not I hate them, O Lord, that hate thee? and am not I grieved with those that rise up against thee?
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/psalms/139#v22)</sup> I hate them with perfect hatred: I count them mine enemies.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/psalms/139#v23)</sup> Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts:
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/psalms/139#v24)</sup> And see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.

- A key point David speaks about all the time is the Lord delivering him from his enemies and those that seek to hurt him.
- Modern Christianity forgives and loves all things, but David says he hates them with a perfect hatred. He hates those that hate God, and that is the way the Lord wants His people to roll.
- This does not mean you go out doing something crazy, but David does not care for those that do not care for the Lord. He counts them his enemies.
- At the end he asks the Lord to search him, know his heart, try him, and see if there be any wicked way in him and lead him in the way everlasting.
  - **[Matthew 18:10](/bible/matthew/18#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/matthew/18#v10)</sup> Take heed that ye despise not one of these little ones; for I say unto you, That in heaven their angels do always behold the face of my Father which is in heaven.

    Every one of us has an angel assigned that beholds the Father's face; one way the Lord sees and knows all things is through the angels who write everything down, though the Lord Himself can see all things at any time He chooses.

# Psalm 140: Deliver Me From the Evil Man

Read the chapter → [Psalms 140](/bible/psalms/140)

**[Psalms 140:1-3](/bible/psalms/140#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/140#v1)</sup> Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man: preserve me from the violent man;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/140#v2)</sup> Which imagine mischiefs in their heart; continually are they gathered together for war.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/140#v3)</sup> They have sharpened their tongues like a serpent; adders’ poison is under their lips. Selah.

- The topic here is the evil man. David is not talking about everyone who commits wickedness but a particular person.
- The key points show us who he is talking about: one who imagines mischief in his heart, who is continually gathered together for war, whose tongue is sharpened like a serpent with adder's poison under his lips.
  - **[Micah 2:1](/bible/micah/2#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/micah/2#v1)</sup> Woe to them that devise iniquity, and work evil upon their beds! when the morning is light, they practise it, because it is in the power of their hand.

    The evil man is the one who devises iniquity and works evil upon his bed; this points to Esau.
  - **[Psalms 55:21](/bible/psalms/55#v21)**
    > <sup>[21](/bible/psalms/55#v21)</sup> The words of his mouth were smoother than butter, but war was in his heart: his words were softer than oil, yet were they drawn swords.

    His words were smoother than butter but war was in his heart; his words softer than oil yet drawn swords, confirming this is the evil man, Esau, the nation of Edom.

**[Psalms 140:4-8](/bible/psalms/140#v4)**
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/140#v4)</sup> Keep me, O Lord, from the hands of the wicked; preserve me from the violent man; who have purposed to overthrow my goings.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/140#v5)</sup> The proud have hid a snare for me, and cords; they have spread a net by the wayside; they have set gins for me. Selah.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/140#v6)</sup> I said unto the Lord, Thou art my God: hear the voice of my supplications, O Lord.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/140#v7)</sup> O God the Lord, the strength of my salvation, thou hast covered my head in the day of battle.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/140#v8)</sup> Grant not, O Lord, the desires of the wicked: further not his wicked device; lest they exalt themselves. Selah.

- David asks the Lord to keep him from the hands of the wicked and the violent man who has purposed to overthrow his goings, and who has hid snares, cords, and nets by the wayside.
- He confesses the Lord as his God, the strength of his salvation, who has covered his head in the day of battle, and asks the Lord not to further the wicked device of the enemy lest they exalt themselves.

**[Psalms 140:9-13](/bible/psalms/140#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/140#v9)</sup> As for the head of those that compass me about, let the mischief of their own lips cover them.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/psalms/140#v10)</sup> Let burning coals fall upon them: let them be cast into the fire; into deep pits, that they rise not up again.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/psalms/140#v11)</sup> Let not an evil speaker be established in the earth: evil shall hunt the violent man to overthrow him.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/psalms/140#v12)</sup> I know that the Lord will maintain the cause of the afflicted, and the right of the poor.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/psalms/140#v13)</sup> Surely the righteous shall give thanks unto thy name: the upright shall dwell in thy presence.

- David asks that the mischief of the enemies' own lips cover them, that burning coals fall on them, and that they be cast into deep pits to rise no more.
- He knows the Lord will maintain the cause of the afflicted and the right of the poor, and that the righteous and upright shall dwell in His presence.

# Psalm 141: A Prayer to Be Kept From Evil

Read the chapter → [Psalms 141](/bible/psalms/141)

**[Psalms 141:1-2](/bible/psalms/141#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/141#v1)</sup> Lord, I cry unto thee: make haste unto me; give ear unto my voice, when I cry unto thee.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/141#v2)</sup> Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense; and the lifting up of my hands as the evening sacrifice.

- David cries to the Lord to make haste to him and to hear his voice, asking that his prayer be set before the Lord as incense and the lifting up of his hands as the evening sacrifice.

**[Psalms 141:3-4](/bible/psalms/141#v3)**
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/141#v3)</sup> Set a watch, O Lord, before my mouth; keep the door of my lips.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/141#v4)</sup> Incline not my heart to any evil thing, to practise wicked works with men that work iniquity: and let me not eat of their dainties.

- Set a watch before my mouth, keep the door of my lips: this is asking the Lord to help him control his mouth, the same as James telling us to bridle the tongue.
- Incline not my heart to any evil thing means do not let my mind and my spirit go after things that are evil; help me walk in the spirit.
- He asks the Lord not to let his heart be inclined to practice wicked works with men that work iniquity, and not to let him eat of their dainties, meaning not to partake or follow the wicked to do evil.
  - **[Exodus 23:2](/bible/exodus/23#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/exodus/23#v2)</sup> Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil; neither shalt thou speak in a cause to decline after many to wrest judgment:

    Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil; there is a law set up for how we govern ourselves.

**[Psalms 141:5](/bible/psalms/141#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/141#v5)</sup> Let the righteous smite me; it shall be a kindness: and let him reprove me; it shall be an excellent oil, which shall not break my head: for yet my prayer also shall be in their calamities.

- Now David says the opposite. Instead of being inclined to work evil, he asks that the righteous correct him, and that correction is counted as kindness and love.
- Correction may be uncomely and it will not be very comely, but it is for good; it shall not break his head, meaning it is not going to hurt him. When you get corrected, that is kindness.
- A danger he names is envying the wicked, because a lot of times we envy those in the world when nothing seems to be happening to them.

**[Psalms 141:6-10](/bible/psalms/141#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/141#v6)</sup> When their judges are overthrown in stony places, they shall hear my words; for they are sweet.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/141#v7)</sup> Our bones are scattered at the grave’s mouth, as when one cutteth and cleaveth wood upon the earth.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/141#v8)</sup> But mine eyes are unto thee, O God the Lord: in thee is my trust; leave not my soul destitute.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/141#v9)</sup> Keep me from the snares which they have laid for me, and the gins of the workers of iniquity.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/psalms/141#v10)</sup> Let the wicked fall into their own nets, whilst that I withal escape.

- David trusts the Lord and asks that his soul not be left destitute, that he be kept from the snares and gins the workers of iniquity have laid, and that the wicked fall into their own nets while he escapes.
- This is the prayer he gives to the Lord: to hear his voice, to keep him from his enemies, and to keep him from wickedness himself. These are the things we keep an eye on in our prayers, thoughts, and studies as we walk through this truth.

# Psalm 142: The Lord My Refuge and Portion

Read the chapter → [Psalms 142](/bible/psalms/142)

**[Psalms 142:1-2](/bible/psalms/142#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/142#v1)</sup> I cried unto the Lord with my voice; with my voice unto the Lord did I make my supplication.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/142#v2)</sup> I poured out my complaint before him; I shewed before him my trouble.

- A key point is that David pours out his complaint before the Lord and shows before Him his trouble. A lot of times Israel goes to the Lord when troubles are going on, when things are getting rough and going bad.

**[Psalms 142:3-4](/bible/psalms/142#v3)**
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/142#v3)</sup> When my spirit was overwhelmed within me, then thou knewest my path. In the way wherein I walked have they privily laid a snare for me.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/142#v4)</sup> I looked on my right hand, and beheld, but there was no man that would know me: refuge failed me; no man cared for my soul.

- When his spirit was overwhelmed within him: everybody has been to a point where things have been too much for them, too much to bear. Even so, David understands the Lord is his refuge, so you can have a hard time but you must keep your mind in the scriptures.
- Verse 4 shows he could not handle it himself; he looked on his right hand and there was no man that would know him, no man cared for his soul.

**[Psalms 142:5-7](/bible/psalms/142#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/142#v5)</sup> I cried unto thee, O Lord: I said, Thou art my refuge and my portion in the land of the living.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/142#v6)</sup> Attend unto my cry; for I am brought very low: deliver me from my persecutors; for they are stronger than I.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/142#v7)</sup> Bring my soul out of prison, that I may praise thy name: the righteous shall compass me about; for thou shalt deal bountifully with me.

- David calls the Lord his refuge and his portion in the land of the living. He is brought very low and asks to be delivered from persecutors that are stronger than he is.
- David has a level of humility to say his persecutors are stronger than him. To have enemies stronger than you, you have to be willing to let the powers we have take that place instead of trying to handle it yourself.
- You can ask the Lord for something and then do the opposite; when you pray you should put in the effort to make it happen. If you want a house, a wife, or a husband, you have to put in the work.
- When we read our four chapters we should read to understand, seeing how the examples of our forefathers and foremothers can be used for us.
  - **[James 4:2](/bible/james/4#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/james/4#v2)</sup> Ye lust, and have not: ye kill, and desire to have, and cannot obtain: ye fight and war, yet ye have not, because ye ask not.

    A lot of times we desire things but do not receive them because we do not ask; ye have not because ye ask not.
  - **[James 4:3](/bible/james/4#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/james/4#v3)</sup> Ye ask, and receive not, because ye ask amiss, that ye may consume it upon your lusts.

    When you do ask and receive not, it is because you ask amiss, to consume it upon your own lust.
  - **[James 1:5](/bible/james/1#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/james/1#v5)</sup> If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.

    If any lack wisdom, let him ask of God who gives to all men liberally; but ask in faith, not wavering, for the Lord gives what He thinks you need, not simply whatever you asked for.
  - **[John 9:31](/bible/john/9#v31)**
    > <sup>[31](/bible/john/9#v31)</sup> Now we know that God heareth not sinners: but if any man be a worshipper of God, and doeth his will, him he heareth.

    God heareth not sinners; a man after the Lord's own heart is a man that keeps the commandments, and the Lord deals with those that are Israel and keep His commandments.
