This session moves through [Psalms 43](/bible/psalms/43) through 46, read for the nation four chapters a day. [Psalm 43](/bible/psalms/43) is the plea of a man cast down, asking the Most High to judge his cause against an ungodly nation and to send out His light and truth to lead him back to the holy hill. The light is defined by the scriptures themselves: the commandment is a lamp and the law is light. The teaching threads through the countenance of the soul, the strength that comes from keeping the commandments, and the health that a merry heart gives even in oppression.

**[Psalms 43:1-2](/bible/psalms/43#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/43#v1)</sup> Judge me, O God, and plead my cause against an ungodly nation: O deliver me from the deceitful and unjust man.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/43#v2)</sup> For thou art the God of my strength: why dost thou cast me off? why go I mourning because of the oppression of the enemy?

- David asks the Most High to judge him and plead his cause against an ungodly nation, and to deliver him from the deceitful and unjust man.
- He calls the Most High the God of his strength, then asks why he is cast off and why he goes mourning because of the oppression of the enemy.

**[Psalms 43:3](/bible/psalms/43#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/43#v3)</sup> O send out thy light and thy truth: let them lead me; let them bring me unto thy holy hill, and to thy tabernacles.

- The plea is for the Most High to send out His light and His truth so they can lead him to the holy hill and the tabernacles.
- The light is not a mystery: it is the commandment and the law. That is what leads a man to the altar of the Most High.
  - **[Proverbs 6:23](/bible/proverbs/6#v23)**
    > <sup>[23](/bible/proverbs/6#v23)</sup> For the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light; and reproofs of instruction are the way of life:

    The commandment is a lamp and the law is light, and reproofs of instruction are the way of life.

**[Psalms 43:4-5](/bible/psalms/43#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/43#v4)</sup> Then will I go unto the altar of God, unto God my exceeding joy: yea, upon the harp will I praise thee, O God my God.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/43#v5)</sup> Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me? hope in God: for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God.

- The altar of the Most High is called his exceeding joy, and he purposes to praise Him upon the harp.
- He questions his own soul, asking why it is cast down and disquieted, and answers himself: hope in God, for He is the health of my countenance.
- The health of the countenance ties to a merry heart. You can always tell when someone carries something heavy; a broken spirit shows on the face. If you truly pay attention to somebody, you can tell when things are not going well with them.
  - **[Psalms 4:5-8](/bible/psalms/4#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/4#v5)</sup> Offer the sacrifices of righteousness, and put your trust in the Lord.
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/4#v6)</sup> There be many that say, Who will shew us any good? Lord, lift thou up the light of thy countenance upon us.
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/4#v7)</sup> Thou hast put gladness in my heart, more than in the time that their corn and their wine increased.
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/4#v8)</sup> I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, Lord, only makest me dwell in safety.

    Offer the sacrifices of righteousness and trust in the Most High; the light of His countenance puts gladness in the heart more than corn and wine increase.
  - **[Proverbs 15:13](/bible/proverbs/15#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/proverbs/15#v13)</sup> A merry heart maketh a cheerful countenance: but by sorrow of the heart the spirit is broken.

    A merry heart makes a cheerful countenance, but by sorrow of the heart the spirit is broken.

# Psalm 44: We Got Not the Land by Our Own Sword

Read the chapter → [Psalms 44](/bible/psalms/44)

[Psalm 44](/bible/psalms/44) recounts how the fathers took the land, not by their own strength but by the right hand of the Most High, and then turns to lament the present captivity. The teaching drives home that the scattering, the shame, the byword status, and the spoiling all trace back to breaking the commandments. This is the difference between coming into the truth and reading and studying, versus staying in Christianity not knowing who your enemy really is.

**[Psalms 44:1-3](/bible/psalms/44#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/44#v1)</sup> We have heard with our ears, O God, our fathers have told us, what work thou didst in their days, in the times of old.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/44#v2)</sup> How thou didst drive out the heathen with thy hand, and plantedst them; how thou didst afflict the people, and cast them out.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/44#v3)</sup> For they got not the land in possession by their own sword, neither did their own arm save them: but thy right hand, and thine arm, and the light of thy countenance, because thou hadst a favour unto them.

- The fathers told what work the Most High did in the times of old, driving out the heathen and planting Israel in the land.
- Israel did not get the land by their own sword or their own arm, but by the right hand and the light of the countenance of the Most High, because He had favor toward them.
- When brothers ask "were you there?" or "how you know what color he was?" the answer is that the fathers heard and witnessed the Red Sea and the wars, and they told the following generations. We are supposed to search the scriptures.

# Psalm 45: Thy Throne, O God, Is Forever

Read the chapter → [Psalms 45](/bible/psalms/45)

[Psalm 45](/bible/psalms/45) is a king's psalm, fairer than the children of men, girding his sword and riding prosperously because of truth and meekness and righteousness. The teaching applies the throne to Christ, the everlasting scepter of righteousness held by the ruler from Judah, and the daughter who must forget her own people and father's house to please her lord.

>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/45#v2)</sup> Thou art fairer than the children of men: grace is poured into thy lips: therefore God hath blessed thee for ever.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/45#v3)</sup> Gird thy sword upon thy thigh, O most mighty, with thy glory and thy majesty.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/45#v4)</sup> And in thy majesty ride prosperously because of truth and meekness and righteousness; and thy right hand shall teach thee terrible things.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/45#v5)</sup> Thine arrows are sharp in the heart of the king’s enemies; whereby the people fall under thee.

- The heart indites a good matter concerning the king, who is fairer than the children of men with grace poured into his lips.
- He girds his sword upon his thigh and rides prosperously because of truth, meekness, and righteousness, and his arrows are sharp in the heart of the king's enemies.

# Psalm 46: God Is Our Refuge and Maketh Wars to Cease

Read the chapter → [Psalms 46](/bible/psalms/46)

[Psalm 46](/bible/psalms/46) declares the Most High a refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble, unshaken though the earth be removed. The teaching centers on the promise that He makes wars to cease to the ends of the earth, tying it to Christ's prophecy of wars and rumors of wars as the beginning of sorrows, and to the last-days beating of swords into plowshares.

**[Psalms 46:1-3](/bible/psalms/46#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/46#v1)</sup> God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/46#v2)</sup> Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea;
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/46#v3)</sup> Though the waters thereof roar and be troubled, though the mountains shake with the swelling thereof. Selah.

- The Most High is a refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble, so we will not fear though the earth be removed and the mountains carried into the sea.
- Though the waters roar and be troubled and the mountains shake, the confidence stands.

**[Psalms 46:4-6](/bible/psalms/46#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/psalms/46#v4)</sup> There is a river, the streams whereof shall make glad the city of God, the holy place of the tabernacles of the most High.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/psalms/46#v5)</sup> God is in the midst of her; she shall not be moved: God shall help her, and that right early.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/psalms/46#v6)</sup> The heathen raged, the kingdoms were moved: he uttered his voice, the earth melted.

- There is a river whose streams make glad the city of God, the tabernacles of the Most High, and God is in the midst of her; He helps her right early.
- The heathen raged and the kingdoms were moved; He uttered His voice and the earth melted.

>
> <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/46#v9)</sup> He maketh wars to cease unto the end of the earth; he breaketh the bow, and cutteth the spear in sunder; he burneth the chariot in the fire.

- He makes wars to cease unto the end of the earth, breaks the bow, cuts the spear, and burns the chariot in the fire.
