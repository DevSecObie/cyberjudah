## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 1 Peter 3 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-12, 14-16, 21, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[1 Peter 3:1](/bible/1-peter/3#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-peter/3#v1)</sup> Likewise, ye wives, be in subjection to your own husbands; that, if any obey not the word, they also may without the word be won by the conversation of the wives;

- Family members who are still alive and have rejected the truth: don’t count them out yet. Remember the apostle Paul did not accept the gospel when he first heard it. They are counted out when they die; while they are still alive there is still hope. Women came into the truth whose husbands did not believe, and Peter’s answer goes for your parents too. “Likewise, ye wives, be in subjection to your own husbands; that, if any obey not the word, they also may without the word be won by the conversation of the wives”: without the word, meaning they don’t know the word.
- “Likewise, ye wives, be in subjection to your own husbands; that, if any obey not the word, they also may without the word be won by the conversation of the wives”. This is for a wife married to a man who is pleased to dwell with her though he does not obey the word. Through her chaste conversation she can win that man over.
- It is through her example: look what the word of God has done for me, and how I deal with you now. He thinks, she used to be loud and obnoxious and argue with me; now when I ask her to do something she does it; she doesn't question my authority or challenge me. The word did that? Let me go see what those brothers in purple and gold are talking about. Through your submission you can move mountains with a man.
- When a husband is wrong and his wife, who was right, still says, okay, I'll do it, I'm sorry, and goes on with her day, it changes him. He is left with hot coals on his head, knowing she is more righteous than he is, because she stood down out of respect for him as the man with the final say.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 7:27-28](/bible/sirach/7#v27)**
    > <sup>[27](/bible/sirach/7#v27)</sup> Honour thy father with thy whole heart, and forget not the sorrows of thy mother.
    >
    > <sup>[28](/bible/sirach/7#v28)</sup> Remember that thou wast begotten of them; and how canst thou recompense them the things that they have done for thee?

    A young man once cursed out his parents to go to Passover and was put to death in a car accident on the way back; the last thing his parents heard from him was curses. “Honour thy father with thy whole heart, and forget not the sorrows of thy mother. Remember that thou wast begotten of them; and how canst thou recompense them the things that they have done for thee?” There is no way you can pay your parents back; all you can do is give glory to the Lord for them.

**[1 Peter 3:2](/bible/1-peter/3#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/1-peter/3#v2)</sup> While they behold your chaste conversation coupled with fear.

- “While they behold your chaste conversation coupled with fear”: the way you conduct yourself. The same way Peter instructed the wives to deal with unbelieving husbands, you brothers and sisters with unbelieving parents win them by your conversation coupled with fear.
- “While they behold your chaste conversation coupled with fear.” Not that the husband is fearful: he sees her fear toward God. Despite what he is doing she still plays her role as a wife; he sees her applying the commandments for a wife, even while he is out of the spirit in that moment.

**[1 Peter 3:3](/bible/1-peter/3#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/1-peter/3#v3)</sup> Whose adorning let it not be that outward adorning of plaiting the hair, and of wearing of gold, or of putting on of apparel;

- “Whose adorning let it not be that outward adorning of plaiting the hair”: it is not saying a woman can’t have outward adorning. Some think a woman has to look bummy and can never get dressed up. Sisters can plait their hair and get fixed up, “and of wearing of gold, or of putting on of apparel”, but their glory must not be in that. A woman can be all hooked up and her spirit rotten.
- “Whose adorning let it not be that outward adorning”: you do not lead with your looks. “Of plaiting the hair”: thinking that once your hair is done, your nails are done, everything is done, you will get things to your advantage. God said do not lead with that. Do not focus on your outward looks and your beauty, because favor is deceitful and beauty is vain. There is nothing wrong with looking nice, but do not lead with it.
- Look at the women who have done plastic surgery after plastic surgery, Vivica Fox and Lil’ Kim: they look like science projects gone wrong. Favor is deceitful: they had favor in their beauty in their youth, but beauty is vain, because eventually you are going to get old.
- “Whose adorning let it not be that outward adorning of plaiting the hair, and of wearing of gold, or of putting on of apparel”: this is why conversation is not just words but actions. After verses 1 and 2 he speaks of the outward show, and says let the adorning be the things that are in the fear of God. Not trying to win him over by looking cute and doing herself up; that is seductive, worldly and carnal.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 36:22-23](/bible/sirach/36#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/sirach/36#v22)</sup> The beauty of a woman cheereth the countenance, and a man loveth nothing better.
    >
    > <sup>[23](/bible/sirach/36#v23)</sup> If there be kindness, meekness, and comfort, in her tongue, then is not her husband like other men.

    We are not saying not to fix yourself up: “The beauty of a woman cheereth the countenance, and a man loveth nothing better.” That is why we encourage our women to look their best. Look your best, but do not lead with that; the primary thing to lead with is the hidden man of the heart. That is where you are supposed to start.

**[1 Peter 3:4](/bible/1-peter/3#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/1-peter/3#v4)</sup> But let it be the hidden man of the heart, in that which is not corruptible, even the ornament of a meek and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God of great price.

- “But let it be the hidden man of the heart”: Christ in her spirit. When you find a woman like that, you will have paradise in your marriage.
- “In that which is not corruptible”: beauty is corruptible, clothes are corruptible, and they fade. A righteous spirit stays with you, and that is how you grow old in righteousness together, watching the grandkids.
- “Even the ornament of a meek and quiet spirit”: an ornament is something you see. You see the ornament of her beauty in her wisdom and in how she deals with her brothers and sisters, along with the outward things; but what makes her most beautiful is what doesn’t fade over time. Meek means obedience. Quiet: she is not always screaming, arguing and fighting, ratchet, when the man has just come in from dealing with all that in the street. Why fight the one who keeps a roof over your head and heat in the house? That is biting the hand that feeds you.
- “Which is in the sight of God of great price”: that righteous spirit is of great price, a price far beyond rubies. Keep your physical self up as much as you can, because age will get us both, but the inner self is what matters, and believe it or not, the inner spirit makes the outside look good.

**[1 Peter 3:5](/bible/1-peter/3#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/1-peter/3#v5)</sup> For after this manner in the old time the holy women also, who trusted in God, adorned themselves, being in subjection unto their own husbands:

- “The holy women also, who trusted in God, adorned themselves, being in subjection unto their own husbands”: what kind of clothes is that? Verse 3 said not to take your glory from outward adorning; here the holy women adorned themselves with being in subjection to their husbands. That is of great price. That is what you kill for.
- “For after this manner in the old time the holy women also, who trusted in God, adorned themselves, being in subjection unto their own husbands”. The key is that they trusted in God. They didn’t say he’s my project, I’m going to change him, fix him. They trusted God enough to play their role harder in times of adversity, so that God could be present in that marriage. God will deal with the husband.

**[1 Peter 3:6](/bible/1-peter/3#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/1-peter/3#v6)</sup> Even as Sara obeyed Abraham, calling him lord: whose daughters ye are, as long as ye do well, and are not afraid with any amazement.

- “Even as Sara obeyed Abraham, calling him lord”: that was the adornment Sarah wore. There are jokes about wives saying my lord this and my lord that on the phone with the sisters and cursing him when the phone hangs up; brothers, fix that. His rib, her lord: he is her head, she understands it, and she wakes up the next day and says amen with him. That is where the beauty is.
- “Whose daughters ye are, as long as ye do well”: daughters of Sarah, as long as you do what we just read. “And are not afraid with any amazement”: trials and things trying to turn you against your husband, things on the job, old dudes looking you up on Facebook and in your DMs wanting to hook up. Don’t even entertain it; block it. Don’t open that door.
- “Even as Sara obeyed Abraham, calling him lord: whose daughters ye are, as long as ye do well, and are not afraid with any amazement.” Not afraid to submit, not afraid that it will run her errant. Being unyielding doesn’t make what the husband is doing right, but staying in her lane with God makes her right; and if she believes and fears God and has confidence in him, he will show her a path forward to reconciliation in the division in her house.
- These things work together: the spirit of submission, understanding why we submit, then the conduct that follows (not just words), and with it meekness and quietness. And biblical submission is not eventually doing what he said after so much trifling, arguing, division and hatred. A wife can comply and still be contemptuous, sarcastic, hostile, dismissive, and have a rebellious spirit. Sarcasm is a covert way of fighting back or asserting superiority without engaging in direct conflict, and it is a disrespectful manifestation of a serious topic. Sarcasm in a funny moment together is fine; every other time it is like nails on a chalkboard. Nasty ass.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 36:23](/bible/sirach/36#v23)**
    > <sup>[23](/bible/sirach/36#v23)</sup> If there be kindness, meekness, and comfort, in her tongue, then is not her husband like other men.

    “Even as Sara obeyed Abraham, calling him lord: whose daughters ye are, as long as ye do well, and are not afraid with any amazement”: this is the kind of wife you want, the kind of marriage you want: no prison wife, no prison marriage, but paradise, because husband and wife is the nucleus of everything; that is why God set it up that way. “If there be kindness, meekness, and comfort, in her tongue, then is not her husband like other men.”

**[1 Peter 3:7](/bible/1-peter/3#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/1-peter/3#v7)</sup> Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered.

- “Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel”: that is why Satan went to her, because she is the weaker vessel. Brothers have to step in so she is not corrupted; your job is to protect her and be a hedge over her. But she has to come in with a basis you can work with, because marriage itself is a trial. Proving can only go so far when you are not living together, and that is no reason to live together in sin. The foundation is that both believe the scriptures. Then the squabbles are small things, how the towels hang. When trouble comes, the question is which one is keeping the commandments: the Bible is what God put together, so one says, the Bible says this, and the other says, you’re right, I was wrong. That is how you keep the peace. If one believes the Bible and the other doesn’t, you are headed for prison, for disaster. That is why the proving process needs heavy emphasis; it is the nucleus of the whole nation of Israel.
- “And as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered”: do you wonder why things don’t go right in families? One is trying to keep the commandments and the other is not, and the Lord is not hearing those prayers. If there is evil in your house on any level, the Lord will not bless it. If you are married to someone in the world, hope to convert them, because at some point you will have to part; I can’t move in the righteousness of the Lord if you won’t get yourself together.
- This is what you end up with if you don’t prove: women online saying they hate their husbands and wait for him to cheat so they can leave and look like the victim, withholding from him until he cracks. She caused it by treating the man like garbage. And sisters, make sure you don’t get caught up with the wrong brothers who will do you in.
- “Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel”. So you husbands, deal with the wife according to knowledge. Deal with the woman according to knowledge. And you give honour to your wife knowing that she is weaker than you.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Thessalonians 4:4](/bible/1-thessalonians/4#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/1-thessalonians/4#v4)</sup> That every one of you should know how to possess his vessel in sanctification and honour;

    This is one portion of what it means to dwell with them according to knowledge and give honor as unto the weaker vessel. “That every one of you should know how to possess his vessel”: first that is talking about you; your body is your vessel, and you need to know how to possess it. When you get a wife, you take possession, so now you possess her vessel too, because the woman came from man: his vessel is his body and her body. “In sanctification and honour”: in cleansing, sanctification and honor.
  - **[Ephesians 5:26-27](/bible/ephesians/5#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/ephesians/5#v26)</sup> That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word,
    >
    > <sup>[27](/bible/ephesians/5#v27)</sup> That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish.

    Christ sanctifies the church and presents it to himself, and so the husband does the wife: “That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word, That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing.”
  - **[Ephesians 4:26-27](/bible/ephesians/4#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/ephesians/4#v26)</sup> Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath:

    Dividing the household gives place to the devil: “Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath: Neither give place to the devil.” You can be upset, but don’t let your wisdom fail. It doesn’t mean make up before you go to bed; it means don’t let your wisdom fade in your anger.

**[1 Peter 3:8](/bible/1-peter/3#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-peter/3#v8)</sup> Finally, be ye all of one mind, having compassion one of another, love as brethren, be pitiful, be courteous:

- “Finally, be ye all of one mind, having compassion one of another, love as brethren, be pitiful, be courteous”: have pity on one another. Even in a marriage you have to love as brethren. Some men are living with a brother sitting in his driveway with a warm can of beer, scared to go into his own house.
- “Finally, be ye all of one mind, having compassion one of another, love as brethren, be pitiful, be courteous”. The Bible says to have compassion one to another, and to love each other as brethren. Love. Get that real love, no fake love. Brothers got to love their wives, wives got to love their brothers. You got to love like brethren.
- Be pitiful, be courteous. Sometime your wife might do something dumb and stupid, and you don't go level 10 all the time. The food is salty, she put too much salt in the food, and you went to level 10. No, you got to be pitiful, courteous. You're like, yo, don't do that again.
- “Finally, be ye all of one mind”. One mind. “having compassion one of another”. Black people cannot spare compassion. We're quick to condemn each other and not forgive each other, but quick to forgive other nations. We pull guns and put each other to death. A white boy walks into a church and shoots a bunch of people, and in less than twenty-four hours it's, I forgive him. Your brother commits a little trespass and you hold a grudge for years: I never want to talk to you or see you again.

**[1 Peter 3:9](/bible/1-peter/3#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/1-peter/3#v9)</sup> Not rendering evil for evil, or railing for railing: but contrariwise blessing; knowing that ye are thereunto called, that ye should inherit a blessing.

- “Not rendering evil for evil, or railing for railing: but contrariwise blessing; knowing that ye are thereunto called, that ye should inherit a blessing.” She say some evil to you, you say something evil to her. No. Brothers, you got to control what come out of your mouth, because words does hurt. When we say evil, we're talking about you calling out names, evil, wicked names, and she's saying wicked, evil, disrespectful stuff. God says it shouldn't be like that.
- Railing for railing: you yelling at her, she yelling at you, she lifting her voice, you lifting your voice. Peter, by the spirit of Christ, said we should not be living like that. God says we should not be rolling like that.
- But contrariwise blessing. Sister, your husband yell at you, and you say, Most High Christ bless you, I'm sorry. That's it. Not, well, I did it because you -- no. Because we are called to inherit the kingdom of heaven. That's what this is all about. And you ain't going to inherit the kingdom if you got a big loud mouth and are disrespectful.
- “Not rendering evil for evil, or railing for railing.” This goes into strife and arguments. Your wife says something evil to you, and you say something evil back, tit for tat. The two of you are in the house cursing each other out, and the children see all of it. Then you come in on Saturday saying shalom with a low bow, while all week you curse each other out nasty.

**[1 Peter 3:10](/bible/1-peter/3#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/1-peter/3#v10)</sup> For he that will love life, and see good days, let him refrain his tongue from evil, and his lips that they speak no guile:

- “For he that will love life, and see good days, let him refrain his tongue from evil, and his lips that they speak no guile”. If you love life, and you want good days -- you want good days in your marriage, you want to have an honourable marriage -- then refrain your tongue from speaking evil against each other, from saying evil to your spouse.
- Sometime your wife might say something and you might want to say something to her that's evil. Control it, refrain your tongue. Because if you say something evil to your wife, she going to hold that for years. Even if you say you sorry, she going to hold on to that.
- When they say evil stuff to us, we forget about it afterwards. She might give you a little nookie or go a little extra with it and you forgot what you was mad about. But these sisters, they be holding on. You say something evil to them, and even though you say you sorry, they hold on to it for years: ten years ago you called me a name, and I told you I'm sorry, but you did say it.
- “For he that will love life, and see good days.” If you want the kingdom of heaven, love life and want good days, “let him refrain his tongue from evil.” The evil here is husband and wife speaking evil to each other. Control your damn mouth. Stop saying hurtful things to your spouse.

**[1 Peter 3:11](/bible/1-peter/3#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/1-peter/3#v11)</sup> Let him eschew evil, and do good; let him seek peace, and ensue it.

- “Let him eschew evil, and do good; let him seek peace, and ensue it.” Eschew evil -- get rid of evil and do good. When evil come in your marriage, get rid of it. Don't give in to it.
- Sometime women do stupid stuff and get you angry, but even in being angry you got to control what you say. Control what you say. Satan plays with their emotion; don't allow him to play with your emotion.
- “Let him eschew evil, and do good”: hate evil, and do good to each other.
- “let him seek peace, and ensue it.” Some sisters sit in the house for days with a damn attitude and won’t say what is bothering them. The Bible says seek peace. Men are not mind readers, not Professor X. If your husband did something that hurt you, come to him and say, you did this to me and it hurt me. He will say, my bad, I didn’t mean it, or tell you why he did it, and at least it is out of the way, instead of walking around secretly mad and expecting him to read your mind. We must seek peace with our spouse.

**[1 Peter 3:12](/bible/1-peter/3#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/1-peter/3#v12)</sup> For the eyes of the Lord are over the righteous, and his ears are open unto their prayers: but the face of the Lord is against them that do evil.

- “For the eyes of the Lord are over the righteous, and his ears are open unto their prayers: but the face of the Lord is against them that do evil.” So God's ears is open. You got to know that, because when you dealing evil with your spouse and your spouse pray to the Lord, the Lord hears it.
- And if you do evil, if you deal evil with your spouse, the Lord's face is against you.
- “For the eyes of the Lord are over the righteous.” This is why: God is watching how you deal with each other. A brother is not going to deal evil with his wife, because God is watching him and will judge him, and it should be the same with the sisters. Judgment is going to come to your black behind if you keep dealing evil with your husband, running your damn mouth and being disrespectful. God sees you.
- “and his ears are open unto their prayers.” “but the face of the Lord is against them that do evil.” Deal evil with each other and the Lord is against you. Do not be in the house calling each other names and fooling yourselves that you are serving God. Stop arguing and saying hurtful things to each other in front of your kids.

**[1 Peter 3:14](/bible/1-peter/3#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/1-peter/3#v14)</sup> But and if ye suffer for righteousness’ sake, happy are ye: and be not afraid of their terror, neither be troubled;

- Peter gave the instructions between marriages for how to endure tribulation and receive blessing from God. We have marriage counsels where a sister is upset because the socks are on the floor and calls her husband lazy, or a sister gives up the faith to go back to the world and be a hoe to negro Tyrone instead of a wife to the husband the Lord gave her, or a brother wants to be a whoremonger instead of a man leading his people. More trying times are coming, when you will be worried about surviving. How much more must a household where man and woman are striving with each other get right?
- “But and if ye suffer for righteousness’ sake”: we must learn to suffer for the sake of Christ and righteousness. “Happy are ye”: that is your peace. Happy are they that keep God’s laws. Prepare for hard and trying times, and do not go railing for railing with one another. Our best tools are praying, fasting, studying and increasing our faith, and following the instructions laid forth.
- “And be not afraid of their terror”: not afraid of being persecuted. For the sake of righteousness we went through hell on earth, yet we had happiness and peace, because the poor of God’s people were rich in faith. This is in a sense the easiest time of our generation, because we are so destroyed we do not even know who we are; before this were Jim Crow and the black codes, when the black church was actually doing something for the community, and before that slavery, and before that the hell of sin. So we learn what is going to happen to us from what happened to us before.

**[1 Peter 3:21](/bible/1-peter/3#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/1-peter/3#v21)</sup> The like figure whereunto even baptism doth also now save us (not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God,) by the resurrection of Jesus Christ:

- Baptism. Nobody ever said getting dipped in water is a sin; what we say is that it is symbolic. Today people want a Shazam effect: they don’t want the discipline of changing their life; they would rather be dipped in some pool water and come up a changed man or woman. It doesn’t happen like that; you are still as wicked as before you went in the water. “The like figure whereunto even baptism doth also now save us”: Peter reveals that baptism does save you. “Not the putting away of the filth of the flesh”: water is what takes the filth from your flesh, and that type of baptism does not save you. “But the answer of a good conscience toward God”: our conscience is found in our mind, and we have a good conscience when we are keeping the commandments in Christ.
- The problem is that a lot of brothers and sisters come from Christianity. Many are called but few are chosen: a lot of people been called in here from Christianity, and growing up your grandmom told you that in order to get the kingdom of heaven you have to be dipped in water. That thought is still in their mind, so they come into Israel and ask to be baptized. Hell no: I’m baptizing you right now, teaching you the laws of God. Which baptism you want, John’s baptism or Christ’s baptism? He that cometh after me shall baptize you with fire: that’s the baptism I want. Every last one of them that leaves up out of here has been water baptized, and you see them burning ganja and all type of stuff; it’s like they got baptized back into being the damn devil.
- “The like figure whereunto even baptism doth also now save us”: baptism does save you. “Not the putting away of the filth of the flesh”: being dipped in a pool or in a river is putting away the filth of the flesh. “But the answer of a good conscience”: this is Christ’s baptism. You must change the way you think; you must change inwardly.
- You a homosexual and you dip yourself in water, you just a wet, clean homosexual. You a lesbian, you dip yourself in water; you a thief, you dip yourself in water, you just a clean thief. Your body is clean, like taking a shower, and that’s all it is.

  Precepts:
  - **[Isaiah 1:16](/bible/isaiah/1#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/isaiah/1#v16)</sup> Wash you, make you clean; put away the evil of your doings from before mine eyes; cease to do evil;

    Water was used symbolically, and John’s baptism was based on scriptures like this: “Wash you, make you clean; put away the evil of your doings.” That is what it symbolized.
  - **[Jeremiah 2:22](/bible/jeremiah/2#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/jeremiah/2#v22)</sup> For though thou wash thee with nitre, and take thee much soap, yet thine iniquity is marked before me, saith the Lord God.

    “For though thou wash thee with nitre, and take thee much soap, yet thine iniquity is marked before me, saith the Lord God”: all that washing makes no change on your inside.
  - **[Matthew 3:6](/bible/matthew/3#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/matthew/3#v6)</sup> And were baptized of him in Jordan, confessing their sins.

    “And were baptized of him in Jordan, confessing their sins”: in the time of John the Baptist they were sincere and confessed their sins. Find anybody on Facebook or YouTube getting dipped in water confessing their sins; all you see is the dip. That is still not following the scriptures.
