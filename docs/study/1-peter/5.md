## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 1 Peter 5 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-10, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[1 Peter 5:1](/bible/1-peter/5#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-peter/5#v1)</sup> The elders which are among you I exhort, who am also an elder, and a witness of the sufferings of Christ, and also a partaker of the glory that shall be revealed:

- “The elders which are among you I exhort, who am also an elder, and a witness of the sufferings of Christ, and also a partaker of the glory that shall be revealed.” When we hear elders, I’m thinking of the deacons that are above me, the Bishops that are above me. You’re thinking elders and it might be the soldier that’s above you or the officer that’s above you. But all of us fall into this in some form.
- “The elders which are among you I exhort”: the elders in this context are the leaders among you.

**[1 Peter 5:2](/bible/1-peter/5#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/1-peter/5#v2)</sup> Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking the oversight thereof, not by constraint, but willingly; not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind;

- “Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking the oversight thereof, not by constraint, but willingly; not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind”. A leader does this with a ready mind, not asking how this can profit him or how he can make money from it. Feed them what? The word of God. Teach them the laws of God, apply the word of God, show them how to redeem themselves, and the people will gain trust. They will respect and trust you if your conversation is how to serve God and how to perfect your walk.
- Not by constraint: not, well, Sabbath's over, I'm out of here, never speaking to anybody during the week. When the leaders get together they spend their time in the scriptures; they laugh and have a good time, but they are in the scriptures. Outside of this, what else do we have? If you've got another life outside this, you're not in the truth. If your best friend is somebody you went to school with, you're still in the world. There is no plan B. Prefer one another; if you can get together outside of work, spend time, because that is how you build relationships. It starts with the leadership: when the men see that the leaders are tight and go through the scriptures together, they will follow suit. If they see you cursing the people out and not working, they will learn the same behavior.
- Peter had to write this because that was the mentality back then. Scripture is given by inspiration of God and is profitable for reproof and for instruction in how to be righteous. It is left for us, so in the last days we read the same thing and know this is the blueprint we follow.
- “Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking the oversight thereof, not by constraint, but willingly; not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind.” We have to focus on building each other up. Of a ready mind: keep thy heart with all diligence, lift up the weak hands and the feeble knees. Get your eye right.

**[1 Peter 5:3](/bible/1-peter/5#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/1-peter/5#v3)</sup> Neither as being lords over God’s heritage, but being ensamples to the flock.

- “Neither as being lords over God’s heritage, but being ensamples to the flock.” A lot of times brothers use their position and become lords over God's heritage. They take advantage because they have the rank, not understanding there is a God they are going to answer to.
- We are to be examples to the people. Imagine the Most High sends Sacramento an influx of people, a thousand people repent, and from the top down there is corruption: a school ten times the size, with leadership cursing the people out, not working, taking money to pay their bills. That thing will fall apart. You want the trust of the people? Be transparent. Show a pattern of good.
- “Neither as being lords over God’s heritage, but being ensamples to the flock”: you show them in your actions.
- “Neither as being lords over God’s heritage”. Nobody is supposed to be lording over you. The world says we are a cult that tells you what to do and where to move. No: we counsel you out of the scriptures and you decide how you move. If you move according to the scriptures, all praises to the Most High; if you move against them and cannot get it right, you have to go. Nobody is lording over you, but to stay here you have to conform to the word of God. Your job as a leader is not to lord over or intimidate, not to be a dictator or a tyrant. The people are not supposed to fear you; they are supposed to respect you.

  Precepts:
  - **[Titus 2:6-10](/bible/titus/2#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/titus/2#v6)</sup> Young men likewise exhort to be sober minded.
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/titus/2#v7)</sup> In all things shewing thyself a pattern of good works: in doctrine shewing uncorruptness, gravity, sincerity,
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/titus/2#v8)</sup> Sound speech, that cannot be condemned; that he that is of the contrary part may be ashamed, having no evil thing to say of you.
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/titus/2#v9)</sup> Exhort servants to be obedient unto their own masters, and to please them well in all things; not answering again;
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/titus/2#v10)</sup> Not purloining, but shewing all good fidelity; that they may adorn the doctrine of God our Saviour in all things.

    Show a pattern of good: “In all things shewing thyself a pattern of good works: in doctrine shewing uncorruptness, gravity, sincerity, Sound speech, that cannot be condemned; that he that is of the contrary part may be ashamed, having no evil thing to say of you.” Sincerity: not fake. You are sincere about this.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 10:21, 25](/bible/sirach/10#v21)**
    > <sup>[21](/bible/sirach/10#v21)</sup> The fear of the Lord goeth before the obtaining of authority: but roughness and pride is the losing thereof.
    >
    > <sup>[25](/bible/sirach/10#v25)</sup> Unto the servant that is wise shall they that are free do service: and he that hath knowledge will not grudge when he is reformed.

    What happens when you lord over the flock? “The fear of the Lord goeth before the obtaining of authority”: before you get a position, you have to have a healthy fear of God. “But roughness and pride is the losing thereof.” Lording over the flock, hurting the flock and quenching its spirit, is how you lose it.

**[1 Peter 5:4](/bible/1-peter/5#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/1-peter/5#v4)</sup> And when the chief Shepherd shall appear, ye shall receive a crown of glory that fadeth not away.

- “And when the chief Shepherd shall appear, ye shall receive a crown of glory that fadeth not away.” At the end of the day, we want this crown. The ranking system we have is for the running of the body, to keep things moving forward. The true crown is when Christ crowns you.
- “And when the chief Shepherd shall appear”, when Christ appears, “ye shall receive a crown of glory that fadeth not away.” We have that crown of glory now in retaining that spirit of Christ in us, but when he arrives, in that day, that crown of glory will not fade away. We won’t have to fight to keep that crown of glory anymore, if you show that you can fight for it now and hold on to it now.
- “And when the chief Shepherd shall appear”: the chief Shepherd is Christ. “Ye shall receive a crown of glory that fadeth not away”: we will be in the kingdom, immortal.

**[1 Peter 5:5](/bible/1-peter/5#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/1-peter/5#v5)</sup> Likewise, ye younger, submit yourselves unto the elder. Yea, all of you be subject one to another, and be clothed with humility: for God resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble.

- “Likewise, ye younger, submit yourselves unto the elder.” Because God set up the elders: he is the one that set them up, for the younger to learn his ways from them and for them to feed you with his wisdom and knowledge. So God tells the younger to humble themselves to the elders, to the leaders. “Yea, all of you be subject one to another”: all of you have respect one for another, but there is a hierarchy in God’s eyes. “And be clothed with humility: for God resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble.”
- “Likewise, ye younger, submit yourselves unto the elder.” He was speaking to the elders, the leaders; now he speaks to the younger: submit to the leader. “Yea, all of you be subject one to another”: you stay in your order and I stay in mine. “And be clothed with humility”: humility is freedom from pride or arrogance, being humble. If you think you are better than your brothers and sisters, how are you better when we are all collectively still at the bottom? We went into slavery together and were born in the ghettos of America together. What are you arrogant for?
- “For God resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble.”

  Precepts:
  - **[Proverbs 6:23](/bible/proverbs/6#v23)**
    > <sup>[23](/bible/proverbs/6#v23)</sup> For the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light; and reproofs of instruction are the way of life:

    “For the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light; and reproofs of instruction are the way of life”. Correction is the way of life, and everyone is going to be corrected. Everybody. So what are you mad for? I didn't like the way it was corrected; I thought I was doing a good job. So what? Do not get sensitive and emotional. Just fix it, take the correction and move the hell on. That is submitting yourselves to the elder.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 10:12](/bible/sirach/10#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/sirach/10#v12)</sup> The beginning of pride is when one departeth from God, and his heart is turned away from his Maker.

    Be clothed with humility, not that arrogance that you are better than your brothers and sisters. “The beginning of pride is when one departeth from God, and his heart is turned away from his Maker.” That is the pride being spoken of: too proud to be corrected.
  - **[1 Corinthians 4:6](/bible/1-corinthians/4#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/1-corinthians/4#v6)</sup> And these things, brethren, I have in a figure transferred to myself and to Apollos for your sakes; that ye might learn in us not to think of men above that which is written, that no one of you be puffed up for one against another.

    Why is it dangerous for a leader to believe nobody can do this but me, none of this would be possible without me? That is a self-willed spirit, a prideful spirit, the spirit of a novice. That leader is making himself out like he is God, and he can hurt the congregation.

**[1 Peter 5:6-7](/bible/1-peter/5#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/1-peter/5#v6)</sup> Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time:
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/1-peter/5#v7)</sup> Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.

- Be aware of your surroundings; the scriptures warned us of that. “Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God”: the word humble is where you get humility from. The reason I think this way is the humility I have for God. Humbling myself to God and reading his Bible put me in a different sense of awareness, a different mindset concerning the world and concerning people. I read in his Bible how human beings are without his word, and God lets it show through his word how people are and what they did to us. A lot of people don’t know it, don’t want to hear it, don’t care about it: ‘ah, that was back then, you reading that old book.’
- “That he may exalt you in due time”: he exalted me in due time with this understanding of the Bible, and now I know we got enemies.
- “Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God”: that is the message of the whole class. “That he may exalt you in due time”: he will raise us up in due time.
- “Casting all your care upon him”: my care is what does God say, what was the historical account of the Israelites. I’m not concerned with you Negroes walking the earth right now; y’all know nothing. The average negro, all he knows is the Super Bowl and Beyonce and Jordans and weed smoke and a club and the latest dances. I cast all my cares upon God; I don’t get no answers from no Negroes, you Negroes are clueless. “For he careth for you”: I know that God has my best interests at heart.

  Precepts:
  - **[Psalms 94:19](/bible/psalms/94#v19)**
    > <sup>[19](/bible/psalms/94#v19)</sup> In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul.

    “In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul.” That is where your comfort comes from. When you are sick, when you are dealing with depression or whatever bad you are going through in your life, we have to learn to comfort ourselves, and this shows where that comfort comes from: the thoughts within.
  - **[1 Thessalonians 4:13-14](/bible/1-thessalonians/4#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/1-thessalonians/4#v13)</sup> But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope.
    >
    > <sup>[14](/bible/1-thessalonians/4#v14)</sup> For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.

    Bad things are going to happen to all of us. Some will get sick, some will be put in prison, some will lose loved ones. This is how we comfort ourselves in our thoughts: “I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope.” The brothers and sisters that die, we shouldn't be sorrowful for them. If you die in Christ you are not like others that have no hope.
  - **[1 Thessalonians 4:18](/bible/1-thessalonians/4#v18)**
    > <sup>[18](/bible/1-thessalonians/4#v18)</sup> Wherefore comfort one another with these words.

    Read with verse 13, them that die, “that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope”: don't sorrow like people that don't have hope, because those of us that die in Christ have hope. Then: “Wherefore comfort one another with these words.”

**[1 Peter 5:8](/bible/1-peter/5#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-peter/5#v8)</sup> Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour:

- “Your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour”: just what we read in Job chapter 1, going to and fro in the earth. It says the devil there, not devils, but the devil. There is a hierarchy in the heavens and upon the earth.
- “Be sober”: I’m sober to what’s going on around me. “Be vigilant”: I’m vigilant wherever I go, wherever I walk; it takes me seconds to read somebody and realize this person ain’t right, whether black or white. “Because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour”: he is going to devour people that put themselves in situations, clueless to God’s words, clueless to their surroundings, clueless to the operation of this world and how people and evil spirits are.
- That is the lesson of Rasheem Carter: beef at work, a roommate who stopped giving him rides, white men in trucks chasing him and yelling racial slurs, and our people still walking around with this sense of no awareness, thinking it’s all good in the hood. It is not, and the audience should not have that mindset.
- “Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour.” This is a biblical fact: we know Satan is lurking, so we are going to be sober and vigilant, watching, head on a swivel like on security, because the moment you look away is when he tries to get you. Imagine somebody outside your house who has told you, I'm trying to break in, and when you slip I'll be up in there. What are you going to do? The Lord told us Satan is looking and waiting to get in, and we have to make sure he doesn't. We have to keep ourselves girded.

    This is Paul: “the Lord stood with me, and strengthened me; that by me the preaching might be fully known, and that all the Gentiles might hear”: all of us scattered abroad.

**[1 Peter 5:9](/bible/1-peter/5#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/1-peter/5#v9)</sup> Whom resist stedfast in the faith, knowing that the same afflictions are accomplished in your brethren that are in the world.

- “Whom resist stedfast in the faith”: I can resist him with the faith I have in God, in his word, and the understanding I have of the gospel, where he said the other nations don’t like us; they’re our enemies. So I’m not letting my guard down. It got nothing to do with me being racist; y’all try to pull that race card on me, no. I know historically these people don’t like me. What’s going to make them magically like my big lips, big nose and dark skin now, when they have a traumatic history with us: the lynchings, the cross burnings, the church bombings? Now all of a sudden I’m going to say well that was in the past? No.
- “Knowing that the same afflictions are accomplished in your brethren that are in the world”: all of us could get these afflictions at any time. When you’re clueless to the Bible you’re clueless to your rival. These people are rival enemies against us, and if y’all get relaxed around them they’re going to take advantage of any opportunity to jump on your ignorance and do what they did to Rasheem.
- “Whom resist stedfast in the faith, knowing that the same afflictions are accomplished in your brethren that are in the world.” Everybody is going through it, the same afflictions. We have to be mindful.
- “Whom resist stedfast in the faith”: you have to resist Satan. “Knowing that the same afflictions are accomplished in your brethren that are in the world”: the same things you are going through, your other brothers are going through. Those afflictions come from your adversary, the devil.

**[1 Peter 5:10](/bible/1-peter/5#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/1-peter/5#v10)</sup> But the God of all grace, who hath called us unto his eternal glory by Christ Jesus, after that ye have suffered a while, make you perfect, stablish, strengthen, settle you.

- “But the God of all grace, who hath called us unto his eternal glory by Christ Jesus, after that ye have suffered a while, make you perfect.” This is the devil’s job: all the temptations and afflictions Satan brings against us are to make us perfect. Even the real bad things: Job lost all his kids, and that was to make him perfect.
- That is a hard saying: without Satan, our adversary, we cannot become perfect, because it is his job, him and his children, to try us. Nobody wants to suffer. But the Lord says, you all want the kingdom, you have to suffer. And if you are acceptable, after you suffer a while you are going to get that kingdom of eternal glory.
- Suffering makes you perfect, “stablish”: it establishes you; “strengthen”: when you go through hard things you become battle hardened, like somebody who has been warring for thirty or forty years; you can’t fight them any old how. “Settle you”: you are settled within your spirit. It is what it is.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 42:24-25](/bible/sirach/42#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/sirach/42#v24)</sup> All things are double one against another: and he hath made nothing imperfect.
    >
    > <sup>[25](/bible/sirach/42#v25)</sup> One thing establisheth the good of another: and who shall be filled with beholding his glory?

    This shows how Satan makes us perfect. “All things are double one against another”: God created good, and he created evil also. “And he hath made nothing imperfect”: Satan was not created imperfect. He was created to do exactly what he is doing.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 2:5](/bible/sirach/2#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/sirach/2#v5)</sup> For gold is tried in the fire, and acceptable men in the furnace of adversity.

    “For gold is tried in the fire, and acceptable men in the furnace of adversity.” The fire, the furnace of adversity, is temptation. Adversity comes from our adversary, and our adversary is that spiritual demon and his children.
