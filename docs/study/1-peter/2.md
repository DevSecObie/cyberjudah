## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 1 Peter 2 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-12, 17-25, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[1 Peter 2:1](/bible/1-peter/2#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-peter/2#v1)</sup> Wherefore laying aside all malice, and all guile, and hypocrisies, and envies, and all evil speakings,

- How do we grow in this truth: individually, as a congregation, in our marriages, as an officer, as a soldier? Everybody goes to the milk and misses verse 1. “Wherefore laying aside all malice”: you have to put away first all the malice that is within you. “And all guile”: guile means deceit. “And hypocrisies, and envies”: envies are jealousies. “And all evil speakings”: all murmurings and complainings.
- A brother was demoted last week for lying over a simple thing. Most of the time, when we ask you, we already know the answer; we call the witnesses, and now you are found out as a liar and deceitful, not worthy of your rank. That is why some brothers stay away from the war room.
- Zerubbabel got to his position not just by his fighting skills, but by noble character and understanding God’s truth. We should all walk in the footsteps of Zerubbabel, and not be deceitful, envious, hateful, full of guile with one another. Frederick Douglass said it is easier to build strong children than to repair broken men. All of us came in broken, broken with sins.
- “Wherefore laying aside all malice, and all guile, and hypocrisies, and envies, and all evil speakings.” You have to remove all of those things out of your spirit first.

**[1 Peter 2:2](/bible/1-peter/2#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/1-peter/2#v2)</sup> As newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word, that ye may grow thereby:

- “As newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word”. The sincere milk of the word is the law, God’s laws. Be like a newborn baby: we have to be drinking the laws of God the way a baby drinks milk. Not once a month, not once a week, but every other hour, in that Bible study. “That ye may grow thereby”: that is the only way you are going to grow, as an individual, as a husband, as an officer, as a soldier, as a leader.
- “As newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word, that ye may grow thereby.” If you want to go from milk to meat, you first have to desire the sincere milk of the word, and grow thereby like a baby.
- If you are not in the scriptures every day, your mind is not right and you are not really in this truth. This is all we have. Four chapters a day: you would have to miss them on purpose. Some are not strong readers, and that is understood, but put on the Bible app or a reading on YouTube and read along; four chapters take about five minutes that way.
- Missing them is consciously saying, not with your words but with your actions, I do not desire the sincere milk of the word. And then you wonder why you don’t grow. The way we know the things we know is that we have been rehearsing this over and over again. When something is spoken that seems deep, it is because we have seen it or experienced it already, and we are trying to show you how to handle it.

- “Unto you therefore which believe he is precious”: those scattered Israelites called strangers and heathens started to believe on Christ; that’s what Paul’s ministry was about. “But unto them which be disobedient, the stone which the builders disallowed”: the disobedient were the Pharisees and scribes of the southern kingdom that rejected Christ; they were supposed to be the builders. “The same is made the head of the corner”: the chief cornerstone house is usually the best house on the block.

The builders are the southern kingdom: the Pharisees, the Sadducees, the scribes, the elders, the rulers.

That is the same thing expected of the brothers in God’s army: clean, disciplined, trained in the scriptures, bringing no shame on the purple and gold. We are not spicks, we are not Dominicans and Haitians, not rappers, not gangsters, not Bloods, not Crips. We are the twelve tribes of the nation of Israel. We come from royalty, from honour and respect, and we are bringing the government of Christ back on this earth: the Sabbath and the feast days are government, order, structure and heritage. And he laid out the obligation of fathers and mothers to raise their children in the love and fear of God.
- This is similar. “Honour all men. Love the brotherhood.” Simeon and Levi are brethren; love the brotherhood. “Fear God. Honour the king”: honour Christ. That is what Toussaint Louverture, of the mighty tribe of Levi, was trying to express as best he could with Esau’s religion. Now we have the laws, the statutes, the commandments and the Most High’s word. It is our obligation as parents to teach our children, to govern ourselves and our people, and to stop following the wicked customs of the heathen.

Can a bird fall in a snare upon the earth, where no gin is for him?”, and came back to verse 3: “Can two walk together, except they be agreed?”

**[1 Peter 2:18](/bible/1-peter/2#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/1-peter/2#v18)</sup> Servants, be subject to your masters with all fear; not only to the good and gentle, but also to the froward.

- “Servants, be subject to your masters with all fear.” This was in the slave Bible; Esau used it. “Not only to the good and gentle, but also to the froward.” Some masters weren’t that nice. The law told them not to deal with Israel with rigour, but some were hard. The froward ones are the ones that don’t really like you.

**[1 Peter 2:24](/bible/1-peter/2#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/1-peter/2#v24)</sup> Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed.

- “Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed.” We were dead in sins, and the law is what shows sin: you can’t sin if there’s no law, and the law was given to Israel.
