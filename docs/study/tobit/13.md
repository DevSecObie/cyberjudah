## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Tobit 13 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-7, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Tobit 13:1-2](/bible/tobit/13#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/tobit/13#v1)</sup> Then Tobit wrote a prayer of rejoicing, and said, Blessed be God that liveth for ever, and blessed be his kingdom.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/tobit/13#v2)</sup> For he doth scourge, and hath mercy: he leadeth down to hell, and bringeth up again: neither is there any that can avoid his hand.

- Listen to what Tobit is saying in his prayer: “For he doth scourge, and hath mercy: he leadeth down to hell, and bringeth up again”. God scourges and has mercy. Where do we read that in the Old Testament concerning the northern kingdom, God punishing but having mercy? It doesn’t use those words, but Hosea is the book.
- “Neither is there any that can avoid his hand”: all of us are going to catch that, nobody can avoid his hand. He’s going to destroy and scourge, and he did that. He scourged, but he did have mercy.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hosea 1:10](/bible/hosea/1#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/hosea/1#v10)</sup> Yet the number of the children of Israel shall be as the sand of the sea, which cannot be measured nor numbered; and it shall come to pass, that in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people, there it shall be said unto them, Ye are the sons of the living God.

    God scourges and has mercy on the northern kingdom: “Yet the number of the children of Israel shall be as the sand of the sea, which cannot be measured nor numbered; and it shall come to pass, that in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people”. This is referring to the northern kingdom. “There it shall be said unto them, Ye are the sons of the living God.”
  - **[2 Kings 25:8-11](/bible/2-kings/25#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/2-kings/25#v8)</sup> And in the fifth month, on the seventh day of the month, which is the nineteenth year of king Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, came Nebuzar–adan, captain of the guard, a servant of the king of Babylon, unto Jerusalem:
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/2-kings/25#v9)</sup> And he burnt the house of the Lord, and the king’s house, and all the houses of Jerusalem, and every great man’s house burnt he with fire.
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/2-kings/25#v10)</sup> And all the army of the Chaldees, that were with the captain of the guard, brake down the walls of Jerusalem round about.
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/2-kings/25#v11)</sup> Now the rest of the people that were left in the city, and the fugitives that fell away to the king of Babylon, with the remnant of the multitude, did Nebuzar–adan the captain of the guard carry away.

    This was Nebuchadnezzar, but read it anyway as an example of the Lord coming in and destroying. The captain of the guard, a servant of the king of Babylon, came unto Jerusalem: “And he burnt the house of the Lord, and the king’s house, and all the houses of Jerusalem, and every great man’s house burnt he with fire.” That’s the scourge we just read about in Tobit.

**[Tobit 13:3](/bible/tobit/13#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/tobit/13#v3)</sup> Confess him before the Gentiles, ye children of Israel: for he hath scattered us among them.

- “Confess him before the Gentiles, ye children of Israel”. He said go confess him before the gentiles. This is a prayer. Isn’t that what we’re doing today? Isn’t that what they did back then? Matthew 28: go into all nations and preach. Confess him before the gentiles, “for he hath scattered us among them.”

**[Tobit 13:4-5](/bible/tobit/13#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/tobit/13#v4)</sup> There declare his greatness, and extol him before all the living: for he is our Lord, and he is the God our Father for ever.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/tobit/13#v5)</sup> And he will scourge us for our iniquities, and will have mercy again, and will gather us out of all nations, among whom he hath scattered us.

- “There declare his greatness, and extol him before all the living”. “And he will scourge us for our iniquities, and will have mercy again, and will gather us out of all nations, among whom he hath scattered us.” He will have mercy again, and he is going to gather: don’t we read that in Isaiah 11?

  Precepts:
  - **[Isaiah 11:11-13](/bible/isaiah/11#v11)**
    > <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/11#v11)</sup> And it shall come to pass in that day, that the Lord shall set his hand again the second time to recover the remnant of his people, which shall be left, from Assyria, and from Egypt, and from Pathros, and from Cush, and from Elam, and from Shinar, and from Hamath, and from the islands of the sea.
    >
    > <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/11#v12)</sup> And he shall set up an ensign for the nations, and shall assemble the outcasts of Israel, and gather together the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth.
    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/isaiah/11#v13)</sup> The envy also of Ephraim shall depart, and the adversaries of Judah shall be cut off: Ephraim shall not envy Judah, and Judah shall not vex Ephraim.

    The same thing Tobit said, that he will gather you out of all nations: “And it shall come to pass in that day, that the Lord shall set his hand again the second time to recover the remnant of his people”. “And he shall set up an ensign for the nations, and shall assemble the outcasts of Israel, and gather together the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth.”

**[Tobit 13:6-7](/bible/tobit/13#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/tobit/13#v6)</sup> If ye turn to him with your whole heart, and with your whole mind, and deal uprightly before him, then will he turn unto you, and will not hide his face from you. Therefore see what he will do with you, and confess him with your whole mouth, and praise the Lord of might, and extol the everlasting King. In the land of my captivity do I praise him, and declare his might and majesty to a sinful nation. O ye sinners, turn and do justice before him: who can tell if he will accept you, and have mercy on you?
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/tobit/13#v7)</sup> I will extol my God, and my soul shall praise the King of heaven, and shall rejoice in his greatness.

- “If ye turn to him with your whole heart, and with your whole mind, and deal uprightly before him, then will he turn unto you, and will not hide his face from you.” “In the land of my captivity do I praise him, and declare his might and majesty to a sinful nation. O ye sinners, turn and do justice before him”. “I will extol my God, and my soul shall praise the King of heaven, and shall rejoice in his greatness.” That’s repentance.
