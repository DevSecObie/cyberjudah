## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Tobit 2 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-14, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Tobit 2:1-2](/bible/tobit/2#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/tobit/2#v1)</sup> Now when I was come home again, and my wife Anna was restored unto me, with my son Tobias, in the feast of Pentecost, which is the holy feast of the seven weeks, there was a good dinner prepared me, in the which I sat down to eat.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/tobit/2#v2)</sup> And when I saw abundance of meat, I said to my son, Go and bring what poor man soever thou shalt find out of our brethren, who is mindful of the Lord; and, lo, I tarry for thee.

- As these perilous times come and Jacob’s trouble is ushered in, we are going to be all we have. One shall betray another. Family members that hate this truth will turn you in for a little fiat money, a bounty on your head, dead or alive. Your mother will choose white Jesus over you.
- Tobit was a man of charity, a perfect example of how we should be. He kept the feast of Pentecost and was mindful of the Lord, and he had an abundance of meat because he prepared for perilous times. In a famine, a brother like Tobit is a good brother. “Go and bring what poor man soever thou shalt find out of our brethren, who is mindful of the Lord; and, lo, I tarry for thee.”
- His wife and son were restored to him, most likely having been imprisoned as enemies of the state; that is the consequence of serving God, and the time is coming when you will have to choose. And it was “in the feast of Pentecost”: Tobit was in captivity, not in Jerusalem, and he kept the holy day. You heretics who say you cannot keep the feasts in captivity, what was Tobit doing?
- He sent his son to bring a poor man of their brethren “who is mindful of the Lord.” He was not the only one serving the Lord in captivity.

**[Tobit 2:3-8](/bible/tobit/2#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/tobit/2#v3)</sup> But he came again, and said, Father, one of our nation is strangled, and is cast out in the marketplace.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/tobit/2#v4)</sup> Then before I had tasted of any meat, I started up, and took him up into a room until the going down of the sun.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/tobit/2#v5)</sup> Then I returned, and washed myself, and ate my meat in heaviness,
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/tobit/2#v6)</sup> Remembering that prophecy of Amos, as he said, Your feasts shall be turned into mourning, and all your mirth into lamentation.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/tobit/2#v7)</sup> Therefore I wept: and after the going down of the sun I went and made a grave, and buried him.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/tobit/2#v8)</sup> But my neighbours mocked me, and said, This man is not yet afraid to be put to death for this matter: who fled away; and yet, lo, he burieth the dead again.

- One of their nation was strangled in the marketplace, and before tasting his meat Tobit took him up and buried him after sunset, and his neighbours mocked him: he did this before and fled, and he still has not learned.
- “One of our nation is strangled, and is cast out in the marketplace. Then before I had tasted of any meat, I started up, and took him up into a room.” He “ate my meat in heaviness”, remembering Amos: “Your feasts shall be turned into mourning, and all your mirth into lamentation.”
- Tobit had a charitable spirit, and that is how we have to move. As times get tough, these are the loyal men you want to be around, to nurture and look out for one another. And do not think that because Tobit was charitable he did not correct anybody. We have to stop this emotional Christianity mindset.

**[Tobit 2:9-14](/bible/tobit/2#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/tobit/2#v9)</sup> The same night also I returned from the burial, and slept by the wall of my courtyard, being polluted, and my face was uncovered:
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/tobit/2#v10)</sup> And I knew not that there were sparrows in the wall, and mine eyes being open, the sparrows muted warm dung into mine eyes, and a whiteness came in mine eyes; and I went to the physicians, but they helped me not: moreover Achiacharus did nourish me, until I went into Elymais.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/tobit/2#v11)</sup> And my wife Anna did take women’s works to do.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/tobit/2#v12)</sup> And when she had sent them home to the owners, they paid her wages, and gave her also besides a kid.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/tobit/2#v13)</sup> And when it was in my house, and began to cry, I said unto her, From whence is this kid? is it not stolen? render it to the owners; for it is not lawful to eat any thing that is stolen.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/tobit/2#v14)</sup> But she replied upon me, It was given for a gift more than the wages. Howbeit I did not believe her, but bade her render it to the owners: and I was abashed at her. But she replied upon me, Where are thine alms and thy righteous deeds? behold, thou and all thy works are known.

- Sleeping by the wall, sparrows muted warm dung into his eyes and he went blind. Anna took women's work to support them, and when she brought home a kid he accused her of stealing it, and she threw it back at him: “Where are thine alms and thy righteous deeds?”
