## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Tobit 8 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 2-9, 18-21, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Tobit 8:2](/bible/tobit/8#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/tobit/8#v2)</sup> And as he went, he remembered the words of Raphael, and took the ashes of the perfumes, and put the heart and the liver of the fish thereupon, and made a smoke therewith.

- “And took the ashes of the perfumes, and put the heart and the liver of the fish thereupon, and made a smoke therewith.” No, this is not voodoo; this is how the Lord wanted it to go down.

**[Tobit 8:3](/bible/tobit/8#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/tobit/8#v3)</sup> The which smell when the evil spirit had smelled, he fled into the utmost parts of Egypt, and the angel bound him.

- “The which smell when the evil spirit had smelled”: see that, the evil spirit, singular. “He fled into the utmost parts of Egypt, and the angel bound him”: meaning he could not leave the area of Egypt; that spirit was bound there.
- You can see that today: certain cities have certain spirits bound in those areas. Chicago, what kind of spirit is bound there? Murder. San Francisco, Oklahoma: each has its spirit bound there. This is how the Bible uses the terminology for Satan.

**[Tobit 8:4-8](/bible/tobit/8#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/tobit/8#v4)</sup> And after that they were both shut in together, Tobias rose out of the bed, and said, Sister, arise, and let us pray that God would have pity on us.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/tobit/8#v5)</sup> Then began Tobias to say, Blessed art thou, O God of our fathers, and blessed is thy holy and glorious name for ever; let the heavens bless thee, and all thy creatures.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/tobit/8#v6)</sup> Thou madest Adam, and gavest him Eve his wife for an helper and stay: of them came mankind: thou hast said, It is not good that man should be alone; let us make unto him an aid like unto himself.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/tobit/8#v7)</sup> And now, O Lord, I take not this my sister for lust, but uprightly: therefore mercifully ordain that we may become aged together.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/tobit/8#v8)</sup> And she said with him, Amen.

- “Thou madest Adam, and gavest him Eve his wife for an helper and stay: of them came mankind.” When a man and a woman come together, they have the ability to produce gods. That is not some plaything; you can’t have children without a wife. She is not something to run through and treat like garbage. How would a father feel, after all the trouble of raising her up, seeing his daughter treated like a piece of meat?
- Of them two came mankind: man and woman have the ability to produce the whole planet. As Officer Eli brought out on the Bible Book of Our Fathers, eight people were on the ark, Noah, his three sons and their wives, and from them the whole earth was peopled. That is how special those women were. Do it right once and righteous sons and daughters come of it: two or three children marry, five or six families become 12, then 24. Women, understand your value. If your camp is not teaching you that, get the hell out; and brothers, if the sisters don’t want the right thing, get up and go, because some women are as nasty as some men.
- “It is not good that man should be alone”: that is back to let them marry. “Let us make unto him an aid like unto himself.” When you marry you are looking for an aid that can actually help you, not a jump-off: one who values what you bring and values that family and that child. And you don’t treat her like a piece of meat.
- Y’all don’t think Abraham and Sarah argued? Oh yes, they argued. Sarah was unhappy at times: she couldn’t have no kids, and her servant Hagar was mocking her. Tobit’s wife Anna was unhappy when her son didn’t come home for a while.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 26:13-14](/bible/sirach/26#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/sirach/26#v13)</sup> The grace of a wife delighteth her husband, and her discretion will fatten his bones.
    >
    > <sup>[14](/bible/sirach/26#v14)</sup> A silent and loving woman is a gift of the Lord; and there is nothing so much worth as a mind well instructed.

    These are the scriptures that tell what she brings to his bones. “The grace of a wife delighteth her husband”: a wife, not a jump-off, not a hoe. “And her discretion will fatten his bones”: she is not stupid. He lights up, he’s happy; she is adding years to his life. That is what you should be thinking about when you look for a wife. You come to her father and ask for her hand, and the father checks you all the way out; if he finds you worthy, you had better take care of her.
  - **[Ecclesiastes 9:9](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/ecclesiastes/9#v9)</sup> Live joyfully with the wife whom thou lovest all the days of the life of thy vanity, which he hath given thee under the sun, all the days of thy vanity: for that is thy portion in this life, and in thy labour which thou takest under the sun.

    There’s your romance: “Live joyfully with the wife whom thou lovest”. Living joyfully is part of the aging together. “All the days of the life of thy vanity”, because we are here only a fleeting moment, seventy or eighty years at best. All the days God gives you, you should live joyfully with your wife; that is a commandment.
  - **[Proverbs 5:18-19](/bible/proverbs/5#v18)**
    > <sup>[18](/bible/proverbs/5#v18)</sup> Let thy fountain be blessed: and rejoice with the wife of thy youth.
    >
    > <sup>[19](/bible/proverbs/5#v19)</sup> Let her be as the loving hind and pleasant roe; let her breasts satisfy thee at all times; and be thou ravished always with her love.

    “Let thy fountain be blessed: and rejoice with the wife of thy youth. Let her be as the loving hind and pleasant roe”: you want passion in the relationship? Here. “Let her breasts satisfy thee at all times; and be thou ravished always with her love.” To be ravished is to be filled with intense delight and rapture: delight, charm, enthrall, captivate, spellbind, bewitch, fascinate. That goes into the power a woman has in her voice and her conversation.

**[Tobit 8:9](/bible/tobit/8#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/tobit/8#v9)</sup> So they slept both that night. And Raguel arose, and went and made a grave,

- “So they slept both that night.” They had a perfect understanding. They were righteously married; he was not taking her for lust but saw her as his helpmeet, to help him make it to old age together. They woke up and praised the Most High, and that was the beginning of their life: a journey together, she on your team.
- In that union the family and children come, building a nation together, she your helpmeet and you her guide. Challenges will come, bills and such, but because you came together properly you will help each other through the trial. What God has joined together, let not man, sin, lust for other women, or adultery when age sets in put asunder. Wanting somebody younger is all lust. Every man must have his own wife and every wife her own husband. You are supposed to love what is between her ears more than what is between her legs. A beautiful woman cheers the countenance, but nobody wants a pretty dummy.

**[Tobit 8:18-21](/bible/tobit/8#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/tobit/8#v18)</sup> Then Raguel bade his servants to fill the grave.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/tobit/8#v19)</sup> And he kept the wedding feast fourteen days.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/tobit/8#v20)</sup> For before the days of the marriage were finished, Raguel had said unto him by an oath, that he should not depart till the fourteen days of the marriage were expired;
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/tobit/8#v21)</sup> And then he should take the half of his goods, and go in safety to his father; and should have the rest when I and my wife be dead.

- Tobias did not die, so Raguel had the grave filled, kept the wedding feast fourteen days, and swore that Tobias should “take the half of his goods” now, “and should have the rest when I and my wife be dead.” Raguel passed his inheritance to his son-in-law of the same tribe.
