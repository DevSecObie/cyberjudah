Read the chapter → [Acts 10](/bible/acts/10)

This session teaches Acts chapter 10 verse by verse across two classes, and it takes the history further than usual on purpose, so there is no confusion. The chapter is built around Cornelius, the centurion of the Italian band, and the vision given to Peter. Read carelessly, the chapter looks like salvation being thrown open to every nation and permission to eat unclean food. Taught precept upon precept, it is about the northern kingdom. The whole class runs on the split: after Rehoboam and Jeroboam, Israel divided into the southern kingdom (Judah, Benjamin, Levi, the Jews) and the northern kingdom (Ephraim and the rest), and the north went off into idolatry until the Most High broke them, took away their nationality, and scattered them among the heathen so that they became "not a people," the "Gentiles." Cornelius, his vision, and the outpouring of the Holy Ghost are the beginning of the Most High gathering that broken northern kingdom back into the fold through Christ, so that the two sticks become one nation again.

**[Acts 10:1](/bible/acts/10#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/acts/10#v1)</sup> There was a certain man in Cesarea called Cornelius, a centurion of the band called the Italian band,

- There was a certain man in Caesarea called Cornelius, a centurion of the Italian band. When our people read "Italian band" they automatically picture a Caucasian, but Caesarea Maritima sat on a seacoast in the land of Israel, and Cornelius was an Israelite officer in the Roman army, not a man of another nation.
- Jews of the diaspora were fully enmeshed in Roman culture, took Greek and Latin names, and built careers in the Roman military, so a black man, an Israelite, serving under the Italian band is nothing strange. It is the same as so-called black people and Latinos serving in the American military today, from the Buffalo Soldiers to a general like Colin Powell.
- Because the Israelites were scattered through every province and took the names of their captivities, scholars cannot tell a Roman Jewish soldier from a Roman by his name alone, and the New Testament was written in Greek because Israel spoke Greek under the Greeks. Correcting a misheard reference to [Luke 3](/bible/luke/3), the centurions of scripture were often Israelite servants in the army.
  - **[Acts 18:2](/bible/acts/18#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/acts/18#v2)</sup> And found a certain Jew named Aquila, born in Pontus, lately come from Italy, with his wife Priscilla; (because that Claudius had commanded all Jews to depart from Rome:) and came unto them.

    A certain Jew named Aquila, born in Pontus, had lately come from Italy, so it was common for Israelites to live in Italy and Rome.
  - **[Acts 22:27](/bible/acts/22#v27)**

    Paul answers plainly, "I also am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin," so his Roman citizenship never made him a Roman by nation.

**[Acts 10:2](/bible/acts/10#v2)**
> <sup>[2](/bible/acts/10#v2)</sup> A devout man, and one that feared God with all his house, which gave much alms to the people, and prayed to God alway.

- Cornelius was a devout man, one that feared the Most High with all his house, gave much alms, and prayed always. To be devout is to be devout according to the law, a man who keeps the commandments, and to fear the Most High is to keep his commandments.
- The law, the statutes, and the judgments were shown to Jacob and to Israel only, never to any other nation, so a man keeping the commandments and called devout is being marked as an Israelite. Israel followed the nations into idolatry; the nations never turned around and started keeping the law of the Most High.
- Scholars themselves suppose from this language that Cornelius was a Jew or a proselyte instructed in the Jewish religion, because the pagan Romans worshipped the sun, the moon, and gods like Mars, Venus, Jupiter, and Hermes, not the commandments.

- Peter saw heaven opened and a vessel descending like a great sheet knit at the four corners, let down to the earth, full of all manner of fourfooted beasts, wild beasts, creeping things, and fowls of the air. Christians use this to justify eating unclean food, but the vision is symbolic and points back to the northern kingdom.
- The vessel is Israel, swallowed up among the nations. The sheet knit and let down from the four corners is the Israelite diaspora, the outcasts of Israel and the dispersed of Judah gathered from the four corners of the earth, which is why the Most High used a sheet from four corners.
- The beasts, creeping things, and fowls are men, because scripture testifies that men in their estate are as beasts, so the whole allegory is about the state of the northern kingdom, not about food.

    > <sup>[29](/bible/ezekiel/39#v29)</sup> Neither will I hide my face any more from them: for I have poured out my spirit upon the house of Israel, saith the Lord God.

    The Most High pours out his spirit upon the house of Israel and hides his face no more from them.
- Ephraim is the wild olive tree grafted back in among the tribes, partaking of the root and fatness, and Paul warns them not to boast against the branches. The north believed on Christ while much of the south, the Pharisees, stumbled at him, but that is no ground to boast.
  - **[Romans 11:17-21](/bible/romans/11#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/romans/11#v17)</sup> And if some of the branches be broken off, and thou, being a wild olive tree, wert graffed in among them, and with them partakest of the root and fatness of the olive tree;
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/romans/11#v18)</sup> Boast not against the branches. But if thou boast, thou bearest not the root, but the root thee.
    >
    > <sup>[19](/bible/romans/11#v19)</sup> Thou wilt say then, The branches were broken off, that I might be graffed in.
    >
    > <sup>[20](/bible/romans/11#v20)</sup> Well; because of unbelief they were broken off, and thou standest by faith. Be not highminded, but fear:
    >
    > <sup>[21](/bible/romans/11#v21)</sup> For if God spared not the natural branches, take heed lest he also spare not thee.

    The wild olive tree is grafted in among the branches and partakes of the root, but is warned not to boast, for the branches were broken off through unbelief.
  - **[Jeremiah 11:16-17](/bible/jeremiah/11#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/jeremiah/11#v16)</sup> The Lord called thy name, A green olive tree, fair, and of goodly fruit: with the noise of a great tumult he hath kindled fire upon it, and the branches of it are broken.
    >
    > <sup>[17](/bible/jeremiah/11#v17)</sup> For the Lord of hosts, that planted thee, hath pronounced evil against thee, for the evil of the house of Israel and of the house of Judah, which they have done against themselves to provoke me to anger in offering incense unto Baal.

    Israel is the green olive tree whose branches are broken for the evil of the house of Israel and the house of Judah.
- The tongues were known languages, not unknown, as taught in [Acts 2](/bible/acts/2), and Peter asks whether any man can forbid water that these should be baptized, then commands them baptized in the name of the Lord. This is baptism in the name of Christ, and they beg him to tarry certain days.
