This session of the Man Up Mondays series continues the Acts of the Apostles, teaching Acts chapter 11 verse by verse. Chapter 11 is a continuation of chapter 10, where Peter went to Cornelius and the "Gentiles" received the word of God. So the whole question of this chapter is: who are the Gentiles, and who was Cornelius? The class walks the history from Moses through the Assyrian, Babylonian, Persian, Greek, and Roman captivities to show that the "Gentiles" grafted in are the scattered, hidden outcasts of the house of Israel, the same nation the Most High swallowed up among the nations and is now cleansing by the word of Christ.

**[Acts 11:1](/bible/acts/11#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/acts/11#v1)</sup> And the apostles and brethren that were in Judea heard that the Gentiles had also received the word of God.

- The apostles and brethren in Judea heard that the Gentiles had also received the word of God, and now Peter is rehearsing what happened in [Acts 10](/bible/acts/10). The "Gentiles" is a reference to Cornelius, who was not Italian by nation; he was an Israelite who served as a general over a hundred men in the Roman army, just as today there are black generals and black soldiers in the American army, and America is an extension of ancient Rome.

He is a respecter of persons concerning his chosen race, and what Peter means is that God shows no partiality in judgment, whether a man is poor or mighty.

The complaint was about Peter dealing with the Gentiles, meaning the northern kingdom, and there had been contention between the northern and southern kingdom ever since the split under Solomon.
- To understand circumcision and uncircumcision you have to go to the origin: circumcision was the token, the sign, the seal of the covenant the Most High made with Abraham, a visible confirmation carried through all the generations.

  - **[Revelation 11:12](/bible/revelation/11#v12)**

    A great voice from heaven says "Come up hither," and they ascend up to heaven in a cloud, Israel raised up again.

**[Acts 11:11-14](/bible/acts/11#v11)**
> <sup>[11](/bible/acts/11#v11)</sup> And, behold, immediately there were three men already come unto the house where I was, sent from Cesarea unto me.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/acts/11#v12)</sup> And the spirit bade me go with them, nothing doubting. Moreover these six brethren accompanied me, and we entered into the man’s house:
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/acts/11#v13)</sup> And he shewed us how he had seen an angel in his house, which stood and said unto him, Send men to Joppa, and call for Simon, whose surname is Peter;
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/acts/11#v14)</sup> Who shall tell thee words, whereby thou and all thy house shall be saved.

- Peter continues the account: immediately three men came to the house from Caesarea, and the Spirit bade him go with them, nothing doubting. Six brethren went with him into the man's house, and Cornelius told how an angel had stood in his house and directed him to send to Joppa for Simon Peter, who would tell him words whereby he and all his house would be saved.

**[Acts 11:15-17](/bible/acts/11#v15)**
> <sup>[15](/bible/acts/11#v15)</sup> And as I began to speak, the Holy Ghost fell on them, as on us at the beginning.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/acts/11#v16)</sup> Then remembered I the word of the Lord, how that he said, John indeed baptized with water; but ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/acts/11#v17)</sup> Forasmuch then as God gave them the like gift as he did unto us, who believed on the Lord Jesus Christ; what was I, that I could withstand God?

- As Peter began to speak, the Holy Ghost fell on them as it did on the apostles at the beginning, and Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how that John baptized with water but they would be baptized with the Holy Ghost. Since the Most High gave these Israelites the same gift he gave the apostles who believed on Christ, Peter asks what he was that he could withstand God.

**[Acts 11:18](/bible/acts/11#v18)**
> <sup>[18](/bible/acts/11#v18)</sup> When they heard these things, they held their peace, and glorified God, saying, Then hath God also to the Gentiles granted repentance unto life.

- When they heard this they held their peace and glorified God, saying the Most High had also to the Gentiles granted repentance unto life. These Gentiles are the Israelites who took on Gentile customs, whether the northern kingdom broken off before Assyria when Pekah the son of Remaliah conspired against Judah, or Judah who became Gentiles under the Greeks.
  - **[Acts 5:30-31](/bible/acts/5#v30)**
    > <sup>[30](/bible/acts/5#v30)</sup> The God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom ye slew and hanged on a tree.
    >
    > <sup>[31](/bible/acts/5#v31)</sup> Him hath God exalted with his right hand to be a Prince and a Saviour, for to give repentance to Israel, and forgiveness of sins.

    The God of our fathers raised up Jesus and exalted him to be a Prince and a Saviour, to give repentance to Israel, so the repentance goes to Israel.

**[Acts 11:19-20](/bible/acts/11#v19)**
> <sup>[19](/bible/acts/11#v19)</sup> Now they which were scattered abroad upon the persecution that arose about Stephen travelled as far as Phenice, and Cyprus, and Antioch, preaching the word to none but unto the Jews only.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/acts/11#v20)</sup> And some of them were men of Cyprus and Cyrene, which, when they were come to Antioch, spake unto the Grecians, preaching the Lord Jesus.

- Those scattered by the persecution over Stephen traveled as far as Phenice, Cyprus, and Antioch, preaching the word to none but the Jews only, going where the Israelites were scattered. Phenice was the coastal region north of Israel, modern Lebanon; Cyprus is an island in the eastern Mediterranean; and Antioch was a major Roman city in ancient Syria, now Antakya, Turkey.
- The reason they preached to the Jews only is the prophecy that the Most High saves the tents of Judah first.
  - **[Zechariah 12:7](/bible/zechariah/12#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/zechariah/12#v7)</sup> The Lord also shall save the tents of Judah first, that the glory of the house of David and the glory of the inhabitants of Jerusalem do not magnify themselves against Judah.

    The Lord shall save the tents of Judah first, that the house of David not magnify itself against Judah.
- Some of them, men of Cyprus and Cyrene, came to Antioch and spake to the Grecians, preaching the Lord Jesus. Those Grecians, or Hellenists, were Greek-speaking Jews. The Holman Christian Standard Bible calls them Hellenists, and the Zondervan compact Bible dictionary defines a Hellenist as a Jew who made Greek his tongue and adopted Greek ideas and practices.

**[Acts 11:21-26](/bible/acts/11#v21)**
> <sup>[21](/bible/acts/11#v21)</sup> And the hand of the Lord was with them: and a great number believed, and turned unto the Lord.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/acts/11#v22)</sup> Then tidings of these things came unto the ears of the church which was in Jerusalem: and they sent forth Barnabas, that he should go as far as Antioch.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/acts/11#v23)</sup> Who, when he came, and had seen the grace of God, was glad, and exhorted them all, that with purpose of heart they would cleave unto the Lord.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/acts/11#v24)</sup> For he was a good man, and full of the Holy Ghost and of faith: and much people was added unto the Lord.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/acts/11#v25)</sup> Then departed Barnabas to Tarsus, for to seek Saul:
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/acts/11#v26)</sup> And when he had found him, he brought him unto Antioch. And it came to pass, that a whole year they assembled themselves with the church, and taught much people. And the disciples were called Christians first in Antioch.

- The hand of the Lord was with them and a great number believed and turned to the Lord. The church at Jerusalem sent Barnabas, a good man full of the Holy Ghost and faith, who exhorted them to cleave to the Lord, then went to Tarsus to seek Saul and brought him to Antioch, where for a whole year they taught much people.
- The disciples were called Christians first in Antioch, and those disciples were Jews. Christians were not all nations or races as men think today; the Greek word Christianos comes from Christos, the anointed one, so a Christian is a kinsman of the Messiah and a follower of Christ, an anointed one.
  - **[Acts 26:28](/bible/acts/26#v28)**
    > <sup>[28](/bible/acts/26#v28)</sup> Then Agrippa said unto Paul, Almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian.

    Agrippa tells Paul, "Almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian," meaning a follower of the anointed one.
  - **[1 Peter 4:16](/bible/1-peter/4#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/1-peter/4#v16)</sup> Yet if any man suffer as a Christian, let him not be ashamed; but let him glorify God on this behalf.

    If any man suffer as a Christian, let him not be ashamed, but glorify God on this behalf.
- Before the followers of Christ were called Christians in Antioch, they were called "this way."
  - **[Acts 9:2](/bible/acts/9#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/acts/9#v2)</sup> And desired of him letters to Damascus to the synagogues, that if he found any of this way, whether they were men or women, he might bring them bound unto Jerusalem.

    Saul sought letters to bind any of "this way," whether men or women, and bring them to Jerusalem.
  - **[Acts 22:4](/bible/acts/22#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/acts/22#v4)</sup> And I persecuted this way unto the death, binding and delivering into prisons both men and women.

    Paul confesses he persecuted "this way" unto the death, binding men and women into prisons.
  - **[John 14:6](/bible/john/14#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/john/14#v6)</sup> Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.

    Jesus says, "I am the way, the truth, and the life," which is why the followers were called this way.

**[Acts 11:27-30](/bible/acts/11#v27)**
> <sup>[27](/bible/acts/11#v27)</sup> And in these days came prophets from Jerusalem unto Antioch.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/acts/11#v28)</sup> And there stood up one of them named Agabus, and signified by the spirit that there should be great dearth throughout all the world: which came to pass in the days of Claudius Cesar.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/acts/11#v29)</sup> Then the disciples, every man according to his ability, determined to send relief unto the brethren which dwelt in Judea:
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/acts/11#v30)</sup> Which also they did, and sent it to the elders by the hands of Barnabas and Saul.

- In those days prophets came from Jerusalem to Antioch, and one named Agabus signified by the Spirit that there would be a great dearth throughout all the world, which came to pass in the days of Claudius Caesar. This famine is historically documented; Elliott's commentary notes the frequent famines of Claudius's reign, and Josephus records a famine affecting Judea and Syria under the procurator Cuspius Fadus around AD 45, when Queen Helena of Adiabene sent supplies of corn and figs, again giving validity to the Bible as an accurate history book.
- The disciples, every man according to his ability, determined to send relief to the brethren in Judea, which they did, sending it to the elders by the hand of Barnabas and Saul. That is the end of [Acts 11](/bible/acts/11).
