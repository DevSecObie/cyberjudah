**[Acts 17:18](/bible/acts/17#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/acts/17#v18)</sup> Then certain philosophers of the Epicureans, and of the Stoicks, encountered him. And some said, What will this babbler say? other some, He seemeth to be a setter forth of strange gods: because he preached unto them Jesus, and the resurrection.

- “Then certain philosophers of the Epicureans, and of the Stoicks, encountered him.” These are Greek philosophers, and they are in the Bible. Justin Martyr spent considerable time with the Stoics and was educated in Platonism. He believed he could be a good Roman and a Christian at the same time, and he introduced the whole idea of Romans and Christians coming together, and he got killed for it; most of these church fathers were groomed in Greco-Roman ideology, Platonism, neoplatonism and Greek philosophy, and formulated ways to interpret the Bible through that education. “And some said, What will this babbler say?” That is what they said about Paul. These debates are Mars’ hill all over again: philosophers back on earth again.

**[Acts 17:21-22](/bible/acts/17#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/acts/17#v21)</sup> (For all the Athenians and strangers which were there spent their time in nothing else, but either to tell, or to hear some new thing.)
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/acts/17#v22)</sup> Then Paul stood in the midst of Mars’ hill, and said, Ye men of Athens, I perceive that in all things ye are too superstitious.

- “For all the Athenians and strangers which were there spent their time in nothing else, but either to tell, or to hear some new thing.” “Then Paul stood in the midst of Mars’ hill”. That is all they want: to hear something new, some new doctrine, some new philosophy.
- This whole doctrine is centuries of Mars’ hill. Athenagoras was of Athens; Basil and Gregory were educated at Athens; Clement called philosophy the Greeks’ preparation for Christian truth; Tertullian, who coined Trinitas, substantia and persona, was raised in the Greco-Roman intellectual environment; Origen theorized ousia and hypostasis. That is what gave birth to the Catholic Church we know today and birthed Western Christianity: Judaism and sun worship, Greco-Roman ideology, merged as one.
- The formula they came up with is not Bible; it is a philosophical discussion on how to interpret the Bible through a Greco-Roman lens. One ousia, essence, and three hypostases, persons: God is one what and three whos. That is Dr. Seuss; their god is from Whoville. It is a two-faced Harvey Dent deity, the Father on one side, the Son on the other, and the Holy Spirit the coin that makes the decisions. If you have identical triplets, that is three persons, but in this ideology one plus one plus one is one. And the hypostatic union, fully God and fully man, makes Christ a demigod, half a man and half a god, like Hercules: one side doesn’t know when he is coming back, and he emptied himself like a teakettle. Not a single biblical writer says Christ is two natures in one person. The words ousia and hypostasis are in the Greek, but they changed their meanings over centuries of controversy.
- The Council of Nicaea did not create Christ; he died long before it. It argued whether he is the Father or the Son, against him being subject to his Father, and the Holy Spirit was nowhere to be found. They had to keep adding to it because it made no sense: Basil added the Holy Spirit, and before Constantinople in 381 AD Gregory himself admits some called the Holy Spirit an activity, some a creature, some God, and others were undecided. They didn’t use the Bible; they were making it up as they went. How are you debating your God? God is clear, simple and plain, and God is not the author of confusion; this is centuries of confusion. Even the Catholic catechism says the church had to develop its own terminology with the help of notions of philosophical origin, substance and person. They used Greek philosophy to explain what the Bible already says. It is Mars’ hill all over again.
