## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Acts 20 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-5, 21-24, 29-32, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Acts 20:1-3](/bible/acts/20#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/acts/20#v1)</sup> And after the uproar was ceased, Paul called unto him the disciples, and embraced them, and departed for to go into Macedonia.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/acts/20#v2)</sup> And when he had gone over those parts, and had given them much exhortation, he came into Greece,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/acts/20#v3)</sup> And there abode three months. And when the Jews laid wait for him, as he was about to sail into Syria, he purposed to return through Macedonia.

- Revelation 3 is part two of the message to the seven churches, and it opens with a little history regarding the seven churches out of the book of Acts. If you have never read the book of Acts you are not going to understand the epistles of Paul, and you are not going to understand the opening chapters of Revelation either. This is the establishing of the seven churches of Asia Minor: Pergamos, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia, Laodicea, Smyrna and Ephesus, the green area on the map. We are going to find out who established them.
- “And after the uproar was ceased, Paul called unto him the disciples, and embraced them, and departed for to go into Macedonia. And when he had gone over those parts, and had given them much exhortation, he came into Greece, And there abode three months.” You see that: he and they abode three months. Captains, we have to get on this level. We might not be able to stay a year, two years, three, Lord's will in the future, but at least three months. We stayed one month in Sierra Leone, and the brothers and sisters that helped us do that are definitely going to get their blessing when the Lord returns, and he may be giving you blessings right now.
- “And when the Jews laid wait for him, as he was about to sail into Syria, he purposed to return through Macedonia.”
- “Paul called unto him the disciples”: remember, the word disciple means students.

**[Acts 20:4-5](/bible/acts/20#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/acts/20#v4)</sup> And there accompanied him into Asia Sopater of Berea; and of the Thessalonians, Aristarchus and Secundus; and Gaius of Derbe, and Timotheus; and of Asia, Tychicus and Trophimus.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/acts/20#v5)</sup> These going before tarried for us at Troas.

- “And there accompanied him into Asia Sopater of Berea; and of the Thessalonians, Aristarchus and Secundus; and Gaius of Derbe, and Timotheus”, that is Timothy, “and of Asia, Tychicus and Trophimus.”
- Paul had a team of men that he travelled with to teach the gospel. Most of these men we don't see named much in Acts, except Timothy, but they were there helping Paul and Timothy spread the gospel. It is nothing different than what we are doing today.
- “And there accompanied him into Asia” This is what we want: there accompanied him into Asia, that green area, Asia or Asia Minor. “Sopater of Berea; and of the Thessalonians, Aristarchus and Secundus; and Gaius of Derbe, and Timotheus; and of Asia, Tychicus and Trophimus.” Those names mentioned that went with Paul to Asia Minor are the names of the men that helped Paul set up Asia Minor. Sopater was one of the Bereans that believed (Acts 17:10-12). Aristarchus and Secundus were of the believers out of Thessalonica (Acts 17:1-4). Gaius and Timotheus were of Derbe, where Paul and Barnabas preached the gospel (Acts 14:20-21; Acts 16:1). Tychicus and Trophimus were of Asia, where all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus, both Jews and Greeks (Acts 19:10).
- When you read the term Greeks, most Christians will say, see, it is for all nations. But the Bible shows who the Greeks were: 2 Maccabees 4 and 6 show wicked men like Jason who made a covenant with the Greeks to bring the Israelites to the Greekish fashion, and laws under which it was not lawful to keep sabbath days or to profess yourself at all to be a Jew, and whoso would not conform to the manners of the Gentiles was put to death. That is the history the Christians are unaware of. And the white scholars know who those Greeks were. The Encyclopedia of the Jewish Diaspora, Origins, Experiences and Culture, volume one, edited by Avrum Ehrlich, one of the books they use in the top theology schools, says that in spite of attempts to show that the Jews in the western diaspora knew Hebrew, it is common knowledge that basically they knew only Greek; the Jews of the West knew neither Hebrew nor Aramaic, and their religious lives, including the reading of the Torah and prayer, were conducted solely in Greek. The Torah was translated into Greek in the third century BCE. In certain rabbinic circles the translation into Greek was viewed as a necessity of the times: the sages recognized that there were entire diaspora communities that spoke only Greek, and that these would be lost to the Jewish people in the absence of an authentic translation. That is why a lot of communities of the Israelites only spoke Greek; they were forced into that. And rabbinic literature almost totally ignores the vast western diaspora that existed at the time, which is particularly noticeable compared with the relatively large number of places in this diaspora mentioned in chapter 2 of Acts, in the chapters describing the journeys of Paul, and in the Pauline epistles, which give a comprehensive picture of the geography of the western Jewish Greek-speaking diaspora in the first century. So the scholars know that Paul was not going to all nations; Paul was going to the Greek-speaking Jews. This is the history our people don't know. You sit in church and be a dummy, a donkey, the Donkey of the Day, not knowing what this history is talking about.

**[Acts 20:22](/bible/acts/20#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/acts/20#v22)</sup> And now, behold, I go bound in the spirit unto Jerusalem, not knowing the things that shall befall me there:

- “And now, behold, I go bound in the spirit unto Jerusalem, not knowing the things that shall befall me there.” Paul was going to Jerusalem not knowing what would happen; they might kill him. When you leave your house, you are traveling. Once you say you want to travel internationally with the brothers and are given a good report, understand there is a chance you might not come back. Always keep that in your head: the minute you forget it you let your guard down, and remembering it keeps you sharp. It is the same every Saturday you go to camp; there is a chance you might not come back.
- Paul did not know what would happen, but he went anyway, because that is what he was called to do. That is what you were called to do. None of us know whether death will come in America or in South America.
