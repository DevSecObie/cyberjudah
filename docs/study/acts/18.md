## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Acts 18 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-2, 8-11, 21-28, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Acts 18:1-2](/bible/acts/18#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/acts/18#v1)</sup> After these things Paul departed from Athens, and came to Corinth;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/acts/18#v2)</sup> And found a certain Jew named Aquila, born in Pontus, lately come from Italy, with his wife Priscilla; (because that Claudius had commanded all Jews to depart from Rome:) and came unto them.

- Paul departed from Athens, came to Corinth, “And found a certain Jew named Aquila, born in Pontus, lately come from Italy, with his wife Priscilla; (because that Claudius had commanded all Jews to depart from Rome:)”. Rome commanded all the Israelites, all the Jews, to leave Rome. That is mass deportation.
- The same thing Claudius Caesar did back then is what we see Trump doing today. The immigration raids are not raiding Europeans; it is the black and Hispanic communities they are going after. Aquila and Priscilla were deported from Rome under Claudius. That deportation only lasted a while, and afterwards the Jews were able to go back to Rome.
- So do not be afraid of ICE and of what is going on with deportations. Everything that happens is of the Lord, and a man’s goings are of the Lord. Whatever happens to you, the Lord allowed it to happen. If they take away your green card or your citizenship and send you back to your country, it is what it is: it is of the Lord. Go over there and teach the word.
- There is nothing new under the sun; the same thing that was done is the same thing that shall be done. “Because that Claudius had commanded all Jews to depart from Rome.” Trump said there will be mass deportation, and Claudius Caesar deported every last Jew living in Rome: all of you get out of my country, we don’t want you here. It is the same thing you are seeing today.

  Precepts:
  - **[Acts 13:1](/bible/acts/13#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/acts/13#v1)</sup> Now there were in the church that was at Antioch certain prophets and teachers; as Barnabas, and Simeon that was called Niger, and Lucius of Cyrene, and Manaen, which had been brought up with Herod the tetrarch, and Saul.

    Who were the Jews? “Now there were in the church that was at Antioch certain prophets and teachers; as Barnabas, and Simeon that was called Niger.” Barnabas was a Jew, and Simeon was a Jew that was called Niger, and Niger means black. So the Jews deported from Rome were black. That is where the word comes from.
  - **[Acts 18:18](/bible/acts/18#v18)**
    > <sup>[18](/bible/acts/18#v18)</sup> And Paul after this tarried there yet a good while, and then took his leave of the brethren, and sailed thence into Syria, and with him Priscilla and Aquila; having shorn his head in Cenchrea: for he had a vow.

    “And Paul after this tarried there yet a good while, and then took his leave of the brethren, and sailed thence into Syria, and with him Priscilla and Aquila”. What were Priscilla and Aquila doing after they were deported from Rome? They went forth evangelizing with Paul. They were doing the work, teaching the word of God in other regions, and that is how they met Paul.
  - **[Acts 18:26](/bible/acts/18#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/acts/18#v26)</sup> And he began to speak boldly in the synagogue: whom when Aquila and Priscilla had heard, they took him unto them, and expounded unto him the way of God more perfectly.

    “And he began to speak boldly in the synagogue: whom when Aquila and Priscilla had heard, they took him unto them, and expounded unto him the way of God more perfectly.” This was Apollos. He knew only John, but he was teaching Christ, and Aquila and Priscilla brought him in and expounded to him the teachings of Christ more perfectly.

**[Acts 18:8](/bible/acts/18#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/acts/18#v8)</sup> And Crispus, the chief ruler of the synagogue, believed on the Lord with all his house; and many of the Corinthians hearing believed, and were baptized.

- “And Crispus, the chief ruler of the synagogue, believed on the Lord with all his house; and many of the Corinthians hearing believed, and were baptized.”

**[Acts 18:9](/bible/acts/18#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/acts/18#v9)</sup> Then spake the Lord to Paul in the night by a vision, Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace:

- “Then spake the Lord to Paul in the night by a vision, Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace.” The Lord came to Paul in a vision and strengthened him not to be afraid, but to speak. When you travel to these places, because of the influx of a certain religion, of the heathen, you may feel discouraged and think the mission will not prosper. That is not why the Lord sent you there. He sent you to do as he commanded.
- That is why getting built up before going is so important. Going into other lands, you learn from men who have already been to similar regions and can give you the strategies of war. We are at war, whether you know it or not, and a war has been waged against you since the day you were in the womb. They say the most dangerous place for a Black child is its mother’s womb; they push abortion in our communities. When you were little and heard all the jingle bell songs in the store, they were warring with you. So the Lord strengthened Paul, because there was a mission he was sending him on.

**[Acts 18:10](/bible/acts/18#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/acts/18#v10)</sup> For I am with thee, and no man shall set on thee to hurt thee: for I have much people in this city.

- “For I am with thee.” As dangerous as many of the places we go to are, the Lord is with us. “And no man shall set on thee to hurt thee”: no man will hurt you to the point that the mission is hindered.
- “For I have much people in this city.” We go to places we have never touched down before: Zanzibar, Madagascar, a place known only from a cartoon. Seeing the brothers marching through Madagascar strengthened us. The Lord used them to strengthen us, as brothers go into lands we only thought were stories in books, and they are real places.

**[Acts 18:11](/bible/acts/18#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/acts/18#v11)</sup> And he continued there a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.

- “And he continued there a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.” The Lord strengthened him, and he did not hold back: he taught the commandments of God and he taught Christ. That is where we are going. The next mission will not be two weeks or four weeks; the bar is raised for every leader. The diaspora is scattered to the four corners of the earth, and who is going to raise them up? We did not come here just for a Sabbath sandwich. There are regions not yet visited, and places with a congregation but no established sanctuary, that need brothers to go there and teach. We are reading and living the Acts of the Apostles.

**[Acts 18:21-24](/bible/acts/18#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/acts/18#v21)</sup> But bade them farewell, saying, I must by all means keep this feast that cometh in Jerusalem: but I will return again unto you, if God will. And he sailed from Ephesus.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/acts/18#v22)</sup> And when he had landed at Cesarea, and gone up, and saluted the church, he went down to Antioch.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/acts/18#v23)</sup> And after he had spent some time there, he departed, and went over all the country of Galatia and Phrygia in order, strengthening all the disciples.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/acts/18#v24)</sup> And a certain Jew named Apollos, born at Alexandria, an eloquent man, and mighty in the scriptures, came to Ephesus.

- Ephesus was the most important Greek city in Ionia, in Asia Minor; its ruins lie near Selçuk in western Turkey, and it owed much of its fame to the temple of Artemis, or Diana. Ashtaroth, the Canaanite fertility figure, has been found throughout Israel from 1400 BC through the conquest, the judges and the monarchy, confirming the Old Testament. Here we read of wicked Diana.
- Paul “sailed from Ephesus”, and then “a certain Jew named Apollos, born at Alexandria, an eloquent man, and mighty in the scriptures, came to Ephesus.”
- “And after he had spent some time there, he departed, and went over all the country of Galatia and Phrygia in order, strengthening all the disciples.” Paul did not stop at Corinth; he went on to Ephesus, and over all the country of Galatia, to strengthen the disciples. The Lord told him in Corinth not to be afraid, for he had much people in that city. We go to these strange lands with all confidence that God’s people are there. The Lord did not send us into a barren land not occupied by the children of Israel.
- Look at the faces of the children at the school in Tanzania, lined up: those are the children of Israel. Camps that say Israelites are only in America are crazy as hell. Tanzania and Zanzibar are 99% Muslim. They had never heard that Jesus Christ was Black, that the Israelites are Black, or that we should keep the commandments of God, and now those children are asking where they can go to hear more. They want to be strengthened by the word of God. That is the men going all over the world to where Christ has never been taught.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Corinthians 14:40](/bible/1-corinthians/14#v40)**
    > <sup>[40](/bible/1-corinthians/14#v40)</sup> Let all things be done decently and in order.

    “Let all things be done decently and in order.” Paul went over the country “in order”. Some brothers and sisters just up and move to a strange land all of a sudden. They are not building up the men, they are not built up themselves, and it causes confusion.

**[Acts 18:25](/bible/acts/18#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/acts/18#v25)</sup> This man was instructed in the way of the Lord; and being fervent in the spirit, he spake and taught diligently the things of the Lord, knowing only the baptism of John.

- “This man was instructed in the way of the Lord; and being fervent in the spirit”. Fervent means fire: he was burning in the spirit, zealous, he had zeal. He was fired up to go out there and teach. When you read, you’ve got to understand what you’re reading. So not only was he an eloquent man, good in the scriptures, but he was fervent in the spirit; he was going out there going crazy teaching the people, the same way John the Revelator says he was in the spirit on the Lord’s day.
- “He spake and taught diligently the things of the Lord, knowing only the baptism of John.” This is important. By this time the disciples had already been rolling with Christ, and Christ was already dead. So he was teaching, but teaching knowing only the baptism of John: he knew about the water baptism, the baptism where you had to confess your sins. That’s what sends us to Matthew 3, to see what John’s baptism was.
- “This man was instructed in the way of the Lord; and being fervent in the spirit, he spake and taught diligently the things of the Lord, knowing only the baptism of John.” All of that, John’s baptism of confessing sins, Abraham’s seed, the bloodline, is what Apollos had in him as a man mighty in the scriptures.

**[Acts 18:26](/bible/acts/18#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/acts/18#v26)</sup> And he began to speak boldly in the synagogue: whom when Aquila and Priscilla had heard, they took him unto them, and expounded unto him the way of God more perfectly.

- “And he began to speak boldly in the synagogue: whom when Aquila and Priscilla had heard, they took him unto them, and expounded unto him the way of God more perfectly”. When they heard Apollos they said, man, this guy’s strong, but he’s missing something. So they sat him down: brother, you’re something else, but do you know this? They told him about Christ; he didn’t know nothing about Christ, all he knew was John’s baptism. They sat him down and said, listen, this is what happened, this is the story. And Apollos didn’t say, listen, I know the scriptures already, you can’t tell me a damn thing. He received it, and he went out there and started teaching it.

**[Acts 18:27](/bible/acts/18#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/acts/18#v27)</sup> And when he was disposed to pass into Achaia, the brethren wrote, exhorting the disciples to receive him: who, when he was come, helped them much which had believed through grace:

- “And when he was disposed to pass into Achaia, the brethren wrote, exhorting the disciples to receive him”. He was so strong they said, listen, when Apollos comes through, take him; that brother is serious. “Who, when he was come, helped them much which had believed through grace”.

**[Acts 18:28](/bible/acts/18#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/acts/18#v28)</sup> For he mightily convinced the Jews, and that publickly, shewing by the scriptures that Jesus was Christ.

- “For he mightily convinced the Jews”. How do you convince somebody mightily if you’re not pulling out proof? If you’re just talking anything, that just sparks a thought. “And that publickly, shewing by the scriptures that Jesus was Christ.” This past week it actually hit me right there: shewing by the scriptures that Jesus was Christ. How many times have we actually done that, used the scriptures to prove that Christ was exactly who we say he is? This is what that brother was doing.
- Was the New Testament available? No, it wasn’t. So he was using the Old Testament. If you were told right now to go into the Bible and prove publicly, mightily, by the scriptures that Jesus is the Christ, what would you pull out? You would have to go to Isaiah and Ezekiel, which have prophecies of Christ, and he’s in Psalms, he’s all over Genesis. That’s all Apollos could go on. That thing is heavy.
