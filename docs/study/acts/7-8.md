## Introduction

This session continues the Man Up Mondays series through the Acts of the Apostles, covering chapters 7 and 8 in one sitting. Chapter 7 is Stephen standing before the council of the Pharisees and Sadducees, rehearsing the whole history of the fathers off the top of his head, from Abraham in Mesopotamia down to Moses and the prophets, to bring the audience up to Christ, the prophet Moses said the Most High would raise up. It ends with Stephen charging them as stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, seeing Christ standing on the right hand of God, and being stoned while Saul consents to his death. Chapter 8 opens with the great persecution that scatters the disciples abroad, and follows Philip into Samaria and down to the Ethiopian eunuch, teaching heavily on the two baptisms, the name of Jesus in place of animal sacrifice, and the remnant of Israel scattered among the nations. The thread of the whole class is the spirit: the Holy Ghost is first and foremost the laws, and the same persecution and the same work that happened back then is what comes on the scene again today.

> <sup>[1](/bible/acts/7#v1)</sup> Then said the high priest, Are these things so?
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/acts/7#v2)</sup> And he said, Men, brethren, and fathers, hearken; The God of glory appeared unto our father Abraham, when he was in Mesopotamia, before he dwelt in Charran,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/acts/7#v3)</sup> And said unto him, Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and come into the land which I shall shew thee.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/acts/7#v4)</sup> Then came he out of the land of the Chaldeans, and dwelt in Charran: and from thence, when his father was dead, he removed him into this land, wherein ye now dwell.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/acts/7#v5)</sup> And he gave him none inheritance in it, no, not so much as to set his foot on: yet he promised that he would give it to him for a possession, and to his seed after him, when as yet he had no child.

- This is Stephen at the council, addressing the audience just before he is put to death. He answers the high priest by going all the way back to the beginning: the God of glory appeared unto our father Abraham when he was in Mesopotamia, before he dwelt in Charran.
- Abraham came out of the land of the Chaldeans, dwelt in Charran, and after his father died the Most High removed him into this land wherein they now dwell, and gave him no inheritance in it, not so much as to set his foot on, yet promised to give it to him and his seed after him when as yet he had no child.
- Notice how Stephen recites this history off the top of his head. Our forefathers read more than their four chapters a day. When you read, you put these things to memory, so you can recite it. A lot of us cannot recite our own history, but we can recite LeBron James and the NBA stats, football stats. These men were studious; they constantly gave attendance to studying. They called Paul mad for reading so many letters. Our forefathers were addicted to this gospel.

- When Moses was full forty years old, it came into his heart to visit his brethren the children of Israel. Contrary to the movie The Ten Commandments, Moses always knew who he was; his own mother raised and nursed him, so he knew his brothers, he was only raised under the household of the Egyptians.
- Seeing one of them suffer wrong, he defended him and smote the Egyptian, supposing his brethren would understand that the Most High by his hand would deliver them, but they understood not. The next day, striving to make peace, he was thrust away with, Who made thee a ruler and a judge over us?
- Only the Most High could set a man to judge. This is the crabs in a barrel spirit: even after you help somebody, they say, Who are you to be over us? Everybody wants to be the head, the chief, a leader, but nobody wants to be a follower, and nobody wants to give credit when credit is due. The Most High was using the status Moses had in Egypt to help his brother, and they used it against him: Wilt thou kill me as thou didst the Egyptian yesterday?
- Certain of us will be put in different positions in this society, in politics, in education, and the Most High will use those accolades to your advantage, the same way he used Moses to go to Pharaoh and not be put to death right off the bat. Then Moses fled and was a stranger in the land of Midian, understanding that if he stayed among his own people they would get him killed, and there he begat two sons.
- People use the excuse that Moses married an Ethiopian to justify interracial marriage, saying maybe the Lord would forgive them the same way. But the Most High says, I will have mercy on whom I will have mercy. You are not Moses. Ask Aaron and Miriam, whom the Most High plagued in the skin and put out of the camp seven days for speaking against a matter that did not concern them.
- Do not point fingers and compare yourself to the person next to you, wondering why one brother is judged for a matter and another is treated differently. Different judgments belong to the Most High. You are not David; do not weigh Saul's sins against David's and assume you know how the Most High will judge, the way men questioned why Uzza was struck for touching the ark. Work out your own soul salvation with fear and trembling, study to be quiet, and mind your own business.

Stephen rehearsed all the history to bring them up to Christ, because the whole ministry throughout Acts is Christ's name preached in the place of animal sacrifice. Even as Pharisees, they knew this prophecy, so he made it plain: the prophet Moses spoke about is that Christ.

# Acts 8: The Word Scattered Abroad, and the Baptism unto the Conscience

Read the chapter → [Acts 8](/bible/acts/8)

**[Acts 8:1](/bible/acts/8#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/acts/8#v1)</sup> And Saul was consenting unto his death. And at that time there was a great persecution against the church which was at Jerusalem; and they were all scattered abroad throughout the regions of Judea and Samaria, except the apostles.

- Saul was consenting unto Stephen's death. This is Saul who becomes Paul, part of the same people persecuting the church, killing disciples and women, thinking he was doing it for the right reasons. That is why the scripture says judge no one before their time; a man as evil as Saul was doing back there became Paul the apostle.
- At that time there was a great persecution against the church at Jerusalem, and they were all scattered abroad throughout Judea and Samaria, except the apostles. Notice that unlike the other epistles, the book of Acts never closes with Amen, showing there is a continuation: the apostles come back on the scene and redo the Acts all over again, so the things that happened back then are the things that happen today.
- There will be a great persecution today. This gospel is a witness against the whole world. When you pray thy kingdom come, thy will be done, you are saying the Most High's will overpowers America's will, Russia's, China's, every nation racing for world domination. To preach a black Christ in the streets is to speak against the system that was designed to keep our people debased, a Christ with many crowns upon his head who takes the crowns of all the nations. They are not going to let a people they despise rise up and preach a gospel that overthrows the world superpowers and do nothing; it is going to be a great persecution. Gird your mind, study, pray, and apply, because wisdom and knowledge will be the stability of our time.

> <sup>[30](/bible/acts/8#v30)</sup> And Philip ran thither to him, and heard him read the prophet Esaias, and said, Understandest thou what thou readest?
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/acts/8#v31)</sup> And he said, How can I, except some man should guide me? And he desired Philip that he would come up and sit with him.

- The eunuch was returning, sitting in his chariot, reading Esaias the prophet, and the Spirit said to Philip, Go near and join thyself to this chariot. Philip ran and heard him read Isaiah and asked, Understandest thou what thou readest? The eunuch answered, How can I, except some man should guide me, and desired Philip to come up and sit with him.
- Notice the humility of the brother. Rather than making his own assumptions, he admitted he did not understand. Some brothers and sisters lack that humility, claiming, I always knew I was Israel, or, the Most High spoke to me while I was in my sins, rather than saying, This is the first time I heard this, teach me. You had to be taught this Bible to know this Bible; the Most High is not just going to hand it to you out of nowhere. Expressing that you do not know keeps you in a low state and away from the spirit of preeminence and vainglory.

**[Acts 8:32-35](/bible/acts/8#v32)**

> <sup>[32](/bible/acts/8#v32)</sup> The place of the scripture which he read was this, He was led as a sheep to the slaughter; and like a lamb dumb before his shearer, so opened he not his mouth:
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/acts/8#v33)</sup> In his humiliation his judgment was taken away: and who shall declare his generation? for his life is taken from the earth.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/acts/8#v34)</sup> And the eunuch answered Philip, and said, I pray thee, of whom speaketh the prophet this? of himself, or of some other man?
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/acts/8#v35)</sup> Then Philip opened his mouth, and began at the same scripture, and preached unto him Jesus.

- The place of the scripture he read was Isaiah: He was led as a sheep to the slaughter, and like a lamb dumb before his shearer, so opened he not his mouth; in his humiliation his judgment was taken away, and who shall declare his generation, for his life is taken from the earth.
- The eunuch asked Philip, Of whom speaketh the prophet this, of himself or of some other man? He did not know, so rather than assume or give his own interpretation, he asked. That is how you should handle what you do not know: write it down and ask, rather than making an assumption and teaching others, which is how heresies come into the body. Then Philip opened his mouth, began at the same scripture, and preached unto him Jesus, because Philip had the Holy Ghost, wisdom, and understanding, having learned under the feet of the apostles.

**[Acts 8:36-38](/bible/acts/8#v36)**

> <sup>[36](/bible/acts/8#v36)</sup> And as they went on their way, they came unto a certain water: and the eunuch said, See, here is water; what doth hinder me to be baptized?
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/acts/8#v37)</sup> And Philip said, If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest. And he answered and said, I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God.
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/acts/8#v38)</sup> And he commanded the chariot to stand still: and they went down both into the water, both Philip and the eunuch; and he baptized him.

- As they went on their way they came to a certain water, and the eunuch said, See, here is water, what doth hinder me to be baptized? Philip said, If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest. He answered, I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God, and he commanded the chariot to stand still, and they went down both into the water, and Philip baptized him.
- This seems to contradict everything taught earlier about Christ's baptism not being water. Philip baptized the eunuch in water because, like Ezra and others, some of the disciples came from John the Baptist's teaching and held to his water baptism; John understood his own place and told his disciples to follow Christ, who must increase while he decreased.
