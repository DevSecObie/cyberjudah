Read the chapter → [Acts 1](/bible/acts/1)

This is the opening of the Man Up Monday series through the Acts of the Apostles, which will lead afterward into the letters of Paul. Many brothers and sisters are approached by Christians who cannot make sense of Paul's letters, and the reason is that those letters are built upon the writings of Acts. Studying Acts gives the clarity needed to answer the Christians and to understand Paul. This session covers [Acts 1:1](/bible/acts/1#v1) through 1:14: Luke's account written to Theophilus, the forty days Christ was seen after his passion, the commandment to wait in Jerusalem for the promise of the Holy Ghost, the ascension by chariot, and the eleven gathered with the women and Mary in the upper room.

**[Acts 1:1](/bible/acts/1#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/acts/1#v1)</sup> The former treatise have I made, O Theophilus, of all that Jesus began both to do and teach,

- Luke wrote both the Gospel of Luke and the book of Acts. The "former treatise" is the Gospel of Luke, which was also addressed to Theophilus, so the two books are two letters from the same hand.
- The scriptures we have are a collection of the apostles' teaching passed down to the young men under them, the seventy, and the other disciples. Luke was not one of the twelve, though it is speculated he was of the seventy, and he was there from the beginning. Just as the bishops and elders pass down history to us today so that we can speak on it though we were not there, that knowledge was passed down to Luke.
- Theophilus' name means love of God. He was an Israelite, not a gentile, and Luke addresses him as "most excellent," a title of nobility or government office, the same way Paul addressed "most noble Festus."

    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/luke/1#v4)</sup> That thou mightest know the certainty of those things, wherein thou hast been instructed.

    Many took in hand to set forth in order a declaration of the things surely believed, delivered by eyewitnesses and ministers of the word; Luke had perfect understanding of all things from the very first.
  - **[Colossians 4:14](/bible/colossians/4#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/colossians/4#v14)</sup> Luke, the beloved physician, and Demas, greet you.

    Paul knew Luke personally and calls him the beloved physician, showing Luke was present throughout the ministry.
  - **[2 Timothy 4:11](/bible/2-timothy/4#v11)**

    Only Luke is with me, take Mark; Luke traveled with Paul, and Mark is the one who wrote the Gospel of Mark.

**[Acts 1:2](/bible/acts/1#v2)**
> <sup>[2](/bible/acts/1#v2)</sup> Until the day in which he was taken up, after that he through the Holy Ghost had given commandments unto the apostles whom he had chosen:

- Luke gave a full historical account of everything Christ did, from his birth to the day he was taken up, and records that Christ, through the Holy Ghost, gave commandments to the apostles he had chosen.
- The end of Luke segues straight into Acts. The commandment was that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.
- "All nations" does not mean the Chinese or the Japanese. Prophetically it means the Israelites scattered among all nations: into Assyria (which is why Jonah preached to Nineveh, where the northern kingdom was exiled), then Babylon, Persia, Media, Greece, and Rome, then into Africa and out through the transatlantic and Indian Ocean slave trades. That prophecy did not happen only once; it happened all through history.

    >
    > <sup>[47](/bible/luke/24#v47)</sup> And that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.

    Thus it behoved Christ to suffer and rise the third day, and that repentance and remission of sins should be preached among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.

    > <sup>[27](/bible/deuteronomy/4#v27)</sup> And the Lord shall scatter you among the nations, and ye shall be left few in number among the heathen, whither the Lord shall lead you.

    The Most High said he would scatter Israel among the nations and leave them few in number among the heathen; this is the scattering that placed our people among every captivity.
  - **[Acts 2:5](/bible/acts/2#v5)**

    There were dwelling at Jerusalem Jews out of every nation under heaven, because every nation that ruled over Israel carried them off into exile.

**[Acts 1:3](/bible/acts/1#v3)**
> <sup>[3](/bible/acts/1#v3)</sup> To whom also he shewed himself alive after his passion by many infallible proofs, being seen of them forty days, and speaking of the things pertaining to the kingdom of God:

- After his passion Christ showed himself alive by many infallible proofs, being seen forty days and speaking of the things pertaining to the kingdom of the Most High. He did not appear for only a day or two; he stayed forty days to open the understanding further.
- Within that time he was seen by many. Cephas is another name for Peter and means stone.

    He was seen of Cephas, then of the twelve, then of above five hundred brethren at once, then of James, then of all the apostles, and last of all of Paul, as one born out of due time.

**[Acts 1:4](/bible/acts/1#v4)**
> <sup>[4](/bible/acts/1#v4)</sup> And, being assembled together with them, commanded them that they should not depart from Jerusalem, but wait for the promise of the Father, which, saith he, ye have heard of me.

- The commandment was to not depart from Jerusalem but to wait for the promise of the Father, which is the Holy Ghost, given only after Christ was glorified.
- Christ could teach only so much in three years because a man can bear only so much. The Comforter would open their understanding in increments after his death, guiding them into all truth and bringing his words back to their remembrance.
- The Holy Ghost comes in an order that begins with the law: turn at his reproof and keep the commandments, and then he pours out his spirit and opens the understanding. Only the Most High can give the wisdom to interpret this book, which is the most purchased yet least understood book on the earth.
  - **[John 16:7](/bible/john/16#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/john/16#v7)</sup> Nevertheless I tell you the truth; It is expedient for you that I go away: for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you; but if I depart, I will send him unto you.

    It is expedient that Christ go away, for if he does not depart the Comforter will not come; he had to die for the Holy Ghost to be given.

    The Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, shall teach them all things and bring all things to their remembrance.

    Turn you at my reproof, and I will pour out my spirit unto you; the first stipulation is turning at his reproof.

    The stiffnecked resist the Holy Ghost; the first form of the Holy Ghost is the law, which they received and did not keep.

**[Acts 1:5](/bible/acts/1#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/acts/1#v5)</sup> For John truly baptized with water; but ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost not many days hence.

- John truly baptized with water, but they would be baptized with the Holy Ghost not many days hence. There is a distinction between what John taught and what Christ brought.
- Baptism comes from the word baptizo, out of the purification laws. John was of the tribe of Levi, his father Zacharias a priest and his mother Elizabeth of the daughters of Aaron, so his water baptism was always symbolic, a preparation leading up to Christ.
- John said he must decrease and Christ must increase, and that happens in increments. Two of John's own disciples heard Christ and followed him, which is why in Acts the disciples were still baptizing with water even though Christ himself baptized not with water but with the Holy Ghost. When you believe on Christ as the scripture says, rivers of living water flow out of your belly, meaning wisdom and understanding flow out of the mind that consumes and filters information; but that Holy Ghost was not yet given because Jesus was not yet glorified.
  - **[Matthew 3:11](/bible/matthew/3#v11)**
    > <sup>[11](/bible/matthew/3#v11)</sup> I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance: but he that cometh after me is mightier than I, whose shoes I am not worthy to bear: he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost, and with fire:

    John baptized with water unto repentance, but he that came after him would baptize with the Holy Ghost and with fire.
  - **[Luke 1:5](/bible/luke/1#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/luke/1#v5)</sup> There was in the days of Herod, the king of Judea, a certain priest named Zacharias, of the course of Abia: and his wife was of the daughters of Aaron, and her name was Elisabeth.

    Zacharias was a priest of the course of Abia and Elizabeth was of the daughters of Aaron, showing John came from the tribe of Levi and the priesthood.

    Jesus made and baptized more disciples than John, though Jesus himself baptized not, but his disciples did, because they had followed John.
  - **[John 7:38-39](/bible/john/7#v38)**

    Out of the believer's belly shall flow rivers of living water; this he spake of the Spirit, for the Holy Ghost was not yet given because Jesus was not yet glorified.
  - **[Proverbs 20:27](/bible/proverbs/20#v27)**
    > <sup>[27](/bible/proverbs/20#v27)</sup> The spirit of man is the candle of the Lord, searching all the inward parts of the belly.

    The spirit of man is the candle of the Most High, searching all the inward parts of the belly; the belly is the mind, and the spirit can be put out like a candle.

**[Acts 1:6](/bible/acts/1#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/acts/1#v6)</sup> When they therefore were come together, they asked of him, saying, Lord, wilt thou at this time restore again the kingdom to Israel?

- Even after all of this they asked whether he would at this time restore again the kingdom to Israel, which shows the Holy Ghost was not yet fully given. It is given in measures, and every man is dealt a measure of faith that he has to grow in; in Christ there was no measure, but the disciples knew only so much at that time.
- They asked because they thought Rome was the last captivity, and John the Baptist thought the same when he sent to ask whether Christ was the one or they should look for another. They knew Daniel's beasts up to Rome because their fathers had lived through them, but they did not yet understand the little horn, Babylon the great and America, or the seven seals that only the Lion of the tribe of Judah could open. That fuller understanding was given later to John, to Peter, and now in these last days.
- The falling away had to come first and the man of sin be revealed; he could not be openly named under Rome, where you were jailed for sedition, so Paul wrote in allegory. He would be revealed in a land like America, where the freedom to speak allows the man of sin to be identified plainly.
- "Restore" means to bring back and make new. The kingdom of heaven is Jerusalem and it always belonged to Israel, not to all nations; it is not pie in the sky when you die.
  - **[Romans 12:3](/bible/romans/12#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/romans/12#v3)</sup> For I say, through the grace given unto me, to every man that is among you, not to think of himself more highly than he ought to think; but to think soberly, according as God hath dealt to every man the measure of faith.

    The Most High dealt to every man the measure of faith, so the disciples' understanding grew in increments.

    John sent from prison to ask, Art thou he that should come, or do we look for another; he too expected deliverance at that time.
  - **[Daniel 7:7](/bible/daniel/7#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/daniel/7#v7)</sup> After this I saw in the night visions, and behold a fourth beast, dreadful and terrible, and strong exceedingly; and it had great iron teeth: it devoured and brake in pieces, and stamped the residue with the feet of it: and it was diverse from all the beasts that were before it; and it had ten horns.

    The fourth beast, dreadful and strong with iron teeth, is Rome; the disciples understood the beasts through Rome but not what came after.

    Only the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, prevailed to open the book and loose the seven seals.
  - **[2 Thessalonians 2:3](/bible/2-thessalonians/2#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/2-thessalonians/2#v3)</sup> Let no man deceive you by any means: for that day shall not come, except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed, the son of perdition;

    That day shall not come except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed, the son of perdition.
  - **[Revelation 21:12](/bible/revelation/21#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/revelation/21#v12)</sup> And had a wall great and high, and had twelve gates, and at the gates twelve angels, and names written thereon, which are the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel:

    The new Jerusalem has twelve gates bearing the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel; the kingdom is for the twelve tribes, not for everyone else.

**[Acts 1:7](/bible/acts/1#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/acts/1#v7)</sup> And he said unto them, It is not for you to know the times or the seasons, which the Father hath put in his own power.

- Christ said it is not for them to know the times or the seasons, which the Father hath put in his own power. Even after his resurrection Christ did not know the time, which disproves the trinity and the idea that Christ is the Father, showing the Son is separate from the Father.
- You come to understand the times and seasons as you live through the prophecies and see prophecy become history.

**[Acts 1:8](/bible/acts/1#v8)**
> <sup>[8](/bible/acts/1#v8)</sup> But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.

- They would receive power after the Holy Ghost came upon them, and be witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judaea, in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.
- Christians misunderstand this power as merely speaking in tongues, but the Bible defines the power of the Holy Ghost by the signs that follow: casting out devils, speaking with new tongues, taking up serpents, drinking any deadly thing unharmed, and laying hands on the sick to recover. Peter was sought to heal by his shadow and Paul by his handkerchief.
  - **[Mark 16:16-18](/bible/mark/16#v16)**

**[Acts 1:9](/bible/acts/1#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/acts/1#v9)</sup> And when he had spoken these things, while they beheld, he was taken up; and a cloud received him out of their sight.

- While they beheld, Christ was taken up and a cloud received him out of their sight. He was taken up in the middle of his speaking after forty days; that was the exit.
- The Most High maketh the clouds his chariot, so this was not merely a cloud but a chariot, an instrument of the angels, the same as the moon being an instrument of the armies above. Christ was received up by a chariot, not carried by little white angels in the clouds as the Christian movies depict.
- The kind of chariot that masks itself in a cloud was seen in 2011 over the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem, a glowing orb that descended and then shot off at about four thousand miles an hour over one of the most secure no-fly zones in the world, witnessed by thousands and reported internationally.
  - **[Psalms 104:3](/bible/psalms/104#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/psalms/104#v3)</sup> Who layeth the beams of his chambers in the waters: who maketh the clouds his chariot: who walketh upon the wings of the wind:

    He layeth the beams of his chambers in the waters and maketh the clouds his chariot; the clouds are his chariots.

**[Acts 1:12-13](/bible/acts/1#v12)**

- They returned from the mount called Olivet, a sabbath day's journey from Jerusalem, and went up into an upper room where the eleven abode; their names are listed.
- The lists differ across the gospels. Simon is also called Peter and Cephas. Simon the Canaanite is the same as Simon Zelotes. Lebbaeus, whose surname was Thaddaeus, is the same as Judas the brother of James, who is also called Jude.
- Judas the brother of James is not Judas Iscariot the traitor. He called himself Jude or Thaddaeus so as not to be associated with the traitor.

  - **[Acts 15:14](/bible/acts/15#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/acts/15#v14)</sup> Simeon hath declared how God at the first did visit the Gentiles, to take out of them a people for his name.

**[Acts 1:14](/bible/acts/1#v14)**
> <sup>[14](/bible/acts/1#v14)</sup> These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brethren.

- They all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and his brethren.
- This was not only the men; the women also had to learn. The men always understood that the women learn alongside them, so a camp that keeps the women out of the congregation is wrong. Our people came into slavery together and will come out of captivity together.
- "His brethren" are Christ's own brothers. During his ministry they did not believe in him and told him to go show himself openly in Judaea, but after his death and the miracles they came to believe. James the Just, the Lord's brother, became an apostle and was later killed.
