Read the chapter → [Acts 9](/bible/acts/9)

This session continues the Man Up Mondays series through the Acts of the Apostles, teaching Acts chapter 9 verse by verse. If you want to understand the writings of Paul, you have to walk the history of Acts first, so this class does something different: as we move through the history, we stop and line it up with the letters Paul wrote, mainly Galatians chapter 1, so you can match the histories with the Epistles. Chapter 9 is a continuation of the persecution that scattered the disciples after Stephen, and it opens on Saul, the man persecuting our people, before the Most High knocks him off his course and makes him a chosen vessel.

**[Acts 9:1-2](/bible/acts/9#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/acts/9#v1)</sup> And Saul, yet breathing out threatenings and slaughter against the disciples of the Lord, went unto the high priest,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/acts/9#v2)</sup> And desired of him letters to Damascus to the synagogues, that if he found any of this way, whether they were men or women, he might bring them bound unto Jerusalem.

- Saul is still breathing out threatenings and slaughter against the disciples, going to the high priest for letters to Damascus so he can bind any of "this way," whether men or women, and drag them bound to Jerusalem. This is why we tell brothers and sisters both to gird up their minds and build their faith by studying, because when they notice you by your last name or your fringes you will be persecuted.
- Before the followers of Christ were called Christians in [Acts 11](/bible/acts/11), they were called "this way," meaning the way of Christ. Some people, when the persecution comes, will change their name back, stop wearing fringes, stop keeping the Sabbath, and hide, saying "that's not me."
  - **[John 14:6](/bible/john/14#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/john/14#v6)</sup> Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.

    Christ is the way, the truth, and the life, so those who followed him were called "this way."

- As Saul journeys near Damascus a light from heaven, a chariot of the Most High, shines round about him, he falls to the earth, and hears a voice: "Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?" Christ was already dead at this time, yet he says "why persecutest thou me," because what you do to one of his little ones you do to him also.
- Saul did not realize he was in error. He thought the disciples were bringing heresy, but it was not heresy, it was the New Covenant being introduced. Trembling and astonished, he immediately humbles himself and says, "Lord, what wilt thou have me to do."

**[Acts 9:7](/bible/acts/9#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/acts/9#v7)</sup> And the men which journeyed with him stood speechless, hearing a voice, but seeing no man.

- The men journeying with Saul stood speechless, hearing a voice but seeing no man. People read this against [Acts 22](/bible/acts/22) and claim a contradiction, but there is none.
- It was a caravan of men. Based on each man's account, one group heard the voice but saw no man, and another group saw the light but heard no voice, just as men running from the same scene report different pieces of it. There is no contradiction in the Bible.

**[Acts 9:13-14](/bible/acts/9#v13)**

- Was Saul doing it in ignorance? Yes. That is why the scripture says judge nothing before the time. The things we all did in the world were worthy of death, so who are we to say a man is beyond repentance? The killer, even a pastor putting us to death in the future, may come to the truth later and say he did it in ignorance, as Christ and Stephen both prayed "Father, forgive them." Even a sister who cursed the teacher out on the streets for months came around and began to listen, because anyone can repent.
- This is the true meaning of loving your enemies. Christianity twists it to mean love the ones who enslaved, raped, and colonized your ancestors, stole your nationality, land, and heritage, miseducated you, gave you a false image of God, and today shut down anyone who says Israel is the true children of God. That is not the enemy the scripture means. The Bible means love the enemies within your own nation, because Paul, who persecuted and killed the disciples, was one of those enemies who could still repent.

> <sup>[32](/bible/acts/9#v32)</sup> And it came to pass, as Peter passed throughout all quarters, he came down also to the saints which dwelt at Lydda.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/acts/9#v33)</sup> And there he found a certain man named Eneas, which had kept his bed eight years, and was sick of the palsy.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/acts/9#v34)</sup> And Peter said unto him, Eneas, Jesus Christ maketh thee whole: arise, and make thy bed. And he arose immediately.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/acts/9#v35)</sup> And all that dwelt at Lydda and Saron saw him, and turned to the Lord.

- As Peter passes through all quarters he comes to the saints at Lydda and finds Aeneas, who had kept his bed eight years sick of the palsy. Peter says, "Aeneas, Jesus Christ maketh thee whole; arise and make thy bed," and he arises immediately.
- All that dwelt at Lydda and Sharon saw him and turned to the Lord.

**[Acts 9:36-43](/bible/acts/9#v36)**
> <sup>[36](/bible/acts/9#v36)</sup> Now there was at Joppa a certain disciple named Tabitha, which by interpretation is called Dorcas: this woman was full of good works and almsdeeds which she did.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/acts/9#v37)</sup> And it came to pass in those days, that she was sick, and died: whom when they had washed, they laid her in an upper chamber.
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/acts/9#v38)</sup> And forasmuch as Lydda was nigh to Joppa, and the disciples had heard that Peter was there, they sent unto him two men, desiring him that he would not delay to come to them.
>
> <sup>[39](/bible/acts/9#v39)</sup> Then Peter arose and went with them. When he was come, they brought him into the upper chamber: and all the widows stood by him weeping, and shewing the coats and garments which Dorcas made, while she was with them.
>
> <sup>[40](/bible/acts/9#v40)</sup> But Peter put them all forth, and kneeled down, and prayed; and turning him to the body said, Tabitha, arise. And she opened her eyes: and when she saw Peter, she sat up.
>
> <sup>[41](/bible/acts/9#v41)</sup> And he gave her his hand, and lifted her up, and when he had called the saints and widows, presented her alive.
>
> <sup>[42](/bible/acts/9#v42)</sup> And it was known throughout all Joppa; and many believed in the Lord.
>
> <sup>[43](/bible/acts/9#v43)</sup> And it came to pass, that he tarried many days in Joppa with one Simon a tanner.

- At Joppa a disciple named Tabitha, called Dorcas, full of good works and almsdeeds, falls sick and dies, and they wash her and lay her in an upper chamber. Women were disciples too, students and followers of Christ, and washing the dead shows our own customs of purifying the body.
- The disciples send for Peter, who puts them all out, kneels, prays, and says, "Tabitha, arise." She opens her eyes and sits up, and he presents her alive to the saints and widows, so that many in Joppa believed. Peter then tarries many days in Joppa with Simon a tanner.
- Dorcas made coats and garments and gave almsdeeds, so she had a good name and no covetous spirit; what she took in she gave back. This is unlike the opportunists who see thousands at Passover and only think of what they can get from the people, coveting to live off of them.
