## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Acts 12 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-11, 18-23, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Acts 12:1-3](/bible/acts/12#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/acts/12#v1)</sup> Now about that time Herod the king stretched forth his hands to vex certain of the church.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/acts/12#v2)</sup> And he killed James the brother of John with the sword.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/acts/12#v3)</sup> And because he saw it pleased the Jews, he proceeded further to take Peter also. (Then were the days of unleavened bread.)

- “Herod the king stretched forth his hands to vex certain of the church. And he killed James the brother of John with the sword. And because he saw it pleased the Jews, he proceeded further to take Peter also.” The government is going to attack us, and there are going to be black people who are happy for the attack. The Roman government attacked the Israelites because it pleased the Jews. So don’t think the same people we are passing our flyers to won’t join with the government.
- “(Then were the days of unleavened bread.)” It was on Passover. He didn’t care that it was the highest holy day. Christ was killed on Passover, and Peter was arrested on Passover. They waited for the most holy day to go and attack the Israelites.
- “Then were the days of unleavened bread.” Herod killed James and, seeing it pleased the Jews, took Peter also in the days of unleavened bread.

**[Acts 12:4](/bible/acts/12#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/acts/12#v4)</sup> And when he had apprehended him, he put him in prison, and delivered him to four quaternions of soldiers to keep him; intending after Easter to bring him forth to the people.

- He kept Peter in prison “intending after Easter to bring him forth to the people.” Easter was a Roman pagan celebration, the goddess of fertility and spring, and it falls in the same season as the passover and the days of unleavened bread. Lent itself means springtime.
- The Europeans took Easter and brought it into Christianity, the same way they brought the feast of Bacchus in through carnival. Carnival leads into Lent, and Lent leads up to Easter.

**[Acts 12:5](/bible/acts/12#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/acts/12#v5)</sup> Peter therefore was kept in prison: but prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him.

- “Peter therefore was kept in prison: but prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him.” When things go wrong, you need the right people to pray for you. Imagine we get snatched up on Passover, or something happens to the men traveling all over on Passover: we want you praying, talking to God without ceasing. That is why you don’t keep prideful people around you, because they are not going to do it; you can’t rely on them. The church prayed that Peter would be preserved, because Herod was killing people, and doing it for the wicked Israelites who aligned themselves with the government, just like the wicked Israelites now who are for Donald Trump.

**[Acts 12:6](/bible/acts/12#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/acts/12#v6)</sup> And when Herod would have brought him forth, the same night Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains: and the keepers before the door kept the prison.

- “The same night Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains: and the keepers before the door kept the prison.” Herod was coming for him; he had soldiers watching him and Peter in chains.

**[Acts 12:7-9](/bible/acts/12#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/acts/12#v7)</sup> And, behold, the angel of the Lord came upon him, and a light shined in the prison: and he smote Peter on the side, and raised him up, saying, Arise up quickly. And his chains fell off from his hands.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/acts/12#v8)</sup> And the angel said unto him, Gird thyself, and bind on thy sandals. And so he did. And he saith unto him, Cast thy garment about thee, and follow me.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/acts/12#v9)</sup> And he went out, and followed him; and wist not that it was true which was done by the angel; but thought he saw a vision.

- “And, behold, the angel of the Lord came upon him, and a light shined in the prison”, and “his chains fell off from his hands.” You are in prison, in chains, asleep, and the angel says, wake up, put your garment on, and the chains come off. Peter followed him “and wist not that it was true which was done by the angel; but thought he saw a vision.” He couldn’t believe what he was going through; Peter thought he was going to die.

**[Acts 12:10](/bible/acts/12#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/acts/12#v10)</sup> When they were past the first and the second ward, they came unto the iron gate that leadeth unto the city; which opened to them of his own accord: and they went out, and passed on through one street; and forthwith the angel departed from him.

- “When they were past the first and the second ward, they came unto the iron gate that leadeth unto the city; which opened to them of his own accord”. He walked through the different sections of the prison, like a prison break where they pass all the gates, with the gates opening by themselves and an angel walking with him. “And forthwith the angel departed from him”: just like that, you are on your own.

**[Acts 12:11](/bible/acts/12#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/acts/12#v11)</sup> And when Peter was come to himself, he said, Now I know of a surety, that the Lord hath sent his angel, and hath delivered me out of the hand of Herod, and from all the expectation of the people of the Jews.

- “And when Peter was come to himself, he said, Now I know of a surety, that the Lord hath sent his angel, and hath delivered me out of the hand of Herod, and from all the expectation of the people of the Jews.” The government and his own people were trying to kill him. It is the same now: the government is going to turn against us, and wicked black people, Hispanic people, people in the Caribbean and in Africa, who are being shown they are the people of the book and refuse to look, are going to turn on us. All we have is the prayer of the church and the angels answering those prayers.

**[Acts 12:18-19](/bible/acts/12#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/acts/12#v18)</sup> Now as soon as it was day, there was no small stir among the soldiers, what was become of Peter.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/acts/12#v19)</sup> And when Herod had sought for him, and found him not, he examined the keepers, and commanded that they should be put to death. And he went down from Judea to Cesarea, and there abode.

- “Now as soon as it was day, there was no small stir among the soldiers, what was become of Peter.” Everybody was talking about it: you let Peter go? Herod “examined the keepers, and commanded that they should be put to death.” Just like that, kill them.

**[Acts 12:20](/bible/acts/12#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/acts/12#v20)</sup> And Herod was highly displeased with them of Tyre and Sidon: but they came with one accord to him, and, having made Blastus the king’s chamberlain their friend, desired peace; because their country was nourished by the king’s country.

- “And Herod was highly displeased with them of Tyre and Sidon”: that is Lebanon. “but they came with one accord to him, and, having made Blastus the king’s chamberlain their friend, desired peace”. Why did they want peace? “because their country was nourished by the king’s country.” One verse, and it speaks volumes.
- Jamaica and Guyana ended their agreement with Cuba and sent the Cuban doctors home, the best doctors in the Caribbean, and Antigua is replacing them with a brigade from Ghana. Why? In 2024 the United States exported a record $5.47 billion in food to the Caribbean, roughly 50% to 55% of the region's food imports. America makes deals with countries: you're not allowed to grow your own food; we'll feed you. So when America says, get rid of all the Cuban doctors, they all say, yes, boss; yes, master; you give us food, master, your will is our command.
- Bill Clinton forced Haiti to lower its tariffs on imported US rice. Haiti can grow its own rice, but America said, don't worry, we'll give you food, and Clinton admitted it destroyed Haitian rice production and helped his farmers in Arkansas. His apology changed nothing. If you depend on somebody for food, when America gets mad at you they cut all supplies, and your whole country starves. It's all a trick bag.
- “And Herod was highly displeased with them of Tyre and Sidon”. Tyre and Sidon were Hamite nations, modern-day Lebanon. “But they came with one accord to him, and, having made Blastus the king’s chamberlain their friend”: they befriended, flattered and gave gifts to Herod's chamberlain, and “desired peace; because their country was nourished by the king’s country.” Herod's country fed Tyre and Sidon, so they had to obey. They didn't want Herod to be mad at them, because if they let his anger go on, he could cut them off from their food supply.

And the people gave a shout, saying, It is the voice of a god, and not of a man.” Black folk always think a white man is God. God always has to remind you who he is.

**[Acts 12:23](/bible/acts/12#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/acts/12#v23)</sup> And immediately the angel of the Lord smote him, because he gave not God the glory: and he was eaten of worms, and gave up the ghost.

- “And immediately the angel of the Lord smote him, because he gave not God the glory”. You think there is not a God listening? God said, what did he say? Go kill him. These are empires that have nothing to do with God, and God is still listening to them talk. He turned Nebuchadnezzar into an animal; he killed Sennacherib for running his mouth that he was going to touch the Israelites; Herod put Peter in prison, ran his mouth, and God sent an angel to kill him.
- “And he was eaten of worms, and gave up the ghost.” God put him in the dirt. That was his honor on earth, his legacy.
- “And immediately the angel of the Lord smote him, because he gave not God the glory: and he was eaten of worms, and gave up the ghost.” Pride destroyed them. All kingdoms fall because of pride, and nobody is exempt: Assyria, Babylon, Greece, Rome, America, it does not matter. God destroying them is the judgment for it.
