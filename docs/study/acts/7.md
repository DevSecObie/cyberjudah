## Introduction

This session continues the Man Up Mondays series through the Acts of the Apostles, covering chapters 7 and 8 in one sitting. Chapter 7 is Stephen standing before the council of the Pharisees and Sadducees, rehearsing the whole history of the fathers off the top of his head, from Abraham in Mesopotamia down to Moses and the prophets, to bring the audience up to Christ, the prophet Moses said the Most High would raise up. It ends with Stephen charging them as stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, seeing Christ standing on the right hand of God, and being stoned while Saul consents to his death. Chapter 8 opens with the great persecution that scatters the disciples abroad, and follows Philip into Samaria and down to the Ethiopian eunuch, teaching heavily on the two baptisms, the name of Jesus in place of animal sacrifice, and the remnant of Israel scattered among the nations. The thread of the whole class is the spirit: the Holy Ghost is first and foremost the laws, and the same persecution and the same work that happened back then is what comes on the scene again today.

**[Acts 7:1-5](/bible/acts/7#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/acts/7#v1)</sup> Then said the high priest, Are these things so?
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/acts/7#v2)</sup> And he said, Men, brethren, and fathers, hearken; The God of glory appeared unto our father Abraham, when he was in Mesopotamia, before he dwelt in Charran,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/acts/7#v3)</sup> And said unto him, Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and come into the land which I shall shew thee.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/acts/7#v4)</sup> Then came he out of the land of the Chaldeans, and dwelt in Charran: and from thence, when his father was dead, he removed him into this land, wherein ye now dwell.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/acts/7#v5)</sup> And he gave him none inheritance in it, no, not so much as to set his foot on: yet he promised that he would give it to him for a possession, and to his seed after him, when as yet he had no child.

- This is Stephen at the council, addressing the audience just before he is put to death. He answers the high priest by going all the way back to the beginning: the God of glory appeared unto our father Abraham when he was in Mesopotamia, before he dwelt in Charran.
- Abraham came out of the land of the Chaldeans, dwelt in Charran, and after his father died the Most High removed him into this land wherein they now dwell, and gave him no inheritance in it, not so much as to set his foot on, yet promised to give it to him and his seed after him when as yet he had no child.
- Notice how Stephen recites this history off the top of his head. Our forefathers read more than their four chapters a day. When you read, you put these things to memory, so you can recite it. A lot of us cannot recite our own history, but we can recite LeBron James and the NBA stats, football stats. These men were studious; they constantly gave attendance to studying. They called Paul mad for reading so many letters. Our forefathers were addicted to this gospel.

**[Acts 7:20-22](/bible/acts/7#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/acts/7#v20)</sup> In which time Moses was born, and was exceeding fair, and nourished up in his father’s house three months:
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/acts/7#v21)</sup> And when he was cast out, Pharaoh’s daughter took him up, and nourished him for her own son.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/acts/7#v22)</sup> And Moses was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians, and was mighty in words and in deeds.

- In that time Moses was born, exceeding fair, nourished up in his father's house three months, and when he was cast out, Pharaoh's daughter took him up and nourished him for her own son.
- Moses was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians and was mighty in words and in deeds. That corrects the doctrine that certain Christian pastors preach, that Moses stuttered and could not speak. You cannot be a stupid man and be a prince over Egypt. He was raised under Pharaoh, so he had to learn everything of Egypt, their gods, their laws, the language. He was not a stupid man, and he did not stutter.

**[Acts 7:23-29](/bible/acts/7#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/acts/7#v23)</sup> And when he was full forty years old, it came into his heart to visit his brethren the children of Israel.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/acts/7#v24)</sup> And seeing one of them suffer wrong, he defended him, and avenged him that was oppressed, and smote the Egyptian:
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/acts/7#v25)</sup> For he supposed his brethren would have understood how that God by his hand would deliver them: but they understood not.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/acts/7#v26)</sup> And the next day he shewed himself unto them as they strove, and would have set them at one again, saying, Sirs, ye are brethren; why do ye wrong one to another?
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/acts/7#v27)</sup> But he that did his neighbour wrong thrust him away, saying, Who made thee a ruler and a judge over us?
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/acts/7#v28)</sup> Wilt thou kill me, as thou diddest the Egyptian yesterday?
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/acts/7#v29)</sup> Then fled Moses at this saying, and was a stranger in the land of Madian, where he begat two sons.

- When Moses was full forty years old, it came into his heart to visit his brethren the children of Israel. Contrary to the movie The Ten Commandments, Moses always knew who he was; his own mother raised and nursed him, so he knew his brothers, he was only raised under the household of the Egyptians.
- Seeing one of them suffer wrong, he defended him and smote the Egyptian, supposing his brethren would understand that the Most High by his hand would deliver them, but they understood not. The next day, striving to make peace, he was thrust away with, Who made thee a ruler and a judge over us?
- Only the Most High could set a man to judge. This is the crabs in a barrel spirit: even after you help somebody, they say, Who are you to be over us? Everybody wants to be the head, the chief, a leader, but nobody wants to be a follower, and nobody wants to give credit when credit is due. The Most High was using the status Moses had in Egypt to help his brother, and they used it against him: Wilt thou kill me as thou didst the Egyptian yesterday?
- Certain of us will be put in different positions in this society, in politics, in education, and the Most High will use those accolades to your advantage, the same way he used Moses to go to Pharaoh and not be put to death right off the bat. Then Moses fled and was a stranger in the land of Midian, understanding that if he stayed among his own people they would get him killed, and there he begat two sons.
- People use the excuse that Moses married an Ethiopian to justify interracial marriage, saying maybe the Lord would forgive them the same way. But the Most High says, I will have mercy on whom I will have mercy. You are not Moses. Ask Aaron and Miriam, whom the Most High plagued in the skin and put out of the camp seven days for speaking against a matter that did not concern them.
- Do not point fingers and compare yourself to the person next to you, wondering why one brother is judged for a matter and another is treated differently. Different judgments belong to the Most High. You are not David; do not weigh Saul's sins against David's and assume you know how the Most High will judge, the way men questioned why Uzza was struck for touching the ark. Work out your own soul salvation with fear and trembling, study to be quiet, and mind your own business.

- Forty years later, Moses now eighty years old, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a flame of fire in a bush at Mount Sinai. Brothers and sisters say they are too old to repent; you are never too old. The Most High used Moses at eighty to deliver the whole nation, and retained his strength.
- The voice of the Lord said, I am the God of thy fathers, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Moses trembled and durst not behold. He was told to put off his shoes, for the place was holy ground, and the Most High said, I have seen the affliction of my people in Egypt, I have heard their groaning, and I am come down to deliver them; now come, I will send thee into Egypt.

**[Acts 7:35-37](/bible/acts/7#v35)**

> <sup>[35](/bible/acts/7#v35)</sup> This Moses whom they refused, saying, Who made thee a ruler and a judge? the same did God send to be a ruler and a deliverer by the hand of the angel which appeared to him in the bush.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/acts/7#v36)</sup> He brought them out, after that he had shewed wonders and signs in the land of Egypt, and in the Red sea, and in the wilderness forty years.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/acts/7#v37)</sup> This is that Moses, which said unto the children of Israel, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear.

- This Moses, whom they refused saying, Who made thee a ruler and a judge, the same did the Most High send to be a ruler and a deliverer by the hand of the angel that appeared in the bush. Be careful with one another; the same person you reject is the same person the Most High raises up over you. This is the spirit of preeminence, not wanting another brother to rule over you because you believe you should be in that position. The Most High sees the heart and knows the spirits he is setting up.
- Moses was the meekest, most humble man. For a prince to go down to people counted as slaves and servants, defend them, and risk himself for a stiffnecked and rebellious people, shows he loved his people even in his position of authority.
- This is that Moses who said, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren like unto me; him shall ye hear. Stephen rehearsed all the history to bring them up to Christ, because the whole ministry throughout Acts is Christ's name preached in the place of animal sacrifice. Even as Pharisees, they knew this prophecy, so he made it plain: the prophet Moses spoke about is that Christ.

- This is he that was in the church in the wilderness with the angel that spoke to him in Mount Sinai, and with our fathers, who received the lively oracles to give unto us. The one in the church in the wilderness is that Christ, the prophet of verse 37.
- Our fathers would not obey him but thrust him from them, and in their hearts turned back to Egypt, telling Aaron, Make us gods to go before us, for as for this Moses we know not what is become of him. They did not obey Christ in the wilderness, just as they did not obey Christ in his day.
- They made a calf, offered sacrifice to the idol, and rejoiced in the works of their own hands, so the Most High gave them up to worship the host of heaven; they took up the tabernacle of Moloch and the star of the god Remphan, figures they made to worship, and he carried them away beyond Babylon.

**[Acts 7:51-53](/bible/acts/7#v51)**

> <sup>[51](/bible/acts/7#v51)</sup> Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, ye do always resist the Holy Ghost: as your fathers did, so do ye.
>
> <sup>[52](/bible/acts/7#v52)</sup> Which of the prophets have not your fathers persecuted? and they have slain them which shewed before of the coming of the Just One; of whom ye have been now the betrayers and murderers:
>
> <sup>[53](/bible/acts/7#v53)</sup> Who have received the law by the disposition of angels, and have not kept it.

- Stephen turns it on them: Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, ye do always resist the Holy Ghost; as your fathers did, so do ye. The Holy Ghost the fathers resisted in the wilderness is the same Holy Ghost resisted in Christ's day, and that Holy Ghost is first and foremost the laws.
- There are three forms of the Holy Ghost taught in this series: first the laws, when you keep the laws of the Most High; then wisdom and understanding, which comes after you keep the laws; and then power, as the apostles received power to speak in tongues and perform miracles.
- Which of the prophets did their fathers not persecute? They slew them that showed before of the coming of the Just One, of whom they had now been the betrayers and murderers, who received the law by the disposition of angels and kept it not.

But Stephen, being full of the Holy Ghost, looked up steadfastly into heaven and saw the glory of the Most High and Jesus standing on the right hand of God, and said, Behold, I see the heavens opened and the Son of man standing on the right hand of God.
- They cried out with a loud voice, stopped their ears, ran upon him with one accord, cast him out of the city, and stoned him; the witnesses laid down their clothes at the feet of a young man named Saul.
- Stephen, calling on the Most High, said, Lord Jesus, receive my spirit, and kneeling down he cried, Lord, lay not this sin to their charge, and fell asleep. This is the same spirit Christ had when he said, Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do.
