Read the chapter → [Ezra 1](/bible/ezra/1)

This session covers [Ezra 1](/bible/ezra/1) through 4, the Persian captivity period from about 539 BC forward. The Most High moves Cyrus king of Persia to free the captives of Judah, Benjamin, and Levi to return to Jerusalem and rebuild the temple. The leader in these first chapters is Zerubbabel, not Ezra. Ezra himself does not appear until chapter 7. The reading follows Zerubbabel bringing the people back, laying the foundation of the temple, and the opposition from the surrounding nations that halts the work until the reign of Darius. The same history is recorded again in 1 Esdras.

**[Ezra 1:1-2](/bible/ezra/1#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/ezra/1#v1)</sup> Now in the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, that the word of the Lord by the mouth of Jeremiah might be fulfilled, the Lord stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, that he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom, and put it also in writing, saying,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ezra/1#v2)</sup> Thus saith Cyrus king of Persia, The Lord God of heaven hath given me all the kingdoms of the earth; and he hath charged me to build him an house at Jerusalem, which is in Judah.

- In the first year of Cyrus the word of the Most High spoken by Jeremiah is fulfilled. The Lord stirs up the spirit of Cyrus to make a proclamation throughout his kingdom.
- Cyrus was one of the first kings of Persia, and the Most High used him to give the order to rebuild Jerusalem. His coming was prophesied long before by name.
  - **[Isaiah 44:28](/bible/isaiah/44#v28)**
    > <sup>[28](/bible/isaiah/44#v28)</sup> That saith of Cyrus, He is my shepherd, and shall perform all my pleasure: even saying to Jerusalem, Thou shalt be built; and to the temple, Thy foundation shall be laid.

    The Most High names Cyrus as his shepherd who will say Jerusalem shall be built and the temple foundation laid.
  - **[Isaiah 45:1](/bible/isaiah/45#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/isaiah/45#v1)</sup> Thus saith the Lord to his anointed, to Cyrus, whose right hand I have holden, to subdue nations before him; and I will loose the loins of kings, to open before him the two leaved gates; and the gates shall not be shut;

    The Lord speaks to his anointed Cyrus, whose right hand he holds to subdue nations before him.
  - **[Jeremiah 29:10](/bible/jeremiah/29#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/jeremiah/29#v10)</sup> For thus saith the Lord, That after seventy years be accomplished at Babylon I will visit you, and perform my good word toward you, in causing you to return to this place.

    After seventy years at Babylon the Most High promised to visit his people and bring them back to their place.

**[Ezra 1:3-4](/bible/ezra/1#v3)**
> <sup>[3](/bible/ezra/1#v3)</sup> Who is there among you of all his people? his God be with him, and let him go up to Jerusalem, which is in Judah, and build the house of the Lord God of Israel, (he is the God,) which is in Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/ezra/1#v4)</sup> And whosoever remaineth in any place where he sojourneth, let the men of his place help him with silver, and with gold, and with goods, and with beasts, beside the freewill offering for the house of God that is in Jerusalem.

- Cyrus calls for any of the Most High's people to go up to Jerusalem and build the house of the Lord God of Israel.
- Those who remained behind were to help the returning ones with silver, gold, goods, and beasts, beside the freewill offering for the house of God.

**[Ezra 1:5-6](/bible/ezra/1#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/ezra/1#v5)</sup> Then rose up the chief of the fathers of Judah and Benjamin, and the priests, and the Levites, with all them whose spirit God had raised, to go up to build the house of the Lord which is in Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ezra/1#v6)</sup> And all they that were about them strengthened their hands with vessels of silver, with gold, with goods, and with beasts, and with precious things, beside all that was willingly offered.

- The chief of the fathers of Judah and Benjamin, with the priests and the Levites, rose up, all whose spirit the Most High had raised to go build the house of the Lord.
- Those around them strengthened their hands with vessels of silver and gold, with goods and beasts and precious things, beside the freewill offerings.

**[Ezra 1:7-8](/bible/ezra/1#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/ezra/1#v7)</sup> Also Cyrus the king brought forth the vessels of the house of the Lord, which Nebuchadnezzar had brought forth out of Jerusalem, and had put them in the house of his gods;
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/ezra/1#v8)</sup> Even those did Cyrus king of Persia bring forth by the hand of Mithredath the treasurer, and numbered them unto Sheshbazzar, the prince of Judah.

- Cyrus brings out the vessels of the house of the Lord that Nebuchadnezzar had carried from Jerusalem and stored among his own goods.
- He sends them back by the hand of Mithredath the treasurer and numbers them to Sheshbazzar the prince of Judah.

**[Ezra 1:9-11](/bible/ezra/1#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/ezra/1#v9)</sup> And this is the number of them: thirty chargers of gold, a thousand chargers of silver, nine and twenty knives,
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/ezra/1#v10)</sup> Thirty basons of gold, silver basons of a second sort four hundred and ten, and other vessels a thousand.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/ezra/1#v11)</sup> All the vessels of gold and of silver were five thousand and four hundred. All these did Sheshbazzar bring up with them of the captivity that were brought up from Babylon unto Jerusalem.

- The vessels are counted out, gold and silver charges, basins, knives, and other vessels totaling 5,400.
- All these Sheshbazzar brings up with the captives that returned from Babylon to Jerusalem. The same account is recorded in 1 Esdras.

# Ezra 3: The Foundation of the Temple Is Laid

Read the chapter → [Ezra 3](/bible/ezra/3)

**[Ezra 3:1-2](/bible/ezra/3#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/ezra/3#v1)</sup> And when the seventh month was come, and the children of Israel were in the cities, the people gathered themselves together as one man to Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/ezra/3#v2)</sup> Then stood up Jeshua the son of Jozadak, and his brethren the priests, and Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, and his brethren, and builded the altar of the God of Israel, to offer burnt offerings thereon, as it is written in the law of Moses the man of God.

- In the seventh month the children of Israel gather as one man to Jerusalem. The seventh month marks the Feast of Tabernacles and the day of atonement, so knowing the month reveals what they were doing.
- Jeshua and Zerubbabel build the altar of the God of Israel to offer burnt offerings as written in the law of Moses.

**[Ezra 3:3-6](/bible/ezra/3#v3)**

>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ezra/3#v5)</sup> And afterward offered the continual burnt offering, both of the new moons, and of all the set feasts of the Lord that were consecrated, and of every one that willingly offered a freewill offering unto the Lord.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/ezra/3#v6)</sup> From the first day of the seventh month began they to offer burnt offerings unto the Lord. But the foundation of the temple of the Lord was not yet laid.

- They keep the Feast of Tabernacles and the daily offerings, but the foundation of the temple was not yet laid.

# Ezra 4: The Adversaries Halt the Building

Read the chapter → [Ezra 4](/bible/ezra/4)

**[Ezra 4:1-2](/bible/ezra/4#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/ezra/4#v1)</sup> Now when the adversaries of Judah and Benjamin heard that the children of the captivity builded the temple unto the Lord God of Israel;

- The adversaries of Judah and Benjamin hear of the building and come to Zerubbabel asking to build with them, claiming to seek the same God since the days of Esarhaddon king of Assur.
- These were peoples the king of Assyria had brought into the land, not true worshippers but taught only to appease the God of the land.
  - **[2 Kings 17:24](/bible/2-kings/17#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/2-kings/17#v24)</sup> And the king of Assyria brought men from Babylon, and from Cuthah, and from Ava, and from Hamath, and from Sepharvaim, and placed them in the cities of Samaria instead of the children of Israel: and they possessed Samaria, and dwelt in the cities thereof.

    The king of Assyria brought men from Babylon and other cities and placed them in Samaria instead of Israel.
  - **[2 Kings 17:25-26](/bible/2-kings/17#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/2-kings/17#v25)</sup> And so it was at the beginning of their dwelling there, that they feared not the Lord: therefore the Lord sent lions among them, which slew some of them.
    >
    > <sup>[26](/bible/2-kings/17#v26)</sup> Wherefore they spake to the king of Assyria, saying, The nations which thou hast removed, and placed in the cities of Samaria, know not the manner of the God of the land: therefore he hath sent lions among them, and, behold, they slay them, because they know not the manner of the God of the land.

    Not fearing the Lord, they were slain by lions, and they reported they knew not the manner of the God of the land.
  - **[2 Kings 17:27-28](/bible/2-kings/17#v27)**
    > <sup>[27](/bible/2-kings/17#v27)</sup> Then the king of Assyria commanded, saying, Carry thither one of the priests whom ye brought from thence; and let them go and dwell there, and let him teach them the manner of the God of the land.
    >
    > <sup>[28](/bible/2-kings/17#v28)</sup> Then one of the priests whom they had carried away from Samaria came and dwelt in Beth–el, and taught them how they should fear the Lord.

    A captive priest was sent back to teach them how to fear the Lord, yet every nation still made gods of their own.

**[Ezra 4:3](/bible/ezra/4#v3)**
> <sup>[3](/bible/ezra/4#v3)</sup> But Zerubbabel, and Jeshua, and the rest of the chief of the fathers of Israel, said unto them, Ye have nothing to do with us to build an house unto our God; but we ourselves together will build unto the Lord God of Israel, as king Cyrus the king of Persia hath commanded us.

- Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the chief of the fathers refuse them: the strangers have nothing to do with building the house of the God of Israel.
- Israel would build to the Lord alone as Cyrus commanded. The forefathers understood that the work of the Most High is not shared with other nations.

**[Ezra 4:4-5](/bible/ezra/4#v4)**
> <sup>[4](/bible/ezra/4#v4)</sup> Then the people of the land weakened the hands of the people of Judah, and troubled them in building,
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/ezra/4#v5)</sup> And hired counsellors against them, to frustrate their purpose, all the days of Cyrus king of Persia, even until the reign of Darius king of Persia.

- The people of the land weaken the hands of Judah and trouble them in building.
- They hire counselors to frustrate the purpose all the days of Cyrus, even until the reign of Darius, a hindrance stretching across the kings in between.

**[Ezra 4:6-7](/bible/ezra/4#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/ezra/4#v6)</sup> And in the reign of Ahasuerus, in the beginning of his reign, wrote they unto him an accusation against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/ezra/4#v7)</sup> And in the days of Artaxerxes wrote Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel, and the rest of their companions, unto Artaxerxes king of Persia; and the writing of the letter was written in the Syrian tongue, and interpreted in the Syrian tongue.

- In the reign of Ahasuerus and later Artaxerxes, the adversaries write accusations against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem.
- The letter was written and interpreted in the Syrian tongue.

**[Ezra 4:8-10](/bible/ezra/4#v8)**
> <sup>[8](/bible/ezra/4#v8)</sup> Rehum the chancellor and Shimshai the scribe wrote a letter against Jerusalem to Artaxerxes the king in this sort:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/ezra/4#v9)</sup> Then wrote Rehum the chancellor, and Shimshai the scribe, and the rest of their companions; the Dinaites, the Apharsathchites, the Tarpelites, the Apharsites, the Archevites, the Babylonians, the Susanchites, the Dehavites, and the Elamites,
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/ezra/4#v10)</sup> And the rest of the nations whom the great and noble Asnappar brought over, and set in the cities of Samaria, and the rest that are on this side the river, and at such a time.

- Rehum the chancellor and Shimshai the scribe write against Jerusalem, joined by the very nations the Assyrian king had set in the cities of Samaria.
- These are the same peoples named in [2 Kings 17](/bible/2-kings/17), brought over by the great and noble Asnappar, now bringing the accusation.

**[Ezra 4:11-16](/bible/ezra/4#v11)**
> <sup>[11](/bible/ezra/4#v11)</sup> This is the copy of the letter that they sent unto him, even unto Artaxerxes the king; Thy servants the men on this side the river, and at such a time.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/ezra/4#v12)</sup> Be it known unto the king, that the Jews which came up from thee to us are come unto Jerusalem, building the rebellious and the bad city, and have set up the walls thereof, and joined the foundations.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/ezra/4#v13)</sup> Be it known now unto the king, that, if this city be builded, and the walls set up again, then will they not pay toll, tribute, and custom, and so thou shalt endamage the revenue of the kings.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/ezra/4#v14)</sup> Now because we have maintenance from the king’s palace, and it was not meet for us to see the king’s dishonour, therefore have we sent and certified the king;
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/ezra/4#v15)</sup> That search may be made in the book of the records of thy fathers: so shalt thou find in the book of the records, and know that this city is a rebellious city, and hurtful unto kings and provinces, and that they have moved sedition within the same of old time: for which cause was this city destroyed.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/ezra/4#v16)</sup> We certify the king that, if this city be builded again, and the walls thereof set up, by this means thou shalt have no portion on this side the river.

- The letter warns the king that if Jerusalem is built the Jews will not pay toll, tribute, or custom, damaging the king's revenue.
- They call it a rebellious and hurtful city and urge the king to search the records to find cause to stop it.

**[Ezra 4:17-22](/bible/ezra/4#v17)**
> <sup>[17](/bible/ezra/4#v17)</sup> Then sent the king an answer unto Rehum the chancellor, and to Shimshai the scribe, and to the rest of their companions that dwell in Samaria, and unto the rest beyond the river, Peace, and at such a time.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/ezra/4#v18)</sup> The letter which ye sent unto us hath been plainly read before me.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/ezra/4#v19)</sup> And I commanded, and search hath been made, and it is found that this city of old time hath made insurrection against kings, and that rebellion and sedition have been made therein.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/ezra/4#v20)</sup> There have been mighty kings also over Jerusalem, which have ruled over all countries beyond the river; and toll, tribute, and custom, was paid unto them.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/ezra/4#v21)</sup> Give ye now commandment to cause these men to cease, and that this city be not builded, until another commandment shall be given from me.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/ezra/4#v22)</sup> Take heed now that ye fail not to do this: why should damage grow to the hurt of the kings?

- The king answers that the records show Jerusalem made insurrection of old and had mighty kings who ruled beyond the river and received tribute.
- He commands the men to cease and the city not be built until another commandment is given from him.

**[Ezra 4:23-24](/bible/ezra/4#v23)**
> <sup>[23](/bible/ezra/4#v23)</sup> Now when the copy of king Artaxerxes’ letter was read before Rehum, and Shimshai the scribe, and their companions, they went up in haste to Jerusalem unto the Jews, and made them to cease by force and power.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/ezra/4#v24)</sup> Then ceased the work of the house of God which is at Jerusalem. So it ceased unto the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.

- When the letter is read, Rehum and Shimshai go up in haste to Jerusalem and make the Jews cease by force and power.
- The work of the house of God ceased until the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia. The same account, with a troop of horsemen halting the work, is told in 1 Esdras.
  - **[1 Esdras 2:30](/bible/1-esdras/2#v30)**
    > <sup>[30](/bible/1-esdras/2#v30)</sup> Then king Artexerxes his letters being read, Rathumus, and Semellius the scribe, and the rest that were in commission with them, removing in haste toward Jerusalem with a troop of horsemen and a multitude of people in battle array, began to hinder the builders; and the building of the temple in Jerusalem ceased until the second year of the reign of Darius king of the Persians.

    The building of the temple in Jerusalem was hindered and ceased until the second year of Darius.
