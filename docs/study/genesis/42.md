**[Genesis 42:1-3](/bible/genesis/42#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/42#v1)</sup> Now when Jacob saw that there was corn in Egypt, Jacob said unto his sons, Why do ye look one upon another?
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/42#v2)</sup> And he said, Behold, I have heard that there is corn in Egypt: get you down thither, and buy for us from thence; that we may live, and not die.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/42#v3)</sup> And Joseph’s ten brethren went down to buy corn in Egypt.

- Jacob saw that there was corn in Egypt and said unto his sons, why do ye look one upon another? Get you down thither, and buy for us from thence, that we may live, and not die.
- This is a repeating thing in our history: when famine came, the fathers moved toward Egypt for sustenance and nourishment. There was a famine in Abram's days and he went down into Egypt, and another famine in Isaac's days, when the Lord told him not to go down.

    > <sup>[10](/bible/genesis/12#v10)</sup> And there was a famine in the land: and Abram went down into Egypt to sojourn there; for the famine was grievous in the land.

    There was a famine in the land, and Abram went down into Egypt to sojourn there, for the famine was grievous.
  - **[Genesis 26:1-2](/bible/genesis/26#v1)**

    >

    In the famine of Isaac's days the Lord appeared unto him and said, go not down into Egypt; the pattern of famine and Egypt runs through the fathers.

**[Genesis 42:4](/bible/genesis/42#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/genesis/42#v4)</sup> But Benjamin, Joseph’s brother, Jacob sent not with his brethren; for he said, Lest peradventure mischief befall him.

- Jacob sent not Benjamin with his brethren; lest peradventure means perhaps mischief befall him. Benjamin was the youngest boy, and he was Rachel's son: Rachel died in hard labour bearing him, calling him Benoni as her soul was departing, but his father called him Benjamin.
- Jacob loved Rachel more than Leah, and Rachel was barren for years; she brought forth only Joseph and Benjamin. With Joseph presumed dead, Benjamin was the last son Jacob had of his beloved wife, so he would not let him go down.

**[Genesis 42:7-9](/bible/genesis/42#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/genesis/42#v7)</sup> And Joseph saw his brethren, and he knew them, but made himself strange unto them, and spake roughly unto them; and he said unto them, Whence come ye? And they said, From the land of Canaan to buy food.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/genesis/42#v8)</sup> And Joseph knew his brethren, but they knew not him.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/genesis/42#v9)</sup> And Joseph remembered the dreams which he dreamed of them, and said unto them, Ye are spies; to see the nakedness of the land ye are come.

- Joseph knew his brethren, but made himself strange unto them and spake roughly unto them; and they knew him not. Time had passed, and they supposed he was dead or a slave; they never expected their brother to be set as second in command and ruler over Egypt.
- They also knew him not because he blended in among the Egyptians: Israel is black as night, just like the ancient Egyptians. When Jacob died, the Canaanites saw the mourning company and took them all for Egyptians; all twelve tribes looked like the people of Egypt.
- And Joseph remembered the dreams which he dreamed of them. The sheaves that made obeisance to his sheaf were now his brethren bowing down themselves before him with their faces to the earth.

  Precepts:
  - **[Genesis 50:10-11](/bible/genesis/50#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/genesis/50#v10)</sup> And they came to the threshingfloor of Atad, which is beyond Jordan, and there they mourned with a great and very sore lamentation: and he made a mourning for his father seven days.
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/genesis/50#v11)</sup> And when the inhabitants of the land, the Canaanites, saw the mourning in the floor of Atad, they said, This is a grievous mourning to the Egyptians: wherefore the name of it was called Abel–mizraim, which is beyond Jordan.

    The Canaanites saw the mourning and said, this is a grievous mourning to the Egyptians; the twelve tribes were mistaken for Egyptians.

**[Genesis 42:15-16](/bible/genesis/42#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/genesis/42#v15)</sup> Hereby ye shall be proved: By the life of Pharaoh ye shall not go forth hence, except your youngest brother come hither.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/genesis/42#v16)</sup> Send one of you, and let him fetch your brother, and ye shall be kept in prison, that your words may be proved, whether there be any truth in you: or else by the life of Pharaoh surely ye are spies.

- Hereby ye shall be proved: ye shall not go forth hence, except your youngest brother come hither. Joseph pressed for Benjamin because that was his own mother's son, his baby brother, whom he wanted to see.

**[Genesis 42:21-24](/bible/genesis/42#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/genesis/42#v21)</sup> And they said one to another, We are verily guilty concerning our brother, in that we saw the anguish of his soul, when he besought us, and we would not hear; therefore is this distress come upon us.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/genesis/42#v22)</sup> And Reuben answered them, saying, Spake I not unto you, saying, Do not sin against the child; and ye would not hear? therefore, behold, also his blood is required.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/genesis/42#v23)</sup> And they knew not that Joseph understood them; for he spake unto them by an interpreter.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/genesis/42#v24)</sup> And he turned himself about from them, and wept; and returned to them again, and communed with them, and took from them Simeon, and bound him before their eyes.

- We are verily guilty concerning our brother, in that we saw the anguish of his soul when he besought us, and we would not hear. Their conscience was activated; they knew this distress had come upon them for what they did to their brother Joseph, not knowing Joseph stood before them.
- Reuben answered them, spake I not unto you, saying, do not sin against the child? Reuben was the one who would not have Joseph slain, but said to sell him not and lay no hand on him.
- They knew not that Joseph understood them, for he spake unto them by an interpreter. He spoke to them in the tongue of the Egyptians and heard every word they said in their own tongue; we have the capacity to understand many languages.
- He turned himself about from them and wept, for he heard everything they said; then he returned, and took from them Simeon, and bound him before their eyes.

**[Genesis 42:35-38](/bible/genesis/42#v35)**

> <sup>[35](/bible/genesis/42#v35)</sup> And it came to pass as they emptied their sacks, that, behold, every man’s bundle of money was in his sack: and when both they and their father saw the bundles of money, they were afraid.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/genesis/42#v36)</sup> And Jacob their father said unto them, Me have ye bereaved of my children: Joseph is not, and Simeon is not, and ye will take Benjamin away: all these things are against me.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/genesis/42#v37)</sup> And Reuben spake unto his father, saying, Slay my two sons, if I bring him not to thee: deliver him into my hand, and I will bring him to thee again.
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/genesis/42#v38)</sup> And he said, My son shall not go down with you; for his brother is dead, and he is left alone: if mischief befall him by the way in the which ye go, then shall ye bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to the grave.

- Every man's bundle of money was in his sack, and they were afraid; and Jacob their father said, me have ye bereaved of my children: Joseph is not, and Simeon is not, and ye will take Benjamin away.
- My son shall not go down with you; his brother is dead, and he is left alone. If mischief befell Benjamin by the way, Jacob's gray hairs would go down with sorrow to the grave: he would die of a broken heart, for Benjamin was the last son he had of Rachel.
