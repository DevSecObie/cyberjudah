## Introduction

This reading covers [Genesis 44](/bible/genesis/44) through 47: the silver cup planted in Benjamin's sack, Joseph made known unto his brethren, Israel's going down into Egypt with threescore and ten souls, and the family planted in Goshen through the famine years. The session opens back at [Genesis 43:30-34](/bible/genesis/43#v30) to bring the account up to speed: the Egyptians might not eat bread with the Hebrews, for that is an abomination unto the Egyptians; even while Israel sojourned in Egypt the Lord put a difference between the Egyptians and Israel, as [Exodus 11:7](/bible/exodus/11#v7) shows. The exposition after the reading draws out the characteristics of Joseph: forgiveness, favor, righteousness, mercy, and perseverance through adversity.

**[Genesis 44:1-2](/bible/genesis/44#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/44#v1)</sup> And he commanded the steward of his house, saying, Fill the men’s sacks with food, as much as they can carry, and put every man’s money in his sack’s mouth.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/44#v2)</sup> And put my cup, the silver cup, in the sack’s mouth of the youngest, and his corn money. And he did according to the word that Joseph had spoken.

- Joseph commanded his steward to fill the men's sacks with food, return every man's money, and put his own silver cup in the sack's mouth of the youngest. The cup was planted so Joseph could hold Benjamin for a short period of time; he was not seeking harm, he was drawing out word of his father Jacob and whether he was yet alive.

**[Genesis 44:11-13](/bible/genesis/44#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/genesis/44#v11)</sup> Then they speedily took down every man his sack to the ground, and opened every man his sack.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/genesis/44#v12)</sup> And he searched, and began at the eldest, and left at the youngest: and the cup was found in Benjamin’s sack.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/genesis/44#v13)</sup> Then they rent their clothes, and laded every man his ass, and returned to the city.

- The steward searched, beginning at the eldest and leaving at the youngest, and the cup was found in Benjamin's sack; they rent their clothes and returned to the city. Benjamin found with the cup gave Joseph cause to hold him, and it set the stage for Judah to speak the whole history.

**[Genesis 44:27-29](/bible/genesis/44#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/genesis/44#v27)</sup> And thy servant my father said unto us, Ye know that my wife bare me two sons:
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/genesis/44#v28)</sup> And the one went out from me, and I said, Surely he is torn in pieces; and I saw him not since:
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/genesis/44#v29)</sup> And if ye take this also from me, and mischief befall him, ye shall bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to the grave.

- Thy servant my father said unto us, Ye know that my wife bare me two sons: that is Jacob speaking of Rachel, who bare Joseph and Benjamin, the eleventh and twelfth sons.
- Ye shall bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to the grave: Jacob never got over Joseph being taken away. Sorrow kills; give not over thy mind to heaviness, for the gladness of the heart is the life of man, and losing Benjamin also would have brought their father to the grave.

**[Genesis 44:30-34](/bible/genesis/44#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/genesis/44#v30)</sup> Now therefore when I come to thy servant my father, and the lad be not with us; seeing that his life is bound up in the lad’s life;
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/genesis/44#v31)</sup> It shall come to pass, when he seeth that the lad is not with us, that he will die: and thy servants shall bring down the gray hairs of thy servant our father with sorrow to the grave.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/genesis/44#v32)</sup> For thy servant became surety for the lad unto my father, saying, If I bring him not unto thee, then I shall bear the blame to my father for ever.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/genesis/44#v33)</sup> Now therefore, I pray thee, let thy servant abide instead of the lad a bondman to my lord; and let the lad go up with his brethren.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/genesis/44#v34)</sup> For how shall I go up to my father, and the lad be not with me? lest peradventure I see the evil that shall come on my father.

- Judah became surety for the lad unto his father, and offered to abide instead of the lad as a bondman: let the lad go up with his brethren. He knew his father's life was bound up in the lad's life, and he would not see the evil come on his father.
- Joseph in all of this was interrogating his brothers about the whereabouts and welfare of their father; holding Benjamin was the device that drew it out of Judah.

# Genesis 45: Joseph Made Known unto His Brethren

**[Genesis 45:1-3](/bible/genesis/45#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/45#v1)</sup> Then Joseph could not refrain himself before all them that stood by him; and he cried, Cause every man to go out from me. And there stood no man with him, while Joseph made himself known unto his brethren.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/45#v2)</sup> And he wept aloud: and the Egyptians and the house of Pharaoh heard.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/45#v3)</sup> And Joseph said unto his brethren, I am Joseph; doth my father yet live? And his brethren could not answer him; for they were troubled at his presence.

- Joseph made himself known, and his brethren could not answer him, for they were troubled at his presence. They could not recognize their own brother: he was sold as a teenager, and he stood before them now a grown man in his years.
- He was also clothed in the customs of Egypt: shaved, in Egyptian raiment, decked out as the second in command, and he had made himself strange to them and spoken by an interpreter though he understood their tongue.
- This does not prove the Israelites looked like the Egyptians; the peoples were all dark people. The change was his age, his dress, and the customs of the land he ruled in.

**[Genesis 45:25-28](/bible/genesis/45#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/genesis/45#v25)</sup> And they went up out of Egypt, and came into the land of Canaan unto Jacob their father,

- Joseph is yet alive, and he is governor over all the land of Egypt. Everything Egypt was at that point in time came through the influence of Joseph; the prosperity of that land was the favor the Lord had with Israel.
- The word of the Lord tried Joseph. Until the time that his word came he sat in the prison; then the king sent and loosed him, made him lord of his house and ruler of all his substance, and Joseph taught his senators wisdom. He was the teacher in Egypt, second in command, a king in all but title.
- To go from prison to governor is favor: the Lord causes the enemy to entreat his remnant well in the time of evil. Keep the commandments, for rulers are not a terror to good works.
- These are the characteristics of Joseph: my son, if thou come to serve the Lord, prepare thy soul for temptation; set thy heart aright and constantly endure, and make not haste in time of trouble. Do not be quick to leave the truth or find excuses in the time of trouble. Gold is tried in the fire, and acceptable men in the furnace of adversity.
- Better is the end of a thing than the beginning thereof: he began sold into slavery and put in prison under false pretenses, and the end of the thing was governor of Egypt, a father to Pharaoh. In the day of prosperity be joyful, but in the day of adversity consider the days that were good, and be patient in spirit: read the Bible more, study more, pray more, apply more.
- If thou faint in the day of adversity, thy strength is small. When trials come, read the afflictions of the forefathers and the attributes they had in those times, and apply them: forgiveness, favor, righteousness, mercy. There is no other way into the kingdom but through tribulation.
- Jacob knew there was corn in Egypt because Joseph had already told Pharaoh exactly what to do to save up in the time of tribulation; when Jacob heard, he sent out the fathers. Applying these attributes makes better men, women, brothers, and sisters, to bring forth the nation unto the kingdom of God.

# Genesis 46: Israel Comes into Egypt, Threescore and Ten Souls

Read the chapter → [Genesis 46](/bible/genesis/46)

**[Genesis 46:26-27](/bible/genesis/46#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/genesis/46#v26)</sup> All the souls that came with Jacob into Egypt, which came out of his loins, besides Jacob’s sons’ wives, all the souls were threescore and six;
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/genesis/46#v27)</sup> And the sons of Joseph, which were born him in Egypt, were two souls: all the souls of the house of Jacob, which came into Egypt, were threescore and ten.

- All the souls of the house of Jacob which came into Egypt were threescore and ten. A score is twenty, so three score is sixty, and ten more makes seventy souls.

**[Genesis 46:31-34](/bible/genesis/46#v31)**

> <sup>[31](/bible/genesis/46#v31)</sup> And Joseph said unto his brethren, and unto his father’s house, I will go up, and shew Pharaoh, and say unto him, My brethren, and my father’s house, which were in the land of Canaan, are come unto me;
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/genesis/46#v32)</sup> And the men are shepherds, for their trade hath been to feed cattle; and they have brought their flocks, and their herds, and all that they have.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/genesis/46#v33)</sup> And it shall come to pass, when Pharaoh shall call you, and shall say, What is your occupation?
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/genesis/46#v34)</sup> That ye shall say, Thy servants’ trade hath been about cattle from our youth even until now, both we, and also our fathers: that ye may dwell in the land of Goshen; for every shepherd is an abomination unto the Egyptians.

- Joseph instructed his brethren to tell Pharaoh their trade hath been about cattle from their youth, both they and also their fathers, that they might dwell in the land of Goshen; for every shepherd is an abomination unto the Egyptians.
- There is history behind that instruction. Somewhere between the fifteenth and seventeenth dynasties Egypt was ruled by the Hyksos, called the shepherd kings, a group of Canaanites or Hittites that occupied the land; the Egyptians did not deal with shepherds.
- Naming their trade set Israel apart in Goshen, which was fertile land, and the favor Joseph had was bestowed upon him through Pharaoh to give them that land.

> <sup>[20](/bible/genesis/47#v20)</sup> And Joseph bought all the land of Egypt for Pharaoh; for the Egyptians sold every man his field, because the famine prevailed over them: so the land became Pharaoh’s.

- When money failed in the land, the Egyptians gave their cattle for bread, and the next year their bodies and their lands; so the land became Pharaoh's. Pharaoh got everybody in servitude off the business dealings of Joseph and the favor the Lord had with him.

**[Genesis 47:23-26](/bible/genesis/47#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/genesis/47#v23)</sup> Then Joseph said unto the people, Behold, I have bought you this day and your land for Pharaoh: lo, here is seed for you, and ye shall sow the land.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/genesis/47#v24)</sup> And it shall come to pass in the increase, that ye shall give the fifth part unto Pharaoh, and four parts shall be your own, for seed of the field, and for your food, and for them of your households, and for food for your little ones.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/genesis/47#v25)</sup> And they said, Thou hast saved our lives: let us find grace in the sight of my lord, and we will be Pharaoh’s servants.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/genesis/47#v26)</sup> And Joseph made it a law over the land of Egypt unto this day, that Pharaoh should have the fifth part; except the land of the priests only, which became not Pharaoh’s.

- Joseph gave the people seed and made it a law over Egypt: give the fifth part unto Pharaoh, and four parts are your own for seed of the field, for your food, and for your households and little ones.
- Joseph teaches how to save money: pay the fifth part, and lay up for the years of trouble. That wisdom is how Egypt got through the famine, and it is the same perseverance to practice now in times of tribulation.

**[Genesis 47:27-31](/bible/genesis/47#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/genesis/47#v27)</sup> And Israel dwelt in the land of Egypt, in the country of Goshen; and they had possessions therein, and grew, and multiplied exceedingly.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/genesis/47#v28)</sup> And Jacob lived in the land of Egypt seventeen years: so the whole age of Jacob was an hundred forty and seven years.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/genesis/47#v29)</sup> And the time drew nigh that Israel must die: and he called his son Joseph, and said unto him, If now I have found grace in thy sight, put, I pray thee, thy hand under my thigh, and deal kindly and truly with me; bury me not, I pray thee, in Egypt:
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/genesis/47#v30)</sup> But I will lie with my fathers, and thou shalt carry me out of Egypt, and bury me in their buryingplace. And he said, I will do as thou hast said.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/genesis/47#v31)</sup> And he said, Swear unto me. And he sware unto him. And Israel bowed himself upon the bed’s head.

- Israel dwelt in the country of Goshen, had possessions therein, and grew and multiplied exceedingly. Jacob lived in Egypt seventeen years, so the whole age of Jacob was an hundred forty and seven years.
- Jacob made Joseph swear to carry him out of Egypt and bury him in the burying place of his fathers. By faith Jacob, when he was a dying, blessed both the sons of Joseph; and by faith Joseph made mention of the departing of the children of Israel and gave commandment concerning his bones. [Hebrews 11](/bible/hebrews/11) is the hall of faith, the faithful men of the sons of God, a chapter to read.
- Neither was there a man born like unto Joseph, a governor of his brethren, a stay of the people, whose bones were regarded of the Lord. To grow in spirit and be like him, put on the spirit of the forefathers.
