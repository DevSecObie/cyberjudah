**[Genesis 29:1-4](/bible/genesis/29#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/29#v1)</sup> Then Jacob went on his journey, and came into the land of the people of the east.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/29#v2)</sup> And he looked, and behold a well in the field, and, lo, there were three flocks of sheep lying by it; for out of that well they watered the flocks: and a great stone was upon the well’s mouth.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/29#v3)</sup> And thither were all the flocks gathered: and they rolled the stone from the well’s mouth, and watered the sheep, and put the stone again upon the well’s mouth in his place.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/genesis/29#v4)</sup> And Jacob said unto them, My brethren, whence be ye? And they said, Of Haran are we.

- Jacob went on his journey out of the land of Canaan and came into the land of the people of the east. On the map he goes north out of Canaan and then east to Haran in Padanaram; that is why the men at the well answer him, Of Haran are we.
- This is the same ground the family had already covered. Isaac charged Jacob to go to Padanaram to the house of Bethuel, and generations earlier Terah had brought Abram from Ur of the Chaldees up along the Euphrates, and they came unto Haran and dwelt there.

  Precepts:
  - **[Genesis 28:1-2](/bible/genesis/28#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/28#v1)</sup> And Isaac called Jacob, and blessed him, and charged him, and said unto him, Thou shalt not take a wife of the daughters of Canaan.
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/28#v2)</sup> Arise, go to Padan–aram, to the house of Bethuel thy mother’s father; and take thee a wife from thence of the daughters of Laban thy mother’s brother.

    Isaac charged Jacob to arise and go to Padanaram to the house of Bethuel; that is where Jacob was headed.
  - **[Genesis 11:31](/bible/genesis/11#v31)**
    > <sup>[31](/bible/genesis/11#v31)</sup> And Terah took Abram his son, and Lot the son of Haran his son’s son, and Sarai his daughter in law, his son Abram’s wife; and they went forth with them from Ur of the Chaldees, to go into the land of Canaan; and they came unto Haran, and dwelt there.

    Terah took Abram from Ur of the Chaldees to go into Canaan, and they came unto Haran and dwelt there; Jacob's road runs back to the family's old dwelling.

**[Genesis 29:5](/bible/genesis/29#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/genesis/29#v5)</sup> And he said unto them, Know ye Laban the son of Nahor? And they said, We know him.

- Jacob asks, Know ye Laban the son of Nahor, and the men of Haran know him. Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran, so Laban dwells in that area because the family remained there when Terah stopped at Haran.
- This ties the family line together: Abraham first journeyed from Ur of the Chaldees northwest to Haran, and now Jacob comes up from Canaan northeast to Haran. Because of the way Jacob traveled, he went back home and is dwelling amongst family.

  Precepts:
  - **[Genesis 11:26-27](/bible/genesis/11#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/genesis/11#v26)</sup> And Terah lived seventy years, and begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran.
    >
    > <sup>[27](/bible/genesis/11#v27)</sup> Now these are the generations of Terah: Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran; and Haran begat Lot.

    Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran; Laban is of Nahor's line, so Jacob has come to his own people.

**[Genesis 29:14-15](/bible/genesis/29#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/genesis/29#v14)</sup> And Laban said to him, Surely thou art my bone and my flesh. And he abode with him the space of a month.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/genesis/29#v15)</sup> And Laban said unto Jacob, Because thou art my brother, shouldest thou therefore serve me for nought? tell me, what shall thy wages be?

- Laban said unto Jacob, Surely thou art my bone and my flesh. In the scriptures that phrase means we are family; Jacob abode with him a month, and Laban, because Jacob was his brother, asked what his wages should be.

  Precepts:
  - **[Judges 9:1-2](/bible/judges/9#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/judges/9#v1)</sup> And Abimelech the son of Jerubbaal went to Shechem unto his mother’s brethren, and communed with them, and with all the family of the house of his mother’s father, saying,
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/judges/9#v2)</sup> Speak, I pray you, in the ears of all the men of Shechem, Whether is better for you, either that all the sons of Jerubbaal, which are threescore and ten persons, reign over you, or that one reign over you? remember also that I am your bone and your flesh.

    Abimelech told his mother's brethren, Remember also that I am your bone and your flesh; the phrase declares, we are family.
  - **[2 Samuel 19:12](/bible/2-samuel/19#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/2-samuel/19#v12)</sup> Ye are my brethren, ye are my bones and my flesh: wherefore then are ye the last to bring back the king?

    Ye are my brethren, ye are my bones and my flesh; further proof that bone and flesh speaks of kindred.

**[Genesis 29:16-18](/bible/genesis/29#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/genesis/29#v16)</sup> And Laban had two daughters: the name of the elder was Leah, and the name of the younger was Rachel.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/genesis/29#v17)</sup> Leah was tender eyed; but Rachel was beautiful and well favoured.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/genesis/29#v18)</sup> And Jacob loved Rachel; and said, I will serve thee seven years for Rachel thy younger daughter.

- Leah was tender eyed, but Rachel was beautiful and well favoured, and Jacob loved Rachel. He had already run to roll the stone from the well's mouth, watered her flock, and kissed her when she came out with the sheep; his eyes were fixed on her from the start.
- Do not be hasty to credit somebody based on their looks. Of course you want somebody you are attracted to, but commending a person for beauty alone gets a lot of people in trouble.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 36:22](/bible/sirach/36#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/sirach/36#v22)</sup> The beauty of a woman cheereth the countenance, and a man loveth nothing better.

    A man loveth nothing better than the beauty of a woman; that is what Jacob saw in Rachel.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 11:2](/bible/sirach/11#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/sirach/11#v2)</sup> Commend not a man for his beauty; neither abhor a man for his outward appearance.

    Do not be hasty to credit somebody for their beauty or their outward appearance.

**[Genesis 29:20](/bible/genesis/29#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/genesis/29#v20)</sup> And Jacob served seven years for Rachel; and they seemed unto him but a few days, for the love he had to her.

- Jacob served seven years for Rachel, and they seemed unto him but a few days for the love he had to her. That is how fine she was to him: with six and a half years still left, it felt like a couple of days.
- Men gather gold and silver and every goodly thing, then let all those things go and fix their eyes fast on a woman comely in favour and beauty. Many run out of their wits for a woman and become servants for their sakes; Jacob became a servant fourteen years, because he was deceived and served another seven.

**[Genesis 29:30-31](/bible/genesis/29#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/genesis/29#v30)</sup> And he went in also unto Rachel, and he loved also Rachel more than Leah, and served with him yet seven other years.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/genesis/29#v31)</sup> And when the Lord saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb: but Rachel was barren.

- Jacob loved Rachel more than Leah and served with Laban yet seven other years. When the Lord saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb; but Rachel was barren. Jacob was showing more favor to Rachel because he was more attracted to Rachel.
- Favour is deceitful and beauty is vain. You are not going to look the same at seventy that you did at twenty-five; our days are threescore years and ten, or by reason of strength fourscore, and outside a handful who retain a semblance of their youth, the looks fade along the way. A woman that feareth the Lord, she shall be praised.

    Favour is deceitful and beauty is vain, but a woman that feareth the Lord, she shall be praised.
