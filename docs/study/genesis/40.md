## Introduction

This is a reading session: [Genesis 40](/bible/genesis/40) through 43 were read straight through, with exposition gathered at the verses below. The reading covers the butler and the baker's dreams in the prison, Pharaoh's two dreams and Joseph's exaltation over Egypt, the first journey of Joseph's brethren to buy corn, and Benjamin brought down at the second journey. Questions were taken at the end of the reading; none produced affirmed teaching beyond what is entered under the verses.

**[Genesis 40:8](/bible/genesis/40#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/genesis/40#v8)</sup> And they said unto him, We have dreamed a dream, and there is no interpreter of it. And Joseph said unto them, Do not interpretations belong to God? tell me them, I pray you.

- We have dreamed a dream, and there is no interpreter of it. Joseph answered on another level: do not interpretations belong to God? Interpretations do belong to God.
- Daniel stood in the same understanding in Babylon; the king confessed that Daniel's God is a God of gods and a revealer of secrets. Joseph already understood that these things belong to the Most High.

  Precepts:
  - **[Daniel 2:47](/bible/daniel/2#v47)**
    > <sup>[47](/bible/daniel/2#v47)</sup> The king answered unto Daniel, and said, Of a truth it is, that your God is a God of gods, and a Lord of kings, and a revealer of secrets, seeing thou couldest reveal this secret.

    Your God is a God of gods and a revealer of secrets; the same God that gave Joseph interpretations gave them to Daniel.

**[Genesis 40:9-13](/bible/genesis/40#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/genesis/40#v9)</sup> And the chief butler told his dream to Joseph, and said to him, In my dream, behold, a vine was before me;
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/genesis/40#v10)</sup> And in the vine were three branches: and it was as though it budded, and her blossoms shot forth; and the clusters thereof brought forth ripe grapes:
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/genesis/40#v11)</sup> And Pharaoh’s cup was in my hand: and I took the grapes, and pressed them into Pharaoh’s cup, and I gave the cup into Pharaoh’s hand.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/genesis/40#v12)</sup> And Joseph said unto him, This is the interpretation of it: The three branches are three days:
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/genesis/40#v13)</sup> Yet within three days shall Pharaoh lift up thine head, and restore thee unto thy place: and thou shalt deliver Pharaoh’s cup into his hand, after the former manner when thou wast his butler.

- The chief butler's dream: a vine with three branches that budded and brought forth ripe grapes, pressed into Pharaoh's cup. Joseph interpreted it at once: the three branches are three days, and within three days Pharaoh would restore the butler to his butlership.

**[Genesis 40:16-19](/bible/genesis/40#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/genesis/40#v16)</sup> When the chief baker saw that the interpretation was good, he said unto Joseph, I also was in my dream, and, behold, I had three white baskets on my head:
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/genesis/40#v17)</sup> And in the uppermost basket there was of all manner of bakemeats for Pharaoh; and the birds did eat them out of the basket upon my head.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/genesis/40#v18)</sup> And Joseph answered and said, This is the interpretation thereof: The three baskets are three days:
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/genesis/40#v19)</sup> Yet within three days shall Pharaoh lift up thy head from off thee, and shall hang thee on a tree; and the birds shall eat thy flesh from off thee.

- The chief baker's three white baskets were also three days, but his end was hanging on a tree with the birds eating his flesh from off him.
- Both men saw variations of three, and in both dreams the three stood for three days. The dream is not always what is seen: the vision of dreams is the resemblance of one thing to another, even as the likeness of a face to a face. You may see yourself steal a piece of bread in a dream, and the meaning is that somebody will steal something from you in the days ahead; one thing resembles another.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 34:3](/bible/sirach/34#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/sirach/34#v3)</sup> The vision of dreams is the resemblance of one thing to another, even as the likeness of a face to a face.

    The vision of dreams is the resemblance of one thing to another; the threes in both dreams stood for three days.

**[Genesis 40:20-23](/bible/genesis/40#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/genesis/40#v20)</sup> And it came to pass the third day, which was Pharaoh’s birthday, that he made a feast unto all his servants: and he lifted up the head of the chief butler and of the chief baker among his servants.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/genesis/40#v21)</sup> And he restored the chief butler unto his butlership again; and he gave the cup into Pharaoh’s hand:
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/genesis/40#v22)</sup> But he hanged the chief baker: as Joseph had interpreted to them.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/genesis/40#v23)</sup> Yet did not the chief butler remember Joseph, but forgat him.

- On the third day, Pharaoh's birthday, it happened just as Joseph laid it out: the chief butler was restored unto his butlership again, and the chief baker was hanged.
- Birthdays are heathen customs, and wicked things take place at them: at this birthday feast a man was put to death before everybody, and on Herod's birthday the daughter of Herodias danced and John the Baptist was beheaded.
- Yet did not the chief butler remember Joseph, but forgat him.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 14:6-8](/bible/matthew/14#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/matthew/14#v6)</sup> But when Herod’s birthday was kept, the daughter of Herodias danced before them, and pleased Herod.
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/matthew/14#v7)</sup> Whereupon he promised with an oath to give her whatsoever she would ask.
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/matthew/14#v8)</sup> And she, being before instructed of her mother, said, Give me here John Baptist’s head in a charger.

    On Herod's birthday the dancing pleased him, and the head of John the Baptist was demanded in a charger; another wicked occurrence on a birthday.
