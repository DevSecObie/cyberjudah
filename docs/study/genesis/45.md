**[Genesis 45:1-3](/bible/genesis/45#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/45#v1)</sup> Then Joseph could not refrain himself before all them that stood by him; and he cried, Cause every man to go out from me. And there stood no man with him, while Joseph made himself known unto his brethren.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/45#v2)</sup> And he wept aloud: and the Egyptians and the house of Pharaoh heard.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/45#v3)</sup> And Joseph said unto his brethren, I am Joseph; doth my father yet live? And his brethren could not answer him; for they were troubled at his presence.

- Joseph made himself known, and his brethren could not answer him, for they were troubled at his presence. They could not recognize their own brother: he was sold as a teenager, and he stood before them now a grown man in his years.
- He was also clothed in the customs of Egypt: shaved, in Egyptian raiment, decked out as the second in command, and he had made himself strange to them and spoken by an interpreter though he understood their tongue.
- This does not prove the Israelites looked like the Egyptians; the peoples were all dark people. The change was his age, his dress, and the customs of the land he ruled in.

**[Genesis 45:4-8](/bible/genesis/45#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/genesis/45#v4)</sup> And Joseph said unto his brethren, Come near to me, I pray you. And they came near. And he said, I am Joseph your brother, whom ye sold into Egypt.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/genesis/45#v5)</sup> Now therefore be not grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that ye sold me hither: for God did send me before you to preserve life.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/genesis/45#v6)</sup> For these two years hath the famine been in the land: and yet there are five years, in the which there shall neither be earing nor harvest.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/genesis/45#v7)</sup> And God sent me before you to preserve you a posterity in the earth, and to save your lives by a great deliverance.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/genesis/45#v8)</sup> So now it was not you that sent me hither, but God: and he hath made me a father to Pharaoh, and lord of all his house, and a ruler throughout all the land of Egypt.

- Be not grieved nor angry with yourselves: it was not you that sent me hither, but God. The Most High sent Joseph before them to preserve life and to save the whole house of Israel by a great deliverance; had he not been in Egypt, they would have died in the famine.
- Joseph kept the law of forgiveness: forgive thy brother not until seven times, but until seventy times seven. His own brothers sold him into slavery, and he forgave them; that is the law Israel must keep with one another today.
- The famine itself was called by the Most High, and the man was sent before them, even Joseph, sold for a servant, whose feet they hurt with fetters. The fetters and the iron are the prison where the king's prisoners were bound.

**[Genesis 45:25-28](/bible/genesis/45#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/genesis/45#v25)</sup> And they went up out of Egypt, and came into the land of Canaan unto Jacob their father,
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/genesis/45#v26)</sup> And told him, saying, Joseph is yet alive, and he is governor over all the land of Egypt. And Jacob’s heart fainted, for he believed them not.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/genesis/45#v27)</sup> And they told him all the words of Joseph, which he had said unto them: and when he saw the wagons which Joseph had sent to carry him, the spirit of Jacob their father revived:
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/genesis/45#v28)</sup> And Israel said, It is enough; Joseph my son is yet alive: I will go and see him before I die.

- Joseph is yet alive, and he is governor over all the land of Egypt. Everything Egypt was at that point in time came through the influence of Joseph; the prosperity of that land was the favor the Lord had with Israel.
- The word of the Lord tried Joseph. Until the time that his word came he sat in the prison; then the king sent and loosed him, made him lord of his house and ruler of all his substance, and Joseph taught his senators wisdom. He was the teacher in Egypt, second in command, a king in all but title.
- To go from prison to governor is favor: the Lord causes the enemy to entreat his remnant well in the time of evil. Keep the commandments, for rulers are not a terror to good works.
- These are the characteristics of Joseph: my son, if thou come to serve the Lord, prepare thy soul for temptation; set thy heart aright and constantly endure, and make not haste in time of trouble. Do not be quick to leave the truth or find excuses in the time of trouble. Gold is tried in the fire, and acceptable men in the furnace of adversity.
- Better is the end of a thing than the beginning thereof: he began sold into slavery and put in prison under false pretenses, and the end of the thing was governor of Egypt, a father to Pharaoh. In the day of prosperity be joyful, but in the day of adversity consider the days that were good, and be patient in spirit: read the Bible more, study more, pray more, apply more.
- If thou faint in the day of adversity, thy strength is small. When trials come, read the afflictions of the forefathers and the attributes they had in those times, and apply them: forgiveness, favor, righteousness, mercy. There is no other way into the kingdom but through tribulation.
- Jacob knew there was corn in Egypt because Joseph had already told Pharaoh exactly what to do to save up in the time of tribulation; when Jacob heard, he sent out the fathers. Applying these attributes makes better men, women, brothers, and sisters, to bring forth the nation unto the kingdom of God.
