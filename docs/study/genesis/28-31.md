## Introduction

This session read [Genesis 28](/bible/genesis/28) through 31 straight through and then opened the key points of each chapter. The recording of part one ends with the chapter 28 teaching; the teaching on chapters 29 through 31 in these notes comes from the part two recording of the session.

**[Genesis 28:1-4](/bible/genesis/28#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/28#v1)</sup> And Isaac called Jacob, and blessed him, and charged him, and said unto him, Thou shalt not take a wife of the daughters of Canaan.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/28#v2)</sup> Arise, go to Padan–aram, to the house of Bethuel thy mother’s father; and take thee a wife from thence of the daughters of Laban thy mother’s brother.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/28#v3)</sup> And God Almighty bless thee, and make thee fruitful, and multiply thee, that thou mayest be a multitude of people;
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/genesis/28#v4)</sup> And give thee the blessing of Abraham, to thee, and to thy seed with thee; that thou mayest inherit the land wherein thou art a stranger, which God gave unto Abraham.

- Isaac called Jacob, blessed him, and charged him: Thou shalt not take a wife of the daughters of Canaan; arise, go to Padanaram, to the house of Bethuel, and take a wife of the daughters of Laban.
- The fathers from the beginning, Noah, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, married wives of their own kindred and were blessed in their children. Abraham gave the same charge for Isaac, and Isaac gives it to Jacob; the instruction was kept in the family line from the beginning.
- With the charge came the blessing of Abraham, passed now to Jacob and his seed: to be fruitful, to multiply into a multitude of people, and to inherit the land promised to Abraham.

# Genesis 29: Bone and Flesh in Haran

**[Genesis 29:1-4](/bible/genesis/29#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/29#v1)</sup> Then Jacob went on his journey, and came into the land of the people of the east.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/29#v2)</sup> And he looked, and behold a well in the field, and, lo, there were three flocks of sheep lying by it; for out of that well they watered the flocks: and a great stone was upon the well’s mouth.

>
> <sup>[4](/bible/genesis/29#v4)</sup> And Jacob said unto them, My brethren, whence be ye? And they said, Of Haran are we.

- Jacob went on his journey out of the land of Canaan and came into the land of the people of the east. On the map he goes north out of Canaan and then east to Haran in Padanaram; that is why the men at the well answer him, Of Haran are we.
- This is the same ground the family had already covered. Isaac charged Jacob to go to Padanaram to the house of Bethuel, and generations earlier Terah had brought Abram from Ur of the Chaldees up along the Euphrates, and they came unto Haran and dwelt there.

- Jacob asks, Know ye Laban the son of Nahor, and the men of Haran know him. Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran, so Laban dwells in that area because the family remained there when Terah stopped at Haran.
- This ties the family line together: Abraham first journeyed from Ur of the Chaldees northwest to Haran, and now Jacob comes up from Canaan northeast to Haran. Because of the way Jacob traveled, he went back home and is dwelling amongst family.

# Genesis 30: Trouble in the Flesh and a Righteous Hire

Read the chapter → [Genesis 30](/bible/genesis/30)

**[Genesis 30:1-2](/bible/genesis/30#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/30#v1)</sup> And when Rachel saw that she bare Jacob no children, Rachel envied her sister; and said unto Jacob, Give me children, or else I die.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/30#v2)</sup> And Jacob’s anger was kindled against Rachel: and he said, Am I in God’s stead, who hath withheld from thee the fruit of the womb?

- Rachel saw that she bare Jacob no children, envied her sister, and told Jacob, Give me children, or else I die. Jacob's anger was kindled against her: Am I in God's stead, who hath withheld from thee the fruit of the womb? Jacob was not the problem; he was having no trouble producing children.
- Their arguing is the trouble in the flesh the scriptures speak of. If thou marry, thou hast not sinned, but such shall have trouble in the flesh: it is not all going to be sweet all the time. Rachel's trouble in the flesh was that she envied her sister and could not bear children, and she took it out on Jacob.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Corinthians 7:28](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v28)**
> <sup>[28](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v28)</sup> But and if thou marry, thou hast not sinned; and if a virgin marry, she hath not sinned. Nevertheless such shall have trouble in the flesh: but I spare you.

    They that marry have not sinned, but such shall have trouble in the flesh; in marriage there will be some hard times.

**[Genesis 30:14-18](/bible/genesis/30#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/genesis/30#v14)</sup> And Reuben went in the days of wheat harvest, and found mandrakes in the field, and brought them unto his mother Leah. Then Rachel said to Leah, Give me, I pray thee, of thy son’s mandrakes.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/genesis/30#v15)</sup> And she said unto her, Is it a small matter that thou hast taken my husband? and wouldest thou take away my son’s mandrakes also? And Rachel said, Therefore he shall lie with thee to night for thy son’s mandrakes.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/genesis/30#v16)</sup> And Jacob came out of the field in the evening, and Leah went out to meet him, and said, Thou must come in unto me; for surely I have hired thee with my son’s mandrakes. And he lay with her that night.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/genesis/30#v17)</sup> And God hearkened unto Leah, and she conceived, and bare Jacob the fifth son.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/genesis/30#v18)</sup> And Leah said, God hath given me my hire, because I have given my maiden to my husband: and she called his name Issachar.

- Reuben found mandrakes in the field in the days of wheat harvest and brought them unto his mother Leah. Rachel asked for them, and Leah answered, Is it a small matter that thou hast taken my husband? and wouldest thou take away my son's mandrakes also? So Rachel granted, He shall lie with thee to night for thy son's mandrakes.
- When Jacob came out of the field in the evening, Leah went out to meet him: Thou must come in unto me, for surely I have hired thee with my son's mandrakes. He lay with her that night, and she bare a son and called him Issachar.
- The mandrake is a flower with several uses; it was used medicinally and as an aphrodisiac, a help for those having problems conceiving. That is why the sisters set such a price on them.

**[Genesis 30:22-24](/bible/genesis/30#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/genesis/30#v22)</sup> And God remembered Rachel, and God hearkened to her, and opened her womb.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/genesis/30#v23)</sup> And she conceived, and bare a son; and said, God hath taken away my reproach:
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/genesis/30#v24)</sup> And she called his name Joseph; and said, The Lord shall add to me another son.

- God remembered Rachel, hearkened unto her, and opened her womb; she conceived and bare a son and called his name Joseph, saying, God hath taken away my reproach.
- It was not the mandrakes that gave her the child; it is only coincidence that the baby came after she had them. The Most High is the one who showed her mercy and opened up her womb, for he maketh the barren woman to keep house and to be a joyful mother of children.

  Precepts:

  - **[Psalms 113:9](/bible/psalms/113#v9)**

    He maketh the barren woman to keep house and to be a joyful mother of children; the Most High is the one who opened Rachel's womb.

- Jacob answered that Laban knew how he had served him and how the cattle fared with him: it was little which thou hadst before I came, and it is now increased unto a multitude; the LORD hath blessed thee since my coming. And now, when shall I provide for mine own house also? A man looks to provide for his own house.

> <sup>[34](/bible/genesis/30#v34)</sup> And Laban said, Behold, I would it might be according to thy word.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/genesis/30#v35)</sup> And he removed that day the he goats that were ringstraked and spotted, and all the she goats that were speckled and spotted, and every one that had some white in it, and all the brown among the sheep, and gave them into the hand of his sons.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/genesis/30#v36)</sup> And he set three days’ journey betwixt himself and Jacob: and Jacob fed the rest of Laban’s flocks.

- Jacob said, Thou shalt not give me any thing. He asked no gift, only terms: he would keep feeding Laban's flock, and the speckled, spotted, and brown cattle among the sheep and goats from that day forward would be his hire.
- That refusal is the spirit our forefathers rolled in. We have to come back to these scriptures and to the understanding of who we are, because our people are lost and have fainted from that spirit; we cannot roll in a handout spirit, and when we repent we have to conduct ourselves as men and provide for our own houses, for the Most High provides.
- So shall my righteousness answer for me in time to come: Jacob set terms that could be checked in the open. Every one that was not speckled and spotted among the goats and brown among the sheep would be counted stolen with him. Judge me on my works; my word is my bond. He said it because he knew they were going to have something bad to say about his increase.
- Laban agreed, then removed that same day the he goats that were ringstraked and spotted, all the speckled and spotted she goats, and the brown among the sheep, and gave them into the hand of his own sons, setting three days' journey between himself and Jacob.

# Genesis 31: The Return Home and Laban's Idols

Read the chapter → [Genesis 31](/bible/genesis/31)

**[Genesis 31:1-2](/bible/genesis/31#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/31#v1)</sup> And he heard the words of Laban’s sons, saying, Jacob hath taken away all that was our father’s; and of that which was our father’s hath he gotten all this glory.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/31#v2)</sup> And Jacob beheld the countenance of Laban, and, behold, it was not toward him as before.

- Jacob heard the words of Laban's sons, saying, Jacob hath taken away all that was our father's, and of that which was our father's hath he gotten all this glory; and Jacob beheld the countenance of Laban, and behold, it was not toward him as before.
- Remember, Laban had just acknowledged, I have learned by experience that the LORD hath blessed me for thy sake; now he is looking at Jacob sideways because of what his sons said. That is exactly why Jacob said, So shall my righteousness answer for me: he dealt righteously in the open so his dealings would be his answer.

**[Genesis 31:3](/bible/genesis/31#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/31#v3)</sup> And the Lord said unto Jacob, Return unto the land of thy fathers, and to thy kindred; and I will be with thee.

- The LORD said unto Jacob, Return unto the land of thy fathers, and to thy kindred; and I will be with thee. The command to go home came with the promise of the Most High's presence.

**[Genesis 31:17-19](/bible/genesis/31#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/genesis/31#v17)</sup> Then Jacob rose up, and set his sons and his wives upon camels;
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/genesis/31#v18)</sup> And he carried away all his cattle, and all his goods which he had gotten, the cattle of his getting, which he had gotten in Padan–aram, for to go to Isaac his father in the land of Canaan.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/genesis/31#v19)</sup> And Laban went to shear his sheep: and Rachel had stolen the images that were her father’s.

- Jacob rose up, set his sons and his wives upon camels, and carried away all his cattle and all the goods he had gotten in Padanaram, to go to Isaac his father in the land of Canaan; he was returning home.
- Laban went to shear his sheep, and Rachel had stolen the images that were her father's. Remember what we read in Proverbs about beauty: yes, she was fine, but she was a thief. She might have been a fine thief, but she was a thief.

  Precepts:
  - **[Proverbs 31:30](/bible/proverbs/31#v30)**
> <sup>[30](/bible/proverbs/31#v30)</sup> Favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain: but a woman that feareth the Lord, she shall be praised.

    Favour is deceitful and beauty is vain; Rachel's beauty did not keep her from being a thief.

**[Genesis 31:30-32](/bible/genesis/31#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/genesis/31#v30)</sup> And now, though thou wouldest needs be gone, because thou sore longedst after thy father’s house, yet wherefore hast thou stolen my gods?
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/genesis/31#v31)</sup> And Jacob answered and said to Laban, Because I was afraid: for I said, Peradventure thou wouldest take by force thy daughters from me.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/genesis/31#v32)</sup> With whomsoever thou findest thy gods, let him not live: before our brethren discern thou what is thine with me, and take it to thee. For Jacob knew not that Rachel had stolen them.

- Laban overtook Jacob and asked, Wherefore hast thou stolen my gods? That question tells you what Laban was: Laban was an idolater.
- The scriptures prove it. Your fathers dwelt on the other side of the flood in old time, even Terah, the father of Abraham and the father of Nachor, and they served other gods. Laban was of the house of Nahor, an idolater just like his fathers. That is why the Most High told Abram to get out from his country and from his kindred: he separated Abram because of what he had set up for Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and for us, that we not be idolaters.
- Jacob answered that he went away because he was afraid Laban would take his daughters from him by force, and said, With whomsoever thou findest thy gods, let him not live. For Jacob knew not that Rachel had stolen them.
