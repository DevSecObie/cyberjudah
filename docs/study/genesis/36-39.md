## Introduction

This reading covers [Genesis 36](/bible/genesis/36) through 39: the generations of Esau, Joseph the dreamer sold into Egypt, Judah and Tamar, and Joseph in Potiphar's house.

**[Genesis 36:1](/bible/genesis/36#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/36#v1)</sup> Now these are the generations of Esau, who is Edom.

- Now these are the generations of Esau, who is Edom. Esau is Edom, and this teaching identifies Edom with the people called the white man today.
- Esau came out of the womb red, all over like an hairy garment; his blood could be seen through his skin for lack of melanin, and his name Esau goes into wasted away. Jacob was not described, because Jacob looked like everyone from Adam down to his time; the man of melanin needed no description.
- Esau was called Edom, which means red, because he sold his birthright for that same red pottage.

  Precepts:
  - **[Genesis 25:24-26](/bible/genesis/25#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/genesis/25#v24)</sup> And when her days to be delivered were fulfilled, behold, there were twins in her womb.
    >
    > <sup>[25](/bible/genesis/25#v25)</sup> And the first came out red, all over like an hairy garment; and they called his name Esau.
    >
    > <sup>[26](/bible/genesis/25#v26)</sup> And after that came his brother out, and his hand took hold on Esau’s heel; and his name was called Jacob: and Isaac was threescore years old when she bare them.

    The first came out red, all over like an hairy garment; the twins were two nations, polar opposites.
  - **[Genesis 25:30](/bible/genesis/25#v30)**
    > <sup>[30](/bible/genesis/25#v30)</sup> And Esau said to Jacob, Feed me, I pray thee, with that same red pottage; for I am faint: therefore was his name called Edom.

    Feed me with that same red pottage; therefore was his name called Edom, which is red.

- Esau took his wives of the daughters of Canaan and of Ishmael. He was the first of the Edomites; there were no women of his nation yet, so his lineage began through those wives. Genesis is Bereshith, the beginning of nations.

**[Genesis 36:6-8](/bible/genesis/36#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/genesis/36#v6)</sup> And Esau took his wives, and his sons, and his daughters, and all the persons of his house, and his cattle, and all his beasts, and all his substance, which he had got in the land of Canaan; and went into the country from the face of his brother Jacob.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/genesis/36#v7)</sup> For their riches were more than that they might dwell together; and the land wherein they were strangers could not bear them because of their cattle.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/genesis/36#v8)</sup> Thus dwelt Esau in mount Seir: Esau is Edom.

- Esau went from the face of his brother because their riches were more than that they might dwell together, the same as with Abram and Lot in [Genesis 13](/bible/genesis/13). Esau was rich at this time, out of the house of Isaac and Abraham.
- Thus dwelt Esau in mount Seir. Mount Seir is the clefts of the rock; the vision of Obadiah speaks to Edom as thou that dwellest in the clefts of the rock, whose pride hath deceived him.

  Precepts:
  - **[Obadiah 1:3](/bible/obadiah/1#v3)**

    The pride of thine heart hath deceived thee, thou that dwellest in the clefts of the rock; that is Edom in mount Seir.

**[Genesis 36:9-11](/bible/genesis/36#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/genesis/36#v9)</sup> And these are the generations of Esau the father of the Edomites in mount Seir:
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/genesis/36#v10)</sup> These are the names of Esau’s sons; Eliphaz the son of Adah the wife of Esau, Reuel the son of Bashemath the wife of Esau.

> <sup>[11](/bible/genesis/36#v11)</sup> And the sons of Eliphaz were Teman, Omar, Zepho, and Gatam, and Kenaz.

- Esau is the father of the Edomites in mount Seir, and his grandson Teman, son of Eliphaz, is identified in this teaching with Germany; the reference work Edom and Germany lays that connection out.
- The prophecy over Teman stands: the wise men shall be destroyed out of Edom and understanding out of the mount of Esau, and the mighty men of Teman shall be dismayed. Much of the world's science and weapons technology has come out of Germany, and the word says that wisdom will be destroyed.

  Precepts:
  - **[Obadiah 1:8-9](/bible/obadiah/1#v8)**

    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/obadiah/1#v9)</sup> And thy mighty men, O Teman, shall be dismayed, to the end that every one of the mount of Esau may be cut off by slaughter.

    The wise men of Edom and the mighty men of Teman shall be destroyed, that every one of the mount of Esau may be cut off.

**[Genesis 36:12](/bible/genesis/36#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/genesis/36#v12)</sup> And Timna was concubine to Eliphaz Esau’s son; and she bare to Eliphaz Amalek: these were the sons of Adah Esau’s wife.

- Timna was concubine to Eliphaz, and she bare Amalek. Amalek came of a concubine, a lesser wife, which is why the word bastard is studied here: in the scriptures it means a child of incest or one born outside the proper marriage.
- The word says a bastard shall dwell in Ashdod, and Ashdod is the area of modern day Tel Aviv; this teaching places the line of Amalek dwelling there today.

  Precepts:

    And a bastard shall dwell in Ashdod; the prophecy names the place where that seed sits.

# Genesis 37: Joseph the Dreamer

Read the chapter → [Genesis 37](/bible/genesis/37)

**[Genesis 37:1-2](/bible/genesis/37#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/37#v1)</sup> And Jacob dwelt in the land wherein his father was a stranger, in the land of Canaan.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/37#v2)</sup> These are the generations of Jacob. Joseph, being seventeen years old, was feeding the flock with his brethren; and the lad was with the sons of Bilhah, and with the sons of Zilpah, his father’s wives: and Joseph brought unto his father their evil report.

- Joseph brought unto his father the evil report of his brethren. That was law, not tale bearing: if a soul hear swearing and be a witness and do not utter it, he shall bear his iniquity. The law was already around, and Joseph kept it.

  Precepts:
  - **[Leviticus 5:1](/bible/leviticus/5#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/leviticus/5#v1)</sup> And if a soul sin, and hear the voice of swearing, and is a witness, whether he hath seen or known of it; if he do not utter it, then he shall bear his iniquity.

    A witness that does not utter what he has seen bears his own iniquity; Joseph uttered the evil report according to that law.

**[Genesis 37:3-4](/bible/genesis/37#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/37#v3)</sup> Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his children, because he was the son of his old age: and he made him a coat of many colours.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/genesis/37#v4)</sup> And when his brethren saw that their father loved him more than all his brethren, they hated him, and could not speak peaceably unto him.

- Israel loved Joseph more than all his children, the son of his old age, and made him a coat of many colours; and his brethren hated him and could not speak peaceably unto him.
- That is real hatred among brothers, and the same spirit is on Israel today, where a brother cannot even receive a shalom in peace.

**[Genesis 37:5-8](/bible/genesis/37#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/genesis/37#v5)</sup> And Joseph dreamed a dream, and he told it his brethren: and they hated him yet the more.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/genesis/37#v6)</sup> And he said unto them, Hear, I pray you, this dream which I have dreamed:
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/genesis/37#v7)</sup> For, behold, we were binding sheaves in the field, and, lo, my sheaf arose, and also stood upright; and, behold, your sheaves stood round about, and made obeisance to my sheaf.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/genesis/37#v8)</sup> And his brethren said to him, Shalt thou indeed reign over us? or shalt thou indeed have dominion over us? And they hated him yet the more for his dreams, and for his words.

- Joseph dreamed, and the sheaves of his brethren made obeisance to his sheaf. The Lord speaks by the prophets in visions and similitudes; the vision of dreams is the resemblance of one thing to another, as the likeness of a face to a face.
- His brethren understood the dream, that he should reign over them, and hated him yet the more for his dreams and for his words.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hosea 12:10](/bible/hosea/12#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/hosea/12#v10)</sup> I have also spoken by the prophets, and I have multiplied visions, and used similitudes, by the ministry of the prophets.

    The Most High multiplies visions and uses similitudes by the ministry of the prophets.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 34:3](/bible/sirach/34#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/sirach/34#v3)</sup> The vision of dreams is the resemblance of one thing to another, even as the likeness of a face to a face.

    The vision of dreams is the resemblance of one thing to another; the sheaf stood for Joseph and the sheaves for his brethren.

**[Genesis 37:9-10](/bible/genesis/37#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/genesis/37#v9)</sup> And he dreamed yet another dream, and told it his brethren, and said, Behold, I have dreamed a dream more; and, behold, the sun and the moon and the eleven stars made obeisance to me.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/genesis/37#v10)</sup> And he told it to his father, and to his brethren: and his father rebuked him, and said unto him, What is this dream that thou hast dreamed? Shall I and thy mother and thy brethren indeed come to bow down ourselves to thee to the earth?

- In the second dream the sun, the moon, and the eleven stars made obeisance to Joseph. Jacob interpreted it at once: shall I and thy mother and thy brethren indeed come to bow down ourselves to thee?
- The same figures mark Israel: the woman clothed with the sun with a crown of twelve stars is the daughter of Zion, the nation of Israel, and the twelve stars are the tribes.
- The dream came true. In the famine the whole house of Jacob came down to Joseph in Egypt, and he placed them in the best of the land and nourished them with bread; they bowed to the one the Most High had set over them.

  Precepts:
  - **[Revelation 12:1](/bible/revelation/12#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/revelation/12#v1)</sup> And there appeared a great wonder in heaven; a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and upon her head a crown of twelve stars:

    The woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet and a crown of twelve stars, figures Israel and her tribes.
  - **[Jeremiah 6:2](/bible/jeremiah/6#v2)**
    > <sup>[2](/bible/jeremiah/6#v2)</sup> I have likened the daughter of Zion to a comely and delicate woman.

    The daughter of Zion is likened to a comely and delicate woman; the woman of the vision is Israel.
  - **[Genesis 47:11-12](/bible/genesis/47#v11)**

**[Genesis 37:11](/bible/genesis/37#v11)**

- His brethren envied him, but his father observed the saying. Envy consumes a man, and such a man shall have no fellowship with wisdom; when envy takes over, wisdom leaves.

  Precepts:
  - **[Wisdom of Solomon 6:23](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/6#v23)**

**[Genesis 38:8-10](/bible/genesis/38#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/genesis/38#v8)</sup> And Judah said unto Onan, Go in unto thy brother’s wife, and marry her, and raise up seed to thy brother.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/genesis/38#v9)</sup> And Onan knew that the seed should not be his; and it came to pass, when he went in unto his brother’s wife, that he spilled it on the ground, lest that he should give seed to his brother.

- Onan was told to go in unto his brother's wife and raise up seed to his brother, and he refused, spilling it on the ground, and the Lord slew him also.
- The sin was refusing to raise up seed to his brother, not the act itself; do not build a doctrine on the wrong point. What happened here later became law: the husband's brother raises up the name of the dead, and he that refuses has his shoe loosed and his face spat in, and his house carries the reproach.

  Precepts:
  - **[Deuteronomy 25:5-9](/bible/deuteronomy/25#v5)**

# Genesis 39: Joseph in Potiphar's House

Read the chapter → [Genesis 39](/bible/genesis/39)

**[Genesis 39:1-6](/bible/genesis/39#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/39#v1)</sup> And Joseph was brought down to Egypt; and Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh, captain of the guard, an Egyptian, bought him of the hands of the Ishmeelites, which had brought him down thither.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/39#v2)</sup> And the Lord was with Joseph, and he was a prosperous man; and he was in the house of his master the Egyptian.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/39#v3)</sup> And his master saw that the Lord was with him, and that the Lord made all that he did to prosper in his hand.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/genesis/39#v4)</sup> And Joseph found grace in his sight, and he served him: and he made him overseer over his house, and all that he had he put into his hand.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/genesis/39#v5)</sup> And it came to pass from the time that he had made him overseer in his house, and over all that he had, that the Lord blessed the Egyptian’s house for Joseph’s sake; and the blessing of the Lord was upon all that he had in the house, and in the field.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/genesis/39#v6)</sup> And he left all that he had in Joseph’s hand; and he knew not ought he had, save the bread which he did eat. And Joseph was a goodly person, and well favoured.

- The Lord was with Joseph in his captivity, and his master saw that the Lord made all that he did to prosper; Potiphar made him overseer over his house, and the Lord blessed the Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake.
- The Most High deals with His people even in captivity and gives them compassion before them who carried them captive; the favor on Joseph is the pattern of that prayer.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Kings 8:50](/bible/1-kings/8#v50)**
    > <sup>[50](/bible/1-kings/8#v50)</sup> And forgive thy people that have sinned against thee, and all their transgressions wherein they have transgressed against thee, and give them compassion before them who carried them captive, that they may have compassion on them:

    Give them compassion before them who carried them captives; the Lord gave Joseph that compassion in Egypt.

**[Genesis 39:7-9](/bible/genesis/39#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/genesis/39#v7)</sup> And it came to pass after these things, that his master’s wife cast her eyes upon Joseph; and she said, Lie with me.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/genesis/39#v8)</sup> But he refused, and said unto his master’s wife, Behold, my master wotteth not what is with me in the house, and he hath committed all that he hath to my hand;
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/genesis/39#v9)</sup> There is none greater in this house than I; neither hath he kept back any thing from me but thee, because thou art his wife: how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?

- His master's wife cast her eyes upon Joseph and said, Lie with me; and he refused: how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?
- Adultery was known as sin from the beginning, long before Sinai. Abraham kept the commandments, statutes, and laws; and Abimelech rebuked Isaac because one of the people might have lain with his wife and brought guiltiness upon them. The nations themselves knew adultery brought guilt.

  Precepts:
  - **[Genesis 26:5](/bible/genesis/26#v5)**

    Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws; the laws are nothing new.
  - **[Genesis 26:10](/bible/genesis/26#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/genesis/26#v10)</sup> And Abimelech said, What is this thou hast done unto us? one of the people might lightly have lien with thy wife, and thou shouldest have brought guiltiness upon us.

    Abimelech knew lightly lying with another man's wife would have brought guiltiness upon them; adultery was known sin.

**[Genesis 39:10-12](/bible/genesis/39#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/genesis/39#v10)</sup> And it came to pass, as she spake to Joseph day by day, that he hearkened not unto her, to lie by her, or to be with her.

> <sup>[11](/bible/genesis/39#v11)</sup> And it came to pass about this time, that Joseph went into the house to do his business; and there was none of the men of the house there within.

> <sup>[12](/bible/genesis/39#v12)</sup> And she caught him by his garment, saying, Lie with me: and he left his garment in her hand, and fled, and got him out.

- She spake to Joseph day by day, and he hearkened not. Then she arranged the empty house, caught him by his garment, and he left his garment in her hand and fled.
- That is the example: flee from sin as from the face of a serpent, for if thou comest too near it, it will bite thee; the teeth thereof are as the teeth of a lion. Joseph knew thirty more seconds of entertaining her would be the bite.

  Precepts:
