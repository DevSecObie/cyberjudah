## Introduction

This reading covers [Genesis 44](/bible/genesis/44) through 47: the silver cup planted in Benjamin's sack, Joseph made known unto his brethren, Israel's going down into Egypt with threescore and ten souls, and the family planted in Goshen through the famine years. The session opens back at [Genesis 43:30-34](/bible/genesis/43#v30) to bring the account up to speed: the Egyptians might not eat bread with the Hebrews, for that is an abomination unto the Egyptians; even while Israel sojourned in Egypt the Lord put a difference between the Egyptians and Israel, as [Exodus 11:7](/bible/exodus/11#v7) shows. The exposition after the reading draws out the characteristics of Joseph: forgiveness, favor, righteousness, mercy, and perseverance through adversity.

**[Genesis 44:1-2](/bible/genesis/44#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/44#v1)</sup> And he commanded the steward of his house, saying, Fill the men’s sacks with food, as much as they can carry, and put every man’s money in his sack’s mouth.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/44#v2)</sup> And put my cup, the silver cup, in the sack’s mouth of the youngest, and his corn money. And he did according to the word that Joseph had spoken.

- Joseph commanded his steward to fill the men's sacks with food, return every man's money, and put his own silver cup in the sack's mouth of the youngest. The cup was planted so Joseph could hold Benjamin for a short period of time; he was not seeking harm, he was drawing out word of his father Jacob and whether he was yet alive.

**[Genesis 44:11-13](/bible/genesis/44#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/genesis/44#v11)</sup> Then they speedily took down every man his sack to the ground, and opened every man his sack.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/genesis/44#v12)</sup> And he searched, and began at the eldest, and left at the youngest: and the cup was found in Benjamin’s sack.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/genesis/44#v13)</sup> Then they rent their clothes, and laded every man his ass, and returned to the city.

- The steward searched, beginning at the eldest and leaving at the youngest, and the cup was found in Benjamin's sack; they rent their clothes and returned to the city. Benjamin found with the cup gave Joseph cause to hold him, and it set the stage for Judah to speak the whole history.

**[Genesis 44:27-29](/bible/genesis/44#v27)**

> <sup>[27](/bible/genesis/44#v27)</sup> And thy servant my father said unto us, Ye know that my wife bare me two sons:
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/genesis/44#v28)</sup> And the one went out from me, and I said, Surely he is torn in pieces; and I saw him not since:
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/genesis/44#v29)</sup> And if ye take this also from me, and mischief befall him, ye shall bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to the grave.

- Thy servant my father said unto us, Ye know that my wife bare me two sons: that is Jacob speaking of Rachel, who bare Joseph and Benjamin, the eleventh and twelfth sons.
- Ye shall bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to the grave: Jacob never got over Joseph being taken away. Sorrow kills; give not over thy mind to heaviness, for the gladness of the heart is the life of man, and losing Benjamin also would have brought their father to the grave.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 30:21-23](/bible/sirach/30#v21)**
    > <sup>[21](/bible/sirach/30#v21)</sup> Give not over thy mind to heaviness, and afflict not thyself in thine own counsel.
    >
    > <sup>[22](/bible/sirach/30#v22)</sup> The gladness of the heart is the life of man, and the joyfulness of a man prolongeth his days.
    >
    > <sup>[23](/bible/sirach/30#v23)</sup> Love thine own soul, and comfort thy heart, remove sorrow far from thee: for sorrow hath killed many, and there is no profit therein.

    Remove sorrow far from thee, for sorrow hath killed many; losing Benjamin would have killed Jacob.

**[Genesis 44:30-34](/bible/genesis/44#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/genesis/44#v30)</sup> Now therefore when I come to thy servant my father, and the lad be not with us; seeing that his life is bound up in the lad’s life;
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/genesis/44#v31)</sup> It shall come to pass, when he seeth that the lad is not with us, that he will die: and thy servants shall bring down the gray hairs of thy servant our father with sorrow to the grave.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/genesis/44#v32)</sup> For thy servant became surety for the lad unto my father, saying, If I bring him not unto thee, then I shall bear the blame to my father for ever.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/genesis/44#v33)</sup> Now therefore, I pray thee, let thy servant abide instead of the lad a bondman to my lord; and let the lad go up with his brethren.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/genesis/44#v34)</sup> For how shall I go up to my father, and the lad be not with me? lest peradventure I see the evil that shall come on my father.

- Judah became surety for the lad unto his father, and offered to abide instead of the lad as a bondman: let the lad go up with his brethren. He knew his father's life was bound up in the lad's life, and he would not see the evil come on his father.
- Joseph in all of this was interrogating his brothers about the whereabouts and welfare of their father; holding Benjamin was the device that drew it out of Judah.
