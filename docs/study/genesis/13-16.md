## Introduction

This reading covers [Genesis 13](/bible/genesis/13) through 16. Genesis is the book of the genetics and the genealogy: it shows how Israel comes from the lineage of Shem, and where every other nation finds its beginning.

**[Genesis 13:1-2](/bible/genesis/13#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/13#v1)</sup> And Abram went up out of Egypt, he, and his wife, and all that he had, and Lot with him, into the south.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/13#v2)</sup> And Abram was very rich in cattle, in silver, and in gold.

- When the scriptures speak of our forefathers being rich, it is not carnal wealth. Abram was rich in cattle, silver, and gold: livestock, land, and the things that take care of a family. That is true wealth.
- We have to be retaught what wealth is: build houses, dwell in them, plant gardens, and eat the fruit of them, and learn to provide for ourselves.

  Precepts:
  - **[Jeremiah 29:5](/bible/jeremiah/29#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/jeremiah/29#v5)</sup> Build ye houses, and dwell in them; and plant gardens, and eat the fruit of them;

    Build houses and plant gardens and eat the fruit of them; that is the wealth the Most High teaches.

**[Genesis 13:5-7](/bible/genesis/13#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/genesis/13#v5)</sup> And Lot also, which went with Abram, had flocks, and herds, and tents.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/genesis/13#v6)</sup> And the land was not able to bear them, that they might dwell together: for their substance was great, so that they could not dwell together.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/genesis/13#v7)</sup> And there was a strife between the herdmen of Abram’s cattle and the herdmen of Lot’s cattle: and the Canaanite and the Perizzite dwelled then in the land.

- Lot was Abram's nephew: Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran, and Haran begat Lot.
- Their substance was so great they could not dwell together. That substance came from Egypt, where Pharaoh entreated Abram well for Sarai's sake and gave him sheep, oxen, servants, and camels; the maidservants from Egypt are where Hagar comes from.

  Precepts:
  - **[Genesis 11:27](/bible/genesis/11#v27)**
    > <sup>[27](/bible/genesis/11#v27)</sup> Now these are the generations of Terah: Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran; and Haran begat Lot.

    Haran begat Lot; Lot was Abram's nephew.
  - **[Genesis 12:16](/bible/genesis/12#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/genesis/12#v16)</sup> And he entreated Abram well for her sake: and he had sheep, and oxen, and he asses, and menservants, and maidservants, and she asses, and camels.

    Pharaoh gave Abram livestock and menservants and maidservants; this is where the household's wealth and Hagar came from.

**[Genesis 13:8-9](/bible/genesis/13#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/genesis/13#v8)</sup> And Abram said unto Lot, Let there be no strife, I pray thee, between me and thee, and between my herdmen and thy herdmen; for we be brethren.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/genesis/13#v9)</sup> Is not the whole land before thee? separate thyself, I pray thee, from me: if thou wilt take the left hand, then I will go to the right; or if thou depart to the right hand, then I will go to the left.

- Abram said, Let there be no strife, for we be brethren. That was the spirit of Abram: he would de-escalate and look for solutions. If you go left I go right; if you go right I go left; you cannot argue with that.
- Be solution minded and fix problems using the scriptures; blessed are the peacemakers.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 5:9](/bible/matthew/5#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/matthew/5#v9)</sup> Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.

    Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called the children of God; Abram was a peacemaker.

**[Genesis 13:10-13](/bible/genesis/13#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/genesis/13#v10)</sup> And Lot lifted up his eyes, and beheld all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered every where, before the Lord destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah, even as the garden of the Lord, like the land of Egypt, as thou comest unto Zoar.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/genesis/13#v11)</sup> Then Lot chose him all the plain of Jordan; and Lot journeyed east: and they separated themselves the one from the other.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/genesis/13#v12)</sup> Abram dwelled in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelled in the cities of the plain, and pitched his tent toward Sodom.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/genesis/13#v13)</sup> But the men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the Lord exceedingly.

- Lot lifted up his eyes and chose the well watered plain of Jordan, fertile like the garden of the Lord, and pitched his tent toward Sodom. Toward Sodom became in Sodom; [Genesis 14:12](/bible/genesis/14#v12) shows Lot dwelt in Sodom.
- The men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the Lord exceedingly: they were lawless, without principles, and given to sexual freedom. That is why the city was overthrown.

  Precepts:
  - **[Genesis 14:12](/bible/genesis/14#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/genesis/14#v12)</sup> And they took Lot, Abram’s brother’s son, who dwelt in Sodom, and his goods, and departed.

    Lot dwelt in Sodom; pitching toward Sodom ended with dwelling in it.

**[Genesis 13:14-17](/bible/genesis/13#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/genesis/13#v14)</sup> And the Lord said unto Abram, after that Lot was separated from him, Lift up now thine eyes, and look from the place where thou art northward, and southward, and eastward, and westward:
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/genesis/13#v15)</sup> For all the land which thou seest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed for ever.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/genesis/13#v16)</sup> And I will make thy seed as the dust of the earth: so that if a man can number the dust of the earth, then shall thy seed also be numbered.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/genesis/13#v17)</sup> Arise, walk through the land in the length of it and in the breadth of it; for I will give it unto thee.

- The Most High reiterated the promise He made when Abram first entered the land: unto thy seed will I give this land, and thy seed shall be as the dust of the earth.
- This is the same covenant language spoken over the children of Israel: as the sand of the sea which cannot be measured nor numbered.

  Precepts:
  - **[Genesis 12:7](/bible/genesis/12#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/genesis/12#v7)</sup> And the Lord appeared unto Abram, and said, Unto thy seed will I give this land: and there builded he an altar unto the Lord, who appeared unto him.

    Unto thy seed will I give this land; the promise was made at Abram's first altar.
  - **[Hosea 1:10](/bible/hosea/1#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/hosea/1#v10)</sup> Yet the number of the children of Israel shall be as the sand of the sea, which cannot be measured nor numbered; and it shall come to pass, that in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people, there it shall be said unto them, Ye are the sons of the living God.

    The number of the children of Israel shall be as the sand of the sea; they shall be called the sons of the living God.

# Genesis 14: The War of the Kings and Melchizedek

Read the chapter → [Genesis 14](/bible/genesis/14)

**[Genesis 14:1-3](/bible/genesis/14#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/14#v1)</sup> And it came to pass in the days of Amraphel king of Shinar, Arioch king of Ellasar, Chedorlaomer king of Elam, and Tidal king of nations;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/14#v2)</sup> That these made war with Bera king of Sodom, and with Birsha king of Gomorrah, Shinab king of Admah, and Shemeber king of Zeboiim, and the king of Bela, which is Zoar.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/14#v3)</sup> All these were joined together in the vale of Siddim, which is the salt sea.

- Four kings, the Mesopotamian kings, made an allegiance against five kings; this was a world war, nations and kingdoms making alliances to war against other nations. Shinar is modern day Iraq.
- The king of Bela is the one king whose name is not given; Bela is the city, which is Zoar. The vale of Siddim is the salt sea, known today as the Dead Sea.

**[Genesis 14:4-11](/bible/genesis/14#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/genesis/14#v4)</sup> Twelve years they served Chedorlaomer, and in the thirteenth year they rebelled.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/genesis/14#v5)</sup> And in the fourteenth year came Chedorlaomer, and the kings that were with him, and smote the Rephaims in Ashteroth Karnaim, and the Zuzims in Ham, and the Emims in Shaveh Kiriathaim,
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/genesis/14#v6)</sup> And the Horites in their mount Seir, unto El–paran, which is by the wilderness.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/genesis/14#v7)</sup> And they returned, and came to En–mishpat, which is Kadesh, and smote all the country of the Amalekites, and also the Amorites, that dwelt in Hazezon–tamar.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/genesis/14#v8)</sup> And there went out the king of Sodom, and the king of Gomorrah, and the king of Admah, and the king of Zeboiim, and the king of Bela (the same is Zoar;) and they joined battle with them in the vale of Siddim;
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/genesis/14#v9)</sup> With Chedorlaomer the king of Elam, and with Tidal king of nations, and Amraphel king of Shinar, and Arioch king of Ellasar; four kings with five.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/genesis/14#v10)</sup> And the vale of Siddim was full of slimepits; and the kings of Sodom and Gomorrah fled, and fell there; and they that remained fled to the mountain.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/genesis/14#v11)</sup> And they took all the goods of Sodom and Gomorrah, and all their victuals, and went their way.

- The five kings served Chedorlaomer twelve years and rebelled in the thirteenth; the four kings defeated them and took the goods of Sodom and Gomorrah.
- The vale of Siddim was full of slimepits: bitumen, tar, petroleum. The same region is rich in oil to this day.

**[Genesis 14:12-16](/bible/genesis/14#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/genesis/14#v12)</sup> And they took Lot, Abram’s brother’s son, who dwelt in Sodom, and his goods, and departed.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/genesis/14#v13)</sup> And there came one that had escaped, and told Abram the Hebrew; for he dwelt in the plain of Mamre the Amorite, brother of Eshcol, and brother of Aner: and these were confederate with Abram.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/genesis/14#v14)</sup> And when Abram heard that his brother was taken captive, he armed his trained servants, born in his own house, three hundred and eighteen, and pursued them unto Dan.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/genesis/14#v15)</sup> And he divided himself against them, he and his servants, by night, and smote them, and pursued them unto Hobah, which is on the left hand of Damascus.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/genesis/14#v16)</sup> And he brought back all the goods, and also brought again his brother Lot, and his goods, and the women also, and the people.

- Because Lot dwelt in Sodom he was carried away captive with his goods, and one that escaped told Abram the Hebrew.
- Abram armed his trained servants, three hundred and eighteen, born in his own house. That tells you Abram trained them: he was a warrior fit for battle who knew how to conduct war, nothing like the frail figure in the whitewashed images.
- He divided his men and moved by night; that is military strategy. With three hundred and eighteen men and his three confederates he smote four kingdoms and brought back Lot, the goods, and the people.
- The victory of battle stands not in the multitude of an host; strength comes from heaven. Whether with many or with them that have no power, the battle is not yours but God's; our forefathers proved it, as when Asa cried to the Lord and a million Ethiopians fell before Judah and Benjamin.

    >
    > <sup>[19](/bible/1-maccabees/3#v19)</sup> For the victory of battle standeth not in the multitude of an host; but strength cometh from heaven.

    It is no hard matter for many to be shut up in the hands of a few; the victory of battle standeth not in the multitude of an host.
  - **[2 Chronicles 20:15](/bible/2-chronicles/20#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/2-chronicles/20#v15)</sup> And he said, Hearken ye, all Judah, and ye inhabitants of Jerusalem, and thou king Jehoshaphat, Thus saith the Lord unto you, Be not afraid nor dismayed by reason of this great multitude; for the battle is not yours, but God’s.

    Be not afraid by reason of this great multitude; the battle is not yours, but God's.
  - **[2 Chronicles 14:9-11](/bible/2-chronicles/14#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/2-chronicles/14#v9)</sup> And there came out against them Zerah the Ethiopian with an host of a thousand thousand, and three hundred chariots; and came unto Mareshah.

    > <sup>[10](/bible/2-chronicles/14#v10)</sup> Then Asa went out against him, and they set the battle in array in the valley of Zephathah at Mareshah.
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/2-chronicles/14#v11)</sup> And Asa cried unto the Lord his God, and said, Lord, it is nothing with thee to help, whether with many, or with them that have no power: help us, O Lord our God; for we rest on thee, and in thy name we go against this multitude. O Lord, thou art our God; let not man prevail against thee.

    Asa rested on the Lord against a thousand thousand Ethiopians; it is nothing with the Lord to help, whether with many or with them that have no power.

**[Genesis 14:18-20](/bible/genesis/14#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/genesis/14#v18)</sup> And Melchizedek king of Salem brought forth bread and wine: and he was the priest of the most high God.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/genesis/14#v19)</sup> And he blessed him, and said, Blessed be Abram of the most high God, possessor of heaven and earth:
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/genesis/14#v20)</sup> And blessed be the most high God, which hath delivered thine enemies into thy hand. And he gave him tithes of all.

- Melchizedek king of Salem brought forth bread and wine; Salem goes into Shalom, peace, so he is the king of peace, and he was the priest of the Most High God.
- Abram paid tithes to Melchizedek. This is way before the Levitical priesthood; the twelve tribes were not yet born. For the order of Melchisedec, study [Hebrews 7](/bible/hebrews/7) and [John 8:56](/bible/john/8#v56).

**[Genesis 14:21-24](/bible/genesis/14#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/genesis/14#v21)</sup> And the king of Sodom said unto Abram, Give me the persons, and take the goods to thyself.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/genesis/14#v22)</sup> And Abram said to the king of Sodom, I have lift up mine hand unto the Lord, the most high God, the possessor of heaven and earth,
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/genesis/14#v23)</sup> That I will not take from a thread even to a shoelatchet, and that I will not take any thing that is thine, lest thou shouldest say, I have made Abram rich:
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/genesis/14#v24)</sup> Save only that which the young men have eaten, and the portion of the men which went with me, Aner, Eshcol, and Mamre; let them take their portion.

- The king of Sodom asked only for the persons and offered Abram all the goods, and Abram refused from a thread even to a shoelatchet, lest the king of Sodom say, I have made Abram rich. He would not give him that satisfaction.
- The young men's portion was not refused: Aner, Eshcol, and Mamre fought, and they earned their portion.

# Genesis 15: The Covenant and the Four Hundred Years

Read the chapter → [Genesis 15](/bible/genesis/15)

**[Genesis 15:1-4](/bible/genesis/15#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/15#v1)</sup> After these things the word of the Lord came unto Abram in a vision, saying, Fear not, Abram: I am thy shield, and thy exceeding great reward.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/15#v2)</sup> And Abram said, Lord God, what wilt thou give me, seeing I go childless, and the steward of my house is this Eliezer of Damascus?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/15#v3)</sup> And Abram said, Behold, to me thou hast given no seed: and, lo, one born in my house is mine heir.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/genesis/15#v4)</sup> And, behold, the word of the Lord came unto him, saying, This shall not be thine heir; but he that shall come forth out of thine own bowels shall be thine heir.

- Abram asked what the Lord would give him, seeing he went childless and the steward of his house, Eliezer of Damascus, stood to inherit everything.
- The word came: This shall not be thine heir; he that shall come forth out of thine own bowels shall be thine heir. The Most High prophesied children to Abram out of his own seed.

**[Genesis 15:5-6](/bible/genesis/15#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/genesis/15#v5)</sup> And he brought him forth abroad, and said, Look now toward heaven, and tell the stars, if thou be able to number them: and he said unto him, So shall thy seed be.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/genesis/15#v6)</sup> And he believed in the Lord; and he counted it to him for righteousness.

- Look now toward heaven and tell the stars: so shall thy seed be. Abram believed the Lord, and it was counted to him for righteousness.
- Righteousness is immortal, and righteousness is defined by the law: it shall be our righteousness if we observe to do all these commandments. Abram's faith showed itself in his works.

  - **[Wisdom of Solomon 1:15](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/1#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/1#v15)</sup> (For righteousness is immortal:)

    Righteousness is immortal; the righteous seed endures forever.
  - **[Deuteronomy 6:25](/bible/deuteronomy/6#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/deuteronomy/6#v25)</sup> And it shall be our righteousness, if we observe to do all these commandments before the Lord our God, as he hath commanded us.

    It shall be our righteousness if we observe to do all these commandments; righteousness goes into keeping the laws.
  - **[Romans 4:1-3](/bible/romans/4#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/romans/4#v1)</sup> What shall we say then that Abraham our father, as pertaining to the flesh, hath found?
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/romans/4#v2)</sup> For if Abraham were justified by works, he hath whereof to glory; but not before God.
    >
    > <sup>[3](/bible/romans/4#v3)</sup> For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness.

    Abraham believed God and it was counted unto him for righteousness; his faith was shown in his works.

**[Genesis 15:9-12](/bible/genesis/15#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/genesis/15#v9)</sup> And he said unto him, Take me an heifer of three years old, and a she goat of three years old, and a ram of three years old, and a turtledove, and a young pigeon.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/genesis/15#v10)</sup> And he took unto him all these, and divided them in the midst, and laid each piece one against another: but the birds divided he not.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/genesis/15#v11)</sup> And when the fowls came down upon the carcases, Abram drove them away.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/genesis/15#v12)</sup> And when the sun was going down, a deep sleep fell upon Abram; and, lo, an horror of great darkness fell upon him.

- Abram divided the heifer, the goat, and the ram, and drove the fowls from the carcases; he was offering sacrifice.
- When the sun went down a deep sleep and a horror of great darkness fell on Abram: he was about to receive a vision of future prophecy. The Most High speaks in a dream, in a vision of the night, and reveals His secret to His servants the prophets.

  Precepts:
  - **[Job 33:15-16](/bible/job/33#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/job/33#v15)</sup> In a dream, in a vision of the night, when deep sleep falleth upon men, in slumberings upon the bed;
    >

    In a dream, in a vision of the night, He openeth the ears of men and sealeth their instruction.

    > <sup>[7](/bible/amos/3#v7)</sup> Surely the Lord God will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets.

    The Lord God revealeth His secret unto His servants the prophets.

**[Genesis 15:13-16](/bible/genesis/15#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/genesis/15#v13)</sup> And he said unto Abram, Know of a surety that thy seed shall be a stranger in a land that is not theirs, and shall serve them; and they shall afflict them four hundred years;
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/genesis/15#v14)</sup> And also that nation, whom they shall serve, will I judge: and afterward shall they come out with great substance.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/genesis/15#v15)</sup> And thou shalt go to thy fathers in peace; thou shalt be buried in a good old age.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/genesis/15#v16)</sup> But in the fourth generation they shall come hither again: for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.

- Know of a surety: thy seed shall be a stranger in a land that is not theirs, shall serve them, and be afflicted four hundred years. This prophecy was fulfilled in Egypt; the sojourning of the children of Israel in Egypt was four hundred and thirty years, and the four hundred was an approximation of that time.
- This verse is not the doctrine about America and 1619. The northern kingdom's captivity on this side began in 1492, and by that counting the years pass four hundred and Israel is still in captivity and still scattered this day; the prophecy of [Genesis 15:13](/bible/genesis/15#v13) was fulfilled at the Exodus.
- They came out with great substance: the children of Israel required jewels of silver and gold and raiment of the Egyptians, and they spoiled Egypt as they left.
- The twelve tribes scattered among the nations today, the people called Blacks, Hispanics, and Native Americans, are the seed this covenant history belongs to.
- Abram himself would go to his fathers in peace and be buried in a good old age; and the seed would return in the fourth generation, for the iniquity of the Amorites was not yet full.

    Israel is yet this day in captivity, scattered for a reproach; the scattering did not end on a four hundred year clock.
  - **[Exodus 12:33-36](/bible/exodus/12#v33)**

# Genesis 16: Hagar and Ishmael

Read the chapter → [Genesis 16](/bible/genesis/16)

**[Genesis 16:1-2](/bible/genesis/16#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/genesis/16#v1)</sup> Now Sarai Abram’s wife bare him no children: and she had an handmaid, an Egyptian, whose name was Hagar.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/16#v2)</sup> And Sarai said unto Abram, Behold now, the Lord hath restrained me from bearing: I pray thee, go in unto my maid; it may be that I may obtain children by her. And Abram hearkened to the voice of Sarai.

- Hagar was Sarai's Egyptian handmaid, from the servants Pharaoh gave in [Genesis 12:16](/bible/genesis/12#v16).
- Sarai, restrained from bearing, gave her maid to Abram that she might obtain children by her.

**[Genesis 16:3-6](/bible/genesis/16#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/16#v3)</sup> And Sarai Abram’s wife took Hagar her maid the Egyptian, after Abram had dwelt ten years in the land of Canaan, and gave her to her husband Abram to be his wife.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/genesis/16#v4)</sup> And he went in unto Hagar, and she conceived: and when she saw that she had conceived, her mistress was despised in her eyes.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/genesis/16#v5)</sup> And Sarai said unto Abram, My wrong be upon thee: I have given my maid into thy bosom; and when she saw that she had conceived, I was despised in her eyes: the Lord judge between me and thee.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/genesis/16#v6)</sup> But Abram said unto Sarai, Behold, thy maid is in thy hand; do to her as it pleaseth thee. And when Sarai dealt hardly with her, she fled from her face.

- When Hagar conceived, her mistress was despised in her eyes. Hagar got high minded; she stopped submitting because she carried Abram's child.
- Sarai laid the wrong on Abram, and Abram answered, Behold, thy maid is in thy hand. He did not say my wife; he had no attachment to Hagar. His sole purpose was the seed, not multiple wives.
- Abram kept the laws of the Most High. Do not use this account to justify taking multiple wives; the purpose here was the promised inheritance, and Sarai dealt with her maid as mistress over her.

**[Genesis 16:7-9](/bible/genesis/16#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/genesis/16#v7)</sup> And the angel of the Lord found her by a fountain of water in the wilderness, by the fountain in the way to Shur.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/genesis/16#v8)</sup> And he said, Hagar, Sarai’s maid, whence camest thou? and whither wilt thou go? And she said, I flee from the face of my mistress Sarai.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/genesis/16#v9)</sup> And the angel of the Lord said unto her, Return to thy mistress, and submit thyself under her hands.

- The angel of the Lord found Hagar by the fountain in the way to Shur and sent her back: Return to thy mistress, and submit thyself under her hands. Humble yourself and go back.

**[Genesis 16:10-12](/bible/genesis/16#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/genesis/16#v10)</sup> And the angel of the Lord said unto her, I will multiply thy seed exceedingly, that it shall not be numbered for multitude.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/genesis/16#v11)</sup> And the angel of the Lord said unto her, Behold, thou art with child, and shalt bear a son, and shalt call his name Ishmael; because the Lord hath heard thy affliction.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/genesis/16#v12)</sup> And he will be a wild man; his hand will be against every man, and every man’s hand against him; and he shall dwell in the presence of all his brethren.

- Ishmael would be a wild man, his hand against every man and every man's hand against him, and he would dwell in the presence of all his brethren.
- Ishmael became the ancestor of the nomad desert tribes inhabiting the area from the Sinai desert across the Negev to southern Jordan, and the Arabs venerate Ishmael as their forefather; Muslim tradition holds that Ishmael and Hagar are buried at the Kaaba in Mecca. The reference work Who's Who in the Bible lays this history out.
- They dwell in the presence of all their brethren to this day, nation beside nation through that whole region.

**[Genesis 16:15-16](/bible/genesis/16#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/genesis/16#v15)</sup> And Hagar bare Abram a son: and Abram called his son’s name, which Hagar bare, Ishmael.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/genesis/16#v16)</sup> And Abram was fourscore and six years old, when Hagar bare Ishmael to Abram.

- Abram was fourscore and six years old, eighty six, when Hagar bare Ishmael. Ishmael was Abram's firstborn, but the covenant did not go to Ishmael; the covenant goes through the twelve tribes of Israel.
