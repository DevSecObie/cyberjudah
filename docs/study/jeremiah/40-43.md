Read the chapter → [Jeremiah 40](/bible/jeremiah/40)

This session covers Jeremiah chapters 40 through 43, the aftermath of Jerusalem's fall to Babylon. The Most High's prophet Jeremiah is loosed from his chains, Gedaliah is set over the poor left in the land, and the same lesson runs through every chapter: the heathen nations themselves knew Israel was carried into captivity because of sin, breaking the Most High's laws. That is how His people are overcome. When the remnant asks whether to flee to Egypt, the Most High tells them plainly to stay in the land and He will build them, but they dissemble in their hearts, refuse the counsel, and run to Egypt to their own destruction. This is the book we are the people of, and these are the same people we are today.

**[Jeremiah 40:1-3](/bible/jeremiah/40#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/jeremiah/40#v1)</sup> The word that came to Jeremiah from the Lord, after that Nebuzar–adan the captain of the guard had let him go from Ramah, when he had taken him being bound in chains among all that were carried away captive of Jerusalem and Judah, which were carried away captive unto Babylon.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/jeremiah/40#v2)</sup> And the captain of the guard took Jeremiah, and said unto him, The Lord thy God hath pronounced this evil upon this place.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/jeremiah/40#v3)</sup> Now the Lord hath brought it, and done according as he hath said: because ye have sinned against the Lord, and have not obeyed his voice, therefore this thing is come upon you.

- The word came to Jeremiah after Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard let him go from Ramah, where he had been bound in chains among all the captives of Jerusalem and Judah being carried away to Babylon; Jeremiah was let go when Jerusalem was seized.
- The captain, a heathen, told Jeremiah that the Most High had pronounced this evil upon the place because the people sinned against Him and would not obey His voice, so this thing had come upon them.
- The nations always knew why we went into captivity; it is sin, the breaking of the Most High's laws, that becomes our weakness and how we are overcome.
  - **[Judith 5:17-18](/bible/judith/5#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/judith/5#v17)</sup> And whilst they sinned not before their God, they prospered, because the God that hateth iniquity was with them.
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/judith/5#v18)</sup> But when they departed from the way which he appointed them, they were destroyed in many battles very sore, and were led captives into a land that was not their’s, and the temple of their God was cast to the ground, and their cities were taken by the enemies.

    Even the heathen knew Israel prospered while keeping the law and was led captive once they departed from it.

**[Jeremiah 40:4-6](/bible/jeremiah/40#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/jeremiah/40#v4)</sup> And now, behold, I loose thee this day from the chains which were upon thine hand. If it seem good unto thee to come with me into Babylon, come; and I will look well unto thee: but if it seem ill unto thee to come with me into Babylon, forbear: behold, all the land is before thee: whither it seemeth good and convenient for thee to go, thither go.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/jeremiah/40#v5)</sup> Now while he was not yet gone back, he said, Go back also to Gedaliah the son of Ahikam the son of Shaphan, whom the king of Babylon hath made governor over the cities of Judah, and dwell with him among the people: or go wheresoever it seemeth convenient unto thee to go. So the captain of the guard gave him victuals and a reward, and let him go.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/jeremiah/40#v6)</sup> Then went Jeremiah unto Gedaliah the son of Ahikam to Mizpah; and dwelt with him among the people that were left in the land.

- The captain loosed Jeremiah from the chains on his hands and gave him an ultimatum, either to come with him to Babylon where he would be looked after well, or to remain in the land as he pleased.
- He directed Jeremiah to Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, whom the king of Babylon had made governor over the cities of Judah, gave him food and a reward, and let him go; Gedaliah was one of Jeremiah's good friends.
- Jeremiah went to Gedaliah at Mizpah and dwelt with him among the people left in the land.

> <sup>[7](/bible/jeremiah/40#v7)</sup> Now when all the captains of the forces which were in the fields, even they and their men, heard that the king of Babylon had made Gedaliah the son of Ahikam governor in the land, and had committed unto him men, and women, and children, and of the poor of the land, of them that were not carried away captive to Babylon;
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/jeremiah/40#v8)</sup> Then they came to Gedaliah to Mizpah, even Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, and Johanan and Jonathan the sons of Kareah, and Seraiah the son of Tanhumeth, and the sons of Ephai the Netophathite, and Jezaniah the son of a Maachathite, they and their men.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/jeremiah/40#v9)</sup> And Gedaliah the son of Ahikam the son of Shaphan sware unto them and to their men, saying, Fear not to serve the Chaldeans: dwell in the land, and serve the king of Babylon, and it shall be well with you.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/jeremiah/40#v10)</sup> As for me, behold, I will dwell at Mizpah to serve the Chaldeans, which will come unto us: but ye, gather ye wine, and summer fruits, and oil, and put them in your vessels, and dwell in your cities that ye have taken.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/jeremiah/40#v11)</sup> Likewise when all the Jews that were in Moab, and among the Ammonites, and in Edom, and that were in all the countries, heard that the king of Babylon had left a remnant of Judah, and that he had set over them Gedaliah the son of Ahikam the son of Shaphan;
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/jeremiah/40#v12)</sup> Even all the Jews returned out of all places whither they were driven, and came to the land of Judah, to Gedaliah, unto Mizpah, and gathered wine and summer fruits very much.

- When all the captains of the forces in the fields heard that the king of Babylon had made Gedaliah governor and had committed to him the men, women, children, and the poor of the land who were not carried away, they came together to him at Mizpah.
- Gedaliah swore to them not to fear serving the Chaldeans but to dwell in the land, gather wine and summer fruits and oil, and serve the king of Babylon, and it would be well with them; the war was over and they were already conquered.
- All the Jews scattered in Moab, Ammon, Edom, and the other countries heard of the remnant left under Gedaliah and returned to the land of Judah, gathering wine and summer fruits in abundance.

>
> <sup>[14](/bible/jeremiah/40#v14)</sup> And said unto him, Dost thou certainly know that Baalis the king of the Ammonites hath sent Ishmael the son of Nethaniah to slay thee? But Gedaliah the son of Ahikam believed them not.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/jeremiah/40#v15)</sup> Then Johanan the son of Kareah spake to Gedaliah in Mizpah secretly, saying, Let me go, I pray thee, and I will slay Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, and no man shall know it: wherefore should he slay thee, that all the Jews which are gathered unto thee should be scattered, and the remnant in Judah perish?
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/jeremiah/40#v16)</sup> But Gedaliah the son of Ahikam said unto Johanan the son of Kareah, Thou shalt not do this thing: for thou speakest falsely of Ishmael.

- Johanan the son of Kareah and the captains of the forces came to Gedaliah at Mizpah and warned him that Baalis the king of the Ammonites had sent Ishmael the son of Nethaniah to slay him, but Gedaliah believed them not.
- Johanan spoke secretly to Gedaliah, offering to kill Ishmael himself so that Gedaliah would not be slain and the gathered remnant scattered.
- Gedaliah forbade Johanan to do it, saying he spoke falsely of Ishmael, and refused to believe the plot.

# Jeremiah 41: Ishmael Murders Gedaliah and Johanan Recovers the Remnant

> <sup>[1](/bible/jeremiah/41#v1)</sup> Now it came to pass in the seventh month, that Ishmael the son of Nethaniah the son of Elishama, of the seed royal, and the princes of the king, even ten men with him, came unto Gedaliah the son of Ahikam to Mizpah; and there they did eat bread together in Mizpah.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/jeremiah/41#v2)</sup> Then arose Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, and the ten men that were with him, and smote Gedaliah the son of Ahikam the son of Shaphan with the sword, and slew him, whom the king of Babylon had made governor over the land.

- Ishmael and his ten men rose up and smote Gedaliah the son of Ahikam with the sword and slew him, the governor the king of Babylon had made over the land.
- Ishmael also slew all the Jews with Gedaliah at Mizpah and the Chaldeans and men of war that were found there.

**[Jeremiah 41:4-9](/bible/jeremiah/41#v4)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/jeremiah/41#v6)</sup> And Ishmael the son of Nethaniah went forth from Mizpah to meet them, weeping all along as he went: and it came to pass, as he met them, he said unto them, Come to Gedaliah the son of Ahikam.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/jeremiah/41#v7)</sup> And it was so, when they came into the midst of the city, that Ishmael the son of Nethaniah slew them, and cast them into the midst of the pit, he, and the men that were with him.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/jeremiah/41#v8)</sup> But ten men were found among them that said unto Ishmael, Slay us not: for we have treasures in the field, of wheat, and of barley, and of oil, and of honey. So he forbare, and slew them not among their brethren.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/jeremiah/41#v9)</sup> Now the pit wherein Ishmael had cast all the dead bodies of the men, whom he had slain because of Gedaliah, was it which Asa the king had made for fear of Baasha king of Israel: and Ishmael the son of Nethaniah filled it with them that were slain.

- The second day after the murder, before anyone knew of it, eighty men came from Shechem, Shiloh, and Samaria with their beards shaven, their clothes rent, and their bodies cut, bringing offerings and incense to the house of the Most High.
- Ishmael went out from Mizpah weeping as he met them, playing a part, and invited them in to Gedaliah; when they came into the midst of the city he and his men slew them and cast them into a pit.
- Ten of the eighty were spared when they told Ishmael they had hidden treasures of wheat, barley, oil, and honey in the field.
- The pit Ishmael filled with the slain was the one Asa the king had made for fear of Baasha king of Israel during the war between them.
  - **[1 Kings 15:22](/bible/1-kings/15#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/1-kings/15#v22)</sup> Then king Asa made a proclamation throughout all Judah; none was exempted: and they took away the stones of Ramah, and the timber thereof, wherewith Baasha had builded; and king Asa built with them Geba of Benjamin, and Mizpah.

    Asa fortified Mizpah in his war with Baasha king of Israel, and that pit became the grave Ishmael used.

>
> <sup>[11](/bible/jeremiah/41#v11)</sup> But when Johanan the son of Kareah, and all the captains of the forces that were with him, heard of all the evil that Ishmael the son of Nethaniah had done,

- When Johanan and the captains heard of all the evil Ishmael had done, not only killing Gedaliah but at least eighty other men, they took their men and went to fight him and found him by the great waters in Gibeon.
- All the people Ishmael had taken captive were glad to see Johanan, turned about, and returned to him, but Ishmael escaped from Johanan with eight men and went to the Ammonites.

> <sup>[16](/bible/jeremiah/41#v16)</sup> Then took Johanan the son of Kareah, and all the captains of the forces that were with him, all the remnant of the people whom he had recovered from Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, from Mizpah, after that he had slain Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, even mighty men of war, and the women, and the children, and the eunuchs, whom he had brought again from Gibeon:
>

>
> <sup>[18](/bible/jeremiah/41#v18)</sup> Because of the Chaldeans: for they were afraid of them, because Ishmael the son of Nethaniah had slain Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, whom the king of Babylon made governor in the land.

- Johanan took the whole remnant he had recovered from Ishmael, the mighty men of war, the women, the children, and the eunuchs, and departed to dwell near Bethlehem on the way toward Egypt.
- They set out to seek refuge in Egypt because they were afraid of the Chaldeans, since Ishmael had slain Gedaliah whom the king of Babylon had made governor; with Gedaliah dead they felt they had no reason to stay.

# Jeremiah 42: The Remnant Seeks Counsel and the Most High Says Stay

Read the chapter → [Jeremiah 42](/bible/jeremiah/42)

**[Jeremiah 42:1-6](/bible/jeremiah/42#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/jeremiah/42#v1)</sup> Then all the captains of the forces, and Johanan the son of Kareah, and Jezaniah the son of Hoshaiah, and all the people from the least even unto the greatest, came near,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/jeremiah/42#v2)</sup> And said unto Jeremiah the prophet, Let, we beseech thee, our supplication be accepted before thee, and pray for us unto the Lord thy God, even for all this remnant; (for we are left but a few of many, as thine eyes do behold us:)
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/jeremiah/42#v3)</sup> That the Lord thy God may shew us the way wherein we may walk, and the thing that we may do.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/jeremiah/42#v4)</sup> Then Jeremiah the prophet said unto them, I have heard you; behold, I will pray unto the Lord your God according to your words; and it shall come to pass, that whatsoever thing the Lord shall answer you, I will declare it unto you; I will keep nothing back from you.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/jeremiah/42#v5)</sup> Then they said to Jeremiah, The Lord be a true and faithful witness between us, if we do not even according to all things for the which the Lord thy God shall send thee to us.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/jeremiah/42#v6)</sup> Whether it be good, or whether it be evil, we will obey the voice of the Lord our God, to whom we send thee; that it may be well with us, when we obey the voice of the Lord our God.

- All the captains, Johanan, Jezaniah, and all the people from the least to the greatest came to Jeremiah the prophet before going into Egypt and begged him to pray to the Most High for them, that they might know the way to walk and what to do.
- Jeremiah agreed to pray and promised to declare everything the Most High answered, keeping nothing back.
- They swore the Most High would be a true and faithful witness against them if they did not obey His voice, whether good or evil, so that it might be well with them.

**[Jeremiah 42:7-12](/bible/jeremiah/42#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/jeremiah/42#v7)</sup> And it came to pass after ten days, that the word of the Lord came unto Jeremiah.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/jeremiah/42#v8)</sup> Then called he Johanan the son of Kareah, and all the captains of the forces which were with him, and all the people from the least even to the greatest,
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/jeremiah/42#v9)</sup> And said unto them, Thus saith the Lord, the God of Israel, unto whom ye sent me to present your supplication before him;
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/jeremiah/42#v10)</sup> If ye will still abide in this land, then will I build you, and not pull you down, and I will plant you, and not pluck you up: for I repent me of the evil that I have done unto you.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/jeremiah/42#v11)</sup> Be not afraid of the king of Babylon, of whom ye are afraid; be not afraid of him, saith the Lord: for I am with you to save you, and to deliver you from his hand.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/jeremiah/42#v12)</sup> And I will shew mercies unto you, that he may have mercy upon you, and cause you to return to your own land.

- After ten days the word of the Most High came to Jeremiah, and he called Johanan and all the people to hear the answer.
- The Most High told them that if they would still abide in the land He would build them and not pull them down, plant them and not pluck them up, for He repented of the evil He had done to them; He does not desire evil against His people, He only wants His laws kept.
- He told them not to be afraid of the king of Babylon of whom they were afraid, for He was with them to save them, deliver them from his hand, and show them mercies so they could return to their own land.

**[Jeremiah 42:13-18](/bible/jeremiah/42#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/jeremiah/42#v13)</sup> But if ye say, We will not dwell in this land, neither obey the voice of the Lord your God,
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/jeremiah/42#v14)</sup> Saying, No; but we will go into the land of Egypt, where we shall see no war, nor hear the sound of the trumpet, nor have hunger of bread; and there will we dwell:
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/jeremiah/42#v15)</sup> And now therefore hear the word of the Lord, ye remnant of Judah; Thus saith the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; If ye wholly set your faces to enter into Egypt, and go to sojourn there;
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/jeremiah/42#v16)</sup> Then it shall come to pass, that the sword, which ye feared, shall overtake you there in the land of Egypt, and the famine, whereof ye were afraid, shall follow close after you there in Egypt; and there ye shall die.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/jeremiah/42#v17)</sup> So shall it be with all the men that set their faces to go into Egypt to sojourn there; they shall die by the sword, by the famine, and by the pestilence: and none of them shall remain or escape from the evil that I will bring upon them.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/jeremiah/42#v18)</sup> For thus saith the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; As mine anger and my fury hath been poured forth upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem; so shall my fury be poured forth upon you, when ye shall enter into Egypt: and ye shall be an execration, and an astonishment, and a curse, and a reproach; and ye shall see this place no more.

- If instead they refused to dwell in the land and said they would go into Egypt to see no war, hear no trumpet, and hunger for no bread, then the sword they feared would overtake them there and the famine would follow close after them, and there they would die.
- All the men who set their faces to go into Egypt would die by the sword, the famine, and the pestilence, and none would remain or escape the evil He would bring; the Most High set before them life or death and told them to choose life.
- As His anger was poured out on the inhabitants of Jerusalem, so His fury would be poured out on them when they entered Egypt, and they would become an execration, an astonishment, a curse, and a reproach, and see the land no more.

**[Jeremiah 42:19-22](/bible/jeremiah/42#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/jeremiah/42#v19)</sup> The Lord hath said concerning you, O ye remnant of Judah; Go ye not into Egypt: know certainly that I have admonished you this day.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/jeremiah/42#v20)</sup> For ye dissembled in your hearts, when ye sent me unto the Lord your God, saying, Pray for us unto the Lord our God; and according unto all that the Lord our God shall say, so declare unto us, and we will do it.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/jeremiah/42#v21)</sup> And now I have this day declared it to you; but ye have not obeyed the voice of the Lord your God, nor any thing for the which he hath sent me unto you.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/jeremiah/42#v22)</sup> Now therefore know certainly that ye shall die by the sword, by the famine, and by the pestilence, in the place whither ye desire to go and to sojourn.

- The Most High plainly told the remnant of Judah to go not into Egypt, and Jeremiah warned them He had admonished them that day.
- They had dissembled in their hearts when they sent him to pray, giving a false appearance and concealing that they did not truly mean to obey; some people speak words they do not believe.
- Because they had not obeyed His voice, they would certainly die by the sword, the famine, and the pestilence in the very place they desired to go; we are these same people, quick to say we will not do what the Most High commands, though now that we understand who we are in these last days many of us want to keep His laws.

# Jeremiah 43: The Proud Men Reject the Word and Flee to Egypt

Read the chapter → [Jeremiah 43](/bible/jeremiah/43)

**[Jeremiah 43:1-4](/bible/jeremiah/43#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/jeremiah/43#v1)</sup> And it came to pass, that when Jeremiah had made an end of speaking unto all the people all the words of the Lord their God, for which the Lord their God had sent him to them, even all these words,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/jeremiah/43#v2)</sup> Then spake Azariah the son of Hoshaiah, and Johanan the son of Kareah, and all the proud men, saying unto Jeremiah, Thou speakest falsely: the Lord our God hath not sent thee to say, Go not into Egypt to sojourn there:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/jeremiah/43#v3)</sup> But Baruch the son of Neriah setteth thee on against us, for to deliver us into the hand of the Chaldeans, that they might put us to death, and carry us away captives into Babylon.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/jeremiah/43#v4)</sup> So Johanan the son of Kareah, and all the captains of the forces, and all the people, obeyed not the voice of the Lord, to dwell in the land of Judah.

- When Jeremiah finished speaking all the words of the Most High, Azariah the son of Hoshaiah, Johanan the son of Kareah, and all the proud men told him he spoke falsely and called him a lying prophet; pride is destruction according to the scriptures.
- They accused Baruch the son of Neriah of setting Jeremiah against them to deliver them into the hand of the Chaldeans, to be put to death or carried captive to Babylon.
- Many people ask for counsel while already having their own minds made up; when the counsel is not what they want to hear they go looking for someone who will tell them what they want, and so these men obeyed not the voice of the Most High to dwell in Judah.

**[Jeremiah 43:5-7](/bible/jeremiah/43#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/jeremiah/43#v5)</sup> But Johanan the son of Kareah, and all the captains of the forces, took all the remnant of Judah, that were returned from all nations, whither they had been driven, to dwell in the land of Judah;
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/jeremiah/43#v6)</sup> Even men, and women, and children, and the king’s daughters, and every person that Nebuzar–adan the captain of the guard had left with Gedaliah the son of Ahikam the son of Shaphan, and Jeremiah the prophet, and Baruch the son of Neriah.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/jeremiah/43#v7)</sup> So they came into the land of Egypt: for they obeyed not the voice of the Lord: thus came they even to Tahpanhes.

- Johanan and all the captains took the whole remnant of Judah, the men, women, children, and the king's daughters that Nebuzaradan had left with Gedaliah, along with Jeremiah the prophet and Baruch.
- They came into the land of Egypt as far as Tahpanhes because they obeyed not the voice of the Most High.

**[Jeremiah 43:8-13](/bible/jeremiah/43#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/jeremiah/43#v8)</sup> Then came the word of the Lord unto Jeremiah in Tahpanhes, saying,
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/jeremiah/43#v9)</sup> Take great stones in thine hand, and hide them in the clay in the brickkiln, which is at the entry of Pharaoh’s house in Tahpanhes, in the sight of the men of Judah;
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/jeremiah/43#v10)</sup> And say unto them, Thus saith the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Behold, I will send and take Nebuchadrezzar the king of Babylon, my servant, and will set his throne upon these stones that I have hid; and he shall spread his royal pavilion over them.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/jeremiah/43#v11)</sup> And when he cometh, he shall smite the land of Egypt, and deliver such as are for death to death; and such as are for captivity to captivity; and such as are for the sword to the sword.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/jeremiah/43#v12)</sup> And I will kindle a fire in the houses of the gods of Egypt; and he shall burn them, and carry them away captives: and he shall array himself with the land of Egypt, as a shepherd putteth on his garment; and he shall go forth from thence in peace.

> <sup>[13](/bible/jeremiah/43#v13)</sup> He shall break also the images of Beth–shemesh, that is in the land of Egypt; and the houses of the gods of the Egyptians shall he burn with fire.

- The word of the Most High came to Jeremiah in Tahpanhes to take great stones and hide them in the clay of the brick kiln at the entry of Pharaoh's house, in the sight of the men of Judah.
- The Most High declared He would send for Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, His servant, set his throne upon those hidden stones, spread his royal pavilion over them, and smite the land of Egypt.
- When Nebuchadnezzar came he would deliver such as were for death to death, such as were for captivity to captivity, and such as were for the sword to the sword; in war each person already has an appointed end that the Most High has set.
- He would kindle a fire in the houses of the gods of Egypt, array himself with the land of Egypt as a shepherd puts on his garment so that none escape, break the images of Bethshemesh, and burn the houses of the gods of the Egyptians with fire.
