Read the chapter → [Jeremiah 40](/bible/jeremiah/40)

This session covers Jeremiah chapters 40 through 43, the aftermath of Jerusalem's fall to Babylon. The Most High's prophet Jeremiah is loosed from his chains, Gedaliah is set over the poor left in the land, and the same lesson runs through every chapter: the heathen nations themselves knew Israel was carried into captivity because of sin, breaking the Most High's laws. That is how His people are overcome. When the remnant asks whether to flee to Egypt, the Most High tells them plainly to stay in the land and He will build them, but they dissemble in their hearts, refuse the counsel, and run to Egypt to their own destruction. This is the book we are the people of, and these are the same people we are today.

**[Jeremiah 40:1-3](/bible/jeremiah/40#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/jeremiah/40#v1)</sup> The word that came to Jeremiah from the Lord, after that Nebuzar–adan the captain of the guard had let him go from Ramah, when he had taken him being bound in chains among all that were carried away captive of Jerusalem and Judah, which were carried away captive unto Babylon.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/jeremiah/40#v2)</sup> And the captain of the guard took Jeremiah, and said unto him, The Lord thy God hath pronounced this evil upon this place.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/jeremiah/40#v3)</sup> Now the Lord hath brought it, and done according as he hath said: because ye have sinned against the Lord, and have not obeyed his voice, therefore this thing is come upon you.

- The word came to Jeremiah after Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard let him go from Ramah, where he had been bound in chains among all the captives of Jerusalem and Judah being carried away to Babylon; Jeremiah was let go when Jerusalem was seized.
- The captain, a heathen, told Jeremiah that the Most High had pronounced this evil upon the place because the people sinned against Him and would not obey His voice, so this thing had come upon them.
- The nations always knew why we went into captivity; it is sin, the breaking of the Most High's laws, that becomes our weakness and how we are overcome.
  - **[Judith 5:17-18](/bible/judith/5#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/judith/5#v17)</sup> And whilst they sinned not before their God, they prospered, because the God that hateth iniquity was with them.
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/judith/5#v18)</sup> But when they departed from the way which he appointed them, they were destroyed in many battles very sore, and were led captives into a land that was not their’s, and the temple of their God was cast to the ground, and their cities were taken by the enemies.

    Even the heathen knew Israel prospered while keeping the law and was led captive once they departed from it.

**[Jeremiah 40:4-6](/bible/jeremiah/40#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/jeremiah/40#v4)</sup> And now, behold, I loose thee this day from the chains which were upon thine hand. If it seem good unto thee to come with me into Babylon, come; and I will look well unto thee: but if it seem ill unto thee to come with me into Babylon, forbear: behold, all the land is before thee: whither it seemeth good and convenient for thee to go, thither go.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/jeremiah/40#v5)</sup> Now while he was not yet gone back, he said, Go back also to Gedaliah the son of Ahikam the son of Shaphan, whom the king of Babylon hath made governor over the cities of Judah, and dwell with him among the people: or go wheresoever it seemeth convenient unto thee to go. So the captain of the guard gave him victuals and a reward, and let him go.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/jeremiah/40#v6)</sup> Then went Jeremiah unto Gedaliah the son of Ahikam to Mizpah; and dwelt with him among the people that were left in the land.

- The captain loosed Jeremiah from the chains on his hands and gave him an ultimatum, either to come with him to Babylon where he would be looked after well, or to remain in the land as he pleased.
- He directed Jeremiah to Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, whom the king of Babylon had made governor over the cities of Judah, gave him food and a reward, and let him go; Gedaliah was one of Jeremiah's good friends.
- Jeremiah went to Gedaliah at Mizpah and dwelt with him among the people left in the land.

**[Jeremiah 40:7-12](/bible/jeremiah/40#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/jeremiah/40#v7)</sup> Now when all the captains of the forces which were in the fields, even they and their men, heard that the king of Babylon had made Gedaliah the son of Ahikam governor in the land, and had committed unto him men, and women, and children, and of the poor of the land, of them that were not carried away captive to Babylon;
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/jeremiah/40#v8)</sup> Then they came to Gedaliah to Mizpah, even Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, and Johanan and Jonathan the sons of Kareah, and Seraiah the son of Tanhumeth, and the sons of Ephai the Netophathite, and Jezaniah the son of a Maachathite, they and their men.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/jeremiah/40#v9)</sup> And Gedaliah the son of Ahikam the son of Shaphan sware unto them and to their men, saying, Fear not to serve the Chaldeans: dwell in the land, and serve the king of Babylon, and it shall be well with you.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/jeremiah/40#v10)</sup> As for me, behold, I will dwell at Mizpah to serve the Chaldeans, which will come unto us: but ye, gather ye wine, and summer fruits, and oil, and put them in your vessels, and dwell in your cities that ye have taken.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/jeremiah/40#v11)</sup> Likewise when all the Jews that were in Moab, and among the Ammonites, and in Edom, and that were in all the countries, heard that the king of Babylon had left a remnant of Judah, and that he had set over them Gedaliah the son of Ahikam the son of Shaphan;
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/jeremiah/40#v12)</sup> Even all the Jews returned out of all places whither they were driven, and came to the land of Judah, to Gedaliah, unto Mizpah, and gathered wine and summer fruits very much.

- When all the captains of the forces in the fields heard that the king of Babylon had made Gedaliah governor and had committed to him the men, women, children, and the poor of the land who were not carried away, they came together to him at Mizpah.
- Gedaliah swore to them not to fear serving the Chaldeans but to dwell in the land, gather wine and summer fruits and oil, and serve the king of Babylon, and it would be well with them; the war was over and they were already conquered.
- All the Jews scattered in Moab, Ammon, Edom, and the other countries heard of the remnant left under Gedaliah and returned to the land of Judah, gathering wine and summer fruits in abundance.

**[Jeremiah 40:13-16](/bible/jeremiah/40#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/jeremiah/40#v13)</sup> Moreover Johanan the son of Kareah, and all the captains of the forces that were in the fields, came to Gedaliah to Mizpah,
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/jeremiah/40#v14)</sup> And said unto him, Dost thou certainly know that Baalis the king of the Ammonites hath sent Ishmael the son of Nethaniah to slay thee? But Gedaliah the son of Ahikam believed them not.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/jeremiah/40#v15)</sup> Then Johanan the son of Kareah spake to Gedaliah in Mizpah secretly, saying, Let me go, I pray thee, and I will slay Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, and no man shall know it: wherefore should he slay thee, that all the Jews which are gathered unto thee should be scattered, and the remnant in Judah perish?
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/jeremiah/40#v16)</sup> But Gedaliah the son of Ahikam said unto Johanan the son of Kareah, Thou shalt not do this thing: for thou speakest falsely of Ishmael.

- Johanan the son of Kareah and the captains of the forces came to Gedaliah at Mizpah and warned him that Baalis the king of the Ammonites had sent Ishmael the son of Nethaniah to slay him, but Gedaliah believed them not.
- Johanan spoke secretly to Gedaliah, offering to kill Ishmael himself so that Gedaliah would not be slain and the gathered remnant scattered.
- Gedaliah forbade Johanan to do it, saying he spoke falsely of Ishmael, and refused to believe the plot.
