Read the chapter → [Jeremiah 52](/bible/jeremiah/52)

This session covers Jeremiah chapter 52, which is essentially the same account as Second Kings 25, the fall of Jerusalem to Babylon. It is heavy history: the puppet king Zedekiah rebels, the city is besieged and starved, his sons are slain before his eyes and his own eyes are put out, the house of the Most High is burned, and the temple treasures are carried off in what amounts to a billion dollar heist. Every stroke of it is the generational curses of [Deuteronomy 28](/bible/deuteronomy/28) and [Leviticus 26](/bible/leviticus/26) coming to pass, warnings given hundreds of years before through Moses and repeated through Jeremiah, that a fierce nation from far would besiege us, starve us until we ate our own children, put our mighty men to death, and carry the remnant into captivity to serve strangers. This is the book we are the people of.

**[Jeremiah 52:1](/bible/jeremiah/52#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/jeremiah/52#v1)</sup> Zedekiah was one and twenty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Hamutal the daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.

- Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he began to reign and reigned eleven years in Jerusalem, and his mother was Hamutal the daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.
- Zedekiah was not of the royal lineage per se, and Zedekiah was not really his name; to see how he became king we go back to when Babylon took the real king away.
  - **[2 Kings 24:15-17](/bible/2-kings/24#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/2-kings/24#v15)</sup> And he carried away Jehoiachin to Babylon, and the king’s mother, and the king’s wives, and his officers, and the mighty of the land, those carried he into captivity from Jerusalem to Babylon.
    >
    > <sup>[16](/bible/2-kings/24#v16)</sup> And all the men of might, even seven thousand, and craftsmen and smiths a thousand, all that were strong and apt for war, even them the king of Babylon brought captive to Babylon.
    >
    > <sup>[17](/bible/2-kings/24#v17)</sup> And the king of Babylon made Mattaniah his father’s brother king in his stead, and changed his name to Zedekiah.

    Nebuchadnezzar carried the real king Jehoiachin to Babylon and made his father's brother Mattaniah king in his stead, changing his name to Zedekiah, a puppet king set up by the oppressor.

**[Jeremiah 52:2-3](/bible/jeremiah/52#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/jeremiah/52#v2)</sup> And he did that which was evil in the eyes of the Lord, according to all that Jehoiakim had done.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/jeremiah/52#v3)</sup> For through the anger of the Lord it came to pass in Jerusalem and Judah, till he had cast them out from his presence, that Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.

- He did that which was evil in the eyes of the Most High as Jehoiakim had done, and through the anger of the Most High against Jerusalem and Judah, Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.
- The Most High had told through Jeremiah that the people would have to serve Babylon seventy years, but Zedekiah decided to rebel against it instead of submitting to the prophecy.
  - **[Jeremiah 25:11-12](/bible/jeremiah/25#v11)**
    > <sup>[11](/bible/jeremiah/25#v11)</sup> And this whole land shall be a desolation, and an astonishment; and these nations shall serve the king of Babylon seventy years.
    >
    > <sup>[12](/bible/jeremiah/25#v12)</sup> And it shall come to pass, when seventy years are accomplished, that I will punish the king of Babylon, and that nation, saith the Lord, for their iniquity, and the land of the Chaldeans, and will make it perpetual desolations.

    The whole land would be a desolation and these nations would serve the king of Babylon seventy years, and only then would the Most High punish Babylon for their iniquity.

**[Jeremiah 52:4-6](/bible/jeremiah/52#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/jeremiah/52#v4)</sup> And it came to pass in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, in the tenth day of the month, that Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon came, he and all his army, against Jerusalem, and pitched against it, and built forts against it round about.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/jeremiah/52#v5)</sup> So the city was besieged unto the eleventh year of king Zedekiah.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/jeremiah/52#v6)</sup> And in the fourth month, in the ninth day of the month, the famine was sore in the city, so that there was no bread for the people of the land.

- In the ninth year of his reign Nebuchadnezzar and all his army came against Jerusalem, pitched against it, and built forts round about, and the city was besieged until the eleventh year of Zedekiah.
- By the fourth month the famine was so sore in the city that there was no bread for the people of the land.
- This should sound familiar; the same siege and famine came on us again under the Romans in 70 A.D. These are generational curses that fall on multiple generations when we break the Most High's commandments.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:46](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v46)**
    > <sup>[46](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v46)</sup> And they shall be upon thee for a sign and for a wonder, and upon thy seed for ever.

    These curses are a sign upon us and upon our seed forever because we served not the Most High with joyfulness and gladness of heart.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:49-53](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v49)**
    > <sup>[49](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v49)</sup> The Lord shall bring a nation against thee from far, from the end of the earth, as swift as the eagle flieth; a nation whose tongue thou shalt not understand;
    >
    > <sup>[50](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v50)</sup> A nation of fierce countenance, which shall not regard the person of the old, nor shew favour to the young:
    >
    > <sup>[51](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v51)</sup> And he shall eat the fruit of thy cattle, and the fruit of thy land, until thou be destroyed: which also shall not leave thee either corn, wine, or oil, or the increase of thy kine, or flocks of thy sheep, until he have destroyed thee.
    >
    > <sup>[52](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v52)</sup> And he shall besiege thee in all thy gates, until thy high and fenced walls come down, wherein thou trustedst, throughout all thy land: and he shall besiege thee in all thy gates throughout all thy land, which the Lord thy God hath given thee.
    >
    > <sup>[53](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v53)</sup> And thou shalt eat the fruit of thine own body, the flesh of thy sons and of thy daughters, which the Lord thy God hath given thee, in the siege, and in the straitness, wherewith thine enemies shall distress thee:

    The Most High would bring a nation from far, swift as the eagle, of fierce countenance, to besiege the gates and destroy the food until the people ate the flesh of their own sons and daughters.
  - **[Jeremiah 5:15-19](/bible/jeremiah/5#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/jeremiah/5#v15)</sup> Lo, I will bring a nation upon you from far, O house of Israel, saith the Lord: it is a mighty nation, it is an ancient nation, a nation whose language thou knowest not, neither understandest what they say.
    >
    > <sup>[16](/bible/jeremiah/5#v16)</sup> Their quiver is as an open sepulchre, they are all mighty men.
    >
    > <sup>[17](/bible/jeremiah/5#v17)</sup> And they shall eat up thine harvest, and thy bread, which thy sons and thy daughters should eat: they shall eat up thy flocks and thine herds: they shall eat up thy vines and thy fig trees: they shall impoverish thy fenced cities, wherein thou trustedst, with the sword.
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/jeremiah/5#v18)</sup> Nevertheless in those days, saith the Lord, I will not make a full end with you.
    >
    > <sup>[19](/bible/jeremiah/5#v19)</sup> And it shall come to pass, when ye shall say, Wherefore doeth the Lord our God all these things unto us? then shalt thou answer them, Like as ye have forsaken me, and served strange gods in your land, so shall ye serve strangers in a land that is not yours.

    That same mighty and ancient nation, whose language we knew not, would eat up our harvest, bread, flocks, and herds, and it came to pass with the Chaldeans.

**[Jeremiah 52:7-11](/bible/jeremiah/52#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/jeremiah/52#v7)</sup> Then the city was broken up, and all the men of war fled, and went forth out of the city by night by the way of the gate between the two walls, which was by the king’s garden; (now the Chaldeans were by the city round about:) and they went by the way of the plain.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/jeremiah/52#v8)</sup> But the army of the Chaldeans pursued after the king, and overtook Zedekiah in the plains of Jericho; and all his army was scattered from him.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/jeremiah/52#v9)</sup> Then they took the king, and carried him up unto the king of Babylon to Riblah in the land of Hamath; where he gave judgment upon him.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/jeremiah/52#v10)</sup> And the king of Babylon slew the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes: he slew also all the princes of Judah in Riblah.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/jeremiah/52#v11)</sup> Then he put out the eyes of Zedekiah; and the king of Babylon bound him in chains, and carried him to Babylon, and put him in prison till the day of his death.

- When the city was broken up the men of war fled by night by the gate between the two walls, but the army of the Chaldeans pursued the king and overtook Zedekiah in the plains of Jericho, his army scattered from him.
- They brought Zedekiah to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where he gave judgment upon him, and the king of Babylon slew Zedekiah's sons before his eyes and slew all the princes of Judah.
- Then they put out Zedekiah's eyes, so the last thing he would ever see was his sons put to death in his face, and they bound him in chains, carried him to Babylon, and kept him in prison till the day of his death; the Most High allowed these heathen to bring hell into Jerusalem.
  - **[Jeremiah 21:7](/bible/jeremiah/21#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/jeremiah/21#v7)</sup> And afterward, saith the Lord, I will deliver Zedekiah king of Judah, and his servants, and the people, and such as are left in this city from the pestilence, from the sword, and from the famine, into the hand of Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon, and into the hand of their enemies, and into the hand of those that seek their life: and he shall smite them with the edge of the sword; he shall not spare them, neither have pity, nor have mercy.

    The Most High foretold He would deliver Zedekiah and the people into Nebuchadnezzar's hand, and he would smite them with the sword without pity, mercy, or sparing.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:36](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v36)**
    > <sup>[36](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v36)</sup> The Lord shall bring thee, and thy king which thou shalt set over thee, unto a nation which neither thou nor thy fathers have known; and there shalt thou serve other gods, wood and stone.

    The Most High would bring the people and their king into a nation neither they nor their fathers had known, to serve other gods of wood and stone.

**[Jeremiah 52:12-14](/bible/jeremiah/52#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/jeremiah/52#v12)</sup> Now in the fifth month, in the tenth day of the month, which was the nineteenth year of Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon, came Nebuzar–adan, captain of the guard, which served the king of Babylon, into Jerusalem,
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/jeremiah/52#v13)</sup> And burned the house of the Lord, and the king’s house; and all the houses of Jerusalem, and all the houses of the great men, burned he with fire:
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/jeremiah/52#v14)</sup> And all the army of the Chaldeans, that were with the captain of the guard, brake down all the walls of Jerusalem round about.

- In the fifth month Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard came to Jerusalem and burned the house of the Most High, the king's house, and all the houses of the great men with fire.
- The Chaldeans birthed this destruction, but Edom, the so-called white man, took part in it; Esau loves burning things, which is ironic since he is destined to burn forever, wasted away by the same fire he loved to waste these nations with.
- Edom were Babylon's personal cheerleaders, calling for Jerusalem and the temple to be razed to the foundation; the daughter of Babylon that Edom cheered on became the mother of harlots, Babylon the great, which is to be destroyed.
  - **[1 Esdras 4:45](/bible/1-esdras/4#v45)**
    > <sup>[45](/bible/1-esdras/4#v45)</sup> Thou also hast vowed to build up the temple, which the Edomites burned when Judea was made desolate by the Chaldees.

    Not only the Chaldeans but the Edomites took part in burning the temple when Judea was made desolate.
  - **[Psalm 137:7-9](/bible/psalms/137#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/psalms/137#v7)</sup> Remember, O Lord, the children of Edom in the day of Jerusalem; who said, Rase it, rase it, even to the foundation thereof.
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/137#v8)</sup> O daughter of Babylon, who art to be destroyed; happy shall he be, that rewardeth thee as thou hast served us.
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/psalms/137#v9)</sup> Happy shall he be, that taketh and dasheth thy little ones against the stones.

    Remember the children of Edom in the day of Jerusalem, who said Rase it, rase it, even to the foundation, and happy shall he be that rewardeth Babylon as she served us.

>
> <sup>[16](/bible/jeremiah/52#v16)</sup> But Nebuzar–adan the captain of the guard left certain of the poor of the land for vinedressers and for husbandmen.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/jeremiah/52#v17)</sup> Also the pillars of brass that were in the house of the Lord, and the bases, and the brasen sea that was in the house of the Lord, the Chaldeans brake, and carried all the brass of them to Babylon.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/jeremiah/52#v18)</sup> The caldrons also, and the shovels, and the snuffers, and the bowls, and the spoons, and all the vessels of brass wherewith they ministered, took they away.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/jeremiah/52#v19)</sup> And the basons, and the firepans, and the bowls, and the caldrons, and the candlesticks, and the spoons, and the cups; that which was of gold in gold, and that which was of silver in silver, took the captain of the guard away.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/jeremiah/52#v20)</sup> The two pillars, one sea, and twelve brasen bulls that were under the bases, which king Solomon had made in the house of the Lord: the brass of all these vessels was without weight.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/jeremiah/52#v21)</sup> And concerning the pillars, the height of one pillar was eighteen cubits; and a fillet of twelve cubits did compass it; and the thickness thereof was four fingers: it was hollow.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/jeremiah/52#v22)</sup> And a chapiter of brass was upon it; and the height of one chapiter was five cubits, with network and pomegranates upon the chapiters round about, all of brass. The second pillar also and the pomegranates were like unto these.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/jeremiah/52#v23)</sup> And there were ninety and six pomegranates on a side; and all the pomegranates upon the network were an hundred round about.

- Nebuzaradan carried away captive certain of the poor of the people, the residue in the city, and those that had fallen to the king of Babylon, but left certain of the poor of the land to be vinedressers and husbandmen.
- The Chaldeans broke the pillars of brass, the bases, and the brazen sea in the house of the Most High and carried all the brass to Babylon, along with the caldrons, shovels, snuffers, bowls, spoons, and every vessel of brass, gold, and silver.
- This was a billion dollar heist of the temple's gold, brass, and silver, and it too was prophecy; it fulfilled what was foretold to Hezekiah when he foolishly showed Babylon all his treasures.
  - **[Isaiah 39:6-7](/bible/isaiah/39#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/39#v6)</sup> Behold, the days come, that all that is in thine house, and that which thy fathers have laid up in store until this day, shall be carried to Babylon: nothing shall be left, saith the Lord.
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/39#v7)</sup> And of thy sons that shall issue from thee, which thou shalt beget, shall they take away; and they shall be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.

    Because Hezekiah showed Babylon all his treasures, the days would come when all in his house would be carried to Babylon and nothing would be left.

>
> <sup>[25](/bible/jeremiah/52#v25)</sup> He took also out of the city an eunuch, which had the charge of the men of war; and seven men of them that were near the king’s person, which were found in the city; and the principal scribe of the host, who mustered the people of the land; and threescore men of the people of the land, that were found in the midst of the city.

>
> <sup>[27](/bible/jeremiah/52#v27)</sup> And the king of Babylon smote them, and put them to death in Riblah in the land of Hamath. Thus Judah was carried away captive out of his own land.

- The captain of the guard took Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the second priest, the three keepers of the door, the eunuch over the men of war, seven of the king's counselors, the principal scribe, and threescore men of the people found in the city.
- He brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where the king smote them and put them to death, and thus Judah was carried away captive out of his own land.
- Babylon made sure to put our mighty men to death, the same tactic the oppressor always used in slavery and segregation, killing the mightiest men to destroy the people's hope so they would no longer rise up against the enemy.
