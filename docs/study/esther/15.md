**[Esther 15:1-3](/bible/esther-greek/15#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther-greek/15#v1)</sup> And upon the third day, when she had ended her prayers, she laid away her mourning garments, and put on her glorious apparel.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther-greek/15#v2)</sup> And being gloriously adorned, after she had called upon God, who is the beholder and saviour of all things, she took two maids with her:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther-greek/15#v3)</sup> And upon the one she leaned, as carrying herself daintily;

- Upon the third day, when she had ended her prayer, Esther laid away her mourning garments and put on her glorious apparel. The prayer came first; the appearance before the king came after.
- Being gloriously adorned, she first called upon God, who is the beholder and saviour of all things, and then took two maids with her. She leaned upon one, carrying herself daintily, while the other bore up her train.

**[Esther 15:4-7](/bible/esther-greek/15#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/esther-greek/15#v4)</sup> And the other followed, bearing up her train.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/esther-greek/15#v5)</sup> And she was ruddy through the perfection of her beauty, and her countenance was cheerful and very amiable: but her heart was in anguish for fear.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/esther-greek/15#v6)</sup> Then having passed through all the doors, she stood before the king, who sat upon his royal throne, and was clothed with all his robes of majesty, all glittering with gold and precious stones; and he was very dreadful.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/esther-greek/15#v7)</sup> Then lifting up his countenance that shone with majesty, he looked very fiercely upon her: and the queen fell down, and was pale, and fainted, and bowed herself upon the head of the maid that went before her.

- Esther was ruddy through the perfection of her beauty, her countenance cheerful and amiable, but her heart was in anguish for fear. The only time one could come before the king was when called, and she was coming without going through the proper order.
- She passed through all the doors and stood before the king, who sat upon his royal throne clothed with robes of majesty glittering with gold and precious stones, and he was very dreadful. When he lifted up his countenance and looked fiercely upon her, the queen fainted and bowed herself upon the head of her maid.
- Then God changed the spirit of the king into mildness. He leaped from his throne, took her in his arms until she came to herself, and comforted her with loving words. The Most High turned the king's heart.

  Precepts:

    Whosoever came to the king into the inner court uncalled would be put to death, except the king held out the golden sceptre.

**[Esther 15:8-11](/bible/esther-greek/15#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/esther-greek/15#v8)</sup> Then God changed the spirit of the king into mildness, who in a fear leaped from his throne, and took her in his arms, till she came to herself again, and comforted her with loving words and said unto her,
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/esther-greek/15#v9)</sup> Esther, what is the matter? I am thy brother, be of good cheer:
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/esther-greek/15#v10)</sup> Thou shalt not die, though our our commandment be general: come near.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/esther-greek/15#v11)</sup> And so be held up his golden sceptre, and laid it upon her neck,

- The king said unto Esther, I am thy brother, be of good cheer; thou shalt not die, though our commandment be general. Though the law was that none uncalled should live, the king made her the exception. He held up his golden sceptre and laid it upon her neck and embraced her.
- Not only did the Most High change the spirit of the king when Esther came in, but he had changed it altogether. The king who was so easily persuaded by Haman at first was now moved by the Most High to be persuaded by Esther.

**[Esther 15:12-16](/bible/esther-greek/15#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/esther-greek/15#v12)</sup> And embraced her, and said, Speak unto me.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/esther-greek/15#v13)</sup> Then said she unto him, I saw thee, my lord, as an angel of God, and my heart was troubled for fear of thy majesty.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/esther-greek/15#v14)</sup> For wonderful art thou, lord, and thy countenance is full of grace.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/esther-greek/15#v15)</sup> And as she was speaking, she fell down for faintness.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/esther-greek/15#v16)</sup> Then the king was troubled, and all his servants comforted her.

- Esther told the king she saw him as an angel of God, and her heart was troubled for fear of his majesty, calling him wonderful and his countenance full of grace. As she was speaking she fell down for faintness.
- The king was troubled and all his servants comforted her. The whole scene shows how the Most High had prepared the way and turned the king toward mercy before a single request was even made.
