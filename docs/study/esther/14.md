**[Esther 14:1-5](/bible/esther-greek/14#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther-greek/14#v1)</sup> Queen Esther also, being in fear of death, resorted unto the Lord:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther-greek/14#v2)</sup> And laid away her glorious apparel, and put on the garments of anguish and mourning: and instead of precious ointments, she covered her head with ashes and dung, and she humbled her body greatly, and all the places of her joy she filled with her torn hair.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther-greek/14#v3)</sup> And she prayed unto the Lord God of Israel, saying, O my Lord, thou only art our King: help me, desolate woman, which have no helper but thee:
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/esther-greek/14#v4)</sup> For my danger is in mine hand.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/esther-greek/14#v5)</sup> From my youth up I have heard in the tribe of my family that thou, O Lord, tookest Israel from among all people, and our fathers from all their predecessors, for a perpetual inheritance, and thou hast performed whatsoever thou didst promise them.

- Queen Esther, in fear of death, resorted unto the Lord. She laid away her glorious apparel and put on the garments of anguish and mourning, covering her head with ashes instead of precious ointments and humbling her body greatly.
- She prayed to the Lord God of Israel, confessing him as her only King and her only helper, for her danger was in her own hand. She was the one who had to go before the king.
- From her youth she had heard in the tribe of her family how the Most High took Israel from among all people and their fathers for a perpetual inheritance, and that he performed whatsoever he promised them. She stands on the covenant history she was taught.

**[Esther 14:6-10](/bible/esther-greek/14#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/esther-greek/14#v6)</sup> And now we have sinned before thee: therefore hast thou given us into the hands of our enemies,
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/esther-greek/14#v7)</sup> Because we worshipped their gods: O Lord, thou art righteous.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/esther-greek/14#v8)</sup> Nevertheless it satisfieth them not, that we are in bitter captivity: but they have stricken hands with their idols,
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/esther-greek/14#v9)</sup> That they will abolish the thing that thou with thy mouth hast ordained, and destroy thine inheritance, and stop the mouth of them that praise thee, and quench the glory of thy house, and of thine altar,
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/esther-greek/14#v10)</sup> And open the mouths of the heathen to set forth the praises of the idols, and to magnify a fleshly king for ever.

- Esther confesses the true reason for the captivity: our people sinned before the Most High and were given into the hands of their enemies because they worshipped other gods. She says plainly, O Lord, thou art righteous. This is the same confession our people must make about why we went into captivity and under these curses.
- She says the enemy is not satisfied that Israel is in bitter captivity; they have joined hands with their idols to abolish what the Most High ordained, to destroy his inheritance, and to stop the mouth of them that praise him. This is the same thing today: they are not satisfied with us serving in captivity, they want to destroy our people.
- The heathen open their mouths to set forth the praises of idols and to magnify a fleshly king for ever. The Persians, Assyrians, and Egyptians all magnified themselves when they were in power.

  Precepts:
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:36](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v36)**
    > <sup>[36](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v36)</sup> The Lord shall bring thee, and thy king which thou shalt set over thee, unto a nation which neither thou nor thy fathers have known; and there shalt thou serve other gods, wood and stone.

    The Most High warned Israel would be carried to a nation they knew not, and there serve other gods, wood and stone.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:64](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v64)**
    > <sup>[64](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v64)</sup> And the Lord shall scatter thee among all people, from the one end of the earth even unto the other; and there thou shalt serve other gods, which neither thou nor thy fathers have known, even wood and stone.

    The Lord would scatter Israel among all people, and there they would serve other gods, which neither they nor their fathers had known.

**[Esther 14:11-14](/bible/esther-greek/14#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/esther-greek/14#v11)</sup> O Lord, give not thy sceptre unto them that be nothing, and let them not laugh at our fall; but turn their device upon themselves, and make him an example, that hath begun this against us.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/esther-greek/14#v12)</sup> Remember, O Lord, make thyself known in time of our affliction, and give me boldness, O King of the nations, and Lord of all power.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/esther-greek/14#v13)</sup> Give me eloquent speech in my mouth before the lion: turn his heart to hate him that fighteth against us, that there may be an end of him, and of all that are likeminded to him:
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/esther-greek/14#v14)</sup> But deliver us with thine hand, and help me that am desolate, and which have no other help but thee.

- Esther asks that the Lord give not his sceptre to them that be nothing, and that he turn the device of the enemy upon himself, making an example of the one who began this against us, meaning Haman. The evil he devised she prays would fall upon his own head.
- She asks the Most High to make himself known in the time of affliction and to give her boldness before the king, whom she calls the lion. She has to stand before the king to make her request, so she asks for eloquent speech and that the king's heart be turned to hate the one who fights against them.
- She pleads to be delivered by the Lord's hand, calling herself desolate with no other helper but the Most High. Her boldness comes from prayer, not from her own position.

**[Esther 14:15-19](/bible/esther-greek/14#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/esther-greek/14#v15)</sup> Thou knowest all things, O Lord; thou knowest that I hate the glory of the unrighteous, and abhor the bed of the uncircumcised, and of all the heathen.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/esther-greek/14#v16)</sup> Thou knowest my necessity: for I abhor the sign of my high estate, which is upon mine head in the days wherein I shew myself, and that I abhor it as a menstruous rag, and that I wear it not when I am private by myself.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/esther-greek/14#v17)</sup> And that thine handmaid hath not eaten at Aman’s table, and that I have not greatly esteemed the king’s feast, nor drunk the wine of the drink offerings.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/esther-greek/14#v18)</sup> Neither had thine handmaid any joy since the day that I was brought hither to this present, but in thee, O Lord God of Abraham.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/esther-greek/14#v19)</sup> O thou mighty God above all, hear the voice of the forlorn and deliver us out of the hands of the mischievous, and deliver me out of my fear.

- Esther tells the Most High that she hates the glory of the unrighteous and abhors the bed of the uncircumcised and of all the heathen. She hated the high estate she carried and wore the crown as a menstruous rag, not wearing it when she was private by herself.
- Unlike our people who love the seats of government they are given and do not fight for their people, Esther despised the position and fought for her people. When she saw evil coming upon Israel, she went to the Lord.
- She testifies that she had not eaten at Aman's table nor greatly esteemed the king's feast nor drunk the wine of the drink offerings, and had known no joy since she was brought there except in the Lord God of Abraham. She asks the mighty God above all to hear the forlorn and deliver her out of her fear.
