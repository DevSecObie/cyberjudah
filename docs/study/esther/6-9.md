Read the chapter → [Esther 6](/bible/esther/6)

This session moves through [Esther 6](/bible/esther/6) to 9. The recap sets the stage: Vashti was replaced, Esther the Benjamite, raised by her uncle Mordecai, was chosen queen and her name changed from Hadassah. Mordecai had saved the king from a plot against his life. Haman the Agagite, an Edomite, hated Mordecai because Mordecai would not bow to him, for the men of Israel do not bow down. Haman got in the king's ear and had a decree published throughout the kingdom that all the Jews be put to death. Chapter 6 opens with the turning of the tide: what Haman meant for evil, the Most High turned to good. Just as the other nations kept chronicles of their history, so did Israel, and this Bible is the record of that history kept through every captivity.

**[Esther 6:1-3](/bible/esther/6#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther/6#v1)</sup> On that night could not the king sleep, and he commanded to bring the book of records of the chronicles; and they were read before the king.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther/6#v2)</sup> And it was found written, that Mordecai had told of Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king’s chamberlains, the keepers of the door, who sought to lay hand on the king Ahasuerus.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther/6#v3)</sup> And the king said, What honour and dignity hath been done to Mordecai for this? Then said the king’s servants that ministered unto him, There is nothing done for him.

- That night the king could not sleep, and he called for the book of records of the chronicles to be read before him. This is how the Most High works, moving even a king's sleeplessness to set His purpose in motion.
- It was found written that Mordecai had told of Bigthana and Teresh, two chamberlains who sought to lay hand on the king, and yet nothing had been done to honor him for it.
- The king asked what honor had been done to Mordecai, and his servants answered that there was nothing done for him.

**[Esther 6:4-5](/bible/esther/6#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/esther/6#v4)</sup> And the king said, Who is in the court? Now Haman was come into the outward court of the king’s house, to speak unto the king to hang Mordecai on the gallows that he had prepared for him.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/esther/6#v5)</sup> And the king’s servants said unto him, Behold, Haman standeth in the court. And the king said, Let him come in.

- The king asked who was in the court, and Haman had just come into the outer court to speak to the king about hanging Mordecai on the gallows he had prepared.
- In the previous chapter Haman's wife had told him to make a gallows fifty cubits high and hang Mordecai on it, so Haman was coming to carry out that plan at the very moment the king had learned Mordecai saved his life.
- The king's servant said Haman stood in the court, and the king called for him to come in.

**[Esther 6:6-9](/bible/esther/6#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/esther/6#v6)</sup> So Haman came in. And the king said unto him, What shall be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour? Now Haman thought in his heart, To whom would the king delight to do honour more than to myself?
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/esther/6#v7)</sup> And Haman answered the king, For the man whom the king delighteth to honour,
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/esther/6#v8)</sup> Let the royal apparel be brought which the king useth to wear, and the horse that the king rideth upon, and the crown royal which is set upon his head:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/esther/6#v9)</sup> And let this apparel and horse be delivered to the hand of one of the king’s most noble princes, that they may array the man withal whom the king delighteth to honour, and bring him on horseback through the street of the city, and proclaim before him, Thus shall it be done to the man whom the king delighteth to honour.

- The king asked Haman what should be done to the man the king delights to honor, and Haman, being so full of himself, assumed the king must mean him, for who in the kingdom was better than he.
- Haman laid out a full plan of royal apparel the king wears, the horse the king rides, and the crown set upon the head, all delivered by a noble prince to array the man and parade him through the city.
- Because of his pride Haman designed the highest honor imaginable, thinking he himself would receive it.

**[Esther 6:10-11](/bible/esther/6#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/esther/6#v10)</sup> Then the king said to Haman, Make haste, and take the apparel and the horse, as thou hast said, and do even so to Mordecai the Jew, that sitteth at the king’s gate: let nothing fail of all that thou hast spoken.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/esther/6#v11)</sup> Then took Haman the apparel and the horse, and arrayed Mordecai, and brought him on horseback through the street of the city, and proclaimed before him, Thus shall it be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour.

- The king told Haman to make haste and do exactly as he said to Mordecai the Jew who sat at the king's gate, letting nothing fail of all he had spoken.
- Haman then took the apparel and the horse, arrayed Mordecai, brought him on horseback through the street, and proclaimed before him. What Haman planned for himself was given to the man he hated. This is how the Most High works, and it should put a smile on your face.

**[Esther 6:12-14](/bible/esther/6#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/esther/6#v12)</sup> And Mordecai came again to the king’s gate. But Haman hasted to his house mourning, and having his head covered.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/esther/6#v13)</sup> And Haman told Zeresh his wife and all his friends every thing that had befallen him. Then said his wise men and Zeresh his wife unto him, If Mordecai be of the seed of the Jews, before whom thou hast begun to fall, thou shalt not prevail against him, but shalt surely fall before him.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/esther/6#v14)</sup> And while they were yet talking with him, came the king’s chamberlains, and hasted to bring Haman unto the banquet that Esther had prepared.

- Mordecai returned to the king's gate, but Haman hurried to his house mourning with his head covered, and told his wife Zeresh and his friends all that had happened.
- His wise men and his wife told him that if Mordecai was of the seed of the Jews, before whom he had begun to fall, he would not prevail but would surely fall before him.
- While they yet talked, the king's chamberlains came to hasten Haman to the banquet Esther had prepared, the banquet she set up after hearing of the decree against her people.

# Esther 7: Haman Hanged on His Own Gallows

Read the chapter → [Esther 7](/bible/esther/7)

**[Esther 7:1-5](/bible/esther/7#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther/7#v1)</sup> So the king and Haman came to banquet with Esther the queen.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther/7#v2)</sup> And the king said again unto Esther on the second day at the banquet of wine, What is thy petition, queen Esther? and it shall be granted thee: and what is thy request? and it shall be performed, even to the half of the kingdom.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther/7#v3)</sup> Then Esther the queen answered and said, If I have found favour in thy sight, O king, and if it please the king, let my life be given me at my petition, and my people at my request:
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/esther/7#v4)</sup> For we are sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, to be slain, and to perish. But if we had been sold for bondmen and bondwomen, I had held my tongue, although the enemy could not countervail the king’s damage.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/esther/7#v5)</sup> Then the king Ahasuerus answered and said unto Esther the queen, Who is he, and where is he, that durst presume in his heart to do so?

- At the banquet of wine the king again asked Esther her petition, promising it would be granted even to the half of the kingdom.
- Esther pleaded for her life and the life of her people, for they had been sold to be destroyed, slain, and to perish, and had it been only bondage she would have held her tongue.
- The king demanded to know who he was that dared presume in his heart to do such a thing.

**[Esther 7:6](/bible/esther/7#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/esther/7#v6)</sup> And Esther said, The adversary and enemy is this wicked Haman. Then Haman was afraid before the king and the queen.

- Esther named the adversary and enemy as this wicked Haman, and Haman was afraid before the king and the queen.
- Sitting at the table, Haman could hardly eat, for he had just arrayed Mordecai and just heard his own household warn him he would fall before the Jews, and now he was exposed at the dinner table.

**[Esther 7:7-8](/bible/esther/7#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/esther/7#v7)</sup> And the king arising from the banquet of wine in his wrath went into the palace garden: and Haman stood up to make request for his life to Esther the queen; for he saw that there was evil determined against him by the king.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/esther/7#v8)</sup> Then the king returned out of the palace garden into the place of the banquet of wine; and Haman was fallen upon the bed whereon Esther was. Then said the king, Will he force the queen also before me in the house? As the word went out of the king’s mouth, they covered Haman’s face.

- The king rose in his wrath and went into the palace garden, and Haman stood up to make request for his life to Esther, seeing that evil was determined against him.
- When the king returned, Haman had fallen upon the bed where Esther was, and the king took it as an assault on the queen in his own house.
- As the word went out of the king's mouth, they covered Haman's face.

**[Esther 7:9-10](/bible/esther/7#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/esther/7#v9)</sup> And Harbonah, one of the chamberlains, said before the king, Behold also, the gallows fifty cubits high, which Haman had made for Mordecai, who had spoken good for the king, standeth in the house of Haman. Then the king said, Hang him thereon.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/esther/7#v10)</sup> So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then was the king’s wrath pacified.

- Harbonah, a chamberlain, told the king of the gallows fifty cubits high that Haman had made for Mordecai, who had spoken good for the king, standing at Haman's house.
- The king commanded Haman be hanged on it, and they hanged Haman on the gallows he had prepared for Mordecai, and the king's wrath was pacified. This is the Most High, a man of war, and this is how He moves.

# Esther 8: The Decree Reversed for the Jews

Read the chapter → [Esther 8](/bible/esther/8)

**[Esther 8:1-2](/bible/esther/8#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther/8#v1)</sup> On that day did the king Ahasuerus give the house of Haman the Jews’ enemy unto Esther the queen. And Mordecai came before the king; for Esther had told what he was unto her.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther/8#v2)</sup> And the king took off his ring, which he had taken from Haman, and gave it unto Mordecai. And Esther set Mordecai over the house of Haman.

- That day the king gave the house of Haman, the Jews' enemy, to Esther, and Mordecai came before the king, for Esther had told what he was to her.
- The king took off the ring he had taken from Haman and gave it to Mordecai, and Esther set Mordecai over the house of Haman. Everything Haman had was given to Mordecai.

**[Esther 8:3-6](/bible/esther/8#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/esther/8#v3)</sup> And Esther spake yet again before the king, and fell down at his feet, and besought him with tears to put away the mischief of Haman the Agagite, and his device that he had devised against the Jews.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/esther/8#v4)</sup> Then the king held out the golden sceptre toward Esther. So Esther arose, and stood before the king,
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/esther/8#v5)</sup> And said, If it please the king, and if I have found favour in his sight, and the thing seem right before the king, and I be pleasing in his eyes, let it be written to reverse the letters devised by Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, which he wrote to destroy the Jews which are in all the king’s provinces:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/esther/8#v6)</sup> For how can I endure to see the evil that shall come unto my people? or how can I endure to see the destruction of my kindred?

- Esther fell down at the king's feet and besought him with tears to put away the mischief of Haman the Agagite and the device he had devised against the Jews.
- The king held out the golden scepter, and Esther rose and asked that letters be written to reverse the letters Haman devised to destroy the Jews in all the provinces.
- She asked how she could endure to see the evil that would come upon her people and the destruction of her kindred.

**[Esther 8:7-8](/bible/esther/8#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/esther/8#v7)</sup> Then the king Ahasuerus said unto Esther the queen and to Mordecai the Jew, Behold, I have given Esther the house of Haman, and him they have hanged upon the gallows, because he laid his hand upon the Jews.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/esther/8#v8)</sup> Write ye also for the Jews, as it liketh you, in the king’s name, and seal it with the king’s ring: for the writing which is written in the king’s name, and sealed with the king’s ring, may no man reverse.

- The king told Esther and Mordecai that he had given Esther the house of Haman and had hanged Haman for laying hand upon the Jews.
- He granted them to write for the Jews in the king's name and seal it with the king's ring, for a writing sealed with the king's ring could no man reverse.
- Esther and Mordecai were given the king's authority, so the reputation and word of the king stood behind whatever they wrote.

**[Esther 8:9-10](/bible/esther/8#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/esther/8#v9)</sup> Then were the king’s scribes called at that time in the third month, that is, the month Sivan, on the three and twentieth day thereof; and it was written according to all that Mordecai commanded unto the Jews, and to the lieutenants, and the deputies and rulers of the provinces which are from India unto Ethiopia, an hundred twenty and seven provinces, unto every province according to the writing thereof, and unto every people after their language, and to the Jews according to their writing, and according to their language.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/esther/8#v10)</sup> And he wrote in the king Ahasuerus’ name, and sealed it with the king’s ring, and sent letters by posts on horseback, and riders on mules, camels, and young dromedaries:

- The king's scribes were called in the third month, Sivan, on the twenty-third day, and it was written to the Jews, lieutenants, deputies, and rulers of the provinces from India to Ethiopia, a hundred twenty and seven provinces, each in its own writing and language.
- This is the span of the Persian empire, and Israel was scattered all through it, from India to Ethiopia, not concentrated in one place but spread out during the captivity, which shows we have history in these lands.

  - **[Deuteronomy 28:64](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v64)**
    > <sup>[64](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v64)</sup> And the Lord shall scatter thee among all people, from the one end of the earth even unto the other; and there thou shalt serve other gods, which neither thou nor thy fathers have known, even wood and stone.

    The Most High foretold Israel would be scattered among all people from one end of the earth unto the other.

**[Esther 8:11-14](/bible/esther/8#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/esther/8#v11)</sup> Wherein the king granted the Jews which were in every city to gather themselves together, and to stand for their life, to destroy, to slay, and to cause to perish, all the power of the people and province that would assault them, both little ones and women, and to take the spoil of them for a prey,
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/esther/8#v12)</sup> Upon one day in all the provinces of king Ahasuerus, namely, upon the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is the month Adar.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/esther/8#v13)</sup> The copy of the writing for a commandment to be given in every province was published unto all people, and that the Jews should be ready against that day to avenge themselves on their enemies.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/esther/8#v14)</sup> So the posts that rode upon mules and camels went out, being hastened and pressed on by the king’s commandment. And the decree was given at Shushan the palace.

- The king granted the Jews in every city to gather, stand for their life, and destroy any power that would assault them, both little ones and women, and to take the spoil.
- Everyone who had listened to Haman's proclamation to kill the Jews was now subject to the Jews' revenge upon their enemies.
- The decree was to be ready upon the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, Adar, the day the Jews would avenge themselves, for nothing pacifies the Most High but vengeance on the enemies of His people.
- The posts rode out hastened on mules, camels, and dromedaries by the king's commandment.

**[Esther 8:15-17](/bible/esther/8#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/esther/8#v15)</sup> And Mordecai went out from the presence of the king in royal apparel of blue and white, and with a great crown of gold, and with a garment of fine linen and purple: and the city of Shushan rejoiced and was glad.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/esther/8#v16)</sup> The Jews had light, and gladness, and joy, and honour.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/esther/8#v17)</sup> And in every province, and in every city, whithersoever the king’s commandment and his decree came, the Jews had joy and gladness, a feast and a good day. And many of the people of the land became Jews; for the fear of the Jews fell upon them.

- Mordecai went out from the king in royal apparel of blue and white, a great crown of gold, and a garment of fine linen and purple, and the city of Shushan rejoiced and was glad.
- The Jews had light, gladness, joy, and honor, and in every province and city where the decree came they had a feast and a good day.
- Many of the people of the land became Jews, for the fear of the Jews fell upon them, a picture of the future when the fear of Israel will be on all nations and they will serve the Most High.

# Esther 9: Purim and the Victory of the Jews

Read the chapter → [Esther 9](/bible/esther/9)

**[Esther 9:1-4](/bible/esther/9#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther/9#v1)</sup> Now in the twelfth month, that is, the month Adar, on the thirteenth day of the same, when the king’s commandment and his decree drew near to be put in execution, in the day that the enemies of the Jews hoped to have power over them, (though it was turned to the contrary, that the Jews had rule over them that hated them;)
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther/9#v2)</sup> The Jews gathered themselves together in their cities throughout all the provinces of the king Ahasuerus, to lay hand on such as sought their hurt: and no man could withstand them; for the fear of them fell upon all people.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther/9#v3)</sup> And all the rulers of the provinces, and the lieutenants, and the deputies, and officers of the king, helped the Jews; because the fear of Mordecai fell upon them.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/esther/9#v4)</sup> For Mordecai was great in the king’s house, and his fame went out throughout all the provinces: for this man Mordecai waxed greater and greater.

- In the twelfth month Adar, on the thirteenth day, when the enemies of the Jews hoped to have power over them, it was turned to the contrary and the Jews had rule over those that hated them.
- The Jews gathered in their cities, and no man could withstand them, for the fear of them fell upon all people, and the rulers, lieutenants, deputies, and officers helped the Jews because the fear of Mordecai fell upon them.
- Mordecai was great in the king's house and his fame went throughout all the provinces, and this man waxed greater and greater. Though Esther was queen, she was still subject to Mordecai, the man of Israel who wrote the edict.

**[Esther 9:5-10](/bible/esther/9#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/esther/9#v5)</sup> Thus the Jews smote all their enemies with the stroke of the sword, and slaughter, and destruction, and did what they would unto those that hated them.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/esther/9#v6)</sup> And in Shushan the palace the Jews slew and destroyed five hundred men.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/esther/9#v7)</sup> And Parshandatha, and Dalphon, and Aspatha,
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/esther/9#v8)</sup> And Poratha, and Adalia, and Aridatha,
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/esther/9#v9)</sup> And Parmashta, and Arisai, and Aridai, and Vajezatha,
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/esther/9#v10)</sup> The ten sons of Haman the son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews, slew they; but on the spoil laid they not their hand.

- The Jews smote all their enemies with the sword, slaughter, and destruction, and did what they would to those that hated them.
- In Shushan the palace they slew and destroyed five hundred men, and they slew the ten sons of Haman the son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews.
- On the spoil they laid not their hands.

**[Esther 9:11-15](/bible/esther/9#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/esther/9#v11)</sup> On that day the number of those that were slain in Shushan the palace was brought before the king.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/esther/9#v12)</sup> And the king said unto Esther the queen, The Jews have slain and destroyed five hundred men in Shushan the palace, and the ten sons of Haman; what have they done in the rest of the king’s provinces? now what is thy petition? and it shall be granted thee: or what is thy request further? and it shall be done.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/esther/9#v13)</sup> Then said Esther, If it please the king, let it be granted to the Jews which are in Shushan to do to morrow also according unto this day’s decree, and let Haman’s ten sons be hanged upon the gallows.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/esther/9#v14)</sup> And the king commanded it so to be done: and the decree was given at Shushan; and they hanged Haman’s ten sons.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/esther/9#v15)</sup> For the Jews that were in Shushan gathered themselves together on the fourteenth day also of the month Adar, and slew three hundred men at Shushan; but on the prey they laid not their hand.

- The number slain in Shushan was brought before the king, who told Esther the Jews had slain five hundred men and the ten sons of Haman, and asked what more she desired.
- Esther asked that the Jews in Shushan be granted the next day also according to the decree, and that Haman's ten sons be hanged on the gallows.
- The king commanded it done, and the Jews in Shushan gathered on the fourteenth day and slew three hundred more, but on the spoil they laid not their hands.

**[Esther 9:16-19](/bible/esther/9#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/esther/9#v16)</sup> But the other Jews that were in the king’s provinces gathered themselves together, and stood for their lives, and had rest from their enemies, and slew of their foes seventy and five thousand, but they laid not their hands on the prey,
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/esther/9#v17)</sup> On the thirteenth day of the month Adar; and on the fourteenth day of the same rested they, and made it a day of feasting and gladness.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/esther/9#v18)</sup> But the Jews that were at Shushan assembled together on the thirteenth day thereof, and on the fourteenth thereof; and on the fifteenth day of the same they rested, and made it a day of feasting and gladness.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/esther/9#v19)</sup> Therefore the Jews of the villages, that dwelt in the unwalled towns, made the fourteenth day of the month Adar a day of gladness and feasting, and a good day, and of sending portions one to another.

- The other Jews in the king's provinces gathered, stood for their lives, had rest from their enemies, and slew of their foes seventy and five thousand, laying not their hands on the prey.
- This was on the thirteenth day of Adar, and on the fourteenth they rested and made a day of feasting and gladness.
- The Jews of Shushan rested on the fifteenth day, so the Jews of the unwalled towns made the fourteenth a day of gladness, feasting, and sending portions one to another. This is where the gift giving of Purim comes from.

**[Esther 9:20-22](/bible/esther/9#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/esther/9#v20)</sup> And Mordecai wrote these things, and sent letters unto all the Jews that were in all the provinces of the king Ahasuerus, both nigh and far,
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/esther/9#v21)</sup> To stablish this among them, that they should keep the fourteenth day of the month Adar, and the fifteenth day of the same, yearly,
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/esther/9#v22)</sup> As the days wherein the Jews rested from their enemies, and the month which was turned unto them from sorrow to joy, and from mourning into a good day: that they should make them days of feasting and joy, and of sending portions one to another, and gifts to the poor.

- Mordecai wrote these things and sent letters to all the Jews near and far to establish that they keep the fourteenth and fifteenth days of Adar yearly.
- These were the days the Jews rested from their enemies, the month turned from sorrow to joy and from mourning to a good day.
- They were to make them days of feasting, joy, sending portions one to another, and gifts to the poor. This is where the feast of Purim comes from, a feast that commemorates the destruction of Israel's enemies.

**[Esther 9:23-25](/bible/esther/9#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/esther/9#v23)</sup> And the Jews undertook to do as they had begun, and as Mordecai had written unto them;
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/esther/9#v24)</sup> Because Haman the son of Hammedatha, the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had devised against the Jews to destroy them, and had cast Pur, that is, the lot, to consume them, and to destroy them;
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/esther/9#v25)</sup> But when Esther came before the king, he commanded by letters that his wicked device, which he devised against the Jews, should return upon his own head, and that he and his sons should be hanged on the gallows.

- The Jews undertook to keep what Mordecai wrote, because Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite had devised against the Jews to destroy them and had cast Pur, that is the lot, to consume them.
- Pur was the Persian custom of casting lots, much like the lot cast for Jonah to see whose fault the storm was.
- When Esther came before the king, he commanded by letters that Haman's wicked device return upon his own head, and that he and his sons be hanged on the gallows.

  - **[Jonah 1:7](/bible/jonah/1#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/jonah/1#v7)</sup> And they said every one to his fellow, Come, and let us cast lots, that we may know for whose cause this evil is upon us. So they cast lots, and the lot fell upon Jonah.

    The mariners cast lots to know for whose cause the evil was upon them, and the lot fell upon Jonah.

**[Esther 9:26-28](/bible/esther/9#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/esther/9#v26)</sup> Wherefore they called these days Purim after the name of Pur. Therefore for all the words of this letter, and of that which they had seen concerning this matter, and which had come unto them,
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/esther/9#v27)</sup> The Jews ordained, and took upon them, and upon their seed, and upon all such as joined themselves unto them, so as it should not fail, that they would keep these two days according to their writing, and according to their appointed time every year;
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/esther/9#v28)</sup> And that these days should be remembered and kept throughout every generation, every family, every province, and every city; and that these days of Purim should not fail from among the Jews, nor the memorial of them perish from their seed.

- These days were called Purim after the name Pur, and the Jews ordained and took upon themselves and their seed to keep these two days every year.
- The days were to be remembered and kept throughout every generation, family, province, and city.
- These days of Purim should not fail from among the Jews nor the memorial of them perish from their seed, which shows Israel still celebrates Purim today.

**[Esther 9:29-32](/bible/esther/9#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/esther/9#v29)</sup> Then Esther the queen, the daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew, wrote with all authority, to confirm this second letter of Purim.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/esther/9#v30)</sup> And he sent the letters unto all the Jews, to the hundred twenty and seven provinces of the kingdom of Ahasuerus, with words of peace and truth,
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/esther/9#v31)</sup> To confirm these days of Purim in their times appointed, according as Mordecai the Jew and Esther the queen had enjoined them, and as they had decreed for themselves and for their seed, the matters of the fastings and their cry.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/esther/9#v32)</sup> And the decree of Esther confirmed these matters of Purim; and it was written in the book.

- Esther the queen, the daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew wrote with all authority to confirm this second letter of Purim.
- Letters were sent to all the Jews in the hundred twenty and seven provinces with words of peace and truth, to confirm the days of Purim in their appointed times.
- The decree of Esther confirmed these matters of Purim, and it was written in the book, which is Israel's own book of history.
