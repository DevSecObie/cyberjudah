## Introduction

This session covers the book of Esther chapters two through five, picking up after chapter one where Vashti had a spirit on her, thought she could disrespect the king, and lost her royal estate. Chapter two brings in a fair young virgin named Hadassah, also called Esther, the uncle's daughter of Mordecai a Benjamite carried away from Jerusalem in the captivity. She is racially ambiguous, a daughter of Israel who does not look like what people expect, which is why the Most High teaches us not to judge after the sight of the eyes but by whether a spirit bears witness with the Bible. Esther is set as queen instead of Vashti, and Mordecai uncovers a plot against the king. Chapters three through five bring in Haman the Agagite, a descendant of the Amalekites Saul failed to utterly destroy, whose hatred of Mordecai becomes a decree to destroy all the Jews, and Esther is moved to stand for her people for such a time as this.

- After the wrath of king Ahasuerus was appeased he remembered Vashti and what she had done and what was decreed against her. His servants counsel that fair young virgins be sought for the king in every province and gathered to Shushan the palace under the custody of the keeper of the women.
- Fair young virgins here does not mean little girls but women of marital age, and the maiden who pleased the king would be made queen instead of Vashti, and the thing pleased the king.
