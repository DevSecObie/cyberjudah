## Introduction

This session finishes the book of Esther by going through the Apocrypha, the four additions known as "The Rest of the Book of Esther," chapters 13 through 16. It picks up where the canonical account left off: Haman has been promoted, Esther has been made queen, and Haman has laid a plot against the Jews because Mardocheus would not bow to him. Chapter 13 gives the copy of the first letter, the decree Haman drafted in the king's name to destroy all the Jews in one day, and then the prayer of Mardocheus. Chapter 14 is Esther's prayer. Chapter 15 shows Esther going before the king. Chapter 16 gives the second letter, the reversal, which calls Israel the children of the most high God. As we read, remember this is not just the story of Esther and Mardocheus and Haman; it is the story of our people, a day of sorrow turned into joy, and it is history to lean on when we go through our own affliction in captivity.

**[Esther 13:1-3](/bible/esther-greek/13#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther-greek/13#v1)</sup> The copy of the letters was this: The great king Artexerxes writeth these things to the princes and governours that are under him from India unto Ethiopia in an hundred and seven and twenty provinces.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther-greek/13#v2)</sup> After that I became lord over many nations and had dominion over the whole world, not lifted up with presumption of my authority, but carrying myself always with equity and mildness, I purposed to settle my subjects continually in a quiet life, and making my kingdom peaceable, and open for passage to the utmost coasts, to renew peace, which is desired of all men.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther-greek/13#v3)</sup> Now when I asked my counsellors how this might be brought to pass, Aman, that excelled in wisdom among us, and was approved for his constant good will and steadfast fidelity, and had the honour of the second place in the kingdom,

- The copy of the first letter opens with the great king Artaxerxes writing to the princes and governors under him, from India unto Ethiopia, over an hundred and seven and twenty provinces. This is the same king called Ahasuerus in the canonical book.
- The king presents himself as one who does not lift himself up in presumption of his authority but carries himself with equity and mildness, purposing to settle his subjects in a quiet life and make the kingdom peaceable. The words sound noble, but the letter itself was written by Haman's scribes; the king gave Haman his seal to send it.
- Aman, that is Haman, is named as the one who excelled in wisdom and held the second place in the kingdom. The whole letter is Haman speaking through the king's authority.

**[Esther 13:4-7](/bible/esther-greek/13#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/esther-greek/13#v4)</sup> Declared unto us, that in all nations throughout the world there was scattered a certain malicious people, that had laws contrary to all nations, and continually despised the commandments of kings, so as the uniting of our kingdoms, honourably intended by us cannot go forward.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/esther-greek/13#v5)</sup> Seeing then we understand that this people alone is continually in opposition unto all men, differing in the strange manner of their laws, and evil affected to our state, working all the mischief they can that our kingdom may not be firmly established:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/esther-greek/13#v6)</sup> Therefore have we commanded, that all they that are signified in writing unto you by Aman, who is ordained over the affairs, and is next unto us, shall all, with their wives and children, be utterly destroyed by the sword of their enemies, without all mercy and pity, the fourteenth day of the twelfth month Adar of this present year:
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/esther-greek/13#v7)</sup> That they, who of old and now also are malicious, may in one day with violence go into the grave, and so ever hereafter cause our affairs to be well settled, and without trouble.

- The letter paints Israel as a malicious people scattered among all nations, with laws contrary to all nations, continually despising the commandments of kings. This is the very picture Haman painted so the uniting of the kingdoms could not go forward.
- The charge is the same slander repeated: that this people alone is continually in opposition unto all men, differing in the strange manner of their laws. Haman wants them seen as a mischievous, malicious nation.
- The command goes out that all the Jews, with their wives and children, be utterly destroyed by the sword without mercy and pity on the fourteenth day of the twelfth month Adar. This is an order for the destruction of the Israelites, young and old together.
- This sounds exactly like the curses laid out for our people in [Deuteronomy 28](/bible/deuteronomy/28), where the enemy shows no favor toward the young or the old. Both the ancient and the present generation were marked for the grave in one day.

- He asks the Lord to spare his people, whose enemies desire to destroy the inheritance that has been the Lord's from the beginning, the portion delivered out of Egypt. He pleads that the Most High turn their sorrow into joy and not destroy the mouths of them that praise him.
- All Israel in like manner cried most earnestly unto the Lord because their death was before their eyes. Mardocheus rent his clothes and cried through the city with a loud and bitter cry, as the canonical account records.

# Esther 14: The Prayer of Esther

**[Esther 14:1-5](/bible/esther-greek/14#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther-greek/14#v1)</sup> Queen Esther also, being in fear of death, resorted unto the Lord:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther-greek/14#v2)</sup> And laid away her glorious apparel, and put on the garments of anguish and mourning: and instead of precious ointments, she covered her head with ashes and dung, and she humbled her body greatly, and all the places of her joy she filled with her torn hair.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther-greek/14#v3)</sup> And she prayed unto the Lord God of Israel, saying, O my Lord, thou only art our King: help me, desolate woman, which have no helper but thee:
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/esther-greek/14#v4)</sup> For my danger is in mine hand.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/esther-greek/14#v5)</sup> From my youth up I have heard in the tribe of my family that thou, O Lord, tookest Israel from among all people, and our fathers from all their predecessors, for a perpetual inheritance, and thou hast performed whatsoever thou didst promise them.

- Queen Esther, in fear of death, resorted unto the Lord. She laid away her glorious apparel and put on the garments of anguish and mourning, covering her head with ashes instead of precious ointments and humbling her body greatly.
- She prayed to the Lord God of Israel, confessing him as her only King and her only helper, for her danger was in her own hand. She was the one who had to go before the king.
- From her youth she had heard in the tribe of her family how the Most High took Israel from among all people and their fathers for a perpetual inheritance, and that he performed whatsoever he promised them. She stands on the covenant history she was taught.

**[Esther 14:6-10](/bible/esther-greek/14#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/esther-greek/14#v6)</sup> And now we have sinned before thee: therefore hast thou given us into the hands of our enemies,
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/esther-greek/14#v7)</sup> Because we worshipped their gods: O Lord, thou art righteous.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/esther-greek/14#v8)</sup> Nevertheless it satisfieth them not, that we are in bitter captivity: but they have stricken hands with their idols,
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/esther-greek/14#v9)</sup> That they will abolish the thing that thou with thy mouth hast ordained, and destroy thine inheritance, and stop the mouth of them that praise thee, and quench the glory of thy house, and of thine altar,
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/esther-greek/14#v10)</sup> And open the mouths of the heathen to set forth the praises of the idols, and to magnify a fleshly king for ever.

- Esther confesses the true reason for the captivity: our people sinned before the Most High and were given into the hands of their enemies because they worshipped other gods. She says plainly, O Lord, thou art righteous. This is the same confession our people must make about why we went into captivity and under these curses.
- She says the enemy is not satisfied that Israel is in bitter captivity; they have joined hands with their idols to abolish what the Most High ordained, to destroy his inheritance, and to stop the mouth of them that praise him. This is the same thing today: they are not satisfied with us serving in captivity, they want to destroy our people.
- The heathen open their mouths to set forth the praises of idols and to magnify a fleshly king for ever. The Persians, Assyrians, and Egyptians all magnified themselves when they were in power.

>
> <sup>[14](/bible/esther-greek/14#v14)</sup> But deliver us with thine hand, and help me that am desolate, and which have no other help but thee.

- Esther asks that the Lord give not his sceptre to them that be nothing, and that he turn the device of the enemy upon himself, making an example of the one who began this against us, meaning Haman. The evil he devised she prays would fall upon his own head.
- She asks the Most High to make himself known in the time of affliction and to give her boldness before the king, whom she calls the lion. She has to stand before the king to make her request, so she asks for eloquent speech and that the king's heart be turned to hate the one who fights against them.
- She pleads to be delivered by the Lord's hand, calling herself desolate with no other helper but the Most High. Her boldness comes from prayer, not from her own position.

**[Esther 14:15-19](/bible/esther-greek/14#v15)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/esther-greek/14#v16)</sup> Thou knowest my necessity: for I abhor the sign of my high estate, which is upon mine head in the days wherein I shew myself, and that I abhor it as a menstruous rag, and that I wear it not when I am private by myself.
>

- Esther tells the Most High that she hates the glory of the unrighteous and abhors the bed of the uncircumcised and of all the heathen. She hated the high estate she carried and wore the crown as a menstruous rag, not wearing it when she was private by herself.
- Unlike our people who love the seats of government they are given and do not fight for their people, Esther despised the position and fought for her people. When she saw evil coming upon Israel, she went to the Lord.
- She testifies that she had not eaten at Aman's table nor greatly esteemed the king's feast nor drunk the wine of the drink offerings, and had known no joy since she was brought there except in the Lord God of Abraham. She asks the mighty God above all to hear the forlorn and deliver her out of her fear.

# Esther 16: The Second Letter and the Reversal

Read the chapter → [Additions to Esther 16](/bible/esther-greek/16)

**[Esther 16:1-6](/bible/esther-greek/16#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther-greek/16#v1)</sup> The great king Artexerxes unto the princes and governors of an hundred and seven and twenty provinces from India unto Ethiopia, and unto all our faithful subjects, greeting.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther-greek/16#v2)</sup> Many, the more often they are honoured with the great bounty of their gracious princes, the more proud they are waxen,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther-greek/16#v3)</sup> And endeavour to hurt not our subjects only, but not being able to bear abundance, do take in hand to practise also against those that do them good:
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/esther-greek/16#v4)</sup> And take not only thankfulness away from among men, but also lifted up with the glorious words of lewd persons, that were never good, they think to escape the justice of God, that seeth all things and hateth evil.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/esther-greek/16#v5)</sup> Oftentimes also fair speech of those, that are put in trust to manage their friends’ affairs, hath caused many that are in authority to be partakers of innocent blood, and hath enwrapped them in remediless calamities:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/esther-greek/16#v6)</sup> Beguiling with the falsehood and deceit of their lewd disposition the innocency and goodness of princes.

- This is the copy of the second letter, again in the name of the great king Artaxerxes to the same provinces from India unto Ethiopia. But this letter was written by Esther with the king's seal, and it runs completely contrary to the first letter that went out under Haman.
- The letter warns that many, the more they are honored by gracious princes, wax the more proud, and endeavor to hurt not only the king's subjects but also those that do them good. This is a description of Haman himself.
- Such men, lifted up with the glorious words of lewd persons, think to escape the justice of God that seeth all things and hateth evil. Fair speech from those put in trust has caused many rulers to be partakers of innocent blood, beguiling the innocency and goodness of princes.

**[Esther 16:7-14](/bible/esther-greek/16#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/esther-greek/16#v7)</sup> Now ye may see this, as we have declared, not so much by ancient histories, as ye may, if ye search what hath been wickedly done of late through the pestilent behaviour of them that are unworthily placed in authority.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/esther-greek/16#v8)</sup> And we must take care for the time to come, that our kingdom may be quiet and peaceable for all men,
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/esther-greek/16#v9)</sup> Both by changing our purposes, and always judging things that are evident with more equal proceeding.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/esther-greek/16#v10)</sup> For Aman, a Macedonian, the son of Amadatha, being indeed a stranger from the Persian blood, and far distant from our goodness, and as a stranger received of us,
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/esther-greek/16#v11)</sup> Had so far forth obtained the favour that we shew toward every nation, as that he was called our father, and was continually honoured of all the next person unto the king.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/esther-greek/16#v12)</sup> But he, not bearing his great dignity, went about to deprive us of our kingdom and life:
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/esther-greek/16#v13)</sup> Having by manifold and cunning deceits sought of us the destruction, as well of Mardocheus, who saved our life, and continually procured our good, as also of blameless Esther, partaker of our kingdom, with their whole nation.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/esther-greek/16#v14)</sup> For by these means he thought, finding us destitute of friends to have translated the kingdom of the Persians to the Macedonians.

- The king says these things may be seen not so much by ancient histories as by what has been wickedly done of late through those unworthily placed in authority. He resolves to take care for the time to come that the kingdom may be quiet and peaceable, changing his purposes and judging with more equal proceeding.
- Aman is exposed as a Macedonian, the son of Amadatha, a stranger from the Persian blood, received as a stranger yet raised to be called the king's father and honored as the next person unto the king. He was not a Persian, but was set as number two in the kingdom.
- Not bearing his great dignity, Haman went about to deprive the king of his kingdom and life, seeking by cunning deceit the destruction of Mardocheus, who saved the king's life, and of blameless Esther, partaker of the kingdom, together with their whole nation. He thought to translate the kingdom of the Persians to the Macedonians.

**[Esther 16:15-20](/bible/esther-greek/16#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/esther-greek/16#v15)</sup> But we find that the Jews, whom this wicked wretch hath delivered to utter destruction, are no evildoers, but live by most just laws:
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/esther-greek/16#v16)</sup> And that they be children of the most high and most mighty, living God, who hath ordered the kingdom both unto us and to our progenitors in the most excellent manner.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/esther-greek/16#v17)</sup> Wherefore ye shall do well not to put in execution the letters sent unto you by Aman the son of Amadatha.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/esther-greek/16#v18)</sup> For he that was the worker of these things, is hanged at the gates of Susa with all his family: God, who ruleth all things, speedily rendering vengeance to him according to his deserts.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/esther-greek/16#v19)</sup> Therefore ye shall publish the copy of this letter in all places, that the Jews may freely live after their own laws.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/esther-greek/16#v20)</sup> And ye shall aid them, that even the same day, being the thirteenth day of the twelfth month Adar, they may be avenged on them, who in the time of their affliction shall set upon them.

- This letter finds that the Jews whom Haman delivered to utter destruction are no evildoers but live by most just laws. This is the direct opposite of the first letter, which called them a people whose laws were contrary and in opposition to all nations.
- The king declares that the Jews are children of the most high and most mighty living God, who ordered the kingdom both unto the king and his progenitors. This is the plain testimony of scripture concerning who Israel is.
- Therefore the first letters sent by Haman are not to be executed, for Haman himself is hanged at the gates of Susa with all his family, the Most High rendering vengeance according to his deserts. The copy of this second letter is to be published everywhere so the Jews may live after their own laws and, on the thirteenth day of Adar, be avenged on those who set upon them.
