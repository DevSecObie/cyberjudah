Read the chapter → [Esther 6](/bible/esther/6)

This session moves through [Esther 6](/bible/esther/6) to 9. The recap sets the stage: Vashti was replaced, Esther the Benjamite, raised by her uncle Mordecai, was chosen queen and her name changed from Hadassah. Mordecai had saved the king from a plot against his life. Haman the Agagite, an Edomite, hated Mordecai because Mordecai would not bow to him, for the men of Israel do not bow down. Haman got in the king's ear and had a decree published throughout the kingdom that all the Jews be put to death. Chapter 6 opens with the turning of the tide: what Haman meant for evil, the Most High turned to good. Just as the other nations kept chronicles of their history, so did Israel, and this Bible is the record of that history kept through every captivity.

**[Esther 6:1-3](/bible/esther/6#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther/6#v1)</sup> On that night could not the king sleep, and he commanded to bring the book of records of the chronicles; and they were read before the king.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther/6#v2)</sup> And it was found written, that Mordecai had told of Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king’s chamberlains, the keepers of the door, who sought to lay hand on the king Ahasuerus.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther/6#v3)</sup> And the king said, What honour and dignity hath been done to Mordecai for this? Then said the king’s servants that ministered unto him, There is nothing done for him.

- That night the king could not sleep, and he called for the book of records of the chronicles to be read before him. This is how the Most High works, moving even a king's sleeplessness to set His purpose in motion.
- It was found written that Mordecai had told of Bigthana and Teresh, two chamberlains who sought to lay hand on the king, and yet nothing had been done to honor him for it.
- The king asked what honor had been done to Mordecai, and his servants answered that there was nothing done for him.

**[Esther 6:4-5](/bible/esther/6#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/esther/6#v4)</sup> And the king said, Who is in the court? Now Haman was come into the outward court of the king’s house, to speak unto the king to hang Mordecai on the gallows that he had prepared for him.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/esther/6#v5)</sup> And the king’s servants said unto him, Behold, Haman standeth in the court. And the king said, Let him come in.

- The king asked who was in the court, and Haman had just come into the outer court to speak to the king about hanging Mordecai on the gallows he had prepared.
- In the previous chapter Haman's wife had told him to make a gallows fifty cubits high and hang Mordecai on it, so Haman was coming to carry out that plan at the very moment the king had learned Mordecai saved his life.
- The king's servant said Haman stood in the court, and the king called for him to come in.

**[Esther 6:6-9](/bible/esther/6#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/esther/6#v6)</sup> So Haman came in. And the king said unto him, What shall be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour? Now Haman thought in his heart, To whom would the king delight to do honour more than to myself?
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/esther/6#v7)</sup> And Haman answered the king, For the man whom the king delighteth to honour,
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/esther/6#v8)</sup> Let the royal apparel be brought which the king useth to wear, and the horse that the king rideth upon, and the crown royal which is set upon his head:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/esther/6#v9)</sup> And let this apparel and horse be delivered to the hand of one of the king’s most noble princes, that they may array the man withal whom the king delighteth to honour, and bring him on horseback through the street of the city, and proclaim before him, Thus shall it be done to the man whom the king delighteth to honour.

- The king asked Haman what should be done to the man the king delights to honor, and Haman, being so full of himself, assumed the king must mean him, for who in the kingdom was better than he.
- Haman laid out a full plan of royal apparel the king wears, the horse the king rides, and the crown set upon the head, all delivered by a noble prince to array the man and parade him through the city.
- Because of his pride Haman designed the highest honor imaginable, thinking he himself would receive it.

**[Esther 6:10-11](/bible/esther/6#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/esther/6#v10)</sup> Then the king said to Haman, Make haste, and take the apparel and the horse, as thou hast said, and do even so to Mordecai the Jew, that sitteth at the king’s gate: let nothing fail of all that thou hast spoken.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/esther/6#v11)</sup> Then took Haman the apparel and the horse, and arrayed Mordecai, and brought him on horseback through the street of the city, and proclaimed before him, Thus shall it be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour.

- The king told Haman to make haste and do exactly as he said to Mordecai the Jew who sat at the king's gate, letting nothing fail of all he had spoken.
- Haman then took the apparel and the horse, arrayed Mordecai, brought him on horseback through the street, and proclaimed before him. What Haman planned for himself was given to the man he hated. This is how the Most High works, and it should put a smile on your face.

**[Esther 6:12-14](/bible/esther/6#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/esther/6#v12)</sup> And Mordecai came again to the king’s gate. But Haman hasted to his house mourning, and having his head covered.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/esther/6#v13)</sup> And Haman told Zeresh his wife and all his friends every thing that had befallen him. Then said his wise men and Zeresh his wife unto him, If Mordecai be of the seed of the Jews, before whom thou hast begun to fall, thou shalt not prevail against him, but shalt surely fall before him.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/esther/6#v14)</sup> And while they were yet talking with him, came the king’s chamberlains, and hasted to bring Haman unto the banquet that Esther had prepared.

- Mordecai returned to the king's gate, but Haman hurried to his house mourning with his head covered, and told his wife Zeresh and his friends all that had happened.
- His wise men and his wife told him that if Mordecai was of the seed of the Jews, before whom he had begun to fall, he would not prevail but would surely fall before him.
- While they yet talked, the king's chamberlains came to hasten Haman to the banquet Esther had prepared, the banquet she set up after hearing of the decree against her people.
