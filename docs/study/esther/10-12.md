## Introduction

This session is short and it crosses a boundary. It opens with the last canonical verses of Esther, chapter 10 verses 1 to 3, where Mardocheus (Mordecai) is set as second unto king Ahasuerus, great among the Jews and accepted of the multitude of his brethren. From there the teaching moves into the KJV Apocrypha, the Rest of the Book of Esther, which fills in what the canonical account left unspoken. Chapter 10 continues into the interpretation of the dream Mardocheus had seen: the little fountain that became a river is Esther, the two dragons are Mardocheus himself and Aman, and the nations gathered were those assembled to destroy the name of the Jews. Chapter 11 gives the dream itself, and chapter 12 records how Mardocheus uncovered the plot of the two eunuchs against the king and how Aman came to hate him for it. The thread the Most High draws out is that these additions do not contradict the canonical book, they line up with it verse for verse, and they show the hand of the Most High saving his people from the enemy who has tried in every generation to wipe them out.

**[Esther 10:1-3](/bible/esther/10#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther/10#v1)</sup> And the king Ahasuerus laid a tribute upon the land, and upon the isles of the sea.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther/10#v2)</sup> And all the acts of his power and of his might, and the declaration of the greatness of Mordecai, whereunto the king advanced him, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther/10#v3)</sup> For Mordecai the Jew was next unto king Ahasuerus, and great among the Jews, and accepted of the multitude of his brethren, seeking the wealth of his people, and speaking peace to all his seed.

- The king Ahasuerus laid a tribute upon the land and upon the isles of the sea, and all the acts of his power and might, with the declaration of the greatness of Mardocheus whereunto the king advanced him, are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia.
- Mardocheus the Jew was next unto king Ahasuerus, great among the Jews and accepted of the multitude of his brethren, seeking the wealth of his people and speaking peace to all his seed.
- The Feast of Purim has now been established, and Mardocheus is lifted up to be number two in the kingdom for the works he did to warn the king and for standing with Esther through everything that led to this day.

**[Esther 10:4-5](/bible/esther/10#v4)**

- Mardocheus declares that the Most High is the one who did all these things, everything that happened with Esther, with Aman trying to get rid of the whole nation, and with Esther set in her position as the king's wife to save her people.
- He remembers a dream he had seen concerning these very matters, and he testifies that nothing of that dream failed to come to pass. He now begins to give the breakdown of the vision.

**[Esther 10:6-13](/bible/esther/10#v6)**

- Mardocheus opens the meaning of the vision. The little fountain that became a river with light and sun and much water is Esther, whom the king married and made queen. The two dragons are Mardocheus himself and Aman.
- The nations were those assembled to destroy the name of the Jews, and his nation is Israel, which cried to the Most High and were saved. The Most High delivered them from all the evils Aman devised, for Aman was trying to wipe them all out.
- The Most High made two lots, one for the people of God and another for all the Gentiles, and these two lots came at the hour and day of judgment before him among all nations. He remembered his people and justified his inheritance.
- These days in the month Adar, the fourteenth and fifteenth, are kept with assembly, joy, and gladness before the Most High throughout the generations. This is the Feast of Purim referenced back in the canonical account.

  - **[Esther 9:27](/bible/esther/9#v27)**
    > <sup>[27](/bible/esther/9#v27)</sup> The Jews ordained, and took upon them, and upon their seed, and upon all such as joined themselves unto them, so as it should not fail, that they would keep these two days according to their writing, and according to their appointed time every year;

    The Jews ordained upon themselves and their seed to keep these two days every year throughout every generation, that they should not fail nor the memorial of them perish; this is the Purim being referenced here.

# Esther 11: The Dream of Mardocheus

Read the chapter → [Additions to Esther 11](/bible/esther-greek/11)

## Introduction

Chapter 11 records the dream itself that Mardocheus had already interpreted in chapter 10. It sets the scene with the sending of the epistle of Purim and then goes back to the second year of the reign of Ahasuerus the great, when Mardocheus, a Jew and a captive carried from Jerusalem, saw a vision of two great dragons, of nations prepared for battle against a righteous people, and of a little fountain that became a great flood. He desired all night to know what the dream meant. This chapter proves the Apocrypha lines up with the canonical book, for the same Mardocheus and the same Babylonian captivity are named in Esther chapter 2.

**[Esther 11:1](/bible/esther-greek/11#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther-greek/11#v1)</sup> In the fourth year of the reign of Ptolemeus and Cleopatra, Dositheus, who said he was a priest and Levite, and Ptolemeus his son, brought this epistle of Phurim, which they said was the same, and that Lysimachus the son of Ptolemeus, that was in Jerusalem, had interpreted it.

- The chapter opens with a note that in the fourth year of the reign of Ptolemeus and Cleopatra, Dositheus, said to be a priest and Levite, and his son Ptolemeus, brought this epistle of Purim, which was interpreted by Lysimachus in Jerusalem.

**[Esther 11:2-4](/bible/esther-greek/11#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/esther-greek/11#v2)</sup> In the second year of the reign of Artexerxes the great, in the first day of the month Nisan, Mardocheus the son of Jairus, the son of Semei, the son of Cisai, of the tribe of Benjamin, had a dream;
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther-greek/11#v3)</sup> Who was a Jew, and dwelt in the city of Susa, a great man, being a servitor in the king’s court.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/esther-greek/11#v4)</sup> He was also one of the captives, which Nabuchodonosor the king of Babylon carried from Jerusalem with Jechonias king of Judea; and this was his dream:

- In the second year of the reign of Artexerxes the great, on the first day of the month Nisan, Mardocheus the son of Jairus, of the tribe of Benjamin, had a dream. He was a Jew dwelling in the city of Susa, a great man and a servitor in the king's court.
- Mardocheus was one of the captives whom Nabuchodonosor king of Babylon carried from Jerusalem with Jechonias king of Judea. This same captivity and lineage are named in the canonical book, which proves the Apocrypha runs right along with the rest of the Bible.

  - **[Esther 2:5-6](/bible/esther/2#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/esther/2#v5)</sup> Now in Shushan the palace there was a certain Jew, whose name was Mordecai, the son of Jair, the son of Shimei, the son of Kish, a Benjamite;
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/esther/2#v6)</sup> Who had been carried away from Jerusalem with the captivity which had been carried away with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon had carried away.

    The canonical book names the same Mardocheus, a Benjamite carried away from Jerusalem with Jeconiah by Nebuchadnezzar, the very account the Apocrypha references.

**[Esther 11:5-11](/bible/esther-greek/11#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/esther-greek/11#v5)</sup> Behold a noise of a tumult, with thunder, and earthquakes, and uproar in the land:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/esther-greek/11#v6)</sup> And, behold, two great dragons came forth ready to fight, and their cry was great.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/esther-greek/11#v7)</sup> And at their cry all nations were prepared to battle, that they might fight against the righteous people.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/esther-greek/11#v8)</sup> And lo a day of darkness and obscurity, tribulation and anguish, affliction and great uproar, upon earth.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/esther-greek/11#v9)</sup> And the whole righteous nation was troubled, fearing their own evils, and were ready to perish.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/esther-greek/11#v10)</sup> Then they cried unto God, and upon their cry, as it were from a little fountain, was made a great flood, even much water.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/esther-greek/11#v11)</sup> The light and the sun rose up, and the lowly were exalted, and devoured the glorious.

- Mardocheus saw a noise of tumult, with thunder, earthquakes, and uproar in the land, and two great dragons came forth ready to fight, and their cry was great. These are the two dragons already named as Mardocheus and Aman.
- At their cry all nations were prepared to battle against the righteous people, and there came a day of darkness, tribulation, anguish, and great uproar upon the earth, and the whole righteous nation was troubled and ready to perish.
- Then they cried unto the Most High, and upon their cry, as from a little fountain, was made a great flood of much water. That little fountain is Esther. The light and the sun rose up, the lowly were exalted, and they devoured the glorious.

**[Esther 11:12](/bible/esther-greek/11#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/esther-greek/11#v12)</sup> Now when Mardocheus, who had seen this dream, and what God had determined to do, was awake, he bare this dream in mind, and until night by all means was desirous to know it.

- When Mardocheus awoke, knowing what the Most High had determined to do, he kept the dream in mind and desired all night by every means to understand it. He wanted to know the meaning of the two dragons, the uproar of the nations, the cry of Israel, and the waters.

# Esther 12: Mardocheus Uncovers the Plot and Aman's Hatred

Read the chapter → [Additions to Esther 12](/bible/esther-greek/12)

## Introduction

Chapter 12 records the plot of the two eunuchs against the king and how Mardocheus uncovered it, saving the king's life, and how Aman the Agagite came to seek his hurt because of it. This account matches the canonical record in Esther chapter 2, where the same two chamberlains sought to lay hands on the king and Mardocheus told it through Esther. The chapter also opens the question of who Aman was, which the teaching traces from the Apocrypha through the canonical book back to Amalek and Esau, showing the enemy's lineage that has warred against Israel throughout history.

**[Esther 12:1-3](/bible/esther-greek/12#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther-greek/12#v1)</sup> And Mardocheus took his rest in the court with Gabatha and Tharra, the two eunuchs of the king, and keepers of the palace.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther-greek/12#v2)</sup> And he heard their devices, and searched out their purposes, and learned that they were about to lay hands upon Artexerxes the king; and so he certified the king of them.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther-greek/12#v3)</sup> Then the king examined the two eunuchs, and after that they had confessed it, they were strangled.

- Mardocheus rested in the court with Gabatha and Tharra, the two eunuchs and keepers of the palace, and he heard their devices, searched out their purposes, and learned they were about to lay hands upon Artexerxes the king, so he certified the king of them.
- This is the same account found in Esther chapter 2, where Mardocheus sat in the king's gate, uncovered the plot of the two chamberlains, and told it through Esther the queen. The king examined the two eunuchs, and after they confessed, they were strangled.

  - **[Esther 2:21-23](/bible/esther/2#v21)**
    > <sup>[21](/bible/esther/2#v21)</sup> In those days, while Mordecai sat in the king’s gate, two of the king’s chamberlains, Bigthan and Teresh, of those which kept the door, were wroth, and sought to lay hand on the king Ahasuerus.
    >
    > <sup>[22](/bible/esther/2#v22)</sup> And the thing was known to Mordecai, who told it unto Esther the queen; and Esther certified the king thereof in Mordecai’s name.
    >
    > <sup>[23](/bible/esther/2#v23)</sup> And when inquisition was made of the matter, it was found out; therefore they were both hanged on a tree: and it was written in the book of the chronicles before the king.

    The canonical book records the same two chamberlains, Bigthan and Teresh, who sought to lay hands on the king, whom Mardocheus exposed through Esther, and who were both hanged on a tree.

**[Esther 12:4-5](/bible/esther-greek/12#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/esther-greek/12#v4)</sup> And the king made a record of these things, and Mardocheus also wrote thereof.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/esther-greek/12#v5)</sup> So the king commanded, Mardocheus to serve in the court, and for this he rewarded him.

- The king made a record of these things, and Mardocheus also wrote of them. The king commanded him to serve in the court and rewarded him for it.
- The canonical account in Esther chapter 2 did not mention the reward at that point, but as the account continues in chapter 3 and on you see how Mardocheus was honored for saving the king's life.

**[Esther 12:6](/bible/esther-greek/12#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/esther-greek/12#v6)</sup> Howbeit Aman the son of Amadathus the Agagite, who was in great honour with the king, sought to molest Mardocheus and his people because of the two eunuchs of the king.

- Aman the son of Amadathus the Agagite, who was in great honour with the king, sought to molest Mardocheus and his people because of the two eunuchs. When Mardocheus told on the eunuchs and had them put to death, Aman was upset and set himself to destroy Mardocheus and all the Israelites.
- The teaching traces who Aman was. He is the same Aman promoted in the canonical book, an Agagite, and the Agagites descend from Agag king of the Amalekites, and Amalek descends from Esau. This is the lineage that has warred against Israel throughout history and has always been unsuccessful, for the Most High delivered his people.

  - **[Esther 3:1](/bible/esther/3#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/esther/3#v1)</sup> After these things did king Ahasuerus promote Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, and advanced him, and set his seat above all the princes that were with him.

    The canonical book promotes the same Aman, the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, setting his seat above all the princes.
  - **[Esther 16:10](/bible/esther-greek/16#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/esther-greek/16#v10)</sup> For Aman, a Macedonian, the son of Amadatha, being indeed a stranger from the Persian blood, and far distant from our goodness, and as a stranger received of us,

    This Apocrypha verse shows Aman was not of Persian blood but a Macedonian, a stranger received among them.
  - **[1 Samuel 15:8](/bible/1-samuel/15#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/1-samuel/15#v8)</sup> And he took Agag the king of the Amalekites alive, and utterly destroyed all the people with the edge of the sword.

    Agag was king of the Amalekites, taken alive by Saul, which is the stock the Agagite Aman comes from.
  - **[Genesis 36:12](/bible/genesis/36#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/genesis/36#v12)</sup> And Timna was concubine to Eliphaz Esau’s son; and she bare to Eliphaz Amalek: these were the sons of Adah Esau’s wife.

    Amalek was born to Eliphaz, Esau's son, showing Aman's descent from Esau, who is Edom.
