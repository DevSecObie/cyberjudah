## Introduction

This session finishes the book of Esther by going through the Apocrypha, the four additions known as "The Rest of the Book of Esther," chapters 13 through 16. It picks up where the canonical account left off: Haman has been promoted, Esther has been made queen, and Haman has laid a plot against the Jews because Mardocheus would not bow to him. Chapter 13 gives the copy of the first letter, the decree Haman drafted in the king's name to destroy all the Jews in one day, and then the prayer of Mardocheus. Chapter 14 is Esther's prayer. Chapter 15 shows Esther going before the king. Chapter 16 gives the second letter, the reversal, which calls Israel the children of the most high God. As we read, remember this is not just the story of Esther and Mardocheus and Haman; it is the story of our people, a day of sorrow turned into joy, and it is history to lean on when we go through our own affliction in captivity.

**[Esther 13:1-3](/bible/esther-greek/13#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/esther-greek/13#v1)</sup> The copy of the letters was this: The great king Artexerxes writeth these things to the princes and governours that are under him from India unto Ethiopia in an hundred and seven and twenty provinces.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/esther-greek/13#v2)</sup> After that I became lord over many nations and had dominion over the whole world, not lifted up with presumption of my authority, but carrying myself always with equity and mildness, I purposed to settle my subjects continually in a quiet life, and making my kingdom peaceable, and open for passage to the utmost coasts, to renew peace, which is desired of all men.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/esther-greek/13#v3)</sup> Now when I asked my counsellors how this might be brought to pass, Aman, that excelled in wisdom among us, and was approved for his constant good will and steadfast fidelity, and had the honour of the second place in the kingdom,

- The copy of the first letter opens with the great king Artaxerxes writing to the princes and governors under him, from India unto Ethiopia, over an hundred and seven and twenty provinces. This is the same king called Ahasuerus in the canonical book.
- The king presents himself as one who does not lift himself up in presumption of his authority but carries himself with equity and mildness, purposing to settle his subjects in a quiet life and make the kingdom peaceable. The words sound noble, but the letter itself was written by Haman's scribes; the king gave Haman his seal to send it.
- Aman, that is Haman, is named as the one who excelled in wisdom and held the second place in the kingdom. The whole letter is Haman speaking through the king's authority.

**[Esther 13:4-7](/bible/esther-greek/13#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/esther-greek/13#v4)</sup> Declared unto us, that in all nations throughout the world there was scattered a certain malicious people, that had laws contrary to all nations, and continually despised the commandments of kings, so as the uniting of our kingdoms, honourably intended by us cannot go forward.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/esther-greek/13#v5)</sup> Seeing then we understand that this people alone is continually in opposition unto all men, differing in the strange manner of their laws, and evil affected to our state, working all the mischief they can that our kingdom may not be firmly established:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/esther-greek/13#v6)</sup> Therefore have we commanded, that all they that are signified in writing unto you by Aman, who is ordained over the affairs, and is next unto us, shall all, with their wives and children, be utterly destroyed by the sword of their enemies, without all mercy and pity, the fourteenth day of the twelfth month Adar of this present year:
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/esther-greek/13#v7)</sup> That they, who of old and now also are malicious, may in one day with violence go into the grave, and so ever hereafter cause our affairs to be well settled, and without trouble.

- The letter paints Israel as a malicious people scattered among all nations, with laws contrary to all nations, continually despising the commandments of kings. This is the very picture Haman painted so the uniting of the kingdoms could not go forward.
- The charge is the same slander repeated: that this people alone is continually in opposition unto all men, differing in the strange manner of their laws. Haman wants them seen as a mischievous, malicious nation.
- The command goes out that all the Jews, with their wives and children, be utterly destroyed by the sword without mercy and pity on the fourteenth day of the twelfth month Adar. This is an order for the destruction of the Israelites, young and old together.
- This sounds exactly like the curses laid out for our people in [Deuteronomy 28](/bible/deuteronomy/28), where the enemy shows no favor toward the young or the old. Both the ancient and the present generation were marked for the grave in one day.

**[Esther 13:8-11](/bible/esther-greek/13#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/esther-greek/13#v8)</sup> Then Mardocheus thought upon all the works of the Lord, and made his prayer unto him,
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/esther-greek/13#v9)</sup> Saying, O Lord, Lord, the King Almighty: for the whole world is in thy power, and if thou hast appointed to save Israel, there is no man that can gainsay thee:
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/esther-greek/13#v10)</sup> For thou hast made heaven and earth, and all the wondrous things under the heaven.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/esther-greek/13#v11)</sup> Thou art Lord of all things, and and there is no man that can resist thee, which art the Lord.

- Mardocheus thought upon all the works of the Lord and made his prayer, confessing that the whole world is in the power of the Most High and that if he has appointed to save Israel, no man can gainsay him. Our forefathers understood their protection was in the Lord, not in the arm of man.
- He confesses the Most High as maker of heaven and earth and all the wondrous things under the heaven, Lord of all things, whom no man can resist. This is the ground of his prayer: the God who made all things can deliver his people.

**[Esther 13:12-18](/bible/esther-greek/13#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/esther-greek/13#v12)</sup> Thou knowest all things, and thou knowest, Lord, that it was neither in contempt nor pride, nor for any desire of glory, that I did not bow down to proud Aman.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/esther-greek/13#v13)</sup> For I could have been content with good will for the salvation of Israel to kiss the soles of his feet.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/esther-greek/13#v14)</sup> But I did this, that I might not prefer the glory of man above the glory of God: neither will I worship any but thee, O God, neither will I do it in pride.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/esther-greek/13#v15)</sup> And now, O Lord God and King, spare thy people: for their eyes are upon us to bring us to nought; yea, they desire to destroy the inheritance, that hath been thine from the beginning.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/esther-greek/13#v16)</sup> Despise not the portion, which thou hast delivered out of Egypt for thine own self.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/esther-greek/13#v17)</sup> Hear my prayer, and be merciful unto thine inheritance: turn our sorrow into joy, that we may live, O Lord, and praise thy name: and destroy not the mouths of them that praise thee, O Lord.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/esther-greek/13#v18)</sup> All Israel in like manner cried most earnestly unto the Lord, because their death was before their eyes.

- Mardocheus tells the Most High that his refusal to bow to proud Aman was neither in contempt nor pride nor desire of glory. He could have kissed the soles of Haman's feet for the salvation of Israel, but he would not prefer the glory of man above the glory of God, nor worship any but the Most High.
- He asks the Lord to spare his people, whose enemies desire to destroy the inheritance that has been the Lord's from the beginning, the portion delivered out of Egypt. He pleads that the Most High turn their sorrow into joy and not destroy the mouths of them that praise him.
- All Israel in like manner cried most earnestly unto the Lord because their death was before their eyes. Mardocheus rent his clothes and cried through the city with a loud and bitter cry, as the canonical account records.
