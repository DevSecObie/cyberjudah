## Introduction

This session moves from Ezra into [Nehemiah 1](/bible/nehemiah/1) and 2. Nehemiah comes about twelve years after Ezra, so there is again a time gap between the two men. Nehemiah is the Persian king's cupbearer, and when he hears that the remnant in Judah is in affliction and the wall of Jerusalem is broken down, he weeps, fasts, and prays, confessing the sins of Israel and pleading the promise of gathering. The Most High moves the king to send him to rebuild, with letters and an armed escort, and the heathen rulers are grieved that a man has come to seek the welfare of the children of Israel. The thread is that Nehemiah, like Ezra, gives all the glory to the good hand of the Most High upon him, never to his own wisdom, and is not ashamed of the work he came to do.

**[Nehemiah 1:1-3](/bible/nehemiah/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/nehemiah/1#v1)</sup> The words of Nehemiah the son of Hachaliah. And it came to pass in the month Chisleu, in the twentieth year, as I was in Shushan the palace,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/nehemiah/1#v2)</sup> That Hanani, one of my brethren, came, he and certain men of Judah; and I asked them concerning the Jews that had escaped, which were left of the captivity, and concerning Jerusalem.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/nehemiah/1#v3)</sup> And they said unto me, The remnant that are left of the captivity there in the province are in great affliction and reproach: the wall of Jerusalem also is broken down, and the gates thereof are burned with fire.

- These are the words of Nehemiah the son of Hachaliah. In the month Chisleu, in the twentieth year, as he was in the palace, Hanani one of his brethren comes with certain men of Judah, and Nehemiah asks concerning the Jews that had escaped the captivity and concerning Jerusalem.
- They tell him the remnant left of the captivity in the province are in great affliction and reproach, the wall of Jerusalem is broken down, and its gates are burned with fire.

**[Nehemiah 1:4-6](/bible/nehemiah/1#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/nehemiah/1#v4)</sup> And it came to pass, when I heard these words, that I sat down and wept, and mourned certain days, and fasted, and prayed before the God of heaven,
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/nehemiah/1#v5)</sup> And said, I beseech thee, O Lord God of heaven, the great and terrible God, that keepeth covenant and mercy for them that love him and observe his commandments:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/nehemiah/1#v6)</sup> Let thine ear now be attentive, and thine eyes open, that thou mayest hear the prayer of thy servant, which I pray before thee now, day and night, for the children of Israel thy servants, and confess the sins of the children of Israel, which we have sinned against thee: both I and my father’s house have sinned.

- When Nehemiah hears these words he sits down, weeps, mourns certain days, and fasts and prays before the God of heaven.
- He beseeches the Lord God of heaven, the great and terrible God that keeps covenant and mercy for them that love him and keep his commandments, that his ear would be attentive and his eyes open.
- He asks that the Most High hear the prayer he prays day and night for the children of Israel, and he confesses the sins of the children of Israel which they have sinned, both himself and his father's house.

**[Nehemiah 1:7-9](/bible/nehemiah/1#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/nehemiah/1#v7)</sup> We have dealt very corruptly against thee, and have not kept the commandments, nor the statutes, nor the judgments, which thou commandedst thy servant Moses.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/nehemiah/1#v8)</sup> Remember, I beseech thee, the word that thou commandedst thy servant Moses, saying, If ye transgress, I will scatter you abroad among the nations:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/nehemiah/1#v9)</sup> But if ye turn unto me, and keep my commandments, and do them; though there were of you cast out unto the uttermost part of the heaven, yet will I gather them from thence, and will bring them unto the place that I have chosen to set my name there.

- He confesses they have dealt very corruptly and not kept the commandments, statutes, or judgments the Most High commanded his servant Moses.
- He pleads the word the Most High commanded Moses, that if they transgress he will scatter them abroad among the nations, but if they turn and keep his commandments and do them, though they were cast out to the uttermost part of heaven, he will gather them from thence and bring them to the place he has chosen to set his name.
- This is Nehemiah quoting [Deuteronomy 28](/bible/deuteronomy/28), holding the Most High to what Moses had already taught in the wilderness, both the scattering and the gathering.

  - **[Deuteronomy 30:1-4](/bible/deuteronomy/30#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/30#v1)</sup> And it shall come to pass, when all these things are come upon thee, the blessing and the curse, which I have set before thee, and thou shalt call them to mind among all the nations, whither the Lord thy God hath driven thee,
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/30#v2)</sup> And shalt return unto the Lord thy God, and shalt obey his voice according to all that I command thee this day, thou and thy children, with all thine heart, and with all thy soul;
    >
    > <sup>[3](/bible/deuteronomy/30#v3)</sup> That then the Lord thy God will turn thy captivity, and have compassion upon thee, and will return and gather thee from all the nations, whither the Lord thy God hath scattered thee.
    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/deuteronomy/30#v4)</sup> If any of thine be driven out unto the outmost parts of heaven, from thence will the Lord thy God gather thee, and from thence will he fetch thee:

    When the blessing and the curse are come upon them and they return to the Most High among the nations, he promises to gather them from all the peoples where he scattered them, even from the outmost parts of heaven, the very promise Nehemiah pleads.

**[Nehemiah 1:10-11](/bible/nehemiah/1#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/nehemiah/1#v10)</sup> Now these are thy servants and thy people, whom thou hast redeemed by thy great power, and by thy strong hand.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/nehemiah/1#v11)</sup> O Lord, I beseech thee, let now thine ear be attentive to the prayer of thy servant, and to the prayer of thy servants, who desire to fear thy name: and prosper, I pray thee, thy servant this day, and grant him mercy in the sight of this man. For I was the king’s cupbearer.

- These are the servants and the people whom the Most High has redeemed by his great power and strong hand, and Nehemiah asks that the Lord's ear be attentive to the prayer of his servant and of the servants who desire to fear his name.
- He asks the Most High to prosper his servant this day and grant him mercy in the sight of this man, for he was the king's cupbearer, whose job was to serve the wine and keep a joyous countenance before the king.

# Nehemiah 2: The King Sends Nehemiah to Rebuild

Read the chapter → [Nehemiah 2](/bible/nehemiah/2)

**[Nehemiah 2:1-2](/bible/nehemiah/2#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/nehemiah/2#v1)</sup> And it came to pass in the month Nisan, in the twentieth year of Artaxerxes the king, that wine was before him: and I took up the wine, and gave it unto the king. Now I had not been beforetime sad in his presence.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/nehemiah/2#v2)</sup> Wherefore the king said unto me, Why is thy countenance sad, seeing thou art not sick? this is nothing else but sorrow of heart. Then I was very sore afraid,

- In the month Nisan in the twentieth year of Artaxerxes the king, wine is before him and Nehemiah takes up the wine and gives it to the king. This is Artaxerxes the first, the king that came after Ahasuerus of the book of Esther.
- Nehemiah had not before been sad in the king's presence, for a cupbearer must be of a cheerful countenance, so the king asks why his countenance is sad seeing he is not sick, saying this is nothing but sorrow of heart, and Nehemiah is very sore afraid.

**[Nehemiah 2:3-4](/bible/nehemiah/2#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/nehemiah/2#v3)</sup> And said unto the king, Let the king live for ever: why should not my countenance be sad, when the city, the place of my fathers’ sepulchres, lieth waste, and the gates thereof are consumed with fire?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/nehemiah/2#v4)</sup> Then the king said unto me, For what dost thou make request? So I prayed to the God of heaven.

- Nehemiah answers, Let the king live for ever, why should not his countenance be sad when the city, the place of his fathers' sepulchres, lies waste and its gates are consumed with fire.
- The king asks what request he makes, and Nehemiah prays to the God of heaven before he answers, seeking the Most High even in that moment before the king.

**[Nehemiah 2:5-6](/bible/nehemiah/2#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/nehemiah/2#v5)</sup> And I said unto the king, If it please the king, and if thy servant have found favour in thy sight, that thou wouldest send me unto Judah, unto the city of my fathers’ sepulchres, that I may build it.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/nehemiah/2#v6)</sup> And the king said unto me, (the queen also sitting by him,) For how long shall thy journey be? and when wilt thou return? So it pleased the king to send me; and I set him a time.

- Nehemiah asks that if it please the king and if his servant have found favour, the king would send him to Judah, to the city of his fathers' sepulchres, that he may build it.
- The king, the queen also sitting by him, asks how long his journey would be and when he would return, so Nehemiah sets him a time, and it pleased the king to send him.

**[Nehemiah 2:7-8](/bible/nehemiah/2#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/nehemiah/2#v7)</sup> Moreover I said unto the king, If it please the king, let letters be given me to the governors beyond the river, that they may convey me over till I come into Judah;
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/nehemiah/2#v8)</sup> And a letter unto Asaph the keeper of the king’s forest, that he may give me timber to make beams for the gates of the palace which appertained to the house, and for the wall of the city, and for the house that I shall enter into. And the king granted me, according to the good hand of my God upon me.

- Nehemiah asks for letters to the governors beyond the river to convey him until he comes into Judah, and a letter to Asaph the keeper of the king's forest to give him timber for the beams of the gates, the wall, and the house he would enter.
- The king grants him all this according to the good hand of his God upon him. Nehemiah had already prayed, and the Most High heard him and put it upon the king's heart to allow him to go.

  - **[Jeremiah 15:11](/bible/jeremiah/15#v11)**
    > <sup>[11](/bible/jeremiah/15#v11)</sup> The Lord said, Verily it shall be well with thy remnant; verily I will cause the enemy to entreat thee well in the time of evil and in the time of affliction.

    The Most High says he will cause the enemy to entreat his remnant well in the time of evil; it is not that the heathen love Israel, but that the Lord moves a king or a friend to deal well with them.
  - **[1 Esdras 8:25](/bible/1-esdras/8#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/1-esdras/8#v25)</sup> Then said Esdras the scribe, Blessed be the only Lord God of my fathers, who hath put these things into the heart of the king, to glorify his house that is in Jerusalem:

    Ezra blessed the Most High for putting it into the heart of the king, showing the good the heathen ruler does is the Lord moving him, not his own love for Israel.

**[Nehemiah 2:9-10](/bible/nehemiah/2#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/nehemiah/2#v9)</sup> Then I came to the governors beyond the river, and gave them the king’s letters. Now the king had sent captains of the army and horsemen with me.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/nehemiah/2#v10)</sup> When Sanballat the Horonite, and Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, heard of it, it grieved them exceedingly that there was come a man to seek the welfare of the children of Israel.

- Nehemiah comes to the governors beyond the river and gives them the king's letters, and the king had sent captains of the army and horsemen with him, an armed escort to see him safe.
- When Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, hear of it, it grieves them exceedingly that a man has come to seek the welfare of the children of Israel, the same opposition seen when the nation tried to rebuild before.

  - **[Ezra 4:4-5](/bible/ezra/4#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/ezra/4#v4)</sup> Then the people of the land weakened the hands of the people of Judah, and troubled them in building,
    >
    > <sup>[5](/bible/ezra/4#v5)</sup> And hired counsellors against them, to frustrate their purpose, all the days of Cyrus king of Persia, even until the reign of Darius king of Persia.

    The people of the land weakened the hands of Judah, troubled them in building, and hired counsellors against them to frustrate their purpose, the same hindering spirit against the rebuilding.
  - **[Ezra 4:12-13](/bible/ezra/4#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/ezra/4#v12)</sup> Be it known unto the king, that the Jews which came up from thee to us are come unto Jerusalem, building the rebellious and the bad city, and have set up the walls thereof, and joined the foundations.
    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/ezra/4#v13)</sup> Be it known now unto the king, that, if this city be builded, and the walls set up again, then will they not pay toll, tribute, and custom, and so thou shalt endamage the revenue of the kings.

    The enemies wrote to the king that if the rebellious city were built and the walls set up, the Jews would no more pay toll, tribute, and custom, and so damage the revenue of kings, the same mindset that fears Israel's rebuilding.

**[Nehemiah 2:11-16](/bible/nehemiah/2#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/nehemiah/2#v11)</sup> So I came to Jerusalem, and was there three days.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/nehemiah/2#v12)</sup> And I arose in the night, I and some few men with me; neither told I any man what my God had put in my heart to do at Jerusalem: neither was there any beast with me, save the beast that I rode upon.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/nehemiah/2#v13)</sup> And I went out by night by the gate of the valley, even before the dragon well, and to the dung port, and viewed the walls of Jerusalem, which were broken down, and the gates thereof were consumed with fire.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/nehemiah/2#v14)</sup> Then I went on to the gate of the fountain, and to the king’s pool: but there was no place for the beast that was under me to pass.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/nehemiah/2#v15)</sup> Then went I up in the night by the brook, and viewed the wall, and turned back, and entered by the gate of the valley, and so returned.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/nehemiah/2#v16)</sup> And the rulers knew not whither I went, or what I did; neither had I as yet told it to the Jews, nor to the priests, nor to the nobles, nor to the rulers, nor to the rest that did the work.

- Nehemiah comes to Jerusalem and is there three days, then arises in the night with a few men, telling no man what his God had put in his heart to do, for saying too much could ruin the plan.
- He goes out by night through the gate of the valley, past the dragon well to the dung port, and views the walls of Jerusalem broken down and the gates consumed with fire, then goes on to the gate of the fountain and the king's pool.
- The rulers do not know where he went or what he did, neither had he yet told the Jews, priests, nobles, rulers, or the rest that did the work.

**[Nehemiah 2:17-18](/bible/nehemiah/2#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/nehemiah/2#v17)</sup> Then said I unto them, Ye see the distress that we are in, how Jerusalem lieth waste, and the gates thereof are burned with fire: come, and let us build up the wall of Jerusalem, that we be no more a reproach.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/nehemiah/2#v18)</sup> Then I told them of the hand of my God which was good upon me; as also the king’s words that he had spoken unto me. And they said, Let us rise up and build. So they strengthened their hands for this good work.

- Nehemiah then tells them, Ye see the distress that we are in, how Jerusalem lies waste and its gates are burned; come and let us build up the wall of Jerusalem that we be no more a reproach.
- He tells them of the good hand of his God upon him and the king's words, and they say, Let us rise up and build, and strengthen their hands for this good work.
- What is admirable in Nehemiah, as in Ezra, is that he never says the work was of his own knowledge or wisdom, but that the Lord's hand was upon him, and he puts that same vision on the men around him so they strengthen their hands to help.

**[Nehemiah 2:19-20](/bible/nehemiah/2#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/nehemiah/2#v19)</sup> But when Sanballat the Horonite, and Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, and Geshem the Arabian, heard it, they laughed us to scorn, and despised us, and said, What is this thing that ye do? will ye rebel against the king?
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/nehemiah/2#v20)</sup> Then answered I them, and said unto them, The God of heaven, he will prosper us; therefore we his servants will arise and build: but ye have no portion, nor right, nor memorial, in Jerusalem.

- When Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite, and Geshem the Arabian hear it, they laugh them to scorn, despise them, and ask what this thing is, and whether they will rebel against the king.
- Nehemiah answers that the God of heaven will prosper them, and his servants will arise and build, but the mockers have no portion nor right nor memorial in Jerusalem. There was no shame in what he came to do.
