## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Romans 4 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-6, 19-25, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Romans 4:1](/bible/romans/4#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/romans/4#v1)</sup> What shall we say then that Abraham our father, as pertaining to the flesh, hath found?

- “What shall we say then that Abraham our father, as pertaining to the flesh, hath found?” All nations do not have Abraham as their father, so Paul cannot have been writing to all nations. Some Christians say “our father” means by faith. No: our father means by race, by ancestry. The saints Paul writes to are Abraham’s children by descent.
- Romans 5 is the last safe haven Christianity has. John 3:16, Galatians 3:28, Romans 11 and Acts 10 have already been conquered. Romans 5 is difficult, and it is their last strong tower, but it has got to come down. And Romans 5 has to be read from Romans 4, because it is all one letter.

**[Romans 4:2](/bible/romans/4#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/romans/4#v2)</sup> For if Abraham were justified by works, he hath whereof to glory; but not before God.

- “For if Abraham were justified by works, he hath whereof to glory; but not before God.” Focus on the word justified, because it comes up again and again. The works are sacrifice. Did Abraham sacrifice early in Genesis? Yes. But his belief, his faith, came first. Faith precedes the works.
- If Abraham were justified by works, he could glory because he sacrificed, but not before God. The Lord counted Abraham righteous on his belief, because his belief went before his works, and his works followed.

**[Romans 4:3](/bible/romans/4#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/romans/4#v3)</sup> For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness.

- “For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness.” What does the scripture say about works? Abraham had faith, and his faith was counted for righteousness; the works followed the faith. That is how Paul quotes Genesis 15: faith first, then works.
- The context is that Paul is writing to Jews who were born and raised in the law, who believed their works were enough. When Paul wrote to them they were still going to the temple and still sacrificing. Paul is telling them: you cannot boast in animal sacrifice; that is not enough anymore. You have to have faith in Christ first and foremost.

  Precepts:
  - **[Genesis 15:1-10](/bible/genesis/15#v1)**

    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/genesis/15#v2)</sup> And Abram said, Lord God, what wilt thou give me, seeing I go childless, and the steward of my house is this Eliezer of Damascus?
    >
    > <sup>[3](/bible/genesis/15#v3)</sup> And Abram said, Behold, to me thou hast given no seed: and, lo, one born in my house is mine heir.
    >
    > <sup>[4](/bible/genesis/15#v4)</sup> And, behold, the word of the Lord came unto him, saying, This shall not be thine heir; but he that shall come forth out of thine own bowels shall be thine heir.
    >
    > <sup>[5](/bible/genesis/15#v5)</sup> And he brought him forth abroad, and said, Look now toward heaven, and tell the stars, if thou be able to number them: and he said unto him, So shall thy seed be.
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/genesis/15#v6)</sup> And he believed in the Lord; and he counted it to him for righteousness.
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/genesis/15#v7)</sup> And he said unto him, I am the Lord that brought thee out of Ur of the Chaldees, to give thee this land to inherit it.
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/genesis/15#v8)</sup> And he said, Lord God, whereby shall I know that I shall inherit it?
    >

    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/genesis/15#v10)</sup> And he took unto him all these, and divided them in the midst, and laid each piece one against another: but the birds divided he not.

    This is the scripture Paul quotes, and it shows what the works were. “Fear not, Abram: I am thy shield, and thy exceeding great reward.” Abram answers, “what wilt thou give me, seeing I go childless, and the steward of my house is this Eliezer of Damascus?” Abraham was too old and Sarah was too old to have children, so he said the oldest servant of his house would be his heir: “lo, one born in my house is mine heir.”

**[Romans 4:4](/bible/romans/4#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/romans/4#v4)</sup> Now to him that worketh is the reward not reckoned of grace, but of debt.

- “Now to him that worketh is the reward not reckoned of grace, but of debt.” To him that works, that offers sacrifices, the reward is not reckoned of grace, or mercy, but of debt. The debt is the law of sacrifice: under sacrifice you are indebted to sacrifice an animal or die. Under Moses there was no mercy and no grace.
- The prophets spoke of the temple being destroyed. Once the temple is destroyed, what happens to your works? If your faith is based solely on sacrificing an animal and the temple is gone, where does your faith go? You are screwed. If you indebted yourself to sacrificing animals as your atonement and the temple is gone, you have condemned yourself already, because there is no animal, no priest and no temple to atone with. So Paul tells them their faith has to supersede the works.

  Precepts:
  - **[Nehemiah 10:33](/bible/nehemiah/10#v33)**
    > <sup>[33](/bible/nehemiah/10#v33)</sup> For the shewbread, and for the continual meat offering, and for the continual burnt offering, of the sabbaths, of the new moons, for the set feasts, and for the holy things, and for the sin offerings to make an atonement for Israel, and for all the work of the house of our God.

    What are the works Paul means by “him that worketh”? Peter said they stumble at the other scriptures, and these are the scriptures Paul cites from. There are many verses that say what the works are; this one condenses it into one verse.
  - **[Hebrews 10:28](/bible/hebrews/10#v28)**
    > <sup>[28](/bible/hebrews/10#v28)</sup> He that despised Moses’ law died without mercy under two or three witnesses:

    On grace: “He that despised Moses’ law died without mercy under two or three witnesses”. Without mercy means without grace. If you did not keep what you were indebted to keep, you got death.

**[Romans 4:5](/bible/romans/4#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/romans/4#v5)</sup> But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness.

- “But to him that worketh not”: him that does not offer sacrifice, “but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly”: him that has faith in Christ, who justifies the ungodly through his death. “his faith is counted for righteousness”, just as Abraham’s was before he sacrificed. Believeth and faith are synonymous.
- While the temple was still standing the argument was: what justifies us, the animals or Christ’s body? Paul had to clear it up with the Jews in Rome, who were still going to the temple. Sacrifice all you want, but if you make your salvation rest on animal sacrifice, you are indebted to it as long as that temple is around, and once it is destroyed you are done. You have to have faith on Christ. That is the context of Romans 4: faith before the works.

**[Romans 4:6](/bible/romans/4#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/romans/4#v6)</sup> Even as David also describeth the blessedness of the man, unto whom God imputeth righteousness without works,

- “Even as David also describeth the blessedness of the man, unto whom God imputeth righteousness without works”. God imputed righteousness to Abraham without the works of sacrifice. The Lord had already acknowledged Abraham’s faith before he sacrificed animals; Abraham was deemed faithful before he ever sacrificed.

**[Romans 4:19](/bible/romans/4#v19)**

> <sup>[19](/bible/romans/4#v19)</sup> And being not weak in faith, he considered not his own body now dead, when he was about an hundred years old, neither yet the deadness of Sara’s womb:

- “And being not weak in faith, he considered not his own body now dead, when he was about an hundred years old, neither yet the deadness of Sara’s womb”. Sarah could not have children: she was ninety years old, and her womb was dead.

**[Romans 4:20](/bible/romans/4#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/romans/4#v20)</sup> He staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief; but was strong in faith, giving glory to God;

- “He staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief”. He was promised the land, and he did not live on it long; he was old and he died. But he did not doubt that his wife would bring forth a child at ninety. He did not need to sacrifice an animal to believe it; he believed it off top. He did not say, You sure, Lord? How do you know? He was like Moses, meek, completely submissive to the Lord: understood, Lord. “but was strong in faith, giving glory to God”: strong in belief.

**[Romans 4:21](/bible/romans/4#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/romans/4#v21)</sup> And being fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform.

- “And being fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform.” He was fully persuaded that his children would be as the sand of the sea and as the stars of heaven. Did he live to see it? No, but he believed it. He was told he would have a child in his old age; he believed it, and it happened. He understood that what the Lord promised, God was able to perform.

**[Romans 4:22](/bible/romans/4#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/romans/4#v22)</sup> And therefore it was imputed to him for righteousness.

- “And therefore it was imputed to him for righteousness.” He was counted righteous because he was strong in faith; he is the father of our faith.
- Paul uses Abraham as the example to show us that we have to have faith in Christ, and that his death and sacrifice is what we need. We no longer need animals. If you put animals before Christ and the temple is gone, you have condemned yourself by default, with no animal to atone with and no priest or temple to atone in.
- Israel was so accustomed to sacrificing animals that Paul said: fine, but make sure you acknowledge Christ and his sacrifice first. If you acknowledge Christ second, or not at all, and put your faith in that animal, you are done. You are in debt.

**[Romans 4:23-24](/bible/romans/4#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/romans/4#v23)</sup> Now it was not written for his sake alone, that it was imputed to him;
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/romans/4#v24)</sup> But for us also, to whom it shall be imputed, if we believe on him that raised up Jesus our Lord from the dead;

- “Now it was not written for his sake alone, that it was imputed to him; But for us also, to whom it shall be imputed, if we believe on him that raised up Jesus our Lord from the dead”. Not for Abraham alone, but for us. That is what we have to believe: we have to believe in Christ. The faith must come first.

**[Romans 4:25](/bible/romans/4#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/romans/4#v25)</sup> Who was delivered for our offences, and was raised again for our justification.

- “Who was delivered for our offences”: delivered, killed, for our offences. Our is a possessive word. “and was raised again for our justification”: raised from the dead for our justification, our reconciliation. Earlier Paul said Christ’s death justifies the ungodly.
- Who is the our? Israel. And the many that are justified? Israel. When you read justification all through Paul’s letters, there should be no confusion about what he is talking about, or whom.

  Precepts:
  - **[Isaiah 53:4-8](/bible/isaiah/53#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/isaiah/53#v4)</sup> Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted.
    >
    > <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/53#v5)</sup> But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/isaiah/53#v6)</sup> All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the Lord hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/isaiah/53#v7)</sup> He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth: he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth.
    >
    > <sup>[8](/bible/isaiah/53#v8)</sup> He was taken from prison and from judgment: and who shall declare his generation? for he was cut off out of the land of the living: for the transgression of my people was he stricken.

    Whose offences was Christ delivered for? “Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows”: our griefs are the same as our offences. This is Isaiah talking about his people. “But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed”: healed, delivered, saved.
  - **[Daniel 9:24-26](/bible/daniel/9#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/daniel/9#v24)</sup> Seventy weeks are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city, to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sins, and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in everlasting righteousness, and to seal up the vision and prophecy, and to anoint the most Holy.
    >
    > <sup>[25](/bible/daniel/9#v25)</sup> Know therefore and understand, that from the going forth of the commandment to restore and to build Jerusalem unto the Messiah the Prince shall be seven weeks, and threescore and two weeks: the street shall be built again, and the wall, even in troublous times.
    >
    > <sup>[26](/bible/daniel/9#v26)</sup> And after threescore and two weeks shall Messiah be cut off, but not for himself: and the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary; and the end thereof shall be with a flood, and unto the end of the war desolations are determined.

    “Seventy weeks are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city”: 490 years, upon Daniel’s people. “to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sins”: that is what Christ did when he died. “and to make reconciliation for iniquity”: reconciliation goes back to the word atonement and the word justification. “and to anoint the most Holy”: that is Christ.
  - **[Isaiah 53:11-12](/bible/isaiah/53#v11)**
    > <sup>[11](/bible/isaiah/53#v11)</sup> He shall see of the travail of his soul, and shall be satisfied: by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many; for he shall bear their iniquities.
    >
    > <sup>[12](/bible/isaiah/53#v12)</sup> Therefore will I divide him a portion with the great, and he shall divide the spoil with the strong; because he hath poured out his soul unto death: and he was numbered with the transgressors; and he bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors.

    “by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many”. By the Father’s knowledge, the righteous servant, Christ, shall justify many: give them justification. Some say, see, many means anybody. “for he shall bear their iniquities”: this goes back to the iniquity of us all, the sheep, in verse 6, and to my people in verse 8. The thought has not changed. The many are Israelites.
