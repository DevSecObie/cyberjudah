## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Romans 11 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-32, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Romans 11:1](/bible/romans/11#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/romans/11#v1)</sup> I say then, Hath God cast away his people? God forbid. For I also am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin.

- With all this, they have something called Christian dispensationalism, or replacement theology: that God’s people are done away with. “I say then, Hath God cast away his people?” Paul was dealing with that back then. “God forbid”: he says no. “For I also am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin.” Paul proves the Israelites were not done away with.
- Replacement theology says the church has taken the twelve tribes of Israel out: you are no more. Paul had to address that same issue. “Hath God cast away his people? God forbid.” The answer is no. God has not cast away the twelve tribes of Israel. “For I also am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin.” On the street, that first verse tells you what the subject is.
- They say the church took the place of the twelve tribes. Here's the answer to that: “Hath God cast away his people? God forbid.” That means no. “For I also am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin.” So God has not cast away his people.

**[Romans 11:2-4](/bible/romans/11#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/romans/11#v2)</sup> God hath not cast away his people which he foreknew. Wot ye not what the scripture saith of Elias? how he maketh intercession to God against Israel, saying,
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/romans/11#v3)</sup> Lord, they have killed thy prophets, and digged down thine altars; and I am left alone, and they seek my life.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/romans/11#v4)</sup> But what saith the answer of God unto him? I have reserved to myself seven thousand men, who have not bowed the knee to the image of Baal.

- “God hath not cast away his people which he foreknew.” Elias cried, “Lord, they have killed thy prophets, and digged down thine altars; and I am left alone”: the same as 1 Kings 19:10, where the killing of the prophets was read earlier. God answered, “I have reserved to myself seven thousand men, who have not bowed the knee to the image of Baal.” Not all were killed off; some still worshipped him.
- “God hath not cast away his people which he foreknew.” Elias made intercession against Israel: “Lord, they have killed thy prophets, and digged down thine altars; and I am left alone, and they seek my life.” And God answered, “I have reserved to myself seven thousand men, who have not bowed the knee to the image of Baal.”
- So he’s telling the northern kingdom: even though you may not see them, the southern kingdom, Judah, is still alive; there are still men reserved to him. In their mind there’s nobody left. Look what happened with Cornelius: it astonished the people, the Jews didn’t believe it, they were vexed and had envy that God was dealing with them. He said, just know there are men reserved who have not bowed a knee.

**[Romans 11:5](/bible/romans/11#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/romans/11#v5)</sup> Even so then at this present time also there is a remnant according to the election of grace.

- “Even so then at this present time also there is a remnant according to the election of grace.” The election of grace deals with the twelve tribes of Israel.
- “Even so then at this present time also there is a remnant according to the election of grace.” There will be a remnant of God’s people who serve the Lord against the odds of the wickedness growing on this earth, who will not bow the knee to Baal or accept the mark of the beast. When persecution arises, many Israelites will go back into the world. Right now it is street teaching and making white folks feel bad. But when Trump or his administration turns the heat up, and the Bible or teaching in the streets is outlawed, we will see what everybody is made of and who will not bow to Baal.
- “Even so then at this present time also there is a remnant according to the election of grace.” There’s a remnant of Judah that’s going to come back.

    “For Jacob my servant's sake, and Israel mine elect, I have even called thee by thy name: I have surnamed thee, though thou hast not known me.” We are the elect. Israel is the elect, not all nations.

**[Romans 11:6](/bible/romans/11#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/romans/11#v6)</sup> And if by grace, then is it no more of works: otherwise grace is no more grace. But if it be of works, then is it no more grace: otherwise work is no more work.

- “And if by grace, then is it no more of works”: a Christian will try to hem you up with that, saying it is not about works, my brother. Christians assume that when Paul uses the word works, he means thou shalt not kill, thou shalt not commit adultery. That is not what Paul is talking about at all. The works are the works of the temple, the works of animal sacrifice, the offerings and things of that nature. “otherwise grace is no more grace.”
- “And if by grace, then is it no more of works”. The remnant is called by grace: mercy in Christ. His death and sacrifice granted us grace. The works are the sacrifices and temple ordinances, still going on when Paul wrote, because the temple had not yet been destroyed. We are not called by the animals we sacrifice; we are called by our acceptance of Christ’s death on the cross and of him being the Messiah.
- “Otherwise grace is no more grace”: if we still accept the sacrificing of animals, Christ’s death is void; we put Christ to the back. “But if it be of works, then is it no more grace: otherwise work is no more work”: sacrifice is done away with because Christ died on the cross.

  Precepts:
  - **[Nehemiah 10:33](/bible/nehemiah/10#v33)**
    > <sup>[33](/bible/nehemiah/10#v33)</sup> For the shewbread, and for the continual meat offering, and for the continual burnt offering, of the sabbaths, of the new moons, for the set feasts, and for the holy things, and for the sin offerings to make an atonement for Israel, and for all the work of the house of our God.

    This shows what the works are: “For the shewbread, and for the continual meat offering, and for the continual burnt offering, of the sabbaths, of the new moons, for the set feasts, and for the holy things, and for the sin offerings to make an atonement for Israel, and for all the work of the house of our God.” The reader must stress it: all the work of the house of our God.

**[Romans 11:7-8](/bible/romans/11#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/romans/11#v7)</sup> What then? Israel hath not obtained that which he seeketh for; but the election hath obtained it, and the rest were blinded
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/romans/11#v8)</sup> (According as it is written, God hath given them the spirit of slumber, eyes that they should not see, and ears that they should not hear;) unto this day.

- “Israel hath not obtained that which he seeketh for; but the election hath obtained it”: the election of grace, those that accept Christ. “And the rest were blinded”: the leaders, who relied on animal sacrifice and temple ceremonies, which they presided over and lived off of from the people. They rejected Christ. “God hath given them the spirit of slumber, eyes that they should not see”: that is to the rulers, the blind Christ spoke of: fools and blind, the blind leading the blind.

**[Romans 11:11](/bible/romans/11#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/romans/11#v11)</sup> I say then, Have they stumbled that they should fall? God forbid: but rather through their fall salvation is come unto the Gentiles, for to provoke them to jealousy.

- “Have they stumbled that they should fall?” The they are the leaders, the rulers, with their eyes darkened, stumbling at that stumbling block. Should they fall forever, be cast away? The thought cannot change from verse 1. “God forbid”: their fall is not forever, it is temporary.
- “But rather through their fall”, their stumbling at Christ, refusing him and killing him, “salvation is come unto the Gentiles”: the northern kingdom, “for to provoke them to jealousy”: the southern kingdom, the elders. That is what Moses said: I will provoke you to jealousy with them that are not a people. Moses saw this already. It is meant to bring them back together again.
- “Have they stumbled that they should fall? God forbid: but rather through their fall salvation is come unto the Gentiles, for to provoke them to jealousy.” That would provoke the southern kingdom to jealousy: this dude was wearing a toga last year, celebrating Bacchus or whatever he was doing, and now the Holy Spirit is on him and he’s prophesying, and we’re like, damn, we’ve got to get ourselves together. That was the prophecy, that it will provoke them.

    “The Lord also shall save the tents of Judah first, that the glory of the house of David and the glory of the inhabitants of Jerusalem do not magnify themselves against Judah.” He raises the tents of Judah first.

    “For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first”, Judah first, “and also to the Greek”: the northern kingdom.

**[Romans 11:12-13](/bible/romans/11#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/romans/11#v12)</sup> Now if the fall of them be the riches of the world, and the diminishing of them the riches of the Gentiles; how much more their fulness?
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/romans/11#v13)</sup> For I speak to you Gentiles, inasmuch as I am the apostle of the Gentiles, I magnify mine office:

- “Now if the fall of them be the riches of the world, and the diminishing of them the riches of the Gentiles; how much more their fulness? For I speak to you Gentiles, inasmuch as I am the apostle of the Gentiles, I magnify mine office”. Peter’s office was to the circumcision; Paul’s was to the uncircumcised. It makes sense: Paul could not prophesy to the circumcision, they would have killed him, because he was one of them. You’ve got to go far out, Paul, go teach some people out there, and he was more than capable.
- Who else should have been capable? All those Hebrews should have been capable, but they didn’t understand Christ. They needed someone to teach them again the first principles of the oracles of God; they didn’t need strong meat, they needed milk, simple things like Deuteronomy 18:18 and Isaiah 56. The Bible was always prophesying of him.
- “Now if the fall of them be the riches of the world”: the fall of the builders, the southern kingdom. The world is Israel. The riches of the world are not money; they are the kingdom. The builders had to reject Christ because it was their lot; once Christ died on the cross he granted us access to the kingdom. “And the diminishing of them”: diminishing goes back to the fall, a dropping lower, decay or loss, as the margin of the Cambridge Bible gives it. “How much more their fulness?”: when they all come back together.

  - **[Isaiah 45:17](/bible/isaiah/45#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/45#v17)</sup> But Israel shall be saved in the Lord with an everlasting salvation: ye shall not be ashamed nor confounded world without end.

    Some say the world is the world. No, the world is us. “But Israel shall be saved in the Lord with an everlasting salvation: ye shall not be ashamed nor confounded world without end.” Israel is the world without end.

**[Romans 11:14-15](/bible/romans/11#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/romans/11#v14)</sup> If by any means I may provoke to emulation them which are my flesh, and might save some of them.

> <sup>[15](/bible/romans/11#v15)</sup> For if the casting away of them be the reconciling of the world, what shall the receiving of them be, but life from the dead?

- “If by any means I may provoke to emulation them which are my flesh, and might save some of them.” “For if the casting away of them be the reconciling of the world”: the falling of the southern kingdom is the reconciling. To reconcile is to bring back together all the tribes; you needed them to fall to bring them back. “What shall the receiving of them be, but life from the dead?”
- “If by any means I may provoke to emulation them which are my flesh”. Emulation goes back to jealousy. His flesh is the southern kingdom. By teaching the northern kingdom, he provokes his own flesh to jealousy, “and might save some of them”: the southern kingdom, the leaders. Write verse 11 next to verse 14 and verse 14 next to verse 11, and underline emulation and jealousy, so you know why when you read it.
- Paul was raised a Pharisee. The southern kingdom consisted primarily of Levi, Benjamin and Judah, also the leaders, the builders. The top elders were Caiaphas and Annas, and below them their disciples, Gamaliel and Paul.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ephesians 2:1](/bible/ephesians/2#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/ephesians/2#v1)</sup> And you hath he quickened, who were dead in trespasses and sins;

    Life from the dead, because they were dead in their sin: “And you hath he quickened, who were dead in trespasses and sins”. He that quickened is Christ: quickened means brought back to life. That is when they received Christ.

**[Romans 11:16](/bible/romans/11#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/romans/11#v16)</sup> For if the firstfruit be holy, the lump is also holy: and if the root be holy, so are the branches.

- It’s the same thing in the New Testament: the two sticks coming together. They thought this was the end of our people. It’s not the end; this is only just beginning. “For if the firstfruit be holy, the lump is also holy: and if the root be holy, so are the branches.” Who’s the root? Christ is that root. And who are the branches? Israel is the branches.
- “For if the firstfruit be holy”: the firstfruit is Christ, our creator, the Rock that begat us; then Abraham, Isaac and Jacob as well. “The lump is also holy”: the lump is Israel. “And if the root be holy”: the root is Christ. “So are the branches”: the branches are us, the offspring, the children.
- “For if the firstfruit be holy, the lump is also holy”: if Christ is holy, he’s the firstfruit, and the lump is also holy. “And if the root be holy, so are the branches.”

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Corinthians 15:23](/bible/1-corinthians/15#v23)**
    > <sup>[23](/bible/1-corinthians/15#v23)</sup> But every man in his own order: Christ the firstfruits; afterward they that are Christ’s at his coming.

    “But every man in his own order: Christ the firstfruits; afterward they that are Christ’s at his coming”: the elect. So the firstfruit of Romans 11 is Christ.
  - **[John 15:1](/bible/john/15#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/john/15#v1)</sup> I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.

    “I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.” Christ is the vine, the root.

**[Romans 11:17](/bible/romans/11#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/romans/11#v17)</sup> And if some of the branches be broken off, and thou, being a wild olive tree, wert graffed in among them, and with them partakest of the root and fatness of the olive tree;

- “And if some of the branches be broken off”: why does it say olive tree? Because the Lord called Israel a green olive tree, and the branches of it are broken (Jeremiah 11:16-17). “And thou, being a wild olive tree”: the wild olive tree was Israel, meaning the northern kingdom. “Wert graffed in among them”. This is saying the same thing Ezekiel was saying. This ain’t about Esau being grafted in, or the Chinese being grafted in. It is Israel, the two kingdoms.
- “And with them partakest of the root and fatness of the olive tree”: the root is Christ, and the fatness is the blessings of the olive tree. God called us an olive tree.
- “And if some of the branches be broken off”: that is Judah broken off. “and thou, being a wild olive tree, wert graffed in among them, and with them partakest of the root and fatness of the olive tree”: the grafting in is the joining of the two sticks in Ezekiel 37.

  Precepts:
  - **[Jeremiah 11:16-17](/bible/jeremiah/11#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/jeremiah/11#v16)</sup> The Lord called thy name, A green olive tree, fair, and of goodly fruit: with the noise of a great tumult he hath kindled fire upon it, and the branches of it are broken.
    >
    > <sup>[17](/bible/jeremiah/11#v17)</sup> For the Lord of hosts, that planted thee, hath pronounced evil against thee, for the evil of the house of Israel and of the house of Judah, which they have done against themselves to provoke me to anger in offering incense unto Baal.

    Why does it say olive tree? “The Lord called thy name, A green olive tree, fair, and of goodly fruit: with the noise of a great tumult he hath kindled fire upon it, and the branches of it are broken.” God called Israel an olive tree, and the branches of it are broken.
  - **[Jeremiah 11:16](/bible/jeremiah/11#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/jeremiah/11#v16)</sup> The Lord called thy name, A green olive tree, fair, and of goodly fruit: with the noise of a great tumult he hath kindled fire upon it, and the branches of it are broken.

    “The Lord called thy name, A green olive tree, fair, and of goodly fruit: with the noise of a great tumult he hath kindled fire upon it, and the branches of it are broken.”

**[Romans 11:18-21](/bible/romans/11#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/romans/11#v18)</sup> Boast not against the branches. But if thou boast, thou bearest not the root, but the root thee.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/romans/11#v19)</sup> Thou wilt say then, The branches were broken off, that I might be graffed in.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/romans/11#v20)</sup> Well; because of unbelief they were broken off, and thou standest by faith. Be not highminded, but fear:
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/romans/11#v21)</sup> For if God spared not the natural branches, take heed lest he also spare not thee.

- “Boast not against the branches”: Paul is talking to the northern kingdom, who accepted Christ, not to boast against the southern kingdom. “But if thou boast, thou bearest not the root, but the root thee”: if you boast against your brothers, you don’t follow Christ; Christ bears you. “Thou wilt say then, The branches were broken off, that I might be graffed in”: Judah fell off so I could come in, so what? We can replace them. Paul says don’t do that: those are your brothers. That is why the class started with love thy neighbour as thyself. They called Christ a Samaritan as a derogatory term; the hatred was around.
- “Boast not against the branches.” He’s telling the Israelites of the northern kingdom: brothers, don’t boast against the branches. “But if thou boast, thou bearest not the root, but the root thee.” You don’t bear Christ; Christ, the root, is the one that allows you to be grafted back into this truth.
- “Boast not against the branches”: don't boast against Judah. “But if thou boast, thou bearest not the root, but the root thee”: the root is Christ. You are not bearing Christ; Christ is the one who keeps the northern kingdom grafted in with Judah.

  Precepts:
  - **[Zechariah 12:7](/bible/zechariah/12#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/zechariah/12#v7)</sup> The Lord also shall save the tents of Judah first, that the glory of the house of David and the glory of the inhabitants of Jerusalem do not magnify themselves against Judah.

    Where did we read don't boast and don't be highminded? In Zechariah 12:7: “that the glory of the house of David and the glory of the inhabitants of Jerusalem do not magnify themselves against Judah.” That the northern kingdom do not magnify themselves against Judah.

**[Romans 11:22](/bible/romans/11#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/romans/11#v22)</sup> Behold therefore the goodness and severity of God: on them which fell, severity; but toward thee, goodness, if thou continue in his goodness: otherwise thou also shalt be cut off.

- “Behold therefore the goodness and severity of God: on them which fell, severity”. He was severe with Judah. Remember what Paul said: since you put it from you, we turn to the Gentiles. He turned to the northern kingdom. “But toward thee, goodness, if thou continue in his goodness”. What is God’s goodness? The law and the commandment (Romans 7:12). So continuing in his goodness is continuing in the commandments. “Otherwise thou also shalt be cut off.”
- “Behold therefore the goodness and severity of God”. The goodness is Christ dying for them; the severity is them stumbling, falling, shrouded in spiritual darkness, their minds blinded. “On them which fell, severity”: the southern kingdom. “But toward thee, goodness”: the northern kingdom, grafted back in, reconciled through Christ’s death. “If thou continue in his goodness”, that root, Christ: “otherwise thou also shalt be cut off”, as they were cast off in their disobedience.

  Precepts:
  - **[Romans 7:12](/bible/romans/7#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/romans/7#v12)</sup> Wherefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good.

    What is God’s goodness, brothers? “Wherefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good.” The commandment is good. So when Romans 11:22 says “if thou continue in his goodness”, that goodness is continuing in the commandments; otherwise thou also shalt be cut off.

**[Romans 11:23](/bible/romans/11#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/romans/11#v23)</sup> And they also, if they abide not still in unbelief, shall be graffed in: for God is able to graff them in again.

- “And they also”: they also of Judah, “if they abide not still in unbelief”, if they repent and get themselves together, “shall be graffed in: for God is able to graff them in again.” The key word is again. This ain’t talking about the Philistines and the Jebusites; they were never in this.
- “And they also, if they abide not still in unbelief, shall be graffed in: for God is able to graff them in again.” Again: they were always there. Grafted in again goes back to reconcile.

**[Romans 11:24](/bible/romans/11#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/romans/11#v24)</sup> For if thou wert cut out of the olive tree which is wild by nature, and wert graffed contrary to nature into a good olive tree: how much more shall these, which be the natural branches, be graffed into their own olive tree?

- “For if thou wert cut out of the olive tree which is wild by nature”: all Israel is called the olive tree. Wild by nature means we were wicked as hell, sinful, into idolatry, breaking all God’s laws. “And wert graffed contrary to nature into a good olive tree”: through Christ they got a chance to repent and get themselves right. “How much more shall these, which be the natural branches”: how much more Judah, which be the natural branches, “be graffed into their own olive tree?” All Israel’s coming back together.
- “For if thou wert cut out of the olive tree which is wild by nature”: the northern kingdom, “and wert graffed contrary to nature into a good olive tree”: contrary to nature because they were uncircumcised, in uncleanness, scattered abroad far off. “How much more shall these, which be the natural branches”: the southern kingdom, “be graffed into their own olive tree?” Judah was closer to the Lord, so when they fall off they can come right back.
