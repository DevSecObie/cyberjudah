## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Romans 5 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1, 3-6, 8-21, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Romans 5:1](/bible/romans/5#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/romans/5#v1)</sup> Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:

- “Therefore being justified by faith”. Romans 5:1 continues from Romans 4:25, just as Romans 11 continues from the last two verses of Romans 10. Paul did not stop and start a new topic. The chapters and verses were added later, to find things faster; it was originally one long letter. That is why he begins with therefore.
- Who, according to the Old Testament, would be justified? Israel. All the seed of Israel shall be justified; my righteous servant shall justify many, by dying for them, by laying on himself the iniquity of them all. Through the death of Christ we are justified by faith, not by animal sacrifice anymore.
- “we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ”: through his body, not animals. What would come to pass, Daniel 9:24-26, is that the temple would be destroyed, so we could not atone with animals anymore. We had to have faith in Christ; otherwise you condemn yourself, indebted to a law you can no longer keep, with the priests scattered abroad and the temple destroyed with not one stone left standing.

  Precepts:
  - **[Isaiah 53:5](/bible/isaiah/53#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/53#v5)</sup> But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.

    Isaiah 53 already said it: “But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities”. You know who the our is now: Israel. “the chastisement of our peace was upon him”: the chastisement on him was our peace with the Father. “and with his stripes we are healed”: healed from our iniquities.

**[Romans 5:3](/bible/romans/5#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/romans/5#v3)</sup> And not only so, but we glory in tribulations also: knowing that tribulation worketh patience;

- The third choice: you can let that bad situation make you durable. Durable means able to withstand wear, pressure or damage: hard-wearing, long-lasting, heavy duty, tough, resistant, strong. A bad situation can make you strong. A bad situation can make you long-lasting. A bad situation can make you durable.
- “And not only so, but we glory in tribulations also”: that is the spiritual man and the spiritual woman. It sounds almost crazy: who rejoices in tribulation? That is the afterthought. Once you sit back and the smoke clears, now you can rejoice: I thought I'd never get through that thing.
- “Knowing that tribulation worketh patience”: only the spiritual man and woman know that tribulation works patience. It is teaching you something. God is trying to teach us all patience.
- “We glory in tribulations also”. Brothers, are we on that level yet? We have to get to it. “Knowing that tribulation worketh patience”. All these spiritual level-ups come from suffering, adversity and tribulation. You don’t level up without going through it. Brothers say, I’m trying to grow in the spirit; from your mouth to God’s ears: oh, you want to grow? I got the perfect recipe.

It sure ain't joyous. “But grievous”: grievous as hell. You lose your house, your health, your mother, your father, your son, your daughter, your job: it is very grievous.

**[Romans 5:4](/bible/romans/5#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/romans/5#v4)</sup> And patience, experience; and experience, hope:

- “And patience, experience”: patience gives us experience, because a lot of us lack experience in this walk. Sure, you have experience in the world, but that is not what God is talking about; he is talking about your experiences in this truth. “And experience, hope”: experience will teach you and give you hope.
- “And patience, experience”. Now when somebody else is going through it, you can give them counsel. Each one can teach one, and you’re not a hypocrite, because you actually went through it. You took your licks, took your stripes, went through the low. You went through a divorce and you stayed in this truth, ten toes down.
- “And experience, hope”. What do you need hope for? Because there is more suffering to come; we ain’t seen nothing yet. So you build up your hope, which is faith.
- “And patience, experience”. With patience in giving the instructions and repeating them, a parent learns his children: give them two or three examples and see which one they cling to, and that becomes how they learn, the same way the parent learns. You can't just tell them this, this and this and expect them to perform it; you walk them through it two or three times until they put their hands on it and it becomes second nature. “And experience, hope”.

> <sup>[5](/bible/romans/5#v5)</sup> And hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us.

- “And hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us.”

It is Proverbs 10:22 again: the blessing of the Lord adds to you and doesn't make you ashamed or take anything away. It gives you the experiences and the affirmations you're looking for even while you go through hard things.

- “But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners”, in sin and darkness, “Christ died for us.”

**[Romans 5:9-10](/bible/romans/5#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/romans/5#v9)</sup> Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/romans/5#v10)</sup> For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.

- “Much more then, being now justified by his blood”. What replaced the animals? His blood. The animals are the works we no longer receive justification from; by his blood we receive justification, atonement, reconciliation.
- “we shall be saved from wrath through him.” The wrath is death, and we are saved from it through him, through his death.
- “For if, when we were enemies”: Israelites were the enemies, “we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son”. When we were enemies to God, we were reconciled back to the Father through the death of his Son.
- What does reconcile mean? To bring back together again. Emphasize the again. Ephesians 2:14-16 says he reconciles both unto God: the both are the southern and northern kingdoms. Was there a time in the Old Testament when all nations were with God together with Israel? No. God had one chosen people. Show where God had a bunch of nations that were his chosen people: never. So how could they be reconciled to him? They were not with him in the first place. Reconciliation does not fit other nations; that word destroys Christianity.

**[Romans 5:11](/bible/romans/5#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/romans/5#v11)</sup> And not only so, but we also joy in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom we have now received the atonement.

- “By whom we have now received the atonement.” Through Jesus Christ we have received the atonement. Jesus Christ is our atonement. The lamb that used to be killed for the atonement, that is Christ. Atonement means to make amends: he is the one who makes amends for us to God, through his blood and through his body. That is how we make amends to God today.
- “And not only so, but we also joy in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom we have now received the atonement.” Some Bibles have a word next to atonement: reconciliation. Reconcile in verse 10 and atonement in verse 11 are the same word. Christ died on the cross and atoned for us, and by dying for us he brought us back to the Father. That is also called adoption.
- Atonement is reparation for a wrong or injury: when Christ died on the cross, he died for our wrongs and our injuries. The Christian theology sense, the reconciliation of God and all humankind, is nonsense, because God was never with all humankind. The word itself is at-one-ment. Who did God come to bring together as one? Israel, both kingdoms. It is joining the two sticks together: unity, unifying both kingdoms. All nations were never one with Israel. Ever.
- Reconciliation is the restoration of a friendly relationship: reuniting, reunion, reconcilement, and propitiation, which is in the Bible too. It is full of peace: that is Isaiah 53 and Romans 5:1.

> <sup>[12](/bible/romans/5#v12)</sup> Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:

- “Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned”. The one man is Adam, the first man. Because of Adam’s sin, death came upon all of us, and that is why we need an atonement.
- “Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world”: Adam, “and death by sin”. Sin and death are synonymous, because the wages of sin is death; they go hand in hand. “and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned”.
- That is where the Christians start: all have sinned. Who is the all? You read it earlier in Romans 3. Yes, Adam brought sin upon all men, and all nations are capable of sinning. Death did pass upon all men. The question is who God held accountable for the sins they committed.
- What Paul is doing in Romans 5 is giving the origin of sin: how sin came into the world, and how Christ came after that to repair us and give us life.

**[Romans 5:13](/bible/romans/5#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/romans/5#v13)</sup> (For until the law sin was in the world: but sin is not imputed when there is no law.

- “For until the law sin was in the world”: until the law of sacrifice under Moses, sin was in the world, because Adam brought it into the world. “but sin is not imputed when there is no law.” Imputed means charged, acknowledged. Sin is not imputed when there is no sacrifice. There always had to be a sacrifice of some kind to impute sin upon someone; sacrifice and death always went hand in hand.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hebrews 9:22](/bible/hebrews/9#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/hebrews/9#v22)</sup> And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.

    What does “sin is not imputed when there is no law” mean? “And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.” Why almost all things? Because there were sins no animal could atone for. Almost all things are purged with blood by the law of sacrifice, and without the shedding of blood there is no remission, no atonement. So sin is not imputed where there is no sacrifice.
  - **[Hebrews 10:18](/bible/hebrews/10#v18)**
    > <sup>[18](/bible/hebrews/10#v18)</sup> Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin.

    “Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin.” When Christ died, there was no more offering for sin: no more animals, no more sacrifices.
  - **[Romans 4:15](/bible/romans/4#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/romans/4#v15)</sup> Because the law worketh wrath: for where no law is, there is no transgression.

    Romans 4:15 says what Romans 5:13 says. “Because the law worketh wrath”: another word for wrath is death. The law of sacrifice works death, because we are sinful by nature. “for where no law is, there is no transgression.” Where there is no sacrifice, there is no death, no sin imputed. Sacrifice and death always went hand in hand; that is why under Moses you died without mercy under two or three witnesses.

**[Romans 5:14](/bible/romans/5#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/romans/5#v14)</sup> Nevertheless death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over them that had not sinned after the similitude of Adam’s transgression, who is the figure of him that was to come.

- “Nevertheless death reigned from Adam to Moses”: the law here is Moses. “even over them that had not sinned after the similitude of Adam’s transgression”: everybody did not sin the same way Adam sinned, but they sinned regardless. “who is the figure of him that was to come.” Adam was a similitude of Christ.

**[Romans 5:15](/bible/romans/5#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/romans/5#v15)</sup> But not as the offence, so also is the free gift. For if through the offence of one many be dead, much more the grace of God, and the gift by grace, which is by one man, Jesus Christ, hath abounded unto many.

- “But not as the offence, so also is the free gift.” Adam was like a Christ figure in the beginning, but Adam brought in the offence; Christ did not, Christ brought the opposite. Christ is the free gift.
- “For if through the offence of one many be dead”: Adam brought death and destruction; paradise was removed from the earth, then the flood came. “much more the grace of God, and the gift by grace, which is by one man, Jesus Christ, hath abounded unto many.” The same way death abounded through Adam and spread, Christ’s death abounds and brings forth life. They parallel each other: one brought death upon the righteous chosen nation, and one brought life upon the chosen nation.

**[Romans 5:16](/bible/romans/5#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/romans/5#v16)</sup> And not as it was by one that sinned, so is the gift: for the judgment was by one to condemnation, but the free gift is of many offences unto justification.

- “And not as it was by one that sinned, so is the gift: for the judgment was by one to condemnation”: Adam brought condemnation, sin and death. “but the free gift is of many offences unto justification.” Whose offences? Israel’s. My righteous servant shall justify many; all the seed of Israel shall be justified.
- Paul is giving the rundown of how sin came into the world and killed off the chosen people in the beginning, how they always came back to keep the commandments of God, and how Christ eventually came to die for the sins of that same chosen line. It is the origin story of sin, and why Christ had to come into the world and die for his people.

  Precepts:
  - **[Romans 4:25](/bible/romans/4#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/romans/4#v25)</sup> Who was delivered for our offences, and was raised again for our justification.

    “the free gift is of many offences unto justification” goes back to Romans 4:25: “Who was delivered for our offences, and was raised again for our justification.” Who is the our? Israel. Who are the many? Israel. Our and many are exactly us.

**[Romans 5:17](/bible/romans/5#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/romans/5#v17)</sup> For if by one man’s offence death reigned by one; much more they which receive abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness shall reign in life by one, Jesus Christ.)

- “For if by one man’s offence death reigned by one; much more they which receive abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness shall reign in life by one, Jesus Christ.” The same way Adam brought death and it reigned in many, Christ died on the cross and life reigned in many. It is the polar opposite.

Who are the all men there? Israel, the same people Isaiah said would be justified.

- “For as by one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous.” When Adam sinned he brought sin into all his children. All nations come out of Adam, and some of them had knowledge of the commandments of God. But whose sins did Christ die for? Who received God’s commandments? Who got the covenant? Israel.
- Yes, all nations have sinned, and God does not like it. But he deals with a specific people, and he gave them specific laws. That is who Paul is talking to.

**[Romans 5:20](/bible/romans/5#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/romans/5#v20)</sup> Moreover the law entered, that the offence might abound. But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound:

- “Moreover the law entered, that the offence might abound.” The law was introduced so sin could be understood: thou shalt not covet, thou shalt not steal, thou shalt not commit adultery. We are by nature a wicked people, so the law entered to guide us, to expose and reveal the sin in us so we can fight it off.
- “But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound”. When we keep God’s commandments and laws, we realize how much we need grace, because of the evil that is in us. We are going to make mistakes; we will rise, fall and stumble. So we need more grace than we got under Moses, because animals cannot atone for everything. Christ had to replace them.

**[Romans 5:21](/bible/romans/5#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/romans/5#v21)</sup> That as sin hath reigned unto death, even so might grace reign through righteousness unto eternal life by Jesus Christ our Lord.

- “That as sin hath reigned unto death, even so might grace reign through righteousness unto eternal life by Jesus Christ our Lord.” Grace is mercy: the mercy there was none of under Moses, when he that despised Moses’ law died without mercy.
