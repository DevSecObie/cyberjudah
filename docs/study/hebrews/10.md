## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Hebrews 10 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-7, 10-14, 20, 22-25, 30, 32-39, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Hebrews 10:1](/bible/hebrews/10#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/hebrews/10#v1)</sup> For the law having a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with those sacrifices which they offered year by year continually make the comers thereunto perfect.

- “For the law having a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with those sacrifices which they offered year by year continually make the comers thereunto perfect.” The same as Hebrews 9:9: those sacrifices could not make him that did the service perfect.

**[Hebrews 10:2-3](/bible/hebrews/10#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/hebrews/10#v2)</sup> For then would they not have ceased to be offered? because that the worshippers once purged should have had no more conscience of sins.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/hebrews/10#v3)</sup> But in those sacrifices there is a remembrance again made of sins every year.

- “For then would they not have ceased to be offered? because that the worshippers once purged should have had no more conscience of sins.” If sacrifice made you perfect, you would not have to offer it again and again. It was temporary.
- “But in those sacrifices”, the law spoken of in verse 1, the meat offerings, drink offerings, purifications, temple law, “there is a remembrance again made of sins every year”: every year, the atonement.

**[Hebrews 10:4-7](/bible/hebrews/10#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/hebrews/10#v4)</sup> For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/hebrews/10#v5)</sup> Wherefore when he cometh into the world, he saith, Sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not, but a body hast thou prepared me:
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/hebrews/10#v6)</sup> In burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin thou hast had no pleasure.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/hebrews/10#v7)</sup> Then said I, Lo, I come (in the volume of the book it is written of me,) to do thy will, O God.

- “For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins.” “Sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not”: the Most High had no care for sacrificing. It was a schoolmaster, a stepping stone leading us to Christ, a practice run to see how we would do before Christ showed up. What he cared about was obedience. Samuel and Judith say the same: the Most High does not care about sacrifices, he cares about obedience.
- “But a body hast thou prepared”: that is Christ’s body. “In burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin thou hast had no pleasure. Then said I, Lo, I come (in the volume of the book it is written of me,) to do thy will, O God.”
- “Wherefore when he cometh into the world, he saith, Sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not, but a body hast thou prepared me”. That’s what to deal with: “when he cometh into the world.” Who did Christ come into the world for, and whose sin did he take away? The prevailing thought is that Jesus came for everybody. That’s the doctrine that’s been preached and pushed throughout the earth, and the scriptures say otherwise.

  Precepts:
  - **[John 1:10-11](/bible/john/1#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/john/1#v10)</sup> He was in the world, and the world was made by him, and the world knew him not.
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/john/1#v11)</sup> He came unto his own, and his own received him not.

    When he cometh into the world: “He was in the world, and the world was made by him, and the world knew him not. He came unto his own, and his own received him not.”
  - **[Mark 8:31](/bible/mark/8#v31)**
    > <sup>[31](/bible/mark/8#v31)</sup> And he began to teach them, that the Son of man must suffer many things, and be rejected of the elders, and of the chief priests, and scribes, and be killed, and after three days rise again.

    “He came unto his own, and his own received him not”: what is that? “And he began to teach them, that the Son of man must suffer many things, and be rejected of the elders, and of the chief priests, and scribes, and be killed, and after three days rise again.” Rejected of who? Of the elders, the chief priests and the scribes. It was the leaders that rejected him, not everybody.
  - **[John 1:29](/bible/john/1#v29)**
    > <sup>[29](/bible/john/1#v29)</sup> The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.

    “The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.” Behold what? The Lamb of God. Which taketh away the sin of the world: the sin of those who God called to sacrifice, to make it plain.

**[Hebrews 10:10](/bible/hebrews/10#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/hebrews/10#v10)</sup> By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.

- “By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all”: when he entered into the temple in the heavens.

**[Hebrews 10:11-12](/bible/hebrews/10#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/hebrews/10#v11)</sup> And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins:
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/hebrews/10#v12)</sup> But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;

- “And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins”. Daily, every day. The Day of Atonement was year by year, and sacrifices were offered every day, because Israel was sinful every day.
- “But this man”, Christ, “after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever”, himself, “sat down on the right hand of God”. He did not stand in the corner; he is not sitting on the Father’s kneecap or his shoulders. He sat down in his own seat on the right hand of God: two thrones, not one.

**[Hebrews 10:13](/bible/hebrews/10#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/hebrews/10#v13)</sup> From henceforth expecting till his enemies be made his footstool.

- “From henceforth expecting till his enemies be made his footstool.” He is sitting there expecting, waiting, so it is time to take us home and get us out of here. Come on, keep the commandments, so I can come down there, kill people and get you out of there. He is sitting there tapping his feet: y’all keep messing up.

**[Hebrews 10:14](/bible/hebrews/10#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/hebrews/10#v14)</sup> For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.

- “For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.” Perfected: completed. We believe in Christ, so we are no longer required to offer up animals. We keep the feast days, but no longer with the sacrificing of animals. We keep the Day of Atonement, but we do not need to offer an animal any more, because Christ did away with that.
- Christianity says Christ did away with thou shalt not kill and thou shalt not steal. How insane that sounds: steal all you want, lie all you want, rape and kill and pillage, just have your heart in Christ, and he will forgive you; believe while you steal and lie and fornicate. That is the church. The Christian church is garbage.

**[Hebrews 10:20](/bible/hebrews/10#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/hebrews/10#v20)</sup> By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh;

- What makes it all more challenging is the veil of flesh. Even with everything we see and know, it is like looking through a veil. Think of a wedding veil: it is see-through, but not clearly. Put the camera behind it, as some movies do, and you can make out the outlines of things but not see them. That is what it is like to be in this flesh, and you have to understand the reality of why we dwell inside the mortal flesh.
- “By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh.” Christ's flesh is called a veil. Our bodies are the same: they almost keep us from seeing anything spiritual clearly. So what we can see must be treated as that much more precious, and that is why it is even more important to nourish our spirits daily. That veil is the flesh.

**[Hebrews 10:22](/bible/hebrews/10#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/hebrews/10#v22)</sup> Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.

- “Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith”. Draw near to God with a true heart and full assurance of faith. Don’t half step it: either you believe it or you don’t. With a true heart, not with dissimulation.
- “Having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience”: the evilness we’re putting behind us and pressing toward the righteousness. Stay to your own mind long enough, stop being around when there’s opportunity to fellowship, and your evil conscience is going to take over, and you’re going to be destroyed even though you see the day approaching. That’s the scariest thing: knowing the end, seeing all of the prophecies come to pass, but you cannot get over the hump because you didn’t let go of that evil conscience.
- “And our bodies washed with pure water.”

**[Hebrews 10:23](/bible/hebrews/10#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/hebrews/10#v23)</sup> Let us hold fast the profession of our faith without wavering; (for he is faithful that promised;)

- “Let us hold fast the profession of our faith without wavering”. You wouldn’t even consider wavering if everything was just cool. It’s saying without wavering, understanding the opposition, the adversity, the perils that we’re going to be going through and are going through right now. “For he is faithful that promised.”

**[Hebrews 10:24-25](/bible/hebrews/10#v24)**

> <sup>[24](/bible/hebrews/10#v24)</sup> And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works:
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/hebrews/10#v25)</sup> Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching.

- “And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together.” It is speaking of coming together, congregating. Any time you are able to come together, you should be trying to come together. Some of us let Satan mess with us through an introverted approach to life, and we only see you here. You don't have to be bubbly and around everybody all the time, but there should be somebody you have a relationship with and see during the week, some function, a mission, a fundraiser, something. When you forsake the assembling, your spirit weakens. Those who separate themselves weaken themselves.
- “And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works”. Like in 2 Maccabees, we got to keep each other in prayer, consider one another, provoke each other to good works, so we can build our faith and our belief to stand in the times of trouble.
- We deal with strangers better than we deal with anybody else. Some brothers sit there while an apologist runs up and talks trash, berates them and mocks them, and they just take it with the phone recording. On the street people are disrespectful and we take it; we find a way to restrain ourselves. Esau keeps walking and we turn our shoulder: you don’t shoulder bump that damn Edomite to the wall. But let your wife forget to do something, and at home you are ready for war, ready to cuss her out, tell her she ain’t ish. A sister comes up and you say get away from me, I got to sit in the room and play a game and doom scroll for two hours before I talk to you. Something ain’t right when you treat a stranger better than the person you exchange fluids with.
- “And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works.” In the congregation we do that: we provoke brothers to love and good works, to keeping the commandments, and when they suffer loss we provoke them to love and good works. Then we go home and provoke our wives to nothing but being angry at us.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hebrews 3:13](/bible/hebrews/3#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/hebrews/3#v13)</sup> But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.

Some get their rest, say I’m out, are gone eight, nine, ten hours, come back, shower, change clothes and go to class, and say what’s up, and that’s it. That’s the relationship.

Remember where judgment starts first.

That time is related to us today. “Whilst ye became companions of them that were so used”: you became friends and brethren of those used like you are being used.

**[Hebrews 10:34](/bible/hebrews/10#v34)**

> <sup>[34](/bible/hebrews/10#v34)</sup> For ye had compassion of me in my bonds, and took joyfully the spoiling of your goods, knowing in yourselves that ye have in heaven a better and an enduring substance.

- “For ye had compassion of me in my bonds, and took joyfully the spoiling of your goods.” Paul is talking to the believers: you had compassion on me when I was arrested, and you took cheerfully the spoiling of your goods. Their homes were raided; everything they owned was taken from them.
- “Knowing in yourselves that ye have in heaven a better and an enduring substance.” That is the level of faith we all have to aspire to: to get to where we can rejoice when that happens to us.
- “For ye had compassion of me in my bonds”: when I was in prison. “And took joyfully the spoiling of your goods”: the robbing of your homes and houses. There was a penalty for speaking against Rome; their homes were robbed and taken. We read right by it without looking into it. It didn't faze them: we are going to get it all back a hundredfold.
- “Knowing in yourselves that ye have in heaven a better and an enduring substance.” That heaven is not pie in the sky; it is going to be on earth. That is the Lord's prayer: thy kingdom come, thy will be done in earth as it is in heaven.

We say the same thing he said, and today there are laws on the books where speaking against Christianity or America falls under extremism.

And the husband has to say, babe, we are going to get all this back and more. Losing it would mess with your faith. It “hath great recompence of reward.”

We can’t cast it away by no means, no matter what we are going through. It is designed to teach you; it has purpose behind it. When the smoke clears, you get an increase in the spirit.
