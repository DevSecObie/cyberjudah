## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Hebrews 9 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-18, 22-26, 28, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Hebrews 9:1-5](/bible/hebrews/9#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/hebrews/9#v1)</sup> Then verily the first covenant had also ordinances of divine service, and a worldly sanctuary.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/hebrews/9#v2)</sup> For there was a tabernacle made; the first, wherein was the candlestick, and the table, and the shewbread; which is called the sanctuary.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/hebrews/9#v3)</sup> And after the second veil, the tabernacle which is called the Holiest of all;

> <sup>[4](/bible/hebrews/9#v4)</sup> Which had the golden censer, and the ark of the covenant overlaid round about with gold, wherein was the golden pot that had manna, and Aaron’s rod that budded, and the tables of the covenant;
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/hebrews/9#v5)</sup> And over it the cherubims of glory shadowing the mercyseat; of which we cannot now speak particularly.

- “The first covenant had also ordinances of divine service, and a worldly sanctuary”: a sanctuary made by man. “There was a tabernacle made; the first, wherein was the candlestick, and the table, and the shewbread; which is called the sanctuary.”
- “Then verily the first covenant had also ordinances of divine service”: the first covenant is temple law. “And a worldly sanctuary”: a building on the earth, a temple. “For there was a tabernacle made; the first, wherein was the candlestick, and the table, and the shewbread; which is called the sanctuary”, or the temple.
- “The first covenant had also ordinances of divine service”, divine service by the priests and high priests, “and a worldly sanctuary”, worldly because it was on earth.
- Let’s read about the ordinances. “The first covenant had also ordinances of divine service, and a worldly sanctuary”: the same ordinances we were reading about in Ephesians 2. The divine service was the different hand washings, the different ordinances concerning sacrificing.

**[Hebrews 9:6-7](/bible/hebrews/9#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/hebrews/9#v6)</sup> Now when these things were thus ordained, the priests went always into the first tabernacle, accomplishing the service of God.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/hebrews/9#v7)</sup> But into the second went the high priest alone once every year, not without blood, which he offered for himself, and for the errors of the people:

- “The priests went always into the first tabernacle, accomplishing the service of God. But into the second went the high priest alone once every year”. The regular priests of the Levitical line could not go into the second tabernacle; only the high priest, Aaron and his sons, the high priests’ lineage. Christ did it once in the heavens when he died for us; on earth the high priest had to do it once every year.
- “The priests went always into the first tabernacle, accomplishing the service of God. But into the second went the high priest alone once every year, not without blood, which he offered for himself, and for the errors of the people”. That is the holiest of holies where the priest went in.
- “The priests went always into the first tabernacle, accomplishing the service of God. But into the second went the high priest alone”. The second part of the temple is the holy of holies, the holiest portion of the temple, and only the high priest was allowed to enter it.
- “Once every year”: that is the atonement of Leviticus 16 and Exodus 30:10, once every year. “Not without blood”: the blood of sin offerings. “Which he offered for himself”, for his own sins, because these high priests were sinful too, “and for the errors of the people”.

**[Hebrews 9:8](/bible/hebrews/9#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/hebrews/9#v8)</sup> The Holy Ghost this signifying, that the way into the holiest of all was not yet made manifest, while as the first tabernacle was yet standing:

- “The Holy Ghost this signifying, that the way into the holiest of all was not yet made manifest”: the way to the holiest of all is the way to God, because God dwelt in the holiest of holies, and the way was not yet manifested.
- What were they doing when they were sacrificing? They were rehearsing a righteous act, rehearsing what it meant to have a connection with God, so they would understand that there had to be a blood sacrifice for salvation. The Jews thought, because they had the blood sacrifice, that they were holier than the rest of the people. Christ said no: I’m going to come and die that perfect sacrifice to gather in one all the nation of Israel. That enmity, that pride the southern kingdom had starting with Judah, was that they were better, and God said no, you’re not: all of you have fallen, and through my grace all of you are going to be redeemed. We will go back into the Old Testament to understand why they thought like that.
- “While as the first tabernacle was yet standing”: Christ, his blood and his dying for us, was not made manifest while the first tabernacle was standing. Even though Christ was crucified, resurrected and went back to the Father, that first tabernacle was still standing and people were still sacrificing. God was giving them a dispensation of time to be fully persuaded on him, and then it was going to be destroyed. Paul was preparing the Hebrews: get ready, this tabernacle is going to be destroyed, 70 A.D. When this falls you better be fully persuaded that Christ is the way, he is the door, he’s the way to God. The northern kingdom readily accepted that; the southern kingdom, many of them, had a problem with it. They didn’t believe the prophecies; even though they knew the laws, it didn’t connect.
- “The Holy Ghost this signifying, that the way into the holiest of all was not yet made manifest, while as the first tabernacle was yet standing.” While the first tabernacle was yet standing, the way into the holiest of all was not yet made manifest.

**[Hebrews 9:9-10](/bible/hebrews/9#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/hebrews/9#v9)</sup> Which was a figure for the time then present, in which were offered both gifts and sacrifices, that could not make him that did the service perfect, as pertaining to the conscience;
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/hebrews/9#v10)</sup> Which stood only in meats and drinks, and divers washings, and carnal ordinances, imposed on them until the time of reformation.

- “Which was a figure for the time then present, in which were offered both gifts and sacrifices, that could not make him that did the service perfect, as pertaining to the conscience”. The sacrificing of Aaron and his sons did not make them perfect. Offering animals did not make a person perfect, because they were still sinful, so the sacrifices were done daily and once a year: they were sinful all the time. As pertaining to the conscience, the mind, because sin is in our minds all the time.
- “Which stood only in meats and drinks”: meat offerings and drink offerings, “and divers washings, and carnal ordinances, imposed on them until the time of reformation.” The reformation is when Christ set foot on the scene and brought the reform of no longer needing sacrifices.
- “Which was a figure for the time then present”: that first tabernacle was a figure, “in which were offered both gifts and sacrifices, that could not make him that did the service perfect”. The sacrificial law was just rehearsing the righteous acts; it was never going to make them perfect, and this is what they struggled with. “As pertaining to the conscience”: as pertaining to your conscience.
- “Which was a figure for the time then present, in which were offered both gifts and sacrifices, that could not make him that did the service perfect.” The gifts and the sacrifices could not make us perfect, “as pertaining to the conscience.” Isaiah 1 is the example of it: we were bringing sacrifice after sacrifice, and our consciences were not perfect. That is why Christ had to come.

**[Hebrews 9:11-14](/bible/hebrews/9#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/hebrews/9#v11)</sup> But Christ being come an high priest of good things to come, by a greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands, that is to say, not of this building;
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/hebrews/9#v12)</sup> Neither by the blood of goats and calves, but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us.

>

- “Christ being come an high priest of good things to come, by a greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands”, “neither by the blood of goats and calves, but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us.” If the blood of bulls and of goats sanctified to the purifying of the flesh, “how much more shall the blood of Christ”, who “offered himself without spot to God, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?”
- Verse 11 explains the reformation: “But Christ being come an high priest of good things to come, by a greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands, that is to say, not of this building”: not the temple that stood on the ground, on earth.
- “Neither by the blood of goats and calves, but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us.” Verse 7 said once every year; now it is once. When Christ died on the cross, he entered into the holy place in the heavens. The tabernacle, which was a tent in Moses’ time, and the building Solomon built on David’s blueprint, were a model, a figure, of the temple in the heavens that Christ entered for us one time, to be our atonement once and for all. By his own blood he removed the blood of goats and calves: that sacrificing is done away with.
- “For if the blood of bulls and of goats, and the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean, sanctifieth to the purifying of the flesh”. The ashes of an heifer were a cleansing agent, like a bleach, put in the water to purify the people. Sprinkling the unclean: baptisms.

**[Hebrews 9:15](/bible/hebrews/9#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/hebrews/9#v15)</sup> And for this cause he is the mediator of the new testament, that by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions that were under the first testament, they which are called might receive the promise of eternal inheritance.

- “And for this cause he is the mediator of the new testament”: the New Testament is the same thing as the New Covenant; in certain translations the word covenant replaces testament. “That by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions that were under the first testament”: the death of the testator brought in the redemption. We read that in Acts 5:29-31 and Matthew 1:21: he shall save his people from their sins. “They which are called might receive the promise of eternal inheritance”: only might, because being called doesn’t mean you’re chosen.
- “And for this cause he is the mediator of the new testament”, the new agreement, “that by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions that were under the first testament”. Who was under the first testament? All twelve tribes of Israel. He died for all of them. Notice Paul has to write this to the Hebrews to get them on page.
- “They which are called might receive the promise of eternal inheritance”: whoever was under the first testament, if they were called, would receive the promise of eternal inheritance. It doesn’t matter who was called, northern or southern kingdom; they would have the opportunity, with him being the high priest.
- “And for this cause he is the mediator of the new testament”: all praise to the Most High, Christ is the mediator of the New Testament. “That by means of death”: him dying for the lost sheep of the house of Israel; not for the Jews only, but for the northern kingdom, the nation of Israel also.

**[Hebrews 9:22](/bible/hebrews/9#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/hebrews/9#v22)</sup> And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.

- “And almost all things are by the law purged with blood”: by the law of sacrifice, the blood of animals. That is the law Paul references all the time in his letters as being done away with.
- Why almost all things? Because under the old covenant certain sins could not be purged. Homosexuality: off with your head. Breaking the Sabbath: off with your head. Choosing not to purify yourself after being unclean: off with your head. Witchcraft: off with your head. No atonement for that, no animal for you, no scapegoat for you: just death.
- “And without shedding of blood is no remission”: without the blood of animals there was no forgiveness, no atonement, no justification.

**[Hebrews 9:23-24](/bible/hebrews/9#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/hebrews/9#v23)</sup> It was therefore necessary that the patterns of things in the heavens should be purified with these; but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/hebrews/9#v24)</sup> For Christ is not entered into the holy places made with hands, which are the figures of the true; but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us:

- “It was therefore necessary that the patterns of things in the heavens”, the temple, “should be purified with these”, with animals; “but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these.” That is Christ.
- “For Christ is not entered into the holy places made with hands, which are the figures of the true”: the holy of holies on earth was a figure, a symbol, of the true temple in the heavens. “But into heaven itself”, into the real temple that the temple on earth is modeled after, “now to appear in the presence of God for us.” He presented himself to the Father on our behalf, and he is our advocate, our reconciliation, our eternal atonement.
- “For Christ is not entered into the holy places made with hands”, the tabernacle pictured on the screen, “which are the figures of the true”, symbolic of the true things in heaven, “but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us”.
- The mercy seat with the glory of God at its centre represents Christ: imagine Christ sitting on the throne, with two huge angels stretching out their wings. He is now the new high priest, the mediator who walks into the holy of holies in the heavens on our behalf.

- “As the high priest entereth into the holy place every year with blood of others”: they went into the holy of holies once a year, but Christ only had to do it once. “But now once in the end of the world hath he appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself.”
- “Nor yet that he should offer himself often, as the high priest entereth into the holy place every year with blood of others”: Christ does not have to do that. “For then must he often have suffered since the foundation of the world”: he would have had to die over and over again.
- “But now once in the end of the world”, in the last days, “hath he appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself.” By the sacrifice of himself, atoning for us.
- Christ knew that he had to come and die in order for both nations, both kingdoms, to be united again and brought back into the fold. “For then must he often have suffered since the foundation of the world: but now once in the end of the world hath he appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself.” Christ put away the sins of Israel by sacrificing himself.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Peter 3:18](/bible/1-peter/3#v18)**
    > <sup>[18](/bible/1-peter/3#v18)</sup> For Christ also hath once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust, that he might bring us to God, being put to death in the flesh, but quickened by the Spirit:

    “For Christ also hath once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust, that he might bring us to God”. To bring us, both the southern and northern kingdom, back to the Father through his death.
  - **[Romans 5:10](/bible/romans/5#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/romans/5#v10)</sup> For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.

    “For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.” By the life of Christ.

**[Hebrews 9:28](/bible/hebrews/9#v28)**

> <sup>[28](/bible/hebrews/9#v28)</sup> So Christ was once offered to bear the sins of many; and unto them that look for him shall he appear the second time without sin unto salvation.

- “So Christ was once offered to bear the sins of many”, many of the Israelites. “And unto them that look for him shall he appear the second time without sin unto salvation.” That is exactly what we are waiting for.
