## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Hebrews 2 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1, 10-18, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Hebrews 2:1](/bible/hebrews/2#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/hebrews/2#v1)</sup> Therefore we ought to give the more earnest heed to the things which we have heard, lest at any time we should let them slip.

- “Therefore we ought to give the more earnest heed to the things which we have heard, lest at any time we should let them slip.” Give the more earnest heed to what was heard of our fathers today, and don’t let it slip. Give it up for our forefather Phinehas, and all of our forefathers.
- We have to move as one, not separately, not as individual entities: one mind, one body. Look at what has been done, and think about what is going to be done; we are just getting started. “Therefore we ought to give the more earnest heed to the things which we have heard, lest at any time we should let them slip.”
- This is how we drift. “Therefore we ought to give the more earnest heed to the things which we have heard, lest at any time we should let them slip.” Let them slip is to let them run out as leaking vessels: there is always leakage if you don't refill it. You have to take more earnest heed.
- Hebrews is written to Jews, scribes and Pharisees who knew the Old Testament; it speaks of things without explaining them. If you don't understand the Old Testament, you won't get Hebrews.

**[Hebrews 2:10](/bible/hebrews/2#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/hebrews/2#v10)</sup> For it became him, for whom are all things, and by whom are all things, in bringing many sons unto glory, to make the captain of their salvation perfect through sufferings.

- “For it became him, for whom are all things, and by whom are all things”: that is John 1:3. “In bringing many sons unto glory”: bringing his own children the same glory he had in the beginning, before the world was. “To make the captain of their salvation perfect through sufferings”: he is our captain of salvation.

**[Hebrews 2:11](/bible/hebrews/2#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/hebrews/2#v11)</sup> For both he that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one: for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brethren,

- “For both he that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one”: we are all of the Father, all connected, as in John 17. “For which cause he is not ashamed to call them brethren.” Why does he call his sons brethren? Two reasons. He created us, so he is our father. And he put himself into the loins of David. So he is not only the root of David, the father of David; he is also the offspring of David, the son of David. He created David and came out of David, as Solomon. That is heavy.

**[Hebrews 2:12](/bible/hebrews/2#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/hebrews/2#v12)</sup> Saying, I will declare thy name unto my brethren, in the midst of the church will I sing praise unto thee.

- “I will declare thy name unto my brethren, in the midst of the church will I sing praise unto thee.” His brethren are called the church. That is who Christ is the head of: his brothers, his people. Not the Catholic church or the Pentecostal church.

**[Hebrews 2:13](/bible/hebrews/2#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/hebrews/2#v13)</sup> And again, I will put my trust in him. And again, Behold I and the children which God hath given me.

- “And again, I will put my trust in him. And again, Behold I and the children which God hath given me.” And again, and again: Paul is pulling from different precepts, line upon line, here quoting Isaiah 8:17-18. He calls them sons, then brethren, then children.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 24:18](/bible/sirach/24#v18)**
    > <sup>[18](/bible/sirach/24#v18)</sup> I am the mother of fair love, and fear, and knowledge, and holy hope: I therefore, being eternal, am given to all my children which are named of him.

    I and the children which God hath given me: it was read earlier in Ecclesiasticus. “I therefore, being eternal, am given to all my children which are named of him.” All my children, named of the Father. The Father named them.
  - **[Isaiah 8:17-18](/bible/isaiah/8#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/isaiah/8#v17)</sup> And I will wait upon the Lord, that hideth his face from the house of Jacob, and I will look for him.
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/isaiah/8#v18)</sup> Behold, I and the children whom the Lord hath given me are for signs and for wonders in Israel from the Lord of hosts, which dwelleth in mount Zion.

    Hebrews is quoting what Isaiah said, but remember it is allegory, layers. “And I will wait upon the Lord, that hideth his face from the house of Jacob, and I will look for him. Behold, I and the children whom the Lord hath given me are for signs and for wonders in Israel.”

**[Hebrews 2:14-15](/bible/hebrews/2#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/hebrews/2#v14)</sup> Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil;
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/hebrews/2#v15)</sup> And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage.

- “Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood”: Christ’s children. How are children formed? Between a father and a mother. “He also himself likewise took part of the same”: he came into the world like everybody else, like his children. “That through death”, because he was immortal, “he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil”: the spiritual entity, and Edom as his children too. “And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage”: which is us.
- “Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same”: Christ took part of the same flesh and blood that we have, like you and me.
- “That through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil.” Through Christ’s death, his sacrifice, he destroyed the power of the devil.
- “Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same.” The children are the disciples, us. Christ took part of the same flesh and blood.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Corinthians 15:54-57](/bible/1-corinthians/15#v54)**
    > <sup>[54](/bible/1-corinthians/15#v54)</sup> So when this corruptible shall have put on incorruption, and this mortal shall have put on immortality, then shall be brought to pass the saying that is written, Death is swallowed up in victory.
    >
    > <sup>[55](/bible/1-corinthians/15#v55)</sup> O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory?
    >
    > <sup>[56](/bible/1-corinthians/15#v56)</sup> The sting of death is sin; and the strength of sin is the law.
    >
    > <sup>[57](/bible/1-corinthians/15#v57)</sup> But thanks be to God, which giveth us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.

    “So when this corruptible”, this body we are living in, “shall have put on incorruption”: that is when we are changed, transformed. “And this mortal shall have put on immortality”: there is a time for this. There is a time coming when corruptible shall put on incorruption and mortality shall put on immortality. We have not reached that point yet; that point comes when Christ returns. Then, at the transformation, “shall be brought to pass the saying that is written, Death is swallowed up in victory.”
  - **[Matthew 28:18](/bible/matthew/28#v18)**
    > <sup>[18](/bible/matthew/28#v18)</sup> And Jesus came and spake unto them, saying, All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth.

    “All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth.” When he resurrected, Christ let the disciples know, and lets us know, that all power is given to him, that God power. He got it all back. The one who through death destroyed him that had the power of death now holds all power.
  - **[Revelation 1:18](/bible/revelation/1#v18)**
    > <sup>[18](/bible/revelation/1#v18)</sup> I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death.

    So what about hell? We just read in Job that when you die you are at peace, so when does the torment come? Christ says, “I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death.”

**[Hebrews 2:16](/bible/hebrews/2#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/hebrews/2#v16)</sup> For verily he took not on him the nature of angels; but he took on him the seed of Abraham.

- “For verily he took not on him the nature of angels”: he was not fully God and fully man; he was fully man. “But he took on him the seed of Abraham”: flesh and blood.
- “He took not on him the nature of angels; but he took on him the seed of Abraham”: flesh and blood.
- This repeats what verse 14 already said, that he was a partaker of flesh and blood. “For verily he took not on him the nature of angels”: not the nature of angelic, divine beings. “But he took on him the seed of Abraham”: flesh and blood, born of a father and a mother.
- This is basic, and we have to have it in our Bible, because they blaspheme the Bible when they say these things. “For verily he took not on him the nature of angels”: he did not take on a heavenly body. “But he took on him the seed of Abraham”: he came through the line of David. So to say he came into the earth and was God and Christ at the same time is to say that everything prophesied in the Bible is not true. He doesn’t believe the Bible. That is what a trinitarian is: a person who doesn’t believe the Bible, and they sound stupid in the end.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hebrews 7:14](/bible/hebrews/7#v14)**
    > <sup>[14](/bible/hebrews/7#v14)</sup> For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood.

    “It is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda.” He sprang out of Judah by his father.

**[Hebrews 2:17](/bible/hebrews/2#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/hebrews/2#v17)</sup> Wherefore in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren, that he might be a merciful and faithful high priest in things pertaining to God, to make reconciliation for the sins of the people.

- “Wherefore in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren.” Put the emphasis on in all things: nothing changed in the way Christ was born on this earth. In all things he was made just like his brethren, and all his brethren were made through a father and a mother. “That he might be a merciful and faithful high priest in things pertaining to God, to make reconciliation for the sins of the people”: his own people.
- “Wherefore in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren.” In all things, not some things. How is that possible except with a father and a mother?
- “Wherefore in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren”: in all things he found it suitable to be made like his brethren, flesh and blood.
- And this is why: “that he might be a merciful and faithful high priest in things pertaining to God, to make reconciliation for the sins of the people.” He took flesh so he could live and die for the people. Angels don't live and die; they live forever. Christ lived and died for the people.

**[Hebrews 2:18](/bible/hebrews/2#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/hebrews/2#v18)</sup> For in that he himself hath suffered being tempted, he is able to succour them that are tempted.

- “For in that he himself hath suffered being tempted, he is able to succour them that are tempted.” Because Christ took on flesh and blood, he can relate to those covered in flesh and blood. If he were that angel he could not relate, because he would not be in a fleshly form. In a fleshly form he understands us: why we sin, why we go off, the battle we have every day within ourselves. He fought that same battle, but he was not overcome by it.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hebrews 4:15](/bible/hebrews/4#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/hebrews/4#v15)</sup> For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.

    Hebrews says it again: “For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.”
