## Introduction

This session covers Luke 10 through 13. The video it comes from is titled for John 6 through 9, but the reading given is Luke, and it is Luke that these notes follow. The seventy are sent out two and two, a lawyer is answered with the parable of the good Samaritan, the disciples are taught how to pray, the woes are pronounced upon the Pharisees and the lawyers, and the last chapter closes over Jerusalem. The thread is repentance and the discerning of the time.

**[Luke 10:29-37](/bible/luke/10#v29)**

> <sup>[29](/bible/luke/10#v29)</sup> But he, willing to justify himself, said unto Jesus, And who is my neighbour?
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/luke/10#v30)</sup> And Jesus answering said, A certain man went down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and fell among thieves, which stripped him of his raiment, and wounded him, and departed, leaving him half dead.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/luke/10#v31)</sup> And by chance there came down a certain priest that way: and when he saw him, he passed by on the other side.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/luke/10#v32)</sup> And likewise a Levite, when he was at the place, came and looked on him, and passed by on the other side.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/luke/10#v33)</sup> But a certain Samaritan, as he journeyed, came where he was: and when he saw him, he had compassion on him,
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/luke/10#v34)</sup> And went to him, and bound up his wounds, pouring in oil and wine, and set him on his own beast, and brought him to an inn, and took care of him.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/luke/10#v35)</sup> And on the morrow when he departed, he took out two pence, and gave them to the host, and said unto him, Take care of him; and whatsoever thou spendest more, when I come again, I will repay thee.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/luke/10#v36)</sup> Which now of these three, thinkest thou, was neighbour unto him that fell among the thieves?
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/luke/10#v37)</sup> And he said, He that shewed mercy on him. Then said Jesus unto him, Go, and do thou likewise.

- Willing to justify himself, he asks, And who is my neighbour?
- A priest and a Levite pass by on the other side. Both were of the southern kingdom, and neither had mercy on their own.
- A Samaritan had compassion, bound up his wounds, and paid for his keeping. Samaria was the head of Ephraim, so the man who showed mercy was of the northern kingdom.
- Go, and do thou likewise. The mercy that was withheld between the two houses is the very thing commanded here.

  Precepts:
  - **[Isaiah 7:9](/bible/isaiah/7#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/isaiah/7#v9)</sup> And the head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is Remaliah’s son. If ye will not believe, surely ye shall not be established.

    The head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is Remaliah's son.

# Luke 11: How to Pray, the Strong Man, and the Woes

> <sup>[1](/bible/luke/11#v1)</sup> And it came to pass, that, as he was praying in a certain place, when he ceased, one of his disciples said unto him, Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/luke/11#v2)</sup> And he said unto them, When ye pray, say, Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done, as in heaven, so in earth.

- Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples. He gives them the words: Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name.

**[Luke 11:5-8](/bible/luke/11#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/luke/11#v5)</sup> And he said unto them, Which of you shall have a friend, and shall go unto him at midnight, and say unto him, Friend, lend me three loaves;
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/luke/11#v6)</sup> For a friend of mine in his journey is come to me, and I have nothing to set before him?
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/luke/11#v7)</sup> And he from within shall answer and say, Trouble me not: the door is now shut, and my children are with me in bed; I cannot rise and give thee.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/luke/11#v8)</sup> I say unto you, Though he will not rise and give him, because he is his friend, yet because of his importunity he will rise and give him as many as he needeth.

- The friend at midnight will not rise because he is his friend, yet because of his importunity he will rise and give him as many as he needeth.
- Importunity is persistence to the point of annoyance. The man kept knocking, and the man inside gave him enough that he would not come back.

**[Luke 11:9-13](/bible/luke/11#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/luke/11#v9)</sup> And I say unto you, Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/luke/11#v10)</sup> For every one that asketh receiveth; and he that seeketh findeth; and to him that knocketh it shall be opened.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/luke/11#v11)</sup> If a son shall ask bread of any of you that is a father, will he give him a stone? or if he ask a fish, will he for a fish give him a serpent?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/luke/11#v12)</sup> Or if he shall ask an egg, will he offer him a scorpion?
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/luke/11#v13)</sup> If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?

- Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you.
- The parable before it is the point: prayer is not said once and left. There is persistence in it, and there is action behind it, because the man in the parable was standing at the door.
- If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?
