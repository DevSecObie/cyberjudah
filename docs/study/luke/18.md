## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Luke 18 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-14, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Luke 18:1](/bible/luke/18#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/luke/18#v1)</sup> And he spake a parable unto them to this end, that men ought always to pray, and not to faint;

- Moses’ song just said he will avenge the blood of his servants and will render vengeance to his adversaries. Christ in Luke 18 is saying the same thing the song of Moses said. “That men ought always to pray, and not to faint”: don’t give up praying. That’s what he’s saying, don’t give up.
- A brother said, what about the Lord’s Prayer? In the Lord’s Prayer he said ‘thy kingdom come, thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven.’ Now we’re going to read his will, the will of the Lord.
- “And he spake a parable unto them to this end, that men ought always to pray, and not to faint”. This parable is about prayer: we ought always to pray and never to faint.
- “That men ought always to pray, and not to faint”: we must always pray. Psalms 55:17 says evening, morning and noon we pray. Always pray.

**[Luke 18:2-3](/bible/luke/18#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/luke/18#v2)</sup> Saying, There was in a city a judge, which feared not God, neither regarded man:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/luke/18#v3)</sup> And there was a widow in that city; and she came unto him, saying, Avenge me of mine adversary.

- “There was in a city a judge, which feared not God, neither regarded man: And there was a widow in that city”: we are that widow. Remember, he said, I was married unto you, but because we sinned, he refused us. “And she came unto him, saying, Avenge me of mine adversary”: the widow, which is us, says avenge me of mine enemies. We want revenge.
- “There was in a city a judge, which feared not God, neither regarded man”: this is the Lord.
- “There was in a city a judge”: write this down, this judge represents the Most High, “which feared not God, neither regarded man”.
- “And there was a widow in that city”: this widow represents Israel. “And she came unto him, saying, Avenge me of mine adversary.” We just read in Lamentations that the adversaries were the nations.

  Precepts:
  - **[Isaiah 54:4-5](/bible/isaiah/54#v4)**

    >
    > <sup>[5](/bible/isaiah/54#v5)</sup> For thy Maker is thine husband; the Lord of hosts is his name; and thy Redeemer the Holy One of Israel; The God of the whole earth shall he be called.

    Who is the widow? “Fear not; for thou shalt not be ashamed: neither be thou confounded; for thou shalt not be put to shame: for thou shalt forget the shame of thy youth, and shalt not remember the reproach of thy widowhood any more.”
  - **[Lamentations 1:1-5](/bible/lamentations/1#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/lamentations/1#v1)</sup> How doth the city sit solitary, that was full of people! how is she become as a widow! she that was great among the nations, and princess among the provinces, how is she become tributary!
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/lamentations/1#v2)</sup> She weepeth sore in the night, and her tears are on her cheeks: among all her lovers she hath none to comfort her: all her friends have dealt treacherously with her, they are become her enemies.

    > <sup>[5](/bible/lamentations/1#v5)</sup> Her adversaries are the chief, her enemies prosper; for the Lord hath afflicted her for the multitude of her transgressions: her children are gone into captivity before the enemy.

    Another one for the widow, a better one. “How doth the city sit solitary, that was full of people! how is she become as a widow!” See that: how is she become as a widow. “She that was great among the nations”: we were great among the nations, “and princess among the provinces, how is she become tributary!”
  - **[Lamentations 1:1](/bible/lamentations/1#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/lamentations/1#v1)</sup> How doth the city sit solitary, that was full of people! how is she become as a widow! she that was great among the nations, and princess among the provinces, how is she become tributary!

    The widow: “How doth the city sit solitary, that was full of people! how is she become as a widow! she that was great among the nations, and princess among the provinces, how is she become tributary!” We are the widow. Christ is making reference back to this in Luke 18.

**[Luke 18:4](/bible/luke/18#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/luke/18#v4)</sup> And he would not for a while: but afterward he said within himself, Though I fear not God, nor regard man;

- “And he would not for a while”: he hid his face from us, like we read in Deuteronomy, in the song. “But afterward he said within himself”: this is what we just read in Deuteronomy 32, then shall the Lord repent himself when he seeth that their power is gone and there is none shut up or left.
- “And he would not for a while”. That is why it seemed like the Lord forgot us; that’s why Isaiah said it seemed like the Lord has forsaken us. That is that part of the parable. Many times we pray to the Lord and it seems like he’s not listening. We see all the atrocities that happen to our people, and we pray and pray and pray every day, and it keeps on happening.
- Then here it comes: “afterward he said within himself, Though I fear not God, nor regard man”.
- “And he would not for a while”: remember Psalms 50, all these things hast thou done and I kept silence. It is the same thing. “But afterward he said within himself, Though I fear not God, nor regard man”: there is no God beside me, and I regard no man.

**[Luke 18:5](/bible/luke/18#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/luke/18#v5)</sup> Yet because this widow troubleth me, I will avenge her, lest by her continual coming she weary me.

- “Yet because this widow troubleth me”: she troubles him with asking, praying day after day. “I will avenge her, lest by her continual coming she weary me.”
- “Yet because this widow troubleth me”: this widow, the Israelites, troubleth him with her constant prayers, constant nagging. “I will avenge her, lest by her continual coming she weary me.”
- “Yet because this widow troubleth me, I will avenge her”: because the twelve tribes of Israel trouble him. “Lest by her continual coming she weary me”: the key is her continual coming. Pray. Pray for vengeance, and don’t stop.
- “Yet because this widow troubleth me, I will avenge her, lest by her continual coming she weary me.” She kept praying to him, kept troubling him, for vengeance on her adversary. That is what we have to do with the Lord when we are going through it: pray and pray and weary him. Let him know: Lord, we are going through it, we need your help, we can’t do it without you.

**[Luke 18:6-7](/bible/luke/18#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/luke/18#v6)</sup> And the Lord said, Hear what the unjust judge saith.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/luke/18#v7)</sup> And shall not God avenge his own elect, which cry day and night unto him, though he bear long with them?

- “And the Lord said, Hear what the unjust judge saith.” Here comes the proof of what it means: hear what the Most High said. “And shall not God avenge his own elect, which cry day and night unto him”, for Tamir Rice, Alton Sterling, Eric Garner, Kendrick Johnson, and all the rest.
- So when we send up our prayers, we got to ask the Lord: Heavenly Father, send fire on this earth, send judgment on the nations, destroy our enemies, destroy their families, destroy their lives. That’s what Christ said to do. You praying for love and peace; he said pray for vengeance. Pray that everyone that put their hands on our people die a gruesome death. That’s what God said to do, that’s what Jesus Christ said to do. We pray for fire and storm, earthquakes, tornadoes, judgment, judgment, judgment, terrorism in this earth, terrorism in this country.
- Believe it or not, that will ease some of the pain that we feel inside. You busy trying to love everybody, but don’t nobody love us. We can’t even get our ruling passed in court to help protect us, our sons and daughters.
- “Hear what the unjust judge saith.” Why do they call the Lord unjust? Because a lot of our people think it is unjust for God to put us in slavery and to punish us. It is not. On the street when we teach, they ask, why would an all-loving God do that? You have kids: don’t you punish your kids when they go off? So likewise the Lord.

**[Luke 18:8](/bible/luke/18#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/luke/18#v8)</sup> I tell you that he will avenge them speedily. Nevertheless when the Son of man cometh, shall he find faith on the earth?

- “I tell you that he will avenge them speedily.” Y’all see that right there. “Nevertheless when the Son of man cometh, shall he find faith on the earth?”
- “I tell you that he will avenge them speedily. Nevertheless when the Son of man cometh, shall he find faith on the earth?” Will there be faith? Will the brothers and sisters still be doing it? Will you believe to pray for vengeance? That is what Christ is asking.
- Last week the brothers marched on the Road of Remembrance in Jackson, Mississippi, to the abandoned house where Tasia Fortune was found hanging from a tree, read the scriptures there, and prayed: Father, we ask for judgment; we ask for vengeance against our enemies.

**[Luke 18:9](/bible/luke/18#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/luke/18#v9)</sup> And he spake this parable unto certain which trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and despised others:

- “Unto certain which trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and despised others”. People who think they are so holy can’t be stood. Go to these Christian churches and a lot of them are like that.

**[Luke 18:10](/bible/luke/18#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/luke/18#v10)</sup> Two men went up into the temple to pray; the one a Pharisee, and the other a publican.

- The publican is a tax collector, and Israel hated the tax collectors because they worked for Rome.

**[Luke 18:11-12](/bible/luke/18#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/luke/18#v11)</sup> The Pharisee stood and prayed thus with himself, God, I thank thee, that I am not as other men are, extortioners, unjust, adulterers, or even as this publican.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/luke/18#v12)</sup> I fast twice in the week, I give tithes of all that I possess.

- “God, I thank thee, that I am not as other men are”. Not only does the Pharisee glorify himself in the sight of God, he puts down a brother next to him that is praying: even like this wicked publican, I ain’t like him. This is what Christ is talking about. “I fast twice in the week, I give tithes of all that I possess.”

**[Luke 18:13](/bible/luke/18#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/luke/18#v13)</sup> And the publican, standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner.

- “The publican, standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven”: that’s humility. He “smote upon his breast”, beat upon his breast, the way you do when you are weeping in sorrow over what you’ve done, the guilt and shame you have: “God be merciful to me a sinner.”

**[Luke 18:14](/bible/luke/18#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/luke/18#v14)</sup> I tell you, this man went down to his house justified rather than the other: for every one that exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.

- “This man went down to his house justified rather than the other: for every one that exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.” So we have to be mindful to keep a humble and meek attitude in this truth always, glory in the Lord, and not set ourselves up as better than one another.
