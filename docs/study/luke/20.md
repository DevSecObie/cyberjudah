## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Luke 20 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-20, 35-36, 38, 46-47, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Luke 20:1-2](/bible/luke/20#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/luke/20#v1)</sup> And it came to pass, that on one of those days, as he taught the people in the temple, and preached the gospel, the chief priests and the scribes came upon him with the elders,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/luke/20#v2)</sup> And spake unto him, saying, Tell us, by what authority doest thou these things? or who is he that gave thee this authority?

- “As he taught the people in the temple, and preached the gospel”: Christ taught at a religious gathering. He was provoking, stirring the pot. “The chief priests and the scribes came upon him with the elders”, saying, “Tell us, by what authority doest thou these things? or who is he that gave thee this authority?” Who told you that you could teach here? Who do you work for?
- “The chief priests and the scribes came upon him with the elders, and spake unto him, saying, Tell us, by what authority doest thou these things? or who is he that gave thee this authority?”

**[Luke 20:3-8](/bible/luke/20#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/luke/20#v3)</sup> And he answered and said unto them, I will also ask you one thing; and answer me:
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/luke/20#v4)</sup> The baptism of John, was it from heaven, or of men?
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/luke/20#v5)</sup> And they reasoned with themselves, saying, If we shall say, From heaven; he will say, Why then believed ye him not?
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/luke/20#v6)</sup> But and if we say, Of men; all the people will stone us: for they be persuaded that John was a prophet.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/luke/20#v7)</sup> And they answered, that they could not tell whence it was.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/luke/20#v8)</sup> And Jesus said unto them, Neither tell I you by what authority I do these things.

- “I will also ask you one thing; and answer me: The baptism of John, was it from heaven, or of men?” The teaching of John, was it of heaven or of man? They had to huddle. If they said from heaven, he would ask why they did not believe him; if they said of men, the people would stone them, “for they be persuaded that John was a prophet”: John had a huge following. So they answered “that they could not tell whence it was.” “Neither tell I you by what authority I do these things”: if you can't answer my question, I'm not answering yours. That's a black man right there.
- “The baptism of John, was it from heaven, or of men?” When John taught, was it of God or of men?
- “And they reasoned with themselves”: they went into a huddle to answer the question. “If we shall say, From heaven; he will say, Why then believed ye him not?” If we say his teaching was from heaven, he'll ask why we rejected him.
- “But and if we say, Of men”: if we say his doctrine was of man, that he was a fake Israelite camp, a regular guy, “all the people will stone us: for they be persuaded that John was a prophet.”

**[Luke 20:9-12](/bible/luke/20#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/luke/20#v9)</sup> Then began he to speak to the people this parable; A certain man planted a vineyard, and let it forth to husbandmen, and went into a far country for a long time.
>

- “A certain man planted a vineyard, and let it forth to husbandmen, and went into a far country for a long time.” The vineyard is us. The husbandmen are the servants, the leaders and elders of the people, and he left us to their leadership.
- “At the season he sent a servant to the husbandmen, that they should give him of the fruit of the vineyard: but the husbandmen beat him, and sent him away empty.” The servant is the prophet sent to collect for the Lord. The elders beat another, as they did Jeremiah, and wounded a third and cast him out.

**[Luke 20:17-18](/bible/luke/20#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/luke/20#v17)</sup> And he beheld them, and said, What is this then that is written, The stone which the builders rejected, the same is become the head of the corner?
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/luke/20#v18)</sup> Whosoever shall fall upon that stone shall be broken; but on whomsoever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder.

- “What is this then that is written, The stone which the builders rejected, the same is become the head of the corner?” According to prophecy, the elders get replaced by the stone. “Whosoever shall fall upon that stone shall be broken”: whoever attacks or kills that stone shall be destroyed. “But on whomsoever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder.” Christ is saying: you are going to kill me, and I am going to come back and kill you.
- “The stone which the builders rejected, the same is become the head of the corner”: that is Psalm 118.
- “Whosoever shall fall upon that stone shall be broken”: whoever kills Christ shall be broken. “But on whomsoever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder”: when he returns and kills them.
