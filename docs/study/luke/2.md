## Introduction

This session covers Luke 2 through 5. It is a reading session, with precepts brought in where they settle a point rather than a full breakdown. The chapters carry the birth of Christ under the taxing of Caesar, his circumcision and presentation according to the law, the preaching of John, the genealogy back to Adam, the temptation in the wilderness, the reading in the synagogue at Nazareth, and the calling of the first disciples. The reason for reading straight through is given in the epistle: give attendance to reading, to exhortation, to doctrine.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Timothy 4:13](/bible/1-timothy/4#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/1-timothy/4#v13)</sup> Till I come, give attendance to reading, to exhortation, to doctrine.

    Till I come, give attendance to reading, to exhortation, to doctrine.

**[Luke 2:1-7](/bible/luke/2#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/luke/2#v1)</sup> And it came to pass in those days, that there went out a decree from Cesar Augustus, that all the world should be taxed.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/luke/2#v2)</sup> (And this taxing was first made when Cyrenius was governor of Syria.)
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/luke/2#v3)</sup> And all went to be taxed, every one into his own city.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/luke/2#v4)</sup> And Joseph also went up from Galilee, out of the city of Nazareth, into Judea, unto the city of David, which is called Bethlehem; (because he was of the house and lineage of David:)
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/luke/2#v5)</sup> To be taxed with Mary his espoused wife, being great with child.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/luke/2#v6)</sup> And so it was, that, while they were there, the days were accomplished that she should be delivered.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/luke/2#v7)</sup> And she brought forth her firstborn son, and wrapped him in swaddling clothes, and laid him in a manger; because there was no room for them in the inn.

- A decree from Caesar Augustus sends every man to his own city to be taxed, and Joseph goes up to Bethlehem because he was of the house and lineage of David.
- She brought forth her firstborn son, and wrapped him in swaddling clothes, and laid him in a manger, because there was no room for them in the inn.

**[Luke 2:8-14](/bible/luke/2#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/luke/2#v8)</sup> And there were in the same country shepherds abiding in the field, keeping watch over their flock by night.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/luke/2#v9)</sup> And, lo, the angel of the Lord came upon them, and the glory of the Lord shone round about them: and they were sore afraid.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/luke/2#v10)</sup> And the angel said unto them, Fear not: for, behold, I bring you good tidings of great joy, which shall be to all people.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/luke/2#v11)</sup> For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/luke/2#v12)</sup> And this shall be a sign unto you; Ye shall find the babe wrapped in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/luke/2#v13)</sup> And suddenly there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly host praising God, and saying,
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/luke/2#v14)</sup> Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men.

- The angel gives the shepherds a sign: ye shall find the babe wrapped in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger.
- The swaddling clothes are the mark of an ordinary entrance into life. The wise man says plainly that there is no king that had any other beginning of birth.
