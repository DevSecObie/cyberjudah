**[Luke 23:1-5](/bible/luke/23#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/luke/23#v1)</sup> And the whole multitude of them arose, and led him unto Pilate.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/luke/23#v2)</sup> And they began to accuse him, saying, We found this fellow perverting the nation, and forbidding to give tribute to Cesar, saying that he himself is Christ a King.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/luke/23#v3)</sup> And Pilate asked him, saying, Art thou the King of the Jews? And he answered him and said, Thou sayest it.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/luke/23#v4)</sup> Then said Pilate to the chief priests and to the people, I find no fault in this man.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/luke/23#v5)</sup> And they were the more fierce, saying, He stirreth up the people, teaching throughout all Jewry, beginning from Galilee to this place.

- They accuse him of perverting the nation and forbidding to give tribute to Caesar, and Pilate says, I find no fault in this man. Matthew's account gives the reason he could see it: he knew that for envy they had delivered him.

**[Luke 23:6-12](/bible/luke/23#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/luke/23#v6)</sup> When Pilate heard of Galilee, he asked whether the man were a Galilean.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/luke/23#v7)</sup> And as soon as he knew that he belonged unto Herod’s jurisdiction, he sent him to Herod, who himself also was at Jerusalem at that time.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/luke/23#v8)</sup> And when Herod saw Jesus, he was exceeding glad: for he was desirous to see him of a long season, because he had heard many things of him; and he hoped to have seen some miracle done by him.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/luke/23#v9)</sup> Then he questioned with him in many words; but he answered him nothing.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/luke/23#v10)</sup> And the chief priests and scribes stood and vehemently accused him.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/luke/23#v11)</sup> And Herod with his men of war set him at nought, and mocked him, and arrayed him in a gorgeous robe, and sent him again to Pilate.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/luke/23#v12)</sup> And the same day Pilate and Herod were made friends together: for before they were at enmity between themselves.

- Herod was exceeding glad, hoping to have seen some miracle done by him, and questioned him in many words, but he answered him nothing.
- He had nothing to say to that house. Its head had sought his life as a child, and this one had beheaded John.

**[Luke 23:13-25](/bible/luke/23#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/luke/23#v13)</sup> And Pilate, when he had called together the chief priests and the rulers and the people,
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/luke/23#v14)</sup> Said unto them, Ye have brought this man unto me, as one that perverteth the people: and, behold, I, having examined him before you, have found no fault in this man touching those things whereof ye accuse him:
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/luke/23#v15)</sup> No, nor yet Herod: for I sent you to him; and, lo, nothing worthy of death is done unto him.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/luke/23#v16)</sup> I will therefore chastise him, and release him.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/luke/23#v17)</sup> (For of necessity he must release one unto them at the feast.)
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/luke/23#v18)</sup> And they cried out all at once, saying, Away with this man, and release unto us Barabbas:
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/luke/23#v19)</sup> (Who for a certain sedition made in the city, and for murder, was cast into prison.)
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/luke/23#v20)</sup> Pilate therefore, willing to release Jesus, spake again to them.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/luke/23#v21)</sup> But they cried, saying, Crucify him, crucify him.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/luke/23#v22)</sup> And he said unto them the third time, Why, what evil hath he done? I have found no cause of death in him: I will therefore chastise him, and let him go.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/luke/23#v23)</sup> And they were instant with loud voices, requiring that he might be crucified. And the voices of them and of the chief priests prevailed.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/luke/23#v24)</sup> And Pilate gave sentence that it should be as they required.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/luke/23#v25)</sup> And he released unto them him that for sedition and murder was cast into prison, whom they had desired; but he delivered Jesus to their will.

- Pilate says a third time, Why, what evil hath he done? I have found no cause of death in him. He knew the man was innocent and gave sentence anyway.
- Barabbas, cast into prison for sedition and murder, is released, and Jesus is delivered to their will.

>

>
> <sup>[34](/bible/luke/23#v34)</sup> Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/luke/23#v35)</sup> And the people stood beholding. And the rulers also with them derided him, saying, He saved others; let him save himself, if he be Christ, the chosen of God.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/luke/23#v36)</sup> And the soldiers also mocked him, coming to him, and offering him vinegar,
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/luke/23#v37)</sup> And saying, If thou be the king of the Jews, save thyself.
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/luke/23#v38)</sup> And a superscription also was written over him in letters of Greek, and Latin, and Hebrew, THIS IS THE KING OF THE JEWS.

- Two malefactors, criminals, are led with him to be put to death, and they crucified him at the place called Calvary.
- Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. The superscription written over him in Greek, Latin and Hebrew reads, This is the King of the Jews.

>
> <sup>[40](/bible/luke/23#v40)</sup> But the other answering rebuked him, saying, Dost not thou fear God, seeing thou art in the same condemnation?
>
> <sup>[41](/bible/luke/23#v41)</sup> And we indeed justly; for we receive the due reward of our deeds: but this man hath done nothing amiss.
>
> <sup>[42](/bible/luke/23#v42)</sup> And he said unto Jesus, Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom.
>
> <sup>[43](/bible/luke/23#v43)</sup> And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.

- One malefactor railed on him; the other rebuked him, took the blame of his own deeds, and said, Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom.
- That man repented at the last hour, and was answered, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.

**[Luke 23:44-46](/bible/luke/23#v44)**

> <sup>[44](/bible/luke/23#v44)</sup> And it was about the sixth hour, and there was a darkness over all the earth until the ninth hour.
>
> <sup>[45](/bible/luke/23#v45)</sup> And the sun was darkened, and the veil of the temple was rent in the midst.
>
> <sup>[46](/bible/luke/23#v46)</sup> And when Jesus had cried with a loud voice, he said, Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit: and having said thus, he gave up the ghost.

- There was a darkness over all the earth until the ninth hour, and the sun was darkened, and the veil of the temple was rent in the midst.
- The rending of the veil is a sign. What stood between the holy place and the holiest was opened, and the sacrificial service under the first testament was coming to its end.

**[Luke 23:50-56](/bible/luke/23#v50)**

> <sup>[50](/bible/luke/23#v50)</sup> And, behold, there was a man named Joseph, a counsellor; and he was a good man, and a just:
>
> <sup>[51](/bible/luke/23#v51)</sup> (The same had not consented to the counsel and deed of them;) he was of Arimathea, a city of the Jews: who also himself waited for the kingdom of God.
>
> <sup>[52](/bible/luke/23#v52)</sup> This man went unto Pilate, and begged the body of Jesus.
>
> <sup>[53](/bible/luke/23#v53)</sup> And he took it down, and wrapped it in linen, and laid it in a sepulchre that was hewn in stone, wherein never man before was laid.
>
> <sup>[54](/bible/luke/23#v54)</sup> And that day was the preparation, and the sabbath drew on.
>
> <sup>[55](/bible/luke/23#v55)</sup> And the women also, which came with him from Galilee, followed after, and beheld the sepulchre, and how his body was laid.
>
> <sup>[56](/bible/luke/23#v56)</sup> And they returned, and prepared spices and ointments; and rested the sabbath day according to the commandment.

- Joseph of Arimathaea, a good man and a just, had not consented to their counsel and deed, and begged the body and laid it in a sepulchre hewn in stone.
- He was buried the same day, which is the custom written in the law. There is no waiting days over a body.
- The women returned, and prepared spices and ointments; and rested the sabbath day according to the commandment. That is written after the death, in the new testament.
