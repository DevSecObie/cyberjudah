## Introduction

This reading closes out the book of Joshua, covering chapters 21 through 24: the cities and suburbs given to the Levites out of every tribe, the altar of witness built by the two and a half tribes on the other side Jordan, Joshua's farewell charge to cleave unto the Most High, and the covenant made at Shechem where all Israel chose to serve the Lord. The book of Joshua, like the books before it, is the history of the tribes; it goes further into how Israel got the land, and precept upon precept some things are more detailed in Numbers while Joshua carries the same history further. The Q&A after the reading is folded into the verses it belongs to.

- Joshua said unto the people, Ye cannot serve the LORD: for he is an holy God; he is a jealous God; he will not forgive your transgressions nor your sins.
- If ye forsake the LORD, and serve strange gods, then he will turn and do you hurt, and consume you, after that he hath done you good. Everything he promised for good had come to pass and Israel was on top in the land; all they had to do was keep the commandments. Going off meant being taken out of that land into captivity, and because that is what Israel decided to do, that is the predicament we are in today.

**[Joshua 24:21-25](/bible/joshua/24#v21)**

>

- The people said unto Joshua, Nay; but we will serve the LORD. It is the same thing Israel told Moses back then; we came into that covenant again, this time without the blood.
- Joshua said, Ye are witnesses against yourselves that ye have chosen you the LORD, to serve him; and they said, We are witnesses. Everybody was in full agreement.
- Put away the strange gods which are among you, and incline your heart unto the LORD God of Israel; and the people said, The LORD our God will we serve, and his voice will we obey.
- So Joshua made a covenant with the people that day, and set them a statute and an ordinance in Shechem, the same thing Moses his predecessor did at the mountain; Joshua was his successor and did the same, though without the blood.
