## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Prayer of Manasses 1 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-14, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Prayer of Manasses 1:1-3](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v1)</sup> O Lord, Almighty God of our fathers, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and of their righteous seed;
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v2)</sup> who hast made heaven and earth, with all the ornament thereof;
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v3)</sup> who hast bound the sea by the word of thy commandment; who hast shut up the deep, and sealed it by thy terrible and glorious name;

- This is not so much a book as a prayer: the same prayer Manasseh prayed when he humbled himself and the Lord heard him. It is the prayer of the king of Judah when he was held captive in Babylon, after Assyria had taken him with fetters and chains of iron because of all the evil he was doing. Listen to him while he’s locked up, praying: “O Lord, Almighty God of our fathers, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and of their righteous seed.”

**[Prayer of Manasses 1:4-5](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v4)</sup> whom all men fear, and tremble before thy power; for the majesty of thy glory cannot be borne, and thine angry threatening toward sinners is importable:
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v5)</sup> but thy merciful promise is unmeasurable and unsearchable;

- “For the majesty of thy glory cannot be borne”: it can’t be carried. “And thine angry threatening toward sinners is importable”: importable means it is included, inserted, like import and export. His angry threatening toward sinners is executed.
- “But thy merciful promise is unmeasurable and unsearchable”: his mercy cannot be measured or searched out.

**[Prayer of Manasses 1:6](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v6)</sup> for thou art the most high Lord, of great compassion, longsuffering, very merciful, and repentest of the evils of men. Thou, O Lord, according to thy great goodness hast promised repentance and forgiveness to them that have sinned against thee: and of thine infinite mercies hast appointed repentance unto sinners, that they may be saved.

- “For thou art the most high Lord, of great compassion, longsuffering, very merciful, and repentest of the evils of men.” The Most High tends to overlook the evils of men, because he knows what’s in man.
- “Thou, O Lord, according to thy great goodness hast promised repentance and forgiveness to them that have sinned against thee: and of thine infinite mercies hast appointed repentance unto sinners, that they may be saved.” He keeps saying it over and over again: repentance, repentance, repentance, repentance of sinners, forgiveness to them that have sinned.

**[Prayer of Manasses 1:7](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v7)</sup> Thou therefore, O Lord, that art the God of the just, hast not appointed repentance to the just, as to Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, which have not sinned against thee; but thou hast appointed repentance unto me that am a sinner:

- “Thou therefore, O Lord, that art the God of the just, hast not appointed repentance to the just”: the just are those that keep the commandments. Obviously, if you’re just you don’t have to repent from anything. “As to Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, which have not sinned against thee”: they did not sin against the Lord; they kept God’s laws.
- “But thou hast appointed repentance unto me that am a sinner.” Understand: under the Mosaic law, Manasseh was condemned on all accounts. This man killed babies. He was an idolater and a murderer; he shed the blood of children from one end of Jerusalem to the other. He was probably influenced by homosexuality, cannibalism; you name it, he basically influenced it. He was basically America, but a king. He was Epstein, pretty much, but a king. He was Diddy: King Diddy Manasseh. And then he got locked up, like Diddy.
- All jokes aside, under the Mosaic law there was no repentance for this man, no animal he could sacrifice. You get death. He was a king worthy of death. His mindset was: I’m King Hezekiah’s son, I’m the king, I do what I want, when I want, how I want, where I want. The Lord said, you’re the king? Cool, and threw him in prison, and now he is breaking down.
- A lot of the celebrities with a king or queen status in this world will not repent. When you’re rich and have a lot of possessions, you tend to think you are God. No one can tell you nothing. You are idolized; they literally call people idols, with a show called American Idol. Oprah said she does not respect a jealous God, because she is a goddess of this world. At that level of status and power you believe nothing is beyond your reach.

**[Prayer of Manasses 1:8](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v8)</sup> for I have sinned above the number of the sands of the sea. My transgressions, O Lord, are multiplied: my transgressions are multiplied, and I am not worthy to behold and see the height of heaven for the multitude of mine iniquities.

- “For I have sinned above the number of the sands of the sea.” He did. “My transgressions, O Lord, are multiplied: my transgressions are multiplied.”
- So does that make you the most wicked person in the world because you have done these things? We all come from different walks of life. Many of us come from broken homes. Deacon Eythan’s own father passed when he was seven, doing street stuff; the family was so poor he wore rain boots to the funeral, and to save money on the casket they broke his father’s legs, put him in a smaller casket and buried him in a potter’s field, an unmarked grave under a bunch of people. That’s where a lot of us come from: fatherless households, motherless households, divided households, fathers in the streets and mothers forced to raise us alone. So we carry a lot of hang-ups, a lot of issues, multiple sins, and we hold on to that stuff and don’t let it go.
- But it is very important that we don’t say, I’m a sinner above everybody else. This class is not to make everybody feel guilty. We were exposed to things at a young age we’re not proud of and indulged in lifestyles we’re not proud of, and in here we are trying to fix ourselves and get right before the Lord returns. If you don’t believe this book, believe what you see on the news: the war with Iran and Israel, the empty aisles in the supermarket, the gas going up. You’re living in it. The Lord is coming nearer; it’s almost time for us to get up out of here. So we have to try our hardest to fight off what we’re battling so we can go home, and all the worries we fight with can burn along with this place.
- “My transgressions, O Lord, are multiplied”: I’ve done troubled things, Lord, many troubled things. “And I am not worthy to behold and see the height of heaven for the multitude of mine iniquities.” Many of us think this: I am not worthy. I am not worthy.

    Paul uses the exact same thing: “let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us.” The weight is sin.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 17:25](/bible/sirach/17#v25)**

**[Prayer of Manasses 1:9](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v9)</sup> I am bowed down with many iron bands, that I cannot lift up mine head, neither have any release: for I have provoked thy wrath, and done evil before thee: I did not thy will, neither kept I thy commandments: I have set up abominations, and have multiplied offences.

- “I am bowed down with many iron bands, that I cannot lift up mine head, neither have any release”: he is locked up in many iron bands. “For I have provoked thy wrath”: I pissed you off. “And done evil before thee: I did not thy will, neither kept I thy commandments”: and the will is keeping his commandments. “I have set up abominations, and have multiplied offences.”

  Precepts:
  - **[Psalms 40:8](/bible/psalms/40#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/40#v8)</sup> I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart.

    Manasseh said, “I did not thy will, neither kept I thy commandments”, and the Lord’s will is his commandments. “I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart.” The law of God is the will of God. God’s will is his laws.

**[Prayer of Manasses 1:12](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/prayer-of-manasseh/1#v12)</sup> wherefore, I humbly beseech thee, forgive me, O Lord, forgive me, and destroy me not with mine iniquities. Be not angry with me for ever, by reserving evil for me; neither condemn me into the lower parts of the earth. For thou art the God, even the God of them that repent;

- “Wherefore, I humbly beseech thee, forgive me, O Lord, forgive me, and destroy me not with mine iniquities.” He humbly beseeches: don’t destroy me for my sins. “Be not angry with me for ever, by reserving evil for me.” What does he mean by reserving evil for you? There is evil reserved for you if you keep playing around.
- “Neither condemn me into the lower parts of the earth”: that goes into the hellfire. “For thou art the God, even the God of them that repent.” He is a God of them that repent.
