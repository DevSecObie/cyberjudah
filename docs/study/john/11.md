## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on John 11 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 23-26, 45-52, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[John 11:23-24](/bible/john/11#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/john/11#v23)</sup> Jesus saith unto her, Thy brother shall rise again.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/john/11#v24)</sup> Martha saith unto him, I know that he shall rise again in the resurrection at the last day.

- Trials are meant to prepare us for days to come. “Jesus saith unto her, Thy brother shall rise again. Martha saith unto him, I know that he shall rise again in the resurrection at the last day.”

**[John 11:25-26](/bible/john/11#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/john/11#v25)</sup> Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live:
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/john/11#v26)</sup> And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

- “I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die.” Here is the key, these last three words: “Believest thou this?” Do you believe that? That is something every one of us has to ask ourselves individually.

**[John 11:45-48](/bible/john/11#v45)**

> <sup>[45](/bible/john/11#v45)</sup> Then many of the Jews which came to Mary, and had seen the things which Jesus did, believed on him.
>
> <sup>[46](/bible/john/11#v46)</sup> But some of them went their ways to the Pharisees, and told them what things Jesus had done.
>
> <sup>[47](/bible/john/11#v47)</sup> Then gathered the chief priests and the Pharisees a council, and said, What do we? for this man doeth many miracles.
>
> <sup>[48](/bible/john/11#v48)</sup> If we let him thus alone, all men will believe on him: and the Romans shall come and take away both our place and nation.

- The scholars themselves bring out that Christ was a black Israelite from Galilee, so the Sanhedrin, the court in Jerusalem that tried and condemned him, was a black court too: black Jews in Jerusalem, black scribes and black Pharisees, collaborating with the white Roman oppressor to keep their political and social privileges, just like they do today. Pilate and Herod were the white ones.
- “Then gathered the chief priests and the Pharisees a council, and said, What do we? for this man doeth many miracles. If we let him thus alone, all men will believe on him: and the Romans shall come and take away both our place and nation.” They had their allegiance to the Roman Empire. The black Sanhedrin had their allegiance to Rome, just like the black Christian church today has its allegiance to America, which is an extension of Rome.
- Under Rome everything seemed somewhat stable, as you read in Matthew, while the factions fought for rule. “If we let him thus alone, all men will believe on him: and the Romans shall come and take away both our place and nation.” If we don’t do something, Rome is going to come in. Right now it is stable, the Romans gave us our positions and our authority, and we can’t let this Christ mess things up. That was the issue.
- Many “believed on him. But some of them went their ways to the Pharisees”. They had seen that this man was dead and Christ raised him up. What the hell is going on here? What more do you need? Why are you still rejecting, still hating, still moved with disbelief?

**[John 11:49-52](/bible/john/11#v49)**

> <sup>[49](/bible/john/11#v49)</sup> And one of them, named Caiaphas, being the high priest that same year, said unto them, Ye know nothing at all,
>
> <sup>[50](/bible/john/11#v50)</sup> Nor consider that it is expedient for us, that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not.
>
> <sup>[51](/bible/john/11#v51)</sup> And this spake he not of himself: but being high priest that year, he prophesied that Jesus should die for that nation;
>
> <sup>[52](/bible/john/11#v52)</sup> And not for that nation only, but that also he should gather together in one the children of God that were scattered abroad.

- “That one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not.” “Being high priest that year, he prophesied that Jesus should die for that nation”: these were men raised in the law, so they knew the prophecy of Christ, but they rejected him because they didn’t want Rome to come take their place. There was money to be made from the temple, and every time Christ said the temple would be destroyed they were pissed off; they were envious and covetous of the authority Rome gave them. They knew this man was the Messiah, but they hated him and rejected him.
- Caiaphas had some understanding: “it is expedient for us, that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not.” He knew because the things written of old time were written for our learning. Christ comes in the volume of the book, prophesied to be the lamb to die for the nation.
- Caiaphas, the high priest, said “it is expedient for us, that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not.” Keep in mind the one fold and one shepherd, and the dispersed among the Gentiles.
- “This spake he not of himself: but being high priest that year, he prophesied that Jesus should die for that nation”. That nation is the southern kingdom, Judah. “And not for that nation only, but that also he should gather together in one the children of God that were scattered abroad.” The children of God scattered abroad are the northern kingdom: the strangers scattered of 1 Peter 1:1, gathered together in one.
