## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on John 11 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 23-26, 45-52, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[John 11:23-24](/bible/john/11#v23)**

> <sup>[23](/bible/john/11#v23)</sup> Jesus saith unto her, Thy brother shall rise again.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/john/11#v24)</sup> Martha saith unto him, I know that he shall rise again in the resurrection at the last day.

- Trials are meant to prepare us for days to come. “Jesus saith unto her, Thy brother shall rise again. Martha saith unto him, I know that he shall rise again in the resurrection at the last day.”

**[John 11:25-26](/bible/john/11#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/john/11#v25)</sup> Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live:
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/john/11#v26)</sup> And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

- “I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die.” Here is the key, these last three words: “Believest thou this?” Do you believe that? That is something every one of us has to ask ourselves individually.

**[John 11:45-48](/bible/john/11#v45)**

> <sup>[45](/bible/john/11#v45)</sup> Then many of the Jews which came to Mary, and had seen the things which Jesus did, believed on him.
>
> <sup>[46](/bible/john/11#v46)</sup> But some of them went their ways to the Pharisees, and told them what things Jesus had done.
>
> <sup>[47](/bible/john/11#v47)</sup> Then gathered the chief priests and the Pharisees a council, and said, What do we? for this man doeth many miracles.
>
> <sup>[48](/bible/john/11#v48)</sup> If we let him thus alone, all men will believe on him: and the Romans shall come and take away both our place and nation.

- Many “believed on him. But some of them went their ways to the Pharisees”. They had seen that this man was dead and Christ raised him up. What the hell is going on here? What more do you need? Why are you still rejecting, still hating, still moved with disbelief?
- You would think the mighty works the Most High allows Israel United in Christ to do would bring brothers to repentance: as much as we hate these guys, the spirit of the Lord is working with them, so let us fall in our place and stop the slander. No. They are consumed by it and must fulfil that evil lot, and if they continue in that footstep they will not get the kingdom of God. I don’t give a damn how many studs you put on your headband; they ain’t going to get you into the kingdom of God.
- “But some of them went their ways to the Pharisees, and told them what things Jesus had done”: the report came back of the miracles Christ had done for the nation. “Then gathered the chief priests and the Pharisees a council, and said, What do we? for this man doeth many miracles.” The Pharisees came together against Christ, seeing the miracles he was doing.
- The scholars themselves bring out that Christ was a black Israelite from Galilee, so the Sanhedrin, the court in Jerusalem that tried and condemned him, was a black court too: black Jews in Jerusalem, black scribes and black Pharisees, collaborating with the white Roman oppressor to keep their political and social privileges, just like they do today. Pilate and Herod were the white ones.

  Precepts:
  - **[Acts 4:15-18](/bible/acts/4#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/acts/4#v15)</sup> But when they had commanded them to go aside out of the council, they conferred among themselves,
    >
    > <sup>[16](/bible/acts/4#v16)</sup> Saying, What shall we do to these men? for that indeed a notable miracle hath been done by them is manifest to all them that dwell in Jerusalem; and we cannot deny it.
    >
    > <sup>[17](/bible/acts/4#v17)</sup> But that it spread no further among the people, let us straitly threaten them, that they speak henceforth to no man in this name.
    >
    > <sup>[18](/bible/acts/4#v18)</sup> And they called them, and commanded them not to speak at all nor teach in the name of Jesus.

    “They conferred among themselves, Saying, What shall we do to these men?” That is what they are doing now: conferring among themselves over these men in purple. “For that indeed a notable miracle hath been done by them is manifest to all them that dwell in Jerusalem”, the Israelites, “and we cannot deny it.” They cannot deny what is happening.
  - **[John 3:1-2](/bible/john/3#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/john/3#v1)</sup> There was a man of the Pharisees, named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews:
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/john/3#v2)</sup> The same came to Jesus by night, and said unto him, Rabbi, we know that thou art a teacher come from God: for no man can do these miracles that thou doest, except God be with him.

    Nicodemus was a Pharisee and a follower of Christ: “There was a man of the Pharisees, named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews: The same came to Jesus by night.” Why did he come to Jesus by night? It was a secret. He was a secret follower, but he believed on Christ.
  - **[Luke 23:50-51](/bible/luke/23#v50)**
    > <sup>[50](/bible/luke/23#v50)</sup> And, behold, there was a man named Joseph, a counsellor; and he was a good man, and a just:
    >
    > <sup>[51](/bible/luke/23#v51)</sup> (The same had not consented to the counsel and deed of them;) he was of Arimathea, a city of the Jews: who also himself waited for the kingdom of God.

    Here is another follower of Christ, Joseph of Arimathea. The scriptures don’t mention him being a Pharisee explicitly, but because of his status he was part of many of their councils. “There was a man named Joseph, a counsellor; and he was a good man, and a just: (The same had not consented to the counsel and deed of them;)”. Not every man was allowed into that council, the council of the Pharisees, the Sanhedrin. This lets you know that Joseph of Arimathea had close relations with the Pharisees, yet he was a follower of Christ.

**[John 11:49-52](/bible/john/11#v49)**

> <sup>[49](/bible/john/11#v49)</sup> And one of them, named Caiaphas, being the high priest that same year, said unto them, Ye know nothing at all,
>
> <sup>[50](/bible/john/11#v50)</sup> Nor consider that it is expedient for us, that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not.
>
> <sup>[51](/bible/john/11#v51)</sup> And this spake he not of himself: but being high priest that year, he prophesied that Jesus should die for that nation;
>
> <sup>[52](/bible/john/11#v52)</sup> And not for that nation only, but that also he should gather together in one the children of God that were scattered abroad.

- “That one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not.” “Being high priest that year, he prophesied that Jesus should die for that nation”: these were men raised in the law, so they knew the prophecy of Christ, but they rejected him because they didn’t want Rome to come take their place. There was money to be made from the temple, and every time Christ said the temple would be destroyed they were pissed off; they were envious and covetous of the authority Rome gave them. They knew this man was the Messiah, but they hated him and rejected him.
- Caiaphas had some understanding: “it is expedient for us, that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not.” He knew because the things written of old time were written for our learning. Christ comes in the volume of the book, prophesied to be the lamb to die for the nation.
- Caiaphas, the high priest, said “it is expedient for us, that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not.” Keep in mind the one fold and one shepherd, and the dispersed among the Gentiles.
- “And one of them, named Caiaphas, being the high priest that same year, said unto them”. The black pastor has something to lose with us gaining notoriety; he sees his position being taken from him. There is no separation between church and state: politicians pay the pastors to tell the congregants where to vote. A pastor goes to Israel, comes back, and starts handing out fifty-thousand-dollar checks, and the people look at him like he is the almighty.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ezekiel 37:22](/bible/ezekiel/37#v22)**
    > <sup>[22](/bible/ezekiel/37#v22)</sup> And I will make them one nation in the land upon the mountains of Israel; and one king shall be king to them all: and they shall be no more two nations, neither shall they be divided into two kingdoms any more at all:

    That goes right back to Isaiah 11 and Isaiah 56:8; that was part of the fulfillment. “And I will make them one nation in the land upon the mountains of Israel; and one king shall be king to them all”: that’s Christ the King. “And they shall be no more two nations”: that’s why Caiaphas mentioned not only one nation but those scattered abroad, because we were split into two nations.
