## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on John 7 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 2, 12-18, 33-38, 40-52, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[John 7:2](/bible/john/7#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/john/7#v2)</sup> Now the Jews’ feast of tabernacles was at hand.

- Christ, the walking embodiment of the tabernacle, the glory that was in the tabernacle and dwelt among us while we dwelt in the wilderness, also kept the feast of tabernacles when he was in the flesh: “Now the Jews’ feast of tabernacles was at hand.”
- Our people in Christianity say the commandments are done away with, yet you have Christ keeping Dedication, keeping Passover, keeping tabernacles. What is wrong with you? You want to follow Esau; the white man has become your god.

**[John 7:12-13](/bible/john/7#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/john/7#v12)</sup> And there was much murmuring among the people concerning him: for some said, He is a good man: others said, Nay; but he deceiveth the people.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/john/7#v13)</sup> Howbeit no man spake openly of him for fear of the Jews.

- “There was much murmuring among the people concerning him”: there was division in Israel, because some believed on Christ and some did not. Some said “He is a good man: others said, Nay; but he deceiveth the people.”
- “And there was much murmuring among the people concerning him”: concerning Christ, and concerning us at the 2819 church. “For some said, He is a good man”: these guys are doing a good thing. “Others said, Nay; but he deceiveth the people”: they're full of hate, they teach hate. “Howbeit no man spake openly of him for fear of the Jews”: for fear of their pastor.

**[John 7:14-15](/bible/john/7#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/john/7#v14)</sup> Now about the midst of the feast Jesus went up into the temple, and taught.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/john/7#v15)</sup> And the Jews marvelled, saying, How knoweth this man letters, having never learned?

- “About the midst of the feast Jesus went up into the temple, and taught.” He taught on the feast of tabernacles. The Jews marvelled: how does he know the law, and he did not go to any of our schools, “having never learned?”
- Jesus went up into the temple and taught, “And the Jews marvelled, saying, How knoweth this man letters, having never learned?” What are the letters? The letters were written by the prophets. Isaiah wrote letters, Jeremiah wrote letters; all the prophets wrote letters. And Paul wrote letters. So Paul was a prophet.
- When we say we follow the law and the prophets, it ain’t just Moses and the Old Testament prophets. It’s more than that.

**[John 7:16](/bible/john/7#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/john/7#v16)</sup> Jesus answered them, and said, My doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me.

- “Jesus answered them, and said, My doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me.” Christ was sent. If a man said, I am God, I am the son and I am the spirit, and the father, me, sent me the son down here with me the spirit, the first thing you would do is see if he has Medicaid so he can see a therapist, and slowly back away: start the car.
- “My doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me.” His doctrine is from the Father, the same way our doctrine should be from our Father. When we go out onto the highways and byways, the Most High did not send us to speak our own words. We have to speak the words that are in this Bible: Christ’s words.

**[John 7:17](/bible/john/7#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/john/7#v17)</sup> If any man will do his will, he shall know of the doctrine, whether it be of God, or whether I speak of myself.

- “If any man will do his will, he shall know of the doctrine, whether it be of God, or whether I speak of myself.” If you are keeping his commandments and doing his will, you are going to understand the Bible and understand the doctrine.
- The reason men could not understand Christ and his purpose is that they were open sinners, unrepentant. He was the one that was for to come, spoken of in Deuteronomy, the Psalms and the prophets, and they did not understand it, because they were not doing the will of God.

  Precepts:
  - **[Psalms 40:8](/bible/psalms/40#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/psalms/40#v8)</sup> I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart.

    To prove the will of God, so no one can say it is made up: “I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart.” The will of God is the laws of God. It is a law that is in your heart, and your heart is your mind. The laws of God are in your mind to do his will.

**[John 7:18](/bible/john/7#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/john/7#v18)</sup> He that speaketh of himself seeketh his own glory: but he that seeketh his glory that sent him, the same is true, and no unrighteousness is in him.

- “He that speaketh of himself seeketh his own glory: but he that seeketh his glory that sent him, the same is true, and no unrighteousness is in him.” Christ sought the glory of the one that sent him.

- “Yet a little while am I with you, and then I go unto him that sent me.” Who sent Jesus? The Father, and he sent him to gather the 12 tribes of Israel. Christ did not seek his own will but the will of the Father that sent him.

**[John 7:34-36](/bible/john/7#v34)**

> <sup>[34](/bible/john/7#v34)</sup> Ye shall seek me, and shall not find me: and where I am, thither ye cannot come.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/john/7#v35)</sup> Then said the Jews among themselves, Whither will he go, that we shall not find him? will he go unto the dispersed among the Gentiles, and teach the Gentiles?
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/john/7#v36)</sup> What manner of saying is this that he said, Ye shall seek me, and shall not find me: and where I am, thither ye cannot come?

- Some of those learned men understood. Remember we read earlier in John 10: other sheep I have, which are not of this fold, them also must I bring, that there might be one fold and one shepherd.
- “Then said the Jews among themselves, Whither will he go, that we shall not find him? will he go unto the dispersed among the Gentiles, and teach the Gentiles?” So they had to have understood. The word dispersed is in the Old Testament: Ezekiel is a good one, and Isaiah is the other. We will get to the Gentile part in a second.
- “Ye shall seek me, and shall not find me”. It was already written in Proverbs that they would seek him early and not find him. When Christ, the apostles and the prophets spoke in the New Testament, their words were reiterating what the Old Testament said, and they could substantiate them with the text.
- “Then said the Jews among themselves, Whither will he go, that we shall not find him? will he go unto the dispersed among the Gentiles, and teach the Gentiles?” The Jews asking were the Pharisees and the chief priests, who had “sent officers to take him.”

**[John 7:37-38](/bible/john/7#v37)**

> <sup>[37](/bible/john/7#v37)</sup> In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink.
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/john/7#v38)</sup> He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.

- “In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink. He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.”
- “In the last day, that great day of the feast”: the eighth day. It is called the great day because this feast was one of the three we were commanded to go up to Jerusalem to keep: Passover, the feast of firstfruits (also called Pentecost), and the feast of tabernacles.
- Why would Christ cry “If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink” on the last day of the feast? Because of what was commanded on the feast in Leviticus 23: sacrifices, offerings, water libations, drink offerings, meat offerings.
- “Out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.” When Christ said, drink of me, and spoke of living waters, he was replacing the water libations and the drink offerings by spilling his blood for the nation of Israel.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Kings 20:20](/bible/2-kings/20#v20)**
    > <sup>[20](/bible/2-kings/20#v20)</sup> And the rest of the acts of Hezekiah, and all his might, and how he made a pool, and a conduit, and brought water into the city, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

    “How he made a pool, and a conduit, and brought water into the city”. King Hezekiah made the pool, the pool of Siloam spoken of in John, around 700 BC. He used it to defend Jerusalem against the siege, and the pool was also used for ritual cleansing, especially during the feast.

    > <sup>[5](/bible/john/9#v5)</sup> As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world.
    >
    > <sup>[6](/bible/john/9#v6)</sup> When he had thus spoken, he spat on the ground, and made clay of the spittle, and he anointed the eyes of the blind man with the clay,
    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/john/9#v7)</sup> And said unto him, Go, wash in the pool of Siloam, (which is by interpretation, Sent.) He went his way therefore, and washed, and came seeing.

    “As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world.” Christ made clay of the spittle, anointed the eyes of the blind man, “And said unto him, Go, wash in the pool of Siloam, (which is by interpretation, Sent.) He went his way therefore, and washed, and came seeing.”

    > <sup>[12](/bible/numbers/28#v12)</sup> And three tenth deals of flour for a meat offering, mingled with oil, for one bullock; and two tenth deals of flour for a meat offering, mingled with oil, for one ram;
    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/numbers/28#v13)</sup> And a several tenth deal of flour mingled with oil for a meat offering unto one lamb; for a burnt offering of a sweet savour, a sacrifice made by fire unto the Lord.
    >
    > <sup>[14](/bible/numbers/28#v14)</sup> And their drink offerings shall be half an hin of wine unto a bullock, and the third part of an hin unto a ram, and a fourth part of an hin unto a lamb: this is the burnt offering of every month throughout the months of the year.

    The meat offerings were flour “mingled with oil” for a bullock, a ram and a lamb, “a sacrifice made by fire unto the Lord.” Then the point: “And their drink offerings shall be half an hin of wine unto a bullock, and the third part of an hin unto a ram, and a fourth part of an hin unto a lamb”. We had to offer drink offerings, and Christ, crying come unto me and drink, replaced them.
