## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on John 15 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-8, 18-21, 23, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[John 15:1](/bible/john/15#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/john/15#v1)</sup> I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.

- “I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.” Christ said, I’m the true vine. That goes back to Isaiah 11, the rod out of the stem of Jesse; it goes back to Isaiah 6:13, the holy seed as the substance of the tree. It starts with Christ.
- “I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.” Christ is the true vine and his Father is the husbandman, the same husbandman whose seed we read of in Ezra.
- Going into Passover season, we focus or refocus, and get rid of the spiritual leaven known as sin. “I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.” Christ is the true vine, the root. And we are the Lord's husbandry: his plants, his trees, which in that day will become the trees of righteousness, as Isaiah said.

  Precepts:
  - **[Revelation 22:16](/bible/revelation/22#v16)**
    > <sup>[16](/bible/revelation/22#v16)</sup> I Jesus have sent mine angel to testify unto you these things in the churches. I am the root and the offspring of David, and the bright and morning star.

    A vine is nothing more than a root sprouting through the ground. “I am the root and the offspring of David, and the bright and morning star.” Christ is that root, the true vine.
  - **[1 Corinthians 3:9](/bible/1-corinthians/3#v9)**
    > <sup>[9](/bible/1-corinthians/3#v9)</sup> For we are labourers together with God: ye are God’s husbandry, ye are God’s building.

    The Father is the husbandman, and we are his husbandry: his plants, his trees. “For we are labourers together with God: ye are God’s husbandry, ye are God’s building.”

**[John 15:2](/bible/john/15#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/john/15#v2)</sup> Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away: and every branch that beareth fruit, he purgeth it, that it may bring forth more fruit.

- “Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away”: if you don’t bear fruit in this truth, brothers and sisters, if you want to still be that old scallywag brother, or you want to be that sister who’s a hoe with a w, you’re gonna be taken away. What it means not to bear fruit is spelled out in the works of the flesh (Galatians 5:19-21).
- “And every branch that beareth fruit, he purgeth it, that it may bring forth more fruit.” What do you call it when you purge a tree? You prune it. You trim it so you keep it growing, so it can grow right. That’s our trials, that’s our tribulations, that’s our temptations: that’s the purging, the pruning of us. We want that part, the branch that bears fruit, and the fruit is the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23).
- “Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away.” The Father is going to take it away, and the fruits are the fruits of the spirit.
- “And every branch that beareth fruit, he purgeth it, that it may bring forth more fruit.” To purge is to prune the tree. If you grow tomatoes, to get a lot of fruit you have to get rid of the dead branches and the extra leaves hanging below, because all that extra takes away the nutrients; pruned, the tree gets more water and more nutrients from the earth and brings forth more fruit.

  Precepts:
  - **[Galatians 5:19-21](/bible/galatians/5#v19)**
    > <sup>[19](/bible/galatians/5#v19)</sup> Now the works of the flesh are manifest, which are these; Adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lasciviousness,
    >
    > <sup>[20](/bible/galatians/5#v20)</sup> Idolatry, witchcraft, hatred, variance, emulations, wrath, strife, seditions, heresies,
    >
    > <sup>[21](/bible/galatians/5#v21)</sup> Envyings, murders, drunkenness, revellings, and such like: of the which I tell you before, as I have also told you in time past, that they which do such things shall not inherit the kingdom of God.

    This is what it means when it says “Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away.” If you don’t bear fruit in this truth, this is what you’re doing: “Now the works of the flesh are manifest, which are these; Adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lasciviousness”. Adultery; fornication, that’s still sexual sin.

> <sup>[3](/bible/john/15#v3)</sup> Now ye are clean through the word which I have spoken unto you.

**[John 15:4](/bible/john/15#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/john/15#v4)</sup> Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me.

- “Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me.” That’s why it all starts with Christ. We can’t do nothing without the king.
- “Abide in me, and I in you.” Christ literally abides in us. Every last one of us who has the word of God sown in us, his spirit is in us.
- “As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me.” You cannot become better or grow fruits by yourself; Christ has to be in you. If you are not bearing fruits, not changing, not becoming a better person, maybe the Lord’s spirit is not dwelling in you. You must abide in Christ in order to bear fruit.
- “Abide in me, and I in you.” We have to stay steadfast and continue to abide in Christ: not loving our pleasures, not loving our lust more than Christ, but continuing to do the will of the Father and his Son. “As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me.”

**[John 15:5](/bible/john/15#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/john/15#v5)</sup> I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.

- “I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.” Christ is the vine, you are the branches.
- “I am the vine, ye are the branches.” Christ is the vine and we are the branches. “He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit”: if you believe in Christ and keep the commandments, you are abiding in Christ, his spirit is dwelling in you, and you bring forth much fruit.
- “For without me ye can do nothing.” Without Christ dwelling in us we can’t overcome anything or do anything in this truth. That is why we always give the Lord the glory. Even the teaching given now is the Lord speaking through his servants, not glory for ourselves, and the growth of IUIC and the things we are doing on this earth are the Lord working through us and using us.

Running at any trouble like a damn dog, like a cat, and going back to Christianity shows you don’t know what the fruit of the spirit is. A lion doesn’t run, and neither does a wolf: we conquer.

That is literally what is done with dead branches that wither: they are burned as firewood. If there are no fruits in you and you wither away, God is going to use you for that.
