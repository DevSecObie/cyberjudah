## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on John 9 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-3, 8-11, 14-16, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[John 9:1-2](/bible/john/9#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/john/9#v1)</sup> And as Jesus passed by, he saw a man which was blind from his birth.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/john/9#v2)</sup> And his disciples asked him, saying, Master, who did sin, this man, or his parents, that he was born blind?

- “And as Jesus passed by, he saw a man which was blind from his birth. And his disciples asked him, saying, Master, who did sin, this man, or his parents, that he was born blind?” The disciples were asking Christ about something Christ had taught them: the regeneration, when the Son of man shall sit on the throne of his glory. How could the man commit the sin he was paying for with blindness from birth, if he was not on this earth before? That is proof we have been here before.
- Christ did not say, what are you talking about, or that they were bugging out for asking. He answered it.
- “He saw a man which was blind from his birth.” The disciples asked, “Master, who did sin, this man, or his parents, that he was born blind?” They understood that your actions, or the actions of your parents, can bring evil upon you: in your finances, at your job, in your health.

  Precepts:
  - **[Matthew 23:28-32](/bible/matthew/23#v28)**
    > <sup>[28](/bible/matthew/23#v28)</sup> Even so ye also outwardly appear righteous unto men, but within ye are full of hypocrisy and iniquity.
    >
    > <sup>[29](/bible/matthew/23#v29)</sup> Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! because ye build the tombs of the prophets, and garnish the sepulchres of the righteous,
    >
    > <sup>[30](/bible/matthew/23#v30)</sup> And say, If we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partakers with them in the blood of the prophets.
    >
    > <sup>[31](/bible/matthew/23#v31)</sup> Wherefore ye be witnesses unto yourselves, that ye are the children of them which killed the prophets.
    >
    > <sup>[32](/bible/matthew/23#v32)</sup> Fill ye up then the measure of your fathers.

    The children do pay for the sins of their fathers, because they are their fathers come back. “Even so ye also outwardly appear righteous unto men, but within ye are full of hypocrisy and iniquity”: the Pharisees were wicked as hell. “Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! because ye build the tombs of the prophets, and garnish the sepulchres of the righteous”: they killed the prophets, murdering the Israelites who came to teach Christ’s gospel to the Israelites.
  - **[Exodus 34:7](/bible/exodus/34#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/exodus/34#v7)</sup> Keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will by no means clear the guilty; visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children, and upon the children’s children, unto the third and to the fourth generation.

    “And that will by no means clear the guilty”: the guilty were those who killed the prophets. “Visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children”: the murder of the prophets visited upon the children, who were the Pharisees at that point, “and upon the children’s children, unto the third and to the fourth generation.” That is what Christ meant by fill ye up the measure of your fathers: they were their fathers, doing the killing back then.

**[John 9:3](/bible/john/9#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/john/9#v3)</sup> Jesus answered, Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents: but that the works of God should be made manifest in him.

- “Jesus answered, Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents: but that the works of God should be made manifest.” In this case the man was not born blind because he played with it; the Most High caused him to be born blind so that he could be healed later. The Lord may allow situations to happen anywhere, even in here, so that he can exercise his mercy and his power on them as an example to the rest of us; people may have needed to see that Christ could heal those sicknesses. The Lord has people’s lives plotted out for what he needs them to be.
- But that is not an excuse to do the evil. Don’t be so confident in it that you go out and play with the Most High’s judgment, because you might never even make it in here. We in here know better.
- “Jesus answered, Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents: but that the works of God should be made manifest in him.” Some things are just of the Lord. The Lord has a purpose for everything, and that man’s suffering had nothing to do with sin.
- We have to stop moving like Israel does as soon as somebody catches hell: the brother probably has something going on; the sister’s car broke down, she probably did something. The brother got hurt at work: you better repent, it’s going to be worse next time. Charity thinketh no evil, family. The Lord has his own divine purpose and his own will that we are not privy to.

**[John 9:8-11](/bible/john/9#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/john/9#v8)</sup> The neighbours therefore, and they which before had seen him that he was blind, said, Is not this he that sat and begged?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/john/9#v9)</sup> Some said, This is he: others said, He is like him: but he said, I am he.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/john/9#v10)</sup> Therefore said they unto him, How were thine eyes opened?
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/john/9#v11)</sup> He answered and said, A man that is called Jesus made clay, and anointed mine eyes, and said unto me, Go to the pool of Siloam, and wash: and I went and washed, and I received sight.

- The blind man Christ healed. His neighbors asked, “How were thine eyes opened?” “A man that is called Jesus made clay, and anointed mine eyes”, and he went and washed, and received sight.

**[John 9:14-16](/bible/john/9#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/john/9#v14)</sup> And it was the sabbath day when Jesus made the clay, and opened his eyes.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/john/9#v15)</sup> Then again the Pharisees also asked him how he had received his sight. He said unto them, He put clay upon mine eyes, and I washed, and do see.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/john/9#v16)</sup> Therefore said some of the Pharisees, This man is not of God, because he keepeth not the sabbath day. Others said, How can a man that is a sinner do such miracles? And there was a division among them.

- “And it was the sabbath day when Jesus made the clay, and opened his eyes.” Some of the Pharisees said, “This man is not of God, because he keepeth not the sabbath day.” Others said, “How can a man that is a sinner do such miracles? And there was a division among them.” Division among the people, again, because of him.
