## Introduction

This reading covers [Leviticus 10](/bible/leviticus/10) through 13: the strange fire of Nadab and Abihu and the charge laid on the priesthood, the dietary law of clean and unclean beasts, the law of purification after childbirth, and the law of the plague of leprosy.

**[Leviticus 10:1-2](/bible/leviticus/10#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/leviticus/10#v1)</sup> And Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, took either of them his censer, and put fire therein, and put incense thereon, and offered strange fire before the Lord, which he commanded them not.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/leviticus/10#v2)</sup> And there went out fire from the Lord, and devoured them, and they died before the Lord.

- Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, took either of them his censer, put fire therein and incense thereon, and offered strange fire before the Lord, which he commanded them not; and fire went out from the Lord and devoured them.
- The instructions for the incense were already given: Aaron shall burn sweet incense every morning and at even, a perpetual incense before the Lord throughout your generations, and ye shall offer no strange incense thereon. There were instructions on what could be burned and what could not; the strange fire is that Nadab and Abihu put strange incense before the Lord.
- When the Most High gave instructions through Moses on what to sacrifice and how, it was with specificity; you were to do it exactly the way he told you.
- They were also not the ones authorized to burn the incense; in the tabernacle service you had to be appointed to certain services and could not take a service upon yourself. Zacharias shows the pattern throughout the generations: he executed the priest's office in the order of his course, and his lot, his appointed job, was to burn incense when he went into the temple of the Lord.
- The fire that went out from the Lord was the same fire that came out from before the Lord in [Leviticus 9](/bible/leviticus/9) and consumed the burnt offering upon the altar.

**[Leviticus 10:6](/bible/leviticus/10#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/leviticus/10#v6)</sup> And Moses said unto Aaron, and unto Eleazar and unto Ithamar, his sons, Uncover not your heads, neither rend your clothes; lest ye die, and lest wrath come upon all the people: but let your brethren, the whole house of Israel, bewail the burning which the Lord hath kindled.

- Aaron, Eleazar, and Ithamar were told, uncover not your heads, neither rend your clothes, lest ye die and wrath come upon all the people; basically they were told not to mourn for them, because Nadab and Abihu died in the midst of sin and what they did was abominable to the Most High. The whole house of Israel would bewail the burning which the Lord kindled.
- Ezekiel is the example of that word: the Most High took away the desire of his eyes, his wife, with a stroke, and told him make no mourning for the dead, neither weep, and Ezekiel went and taught the people that morning, and at even his wife died.
- The same standard fell on Jehoiakim the son of Josiah: they shall not lament for him, saying, Ah my brother, or, Ah lord.

**[Leviticus 10:8-11](/bible/leviticus/10#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/leviticus/10#v8)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Aaron, saying,
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/leviticus/10#v9)</sup> Do not drink wine nor strong drink, thou, nor thy sons with thee, when ye go into the tabernacle of the congregation, lest ye die: it shall be a statute for ever throughout your generations:
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/leviticus/10#v10)</sup> And that ye may put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean;
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/leviticus/10#v11)</sup> And that ye may teach the children of Israel all the statutes which the Lord hath spoken unto them by the hand of Moses.

- Some read this and think Aaron and his sons could never drink, but going precept upon precept, this speaks of when they went into the tabernacle of the congregation: they were at work doing the Lord's work, and there was no drinking on the job.
- Ezekiel lays out the priests' work rules. The priests the Levites, the sons of Zadok, kept the charge of the sanctuary; when they entered the gates of the inner court they were clocking in, clothed in linen garments and linen bonnets, girded with nothing that causeth sweat; and going out to the people in the utter court they put off the garments wherein they ministered and put on other garments, like taking off a work uniform.
- Neither shall any priest drink wine when they enter into the inner court; that is exactly what verse 9 speaks of.
- They had to be sober minded because they taught the people the difference between the holy and profane and caused them to discern between the unclean and the clean, and in controversy they stood in judgment, judging according to the judgments of the Most High.
- On the priests' hair: they were not to shave their heads nor suffer their locks to grow long, but only poll their heads. Polling the head means keeping the hair trimmed and maintained; you can grow it, but keep it up.
