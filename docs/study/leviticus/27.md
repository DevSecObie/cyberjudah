**[Leviticus 27:1-2](/bible/leviticus/27#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/leviticus/27#v1)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Moses, saying,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/leviticus/27#v2)</sup> Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When a man shall make a singular vow, the persons shall be for the Lord by thy estimation.

- A singular vow is a man binding himself to the Most High: promising that in exchange for a blessing he will give himself, or what is his, to the service of the Lord. The persons vowed were valued by the estimation of the priest.
- Hannah is the example: in bitterness of soul she vowed that if the Lord gave her a man child she would give him unto the Lord all the days of his life, and that is how the story of Samuel began.
- Christ taught on this because a vow is dangerous: you must pay your vow. Better not to swear at all, but let your communication be yea, yea; that is why we just say Lord's will, Lord's will I get this done.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 Samuel 1:10-11](/bible/1-samuel/1#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/1-samuel/1#v10)</sup> And she was in bitterness of soul, and prayed unto the Lord, and wept sore.
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/1-samuel/1#v11)</sup> And she vowed a vow, and said, O Lord of hosts, if thou wilt indeed look on the affliction of thine handmaid, and remember me, and not forget thine handmaid, but wilt give unto thine handmaid a man child, then I will give him unto the Lord all the days of his life, and there shall no razor come upon his head.

    Hannah vowed a vow: give unto thine handmaid a man child, and I will give him unto the Lord all the days of his life; the pattern of a singular vow.
  - **[Matthew 5:33-37](/bible/matthew/5#v33)**
    > <sup>[33](/bible/matthew/5#v33)</sup> Again, ye have heard that it hath been said by them of old time, Thou shalt not forswear thyself, but shalt perform unto the Lord thine oaths:
    >
    > <sup>[34](/bible/matthew/5#v34)</sup> But I say unto you, Swear not at all; neither by heaven; for it is God’s throne:
    >
    > <sup>[35](/bible/matthew/5#v35)</sup> Nor by the earth; for it is his footstool: neither by Jerusalem; for it is the city of the great King.
    >
    > <sup>[36](/bible/matthew/5#v36)</sup> Neither shalt thou swear by thy head, because thou canst not make one hair white or black.
    >
    > <sup>[37](/bible/matthew/5#v37)</sup> But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil.

    Perform unto the Lord thine oaths; but swear not at all, and let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay.

**[Leviticus 27:3-8](/bible/leviticus/27#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/leviticus/27#v3)</sup> And thy estimation shall be of the male from twenty years old even unto sixty years old, even thy estimation shall be fifty shekels of silver, after the shekel of the sanctuary.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/leviticus/27#v4)</sup> And if it be a female, then thy estimation shall be thirty shekels.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/leviticus/27#v5)</sup> And if it be from five years old even unto twenty years old, then thy estimation shall be of the male twenty shekels, and for the female ten shekels.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/leviticus/27#v6)</sup> And if it be from a month old even unto five years old, then thy estimation shall be of the male five shekels of silver, and for the female thy estimation shall be three shekels of silver.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/leviticus/27#v7)</sup> And if it be from sixty years old and above; if it be a male, then thy estimation shall be fifteen shekels, and for the female ten shekels.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/leviticus/27#v8)</sup> But if he be poorer than thy estimation, then he shall present himself before the priest, and the priest shall value him; according to his ability that vowed shall the priest value him.

- The estimation is the value set for redeeming a person out of the vow. A male from twenty to sixty years old, the prime of life, was fifty shekels of silver after the shekel of the sanctuary, and a female thirty shekels.
- From five years to twenty years old the male was twenty shekels and the female ten; from a month old to five years the male was five shekels and the female three; and from sixty years old and above the male was fifteen shekels and the female ten.
- If the man was poorer than the estimation and could not pay it, he presented himself before the priest, and the priest valued him according to the ability of him that vowed, his worth and what he was able to bring forth.

**[Leviticus 27:9-13](/bible/leviticus/27#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/leviticus/27#v9)</sup> And if it be a beast, whereof men bring an offering unto the Lord, all that any man giveth of such unto the Lord shall be holy.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/leviticus/27#v10)</sup> He shall not alter it, nor change it, a good for a bad, or a bad for a good: and if he shall at all change beast for beast, then it and the exchange thereof shall be holy.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/leviticus/27#v11)</sup> And if it be any unclean beast, of which they do not offer a sacrifice unto the Lord, then he shall present the beast before the priest:
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/leviticus/27#v12)</sup> And the priest shall value it, whether it be good or bad: as thou valuest it, who art the priest, so shall it be.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/leviticus/27#v13)</sup> But if he will at all redeem it, then he shall add a fifth part thereof unto thy estimation.

- If a beast was brought as an offering to the Most High it had to be holy, not blemished; it could not be altered or changed, a good for a bad or a bad for a good, and if it was changed at all, both it and the exchange became holy.
- An unclean beast, one with a blemish that could not be sacrificed, was presented before the priest and the priest valued it, good or bad; and to redeem it, a fifth part had to be added to the estimation to get that beast back.

**[Leviticus 27:14-15](/bible/leviticus/27#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/leviticus/27#v14)</sup> And when a man shall sanctify his house to be holy unto the Lord, then the priest shall estimate it, whether it be good or bad: as the priest shall estimate it, so shall it stand.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/leviticus/27#v15)</sup> And if he that sanctified it will redeem his house, then he shall add the fifth part of the money of thy estimation unto it, and it shall be his.

- A man could sanctify his house to be holy unto the Most High, and the priest estimated it, good or bad, and as the priest estimated it, so it stood.
- To redeem the house he added the fifth part of the money of the estimation: if the house was worth a hundred thousand, he paid that hundred thousand plus the extra twenty thousand, and it was his again.

**[Leviticus 27:16-25](/bible/leviticus/27#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/leviticus/27#v16)</sup> And if a man shall sanctify unto the Lord some part of a field of his possession, then thy estimation shall be according to the seed thereof: an homer of barley seed shall be valued at fifty shekels of silver.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/leviticus/27#v17)</sup> If he sanctify his field from the year of jubile, according to thy estimation it shall stand.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/leviticus/27#v18)</sup> But if he sanctify his field after the jubile, then the priest shall reckon unto him the money according to the years that remain, even unto the year of the jubile, and it shall be abated from thy estimation.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/leviticus/27#v19)</sup> And if he that sanctified the field will in any wise redeem it, then he shall add the fifth part of the money of thy estimation unto it, and it shall be assured to him.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/leviticus/27#v20)</sup> And if he will not redeem the field, or if he have sold the field to another man, it shall not be redeemed any more.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/leviticus/27#v21)</sup> But the field, when it goeth out in the jubile, shall be holy unto the Lord, as a field devoted; the possession thereof shall be the priest’s.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/leviticus/27#v22)</sup> And if a man sanctify unto the Lord a field which he hath bought, which is not of the fields of his possession;
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/leviticus/27#v23)</sup> Then the priest shall reckon unto him the worth of thy estimation, even unto the year of the jubile: and he shall give thine estimation in that day, as a holy thing unto the Lord.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/leviticus/27#v24)</sup> In the year of the jubile the field shall return unto him of whom it was bought, even to him to whom the possession of the land did belong.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/leviticus/27#v25)</sup> And all thy estimations shall be according to the shekel of the sanctuary: twenty gerahs shall be the shekel.

- A field sanctified to the Most High was estimated according to the seed thereof, an homer of barley seed at fifty shekels of silver; from the year of jubile the estimation stood full, but after the jubile the priest reckoned the money according to the years that remained, and it was abated from the estimation.
- The jubile was the year of release, when the land had to rest, so the value was reckoned by how close the jubile was: the fewer years left to reap the field, the lower the estimation. It is like trading in a car; a two year old car with twenty thousand miles brings more value than an old car with a hundred and fifty thousand miles on it.
- To redeem a sanctified field the fifth part was added to the estimation. A field not redeemed, or sold to another man, could not be redeemed any more, but went out in the jubile holy unto the Lord, a devoted field, the possession of the priest; and in the year of jubile a bought field returned to him of whom it was bought.
- All the estimations were according to the shekel of the sanctuary, twenty gerahs to the shekel; that was the standard, the set price.

**[Leviticus 27:26-29](/bible/leviticus/27#v26)**

> <sup>[26](/bible/leviticus/27#v26)</sup> Only the firstling of the beasts, which should be the Lord’s firstling, no man shall sanctify it; whether it be ox, or sheep: it is the Lord’s.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/leviticus/27#v27)</sup> And if it be of an unclean beast, then he shall redeem it according to thine estimation, and shall add a fifth part of it thereto: or if it be not redeemed, then it shall be sold according to thy estimation.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/leviticus/27#v28)</sup> Notwithstanding no devoted thing, that a man shall devote unto the Lord of all that he hath, both of man and beast, and of the field of his possession, shall be sold or redeemed: every devoted thing is most holy unto the Lord.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/leviticus/27#v29)</sup> None devoted, which shall be devoted of men, shall be redeemed; but shall surely be put to death.

- The firstling of the beasts could not be sanctified by any man, because as a firstling it was the Lord's already, whether ox or sheep; an unclean firstling was redeemed by the estimation with the fifth part added, or sold according to the estimation.
- No devoted thing that a man devoted unto the Most High, of man, beast, or field of his possession, could be sold or redeemed: every devoted thing is most holy unto the Lord, and a devoted man was surely put to death.

**[Leviticus 27:30-34](/bible/leviticus/27#v30)**

> <sup>[30](/bible/leviticus/27#v30)</sup> And all the tithe of the land, whether of the seed of the land, or of the fruit of the tree, is the Lord’s: it is holy unto the Lord.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/leviticus/27#v31)</sup> And if a man will at all redeem ought of his tithes, he shall add thereto the fifth part thereof.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/leviticus/27#v32)</sup> And concerning the tithe of the herd, or of the flock, even of whatsoever passeth under the rod, the tenth shall be holy unto the Lord.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/leviticus/27#v33)</sup> He shall not search whether it be good or bad, neither shall he change it: and if he change it at all, then both it and the change thereof shall be holy; it shall not be redeemed.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/leviticus/27#v34)</sup> These are the commandments, which the Lord commanded Moses for the children of Israel in mount Sinai.

- All the tithe of the land, of the seed of the land or the fruit of the tree, is the Lord's; it is holy unto the Lord. To redeem out of the tithes a man added the fifth part thereto.
- The tithe of the herd and the flock, the tenth of whatsoever passeth under the rod, was holy unto the Lord; he was not to search whether it was good or bad or change it, and if he changed it at all, both it and the change were holy and could not be redeemed.
- The quick recap of the chapter: when a brother or sister vowed themselves to the Most High, the redemption from that vow went according to the estimation, by age and by sex, and likewise a house or a field; all these things go into how the priest calculated the estimation of these particular things to release you from the vow. The chapter can be tricky, so read it over and over.
