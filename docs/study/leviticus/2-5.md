## Introduction

This reading covers [Leviticus 2](/bible/leviticus/2) through 5: the meat offering of fine flour, the peace offering without blemish, the sin offering for sins of ignorance, and the trespass offering. Blessed is he that readeth, and they that hear the words of this prophecy ([Revelation 1:3](/bible/revelation/1#v3)); leadership was in the spirit to allow the reading of four chapters a day, recording it and touching on everything to break down the word. Leviticus goes into the Levitical laws: the laws of sacrifice, the moral laws, the civil laws, the ceremonial laws. Meditate on the milk, the first five books. The pastors of the world tell our people not to read the Old Testament, that it is done away with, but there is a lot of symbolic prophecy in these first five books that must be known in order to understand the New Testament and to understand Christ.

**[Leviticus 2:1-3](/bible/leviticus/2#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/leviticus/2#v1)</sup> And when any will offer a meat offering unto the Lord, his offering shall be of fine flour; and he shall pour oil upon it, and put frankincense thereon:
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/leviticus/2#v2)</sup> And he shall bring it to Aaron’s sons the priests: and he shall take thereout his handful of the flour thereof, and of the oil thereof, with all the frankincense thereof; and the priest shall burn the memorial of it upon the altar, to be an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the Lord:
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/leviticus/2#v3)</sup> And the remnant of the meat offering shall be Aaron’s and his sons’: it is a thing most holy of the offerings of the Lord made by fire.

- The meat offering is of fine flour, with oil poured upon it and frankincense put thereon; the priest burns his handful of it as a memorial upon the altar, an offering made by fire of a sweet savour unto the Lord, and the remnant belongs to Aaron and his sons.
- Five primary offerings run through these chapters. The burnt offering, usually a male burnt at the altar, with the skin for the priests, was glorified by the Most High. The meal offering, the one read here, was made of fine flour with oil and frankincense; no leaven and no honey were permitted, it had to be seasoned with salt, and it was baked in an oven, a pan, or a frying pan.
- The peace offering could be either male or female: the blood, the fat, and the kidneys were the Lord's, and the breast and the right shoulder were for the priest. The sin offering was blood and fat on the altar, with the bullock burned without the camp, for the judgment of sin. The trespass offering was for atonement and forgiveness, the blood and fat of a ram on the altar, with the principal restored and the fifth part added. There are also wave offerings and heave offerings, but these five are the primary offerings.

- [Leviticus 2:7](/bible/leviticus/2#v7) always goes viral on social media, as if the meat offering baken in the frying pan relates to fried chicken. That is not what it is talking about; be mindful of learning from posts instead of from the scriptures.
- The chapter itself defines the meat: go back and read verse 1, and the offering is of fine flour, not of fried chicken. When you have questions, go back to the beginning verses of the chapter, and you get the true understanding.
- Meat in the scriptures is not always talking about animals. Every tree yielding seed was given for meat; the inside of a fruit is called the meat of that fruit, the meat of an orange, an apple, a pear, a watermelon.

- No meat offering brought unto the Lord shall be made with leaven, and no honey in any offering made by fire. This is where the Passover and Christ come in: it was all a shadow of things to come, just as all leaven is put out when the Passover comes around. The leaven is read about more in chapter 3.

**[Leviticus 2:12](/bible/leviticus/2#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/leviticus/2#v12)</sup> As for the oblation of the firstfruits, ye shall offer them unto the Lord: but they shall not be burnt on the altar for a sweet savour.

- The oblation of the firstfruits is offered unto the Lord but not burnt on the altar for a sweet savour. Firstfruits is another holy day, with the Pentecost; all of it was symbolic.
- Cain and Abel show both kinds of offering. Cain was a tiller of the ground, part of the agriculture, and brought of the fruit of the ground, the firstfruits; Abel brought of the firstlings of his flock and of the fat thereof. The Lord had respect unto Abel and to his offering, but unto Cain and to his offering he had not respect.
- Sacrifices are symbolic of your works. The Most High knows how much you can do for this truth, whether you are being slack, slothful, and cheap, or going above and beyond; Abel went above and beyond, and Cain was mediocre. Our sacrifices today are keeping the commandments, the work we put in, the alms we give, and the time we sacrifice.

# Leviticus 3: The Peace Offering Without Blemish

> <sup>[1](/bible/leviticus/3#v1)</sup> And if his oblation be a sacrifice of peace offering, if he offer it of the herd; whether it be a male or female, he shall offer it without blemish before the Lord.

- The peace offering of the herd, whether male or female, must be offered without blemish before the Lord: no bruise, no cut, no stain, nothing superfluous like an extra member. Whatever the sacrifice is for the Lord, it has to be the best of the best, a flawless sacrifice.
- The Passover lamb shows how that was carried out. The lamb was without blemish, a male of the first year, taken out from the sheep and kept from the goats, because if it got in a fight it would take a blemish, a bruise, a cut. It was kept up to the fourteenth day, isolated and given nothing but water, so that it purified itself; and it was eaten roast with fire, not raw and not sodden with water, with the purtenance thereof, for after those days there was nothing left within it.

**[Leviticus 3:17](/bible/leviticus/3#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/leviticus/3#v17)</sup> It shall be a perpetual statute for your generations throughout all your dwellings, that ye eat neither fat nor blood.

- It shall be a perpetual statute that ye eat neither fat nor blood. This is a controversial scripture: brothers and sisters ask whether we are cut off for eating fat. Having that question is a good thing; it means you are reading to understand, not reading just to say you read four chapters.
- [Leviticus 7](/bible/leviticus/7) answers it in context: whosoever eateth the fat of the beast of which men offer an offering made by fire unto the Lord shall be cut off. This is the law of sacrifice, which we are no longer under; Christ is the end of the law for righteousness. The sacrifices stopped after 70 AD at the fall of Jerusalem, when there was no longer a temple. And there is no way to take off all the fat of the meat you eat; we are not koshering our meat.

- The sin offering is for a soul that sins through ignorance against any of the commandments. You sin through ignorance when you get complacent: it was a double Sabbath and you forgot, and you bought or sold something you were not supposed to; an honest mistake. Even then it was not a verbal sorry; you literally brought an offering for your mistake.
- The offering was your collateral in those times. There were no dollar bills; being rich meant land, cattle, flocks, and agriculture, what the scriptures call milk and honey, as with Job, who was rich in land and livestock. Your sins cost you out of that substance.
- Even the priest that is anointed committed sin, according to the sin of the people, and had to bring a young bullock without blemish unto the Lord for a sin offering. The priests went always into the first tabernacle accomplishing the service of God, but the high priest went alone once every year into the second, not without blood, which he offered for himself and for the errors of the people.
- The people got redundant with it: every priest stood daily offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins. They kept sinning and kept bringing sacrifices instead of repenting, thinking it was okay to sin as long as the offerings kept coming. It is the same spirit today, wicked all weekend and a hundred dollars in the plate on Sunday; the Most High does not want your money, he wants you to repent. Only you can put away your sins, when you repent and stop sinning.
- But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God. Christ took away the first covenant to establish the second, and we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all. Men had been sacrificing since the days of Adam; when Christ came, one sacrifice ended it, because that lamb had no blemish. The law of sacrifice was always temporary, a shadow of good things to come, and the good thing that came was Christ.

A soft answer turneth away wrath; you do not render evil for evil and blast a brother, you build him up with a humble mind, and that is how souls are won. **[Leviticus 5:2-3](/bible/leviticus/5#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/leviticus/5#v2)</sup> Or if a soul touch any unclean thing, whether it be a carcase of an unclean beast, or a carcase of unclean cattle, or the carcase of unclean creeping things, and if it be hidden from him; he also shall be unclean, and guilty.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/leviticus/5#v3)</sup> Or if he touch the uncleanness of man, whatsoever uncleanness it be that a man shall be defiled withal, and it be hid from him; when he knoweth of it, then he shall be guilty.

- Touching any unclean thing, the carcase of an unclean beast or the uncleanness of man, leaves a soul unclean and guilty. This goes into eating, not mere contact; the classes have touched on this already, and it stands.
- It is the same as telling your son or daughter, get your hand out of the cookie jar: you say do not touch it, not do not eat it, because you know what the intention is. The Lord deals with us the same way; do not be touching that stuff means do not eat it, do not defile yourself.
- The touching itself pertained to the sacrifice: Israel was separated from their uncleanness that they die not when they defile the tabernacle, so a man that had touched the unclean thing could not come in to sacrifice. There is no more sacrifice, so the touching-defilement laws do not apply today; a brush of boar bristles is not sin.

  Precepts:
  - **[Leviticus 15:31](/bible/leviticus/15#v31)**

    Thus shall ye separate the children of Israel from their uncleanness, that they die not, when they defile my tabernacle; the defilement laws guarded the sacrifice.

When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it, for better is it that thou shouldest not vow than that thou shouldest vow and not pay; suffer not thy mouth to cause thy flesh to sin. - The offering scaled to what a man could bring: if he be not able to bring a lamb, two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering; and if he be not able to bring those, the tenth part of an ephah of fine flour, with no oil and no frankincense upon it, for it is a sin offering.
- The offerings were the collateral of those times, and families felt the after-effects of always sacrificing: the livestock and the agriculture went for your sins, and the household lacked because of it.
- Israel took the nourishment of the Lord for granted, and he grew tired of the empty offerings: put your burnt offerings unto your sacrifices, and eat flesh. He spake not unto our fathers concerning burnt offerings, but this thing commanded he: obey my voice, and I will be your God, and ye shall be my people. He never wanted the sacrifices above obedience; today he does not want an apology or money in the plate, he wants you to obey his voice and repent.
