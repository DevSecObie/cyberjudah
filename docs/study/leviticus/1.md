## Introduction

This chapter was read in the same session as [Exodus 38](/bible/exodus/38) through 40. Leviticus comes from Levitical, whose root word is Levi: the Levites were given the priesthood of the Lord, so this book deals with the laws and the priesthood. Chapter 1 lays out the three categories of the burnt offering, of the herd, of the flocks, and of fowls. Knowing the distinctions of the offerings is not what saves you, but it is your heritage and your history, and you should not be ignorant of it.

**[Leviticus 1:1-3](/bible/leviticus/1#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/leviticus/1#v1)</sup> And the Lord called unto Moses, and spake unto him out of the tabernacle of the congregation, saying,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/leviticus/1#v2)</sup> Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, If any man of you bring an offering unto the Lord, ye shall bring your offering of the cattle, even of the herd, and of the flock.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/leviticus/1#v3)</sup> If his offering be a burnt sacrifice of the herd, let him offer a male without blemish: he shall offer it of his own voluntary will at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation before the Lord.

- The Lord called unto Moses out of the tabernacle of the congregation, the tent just reared up at the end of [Exodus 40](/bible/exodus/40), and gave the law of the offerings brought of the cattle, of the herd and of the flock.
- The burnt sacrifice of the herd is of the cattle, such as the bullock and the heifer. It had to be a male without blemish, offered of his own voluntary will at the door of the tabernacle.
- In the book of Malachi the priests were finessing and corrupting this law: a man would bring a male without blemish, and the priest would keep it and sacrifice a blemished one, with a broken leg or a blind eye. That corruption is even more of a reason why Christ had to come and be the perfect sacrifice once and for all.

  Precepts:
  - **[Malachi 1:14](/bible/malachi/1#v14)**
> <sup>[14](/bible/malachi/1#v14)</sup> But cursed be the deceiver, which hath in his flock a male, and voweth, and sacrificeth unto the Lord a corrupt thing: for I am a great King, saith the Lord of hosts, and my name is dreadful among the heathen.

    Cursed be the deceiver which hath in his flock a male, and sacrificeth unto the Lord a corrupt thing; the priests corrupted the law of the unblemished male.

**[Leviticus 1:4-9](/bible/leviticus/1#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/leviticus/1#v4)</sup> And he shall put his hand upon the head of the burnt offering; and it shall be accepted for him to make atonement for him.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/leviticus/1#v5)</sup> And he shall kill the bullock before the Lord: and the priests, Aaron’s sons, shall bring the blood, and sprinkle the blood round about upon the altar that is by the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/leviticus/1#v6)</sup> And he shall flay the burnt offering, and cut it into his pieces.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/leviticus/1#v7)</sup> And the sons of Aaron the priest shall put fire upon the altar, and lay the wood in order upon the fire:
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/leviticus/1#v8)</sup> And the priests, Aaron’s sons, shall lay the parts, the head, and the fat, in order upon the wood that is on the fire which is upon the altar:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/leviticus/1#v9)</sup> But his inwards and his legs shall he wash in water: and the priest shall burn all on the altar, to be a burnt sacrifice, an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the Lord.

- He put his hand upon the head of the burnt offering, and it was accepted for him to make an atonement for him; the bullock was killed before the Lord, the blood sprinkled round about upon the altar by the priests, and the parts laid in order upon the wood on the fire.
- Notice the fat is laid on the altar. Do not stumble when chapter 3 says not to eat fat: that deals with the fat of the sacrifice that the priest sent up in the offering, not the fat on a steak off your grill.
- The offering made by fire is a sweet savour unto the Lord. Christ walked in love and gave himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour: he fulfilled and replaced all these offerings, once and for all, in his death and resurrection.
- This is the context so much of Paul's letters sit in: he was convincing the congregations that they no longer had to sacrifice animals, because Pharisees were creeping in trying to turn them back to those things; therefore let no man judge you in meat or in drink.
- In our captivity, with no Levitical priesthood and no temple, keeping the commandments is the offering and the reasonable service: he that keepeth the law bringeth offerings enough, and we present our bodies a living sacrifice, sacrificing the lusts and pleasures of the flesh to follow the footsteps of Christ. And [Ezekiel 20](/bible/ezekiel/20) shows that when Israel is brought back through the wilderness, offerings will be required again in the holy mountain.

  Precepts:
  - **[Ephesians 5:2](/bible/ephesians/5#v2)**
> <sup>[2](/bible/ephesians/5#v2)</sup> And walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour.

    Christ gave himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour; he is the sacrifice once for all.
  - **[Colossians 2:16](/bible/colossians/2#v16)**
> <sup>[16](/bible/colossians/2#v16)</sup> Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days:

    Let no man judge you in meat, or in drink; Paul defended the congregations from those pushing them back to the sacrifices.
  - **[Ecclesiasticus 35:1](/bible/sirach/35#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/35#v1)</sup> He that keepeth the law bringeth offerings enough: he that taketh heed to the commandment offereth a peace offering.

    Keeping the law brings offerings enough; that is our offering with no temple standing.
  - **[Romans 12:1](/bible/romans/12#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/romans/12#v1)</sup> I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.

    Present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.
  - **[Ezekiel 20:40](/bible/ezekiel/20#v40)**
> <sup>[40](/bible/ezekiel/20#v40)</sup> For in mine holy mountain, in the mountain of the height of Israel, saith the Lord God, there shall all the house of Israel, all of them in the land, serve me: there will I accept them, and there will I require your offerings, and the firstfruits of your oblations, with all your holy things.

    In the holy mountain, at the return, all Israel shall serve the Most High, and there He will require the offerings again.

**[Leviticus 1:10-13](/bible/leviticus/1#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/leviticus/1#v10)</sup> And if his offering be of the flocks, namely, of the sheep, or of the goats, for a burnt sacrifice; he shall bring it a male without blemish.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/leviticus/1#v11)</sup> And he shall kill it on the side of the altar northward before the Lord: and the priests, Aaron’s sons, shall sprinkle his blood round about upon the altar.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/leviticus/1#v12)</sup> And he shall cut it into his pieces, with his head and his fat: and the priest shall lay them in order on the wood that is on the fire which is upon the altar:
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/leviticus/1#v13)</sup> But he shall wash the inwards and the legs with water: and the priest shall bring it all, and burn it upon the altar: it is a burnt sacrifice, an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the Lord.

- The second category is the burnt sacrifice of the flocks: the sheep and the goats, a male without blemish, killed on the side of the altar northward before the Lord, the pieces laid in order and washed, and all burnt upon the altar, a sweet savour unto the Lord.

**[Leviticus 1:14-17](/bible/leviticus/1#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/leviticus/1#v14)</sup> And if the burnt sacrifice for his offering to the Lord be of fowls, then he shall bring his offering of turtledoves, or of young pigeons.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/leviticus/1#v15)</sup> And the priest shall bring it unto the altar, and wring off his head, and burn it on the altar; and the blood thereof shall be wrung out at the side of the altar:
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/leviticus/1#v16)</sup> And he shall pluck away his crop with his feathers, and cast it beside the altar on the east part, by the place of the ashes:
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/leviticus/1#v17)</sup> And he shall cleave it with the wings thereof, but shall not divide it asunder: and the priest shall burn it upon the altar, upon the wood that is upon the fire: it is a burnt sacrifice, an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the Lord.

- The third category is the burnt sacrifice of fowls, meaning birds: turtledoves or young pigeons. That is the offering meant when a woman after childbirth brings two turtledoves or two young pigeons, as in [Leviticus 12](/bible/leviticus/12).

  Precepts:
  - **[Leviticus 12:8](/bible/leviticus/12#v8)**
> <sup>[8](/bible/leviticus/12#v8)</sup> And if she be not able to bring a lamb, then she shall bring two turtles, or two young pigeons; the one for the burnt offering, and the other for a sin offering: and the priest shall make an atonement for her, and she shall be clean.

    If she be not able to bring a lamb, she shall bring two turtles or two young pigeons; the offering of fowls served her purifying.
