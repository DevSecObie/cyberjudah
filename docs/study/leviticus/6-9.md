## Introduction

This reading covers [Leviticus 6](/bible/leviticus/6) through 9: the trespass offering and restitution, the laws of the burnt, meat, sin, trespass, and peace offerings, the consecration of Aaron and his sons, and the eighth day when the glory of the Lord appeared to all the people. Leviticus is straightforward like the rest of the law, and these chapters pertain heavily to the book of Hebrews: the law of sacrifice is the volume of the book that is written of Christ, and once you understand the law you begin to understand the letters of Paul. The session opened by backtracking into [Leviticus 5](/bible/leviticus/5) on the witness law, unclean things, and oaths; that teaching belongs to the [Leviticus 5](/bible/leviticus/5) chapter set and is logged there.

**[Leviticus 6:1-5](/bible/leviticus/6#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/leviticus/6#v1)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Moses, saying,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/leviticus/6#v2)</sup> If a soul sin, and commit a trespass against the Lord, and lie unto his neighbour in that which was delivered him to keep, or in fellowship, or in a thing taken away by violence, or hath deceived his neighbour;
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/leviticus/6#v3)</sup> Or have found that which was lost, and lieth concerning it, and sweareth falsely; in any of all these that a man doeth, sinning therein:
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/leviticus/6#v4)</sup> Then it shall be, because he hath sinned, and is guilty, that he shall restore that which he took violently away, or the thing which he hath deceitfully gotten, or that which was delivered him to keep, or the lost thing which he found,
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/leviticus/6#v5)</sup> Or all that about which he hath sworn falsely; he shall even restore it in the principal, and shall add the fifth part more thereto, and give it unto him to whom it appertaineth, in the day of his trespass offering.

- If a soul lie unto his neighbour in that which was delivered him to keep, or in fellowship, or deceive his neighbour, or find that which was lost and lie concerning it, and swear falsely, the Lord counts all of it as a thing taken away by violence: verse 4 says he shall restore that which he took violently away. Deceiving your neighbour, going into agreement and not holding up your end, or lying about what was given you to hold is a violent act toward your neighbour, and a Ponzi scheme or pyramid scheme sits right in that law.
- He shall restore it in the principal: the value of what was taken had to be given back, with more added on top. A sorry would not settle it with the Lord; you could end up in debt for years over sticky fingers.
- Stealing was not a sin unto death, but the Lord made the restitution heavy so you would never commit it again. It goes back to the commandment, thou shalt not steal, and to the civil law where an ox was restored fivefold and a sheep fourfold.

**[Leviticus 6:6-7](/bible/leviticus/6#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/leviticus/6#v6)</sup> And he shall bring his trespass offering unto the Lord, a ram without blemish out of the flock, with thy estimation, for a trespass offering, unto the priest:

- The trespass offering was a ram without blemish, perfect as the Most High made it, and the priest made an atonement for him before the Lord and it was forgiven him. The sacrifice stood in your place for your sin.
- The whole law of sacrifice points to Christ, who came in the volume of the book. He is the offering without blemish and the atonement, and who has become the high priest is Jesus the Christ.
- Israel became the enemy of the Most High by sin. There are two kinds of enemies: the nations that are the natural enemies of the Most High, and Israel, who rebelled and vexed his holy spirit, and he was turned to be their enemy. Through the death of his Son the nation is reconciled, brought back together with its God.
- The letters of Paul were written to Israelites living in those lands and called by their names, Romans and Corinthians, the same way we are called American or Jamaican today; written today they would be the letter to the Jamaicans or the letter to the Puerto Ricans.

**[Leviticus 6:8-9](/bible/leviticus/6#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/leviticus/6#v8)</sup> And the Lord spake unto Moses, saying,
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/leviticus/6#v9)</sup> Command Aaron and his sons, saying, This is the law of the burnt offering: It is the burnt offering, because of the burning upon the altar all night unto the morning, and the fire of the altar shall be burning in it.

- The burnt offering burned upon the altar all night unto the morning, and the fire of the altar was burning in it. When Exodus says kindle no fire on the sabbath day, that speaks of making food; the Levites were commanded to keep a fire burning on the altar continually, sabbath included.
- Christ used this very law on the Pharisees: have ye not read how that on the sabbath days the priests in the temple profane the sabbath, and are blameless? The priests had fire going in the temple every sabbath by commandment.
- I will have mercy, and not sacrifice: the Most High desired the knowledge of God and obedience more than burnt offerings. Israel leaned so heavily on the sacrifices instead of applying the commandments that they were confounded and could not see Christ as the Son of God when he came.
- Jeremiah carries the same word: I spake not unto your fathers concerning burnt offerings or sacrifices, but this thing commanded I them, obey my voice. Yet they hearkened not, but walked in the counsels of their evil heart and went backward and not forward; speak all these words unto them, but they will not hearken. That is seen every sabbath on the streets.

Unless the Spirit of the Lord deals with you, you will never understand the Bible or the laws; that is why there was such contention between Christ and the Pharisees, for the Spirit had not revealed to them who he was.

>
> <sup>[17](/bible/leviticus/7#v17)</sup> But the remainder of the flesh of the sacrifice on the third day shall be burnt with fire.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/leviticus/7#v18)</sup> And if any of the flesh of the sacrifice of his peace offerings be eaten at all on the third day, it shall not be accepted, neither shall it be imputed unto him that offereth it: it shall be an abomination, and the soul that eateth of it shall bear his iniquity.

- This is the law of the sacrifice of peace offerings. If it was offered for a thanksgiving, the flesh was eaten the same day it was offered; none was left until the morning.
- If the sacrifice was a vow or a voluntary offering, it was eaten the same day and the remainder on the morrow also; that is the difference between the two. But flesh remaining on the third day was burnt with fire, and if any was eaten on the third day it was an abomination, and the offering was not accepted.

- For today the LORD will appear unto you: they brought what Moses commanded before the tabernacle, and all the congregation drew near and stood before the LORD, the whole nation gathered in front of the tabernacle.

**[Leviticus 9:7-14](/bible/leviticus/9#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/leviticus/9#v7)</sup> And Moses said unto Aaron, Go unto the altar, and offer thy sin offering, and thy burnt offering, and make an atonement for thyself, and for the people: and offer the offering of the people, and make an atonement for them; as the Lord commanded.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/leviticus/9#v8)</sup> Aaron therefore went unto the altar, and slew the calf of the sin offering, which was for himself.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/leviticus/9#v9)</sup> And the sons of Aaron brought the blood unto him: and he dipped his finger in the blood, and put it upon the horns of the altar, and poured out the blood at the bottom of the altar:
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/leviticus/9#v10)</sup> But the fat, and the kidneys, and the caul above the liver of the sin offering, he burnt upon the altar; as the Lord commanded Moses.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/leviticus/9#v11)</sup> And the flesh and the hide he burnt with fire without the camp.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/leviticus/9#v12)</sup> And he slew the burnt offering; and Aaron’s sons presented unto him the blood, which he sprinkled round about upon the altar.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/leviticus/9#v13)</sup> And they presented the burnt offering unto him, with the pieces thereof, and the head: and he burnt them upon the altar.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/leviticus/9#v14)</sup> And he did wash the inwards and the legs, and burnt them upon the burnt offering on the altar.

- Moses said unto Aaron, go unto the altar, and offer thy sin offering and thy burnt offering, and make an atonement for thyself, and for the people. The priest had to make an atonement for himself first, and then offer the offering of the people.
- That is the order Hebrews opens up: the high priests of the law needed daily to offer up sacrifice first for their own sins and then for the people's; but Christ, the high priest after the order of Melchisedec, is holy, harmless, and undefiled, and offered up himself once.
- Aaron dipped his finger in the blood and put it upon the horns of the altar, burnt the fat and kidneys and caul, the guts, upon the altar, and the flesh and the hide he burnt with fire without the camp; you keep reading it over and over, without the camp.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hebrews 7:26-27](/bible/hebrews/7#v26)**
    > <sup>[26](/bible/hebrews/7#v26)</sup> For such an high priest became us, who is holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and made higher than the heavens;
    >
    > <sup>[27](/bible/hebrews/7#v27)</sup> Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people’s: for this he did once, when he offered up himself.

    Such an high priest, holy, harmless, undefiled, who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people's: for this he did once, when he offered up himself.

**[Leviticus 9:15-21](/bible/leviticus/9#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/leviticus/9#v15)</sup> And he brought the people’s offering, and took the goat, which was the sin offering for the people, and slew it, and offered it for sin, as the first.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/leviticus/9#v16)</sup> And he brought the burnt offering, and offered it according to the manner.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/leviticus/9#v17)</sup> And he brought the meat offering, and took an handful thereof, and burnt it upon the altar, beside the burnt sacrifice of the morning.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/leviticus/9#v18)</sup> He slew also the bullock and the ram for a sacrifice of peace offerings, which was for the people: and Aaron’s sons presented unto him the blood, which he sprinkled upon the altar round about,
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/leviticus/9#v19)</sup> And the fat of the bullock and of the ram, the rump, and that which covereth the inwards, and the kidneys, and the caul above the liver:
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/leviticus/9#v20)</sup> And they put the fat upon the breasts, and he burnt the fat upon the altar:
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/leviticus/9#v21)</sup> And the breasts and the right shoulder Aaron waved for a wave offering before the Lord; as Moses commanded.

- Aaron brought the people's offering: the goat of the sin offering was slain and offered for sin as the first, the burnt offering was offered according to the manner, and he took an handful of the meat offering, the grain, and burnt it upon the altar.
- He slew the bullock and the ram for a sacrifice of peace offerings for the people, the word read in Ephesians of Christ our peace, and the blood was sprinkled upon the altar round about, the blood the law keeps returning to.
- The breasts and the right shoulder Aaron waved for a wave offering before the LORD, a washed piece lifted and waved, as Moses commanded.

The whole nation standing before the tabernacle saw the Most High answer the offering with fire.
