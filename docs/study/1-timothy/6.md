## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 1 Timothy 6 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 6-12, 17-19, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[1 Timothy 6:6](/bible/1-timothy/6#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/1-timothy/6#v6)</sup> But godliness with contentment is great gain.

- “But godliness with contentment is great gain.” That little verse will save your life. Be content with the things the Lord gives you.

**[1 Timothy 6:7](/bible/1-timothy/6#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/1-timothy/6#v7)</sup> For we brought nothing into this world, and it is certain we can carry nothing out.

- “For we brought nothing into this world, and it is certain we can carry nothing out.” You come in naked, and the only thing you get when you leave, depending on where and how you die, is a coffin and a garment. You are taking nothing with you, so why would you mess up your eternal life for money, cars and houses, vain things that do not profit?

**[1 Timothy 6:8](/bible/1-timothy/6#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-timothy/6#v8)</sup> And having food and raiment let us be therewith content.

- “And having food and raiment let us be therewith content.” The covetous man says, I've got food and clothes, but I need money for all the new clothes; I'm not happy with the little I have; I want everything right now at any expense.

**[1 Timothy 6:9](/bible/1-timothy/6#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/1-timothy/6#v9)</sup> But they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare, and into many foolish and hurtful lusts, which drown men in destruction and perdition.

- Back to the Boulé. “But they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare”, a trap, “and into many foolish and hurtful lusts, which drown men in destruction and perdition.” Ask Diddy. Ask R. Kelly.
- “But they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare, and into many foolish and hurtful lusts, which drown men in destruction and perdition.” If all you want to do is get paid, you are going to die. Like 50 said, get rich or die trying: you're going to die.

**[1 Timothy 6:10](/bible/1-timothy/6#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/1-timothy/6#v10)</sup> For the love of money is the root of all evil: which while some coveted after, they have erred from the faith, and pierced themselves through with many sorrows.

- “For the love of money is the root of all evil.” Tell the Boulé and the bourgeoisie that, because they love money. “Which while some coveted after”: that’s the problem, they coveted after it, “they have erred from the faith”, from the faith of Christ, “and pierced themselves through with many sorrows.”
- “For the love of money is the root of all evil.” If you love money too much you will do anything for it. If you rob and steal and lie to get paid, you will kill for it too. And when you defraud your brother, that is hatred. Brothers among us are broke, trying to make a living; if you love your brothers, why would you defraud a brother who is broke just like you? And don't go cozying up under the few brothers who have money because they have money.
- “Which while some coveted after”: you are covetous when you see the things you want and can't get them, and you find every other means to get them. Some of you need to work for somebody and leave business alone, because business has to be done righteously, and a covetous man's business never prospers, or prospers only in unrighteousness until he does it to the wrong person. Remember the two wicked elders out of Babylon who thought they could keep doing in Israel what they had always done, and it did not go well for them with Susanna: they were both put to death. Getting away with things makes you think you are good, but everything comes back to haunt you in this truth. We have to be men of honor.
- A man who says he would sell his soul for a hundred million dollars has shown his character, and some sell it for a dollar: the crackhead sells his for a five dollar rock, the covetous man for a cheeseburger, a happy meal. If you are constantly sinning to acquire, you are selling your soul. Character makes a man predictable and dependable; once people know your character they know how to deal with you. If you need five dollars I could give you five dollars, but I will never do business with a covetous man.

  Precepts:
  - **[Proverbs 14:1](/bible/proverbs/14#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/14#v1)</sup> Every wise woman buildeth her house: but the foolish plucketh it down with her hands.

    You are looking at what that brother is doing and you are mad at your husband because he is not doing the same, and he is probably trying to get where he needs to be. But “Every wise woman buildeth her house”. You don’t know what she is doing for him to act like that. Why aren’t you studying her? Ask her how she builds up her house.

**[1 Timothy 6:11-12](/bible/1-timothy/6#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/1-timothy/6#v11)</sup> But thou, O man of God, flee these things; and follow after righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, meekness.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-timothy/6#v12)</sup> Fight the good fight of faith, lay hold on eternal life, whereunto thou art also called, and hast professed a good profession before many witnesses.

- “But thou, O man of God, flee these things; and follow after righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, meekness. Fight the good fight of faith, lay hold on eternal life.”

**[1 Timothy 6:17](/bible/1-timothy/6#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/1-timothy/6#v17)</sup> Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy;

- “Charge them that are rich in this world”: we are to charge the rich, “that they be not highminded.” The high-minded separated themselves from the masses of the people and thought they were better. “Nor trust in uncertain riches”: ask Andrew Tate, whose money they seized. “But in the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy.”
- The question was how one can be a tested man of God when all he knows is comfort and wealth. Your test is to help others. God may not plague some of you, but he’s going to watch what you do with your comfort and your wealth. A lot of people are comfortable and wealthy, watching us and sitting on the sidelines while we’re at war and under attack.
- “Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches”: just because you got money, that don’t mean you can’t get touched. Our job is to charge you, to hold you accountable. “But in the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy”: he gave you your comfort and your wealth to enjoy.

**[1 Timothy 6:18-19](/bible/1-timothy/6#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/1-timothy/6#v18)</sup> That they do good, that they be rich in good works, ready to distribute, willing to communicate;
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/1-timothy/6#v19)</sup> Laying up in store for themselves a good foundation against the time to come, that they may lay hold on eternal life.

- “That they do good, that they be rich in good works, ready to distribute”: that goes back to Acts 4, “willing to communicate; Laying up in store for themselves a good foundation against the time to come.”
- “That they do good, that they be rich in good works”: your job with your comfort and your wealth is to do good. You may not be getting the hell that we’re catching, but you can have good works to assist the men that are on the front line in battle. If I had money, all of it would be to help the men that are fighting for my nation.
- White people do that: they support their troops. The white soldier comes home in his uniform and everybody sees him in the store, and they send their kids up to give him a hug, because he’s fighting for their country. They know what platoon, they know where you serve, they make sure the families are taken care of. Don’t expect that from black people; all they know about is basketball players and rap lyrics and Beyonce. We are the best fighters on the face of the planet, the men of God, and we get no support. Christ said that, that we are without honor. And there are a lot of you who do help even though you’re not rich, and the fact that you care means more than anything. Your reward is with God.
- “Ready to distribute”: ready to distribute to the men that are fighting to bring forth this nation. It’s your job. There are some who are going to help on the sideline, because these men put their lives on the line and at any time anything could go wrong with them. When the bishop was sick, we didn’t ask nobody for help. More people should have stepped up: you don’t know what his house is going through, what his family is going through; he needed around-the-clock care. White people set up GoFundMes; they don’t even have to know Tommy, they don’t have to know Billy Ray, all they need to know is a white man is sick, and they come out. They defend their covenant. Black people, forget about even asking them to send two dollars.

    Christ covered it, that your reward is with God. “And he looked up, and saw the rich men casting their gifts into the treasury”: it was customary to help the church, the treasury of the church. “And he saw also a certain poor widow casting in thither two mites.” What she gave wouldn’t put a dent in it; they wouldn’t even know whether she put it in or not. Look it up and two mites is like half a penny. People say, oh, I only had twenty dollars, I hope this helps get you lunch. No, that’s a big deal.
