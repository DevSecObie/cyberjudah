## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on Philippians 1 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 9-11, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[Philippians 1:9](/bible/philippians/1#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/philippians/1#v9)</sup> And this I pray, that your love may abound yet more and more in knowledge and in all judgment;

- The great souled man, the magnanimous man, knew himself accurately and did not lie to himself. Grandiosity, feeling like you can do anything, believing you’re it, can be fine if it’s done in humility and meekness. But if you lie to yourself it’s going to burn your house down, because you can’t be honest with yourself: you think you can do no wrong and everybody else is wrong. That goes all the way back to keep thy heart with all diligence.
- A clarifying question repeats the question, maybe with a similar word, to try to understand. A clarifying question doesn’t pull a scripture and come with a whole other thing; that’s called a Jezebel spirit, trying to get you to move away from the topic, and yes, a man could have that.
- You got to be honest with yourself. Some of you sisters think you can cook certain foods: hell no. You think you’re going to put your own twist on it and make it better, and it don’t go that way. You got to know what you can’t handle. That’s a small example, but see how it can be detrimental when it comes to sin: you think you can be in an environment or a situation and resist it, and then you allow yourself to be in those scenarios. Some of you are like that with your worldly family. Nobody’s saying cut them off, but you got to be mindful of the time you’re with them and redeem the time, cleanse yourself from the pollution that was around you, because they ain’t talking no scriptures with you, and you wonder why am I carnal. It can get you jacked up in relationships too: you think you have more value than you really do, and you wonder why you always have bad relationships. Maybe it’s you.
- Break the word down: magna means great, animus means soul. Magnanimous is a lofty and courageous spirit. We have to stand boldly in Christ, knowing when that day comes, with a courageous spirit to resist the temptations and apply discipline in those situations. It is nobility, high-minded and noble, and that goes to the crown. We’re dealing with our soul: having the fullness and measure of the stature of Christ in us, having that as our mind as we move forward.

**[Philippians 1:10](/bible/philippians/1#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/philippians/1#v10)</sup> That ye may approve things that are excellent; that ye may be sincere and without offence till the day of Christ;

- “That ye may approve things that are excellent”: until you’re honest with yourself you can’t look at things honestly without lying to yourself, and you can’t approve things that are excellent. “That ye may be sincere and without offence till the day of Christ”.

**[Philippians 1:11](/bible/philippians/1#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/philippians/1#v11)</sup> Being filled with the fruits of righteousness, which are by Jesus Christ, unto the glory and praise of God.

- “Being filled with the fruits of righteousness, which are by Jesus Christ”: again, the mind of Christ. That’s what we must move with; he is in you. That’s the great soul man, brothers and sisters: absent of Christ inside of you there is no great soul man. That’s the nobility. Who’s the king? Christ.
- So when we talk about how we should be, it ain’t to puff yourself up. It’s to have that pride and honesty in yourself that Jesus Christ is in me, and you’ll have different moments and interactions where that’s going to manifest itself and let you show it forth. “Unto the glory and praise of God”: not for your glory and praise, but to the glory and praise of God.
