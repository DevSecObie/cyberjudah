## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 2 Corinthians 3 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-4, 6-18, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[2 Corinthians 3:1](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v1)</sup> Do we begin again to commend ourselves? or need we, as some others, epistles of commendation to you, or letters of commendation from you?

- “Need we, as some others, epistles of commendation to you, or letters of commendation from you?” Commendation is like merit and praise: do we need you to tell us how great we are, how fire the class was? Say it if you are compelled, but it is not a necessity.

**[2 Corinthians 3:2](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v2)**

> <sup>[2](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v2)</sup> Ye are our epistle written in our hearts, known and read of all men:

- “Ye are our epistle written in our hearts, known and read of all men.” What we need from you is to replicate what we are doing. We have fire, you have fire; we teach, gather and build, you teach, gather and build. That makes you the letter of commendation. Our success is measured in you, not in numbers but in the quality of the behavior and the consistency and longevity of what is put forth. One day the corporation on paper won’t exist, but we are not going anywhere.

**[2 Corinthians 3:3](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v3)</sup> Forasmuch as ye are manifestly declared to be the epistle of Christ ministered by us, written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God; not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart.

- “Written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God”: that spirit entering each one of us. “Not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart”: because you see action and movement behind it. The fleshy heart goes into behaviors.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hebrews 8:10](/bible/hebrews/8#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/hebrews/8#v10)</sup> For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, saith the Lord; I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts: and I will be to them a God, and they shall be to me a people:

    Think of the fleshy heart and what it represents. “This is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, saith the Lord; I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts: and I will be to them a God, and they shall be to me a people.”

**[2 Corinthians 3:4](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v4)</sup> And such trust have we through Christ to God-ward:

- “And such trust have we through Christ to God-ward.” That is the confidence that comes from seeing those things in the people.

**[2 Corinthians 3:6](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v6)</sup> Who also hath made us able ministers of the new testament; not of the letter, but of the spirit: for the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life.

- “Not of the letter, but of the spirit: for the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life.” Scoffers will seize on this as if it means not keeping the law. The whole point of reading it is the epistle: we need to replicate that fire and zeal, and part of the work is having others do the same.
- “Who also hath made us able ministers of the new testament; not of the letter, but of the spirit”. The letter is the old testament, the Mosaic law of sacrifice; the spirit is Christ, his death. “For the letter killeth”: he that despised Moses’ law died without mercy. The letter, the old covenant sacrifice, gets you condemned and killed, because you could not atone for everything. “But the spirit giveth life”: Christ gives us life. The words that Christ spoke unto us, they are spirit and they are life.

  Precepts:
  - **[Acts 13:38-39](/bible/acts/13#v38)**
    > <sup>[38](/bible/acts/13#v38)</sup> Be it known unto you therefore, men and brethren, that through this man is preached unto you the forgiveness of sins:
    >
    > <sup>[39](/bible/acts/13#v39)</sup> And by him all that believe are justified from all things, from which ye could not be justified by the law of Moses.

    “Through this man is preached unto you the forgiveness of sins”: through Christ. “And by him all that believe are justified from all things, from which ye could not be justified by the law of Moses.”

**[2 Corinthians 3:7](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v7)</sup> But if the ministration of death, written and engraven in stones, was glorious, so that the children of Israel could not stedfastly behold the face of Moses for the glory of his countenance; which glory was to be done away:

- “But if the ministration of death, written and engraven in stones”: that is Moses. The old covenant is called the ministration of death, the law of sin and death, because if you didn’t keep certain laws you got administered some death. It “was glorious”: those stones of the commandments were glorious.
- Moses went up and sat with Christ in the mount forty days and forty nights, and Christ’s glory was on him, “so that the children of Israel could not stedfastly behold the face of Moses for the glory of his countenance”. He had to wear a veil so Israel could see him and not be blinded. “Which glory was to be done away”: the Mosaic law, the sacrifice.

**[2 Corinthians 3:8-9](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v8)</sup> How shall not the ministration of the spirit be rather glorious?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v9)</sup> For if the ministration of condemnation be glory, much more doth the ministration of righteousness exceed in glory.

- “How shall not the ministration of the spirit be rather glorious?” The ministration of the spirit is the new testament, the ministration of life: no condemnation in Christ Jesus, Romans 8:1. First he called the old the ministration of death; now he calls it “the ministration of condemnation”. “Much more doth the ministration of righteousness exceed in glory”: the new covenant is better because under it you can repent of all sins that you could not under the old, where you faced condemnation.

**[2 Corinthians 3:10-11](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v10)**

> <sup>[10](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v10)</sup> For even that which was made glorious had no glory in this respect, by reason of the glory that excelleth.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v11)</sup> For if that which is done away was glorious, much more that which remaineth is glorious.

- Now, to speak plainly. “For if that which is done away was glorious”: that which is done away is the old covenant of animal sacrifice. It was glorious at that time. “Much more that which remaineth is glorious”: what remains, the rest of them laws, are still glorious.
- The law of Moses, the sacrifices, the temple, the priests’ dress, the holy of holies, the ephod, the blood sprinkled: all of it was glorious, a sight to behold. But it “had no glory in this respect, by reason of the glory that excelleth”: the ministration of life, Christ’s new testament, because under it everyone can atone for everything they could not under Moses.
- “For if that which is done away was glorious”: is done away, not will be. We are under the new covenant now. “Much more that which remaineth is glorious”: the new covenant is better, more glorious.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hebrews 8:8-10](/bible/hebrews/8#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/hebrews/8#v8)</sup> For finding fault with them, he saith, Behold, the days come, saith the Lord, when I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah:
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/hebrews/8#v9)</sup> Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers in the day when I took them by the hand to lead them out of the land of Egypt; because they continued not in my covenant, and I regarded them not, saith the Lord.
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/hebrews/8#v10)</sup> For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, saith the Lord; I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts: and I will be to them a God, and they shall be to me a people:

    What is done away, and what remains? “For finding fault with them”: finding fault with Israel, “he saith, Behold, the days come, saith the Lord, when I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah: Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers in the day when I took them by the hand to lead them out of the land of Egypt; because they continued not in my covenant”: we didn’t continue in the old covenant. “And I regarded them not”: he didn’t regard us, and we went into slavery. That’s what that means.

**[2 Corinthians 3:12](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v12)</sup> Seeing then that we have such hope, we use great plainness of speech:

- “Seeing then that we have such hope, we use great plainness of speech”. We understand the old covenant was glorious in that time, but when Christ came he took the place of animal sacrifice, for the children of Israel. The old covenant changed into the new covenant for Israel, not all people on the planet earth. That’s great plainness of speech.
- So when you teach, brothers, speak plain. Don’t make it so confused that people say, what are you talking about? Like the church: we are not under the law. Shut the hell up, what are you saying, preacher? The hell is this? I ain’t learning nothing up in here. When we teach we got to make it plain to the people, not in metaphors. When we read the letters of Paul, which are written hard, we make it plain to the people: this is what it means, and these are the precepts that explain it.
- “Seeing then that we have such hope”, such faith, “we use great plainness of speech”: Paul is writing this very clear and plain to understand.
- “Seeing then that we have such hope, we use great plainness of speech”. That hope is faith, and that faith is in Christ. When we have faith and hope in Christ we use great plainness of speech, because, remember, wisdom is plain and clear.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hebrews 11:13-15](/bible/hebrews/11#v13)**
    > <sup>[13](/bible/hebrews/11#v13)</sup> These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off, and were persuaded of them, and embraced them, and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth.
    >
    > <sup>[14](/bible/hebrews/11#v14)</sup> For they that say such things declare plainly that they seek a country.
    >
    > <sup>[15](/bible/hebrews/11#v15)</sup> And truly, if they had been mindful of that country from whence they came out, they might have had opportunity to have returned.

    People say, okay, so Christ came to the Israelites, he took the place of animal sacrifice to die for the sins of Israel; now what? “These all died in faith”: our forefathers. Above it talks about Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham. “Not having received the promises”: talking about the kingdom, “but having seen them afar off”: all of our fathers saw the kingdom afar off. “And were persuaded of them, and embraced them”: they believed in that God and embraced the teachings, “and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth.” All of us are strangers and pilgrims on this earth.
  - **[2 Corinthians 2:17](/bible/2-corinthians/2#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/2-corinthians/2#v17)</sup> For we are not as many, which corrupt the word of God: but as of sincerity, but as of God, in the sight of God speak we in Christ.

    “For we are not as many, which corrupt the word of God”. You don’t corrupt God’s word. We read it plainly, clear and concise. The Bible is self-explanatory and self-defining, here a little and there a little. “But as of sincerity, but as of God, in the sight of God speak we in Christ”: plainly and clear.
  - **[2 Corinthians 11:3](/bible/2-corinthians/11#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/2-corinthians/11#v3)</sup> But I fear, lest by any means, as the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtilty, so your minds should be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ.

    “But I fear, lest by any means, as the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtilty, so your minds should be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ.” The serpent beguiled Eve through idolatry, through a different doctrine. That is how Satan got into Eve’s head. And that is why the Christian church is filled with women, mama’s boys and weak men: the same way Satan beguiled Eve back then, the church does the exact same thing, because it is a doctrine of Satan in there.

**[2 Corinthians 3:13-14](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v13)</sup> And not as Moses, which put a vail over his face, that the children of Israel could not stedfastly look to the end of that which is abolished:
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v14)</sup> But their minds were blinded: for until this day remaineth the same vail untaken away in the reading of the old testament; which vail is done away in Christ.

- “And not as Moses, which put a vail over his face, that the children of Israel could not stedfastly look to the end of that which is abolished”. Moses went up on the mountain and got the laws, and when he came back there was a glow on his face, so bright they had to put a veil and cover Moses’ face. That glow of light represented Christ.
- What was abolished? Animal sacrifice was abolished, because Christ took its place. The old covenant was moved out the way and the new covenant came in.
- “And not as Moses, which put a vail over his face, that the children of Israel could not stedfastly look to the end of that which is abolished”. The glory on Moses’ face symbolised Christ, and the people being blinded by it represented their not being able to see Christ and the old covenant being done away with. That was the blindness of the Pharisees: they were covetous, it was their money, and they were wicked as hell, so they could not see Christ dying on the cross and redeeming us out of animal sacrifice, and they had him killed. Once Christ died their office was done away too, because he became the high priest. The same blindness that fell on our people when Moses came down from the mountain with his face shining is the blindness that kept them from seeing the old covenant done away.
- “But their minds were blinded”: they read the Bible and say, Nope, it can’t be, not possible. “For until this day”, until the time Paul wrote, “remaineth the same vail untaken away in the reading of the old testament”: the letter, the ministration of condemnation or death, read by the Pharisees and scribes who cannot understand it. “Which vail is done away in Christ.”

  Precepts:
  - **[Hebrews 10:8-9](/bible/hebrews/10#v8)**
    > <sup>[8](/bible/hebrews/10#v8)</sup> Above when he said, Sacrifice and offering and burnt offerings and offering for sin thou wouldest not, neither hadst pleasure therein; which are offered by the law;
    >
    > <sup>[9](/bible/hebrews/10#v9)</sup> Then said he, Lo, I come to do thy will, O God. He taketh away the first, that he may establish the second.

    A follow-up scripture. “Sacrifice and offering and burnt offerings and offering for sin thou wouldest not”: stress that, God did not want that, “neither hadst pleasure therein; which are offered by the law”: the law said to sacrifice x amount of animals on this day and that day.

**[2 Corinthians 3:15-16](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v15)</sup> But even unto this day, when Moses is read, the vail is upon their heart.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v16)</sup> Nevertheless when it shall turn to the Lord, the vail shall be taken away.

- “But even unto this day, when Moses is read, the vail is upon their heart.” “Nevertheless when it shall turn to the Lord”, when they repent and acknowledge that Christ is our saviour and died for us, “the vail shall be taken away”: removed from their eyes, from their minds.

**[2 Corinthians 3:17](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v17)</sup> Now the Lord is that Spirit: and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty.

- “Now the Lord is that Spirit”: Christ is that spirit Moses dealt with, that made him shine. “And where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty.” Christ has made us free from the law of sin and death: the law of liberty in James. Where Christ is, there is liberty, freedom, atonement for all the things you could not atone for under Moses and animal sacrifice.

  Precepts:
  - **[James 1:25](/bible/james/1#v25)**
    > <sup>[25](/bible/james/1#v25)</sup> But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed.

    “But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed.” Where the spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty: that is the perfect law of liberty. Liberty, liberty, liberty.

**[2 Corinthians 3:18](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/2-corinthians/3#v18)</sup> But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord.

- “But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord.” This is back to the face in a glass of James 1. This book is the glass of the glory of the Lord, and we behold it with an open face.
- An open face is a face set unto the Lord God, seeking him by prayer and supplication, going to the book and confessing that we have sinned. When we really behold his face openly, we will change, into the same image from glory to glory. There is supposed to be a change in us when we accept, we pray, and we understand that we have sinned against God.
