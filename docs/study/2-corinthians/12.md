## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 2 Corinthians 12 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 1-4, 6-9, 14-16, 20-21, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[2 Corinthians 12:1-3](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v1)</sup> It is not expedient for me doubtless to glory. I will come to visions and revelations of the Lord.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v2)</sup> I knew a man in Christ above fourteen years ago, (whether in the body, I cannot tell; or whether out of the body, I cannot tell: God knoweth;) such an one caught up to the third heaven.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v3)</sup> And I knew such a man, (whether in the body, or out of the body, I cannot tell: God knoweth;)

- “I will come to visions and revelations of the Lord. I knew a man in Christ above fourteen years ago”, “such an one caught up to the third heaven.” Paul is speaking of himself, of a certain situation.

**[2 Corinthians 12:4](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v4)</sup> How that he was caught up into paradise, and heard unspeakable words, which it is not lawful for a man to utter.

- “How that he was caught up into paradise, and heard unspeakable words, which it is not lawful for a man to utter.” It is not lawful, because this is in the third heaven, and the Father is in the third heaven.

**[2 Corinthians 12:6](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v6)</sup> For though I would desire to glory, I shall not be a fool; for I will say the truth: but now I forbear, lest any man should think of me above that which he seeth me to be, or that he heareth of me.

- This is for those who went to God and fasted two days, three days, asking him to take away the porn spirit, and it is still there. “For though I would desire to glory, I shall not be a fool.” This is why we do not glory in ourselves in this truth: we understand the fight and what the brothers are going through. “Lest any man should think of me above that which he seeth me to be, or that he heareth of me.”

**[2 Corinthians 12:7](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v7)</sup> And lest I should be exalted above measure through the abundance of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn in the flesh, the messenger of Satan to buffet me, lest I should be exalted above measure.

- “And lest I should be exalted above measure through the abundance of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn in the flesh, the messenger of Satan to buffet me.” Read with another scripture, that thorn in the flesh is evil concupiscence: a strong sexual desire. Paul was dealing with evil, wicked sexual thoughts in his mind, and it was given to buffet him and keep him humble, so he would not exalt himself.

**[2 Corinthians 12:8](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v8)</sup> For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me.

- “For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me.” Three times he went to God and asked that the thorn, that lust, leave him. The same way some men ask God, Lord, I want to stop watching this stuff, and a week later they are back on it again.

**[2 Corinthians 12:9](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v9)**

> <sup>[9](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v9)</sup> And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.

- “My grace is sufficient for thee”: my mercy is sufficient for you. When you mess up and let it weigh you down and destroy you, Christ’s mercies are enough for you. We want God to take away our shortcomings and the lust inside, but if he took away all the weakness inside of you he would be making you perfect, and he is not going to do that.
- “For my strength is made perfect in weakness.” When you finally overcome porn, you become stronger. If the Lord just took that spirit from you, how would you learn to fight off lust, and how could you teach somebody else to overcome theirs, never having fought it? Christ’s mercy is good enough: if you fall, get back up, fix it and stop doing it. He is not making it easy by taking it from you. Keep fighting that demon, overcome it, slay it; that is how you become stronger, and then you are able to teach brothers how to overcome that demon.
- “My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.” Christ said his strength is made perfect in our weaknesses.
- “Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.” The things you have within you, you have to fight them, and that is how the power of Christ is manifest. You can't keep giving in to alcoholism, to depression and low self-esteem.

**[2 Corinthians 12:14](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v14)</sup> Behold, the third time I am ready to come to you; and I will not be burdensome to you: for I seek not yours, but you: for the children ought not to lay up for the parents, but the parents for the children.

- “I will not be burdensome to you: for I seek not yours, but you.” This is the spirit we must have. Many of us deceive ourselves that we are fervent in spirit, the way people lie to themselves that they are in a calorie deficit while the scale never budges. Anybody who has been an activist or revolutionary has this drive, and ours should be bolder and grander because we have the sure word: none of these shall fail.
- “I will not be burdensome to you: for I seek not yours, but you”. Paul had to deal with the Corinthians on numerous levels. He was very open with them about what he was dealing with, in order to win them over. As you read in Acts, many of the Corinthians believed, but there was still a fraction of negroes in the church of Corinth that Paul always had to check, reprimand and get on about various things, because of the way they dealt with him as the elder over the congregation. They always questioned him about what he was doing with the alms that came in.
- Paul says, I’m not burdensome to you, because I don’t need your stuff, I don’t need your baggage. What I need is you. You’re what the Lord wants.

**[2 Corinthians 12:15](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v15)**

> <sup>[15](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v15)</sup> And I will very gladly spend and be spent for you; though the more abundantly I love you, the less I be loved.

- “I will very gladly spend and be spent for you.” To spend is to use up, consume or exhaust, and to give one’s blood, life and so on for some cause. Paul is willing to exhaust and consume himself for compassion on his people. That is the impatience and drive, not the self-defeating attitude of just waiting on the Lord to give the increase.
- Many brothers say they would die for you, take a bullet, but cannot put in the effort to be spent for their people in the day-to-day work. To be spent means there is nothing left. The longer, slower death is giving your blood and life by devoting your life to the Lord in Christ, and so to your brothers and sisters and your people, choosing every moment to let that fire reign. You may get frustrated, vexed and annoyed dealing with Israel, but never tired of fulfilling your purpose.
- “Though the more abundantly I love you, the less I be loved.” The more he pours himself into his people, the less he is appreciated; they mumble that he is too burdensome, too intense, not brotherly. But if a child is about to put a knife in an outlet, or a brother stands in front of a bus, you tackle him and hurt yourselves in the process to save him. There is a war and a mission here.
- The margin of some Bibles reads your souls. It is not the carnal brother or sister he is spent for, but the soul and spirit inside. We perceive things earthly, but we have to move spiritually.

  Precepts:
  - **[Hebrews 13:17](/bible/hebrews/13#v17)**
    > <sup>[17](/bible/hebrews/13#v17)</sup> Obey them that have the rule over you, and submit yourselves: for they watch for your souls, as they that must give account, that they may do it with joy, and not with grief: for that is unprofitable for you.

    Same theme. “Obey them that have the rule over you, and submit yourselves: for they watch for your souls.” Submit means do what is being taught and apply it. Paul gladly spends himself for their souls, and here the rulers watch for their souls.

**[2 Corinthians 12:16](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v16)</sup> But be it so, I did not burden you: nevertheless, being crafty, I caught you with guile.

- “Nevertheless, being crafty, I caught you with guile.” Guile is skill in achieving one’s end through indirect, subtle or underhanded means. It is also deception, the inclination or practice of misleading others through lies or trickery. Paul told the church of Corinth, I caught you in the practice of misleading others through lies or trickery.
- In order to follow Christ there are things we have to deny within ourselves. You may have never killed anyone, never beat anyone up, never had a fight a day in your life, but you’ve got that gift of gab to get over on your people. We say we would never fall for a scam, but brothers and sisters fall for things like this all the time, overtly and covertly: can I come stay with you for a while? And they post up and never leave. That is guile, that is trickery, coming in with ulterior motives.
- We talk about beating each other up, robbing, stealing, killing and adultery, but these subtle things must be purged from us as well. Paul taught the church of Corinth himself and caught them in it, so it is not something in the scriptures we don’t deal with. How much more today, with all these distractions and devices? Pathological liars, habitual liars, lying about small things and big things, and when they get caught they try to make it like you’re the problem. Even people in the world are talking about it. Among our people is the same spirit Paul caught in Corinth.

**[2 Corinthians 12:20](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v20)**

> <sup>[20](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v20)</sup> For I fear, lest, when I come, I shall not find you such as I would, and that I shall be found unto you such as ye would not: lest there be debates, envyings, wraths, strifes, backbitings, whisperings, swellings, tumults:

- Paul tells the Corinthians, “I shall not find you such as I would, and that I shall be found unto you such as ye would not”. Whatever spirit they expected him to come in, that is not the spirit he was coming in. They were constantly accusing Paul of things he was not doing, and Paul moved in the spirit of Christ: I don't know what you expected, but this is how I am moving.
- He fears to find “debates, envyings, wraths, strifes, backbitings, whisperings, swellings, tumults”. These debates are not in righteousness but in unrighteousness. A tumult is when the city is in an uproar, and the magistrates, the centurions, the police have to be called to calm it down.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Corinthians 10:10-11](/bible/2-corinthians/10#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/2-corinthians/10#v10)</sup> For his letters, say they, are weighty and powerful; but his bodily presence is weak, and his speech contemptible.
    >
    > <sup>[11](/bible/2-corinthians/10#v11)</sup> Let such an one think this, that, such as we are in word by letters when we are absent, such will we be also in deed when we are present.

    Why would Paul have to come that way? Chapter by chapter he had pointed out their sins: not keeping company with a fornicator, lasciviousness and all manner of sexual sins, the extortions, the reviling. And “his letters, say they, are weighty and powerful”. The they is the church of Corinth, the elders and scribes and Pharisees there. Paul's epistles are heavy, absolutely powerful; “but his bodily presence is weak, and his speech contemptible.”
  - **[Acts 18:1-17](/bible/acts/18#v1)**

    >

    >
    > <sup>[7](/bible/acts/18#v7)</sup> And he departed thence, and entered into a certain man’s house, named Justus, one that worshipped God, whose house joined hard to the synagogue.

    > <sup>[9](/bible/acts/18#v9)</sup> Then spake the Lord to Paul in the night by a vision, Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace:
    >
    > <sup>[10](/bible/acts/18#v10)</sup> For I am with thee, and no man shall set on thee to hurt thee: for I have much people in this city.
    >

    >
    > <sup>[13](/bible/acts/18#v13)</sup> Saying, This fellow persuadeth men to worship God contrary to the law.
    >
    > <sup>[14](/bible/acts/18#v14)</sup> And when Paul was now about to open his mouth, Gallio said unto the Jews, If it were a matter of wrong or wicked lewdness, O ye Jews, reason would that I should bear with you:
    >
    > <sup>[15](/bible/acts/18#v15)</sup> But if it be a question of words and names, and of your law, look ye to it; for I will be no judge of such matters.

    “After these things Paul departed from Athens, and came to Corinth”: Paul was in Corinth, and his epistles were to the same people he dealt with there. That is why he could write, if I come again: he had already visited them. “Crispus, the chief ruler of the synagogue, believed on the Lord with all his house; and many of the Corinthians hearing believed, and were baptized.” Many of the Corinthians believed, not all.
  - **[Proverbs 29:1](/bible/proverbs/29#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/29#v1)</sup> He, that being often reproved hardeneth his neck, shall suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy.

    “He, that being often reproved hardeneth his neck.” The same correction given to the church of Corinth caused some of them to grow hardened, bitter behind the correction. So some of the building blocks of bitterness stem from the hatred of correction, like a stubborn little child: why they got to point out my stuff? Why can't I do what I want to do?

**[2 Corinthians 12:21](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v21)**

> <sup>[21](/bible/2-corinthians/12#v21)</sup> And lest, when I come again, my God will humble me among you, and that I shall bewail many which have sinned already, and have not repented of the uncleanness and fornication and lasciviousness which they have committed.

- “My God will humble me among you, and that I shall bewail many which have sinned already”: Paul will be sorrowful for those already in the midst of sin, the same sins read in the verse above and in 1 Corinthians 6. But here is the key: “and have not repented of the uncleanness and fornication and lasciviousness which they have committed.” They did not repent.
- There are things every one of us battles with, things we must fix and improve on. But for the Lord to give that assistance, there must be repentance involved. Everybody says that is a given; everybody does not know it.
- Paul names two sins here, the fornication and the lasciviousness, but in 1 Corinthians 6 they stood with the extortion, the thefts and the reviling, all together and all unrighteousness. Some things Paul expounds on and others he keeps short. Some of the Corinthians would not repent of the transgressions they committed.

  Precepts:
  - **[2 Corinthians 13:1-2](/bible/2-corinthians/13#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/2-corinthians/13#v1)</sup> This is the third time I am coming to you. In the mouth of two or three witnesses shall every word be established.
    >
    > <sup>[2](/bible/2-corinthians/13#v2)</sup> I told you before, and foretell you, as if I were present, the second time; and being absent now I write to them which heretofore have sinned, and to all other, that, if I come again, I will not spare:

    Read straight on: “This is the third time I am coming to you. In the mouth of two or three witnesses shall every word be established.” Paul has dealt with this matter with them on numerous occasions. “I told you before, and foretell you, as if I were present, the second time; and being absent now I write to them which heretofore have sinned”: he is still dealing with those that sinned and did not repent.
  - **[2 Corinthians 13:10](/bible/2-corinthians/13#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/2-corinthians/13#v10)</sup> Therefore I write these things being absent, lest being present I should use sharpness, according to the power which the Lord hath given me to edification, and not to destruction.

    “Therefore I write these things being absent, lest being present I should use sharpness”: were he present, he would use sharpness, he would be fierce, “according to the power which the Lord hath given me to edification, and not to destruction.” The correction is to build them up, not to destroy them.
