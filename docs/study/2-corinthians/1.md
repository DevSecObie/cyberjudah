## Introduction

These notes gather what the CyberJudah classes taught on 2 Corinthians 1 in their scripture breakdowns: verses 3-6, each with the class's breakdown and the precepts read with it.

**[2 Corinthians 1:3](/bible/2-corinthians/1#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/2-corinthians/1#v3)</sup> Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort;

- God is “the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort;” The source of comfort is the heavenly Father, approached through searching the scriptures and seeing our own struggles in the experiences of the forefathers.
- The low attention given to classes on alcohol, compared with the excitement over a public debate or someone being cut by the scriptures, exposes an appetite for escape. It is easier to watch another person corrected than to face a problem in oneself. The same avoidance appears when instruction about fornication or another personal sin is passed over without being heard. Comfort from God must be joined to willingness to accept the correction that reveals the problem.

  Precepts:
  - **[Psalms 119:76-77](/bible/psalms/119#v76)**
    > <sup>[76](/bible/psalms/119#v76)</sup> Let, I pray thee, thy merciful kindness be for my comfort, according to thy word unto thy servant.
    >
    > <sup>[77](/bible/psalms/119#v77)</sup> Let thy tender mercies come unto me, that I may live: for thy law is my delight.

    “Let, I pray thee, thy merciful kindness be for my comfort, according to thy word unto thy servant.” God's merciful kindness provides comfort because he has given time and grace to repent. There remains an opportunity to be made right and delivered from captivity. That opportunity should encourage us rather than make us complacent.

**[2 Corinthians 1:4](/bible/2-corinthians/1#v4)**

> <sup>[4](/bible/2-corinthians/1#v4)</sup> Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

- “Who comforteth us in all our tribulation.” We are going to go through tribulations, brothers, but the Lord is going to comfort us in all our tribulation, “that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.”

**[2 Corinthians 1:5](/bible/2-corinthians/1#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/2-corinthians/1#v5)</sup> For as the sufferings of Christ abound in us, so our consolation also aboundeth by Christ.

- “For as the sufferings of Christ abound in us, so our consolation also aboundeth by Christ.” Brothers and sisters, we must suffer in this truth; we must go through affliction. The only way we are going to get that prize is by going through affliction and suffering, enduring in affliction, keeping the word in affliction and not giving up.

**[2 Corinthians 1:6](/bible/2-corinthians/1#v6)**

> <sup>[6](/bible/2-corinthians/1#v6)</sup> And whether we be afflicted, it is for your consolation and salvation, which is effectual in the enduring of the same sufferings which we also suffer: or whether we be comforted, it is for your consolation and salvation.

- “And whether we be afflicted, it is for your consolation and salvation, which is effectual in the enduring.” The enduring is the key thing: you must endure. So if Trump says he is going to deport you for teaching, don’t stop teaching. Wherever you get deported to, go and teach the gospel.
- As men of God we should not be living in fear. Fear no man and fear no executive order. The only one we should fear is our God.
