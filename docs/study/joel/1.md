## Introduction

This session covers [Joel 1](/bible/joel/1) through 3 and [Amos 1](/bible/amos/1). Joel does not open by naming the reigning king the way many of the other prophets do, so the scholars had trouble pinning down when the book was written. Going precept upon precept, the teaching places Joel in the time going into the Babylonian captivity, when Israel still had their own land, crops, and vineyards and had to endure the curses of [Deuteronomy 28](/bible/deuteronomy/28). The Most High warns of a plague of locusts on the crops and of a nation strong as a lion coming up out of the north, which is Babylon, the destroyer of the Gentiles. After verse 6 the teachers noted a scheduling conflict and read the remaining chapters straight through without going precept upon precept.

**[Joel 1:1](/bible/joel/1#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/joel/1#v1)</sup> The word of the Lord that came to Joel the son of Pethuel.

- The word of the Lord came to Joel the son of Pethuel. Because this opening does not name a king, the scholars had issues determining the time period, but precept upon precept shows us when it was.

**[Joel 1:2-3](/bible/joel/1#v2)**
> <sup>[2](/bible/joel/1#v2)</sup> Hear this, ye old men, and give ear, all ye inhabitants of the land. Hath this been in your days, or even in the days of your fathers?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/joel/1#v3)</sup> Tell ye your children of it, and let your children tell their children, and their children another generation.

- The Most High tells the old men and all the inhabitants of the land to hear this and to ask whether the like has been in their days or the days of their fathers.
- Tell your children of it, and let your children tell their children, and their children another generation. What is about to come on the crops is to be handed down from generation to generation.

**[Joel 1:4](/bible/joel/1#v4)**
> <sup>[4](/bible/joel/1#v4)</sup> That which the palmerworm hath left hath the locust eaten; and that which the locust hath left hath the cankerworm eaten; and that which the cankerworm hath left hath the caterpiller eaten.

- That which the palmerworm left the locust ate, and that which the locust left the cankerworm ate, and that which the cankerworm left the caterpillar ate. This is the plague coming on the crops through the locusts and cankerworms.
- This came upon Israel during the time going into the Babylonian captivity, when they still had their own land, crops, and vineyards, and had to endure the curses of [Deuteronomy 28](/bible/deuteronomy/28). These are curses you do not usually hear brought out on the street, because they fell on us when we actually had our own land.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:38](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v38)**
    > <sup>[38](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v38)</sup> Thou shalt carry much seed out into the field, and shalt gather but little in; for the locust shall consume it.

    Thou shalt carry much seed into the field and gather but little in, for the locust shall consume it, which is exactly what Joel just read.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:39](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v39)**
    > <sup>[39](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v39)</sup> Thou shalt plant vineyards, and dress them, but shalt neither drink of the wine, nor gather the grapes; for the worms shall eat them.

    Thou shalt plant vineyards and dress them but neither drink of the wine nor gather the grapes, for the worms shall eat them.

**[Joel 1:5](/bible/joel/1#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/joel/1#v5)</sup> Awake, ye drunkards, and weep; and howl, all ye drinkers of wine, because of the new wine; for it is cut off from your mouth.

- Awake ye drunkards and weep, and howl all ye drinkers of wine, because the new wine is cut off from your mouth. The new wine is cut off because the palmerworms and cankerworms ate up the vineyards where the grapes for the wine were grown, exactly as [Deuteronomy 28:39](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v39) said.

**[Joel 1:6](/bible/joel/1#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/joel/1#v6)</sup> For a nation is come up upon my land, strong, and without number, whose teeth are the teeth of a lion, and he hath the cheek teeth of a great lion.

- A nation has come up upon his land, strong and without number, whose teeth are the teeth of a lion and the cheek teeth of a great lion. This is how we know the time period; if you are familiar with the book of Daniel, the nation represented as the lion is Babylon.
  - **[Jeremiah 4:6](/bible/jeremiah/4#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/jeremiah/4#v6)</sup> Set up the standard toward Zion: retire, stay not: for I will bring evil from the north, and a great destruction.

    Set up the standard toward Zion, for the Most High will bring evil and a great destruction from the north.
  - **[Jeremiah 4:7](/bible/jeremiah/4#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/jeremiah/4#v7)</sup> The lion is come up from his thicket, and the destroyer of the Gentiles is on his way; he is gone forth from his place to make thy land desolate; and thy cities shall be laid waste, without an inhabitant.

    The lion is come up from his thicket and the destroyer of the Gentiles is on his way to make the land desolate; this is not Christ but a nation that is not us coming to lay Israel's cities waste.
  - **[Ezekiel 26:7](/bible/ezekiel/26#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/ezekiel/26#v7)</sup> For thus saith the Lord God; Behold, I will bring upon Tyrus Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon, a king of kings, from the north, with horses, and with chariots, and with horsemen, and companies, and much people.

    The Most High brings Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, a king of kings, from the north; Babylon is called the destroyer of the Gentiles because the Most High used them as a sword against Tyrus and the other heathen nations, not just against Israel.

**[Joel 1:7-10](/bible/joel/1#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/joel/1#v7)</sup> He hath laid my vine waste, and barked my fig tree: he hath made it clean bare, and cast it away; the branches thereof are made white.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/joel/1#v8)</sup> Lament like a virgin girded with sackcloth for the husband of her youth.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/joel/1#v9)</sup> The meat offering and the drink offering is cut off from the house of the Lord; the priests, the Lord’s ministers, mourn.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/joel/1#v10)</sup> The field is wasted, the land mourneth; for the corn is wasted: the new wine is dried up, the oil languisheth.

- The enemy laid the vine waste and barked the fig tree, making it clean bare. The land mourns because the corn is wasted, the new wine is dried up, and the oil languishes.
- The meat offering and the drink offering are cut off from the house of the Lord, and the priests, the Lord's ministers, mourn.

**[Joel 1:11-14](/bible/joel/1#v11)**
> <sup>[11](/bible/joel/1#v11)</sup> Be ye ashamed, O ye husbandmen; howl, O ye vinedressers, for the wheat and for the barley; because the harvest of the field is perished.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/joel/1#v12)</sup> The vine is dried up, and the fig tree languisheth; the pomegranate tree, the palm tree also, and the apple tree, even all the trees of the field, are withered: because joy is withered away from the sons of men.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/joel/1#v13)</sup> Gird yourselves, and lament, ye priests: howl, ye ministers of the altar: come, lie all night in sackcloth, ye ministers of my God: for the meat offering and the drink offering is withholden from the house of your God.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/joel/1#v14)</sup> Sanctify ye a fast, call a solemn assembly, gather the elders and all the inhabitants of the land into the house of the Lord your God, and cry unto the Lord,

- The husbandmen and vinedressers are ashamed because the harvest of the field is perished; the vine, the fig tree, the pomegranate, the palm, and the apple tree are all withered, and joy is withered away from the sons of men.
- The Most High calls the priests to gird themselves and lament, to sanctify a fast, call a solemn assembly, gather the elders and all the inhabitants into the house of the Lord, and cry unto him.

**[Joel 1:15-20](/bible/joel/1#v15)**
> <sup>[15](/bible/joel/1#v15)</sup> Alas for the day! for the day of the Lord is at hand, and as a destruction from the Almighty shall it come.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/joel/1#v16)</sup> Is not the meat cut off before our eyes, yea, joy and gladness from the house of our God?
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/joel/1#v17)</sup> The seed is rotten under their clods, the garners are laid desolate, the barns are broken down; for the corn is withered.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/joel/1#v18)</sup> How do the beasts groan! the herds of cattle are perplexed, because they have no pasture; yea, the flocks of sheep are made desolate.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/joel/1#v19)</sup> O Lord, to thee will I cry: for the fire hath devoured the pastures of the wilderness, and the flame hath burned all the trees of the field.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/joel/1#v20)</sup> The beasts of the field cry also unto thee: for the rivers of waters are dried up, and the fire hath devoured the pastures of the wilderness.

- The day of the Lord is at hand and shall come as a destruction from the Almighty; the meat is cut off, the seed is rotten, the barns are broken down, and the beasts and cattle groan because they have no pasture.
- The prophet cries unto the Lord because the fire has devoured the pastures of the wilderness and the rivers of waters are dried up.
