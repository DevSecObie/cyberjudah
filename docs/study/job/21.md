Read the chapter → [Job 21](/bible/job/21)

This session covers [Job 21](/bible/job/21) through 24 on the feast of dedication. Job answers his friends by pressing the hard question they keep dodging: if calamity always falls on the wicked, then why do the wicked live, grow old, and grow mighty in power? Job walks through the visible prosperity of the wicked, the twofold reckoning of the wicked of Israel and the wicked of Esau, and the certainty that the wicked are reserved to the day of destruction. Eliphaz answers back in [Job 22](/bible/job/22) with accusations and a call to acquaint yourself with the Most High, Job replies in [Job 23](/bible/job/23) that he would come even to the seat of God and come forth as gold, and [Job 24](/bible/job/24) lays out the crimes of the oppressor who removes the landmarks and gathers all nations to himself.

**[Job 21:1-6](/bible/job/21#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/job/21#v1)</sup> But Job answered and said,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/21#v2)</sup> Hear diligently my speech, and let this be your consolations.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/21#v3)</sup> Suffer me that I may speak; and after that I have spoken, mock on.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/21#v4)</sup> As for me, is my complaint to man? and if it were so, why should not my spirit be troubled?
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/21#v5)</sup> Mark me, and be astonished, and lay your hand upon your mouth.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/21#v6)</sup> Even when I remember I am afraid, and trembling taketh hold on my flesh.

- Job tells his friends to hear his speech and let this be their consolation. Suffer me that I may speak, and after I have spoken, mock on. Let Job speak, then see if you have anything to say, or mock him.
- Job asks a direct question: is my complaint to man? If it were so, why should not my spirit be troubled? His complaint is not against men, so his trouble is justified.
- Mark me and be astonished, and lay your hand upon your mouth. When Job remembers, he is afraid, and trembling takes hold on his flesh.

**[Job 21:7](/bible/job/21#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/job/21#v7)</sup> Wherefore do the wicked live, become old, yea, are mighty in power?

- Wherefore do the wicked live, become old, yea, are mighty in power? This verse opens the whole thread on how the wicked do prosper.
  - **[Ecclesiastes 8:11](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v11)**
    > <sup>[11](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v11)</sup> Because sentence against an evil work is not executed speedily, therefore the heart of the sons of men is fully set in them to do evil.

    Because sentence against an evil work is not executed speedily, the heart of men is set to do evil.
  - **[Ecclesiastes 8:12](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/ecclesiastes/8#v12)</sup> Though a sinner do evil an hundred times, and his days be prolonged, yet surely I know that it shall be well with them that fear God, which fear before him:

    Though a sinner do evil a hundred times and his days be prolonged, yet it shall be well with them that fear God.

**[Job 21:8-10](/bible/job/21#v8)**

> <sup>[8](/bible/job/21#v8)</sup> Their seed is established in their sight with them, and their offspring before their eyes.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/21#v9)</sup> Their houses are safe from fear, neither is the rod of God upon them.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/21#v10)</sup> Their bull gendereth, and faileth not; their cow calveth, and casteth not her calf.

- Their seed is established in their sight with them, and their offspring before their eyes. Their houses are safe from fear, neither is the rod of God upon them. This is why they think the way they do, because no rod falls on them.
- This is going twofold, dealing with the wicked of Israel and the wicked of Esau. This passage is the pride of Esau.
- Their bulls gender and fail not, their cow calveth and casteth not her calf. They are even prosperous in their animals.
  - **[Psalm 49:11](/bible/psalms/49#v11)**
    > <sup>[11](/bible/psalms/49#v11)</sup> Their inward thought is, that their houses shall continue for ever, and their dwelling places to all generations; they call their lands after their own names.

    Their inward thought is that their houses shall continue forever, and they call their lands after their own name.
  - **[Psalm 49:12](/bible/psalms/49#v12)**
    > <sup>[12](/bible/psalms/49#v12)</sup> Nevertheless man being in honour abideth not: he is like the beasts that perish.

    Nevertheless man being in honor abideth not, so the honor does not last.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:31](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v31)**
    > <sup>[31](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v31)</sup> Thine ox shall be slain before thine eyes, and thou shalt not eat thereof: thine ass shall be violently taken away from before thy face, and shall not be restored to thee: thy sheep shall be given unto thine enemies, and thou shalt have none to rescue them.

    When Israel is wicked, curses fall as a nation: the ox slain before your eyes, the ass taken away, the seed given to the enemy with none to rescue.

**[Job 21:11-13](/bible/job/21#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/job/21#v11)</sup> They send forth their little ones like a flock, and their children dance.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/21#v12)</sup> They take the timbrel and harp, and rejoice at the sound of the organ.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/21#v13)</sup> They spend their days in wealth, and in a moment go down to the grave.

- They send forth their little ones like a flock, and their children dance. They take the timbrel and harp and rejoice at the sound of the organ. This is the wicked holiday, Christmas: they make merry, make songs, and send gifts.
- They spend their days in wealth, and in a moment go down to the grave. The wealth is real, but the end comes suddenly.
  - **[Revelation 11:10](/bible/revelation/11#v10)**
    > <sup>[10](/bible/revelation/11#v10)</sup> And they that dwell upon the earth shall rejoice over them, and make merry, and shall send gifts one to another; because these two prophets tormented them that dwelt on the earth.

    They that dwell on the earth rejoice, make merry, and send gifts one to another, mocking the two prophets, the northern and southern kingdom of Israel.
  - **[Zechariah 2:6](/bible/zechariah/2#v6)**
    > <sup>[6](/bible/zechariah/2#v6)</sup> Ho, ho, come forth, and flee from the land of the north, saith the Lord: for I have spread you abroad as the four winds of the heaven, saith the Lord.

    Ho, ho, come forth and flee from the land of the north. Santa Claus mocks you by quoting this while holding you in captivity; they hide the truth in your face to see how dumbed down we are.

**[Job 21:14-16](/bible/job/21#v14)**

> <sup>[14](/bible/job/21#v14)</sup> Therefore they say unto God, Depart from us; for we desire not the knowledge of thy ways.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/21#v15)</sup> What is the Almighty, that we should serve him? and what profit should we have, if we pray unto him?
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/21#v16)</sup> Lo, their good is not in their hand: the counsel of the wicked is far from me.

- Therefore they say unto God, depart from us, for we desire not the knowledge of thy ways. This is why the fool has said in his heart there is no God, and why they call themselves atheists.
- What is the Almighty that we should serve him, and what profit shall we have if we pray unto him? Lo, their good is not in their hand. The counsel of the wicked is far from Job.
- The scripture says they were not born right; the soul that is lifted up is not right in them.

**[Job 21:17-21](/bible/job/21#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/job/21#v17)</sup> How oft is the candle of the wicked put out! and how oft cometh their destruction upon them! God distributeth sorrows in his anger.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/21#v18)</sup> They are as stubble before the wind, and as chaff that the storm carrieth away.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/21#v19)</sup> God layeth up his iniquity for his children: he rewardeth him, and he shall know it.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/21#v20)</sup> His eyes shall see his destruction, and he shall drink of the wrath of the Almighty.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/21#v21)</sup> For what pleasure hath he in his house after him, when the number of his months is cut off in the midst?

- How oft is the candle of the wicked put out? How oft cometh their destruction upon them? God distributeth sorrows in his anger. They are as stubble before the wind and as chaff that the storm carries away.
- God layeth up his iniquity for his children; he rewardeth him, and he shall know it. His eyes shall see his destruction, and he shall drink of the wrath of the Almighty. They are going to drink from the wrath of God.
- For what pleasure hath he in his house after him, when the number of his months is cut off in the midst? The prosperity means nothing once his time is cut short.

**[Job 21:22-26](/bible/job/21#v22)**

> <sup>[22](/bible/job/21#v22)</sup> Shall any teach God knowledge? seeing he judgeth those that are high.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/job/21#v23)</sup> One dieth in his full strength, being wholly at ease and quiet.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/job/21#v24)</sup> His breasts are full of milk, and his bones are moistened with marrow.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/job/21#v25)</sup> And another dieth in the bitterness of his soul, and never eateth with pleasure.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/job/21#v26)</sup> They shall lie down alike in the dust, and the worms shall cover them.

- Shall any teach God knowledge, seeing he judgeth those that are high? One dies in his full strength, wholly at ease and quiet, his breasts full of milk and his bones moistened with marrow, in his prime.
- Another dies in the bitterness of his soul and never eateth with pleasure, poor and needy. They shall lie down alike in the dust, from the best to the worst, and the worms shall cover them.

**[Job 21:31-34](/bible/job/21#v31)**

> <sup>[31](/bible/job/21#v31)</sup> Who shall declare his way to his face? and who shall repay him what he hath done?
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/job/21#v32)</sup> Yet shall he be brought to the grave, and shall remain in the tomb.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/job/21#v33)</sup> The clods of the valley shall be sweet unto him, and every man shall draw after him, as there are innumerable before him.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/job/21#v34)</sup> How then comfort ye me in vain, seeing in your answers there remaineth falsehood?

- Who shall declare his way to his face, and who shall repay him what he hath done? He is brought to the grave and remains in the tomb, and every man draws after him as there are innumerable before him.
- All souls belong to God; he can bring you back in regeneration to judge you, just as we were brought forth into captivity over here on slave ships for breaking the commandments. That is living proof God controls the souls.
- Job says their counsel comforts him in vain, seeing in their answers there remaineth falsehood. When you read the whole book, God lets them know that Job was right.
