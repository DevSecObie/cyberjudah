## Introduction

This session reads Job chapters 5 through 8. The teaching stays close to the text to build understanding of what is happening to Job and why the Most High allows His righteous to be afflicted. Eliphaz counsels Job that the Most High disappoints the devices of the crafty, saves the poor of His people, and chastens the man He loves. Then Job answers, pleading his case and asking to be taught wherein he has erred. In chapter 7 Job describes his anguish, sleepless nights, and loathsome flesh, wishing for death yet never cursing God. In chapter 8 Bildad the Shuhite argues that the Most High does not pervert judgment, and that Job must have sinned. Throughout, the teacher applies Job's affliction to the last days: the plagues and pandemics Esau creates, the hedge God places around His people, and the call to keep the commandments and endure until the end.

**[Job 5:1-5](/bible/job/5#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/job/5#v1)</sup> Call now, if there be any that will answer thee; and to which of the saints wilt thou turn?
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/5#v2)</sup> For wrath killeth the foolish man, and envy slayeth the silly one.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/5#v3)</sup> I have seen the foolish taking root: but suddenly I cursed his habitation.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/5#v4)</sup> His children are far from safety, and they are crushed in the gate, neither is there any to deliver them.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/5#v5)</sup> Whose harvest the hungry eateth up, and taketh it even out of the thorns, and the robber swalloweth up their substance.

- Eliphaz opens by challenging Job to call and see which of the saints will answer him, then declares that wrath kills the foolish man and envy slays the silly one.
- He has seen the foolish taking root, but suddenly he cursed his habitation. The foolish man's children are far from safety and crushed in the gate with none to deliver them.
- The hungry eat up his harvest and the robber swallows up his substance. This paints the collapse that comes upon the man without wisdom.

**[Job 5:6-7](/bible/job/5#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/5#v6)</sup> Although affliction cometh not forth of the dust, neither doth trouble spring out of the ground;
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/5#v7)</sup> Yet man is born unto trouble, as the sparks fly upward.

- Affliction does not come out of the dust and trouble does not spring out of the ground on its own; man is born unto trouble as the sparks fly upward.
- Trouble is part of the human condition. It rises as naturally as sparks off a fire.

**[Job 5:8-9](/bible/job/5#v8)**
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/5#v8)</sup> I would seek unto God, and unto God would I commit my cause:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/5#v9)</sup> Which doeth great things and unsearchable; marvellous things without number:

- Eliphaz counsels that he would seek unto God and commit his cause unto Him, the One who does great and unsearchable things, marvelous things without number.
- The right response in affliction is to turn the whole matter over to the Most High rather than to despair.

**[Job 5:10-13](/bible/job/5#v10)**
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/5#v10)</sup> Who giveth rain upon the earth, and sendeth waters upon the fields:
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/5#v11)</sup> To set up on high those that be low; that those which mourn may be exalted to safety.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/5#v12)</sup> He disappointeth the devices of the crafty, so that their hands cannot perform their enterprise.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/5#v13)</sup> He taketh the wise in their own craftiness: and the counsel of the froward is carried headlong.

- The Most High gives rain upon the earth and sends water upon the fields, sets the low on high, and exalts those that mourn to safety.
- He disappoints the devices of the crafty so that their hands cannot perform their enterprises. This is what the Lord does when Esau comes out with new vaccines, new zoonosis, and new pandemics, splitting and mixing genes between animals to spread sickness around the earth. When it comes to His righteous, the Most High disappoints their hands so they cannot carry out their plans.
- He takes the wise in their own craftiness so the counsel of the froward is carried headlong. Their schemes fall back on themselves. The coronavirus they released did not affect us the way they wanted, but it began to kill a lot of them, and they said they had to manipulate the genes so it could attack more of these undesirables. They trip headlong into their own devices.

**[Job 5:14-16](/bible/job/5#v14)**
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/5#v14)</sup> They meet with darkness in the daytime, and grope in the noonday as in the night.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/5#v15)</sup> But he saveth the poor from the sword, from their mouth, and from the hand of the mighty.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/5#v16)</sup> So the poor hath hope, and iniquity stoppeth her mouth.

- The crafty meet with darkness in the daytime and grope in the noonday as in the night; they walk in sin and darkness.
- But the Most High saves the poor from the sword, from their mouth, and from the hand of the mighty. Even though the heathen rage and their wrath comes at us to kill us, the Lord saves the poor.
- The poor of the Bible is the poor of His people, those that keep His laws, statutes, and commandments. So the poor have hope, and iniquity stops her mouth.
  - **[Isaiah 14:32](/bible/isaiah/14#v32)**
    > <sup>[32](/bible/isaiah/14#v32)</sup> What shall one then answer the messengers of the nation? That the Lord hath founded Zion, and the poor of his people shall trust in it.

    The Lord founded Zion, and the poor of His people shall trust in it, identifying the poor as His chosen.

**[Job 5:17-18](/bible/job/5#v17)**
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/5#v17)</sup> Behold, happy is the man whom God correcteth: therefore despise not thou the chastening of the Almighty:
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/5#v18)</sup> For he maketh sore, and bindeth up: he woundeth, and his hands make whole.

- Happy is the man whom God corrects; therefore despise not the chastening of the Almighty. This is a heavy truth: correction is a blessing, not a punishment unto death.
- The Most High does not chasten us to kill us but to get us better, to show us the way He wants us to go and the instructions we can apply to our lives so we do not keep learning the same lessons over and over.
- He makes sore and binds up; He wounds and His hands make whole. The same hand that afflicts is the hand that heals.
  - **[Psalm 119:67](/bible/psalms/119#v67)**
    > <sup>[67](/bible/psalms/119#v67)</sup> Before I was afflicted I went astray: but now have I kept thy word.

    Before affliction Job strayed, but the chastening turned him back to keep God's word.
  - **[Psalm 119:71](/bible/psalms/119#v71)**
    > <sup>[71](/bible/psalms/119#v71)</sup> It is good for me that I have been afflicted; that I might learn thy statutes.

    It is good to have been afflicted, that one might learn God's statutes.

**[Job 5:19-21](/bible/job/5#v19)**
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/5#v19)</sup> He shall deliver thee in six troubles: yea, in seven there shall no evil touch thee.

> <sup>[20](/bible/job/5#v20)</sup> In famine he shall redeem thee from death: and in war from the power of the sword.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/5#v21)</sup> Thou shalt be hid from the scourge of the tongue: neither shalt thou be afraid of destruction when it cometh.

- In famine He redeems from death, and in war from the power of the sword. Deliverance comes by keeping the laws and commandments, and when you fall, getting back up through fasting, praying, charity, and forgiveness of your brothers and sisters, which are heavily important in these last days.

  - **[Revelation 10:7](/bible/revelation/10#v7)**
    > <sup>[7](/bible/revelation/10#v7)</sup> But in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he shall begin to sound, the mystery of God should be finished, as he hath declared to his servants the prophets.

    The seventh angel's sounding marks the finishing of the mystery, when it is time to be delivered out of this place.

- He shall be in league with the stones of the field, and the beasts of the field shall be at peace with him, and he shall know his tabernacle is in peace.

  - **[Isaiah 65:13](/bible/isaiah/65#v13)**

    His servants who keep His commandments shall eat, drink, and rejoice, but those who refuse the truth shall be hungry, thirsty, and ashamed.

# Job 6: Job Pleads for His Cause to Be Weighed

Read the chapter → [Job 6](/bible/job/6)

**[Job 6:1-4](/bible/job/6#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/job/6#v1)</sup> But Job answered and said,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/6#v2)</sup> Oh that my grief were throughly weighed, and my calamity laid in the balances together!
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/6#v3)</sup> For now it would be heavier than the sand of the sea: therefore my words are swallowed up.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/6#v4)</sup> For the arrows of the Almighty are within me, the poison whereof drinketh up my spirit: the terrors of God do set themselves in array against me.

- Job answers, wishing his grief were thoroughly weighed and his calamity laid in the balances; it would be heavier than the sand of the sea, so his words are swallowed up.
- The arrows of the Almighty are within him, their poison drinking up his spirit, and the terrors of God set themselves in array against him. He feels the full weight of the Most High's hand.

**[Job 6:5-7](/bible/job/6#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/6#v5)</sup> Doth the wild ass bray when he hath grass? or loweth the ox over his fodder?
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/6#v6)</sup> Can that which is unsavoury be eaten without salt? or is there any taste in the white of an egg?
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/6#v7)</sup> The things that my soul refused to touch are as my sorrowful meat.

- Does the wild donkey bray when it has grass, or the ox low over his fodder? A satisfied creature does not cry out; Job cries because he truly suffers.
- Can that which is unsavory be eaten without salt, or is there taste in the white of an egg? The things his soul refused to touch have become his sorrowful meat.

**[Job 6:8-10](/bible/job/6#v8)**
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/6#v8)</sup> Oh that I might have my request; and that God would grant me the thing that I long for!
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/6#v9)</sup> Even that it would please God to destroy me; that he would let loose his hand, and cut me off!
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/6#v10)</sup> Then should I yet have comfort; yea, I would harden myself in sorrow: let him not spare; for I have not concealed the words of the Holy One.

- Job longs that the Most High would grant his request and destroy him, letting loose His hand to cut him off. He would find comfort in that.
- He would harden himself in sorrow and not spare, for he has not concealed the words of the Holy One. Even wishing for death, Job holds fast to God's word.

**[Job 6:11-13](/bible/job/6#v11)**
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/6#v11)</sup> What is my strength, that I should hope? and what is mine end, that I should prolong my life?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/6#v12)</sup> Is my strength the strength of stones? or is my flesh of brass?
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/6#v13)</sup> Is not my help in me? and is wisdom driven quite from me?

- What is his strength that he should hope, and what is his end that he should prolong his life? His strength is not the strength of stones, nor his flesh of brass.
- His help is not in himself, and wisdom is driven quite from him. He confesses he has no resource left in his own power.

**[Job 6:14-17](/bible/job/6#v14)**
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/6#v14)</sup> To him that is afflicted pity should be shewed from his friend; but he forsaketh the fear of the Almighty.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/6#v15)</sup> My brethren have dealt deceitfully as a brook, and as the stream of brooks they pass away;
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/6#v16)</sup> Which are blackish by reason of the ice, and wherein the snow is hid:
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/6#v17)</sup> What time they wax warm, they vanish: when it is hot, they are consumed out of their place.

- To the afflicted, pity should be shown from his friend, but the one who withholds it forsakes the fear of the Almighty.
- Job's brethren dealt deceitfully as a brook, as streams that pass away, blackish with ice and hidden snow, that vanish when they wax warm and are consumed out of their place when it is hot. His friends failed him when he needed them most.

**[Job 6:18-23](/bible/job/6#v18)**
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/6#v18)</sup> The paths of their way are turned aside; they go to nothing, and perish.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/6#v19)</sup> The troops of Tema looked, the companies of Sheba waited for them.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/6#v20)</sup> They were confounded because they had hoped; they came thither, and were ashamed.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/6#v21)</sup> For now ye are nothing; ye see my casting down, and are afraid.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/job/6#v22)</sup> Did I say, Bring unto me? or, Give a reward for me of your substance?
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/job/6#v23)</sup> Or, Deliver me from the enemy’s hand? or, Redeem me from the hand of the mighty?

- The paths of their way are turned aside; they go to nothing and perish. The troops of Tema and companies of Sheba looked and waited, and were confounded and ashamed because they had hoped.
- Now Job's friends are nothing; they see his casting down and are afraid. He never asked them to bring a reward, deliver him from the enemy's hand, or redeem him from the mighty. He asked nothing of their substance.

**[Job 6:24-27](/bible/job/6#v24)**
> <sup>[24](/bible/job/6#v24)</sup> Teach me, and I will hold my tongue: and cause me to understand wherein I have erred.
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/job/6#v25)</sup> How forcible are right words! but what doth your arguing reprove?
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/job/6#v26)</sup> Do ye imagine to reprove words, and the speeches of one that is desperate, which are as wind?
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/job/6#v27)</sup> Yea, ye overwhelm the fatherless, and ye dig a pit for your friend.

- Teach me, and I will hold my tongue; cause me to understand wherein I have erred. Job asks to be shown his fault so he can understand the errors he has made and learn the lesson of his affliction.
- When we go through correction and trials, we must ask the Most High what we can learn from the situation. Let no man, when he sins, say his chastening is from the Lord if it comes from his own wrongdoing; grand larceny or grand theft auto lands you in jail because of you. But when you are on the right path and affliction comes, ask the Most High what you can learn.
- How forcible are right words, but what does your arguing reprove? Job asks how their anger fixes anything. He does not know what he did, so their reproof only feels like wind.

**[Job 6:28-30](/bible/job/6#v28)**
> <sup>[28](/bible/job/6#v28)</sup> Now therefore be content, look upon me; for it is evident unto you if I lie.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/job/6#v29)</sup> Return, I pray you, let it not be iniquity; yea, return again, my righteousness is in it.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/job/6#v30)</sup> Is there iniquity in my tongue? cannot my taste discern perverse things?

- Job asks them to look upon him, for it would be evident if he lied. Return, he says, let it not be iniquity; his righteousness is in it.
- Is there iniquity in his tongue? Cannot his taste discern perverse things? He invites them to judge him from head to toe to see if he is in sin, just as David asked God to search him.

# Job 7: Job's Anguish and the Shortness of His Days

Read the chapter → [Job 7](/bible/job/7)

**[Job 7:1-2](/bible/job/7#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/job/7#v1)</sup> Is there not an appointed time to man upon earth? are not his days also like the days of an hireling?
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/7#v2)</sup> As a servant earnestly desireth the shadow, and as an hireling looketh for the reward of his work:

- Is there not an appointed time to man upon earth, and are not his days like the days of a hireling? A hireling is a laborer who works for wages, a wage earner or paid servant who does his job and gets paid, no strings attached.
- As a servant earnestly desires the shadow, and a hireling looks for the reward of his work, so man's life is short. We come and then we go; we are here one day and gone the next.

**[Job 7:3-5](/bible/job/7#v3)**
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/7#v3)</sup> So am I made to possess months of vanity, and wearisome nights are appointed to me.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/7#v4)</sup> When I lie down, I say, When shall I arise, and the night be gone? and I am full of tossings to and fro unto the dawning of the day.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/7#v5)</sup> My flesh is clothed with worms and clods of dust; my skin is broken, and become loathsome.

- Job is made to possess months of vanity, and wearisome nights are appointed to him. When he lies down he asks when he shall arise, and he is full of tossing to and fro until the dawning of the day. His spirit is in such anguish he cannot rest.
- This is the tossing of Deuteronomy 28: when it is day he wishes it were night, and when it is night he wishes it were day.
- His flesh is clothed with worms and clods of dust; his skin is broken and become loathsome. Satan smote him in his skin, and his flesh was covered in boils, pus, and worms.
  - **[Deuteronomy 28:66](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v66)**
    > <sup>[66](/bible/deuteronomy/28#v66)</sup> And thy life shall hang in doubt before thee; and thou shalt fear day and night, and shalt have none assurance of thy life:

    Life hangs in doubt, fearing day and night, wishing morning were evening and evening were morning.

**[Job 7:6-10](/bible/job/7#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/7#v6)</sup> My days are swifter than a weaver’s shuttle, and are spent without hope.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/7#v7)</sup> O remember that my life is wind: mine eye shall no more see good.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/7#v8)</sup> The eye of him that hath seen me shall see me no more: thine eyes are upon me, and I am not.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/7#v9)</sup> As the cloud is consumed and vanisheth away: so he that goeth down to the grave shall come up no more.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/7#v10)</sup> He shall return no more to his house, neither shall his place know him any more.

- His days are swifter than a weaver's shuttle and spent without hope; his life is wind, and his eyes shall see no more good.
- The eye that has seen him shall see him no more; as the cloud is consumed and vanishes, so he that goes down to the grave shall come up no more, nor return to his house.

**[Job 7:11-15](/bible/job/7#v11)**
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/7#v11)</sup> Therefore I will not refrain my mouth; I will speak in the anguish of my spirit; I will complain in the bitterness of my soul.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/7#v12)</sup> Am I a sea, or a whale, that thou settest a watch over me?
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/7#v13)</sup> When I say, My bed shall comfort me, my couch shall ease my complaint;
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/7#v14)</sup> Then thou scarest me with dreams, and terrifiest me through visions:
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/7#v15)</sup> So that my soul chooseth strangling, and death rather than my life.

- Therefore Job will not refrain his mouth; he will speak in the anguish of his spirit and complain in the bitterness of his soul. He asks if he is a sea or a whale that God sets a watch over him.
- When he says his bed shall comfort him and his couch shall ease his complaint, then God scares him with dreams and terrifies him through visions, so his soul chooses strangling and death rather than life.
- Job is going through the affliction the Lord allowed. There is no God versus the devil; the devil does the Lord's bidding. There was no great war in the heavens where Satan was kicked out; that is mythology, like Zeus, not the Bible. The Most High removed the hedge and told Satan he could touch Job but not kill him.

**[Job 7:16-19](/bible/job/7#v16)**
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/7#v16)</sup> I loathe it; I would not live alway: let me alone; for my days are vanity.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/7#v17)</sup> What is man, that thou shouldest magnify him? and that thou shouldest set thine heart upon him?
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/7#v18)</sup> And that thou shouldest visit him every morning, and try him every moment?
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/7#v19)</sup> How long wilt thou not depart from me, nor let me alone till I swallow down my spittle?

- Job loathes his life and would not live always; he asks to be let alone, for his days are vanity. What is man that God should magnify him, set His heart upon him, visit him every morning, and try him every moment?
- How long will God not depart from him, nor let him alone till he swallows down his spittle? Job feels so tried that he cannot even swallow his own spit without being afflicted.
- Some in this truth will go through these same things: sickness in the body, sickness in the mind, bad dreams, visions, nightmares, and terrors. We must gird up our loins and endure.

**[Job 7:20-21](/bible/job/7#v20)**
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/7#v20)</sup> I have sinned; what shall I do unto thee, O thou preserver of men? why hast thou set me as a mark against thee, so that I am a burden to myself?
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/7#v21)</sup> And why dost thou not pardon my transgression, and take away mine iniquity? for now shall I sleep in the dust; and thou shalt seek me in the morning, but I shall not be.

- Job asks what he shall do if he has sinned, calling God the preserver of men, and why he is set as a mark and made a burden to himself.
- Why does God not pardon his transgression and take away his iniquity, for soon he shall sleep in the dust and be sought in the morning but not be found?
- Job thought he was going to die, loathing his own flesh and sight. We do the same for far less than Job endured, turning off our phones, sitting in dark rooms, and staying away from the feast when we should come out, put on the mask, and rejoice. It is a feast; come out of that depression and have a good time.

# Job 8: Bildad Argues the Most High Does Not Pervert Judgment

Read the chapter → [Job 8](/bible/job/8)

**[Job 8:1-4](/bible/job/8#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/job/8#v1)</sup> Then answered Bildad the Shuhite, and said,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/8#v2)</sup> How long wilt thou speak these things? and how long shall the words of thy mouth be like a strong wind?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/8#v3)</sup> Doth God pervert judgment? or doth the Almighty pervert justice?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/8#v4)</sup> If thy children have sinned against him, and he have cast them away for their transgression;

- Bildad the Shuhite answers: how long will Job speak these things, and how long shall the words of his mouth be like a strong wind? He accuses Job of just talking on and on.
- Does God pervert judgment, or does the Almighty pervert justice? Bildad asks if Job is claiming God is being unjust, picking on a man so righteous.
- If Job's children sinned against Him, He cast them away for their transgression. This points back to Job's children celebrating their days, whom Job sanctified because he knew they might have sinned.

**[Job 8:5-7](/bible/job/8#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/8#v5)</sup> If thou wouldest seek unto God betimes, and make thy supplication to the Almighty;
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/8#v6)</sup> If thou wert pure and upright; surely now he would awake for thee, and make the habitation of thy righteousness prosperous.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/8#v7)</sup> Though thy beginning was small, yet thy latter end should greatly increase.

- If Job would seek unto God early and make supplication to the Almighty, and if he were pure and upright, then God would awake for him and make the habitation of his righteousness prosperous.
- Though his beginning was small, his latter end should greatly increase. Bildad promises restoration, but on the false premise that Job is not already upright.
  - **[Job 1:1](/bible/job/1#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/job/1#v1)</sup> There was a man in the land of Uz, whose name was Job; and that man was perfect and upright, and one that feared God, and eschewed evil.

    Job was perfect and upright, one that feared God and eschewed evil, contradicting Bildad's assumption.
  - **[Job 1:5](/bible/job/1#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/job/1#v5)</sup> And it was so, when the days of their feasting were gone about, that Job sent and sanctified them, and rose up early in the morning, and offered burnt offerings according to the number of them all: for Job said, It may be that my sons have sinned, and cursed God in their hearts. Thus did Job continually.

    Job offered burnt offerings for his ten children in case they had sinned and cursed God in their hearts.

**[Job 8:8-10](/bible/job/8#v8)**
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/8#v8)</sup> For enquire, I pray thee, of the former age, and prepare thyself to the search of their fathers:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/8#v9)</sup> (For we are but of yesterday, and know nothing, because our days upon earth are a shadow:)
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/8#v10)</sup> Shall not they teach thee, and tell thee, and utter words out of their heart?

- Inquire of the former age and prepare thyself to the search of their fathers, for we are but of yesterday and know nothing, our days upon earth being a shadow.
- Shall not they teach thee and tell thee and utter words out of their heart? This is what we do in this truth: search our history, go back into the scriptures, learn our mistakes and our names, and walk in the spirit of strength and prophecy the fathers had.

**[Job 8:11-15](/bible/job/8#v11)**
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/8#v11)</sup> Can the rush grow up without mire? can the flag grow without water?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/8#v12)</sup> Whilst it is yet in his greenness, and not cut down, it withereth before any other herb.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/8#v13)</sup> So are the paths of all that forget God; and the hypocrite’s hope shall perish:
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/8#v14)</sup> Whose hope shall be cut off, and whose trust shall be a spider’s web.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/8#v15)</sup> He shall lean upon his house, but it shall not stand: he shall hold it fast, but it shall not endure.

- Can the rush grow up without mire, or the flag grow without water? These are plants by the water; without their source they wither before any other herb while still in their greenness.
- So are the paths of all that forget God, and the hypocrite's hope shall perish. His hope shall be cut off and his trust shall be a spider's web; any strong thing breaks through it, for it has no foundation.
- He shall lean upon his house, but it shall not stand; he shall hold it fast, but it shall not endure, because there is no foundation in the one who forgets God.

**[Job 8:16-19](/bible/job/8#v16)**
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/8#v16)</sup> He is green before the sun, and his branch shooteth forth in his garden.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/8#v17)</sup> His roots are wrapped about the heap, and seeth the place of stones.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/8#v18)</sup> If he destroy him from his place, then it shall deny him, saying, I have not seen thee.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/8#v19)</sup> Behold, this is the joy of his way, and out of the earth shall others grow.

- The wicked is green before the sun, his branch shooting forth in his garden, his roots wrapped about the heap and seeing the place of stones.
- But if he is destroyed from his place, it shall deny him, saying it never saw him. This is the joy of his way, and out of the earth shall others grow in his place.

**[Job 8:20-22](/bible/job/8#v20)**
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/8#v20)</sup> Behold, God will not cast away a perfect man, neither will he help the evil doers:
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/8#v21)</sup> Till he fill thy mouth with laughing, and thy lips with rejoicing.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/job/8#v22)</sup> They that hate thee shall be clothed with shame; and the dwelling place of the wicked shall come to nought.

- God will not cast away a perfect man, neither will He help the evildoers. He will yet fill Job's mouth with laughing and his lips with rejoicing.
- They that hate Job shall be clothed with shame, and the dwelling place of the wicked shall come to nought. Bildad concludes that Job must present his case, for God is a just judge who does what is right to whom it is right. In the chapters to come Job will present his case and the Most High will give His answer.
