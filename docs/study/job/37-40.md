Read the chapter → [Job 37](/bible/job/37)

This session covers [Job 37](/bible/job/37) through 40, and it is almost the end of the book of Job. In the previous chapter the young man Elihu was basically correct, showing Job the works of the Lord. In this chapter he continues on with the works of the Lord, and then the Lord Himself gives a response to Job. No time is wasted; we go right into [Job 37](/bible/job/37) verse 1.

**[Job 37:1-8](/bible/job/37#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/job/37#v1)</sup> At this also my heart trembleth, and is moved out of his place.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/37#v2)</sup> Hear attentively the noise of his voice, and the sound that goeth out of his mouth.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/37#v3)</sup> He directeth it under the whole heaven, and his lightning unto the ends of the earth.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/37#v4)</sup> After it a voice roareth: he thundereth with the voice of his excellency; and he will not stay them when his voice is heard.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/37#v5)</sup> God thundereth marvellously with his voice; great things doeth he, which we cannot comprehend.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/37#v6)</sup> For he saith to the snow, Be thou on the earth; likewise to the small rain, and to the great rain of his strength.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/37#v7)</sup> He sealeth up the hand of every man; that all men may know his work.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/37#v8)</sup> Then the beasts go into dens, and remain in their places.

- A lot of stuff is going on here. Elihu is basically describing the works of the Most High, and it is crazy, because when it rains the rain does not go past a certain level. As much as it rains, as much as it floods, the waters do not overflow the seas; the rivers never overflow unless they have permission from the Lord.
- Everything has permission from the Lord; nothing moves without Him. Even the animals, the bears, the rabbits, certain foxes, they have dens and holes that they hide in from the weather, and they all do it at the same time.
- Watch a lot of nature shows to get the understanding of some of this. That is how you catch the meaning of what is being described here.

**[Job 37:9-13](/bible/job/37#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/37#v9)</sup> Out of the south cometh the whirlwind: and cold out of the north.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/37#v10)</sup> By the breath of God frost is given: and the breadth of the waters is straitened.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/37#v11)</sup> Also by watering he wearieth the thick cloud: he scattereth his bright cloud:
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/37#v12)</sup> And it is turned round about by his counsels: that they may do whatsoever he commandeth them upon the face of the world in the earth.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/37#v13)</sup> He causeth it to come, whether for correction, or for his land, or for mercy.

- The winds, the frost, the waters, everything comes whether for correction, or for His land, or for mercy.
- The flood during Noah's time came for correction. The rain and the hail around Egypt's time came for correction.
- Rain also comes for the land's sake; the land needs to be watered, vegetation needs to grow. And waters and rain also come for mercy's sake.
  - **[Leviticus 27:30](/bible/leviticus/27#v30)**
    > <sup>[30](/bible/leviticus/27#v30)</sup> And all the tithe of the land, whether of the seed of the land, or of the fruit of the tree, is the Lord’s: it is holy unto the Lord.

    When we bring tithes, which is food, to Jerusalem, you give the tenth part that the Lord blessed you with; that tenth part is the mercy that He shows us.

**[Job 37:14-20](/bible/job/37#v14)**
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/37#v14)</sup> Hearken unto this, O Job: stand still, and consider the wondrous works of God.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/37#v15)</sup> Dost thou know when God disposed them, and caused the light of his cloud to shine?
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/37#v16)</sup> Dost thou know the balancings of the clouds, the wondrous works of him which is perfect in knowledge?
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/37#v17)</sup> How thy garments are warm, when he quieteth the earth by the south wind?
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/37#v18)</sup> Hast thou with him spread out the sky, which is strong, and as a molten looking glass?
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/37#v19)</sup> Teach us what we shall say unto him; for we cannot order our speech by reason of darkness.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/37#v20)</sup> Shall it be told him that I speak? if a man speak, surely he shall be swallowed up.

- Hearken unto this, O Job; stand still and consider the wondrous works of God. Consider the wondrous works of Him which is perfect in knowledge, how thy garments are warm when He quieteth the earth by the south wind.
- He spread out the sky, which is strong, and as a molten looking glass. Teach us what we shall say unto Him, for we cannot order our speech by reason of darkness.
- Now men see not the bright light which is in the clouds, but the wind passeth and cleanseth them; fair weather cometh out of the north.

**[Job 37:21-24](/bible/job/37#v21)**
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/37#v21)</sup> And now men see not the bright light which is in the clouds: but the wind passeth, and cleanseth them.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/job/37#v22)</sup> Fair weather cometh out of the north: with God is terrible majesty.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/job/37#v23)</sup> Touching the Almighty, we cannot find him out: he is excellent in power, and in judgment, and in plenty of justice: he will not afflict.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/job/37#v24)</sup> Men do therefore fear him: he respecteth not any that are wise of heart.

- God is terrible with majesty. Touching the Almighty, we cannot find Him out; He is excellent in power, and in judgment, and in plenty of justice, and He will not afflict.
- Men do therefore fear Him. He respecteth not any that are wise of heart. So it does not matter how much knowledge you have; there is still no respect of persons with the Lord.
- You shall receive for the good that you have done, and you shall receive for the evil that you have done. You could be the wisest person on earth, like Solomon, and it still holds.

# Job 38: Where Wast Thou

Read the chapter → [Job 38](/bible/job/38)

>

- Now the Lord answered Job out of the whirlwind and said, who is this that darkeneth counsel by words without knowledge? Gird up now thy loins like a man, for I will demand of thee, and answer thou Me.
- This is the Lord talking to Job. Before, in the previous chapter, it was Elihu speaking; now the Most High Himself steps in.

> <sup>[7](/bible/job/38#v7)</sup> When the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy?

> <sup>[8](/bible/job/38#v8)</sup> Or who shut up the sea with doors, when it brake forth, as if it had issued out of the womb?

>
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/38#v11)</sup> And said, Hitherto shalt thou come, but no further: and here shall thy proud waves be stayed?

- Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? Who hath laid the measures thereof, or who hath stretched the line upon it? Whereupon are the foundations thereof fastened, or who laid the cornerstone thereof, when the morning stars sang together and all the sons of God shouted for joy?
- Who shut up the sea with doors, when it brake forth as if it had issued out of the womb, when I made the cloud the garment thereof and thick darkness a swaddlingband for it, and set bars and doors, and said, Hitherto shalt thou come, but no further?
- That is exactly what was described earlier. The Lord tells the sea, do not come any further than this. That is why the water goes up on the seashore and goes right back, even in the storm. It goes up on the seashore to a certain point and comes right on back. Only if the Lord says continue going, flood this place, only then does it do it.

**[Job 38:12-18](/bible/job/38#v12)**
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/38#v12)</sup> Hast thou commanded the morning since thy days; and caused the dayspring to know his place;
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/38#v13)</sup> That it might take hold of the ends of the earth, that the wicked might be shaken out of it?
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/38#v14)</sup> It is turned as clay to the seal; and they stand as a garment.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/38#v15)</sup> And from the wicked their light is withholden, and the high arm shall be broken.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/38#v16)</sup> Hast thou entered into the springs of the sea? or hast thou walked in the search of the depth?
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/38#v17)</sup> Have the gates of death been opened unto thee? or hast thou seen the doors of the shadow of death?
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/38#v18)</sup> Hast thou perceived the breadth of the earth? declare if thou knowest it all.

- Hast thou commanded the morning since thy days, and caused the dayspring to know his place, that it might take hold of the ends of the earth, that the wicked might be shaken out of it? From the wicked their light is withholden, and the high arm shall be broken.
- Hast thou entered into the springs of the sea, or hast thou walked in the search of the depth? Have the gates of death been opened unto thee, or hast thou seen the doors of the shadow of death?
- This is the Lord asking Job all of these questions, and we have no answer to it today. Have the gates of death been opened unto thee? We never saw that before. One person saw it, and that was Christ.
- Hast thou entered into the springs of the sea, or hast thou walked in the search of the depth? We Black people do not even like to go in the ocean. We could be on the ship and we are still scared; Black people do not like to go out in the ocean like that and try to search it. But Esau does all of these things.
- That is what has been noticed with all of these questions the Lord asks. Esau tries to go find these things out; that is why he has shows on nature and on the universe. They try to measure everything, even light; they say light moves at like six trillion miles a year, that is one light year.

> <sup>[24](/bible/job/38#v24)</sup> By what way is the light parted, which scattereth the east wind upon the earth?

- Where is the way where light dwelleth? And as for darkness, where is the place thereof? Knowest thou it because thou wast then born, or because the number of thy days is great?
- Hast thou entered into the treasures of the snow, or hast thou seen the treasures of the hail, which I have reserved against the time of trouble, against the day of battle and war?
- By what way is the light parted, which scattereth the east wind upon the earth? Who hath divided a watercourse for the overflowing of waters, or a way for the lightning of thunder, to cause it to rain on the earth where no man is?
- Hath the rain a father? Or who hath begotten the drops of dew? Out of whose womb came the ice? This is something that is way beyond any understanding that we have, way beyond.

- Who hath put wisdom in the inward parts, or who hath given understanding to the heart? Who provideth for the raven his food, when his young ones cry unto God, they wander for lack of meat?
- None of us can call lightning. Sometimes we wish we could call lightning down, but we cannot do that. So He is asking Job all of these questions: you justify yourself, but can you do this? Can you tell Me where the wind comes from? Can you put lightning in your hand? Can you tell Me where light is parted from, where the east wind comes from?

# Job 40: Humble Thyself Before the Most High

- Hast thou an arm like God? Or canst thou thunder with a voice like Him? Deck thyself now with majesty and excellency, and array thyself with glory and beauty.
