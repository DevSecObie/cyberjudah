Read the chapter → [Job 9](/bible/job/9)

This session covers Job chapters 9 through 12, script 365 of the four chapters a day reading. Job answers his friend and pleads his case, but the thread of his answer is that no man can be just before the Most High or contend with him in judgment. The Most High is wise in heart and mighty in strength; nobody who hardens his heart against him prospers. Job confesses that the Most High is not a man like he is, that his thoughts and ways are far above ours, and that even a righteous man cannot bring his case to meet the judgment of God. Along the way the teaching opens who the wicked are that the earth is given into, and who the judges are whose faces are covered.

**[Job 9:1-4](/bible/job/9#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/job/9#v1)</sup> Then Job answered and said,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/9#v2)</sup> I know it is so of a truth: but how should man be just with God?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/9#v3)</sup> If he will contend with him, he cannot answer him one of a thousand.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/9#v4)</sup> He is wise in heart, and mighty in strength: who hath hardened himself against him, and hath prospered?

- Job answers and says a man cannot make himself just before God; if he tries to show his own righteousness to God it is not going to match up, it just is not going to match up.
- If a man would contend with the Most High, he cannot answer him one of a thousand.
- The Most High is wise in heart and mighty in strength; nobody who has hardened himself against him has prospered.
- Look at Pharaoh, whose heart was hardened against God and did not prosper; this is the answer to the brothers and sisters who say God did not hear my prayer, it is me versus God, I hate God, God is dead.
- The brother who got shot, Boosie with the diabetes, said to hell with God, f God, if God said this look at you now; the prayer is that our brother repents and that all our brothers with hard hearts toward God repent, because nobody prospers with a hard mind against God.

**[Job 9:5-10](/bible/job/9#v5)**

> <sup>[5](/bible/job/9#v5)</sup> Which removeth the mountains, and they know not: which overturneth them in his anger.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/9#v6)</sup> Which shaketh the earth out of her place, and the pillars thereof tremble.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/9#v7)</sup> Which commandeth the sun, and it riseth not; and sealeth up the stars.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/9#v8)</sup> Which alone spreadeth out the heavens, and treadeth upon the waves of the sea.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/9#v9)</sup> Which maketh Arcturus, Orion, and Pleiades, and the chambers of the south.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/9#v10)</sup> Which doeth great things past finding out; yea, and wonders without number.

- The Most High removeth the mountains and they know not, overturneth them in his anger, and shaketh the earth out of her place so that the pillars tremble.
- He commandeth the sun and it riseth not, and sealeth up the stars; he alone spreadeth out the heavens and treadeth upon the waves of the sea.
- This is the same spirit read in Genesis that moved across the void and the darkness; that was the Most High.
- He maketh Arcturus, Orion, and the Pleiades and the chambers of the south; those are the constellations, and the Most High calls the stars by name.
- He doeth great things past finding out and wonders without number.

**[Job 9:11-15](/bible/job/9#v11)**

> <sup>[11](/bible/job/9#v11)</sup> Lo, he goeth by me, and I see him not: he passeth on also, but I perceive him not.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/9#v12)</sup> Behold, he taketh away, who can hinder him? who will say unto him, What doest thou?
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/9#v13)</sup> If God will not withdraw his anger, the proud helpers do stoop under him.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/9#v14)</sup> How much less shall I answer him, and choose out my words to reason with him?
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/9#v15)</sup> Whom, though I were righteous, yet would I not answer, but I would make supplication to my judge.

- The Most High goeth by Job and he sees him not; he passeth on and Job perceives him not.
- The eyes of the Most High are in every place; there is nothing we can do to escape him, for his omnipresence and omnipotence reach wherever we go. We can run but we cannot hide.
- When he taketh away, who can hinder him? Who will say unto him, what doest thou? No man can say to God, Lord what are you doing, I do not agree, I veto that, I object your honor.
- If God will not withdraw his anger, the proud helpers do stoop under him; how much less then shall Job answer him and choose out his words to reason with him.
- Though Job were righteous, yet would he not answer, but would make supplication to his judge.
  - **[Proverbs 15:3](/bible/proverbs/15#v3)**
    > <sup>[3](/bible/proverbs/15#v3)</sup> The eyes of the Lord are in every place, beholding the evil and the good.

    The eyes of the Most High are in every place, beholding the evil and the good, so there is no hiding from him.

**[Job 9:16-24](/bible/job/9#v16)**

> <sup>[16](/bible/job/9#v16)</sup> If I had called, and he had answered me; yet would I not believe that he had hearkened unto my voice.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/9#v17)</sup> For he breaketh me with a tempest, and multiplieth my wounds without cause.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/9#v18)</sup> He will not suffer me to take my breath, but filleth me with bitterness.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/9#v19)</sup> If I speak of strength, lo, he is strong: and if of judgment, who shall set me a time to plead?
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/9#v20)</sup> If I justify myself, mine own mouth shall condemn me: if I say, I am perfect, it shall also prove me perverse.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/9#v21)</sup> Though I were perfect, yet would I not know my soul: I would despise my life.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/job/9#v22)</sup> This is one thing, therefore I said it, He destroyeth the perfect and the wicked.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/job/9#v23)</sup> If the scourge slay suddenly, he will laugh at the trial of the innocent.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/job/9#v24)</sup> The earth is given into the hand of the wicked: he covereth the faces of the judges thereof; if not, where, and who is he?

- If Job had called and the Most High had answered, yet would he not believe that he had hearkened to his voice, for he breaketh him with a tempest and multiplieth his wounds without cause.
- If it be a matter of strength, the Most High is strong; if of judgment, who shall set Job a time to plead?
- If Job justified himself, his own mouth would condemn him; if he said he was perfect, that would also prove him perverse.
- This is one thing Job says plainly: the Most High destroyeth both the perfect and the wicked, for every man has his time under the sun; the righteous perish off the earth and the wicked perish off the earth, but we have different judgments at the end.
- The earth is given into the hand of the wicked; Job is pleading his case, saying he cannot speak against God, but here is one thing he does see, that the earth is handed to the wicked.
- Who are the wicked according to the Bible? It is Edom, Esau, and this is prophetic; during Job's time Esau was in the clefts of the rocks, the cave peoples, and he was not rich, but the wicked always prosper.
- Now they prosper with the buildings, the Maseratis, the new Hummers, while the righteous of the Most High are steady being afflicted, getting a little mercy, coming through, staying strong.
- He covereth the faces of the judges thereof; the judges are the Israelites, the saints, for we have a book of Judges. People say only God can judge me, but that will not fly, because those who know his laws and keep his statutes and commandments are able to judge the small matters between brother and brother, sister and sister, neighbors, and situations in the church.
- From the beginning to the end of the Bible all his saints were dark; his people were the blacks, Hispanics, and Native Americans, and there were also light-skinned prophets, but the point is he is covering the faces.
- He covers the face of Christ so that no longer is he shown as the black man he has been since the beginning; now they paint him pale-faced with long hair, and that is not the God or the son of the Bible, that is the antichrist.
- People say we did not do that, it is not our fault you are in poverty, the war on drugs; but if they are not the wicked the Bible speaks of, then who is and where is he, because according to his works and who he is, he is the wicked. We are talking about the Edomite nations, and it does not matter if they come from Russia, Poland, Greece, or Rome, they are the same people.
  - **[Malachi 1:4](/bible/malachi/1#v4)**
    > <sup>[4](/bible/malachi/1#v4)</sup> Whereas Edom saith, We are impoverished, but we will return and build the desolate places; thus saith the Lord of hosts, They shall build, but I will throw down; and they shall call them, The border of wickedness, and, The people against whom the Lord hath indignation for ever.

    Edom says we are impoverished but we will return and build the desolate places, yet the Most High will throw them down and call them the border of wickedness, the people against whom he has indignation forever.

> <sup>[28](/bible/job/9#v28)</sup> I am afraid of all my sorrows, I know that thou wilt not hold me innocent.

- If he be wicked, why then does he labor in vain?
- If he wash himself with snow water and make his hands never so clean, yet the Most High would plunge him into the ditch and his own clothes would abhor him.

**[Job 9:32-35](/bible/job/9#v32)**

> <sup>[32](/bible/job/9#v32)</sup> For he is not a man, as I am, that I should answer him, and we should come together in judgment.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/job/9#v33)</sup> Neither is there any daysman betwixt us, that might lay his hand upon us both.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/job/9#v34)</sup> Let him take his rod away from me, and let not his fear terrify me:
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/job/9#v35)</sup> Then would I speak, and not fear him; but it is not so with me.

- The Most High is not a man as Job is, that Job should answer him and that they should come together in judgment.
- The Most High's thoughts are above our thoughts and his ways above our ways; a man could say one thing and God thinks something totally different, so they will not come together in judgment on why Job is going through what he is going through.
- Many times we think we know why the Most High is doing certain things; if we commit a sin against him then most likely that is the reason, but some who keep the commandments, law, and statutes still assume he is judging them for something when in truth these afflictions have to come.
- There is no daysman between them who might lay his hand upon them both; if the Most High would take away his rod and not let his fear terrify Job, then Job would speak and not fear him, but it is not so.
- Like a child given two ultimatums, a spanking or bed, who takes the bed; the Most High wants to correct Job, but Job would rather take the bed.
