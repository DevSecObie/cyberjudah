Read the chapter → [Job 41](/bible/job/41)

This session of "4 Chapters a Day" covers [Job 41](/bible/job/41) through [Psalm 2](/bible/psalms/2). [Job 41](/bible/job/41) is the Most High's description of Leviathan, the beast He set to rule the sea, and [Job 42](/bible/job/42) closes the book with Job's repentance, the rebuke of his three friends, and the turning of his captivity when the Most High blessed his latter end more than his beginning. The teacher ties Job's trial to our own walk: we endure to the end as Job did, and the Most High will turn the captivity of the Israelites and repay them far more than double what they have now.

**[Job 41:1-2](/bible/job/41#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/job/41#v1)</sup> Canst thou draw out leviathan with an hook? or his tongue with a cord which thou lettest down?
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/41#v2)</sup> Canst thou put an hook into his nose? or bore his jaw through with a thorn?

- The Most High is speaking about Leviathan, one of the beasts He created. He made two great beasts, Behemoth to rule the earth and Leviathan to rule the waters, and Leviathan is the one that deals in the waters.
- He asks whether you can draw out Leviathan with a hook or his tongue with a cord, or put a hook into his nose or bore his jaw with a thorn, comparing this massive creature to fishing for a small fish. Leviathan is a massive, massive creature.
  - **[Job 40:15](/bible/job/40#v15)**
    > <sup>[15](/bible/job/40#v15)</sup> Behold now behemoth, which I made with thee; he eateth grass as an ox.

    The previous chapter opens Behemoth, the beast set to rule the earth, before chapter 41 turns to Leviathan of the sea.

**[Job 41:3-11](/bible/job/41#v3)**

> <sup>[3](/bible/job/41#v3)</sup> Will he make many supplications unto thee? will he speak soft words unto thee?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/41#v4)</sup> Will he make a covenant with thee? wilt thou take him for a servant for ever?
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/41#v5)</sup> Wilt thou play with him as with a bird? or wilt thou bind him for thy maidens?
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/41#v6)</sup> Shall the companions make a banquet of him? shall they part him among the merchants?
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/41#v7)</sup> Canst thou fill his skin with barbed irons? or his head with fish spears?
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/41#v8)</sup> Lay thine hand upon him, remember the battle, do no more.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/41#v9)</sup> Behold, the hope of him is in vain: shall not one be cast down even at the sight of him?
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/41#v10)</sup> None is so fierce that dare stir him up: who then is able to stand before me?
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/41#v11)</sup> Who hath prevented me, that I should repay him? whatsoever is under the whole heaven is mine.

- The Most High asks whether Leviathan will make many supplications, speak soft words, make a covenant, or be taken for a servant forever. This beast is not something a man can tame, play with like a bird, or bind for his maidens.
- No one can fill his skin with barbed irons or his head with fish spears. Lay a hand upon him, remember the battle, and do no more, for the hope of taking him is in vain and a man is cast down even at the sight of him.
- None is so fierce that he dare stir Leviathan up. Then the Most High turns it: who can stand before Him, who has ever given to Him first that He should repay, for whatsoever is under the whole heaven is His.

**[Job 41:12-17](/bible/job/41#v12)**

> <sup>[12](/bible/job/41#v12)</sup> I will not conceal his parts, nor his power, nor his comely proportion.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/41#v13)</sup> Who can discover the face of his garment? or who can come to him with his double bridle?
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/41#v14)</sup> Who can open the doors of his face? his teeth are terrible round about.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/41#v15)</sup> His scales are his pride, shut up together as with a close seal.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/41#v16)</sup> One is so near to another, that no air can come between them.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/41#v17)</sup> They are joined one to another, they stick together, that they cannot be sundered.

- The Most High will not conceal Leviathan's parts, his power, nor his comely proportion. No one can uncover the face of his garment or come at him with a double bridle.
- His teeth are terrible round about, and his scales are his pride, shut up together as with a close seal. They are so near one to another that no air can come between them, joined and stuck together so they cannot be sundered. These are tight scales.

**[Job 41:18-24](/bible/job/41#v18)**

> <sup>[18](/bible/job/41#v18)</sup> By his neesings a light doth shine, and his eyes are like the eyelids of the morning.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/41#v19)</sup> Out of his mouth go burning lamps, and sparks of fire leap out.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/41#v20)</sup> Out of his nostrils goeth smoke, as out of a seething pot or caldron.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/41#v21)</sup> His breath kindleth coals, and a flame goeth out of his mouth.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/job/41#v22)</sup> In his neck remaineth strength, and sorrow is turned into joy before him.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/job/41#v23)</sup> The flakes of his flesh are joined together: they are firm in themselves; they cannot be moved.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/job/41#v24)</sup> His heart is as firm as a stone; yea, as hard as a piece of the nether millstone.

- By his sneezings a light shines, and his eyes are like the eyelids of the morning. Out of his mouth go burning lamps and sparks of fire leap out. Out of his nostrils goes smoke as out of a seething pot or cauldron, his breath kindles coals, and a flame goes out of his mouth.
- This is where the world gets its fire-breathing dragons and creatures like Godzilla. People treat these as mythological, but the Most High is describing a real beast He created. They did not just make these up, they found them in the scriptures, even as the Apocrypha describes the strange beasts in Egypt when it was dark. No man will just run into a Leviathan or a Behemoth, but the scripture is describing this particular beast.
- In his neck remains his strength, and sorrow is turned into joy before him. The flakes of his flesh are joined together, firm in themselves and unmovable, and his heart is as firm as a stone, as hard as a piece of the nether millstone.

**[Job 41:25-34](/bible/job/41#v25)**

> <sup>[25](/bible/job/41#v25)</sup> When he raiseth up himself, the mighty are afraid: by reason of breakings they purify themselves.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/job/41#v26)</sup> The sword of him that layeth at him cannot hold: the spear, the dart, nor the habergeon.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/job/41#v27)</sup> He esteemeth iron as straw, and brass as rotten wood.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/job/41#v28)</sup> The arrow cannot make him flee: slingstones are turned with him into stubble.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/job/41#v29)</sup> Darts are counted as stubble: he laugheth at the shaking of a spear.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/job/41#v30)</sup> Sharp stones are under him: he spreadeth sharp pointed things upon the mire.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/job/41#v31)</sup> He maketh the deep to boil like a pot: he maketh the sea like a pot of ointment.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/job/41#v32)</sup> He maketh a path to shine after him; one would think the deep to be hoary.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/job/41#v33)</sup> Upon earth there is not his like, who is made without fear.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/job/41#v34)</sup> He beholdeth all high things: he is a king over all the children of pride.

- When Leviathan raises himself up, the mighty are afraid. This is a beast that would terrify the mightiest of men, and no man is going to come up and fight him, because the Most High made him to rule the sea, strong and great above all the creatures in his domain.
- The sword, the spear, the dart, and the habergeon cannot hold against him. He esteems iron as straw and brass as rotten wood, so weapons of iron and brass do nothing. The arrow cannot make him flee, sling stones turn into stubble, darts are counted as stubble, and he laughs at the shaking of a spear.
- He makes the deep to boil like a pot and the sea like a pot of ointment, and makes a path to shine after him. Upon earth there is not his like, a creature made without fear, and he is a king over all the children of pride. It sounds so far-fetched that it is hard even to imagine, and the scriptures say the kingdom will be a place we cannot even imagine, so we cannot imagine what these beasts look like either.
