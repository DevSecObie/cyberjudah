**[Job 8:1-4](/bible/job/8#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/job/8#v1)</sup> Then answered Bildad the Shuhite, and said,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/8#v2)</sup> How long wilt thou speak these things? and how long shall the words of thy mouth be like a strong wind?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/8#v3)</sup> Doth God pervert judgment? or doth the Almighty pervert justice?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/8#v4)</sup> If thy children have sinned against him, and he have cast them away for their transgression;

- Bildad the Shuhite answers: how long will Job speak these things, and how long shall the words of his mouth be like a strong wind? He accuses Job of just talking on and on.
- Does God pervert judgment, or does the Almighty pervert justice? Bildad asks if Job is claiming God is being unjust, picking on a man so righteous.
- If Job's children sinned against Him, He cast them away for their transgression. This points back to Job's children celebrating their days, whom Job sanctified because he knew they might have sinned.

**[Job 8:5-7](/bible/job/8#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/8#v5)</sup> If thou wouldest seek unto God betimes, and make thy supplication to the Almighty;
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/8#v6)</sup> If thou wert pure and upright; surely now he would awake for thee, and make the habitation of thy righteousness prosperous.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/8#v7)</sup> Though thy beginning was small, yet thy latter end should greatly increase.

- If Job would seek unto God early and make supplication to the Almighty, and if he were pure and upright, then God would awake for him and make the habitation of his righteousness prosperous.
- Though his beginning was small, his latter end should greatly increase. Bildad promises restoration, but on the false premise that Job is not already upright.
  - **[Job 1:1](/bible/job/1#v1)**
    > <sup>[1](/bible/job/1#v1)</sup> There was a man in the land of Uz, whose name was Job; and that man was perfect and upright, and one that feared God, and eschewed evil.

    Job was perfect and upright, one that feared God and eschewed evil, contradicting Bildad's assumption.
  - **[Job 1:5](/bible/job/1#v5)**
    > <sup>[5](/bible/job/1#v5)</sup> And it was so, when the days of their feasting were gone about, that Job sent and sanctified them, and rose up early in the morning, and offered burnt offerings according to the number of them all: for Job said, It may be that my sons have sinned, and cursed God in their hearts. Thus did Job continually.

    Job offered burnt offerings for his ten children in case they had sinned and cursed God in their hearts.

**[Job 8:8-10](/bible/job/8#v8)**
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/8#v8)</sup> For enquire, I pray thee, of the former age, and prepare thyself to the search of their fathers:
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/8#v9)</sup> (For we are but of yesterday, and know nothing, because our days upon earth are a shadow:)
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/8#v10)</sup> Shall not they teach thee, and tell thee, and utter words out of their heart?

- Inquire of the former age and prepare thyself to the search of their fathers, for we are but of yesterday and know nothing, our days upon earth being a shadow.
- Shall not they teach thee and tell thee and utter words out of their heart? This is what we do in this truth: search our history, go back into the scriptures, learn our mistakes and our names, and walk in the spirit of strength and prophecy the fathers had.

**[Job 8:11-15](/bible/job/8#v11)**
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/8#v11)</sup> Can the rush grow up without mire? can the flag grow without water?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/8#v12)</sup> Whilst it is yet in his greenness, and not cut down, it withereth before any other herb.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/8#v13)</sup> So are the paths of all that forget God; and the hypocrite’s hope shall perish:
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/8#v14)</sup> Whose hope shall be cut off, and whose trust shall be a spider’s web.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/8#v15)</sup> He shall lean upon his house, but it shall not stand: he shall hold it fast, but it shall not endure.

- Can the rush grow up without mire, or the flag grow without water? These are plants by the water; without their source they wither before any other herb while still in their greenness.
- So are the paths of all that forget God, and the hypocrite's hope shall perish. His hope shall be cut off and his trust shall be a spider's web; any strong thing breaks through it, for it has no foundation.
- He shall lean upon his house, but it shall not stand; he shall hold it fast, but it shall not endure, because there is no foundation in the one who forgets God.

**[Job 8:16-19](/bible/job/8#v16)**
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/8#v16)</sup> He is green before the sun, and his branch shooteth forth in his garden.
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/8#v17)</sup> His roots are wrapped about the heap, and seeth the place of stones.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/8#v18)</sup> If he destroy him from his place, then it shall deny him, saying, I have not seen thee.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/8#v19)</sup> Behold, this is the joy of his way, and out of the earth shall others grow.

- The wicked is green before the sun, his branch shooting forth in his garden, his roots wrapped about the heap and seeing the place of stones.
- But if he is destroyed from his place, it shall deny him, saying it never saw him. This is the joy of his way, and out of the earth shall others grow in his place.

**[Job 8:20-22](/bible/job/8#v20)**
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/8#v20)</sup> Behold, God will not cast away a perfect man, neither will he help the evil doers:
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/8#v21)</sup> Till he fill thy mouth with laughing, and thy lips with rejoicing.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/job/8#v22)</sup> They that hate thee shall be clothed with shame; and the dwelling place of the wicked shall come to nought.

- God will not cast away a perfect man, neither will He help the evildoers. He will yet fill Job's mouth with laughing and his lips with rejoicing.
- They that hate Job shall be clothed with shame, and the dwelling place of the wicked shall come to nought. Bildad concludes that Job must present his case, for God is a just judge who does what is right to whom it is right. In the chapters to come Job will present his case and the Most High will give His answer.
