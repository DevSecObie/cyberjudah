**[Job 31:1-6](/bible/job/31#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/job/31#v1)</sup> I made a covenant with mine eyes; why then should I think upon a maid?
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/31#v2)</sup> For what portion of God is there from above? and what inheritance of the Almighty from on high?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/31#v3)</sup> Is not destruction to the wicked? and a strange punishment to the workers of iniquity?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/31#v4)</sup> Doth not he see my ways, and count all my steps?
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/31#v5)</sup> If I have walked with vanity, or if my foot hath hasted to deceit;
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/31#v6)</sup> Let me be weighed in an even balance, that God may know mine integrity.

- Job made a covenant with his eyes, so why then should he think upon a maid? He asks what portion of God is there from above and what inheritance of the Almighty from on high. Is not destruction to the wicked and a strange punishment to the workers of iniquity?
- This is the chapter where it is clear and obvious that Job is starting to justify himself, which is where he actually made his mistake. He asks that he be weighed in an even balance that God may know his integrity.

**[Job 31:7-12](/bible/job/31#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/31#v7)</sup> If my step hath turned out of the way, and mine heart walked after mine eyes, and if any blot hath cleaved to mine hands;
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/31#v8)</sup> Then let me sow, and let another eat; yea, let my offspring be rooted out.
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/31#v9)</sup> If mine heart have been deceived by a woman, or if I have laid wait at my neighbour’s door;
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/31#v10)</sup> Then let my wife grind unto another, and let others bow down upon her.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/31#v11)</sup> For this is an heinous crime; yea, it is an iniquity to be punished by the judges.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/31#v12)</sup> For it is a fire that consumeth to destruction, and would root out all mine increase.

- If his step turned out of the way, or his heart walked after his eyes, or any blot cleaved to his hands, then let him sow and let another eat, and let his offspring be rooted out.
- If his heart was deceived by a woman or he laid wait at his neighbor's door, then let his wife grind unto another and let others bow down upon her. Job is saying if he committed adultery let the same come back on him, for this is a heinous crime, a fire that consumeth to destruction and would root out all his increase.

**[Job 31:13-15](/bible/job/31#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/31#v13)</sup> If I did despise the cause of my manservant or of my maidservant, when they contended with me;
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/31#v14)</sup> What then shall I do when God riseth up? and when he visiteth, what shall I answer him?
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/31#v15)</sup> Did not he that made me in the womb make him? and did not one fashion us in the womb?

- If Job despised the cause of his manservant or maidservant when they contended with him, what then would he do when God riseth up and visiteth? What would he answer?
- Did not He that made Job in the womb make his servant also, and did not one fashion them both in the womb?

**[Job 31:16-23](/bible/job/31#v16)**
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/31#v16)</sup> If I have withheld the poor from their desire, or have caused the eyes of the widow to fail;
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/31#v17)</sup> Or have eaten my morsel myself alone, and the fatherless hath not eaten thereof;
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/31#v18)</sup> (For from my youth he was brought up with me, as with a father, and I have guided her from my mother’s womb;)
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/31#v19)</sup> If I have seen any perish for want of clothing, or any poor without covering;
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/31#v20)</sup> If his loins have not blessed me, and if he were not warmed with the fleece of my sheep;
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/31#v21)</sup> If I have lifted up my hand against the fatherless, when I saw my help in the gate:
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/job/31#v22)</sup> Then let mine arm fall from my shoulder blade, and mine arm be broken from the bone.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/job/31#v23)</sup> For destruction from God was a terror to me, and by reason of his highness I could not endure.

- If Job withheld the poor from their desire, caused the widow's eyes to fail, or ate his morsel alone while the fatherless did not eat, then let judgment come. From his youth the fatherless was brought up with him as with a father, and he guided the widow from his mother's womb.
- If he saw any perish for lack of clothing, or any poor without covering, whose loins did not bless him when warmed with the fleece of his sheep; if he lifted up his hand against the fatherless when he saw his help in the gate, then let his arm fall from his shoulder blade and be broken from the bone. He is justifying himself, saying I help people, I do this, I do that. For destruction from God was a terror to him and by reason of His highness he could not endure.

**[Job 31:24-28](/bible/job/31#v24)**
> <sup>[24](/bible/job/31#v24)</sup> If I have made gold my hope, or have said to the fine gold, Thou art my confidence;
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/job/31#v25)</sup> If I rejoiced because my wealth was great, and because mine hand had gotten much;
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/job/31#v26)</sup> If I beheld the sun when it shined, or the moon walking in brightness;
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/job/31#v27)</sup> And my heart hath been secretly enticed, or my mouth hath kissed my hand:
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/job/31#v28)</sup> This also were an iniquity to be punished by the judge: for I should have denied the God that is above.

- If Job made gold his hope or said to the fine gold, Thou art my confidence; if he rejoiced because his wealth was great and his hand had gotten much; then it would be iniquity.
- If he beheld the sun when it shined or the moon walking in brightness, and his heart was secretly enticed, or his mouth kissed his hand, this also were an iniquity to be punished by the judge, for he should have denied the God that is above.

**[Job 31:29-34](/bible/job/31#v29)**
> <sup>[29](/bible/job/31#v29)</sup> If I rejoiced at the destruction of him that hated me, or lifted up myself when evil found him:
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/job/31#v30)</sup> Neither have I suffered my mouth to sin by wishing a curse to his soul.
>
> <sup>[31](/bible/job/31#v31)</sup> If the men of my tabernacle said not, Oh that we had of his flesh! we cannot be satisfied.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/job/31#v32)</sup> The stranger did not lodge in the street: but I opened my doors to the traveller.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/job/31#v33)</sup> If I covered my transgressions as Adam, by hiding mine iniquity in my bosom:
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/job/31#v34)</sup> Did I fear a great multitude, or did the contempt of families terrify me, that I kept silence, and went not out of the door?

- Job did not rejoice at the destruction of him that hated him nor lift up himself when evil found him; neither did he suffer his mouth to sin by wishing a curse to his enemy's soul.
- The men of his tabernacle did not go unsatisfied; the stranger did not lodge in the street, for he opened his doors to the traveler. He did not cover his transgression as Adam by hiding his iniquity in his bosom, nor did he fear a great multitude or the contempt of families to the point of keeping silence.

**[Job 31:35-40](/bible/job/31#v35)**
> <sup>[35](/bible/job/31#v35)</sup> Oh that one would hear me! behold, my desire is, that the Almighty would answer me, and that mine adversary had written a book.
>
> <sup>[36](/bible/job/31#v36)</sup> Surely I would take it upon my shoulder, and bind it as a crown to me.
>
> <sup>[37](/bible/job/31#v37)</sup> I would declare unto him the number of my steps; as a prince would I go near unto him.
>
> <sup>[38](/bible/job/31#v38)</sup> If my land cry against me, or that the furrows likewise thereof complain;
>
> <sup>[39](/bible/job/31#v39)</sup> If I have eaten the fruits thereof without money, or have caused the owners thereof to lose their life:
>
> <sup>[40](/bible/job/31#v40)</sup> Let thistles grow instead of wheat, and cockle instead of barley. The words of Job are ended.

- Job desires that the Almighty would answer him, and that his adversary had written a book. Surely he would take it upon his shoulder and bind it as a crown, and declare unto Him the number of his steps as a prince.
- If his land cried against him or its furrows complained, if he ate its fruits without money or caused its owners to lose their life, then let thistles grow instead of wheat and cockle instead of barley. The words of Job are ended.
