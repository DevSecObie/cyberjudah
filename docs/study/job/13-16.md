Read the chapter → [Job 13](/bible/job/13)

This session moves through [Job 13](/bible/job/13) to 16. From this point Job is replying to his friends who came to speak to him about his suffering. Bildad the Shuhite, Zophar the Naamathite, and Eliphaz the Temanite have accused Job of being in sin, claiming that is the reason God is putting him through boils on his body, the loss of his children, his cattle, and everything he had. Job knows better. He contends with everything his friends say, because he understands as a wise man that what he is going through is not of man but of the Most High God, and that the same God who brought these things upon him is the God who will remove them. The book is interesting because much of what Job goes through can be applied to our current situation in this captivity, and it carries deep dark parables underneath the words.

**[Job 13:1-2](/bible/job/13#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/job/13#v1)</sup> Lo, mine eye hath seen all this, mine ear hath heard and understood it.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/13#v2)</sup> What ye know, the same do I know also: I am not inferior unto you.

- Job responds to what his friends think they know. He tells them the knowledge you think you have, I myself already know, and I am not inferior unto you in understanding.

**[Job 13:3-5](/bible/job/13#v3)**
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/13#v3)</sup> Surely I would speak to the Almighty, and I desire to reason with God.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/13#v4)</sup> But ye are forgers of lies, ye are all physicians of no value.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/13#v5)</sup> O that ye would altogether hold your peace! and it should be your wisdom.

- Job desires to speak to the Almighty and to reason with God directly, not with these friends. He calls them forgers of lies and physicians of no value, because the things they are trying to tell him cannot help him.
- In verse 5 he is basically telling them to shut up. If they would hold their tongue that would be wisdom in the sight of the Most High, because the things they speak are not true and Job is not ignorant of what is being done to him by the hand of God.

**[Job 13:6-11](/bible/job/13#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/13#v6)</sup> Hear now my reasoning, and hearken to the pleadings of my lips.
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/13#v7)</sup> Will ye speak wickedly for God? and talk deceitfully for him?
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/13#v8)</sup> Will ye accept his person? will ye contend for God?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/13#v9)</sup> Is it good that he should search you out? or as one man mocketh another, do ye so mock him?
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/13#v10)</sup> He will surely reprove you, if ye do secretly accept persons.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/13#v11)</sup> Shall not his excellency make you afraid? and his dread fall upon you?

- Job warns his friends about speaking wickedly for God and talking deceitfully for Him, accepting God's person to defend their own accusations.
- He asks whether it is good that God should search them out. If they secretly accept persons and mock God, His excellency will make them afraid and His dread will fall upon them. Their remembrance is like ashes, their bodies like bodies of clay.

**[Job 13:12-16](/bible/job/13#v12)**
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/13#v12)</sup> Your remembrances are like unto ashes, your bodies to bodies of clay.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/13#v13)</sup> Hold your peace, let me alone, that I may speak, and let come on me what will.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/13#v14)</sup> Wherefore do I take my flesh in my teeth, and put my life in mine hand?
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/13#v15)</sup> Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him: but I will maintain mine own ways before him.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/13#v16)</sup> He also shall be my salvation: for an hypocrite shall not come before him.

- Job tells them to hold their peace and let him alone to speak, and let come on him what will. He is content to let whatever is going to happen to him happen by the hand of God.
- Though he take his flesh in his teeth and put his life in his hand, though God slay him, yet will he trust in Him and maintain his own ways before Him. God also shall be his salvation, for a hypocrite shall not come before Him.

**[Job 13:17-19](/bible/job/13#v17)**
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/13#v17)</sup> Hear diligently my speech, and my declaration with your ears.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/13#v18)</sup> Behold now, I have ordered my cause; I know that I shall be justified.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/13#v19)</sup> Who is he that will plead with me? for now, if I hold my tongue, I shall give up the ghost.

- Job asks them to hear his speech diligently. He has ordered his cause and knows that he shall be justified. He asks who will plead with him, for if he holds his tongue he shall give up the ghost.

**[Job 13:20-23](/bible/job/13#v20)**
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/13#v20)</sup> Only do not two things unto me: then will I not hide myself from thee.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/13#v21)</sup> Withdraw thine hand far from me: and let not thy dread make me afraid.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/job/13#v22)</sup> Then call thou, and I will answer: or let me speak, and answer thou me.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/job/13#v23)</sup> How many are mine iniquities and sins? make me to know my transgression and my sin.

- Job asks God for only two things: withdraw His hand from him, and let not His dread make him afraid. Then let God call and Job will answer, or let Job speak and God answer him.
- He asks God to make him know how many are his iniquities and sins. Job is pleading with the Most High to show him what sin he is in, if he is in sin at all, for the things he is going through.

**[Job 13:24-28](/bible/job/13#v24)**
> <sup>[24](/bible/job/13#v24)</sup> Wherefore hidest thou thy face, and holdest me for thine enemy?
>
> <sup>[25](/bible/job/13#v25)</sup> Wilt thou break a leaf driven to and fro? and wilt thou pursue the dry stubble?
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/job/13#v26)</sup> For thou writest bitter things against me, and makest me to possess the iniquities of my youth.
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/job/13#v27)</sup> Thou puttest my feet also in the stocks, and lookest narrowly unto all my paths; thou settest a print upon the heels of my feet.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/job/13#v28)</sup> And he, as a rotten thing, consumeth, as a garment that is moth eaten.

- Job asks why God hides His face and holds him for an enemy. He feels broken like a leaf driven to and fro and dry stubble pursued.
- He says God writes bitter things against him and makes him possess the iniquities of his youth, putting his feet in stocks and setting a print upon the heels of his feet, while he consumes away as a rotten and moth-eaten garment.

# Job 14: Man That Is Born Of A Woman

Read the chapter → [Job 14](/bible/job/14)

**[Job 14:1-6](/bible/job/14#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/job/14#v1)</sup> Man that is born of a woman is of few days, and full of trouble.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/14#v2)</sup> He cometh forth like a flower, and is cut down: he fleeth also as a shadow, and continueth not.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/14#v3)</sup> And dost thou open thine eyes upon such an one, and bringest me into judgment with thee?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/14#v4)</sup> Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? not one.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/14#v5)</sup> Seeing his days are determined, the number of his months are with thee, thou hast appointed his bounds that he cannot pass;
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/14#v6)</sup> Turn from him, that he may rest, till he shall accomplish, as an hireling, his day.

- Man that is born of a woman is of few days and full of trouble. He comes forth like a flower and is cut down, fleeing as a shadow that does not continue.
- Job asks who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean, answering: not one. A man's days are determined and his months numbered, his bounds appointed by God that he cannot pass.

**[Job 14:7-12](/bible/job/14#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/14#v7)</sup> For there is hope of a tree, if it be cut down, that it will sprout again, and that the tender branch thereof will not cease.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/14#v8)</sup> Though the root thereof wax old in the earth, and the stock thereof die in the ground;
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/14#v9)</sup> Yet through the scent of water it will bud, and bring forth boughs like a plant.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/14#v10)</sup> But man dieth, and wasteth away: yea, man giveth up the ghost, and where is he?
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/14#v11)</sup> As the waters fail from the sea, and the flood decayeth and drieth up:
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/14#v12)</sup> So man lieth down, and riseth not: till the heavens be no more, they shall not awake, nor be raised out of their sleep.

- There is hope of a tree: if it be cut down it will sprout again through the scent of water, and its tender branch will not cease. But man dies and wastes away, gives up the ghost, and where is he.
- As the waters fail from the sea and the flood decays and dries up, so man lies down and rises not till the heavens be no more. He shall not awake nor be raised out of his sleep.

**[Job 14:13-15](/bible/job/14#v13)**
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/14#v13)</sup> O that thou wouldest hide me in the grave, that thou wouldest keep me secret, until thy wrath be past, that thou wouldest appoint me a set time, and remember me!
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/14#v14)</sup> If a man die, shall he live again? all the days of my appointed time will I wait, till my change come.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/14#v15)</sup> Thou shalt call, and I will answer thee: thou wilt have a desire to the work of thine hands.

- Job wishes God would hide him in the grave and keep him secret until His wrath be past, appointing him a set time and then remembering him.
- He asks the question directly: if a man die, shall he live again. All the days of his appointed time he will wait till his change come. When God calls he will answer, for God will have a desire to the work of His hands.
- Job is pleading with the heavenly Father over what he is going through, speaking on the life of a man and what that life is worth once it has been given to him of the Most High God.

**[Job 14:16-22](/bible/job/14#v16)**
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/14#v16)</sup> For now thou numberest my steps: dost thou not watch over my sin?
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/14#v17)</sup> My transgression is sealed up in a bag, and thou sewest up mine iniquity.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/14#v18)</sup> And surely the mountain falling cometh to nought, and the rock is removed out of his place.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/14#v19)</sup> The waters wear the stones: thou washest away the things which grow out of the dust of the earth; and thou destroyest the hope of man.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/14#v20)</sup> Thou prevailest for ever against him, and he passeth: thou changest his countenance, and sendest him away.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/14#v21)</sup> His sons come to honour, and he knoweth it not; and they are brought low, but he perceiveth it not of them.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/job/14#v22)</sup> But his flesh upon him shall have pain, and his soul within him shall mourn.

- Job says God numbers his steps and watches over his sin, sealing his transgression up in a bag and sewing up his iniquity.
- As the mountain falling comes to nothing and the rock is removed and the waters wear the stones, so God destroys the hope of man. He prevails forever against him and sends him away, and the man does not even know when his sons come to honor or are brought low. His flesh upon him shall have pain and his soul within him shall mourn.

# Job 15: Vain Knowledge

Read the chapter → [Job 15](/bible/job/15)

Now Eliphaz the Temanite answers again and reprimands Job for what he is saying.

**[Job 15:1-6](/bible/job/15#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/job/15#v1)</sup> Then answered Eliphaz the Temanite, and said,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/15#v2)</sup> Should a wise man utter vain knowledge, and fill his belly with the east wind?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/15#v3)</sup> Should he reason with unprofitable talk? or with speeches wherewith he can do no good?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/15#v4)</sup> Yea, thou castest off fear, and restrainest prayer before God.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/15#v5)</sup> For thy mouth uttereth thine iniquity, and thou choosest the tongue of the crafty.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/15#v6)</sup> Thine own mouth condemneth thee, and not I: yea, thine own lips testify against thee.

- Eliphaz asks whether a wise man should utter vain knowledge and fill his belly with the east wind, or reason with unprofitable talk that does no good.
- He accuses Job of casting off fear and restraining prayer before God. He says Job's own mouth utters his iniquity and his own lips testify against him, condemning him rather than Eliphaz.

**[Job 15:7-13](/bible/job/15#v7)**
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/15#v7)</sup> Art thou the first man that was born? or wast thou made before the hills?
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/15#v8)</sup> Hast thou heard the secret of God? and dost thou restrain wisdom to thyself?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/15#v9)</sup> What knowest thou, that we know not? what understandest thou, which is not in us?
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/15#v10)</sup> With us are both the grayheaded and very aged men, much elder than thy father.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/15#v11)</sup> Are the consolations of God small with thee? is there any secret thing with thee?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/15#v12)</sup> Why doth thine heart carry thee away? and what do thy eyes wink at,
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/15#v13)</sup> That thou turnest thy spirit against God, and lettest such words go out of thy mouth?

- Eliphaz asks if Job was the first man born, or made before the hills, or has heard the secret of God, that he restrains wisdom to himself. What does Job know that they do not know.
- He points out there are gray-headed and very aged men among them, much older than Job's father. He asks why Job's heart carries him away and turns his spirit against God, letting such words go out of his mouth.

**[Job 15:14-16](/bible/job/15#v14)**
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/15#v14)</sup> What is man, that he should be clean? and he which is born of a woman, that he should be righteous?
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/15#v15)</sup> Behold, he putteth no trust in his saints; yea, the heavens are not clean in his sight.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/15#v16)</sup> How much more abominable and filthy is man, which drinketh iniquity like water?

- Eliphaz asks what is man that he should be clean, or one born of a woman that he should be righteous. God puts no trust in His saints, and the very heavens are not clean in His sight.
- How much more abominable and filthy is man, who drinks iniquity like water.

**[Job 15:17-24](/bible/job/15#v17)**
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/15#v17)</sup> I will shew thee, hear me; and that which I have seen I will declare;
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/15#v18)</sup> Which wise men have told from their fathers, and have not hid it:
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/15#v19)</sup> Unto whom alone the earth was given, and no stranger passed among them.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/15#v20)</sup> The wicked man travaileth with pain all his days, and the number of years is hidden to the oppressor.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/15#v21)</sup> A dreadful sound is in his ears: in prosperity the destroyer shall come upon him.
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/job/15#v22)</sup> He believeth not that he shall return out of darkness, and he is waited for of the sword.
>
> <sup>[23](/bible/job/15#v23)</sup> He wandereth abroad for bread, saying, Where is it? he knoweth that the day of darkness is ready at his hand.
>
> <sup>[24](/bible/job/15#v24)</sup> Trouble and anguish shall make him afraid; they shall prevail against him, as a king ready to the battle.

- Eliphaz says he will declare what he has seen, teaching handed down by wise men from their fathers, to whom alone the land was given and no stranger passed among them.
- He describes the wicked man travailing with pain all his days. A dreadful sound is in his ears; in prosperity the destroyer comes upon him. He is waited for by the sword, wanders abroad for bread, and knows the day of darkness is ready at his hand. Trouble and anguish make him afraid and prevail against him as a king ready to battle.

**[Job 15:25-30](/bible/job/15#v25)**
> <sup>[25](/bible/job/15#v25)</sup> For he stretcheth out his hand against God, and strengtheneth himself against the Almighty.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/job/15#v26)</sup> He runneth upon him, even on his neck, upon the thick bosses of his bucklers:
>
> <sup>[27](/bible/job/15#v27)</sup> Because he covereth his face with his fatness, and maketh collops of fat on his flanks.
>
> <sup>[28](/bible/job/15#v28)</sup> And he dwelleth in desolate cities, and in houses which no man inhabiteth, which are ready to become heaps.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/job/15#v29)</sup> He shall not be rich, neither shall his substance continue, neither shall he prolong the perfection thereof upon the earth.
>
> <sup>[30](/bible/job/15#v30)</sup> He shall not depart out of darkness; the flame shall dry up his branches, and by the breath of his mouth shall he go away.

- The wicked man stretched out his hand against God and strengthened himself against the Almighty, running upon Him on the thick bosses of his bucklers.
- He covered his face with fatness and made collops of fat on his flanks, dwelling in desolate cities and houses ready to become heaps. He shall not be rich, his substance shall not continue, and he shall not depart out of darkness. The flame shall dry up his branches, and by the breath of God's mouth he shall go away.

**[Job 15:31-35](/bible/job/15#v31)**
> <sup>[31](/bible/job/15#v31)</sup> Let not him that is deceived trust in vanity: for vanity shall be his recompence.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/job/15#v32)</sup> It shall be accomplished before his time, and his branch shall not be green.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/job/15#v33)</sup> He shall shake off his unripe grape as the vine, and shall cast off his flower as the olive.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/job/15#v34)</sup> For the congregation of hypocrites shall be desolate, and fire shall consume the tabernacles of bribery.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/job/15#v35)</sup> They conceive mischief, and bring forth vanity, and their belly prepareth deceit.

- Eliphaz warns that the one deceived should not trust in vanity, for vanity shall be his recompense, accomplished before his time, and his branch shall not be green.
- He shall shake off his unripe grape as the vine and cast off his flower as the olive. The congregation of the hypocrites shall be desolate and fire shall consume the tabernacles of bribery. They conceive mischief and bring forth vanity, and their belly prepares deceit.

# Job 16: Miserable Comforters Are Ye All

Read the chapter → [Job 16](/bible/job/16)

In chapter 16 Job answers again, complaining about his situation because he knows he is not in the sin his friends claim.

**[Job 16:1-5](/bible/job/16#v1)**
> <sup>[1](/bible/job/16#v1)</sup> Then Job answered and said,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/16#v2)</sup> I have heard many such things: miserable comforters are ye all.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/16#v3)</sup> Shall vain words have an end? or what emboldeneth thee that thou answerest?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/16#v4)</sup> I also could speak as ye do: if your soul were in my soul’s stead, I could heap up words against you, and shake mine head at you.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/16#v5)</sup> But I would strengthen you with my mouth, and the moving of my lips should asswage your grief.

- Job tells his friends he has heard many such things, and calls them miserable comforters, all of them. Their company is miserable and they are no help.
- He asks whether vain words shall have an end. He says if their souls were in his soul's stead, he could heap up words against them and shake his head at them too, but instead he would strengthen them with his mouth and the moving of his lips would ease their grief.
- When you have a person that is sick and really going through it, instead of comforting them you tear them down, telling them they are suffering because of what they did. They have no knowledge that from the beginning of this book the Most High is letting Satan have his way with Job to see if he will curse God. The friends do not know that; they only think of things from his youth.

**[Job 16:6-8](/bible/job/16#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/16#v6)</sup> Though I speak, my grief is not asswaged: and though I forbear, what am I eased?
>
> <sup>[7](/bible/job/16#v7)</sup> But now he hath made me weary: thou hast made desolate all my company.
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/16#v8)</sup> And thou hast filled me with wrinkles, which is a witness against me: and my leanness rising up in me beareth witness to my face.

- Job says though he speak, his grief is not eased, and though he forbear, he is not eased. The word "asswaged" here means to lessen the intensity, unable to ease, so it reads that his grief is not eased and by forbearing what is he eased.
- He says God has made him weary and made desolate all his company, and filled him with wrinkles which is a witness against him, his leanness rising up and bearing witness to his face.

**[Job 16:9-14](/bible/job/16#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/16#v9)</sup> He teareth me in his wrath, who hateth me: he gnasheth upon me with his teeth; mine enemy sharpeneth his eyes upon me.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/16#v10)</sup> They have gaped upon me with their mouth; they have smitten me upon the cheek reproachfully; they have gathered themselves together against me.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/16#v11)</sup> God hath delivered me to the ungodly, and turned me over into the hands of the wicked.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/16#v12)</sup> I was at ease, but he hath broken me asunder: he hath also taken me by my neck, and shaken me to pieces, and set me up for his mark.
>
> <sup>[13](/bible/job/16#v13)</sup> His archers compass me round about, he cleaveth my reins asunder, and doth not spare; he poureth out my gall upon the ground.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/16#v14)</sup> He breaketh me with breach upon breach, he runneth upon me like a giant.

- Job describes God tearing him in wrath and gnashing upon him with His teeth, his enemy sharpening his eyes upon him. Men gape upon him with their mouths and smite him upon the cheek reproachfully, gathering themselves together against him.
- God has delivered him to the ungodly and turned him over into the hands of the wicked. He was at ease but was broken asunder, taken by the neck and shaken to pieces, set up as a mark for God's archers. God cleaves his reins asunder, pours out his gall upon the ground, breaks him with breach upon breach, and runs upon him like a giant.

**[Job 16:15-17](/bible/job/16#v15)**
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/16#v15)</sup> I have sewed sackcloth upon my skin, and defiled my horn in the dust.
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/16#v16)</sup> My face is foul with weeping, and on my eyelids is the shadow of death;
>
> <sup>[17](/bible/job/16#v17)</sup> Not for any injustice in mine hands: also my prayer is pure.

- Job has sewed sackcloth upon his skin and defiled his horn in the dust. His face is foul with weeping and on his eyelids is the shadow of death.
- Yet this is not for any injustice in his hands, and his prayer is pure.

**[Job 16:18-22](/bible/job/16#v18)**
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/16#v18)</sup> O earth, cover not thou my blood, and let my cry have no place.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/16#v19)</sup> Also now, behold, my witness is in heaven, and my record is on high.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/16#v20)</sup> My friends scorn me: but mine eye poureth out tears unto God.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/job/16#v21)</sup> O that one might plead for a man with God, as a man pleadeth for his neighbour!
>
> <sup>[22](/bible/job/16#v22)</sup> When a few years are come, then I shall go the way whence I shall not return.

- Job cries out for the earth not to cover his blood and for his cry to have no resting place. His witness is in heaven and his record is on high.
- His friends scorn him, but his eye pours out tears unto God. He longs that one might plead for a man with God as a man pleads for his neighbor. When a few years are come, then he shall go the way whence he shall not return.
- Job knows he is not in the sin these brothers claim; he contests everything they say. He understands the whole purpose of his situation is Satan trying to prove that a man put through certain things will curse God, and that was not Job's case. What he is going through can be applied to our captivity in this country today.
  - **[Proverbs 20:24](/bible/proverbs/20#v24)**
    > <sup>[24](/bible/proverbs/20#v24)</sup> Man’s goings are of the Lord; how can a man then understand his own way?

    A man's goings are of the Most High, so how can a man understand his own way; our days are ordered by God, and we must move with purpose knowing this.
  - **[Jeremiah 10:23](/bible/jeremiah/10#v23)**
    > <sup>[23](/bible/jeremiah/10#v23)</sup> O Lord, I know that the way of man is not in himself: it is not in man that walketh to direct his steps.

    The way of a man is not in himself; it is not in man that walks to direct his own steps, confirming that Job's trials and our walk are under the hand of the Most High.
