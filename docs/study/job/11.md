Read the chapter → [Job 11](/bible/job/11)

Zophar the Naamathite answers Job and rebukes his many words, telling him that God would show him the secrets of wisdom and that God exacteth of him less than his iniquity deserves. Zophar declares that the Most High cannot be searched out to perfection, being higher than heaven, deeper than hell, longer than the earth, and broader than the sea. He then counsels Job that if he puts iniquity far away and lets not wickedness dwell in his tabernacle, he shall be steadfast, secure, and full of hope, while the eyes of the wicked shall fail.

**[Job 11:1-6](/bible/job/11#v1)**

> <sup>[1](/bible/job/11#v1)</sup> Then answered Zophar the Naamathite, and said,
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/job/11#v2)</sup> Should not the multitude of words be answered? and should a man full of talk be justified?
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/job/11#v3)</sup> Should thy lies make men hold their peace? and when thou mockest, shall no man make thee ashamed?
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/job/11#v4)</sup> For thou hast said, My doctrine is pure, and I am clean in thine eyes.
>
> <sup>[5](/bible/job/11#v5)</sup> But oh that God would speak, and open his lips against thee;
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/job/11#v6)</sup> And that he would shew thee the secrets of wisdom, that they are double to that which is! Know therefore that God exacteth of thee less than thine iniquity deserveth.

- Zophar the Naamathite answers and asks whether the multitude of words should be left unanswered and whether a man full of talk should be justified.
- He asks whether Job's lies should make men hold their peace, and whether when Job mocks no man shall make him ashamed.
- Job has said his doctrine is pure and he is clean in God's eyes, and Zophar is mocking that.
- Zophar wishes that God would speak and open his lips against Job and show him the secrets of wisdom, that they are double to that which is known; only when the Most High opens your eyes and reveals the secrets of wisdom will you understand.
- Zophar says God exacteth of Job less than his iniquity deserves; this is a heavy precept, because it means the Most High gives us less than we deserve, so Job has a lot of mercy on him even though he cannot see it.
- Even so, Job questioned but he did not curse God.

**[Job 11:7-12](/bible/job/11#v7)**

> <sup>[7](/bible/job/11#v7)</sup> Canst thou by searching find out God? canst thou find out the Almighty unto perfection?
>
> <sup>[8](/bible/job/11#v8)</sup> It is as high as heaven; what canst thou do? deeper than hell; what canst thou know?
>
> <sup>[9](/bible/job/11#v9)</sup> The measure thereof is longer than the earth, and broader than the sea.
>
> <sup>[10](/bible/job/11#v10)</sup> If he cut off, and shut up, or gather together, then who can hinder him?
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/job/11#v11)</sup> For he knoweth vain men: he seeth wickedness also; will he not then consider it?
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/job/11#v12)</sup> For vain man would be wise, though man be born like a wild ass’s colt.

- Canst thou by searching find out God? Canst thou find out the Almighty unto perfection?
- It is as high as heaven, what canst thou do; deeper than hell, what canst thou know.
- The measure thereof is longer than the earth and broader than the sea; you cannot go high enough, wide enough, or low enough to find out the perfection of God. This is where the song ain't no mountain high enough comes from.
- If the Most High cut off, shut up, or gather together, then who can hinder him?
- He knoweth vain men and seeth wickedness; will he not then consider it?
- Vain man would be wise, though man be born like a wild ass's colt.

**[Job 11:13-16](/bible/job/11#v13)**

> <sup>[13](/bible/job/11#v13)</sup> If thou prepare thine heart, and stretch out thine hands toward him;
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/job/11#v14)</sup> If iniquity be in thine hand, put it far away, and let not wickedness dwell in thy tabernacles.
>
> <sup>[15](/bible/job/11#v15)</sup> For then shalt thou lift up thy face without spot; yea, thou shalt be stedfast, and shalt not fear:
>
> <sup>[16](/bible/job/11#v16)</sup> Because thou shalt forget thy misery, and remember it as waters that pass away:

- Zophar tells Job to prepare his heart and stretch out his hands toward the Most High.
- If iniquity be in thy hand, put it far away, and let not wickedness dwell in thy tabernacles; this is Job speaking to us as well, to put off the uncleanliness, the wickedness, the impure minds and ways we had in the world.
- Not letting wickedness dwell in thy tabernacle means the temple, the spirit here, purifying yourself and dedicating it back to the Most High, reforming your mind on a daily basis.
- Then shalt thou lift up thy face without spot; yea, thou shalt be steadfast and shalt not fear.
- Thou shalt forget thy misery and remember it as waters that pass away.

**[Job 11:17-20](/bible/job/11#v17)**

> <sup>[17](/bible/job/11#v17)</sup> And thine age shall be clearer than the noonday; thou shalt shine forth, thou shalt be as the morning.
>
> <sup>[18](/bible/job/11#v18)</sup> And thou shalt be secure, because there is hope; yea, thou shalt dig about thee, and thou shalt take thy rest in safety.
>
> <sup>[19](/bible/job/11#v19)</sup> Also thou shalt lie down, and none shall make thee afraid; yea, many shall make suit unto thee.
>
> <sup>[20](/bible/job/11#v20)</sup> But the eyes of the wicked shall fail, and they shall not escape, and their hope shall be as the giving up of the ghost.

- Thine age shall be clearer than the noonday; thou shalt shine forth and be as the morning.
- Thou shalt be secure because there is hope; thou shalt dig about thee and take thy rest in safety.
- Thou shalt lie down and none shall make thee afraid; yea, many shall make suit unto thee.
- But the eyes of the wicked shall fail; they shall not escape, and their hope shall be as the giving up of the ghost, which is the breath that leaves the body when they die.
