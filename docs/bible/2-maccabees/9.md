<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> About that time came Antiochus with dishonour out of the country of Persia

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> For he had entered the city called Persepolis, and went about to rob the temple, and to hold the city; whereupon the multitude running to defend themselves with their weapons put them to flight; and so it happened, that Antiochus being put to flight of the inhabitants returned with shame.

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> Now when he came to Ecbatane, news was brought him what had happened unto Nicanor and Timotheus.

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> Then swelling with anger. he thought to avenge upon the Jews the disgrace done unto him by those that made him flee. Therefore commanded he his chariotman to drive without ceasing, and to dispatch the journey, the judgment of God now following him. For he had spoken proudly in this sort, That he would come to Jerusalem and make it a common burying place of the Jews.

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> But the Lord Almighty, the God of Isreal, smote him with an incurable and invisible plague: or as soon as he had spoken these words, a pain of the bowels that was remediless came upon him, and sore torments of the inner parts;

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> And that most justly: for he had tormented other men’s bowels with many and strange torments.

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> Howbeit he nothing at all ceased from his bragging, but still was filled with pride, breathing out fire in his rage against the Jews, and commanding to haste the journey: but it came to pass that he fell down from his chariot, carried violently; so that having a sore fall, all the members of his body were much pained.

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> And thus he that a little afore thought he might command the waves of the sea, (so proud was he beyond the condition of man) and weigh the high mountains in a balance, was now cast on the ground, and carried in an horselitter, shewing forth unto all the manifest power of God.

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> So that the worms rose up out of the body of this wicked man, and whiles he lived in sorrow and pain, his flesh fell away, and the filthiness of his smell was noisome to all his army.

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> And the man, that thought a little afore he could reach to the stars of heaven, no man could endure to carry for his intolerable stink.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> Here therefore, being plagued, he began to leave off his great pride, and to come to the knowledge of himself by the scourge of God, his pain increasing every moment.

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> And when he himself could not abide his own smell, he said these words, It is meet to be subject unto God, and that a man that is mortal should not proudly think of himself if he were God.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> This wicked person vowed also unto the Lord, who now no more would have mercy upon him, saying thus,

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> That the holy city (to the which he was going in haste to lay it even with the ground, and to make it a common buryingplace,) he would set at liberty:

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> And as touching the Jews, whom he had judged not worthy so much as to be buried, but to be cast out with their children to be devoured of the fowls and wild beasts, he would make them all equals to the citizens of Athens:

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> And the holy temple, which before he had spoiled, he would garnish with goodly gifts, and restore all the holy vessels with many more, and out of his own revenue defray the charges belonging to the sacrifices:

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> Yea, and that also he would become a Jew himself, and go through all the world that was inhabited, and declare the power of God.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> But for all this his pains would not cease: for the just judgment of God was come upon him: therefore despairing of his health, he wrote unto the Jews the letter underwritten, containing the form of a supplication, after this manner:

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> Antiochus, king and governor, to the good Jews his citizens wisheth much joy, health, and prosperity:

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> If ye and your children fare well, and your affairs be to your contentment, I give very great thanks to God, having my hope in heaven.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> As for me, I was weak, or else I would have remembered kindly your honour and good will returning out of Persia, and being taken with a grievous disease, I thought it necessary to care for the common safety of all:

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> Not distrusting mine health, but having great hope to escape this sickness.

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> But considering that even my father, at what time he led an army into the high countries. appointed a successor,

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> To the end that, if any thing fell out contrary to expectation, or if any tidings were brought that were grievous, they of the land, knowing to whom the state was left, might not be troubled:

<a id="v25"></a><sup class="vn">25</sup> Again, considering how that the princes that are borderers and neighbours unto my kingdom wait for opportunities, and expect what shall be the event. I have appointed my son Antiochus king, whom I often committed and commended unto many of you, when I went up into the high provinces; to whom I have written as followeth:

<a id="v26"></a><sup class="vn">26</sup> Therefore I pray and request you to remember the benefits that I have done unto you generally, and in special, and that every man will be still faithful to me and my son.

<a id="v27"></a><sup class="vn">27</sup> For I am persuaded that he understanding my mind will favourably and graciously yield to your desires.

<a id="v28"></a><sup class="vn">28</sup> Thus the murderer and blasphemer having suffered most grievously, as he entreated other men, so died he a miserable death in a strange country in the mountains.

<a id="v29"></a><sup class="vn">29</sup> And Philip, that was brought up with him, carried away his body, who also fearing the son of Antiochus went into Egypt to Ptolemeus Philometor.
