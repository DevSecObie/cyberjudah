<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> Now when the holy city was inhabited with all peace, and the laws were kept very well, because of the godliness of Onias the high priest, and his hatred of wickedness,

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> It came to pass that even the kings themselves did honour the place, and magnify the temple with their best gifts;

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> Insomuch that Seleucus of Asia of his own revenues bare all the costs belonging to the service of the sacrifices.

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> But one Simon of the tribe of Benjamin, who was made governor of the temple, fell out with the high priest about disorder in the city.

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> And when he could not overcome Onias, he gat him to Apollonius the son of Thraseas, who then was governor of Celosyria and Phenice,

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> And told him that the treasury in Jerusalem was full of infinite sums of money, so that the multitude of their riches, which did not pertain to the account of the sacrifices, was innumerable, and that it was possible to bring all into the king’s hand.

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> Now when Apollonius came to the king, and had shewed him of the money whereof he was told, the king chose out Heliodorus his treasurer, and sent him with a commandment to bring him the foresaid money.

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> So forthwith Heliodorus took his journey; under a colour of visiting the cities of Celosyria and Phenice, but indeed to fulfil the king’s purpose.

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> And when he was come to Jerusalem, and had been courteously received of the high priest of the city, he told him what intelligence was given of the money, and declared wherefore he came, and asked if these things were so indeed.

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> Then the high priest told him that there was such money laid up for the relief of widows and fatherless children:

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> And that some of it belonged to Hircanus son of Tobias, a man of great dignity, and not as that wicked Simon had misinformed: the sum whereof in all was four hundred talents of silver, and two hundred of gold:

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> And that it was altogether impossible that such wrongs should be done unto them, that had committed it to the holiness of the place, and to the majesty and inviolable sanctity of the temple, honoured over all the world.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> But Heliodorus, because of the king’s commandment given him, said, That in any wise it must be brought into the king’s treasury.

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> So at the day which he appointed he entered in to order this matter: wherefore there was no small agony throughout the whole city.

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> But the priests, prostrating themselves before the altar in their priests’ vestments, called unto heaven upon him that made a law concerning things given to he kept, that they should safely be preserved for such as had committed them to be kept.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> Then whoso had looked the high priest in the face, it would have wounded his heart: for his countenance and the changing of his colour declared the inward agony of his mind.

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> For the man was so compassed with fear and horror of the body, that it was manifest to them that looked upon him, what sorrow he had now in his heart.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> Others ran flocking out of their houses to the general supplication, because the place was like to come into contempt.

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> And the women, girt with sackcloth under their breasts, abounded in the streets, and the virgins that were kept in ran, some to the gates, and some to the walls, and others looked out of the windows.

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> And all, holding their hands toward heaven, made supplication.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> Then it would have pitied a man to see the falling down of the multitude of all sorts, and the fear of the high priest being in such an agony.

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> They then called upon the Almighty Lord to keep the things committed of trust safe and sure for those that had committed them.

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> Nevertheless Heliodorus executed that which was decreed.

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> Now as he was there present himself with his guard about the treasury, the Lord of spirits, and the Prince of all power, caused a great apparition, so that all that presumed to come in with him were astonished at the power of God, and fainted, and were sore afraid.

<a id="v25"></a><sup class="vn">25</sup> For there appeared unto them an horse with a terrible rider upon him, and adorned with a very fair covering, and he ran fiercely, and smote at Heliodorus with his forefeet, and it seemed that he that sat upon the horse had complete harness of gold.

<a id="v26"></a><sup class="vn">26</sup> Moreover two other young men appeared before him, notable in strength, excellent in beauty, and comely in apparel, who stood by him on either side; and scourged him continually, and gave him many sore stripes.

<a id="v27"></a><sup class="vn">27</sup> And Heliodorus fell suddenly unto the ground, and was compassed with great darkness: but they that were with him took him up, and put him into a litter.

<a id="v28"></a><sup class="vn">28</sup> Thus him, that lately came with a great train and with all his guard into the said treasury, they carried out, being unable to help himself with his weapons: and manifestly they acknowledged the power of God.

<a id="v29"></a><sup class="vn">29</sup> For he by the hand of God was cast down, and lay speechless without all hope of life.

<a id="v30"></a><sup class="vn">30</sup> But they praised the Lord, that had miraculously honoured his own place: for the temple; which a little afore was full of fear and trouble, when the Almighty Lord appeared, was filled with joy and gladness.

<a id="v31"></a><sup class="vn">31</sup> Then straightways certain of Heliodorus’ friends prayed Onias, that he would call upon the most High to grant him his life, who lay ready to give up the ghost.

<a id="v32"></a><sup class="vn">32</sup> So the high priest, suspecting lest the king should misconceive that some treachery had been done to Heliodorus by the Jews, offered a sacrifice for the health of the man.

<a id="v33"></a><sup class="vn">33</sup> Now as the high priest was making an atonement, the same young men in the same clothing appeared and stood beside Heliodorus, saying, Give Onias the high priest great thanks, insomuch as for his sake the Lord hath granted thee life:

<a id="v34"></a><sup class="vn">34</sup> And seeing that thou hast been scourged from heaven, declare unto all men the mighty power of God. And when they had spoken these words, they appeared no more.

<a id="v35"></a><sup class="vn">35</sup> So Heliodorus, after he had offered sacrifice unto the Lord, and made great vows unto him that had saved his life, and saluted Onias, returned with his host to the king.

<a id="v36"></a><sup class="vn">36</sup> Then testified he to all men the works of the great God, which he had seen with his eyes.

<a id="v37"></a><sup class="vn">37</sup> And when the king Heliodorus, who might be a fit man to be sent yet once again to Jerusalem, he said,

<a id="v38"></a><sup class="vn">38</sup> If thou hast any enemy or traitor, send him thither, and thou shalt receive him well scourged, if he escape with his life: for in that place, no doubt; there is an especial power of God.

<a id="v39"></a><sup class="vn">39</sup> For he that dwelleth in heaven hath his eye on that place, and defendeth it; and he beateth and destroyeth them that come to hurt it.

<a id="v40"></a><sup class="vn">40</sup> And the things concerning Heliodorus, and the keeping of the treasury, fell out on this sort.
