<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> Dead flies cause the ointment of the apothecary to send forth a stinking savour: so doth a little folly him that is in reputation for wisdom and honour.

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> A wise man’s heart is at his right hand; but a fool’s heart at his left.

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> Yea also, when he that is a fool walketh by the way, his wisdom faileth him, and he saith to every one that he is a fool.

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> If the spirit of the ruler rise up against thee, leave not thy place; for yielding pacifieth great offences.

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> There is an evil which I have seen under the sun, as an error which proceedeth from the ruler:

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> Folly is set in great dignity, and the rich sit in low place.

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> I have seen servants upon horses, and princes walking as servants upon the earth.

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> He that diggeth a pit shall fall into it; and whoso breaketh an hedge, a serpent shall bite him.

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> Whoso removeth stones shall be hurt therewith; and he that cleaveth wood shall be endangered thereby.

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> If the iron be blunt, and he do not whet the edge, then must he put to more strength: but wisdom is profitable to direct.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> Surely the serpent will bite without enchantment; and a babbler is no better.

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> The words of a wise man’s mouth are gracious; but the lips of a fool will swallow up himself.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> The beginning of the words of his mouth is foolishness: and the end of his talk is mischievous madness.

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> A fool also is full of words: a man cannot tell what shall be; and what shall be after him, who can tell him?

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> The labour of the foolish wearieth every one of them, because he knoweth not how to go to the city.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> Woe to thee, O land, when thy king is a child, and thy princes eat in the morning!

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> Blessed art thou, O land, when thy king is the son of nobles, and thy princes eat in due season, for strength, and not for drunkenness!

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> By much slothfulness the building decayeth; and through idleness of the hands the house droppeth through.

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> A feast is made for laughter, and wine maketh merry: but money answereth all things.

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> Curse not the king, no not in thy thought; and curse not the rich in thy bedchamber: for a bird of the air shall carry the voice, and that which hath wings shall tell the matter.
