<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> Of these things be not thou ashamed, and accept no person to sin thereby:

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> Of the law of the most High, and his covenant; and of judgment to justify the ungodly;

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> Of reckoning with thy partners and travellers; or of the gift of the heritage of friends;

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> Of exactness of balance and weights; or of getting much or little;

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> And of merchants’ indifferent selling; of much correction of children; and to make the side of an evil servant to bleed.

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> Sure keeping is good, where an evil wife is; and shut up, where many hands are.

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> Deliver all things in number and weight; and put all in writing that thou givest out, or receivest in.

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> Be not ashamed to inform the unwise and foolish, and the extreme aged that contendeth with those that are young: thus shalt thou be truly learned, and approved of all men living.

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> The father waketh for the daughter, when no man knoweth; and the care for her taketh away sleep: when she is young, lest she pass away the flower of her age; and being married, lest she should be hated:

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> In her virginity, lest she should be defiled and gotten with child in her father’s house; and having an husband, lest she should misbehave herself; and when she is married, lest she should be barren.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> Keep a sure watch over a shameless daughter, lest she make thee a laughingstock to thine enemies, and a byword in the city, and a reproach among the people, and make thee ashamed before the multitude.

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> Behold not every body’s beauty, and sit not in the midst of women.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> For from garments cometh a moth, and from women wickedness.

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> Better is the churlishness of a man than a courteous woman, a woman, I say, which bringeth shame and reproach.

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> I will now remember the works of the Lord, and declare the things that I have seen: In the words of the Lord are his works.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> The sun that giveth light looketh upon all things, and the work thereof is full of the glory of the Lord.

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> The Lord hath not given power to the saints to declare all his marvellous works, which the Almighty Lord firmly settled, that whatsoever is might be established for his glory.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> He seeketh out the deep, and the heart, and considereth their crafty devices: for the Lord knoweth all that may be known, and he beholdeth the signs of the world.

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> He declareth the things that are past, and for to come, and revealeth the steps of hidden things.

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> No thought escapeth him, neither any word is hidden from him.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> He hath garnished the excellent works of his wisdom, and he is from everlasting to everlasting: unto him may nothing be added, neither can he be diminished, and he hath no need of any counsellor.

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> Oh how desirable are all his works! and that a man may see even to a spark.

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> All these things live and remain for ever for all uses, and they are all obedient.

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> All things are double one against another: and he hath made nothing imperfect.

<a id="v25"></a><sup class="vn">25</sup> One thing establisheth the good or another: and who shall be filled with beholding his glory?
