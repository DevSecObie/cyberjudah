<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> In three things I was beautified, and stood up beautiful both before God and men: the unity of brethren, the love of neighbours, a man and a wife that agree together.

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> Three sorts of men my soul hateth, and I am greatly offended at their life: a poor man that is proud, a rich man that is a liar, and an old adulterer that doateth.

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> If thou hast gathered nothing in thy youth, how canst thou find any thing in thine age?

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> O how comely a thing is judgment for gray hairs, and for ancient men to know counsel!

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> O how comely is the wisdom of old men, and understanding and counsel to men of honour.

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> Much experience is the crown of old men, and the fear of God is their glory.

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> There be nine things which I have judged in mine heart to be happy, and the tenth I will utter with my tongue: A man that hath joy of his children; and he that liveth to see the fall of his enemy:

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> Well is him that dwelleth with a wife of understanding, and that hath not slipped with his tongue, and that hath not served a man more unworthy than himself:

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> Well is him that hath found prudence, and he that speaketh in the ears of them that will hear:

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> O how great is he that findeth wisdom! yet is there none above him that feareth the Lord.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> But the love of the Lord passeth all things for illumination: he that holdeth it, whereto shall he be likened?

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> The fear of the Lord is the beginning of his love: and faith is the beginning of cleaving unto him.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> Give me any plague, but the plague of the heart: and any wickedness, but the wickedness of a woman:

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> And any affliction, but the affliction from them that hate me: and any revenge, but the revenge of enemies.

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> There is no head above the head of a serpent; and there is no wrath above the wrath of an enemy.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> I had rather dwell with a lion and a dragon, than to keep house with a wicked woman.

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> The wickedness of a woman changeth her face, and darkeneth her countenance like sackcloth.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> Her husband shall sit among his neighbours; and when he heareth it shall sigh bitterly.

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> All wickedness is but little to the wickedness of a woman: let the portion of a sinner fall upon her.

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> As the climbing up a sandy way is to the feet of the aged, so is a wife full of words to a quiet man.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> Stumble not at the beauty of a woman, and desire her not for pleasure.

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> A woman, if she maintain her husband, is full of anger, impudence, and much reproach.

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> A wicked woman abateth the courage, maketh an heavy countenance and a wounded heart: a woman that will not comfort her husband in distress maketh weak hands and feeble knees.

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> Of the woman came the beginning of sin, and through her we all die.

<a id="v25"></a><sup class="vn">25</sup> Give the water no passage; neither a wicked woman liberty to gad abroad.

<a id="v26"></a><sup class="vn">26</sup> If she go not as thou wouldest have her, cut her off from thy flesh, and give her a bill of divorce, and let her go.
