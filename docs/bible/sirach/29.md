<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> He that is merciful will lend unto his neighbour; and he that strengtheneth his hand keepeth the commandments.

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> Lend to thy neighbour in time of his need, and pay thou thy neighbour again in due season.

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> Keep thy word, and deal faithfully with him, and thou shalt always find the thing that is necessary for thee.

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> Many, when a thing was lent them, reckoned it to be found, and put them to trouble that helped them.

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> Till he hath received, he will kiss a man’s hand; and for his neighbour’s money he will speak submissly: but when he should repay, he will prolong the time, and return words of grief, and complain of the time.

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> If he prevail, he shall hardly receive the half, and he will count as if he had found it: if not, he hath deprived him of his money, and he hath gotten him an enemy without cause: he payeth him with cursings and railings; and for honour he will pay him disgrace.

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> Many therefore have refused to lend for other men’s ill dealing, fearing to be defrauded.

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> Yet have thou patience with a man in poor estate, and delay not to shew him mercy.

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> Help the poor for the commandment’s sake, and turn him not away because of his poverty.

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> Lose thy money for thy brother and thy friend, and let it not rust under a stone to be lost.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> Lay up thy treasure according to the commandments of the most High, and it shall bring thee more profit than gold.

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> Shut up alms in thy storehouses: and it shall deliver thee from all affliction.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> It shall fight for thee against thine enemies better than a mighty shield and strong spear.

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> An honest man is surety for his neighbour: but he that is impudent will forsake him.

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> Forget not the friendship of thy surety, for he hath given his life for thee.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> A sinner will overthrow the good estate of his surety:

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> And he that is of an unthankful mind will leave him in danger that delivered him.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> Suretiship hath undone many of good estate, and shaken them as a wave of the sea: mighty men hath it driven from their houses, so that they wandered among strange nations.

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> A wicked man transgressing the commandments of the Lord shall fall into suretiship: and he that undertaketh and followeth other men’s business for gain shall fall into suits.

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> Help thy neighbour according to thy power, and beware that thou thyself fall not into the same.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> The chief thing for life is water, and bread, and clothing, and an house to cover shame.

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> Better is the life of a poor man in a mean cottage, than delicate fare in another man’s house.

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> Be it little or much, hold thee contented, that thou hear not the reproach of thy house.

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> For it is a miserable life to go from house to house: for where thou art a stranger, thou darest not open thy mouth.

<a id="v25"></a><sup class="vn">25</sup> Thou shalt entertain, and feast, and have no thanks: moreover thou shalt hear bitter words:

<a id="v26"></a><sup class="vn">26</sup> Come, thou stranger, and furnish a table, and feed me of that thou hast ready.

<a id="v27"></a><sup class="vn">27</sup> Give place, thou stranger, to an honourable man; my brother cometh to be lodged, and I have need of mine house.

<a id="v28"></a><sup class="vn">28</sup> These things are grievous to a man of understanding; the upbraiding of houseroom, and reproaching of the lender.
