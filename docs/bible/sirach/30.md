<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> He that loveth his son causeth him oft to feel the rod, that he may have joy of him in the end.

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> He that chastiseth his son shall have joy in him, and shall rejoice of him among his acquaintance.

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> He that teacheth his son grieveth the enemy: and before his friends he shall rejoice of him.

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> Though his father die, yet he is as though he were not dead: for he hath left one behind him that is like himself.

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> While he lived, he saw and rejoiced in him: and when he died, he was not sorrowful.

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> He left behind him an avenger against his enemies, and one that shall requite kindness to his friends.

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> He that maketh too much of his son shall bind up his wounds; and his bowels will be troubled at every cry.

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> An horse not broken becometh headstrong: and a child left to himself will be wilful.

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> Cocker thy child, and he shall make thee afraid: play with him, and he will bring thee to heaviness.

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> Laugh not with him, lest thou have sorrow with him, and lest thou gnash thy teeth in the end.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> Give him no liberty in his youth, and wink not at his follies.

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> Bow down his neck while he is young, and beat him on the sides while he is a child, lest he wax stubborn, and be disobedient unto thee, and so bring sorrow to thine heart.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> Chastise thy son, and hold him to labour, lest his lewd behaviour be an offence unto thee.

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> Better is the poor, being sound and strong of constitution, than a rich man that is afflicted in his body.

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> Health and good estate of body are above all gold, and a strong body above infinite wealth.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> There is no riches above a sound body, and no joy above the joy of the heart.

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> Death is better than a bitter life or continual sickness.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> Delicates poured upon a mouth shut up are as messes of meat set upon a grave.

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> What good doeth the offering unto an idol? for neither can it eat nor smell: so is he that is persecuted of the Lord.

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> He seeth with his eyes and groaneth, as an eunuch that embraceth a virgin and sigheth.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> Give not over thy mind to heaviness, and afflict not thyself in thine own counsel.

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> The gladness of the heart is the life of man, and the joyfulness of a man prolongeth his days.

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> Love thine own soul, and comfort thy heart, remove sorrow far from thee: for sorrow hath killed many, and there is no profit therein.

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> Envy and wrath shorten the life, and carefulness bringeth age before the time.

<a id="v25"></a><sup class="vn">25</sup> A cheerful and good heart will have a care of his meat and diet.
