<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> A slothful man is compared to a filthy stone, and every one will hiss him out to his disgrace.

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> A slothful man is compared to the filth of a dunghill: every man that takes it up will shake his hand.

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> An evilnurtured man is the dishonour of his father that begat him: and a foolish daughter is born to his loss.

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> A wise daughter shall bring an inheritance to her husband: but she that liveth dishonestly is her father’s heaviness.

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> She that is bold dishonoureth both her father and her husband, but they both shall despise her.

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> A tale out of season is as musick in mourning: but stripes and correction of wisdom are never out of time.

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> Whoso teacheth a fool is as one that glueth a potsherd together, and as he that waketh one from a sound sleep.

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> He that telleth a tale to a fool speaketh to one in a slumber: when he hath told his tale, he will say, What is the matter?

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> If children live honestly, and have wherewithal, they shall cover the baseness of their parents.

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> But children, being haughty, through disdain and want of nurture do stain the nobility of their kindred.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> Weep for the dead, for he hath lost the light: and weep for the fool, for he wanteth understanding: make little weeping for the dead, for he is at rest: but the life of the fool is worse than death.

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> Seven days do men mourn for him that is dead; but for a fool and an ungodly man all the days of his life.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> Talk not much with a fool, and go not to him that hath no understanding: beware of him, lest thou have trouble, and thou shalt never be defiled with his fooleries: depart from him, and thou shalt find rest, and never be disquieted with madness.

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> What is heavier than lead? and what is the name thereof, but a fool?

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> Sand, and salt, and a mass of iron, is easier to bear, than a man without understanding.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> As timber girt and bound together in a building cannot be loosed with shaking: so the heart that is stablished by advised counsel shall fear at no time.

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> A heart settled upon a thought of understanding is as a fair plaistering on the wall of a gallery.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> Pales set on an high place will never stand against the wind: so a fearful heart in the imagination of a fool cannot stand against any fear.

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> He that pricketh the eye will make tears to fall: and he that pricketh the heart maketh it to shew her knowledge.

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> Whoso casteth a stone at the birds frayeth them away: and he that upbraideth his friend breaketh friendship.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> Though thou drewest a sword at thy friend, yet despair not: for there may be a returning to favour.

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> If thou hast opened thy mouth against thy friend, fear not; for there may be a reconciliation: except for upbraiding, or pride, or disclosing of secrets, or a treacherous wound: for for these things every friend will depart.

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> Be faithful to thy neighbour in his poverty, that thou mayest rejoice in his prosperity: abide stedfast unto him in the time of his trouble, that thou mayest be heir with him in his heritage: for a mean estate is not always to be contemned: nor the rich that is foolish to be had in admiration.

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> As the vapour and smoke of a furnace goeth before the fire; so reviling before blood.

<a id="v25"></a><sup class="vn">25</sup> I will not be ashamed to defend a friend; neither will I hide myself from him.

<a id="v26"></a><sup class="vn">26</sup> And if any evil happen unto me by him, every one that heareth it will beware of him.

<a id="v27"></a><sup class="vn">27</sup> Who shall set a watch before my mouth, and a seal of wisdom upon my lips, that I fall not suddenly by them, and that my tongue destroy me not?
