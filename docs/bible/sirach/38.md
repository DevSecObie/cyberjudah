<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> Honour a physician with the honour due unto him for the uses which ye may have of him: for the Lord hath created him.

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> For of the most High cometh healing, and he shall receive honour of the king.

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> The skill of the physician shall lift up his head: and in the sight of great men he shall be in admiration.

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> The Lord hath created medicines out of the earth; and he that is wise will not abhor them.

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> Was not the water made sweet with wood, that the virtue thereof might be known?

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> And he hath given men skill, that he might be honoured in his marvellous works.

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> With such doth he heal men, and taketh away their pains.

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> Of such doth the apothecary make a confection; and of his works there is no end; and from him is peace over all the earth,

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> My son, in thy sickness be not negligent: but pray unto the Lord, and he will make thee whole.

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> Leave off from sin, and order thine hands aright, and cleanse thy heart from all wickedness.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> Give a sweet savour, and a memorial of fine flour; and make a fat offering, as not being.

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> Then give place to the physician, for the Lord hath created him: let him not go from thee, for thou hast need of him.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> There is a time when in their hands there is good success.

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> For they shall also pray unto the Lord, that he would prosper that, which they give for ease and remedy to prolong life.

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> He that sinneth before his Maker, let him fall into the hand of the physician.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> My son, let tears fall down over the dead, and begin to lament, as if thou hadst suffered great harm thyself; and then cover his body according to the custom, and neglect not his burial.

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> Weep bitterly, and make great moan, and use lamentation, as he is worthy, and that a day or two, lest thou be evil spoken of: and then comfort thyself for thy heaviness.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> For of heaviness cometh death, and the heaviness of the heart breaketh strength.

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> In affliction also sorrow remaineth: and the life of the poor is the curse of the heart.

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> Take no heaviness to heart: drive it away, and member the last end.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> Forget it not, for there is no turning again: thou shalt not do him good, but hurt thyself.

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> Remember my judgment: for thine also shall be so; yesterday for me, and to day for thee.

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> When the dead is at rest, let his remembrance rest; and be comforted for him, when his Spirit is departed from him.

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> The wisdom of a learned man cometh by opportunity of leisure: and he that hath little business shall become wise.

<a id="v25"></a><sup class="vn">25</sup> How can he get wisdom that holdeth the plough, and that glorieth in the goad, that driveth oxen, and is occupied in their labours, and whose talk is of bullocks?

<a id="v26"></a><sup class="vn">26</sup> He giveth his mind to make furrows; and is diligent to give the kine fodder.

<a id="v27"></a><sup class="vn">27</sup> So every carpenter and workmaster, that laboureth night and day: and they that cut and grave seals, and are diligent to make great variety, and give themselves to counterfeit imagery, and watch to finish a work:

<a id="v28"></a><sup class="vn">28</sup> The smith also sitting by the anvil, and considering the iron work, the vapour of the fire wasteth his flesh, and he fighteth with the heat of the furnace: the noise of the hammer and the anvil is ever in his ears, and his eyes look still upon the pattern of the thing that he maketh; he setteth his mind to finish his work, and watcheth to polish it perfectly:

<a id="v29"></a><sup class="vn">29</sup> So doth the potter sitting at his work, and turning the wheel about with his feet, who is alway carefully set at his work, and maketh all his work by number;

<a id="v30"></a><sup class="vn">30</sup> He fashioneth the clay with his arm, and boweth down his strength before his feet; he applieth himself to lead it over; and he is diligent to make clean the furnace:

<a id="v31"></a><sup class="vn">31</sup> All these trust to their hands: and every one is wise in his work.

<a id="v32"></a><sup class="vn">32</sup> Without these cannot a city be inhabited: and they shall not dwell where they will, nor go up and down:

<a id="v33"></a><sup class="vn">33</sup> They shall not be sought for in publick counsel, nor sit high in the congregation: they shall not sit on the judges’ seat, nor understand the sentence of judgment: they cannot declare justice and judgment; and they shall not be found where parables are spoken.

<a id="v34"></a><sup class="vn">34</sup> But they will maintain the state of the world, and all their desire is in the work of their craft.
