<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> As snow in summer, and as rain in harvest, so honour is not seemly for a fool.

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> As the bird by wandering, as the swallow by flying, so the curse causeless shall not come.

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> A whip for the horse, a bridle for the ass, and a rod for the fool’s back.

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou also be like unto him.

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit.

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> He that sendeth a message by the hand of a fool cutteth off the feet, and drinketh damage.

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> The legs of the lame are not equal: so is a parable in the mouth of fools.

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> As he that bindeth a stone in a sling, so is he that giveth honour to a fool.

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> As a thorn goeth up into the hand of a drunkard, so is a parable in the mouth of fools.

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> The great God that formed all things both rewardeth the fool, and rewardeth transgressors.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> As a dog returneth to his vomit, so a fool returneth to his folly.

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> Seest thou a man wise in his own conceit? there is more hope of a fool than of him.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> The slothful man saith, There is a lion in the way; a lion is in the streets.

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> As the door turneth upon his hinges, so doth the slothful upon his bed.

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> The slothful hideth his hand in his bosom; it grieveth him to bring it again to his mouth.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> The sluggard is wiser in his own conceit than seven men that can render a reason.

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> He that passeth by, and meddleth with strife belonging not to him, is like one that taketh a dog by the ears.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> As a mad man who casteth firebrands, arrows, and death,

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> So is the man that deceiveth his neighbour, and saith, Am not I in sport?

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> Where no wood is, there the fire goeth out: so where there is no talebearer, the strife ceaseth.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> As coals are to burning coals, and wood to fire; so is a contentious man to kindle strife.

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly.

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> Burning lips and a wicked heart are like a potsherd covered with silver dross.

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> He that hateth dissembleth with his lips, and layeth up deceit within him;

<a id="v25"></a><sup class="vn">25</sup> When he speaketh fair, believe him not: for there are seven abominations in his heart.

<a id="v26"></a><sup class="vn">26</sup> Whose hatred is covered by deceit, his wickedness shall be shewed before the whole congregation.

<a id="v27"></a><sup class="vn">27</sup> Whoso diggeth a pit shall fall therein: and he that rolleth a stone, it will return upon him.

<a id="v28"></a><sup class="vn">28</sup> A lying tongue hateth those that are afflicted by it; and a flattering mouth worketh ruin.
