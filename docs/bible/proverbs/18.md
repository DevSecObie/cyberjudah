<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> Through desire a man, having separated himself, seeketh and intermeddleth with all wisdom.

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> A fool hath no delight in understanding, but that his heart may discover itself.

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> When the wicked cometh, then cometh also contempt, and with ignominy reproach.

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> The words of a man’s mouth are as deep waters, and the wellspring of wisdom as a flowing brook.

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> It is not good to accept the person of the wicked, to overthrow the righteous in judgment.

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> A fool’s lips enter into contention, and his mouth calleth for strokes.

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> A fool’s mouth is his destruction, and his lips are the snare of his soul.

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly.

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> He also that is slothful in his work is brother to him that is a great waster.

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> The name of the Lord is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> The rich man’s wealth is his strong city, and as an high wall in his own conceit.

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> Before destruction the heart of man is haughty, and before honour is humility.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him.

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> The spirit of a man will sustain his infirmity; but a wounded spirit who can bear?

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> The heart of the prudent getteth knowledge; and the ear of the wise seeketh knowledge.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> A man’s gift maketh room for him, and bringeth him before great men.

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> The lot causeth contentions to cease, and parteth between the mighty.

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> A brother offended is harder to be won than a strong city: and their contentions are like the bars of a castle.

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> A man’s belly shall be satisfied with the fruit of his mouth; and with the increase of his lips shall he be filled.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof.

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> Whoso findeth a wife findeth a good thing, and obtaineth favour of the Lord.

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> The poor useth intreaties; but the rich answereth roughly.

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> A man that hath friends must shew himself friendly: and there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother.
