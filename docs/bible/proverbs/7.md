<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> My son, keep my words, and lay up my commandments with thee.

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> Keep my commandments, and live; and my law as the apple of thine eye.

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> Bind them upon thy fingers, write them upon the table of thine heart.

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> Say unto wisdom, Thou art my sister; and call understanding thy kinswoman:

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> That they may keep thee from the strange woman, from the stranger which flattereth with her words.

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> For at the window of my house I looked through my casement,

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> And beheld among the simple ones, I discerned among the youths, a young man void of understanding,

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> Passing through the street near her corner; and he went the way to her house,

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> In the twilight, in the evening, in the black and dark night:

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> And, behold, there met him a woman with the attire of an harlot, and subtil of heart.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> (She is loud and stubborn; her feet abide not in her house:

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> Now is she without, now in the streets, and lieth in wait at every corner.)

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> So she caught him, and kissed him, and with an impudent face said unto him,

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> I have peace offerings with me; this day have I payed my vows.

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> Therefore came I forth to meet thee, diligently to seek thy face, and I have found thee.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> I have decked my bed with coverings of tapestry, with carved works, with fine linen of Egypt.

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> I have perfumed my bed with myrrh, aloes, and cinnamon.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> Come, let us take our fill of love until the morning: let us solace ourselves with loves.

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> For the goodman is not at home, he is gone a long journey:

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> He hath taken a bag of money with him, and will come home at the day appointed.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> With her much fair speech she caused him to yield, with the flattering of her lips she forced him.

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> He goeth after her straightway, as an ox goeth to the slaughter, or as a fool to the correction of the stocks;

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> Till a dart strike through his liver; as a bird hasteth to the snare, and knoweth not that it is for his life.

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> Hearken unto me now therefore, O ye children, and attend to the words of my mouth.

<a id="v25"></a><sup class="vn">25</sup> Let not thine heart decline to her ways, go not astray in her paths.

<a id="v26"></a><sup class="vn">26</sup> For she hath cast down many wounded: yea, many strong men have been slain by her.

<a id="v27"></a><sup class="vn">27</sup> Her house is the way to hell, going down to the chambers of death.
