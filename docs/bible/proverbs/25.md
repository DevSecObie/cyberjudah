<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> These are also proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out.

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter.

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> The heaven for height, and the earth for depth, and the heart of kings is unsearchable.

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> Take away the dross from the silver, and there shall come forth a vessel for the finer.

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> Take away the wicked from before the king, and his throne shall be established in righteousness.

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> Put not forth thyself in the presence of the king, and stand not in the place of great men:

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> For better it is that it be said unto thee, Come up hither; than that thou shouldest be put lower in the presence of the prince whom thine eyes have seen.

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> Go not forth hastily to strive, lest thou know not what to do in the end thereof, when thy neighbour hath put thee to shame.

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> Debate thy cause with thy neighbour himself; and discover not a secret to another:

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> Lest he that heareth it put thee to shame, and thine infamy turn not away.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in pictures of silver.

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> As an earring of gold, and an ornament of fine gold, so is a wise reprover upon an obedient ear.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> As the cold of snow in the time of harvest, so is a faithful messenger to them that send him: for he refresheth the soul of his masters.

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> Whoso boasteth himself of a false gift is like clouds and wind without rain.

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> By long forbearing is a prince persuaded, and a soft tongue breaketh the bone.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> Hast thou found honey? eat so much as is sufficient for thee, lest thou be filled therewith, and vomit it.

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> Withdraw thy foot from thy neighbour’s house; lest he be weary of thee, and so hate thee.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> A man that beareth false witness against his neighbour is a maul, and a sword, and a sharp arrow.

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> Confidence in an unfaithful man in time of trouble is like a broken tooth, and a foot out of joint.

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> As he that taketh away a garment in cold weather, and as vinegar upon nitre, so is he that singeth songs to an heavy heart.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread to eat; and if he be thirsty, give him water to drink:

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> For thou shalt heap coals of fire upon his head, and the Lord shall reward thee.

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> The north wind driveth away rain: so doth an angry countenance a backbiting tongue.

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> It is better to dwell in the corner of the housetop, than with a brawling woman and in a wide house.

<a id="v25"></a><sup class="vn">25</sup> As cold waters to a thirsty soul, so is good news from a far country.

<a id="v26"></a><sup class="vn">26</sup> A righteous man falling down before the wicked is as a troubled fountain, and a corrupt spring.

<a id="v27"></a><sup class="vn">27</sup> It is not good to eat much honey: so for men to search their own glory is not glory.

<a id="v28"></a><sup class="vn">28</sup> He that hath no rule over his own spirit is like a city that is broken down, and without walls.
