<a id="v1"></a><sup class="vn">1</sup> Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth.

<a id="v2"></a><sup class="vn">2</sup> Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth; a stranger, and not thine own lips.

<a id="v3"></a><sup class="vn">3</sup> A stone is heavy, and the sand weighty; but a fool’s wrath is heavier than them both.

<a id="v4"></a><sup class="vn">4</sup> Wrath is cruel, and anger is outrageous; but who is able to stand before envy?

<a id="v5"></a><sup class="vn">5</sup> Open rebuke is better than secret love.

<a id="v6"></a><sup class="vn">6</sup> Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.

<a id="v7"></a><sup class="vn">7</sup> The full soul loatheth an honeycomb; but to the hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet.

<a id="v8"></a><sup class="vn">8</sup> As a bird that wandereth from her nest, so is a man that wandereth from his place.

<a id="v9"></a><sup class="vn">9</sup> Ointment and perfume rejoice the heart: so doth the sweetness of a man’s friend by hearty counsel.

<a id="v10"></a><sup class="vn">10</sup> Thine own friend, and thy father’s friend, forsake not; neither go into thy brother’s house in the day of thy calamity: for better is a neighbour that is near than a brother far off.

<a id="v11"></a><sup class="vn">11</sup> My son, be wise, and make my heart glad, that I may answer him that reproacheth me.

<a id="v12"></a><sup class="vn">12</sup> A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself; but the simple pass on, and are punished.

<a id="v13"></a><sup class="vn">13</sup> Take his garment that is surety for a stranger, and take a pledge of him for a strange woman.

<a id="v14"></a><sup class="vn">14</sup> He that blesseth his friend with a loud voice, rising early in the morning, it shall be counted a curse to him.

<a id="v15"></a><sup class="vn">15</sup> A continual dropping in a very rainy day and a contentious woman are alike.

<a id="v16"></a><sup class="vn">16</sup> Whosoever hideth her hideth the wind, and the ointment of his right hand, which bewrayeth itself.

<a id="v17"></a><sup class="vn">17</sup> Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.

<a id="v18"></a><sup class="vn">18</sup> Whoso keepeth the fig tree shall eat the fruit thereof: so he that waiteth on his master shall be honoured.

<a id="v19"></a><sup class="vn">19</sup> As in water face answereth to face, so the heart of man to man.

<a id="v20"></a><sup class="vn">20</sup> Hell and destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are never satisfied.

<a id="v21"></a><sup class="vn">21</sup> As the fining pot for silver, and the furnace for gold; so is a man to his praise.

<a id="v22"></a><sup class="vn">22</sup> Though thou shouldest bray a fool in a mortar among wheat with a pestle, yet will not his foolishness depart from him.

<a id="v23"></a><sup class="vn">23</sup> Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds.

<a id="v24"></a><sup class="vn">24</sup> For riches are not for ever: and doth the crown endure to every generation?

<a id="v25"></a><sup class="vn">25</sup> The hay appeareth, and the tender grass sheweth itself, and herbs of the mountains are gathered.

<a id="v26"></a><sup class="vn">26</sup> The lambs are for thy clothing, and the goats are the price of the field.

<a id="v27"></a><sup class="vn">27</sup> And thou shalt have goats’ milk enough for thy food, for the food of thy household, and for the maintenance for thy maidens.
