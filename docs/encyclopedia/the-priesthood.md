Aaron, his sons, and the Levites: the garments, the duties, the anointing, and the order of the priesthood.

## Genesis

**[Genesis 9:5](/bible/genesis/9#v5)**  taught in [Genesis 9-12](/study/genesis/9-12)

- The Most High requiring blood at the hand of every beast is animal sacrifice, still in place with Noah, passed down generation to generation.
- The judgment for sin has always been death. When Eve sinned and caused Adam to sin, either they had to die, or something had to stand in their place; the blood of the animal stood in for them.
- That is the scapegoat: it takes the wrongdoings, mistakes, and faults of others and places them on the animal. That is why Cain and Abel were bringing offerings, because they knew of the sacrifice from [Genesis 3](/bible/genesis/3).
- Precept **[Leviticus 16:8-10](/bible/leviticus/16#v8)**: The scapegoat is presented alive to make an atonement, standing in for the faults of others.

**[Genesis 14:18-20](/bible/genesis/14#v18)**  taught in [Genesis 13-16](/study/genesis/13-16)

- Melchizedek king of Salem brought forth bread and wine; Salem goes into Shalom, peace, so he is the king of peace, and he was the priest of the Most High God.
- Abram paid tithes to Melchizedek. This is way before the Levitical priesthood; the twelve tribes were not yet born. For the order of Melchisedec, study [Hebrews 7](/bible/hebrews/7) and [John 8:56](/bible/john/8#v56).

**[Genesis 35:9-11](/bible/genesis/35#v9)**  taught in [Genesis 32-35](/study/genesis/32-35)

- The nation and company of nations of Abraham's covenant is Israel, plain and clear, and kings come out of Israel's loins. Christ washed us from our sins and made us kings and priests unto God; the men of Israel are kings in the sight of the Most High.
- Precept **[Revelation 1:5-6](/bible/revelation/1#v5)**: Christ washed us from our sins and hath made us kings and priests unto God.

**[Genesis 41:45](/bible/genesis/41#v45)**  taught in [Genesis 40-43](/study/genesis/40-43)

- Pharaoh called Joseph's name Zaphnathpaaneah and gave him Asenath the daughter of Potipherah priest of On to wife. These heathens always like to change our names; that is one reason America is called spiritual Egypt, because they do the same thing that ancient Egypt did.

**[Genesis 41:50-52](/bible/genesis/41#v50)**  taught in [Genesis 40-43](/study/genesis/40-43)

- Joseph called the firstborn Manasseh, forgetting, for God had made him forget all his toil and all his father's house; and the second called he Ephraim, fruitful, for God had caused him to be fruitful in the land of his affliction.
- Ephraim was the second born, yet Ephraim possessed the kingship: the first king of the Northern Kingdom, Jeroboam, was of the tribe of Ephraim, not of Manasseh. Ephraim is the head tribe of the Northern Kingdom.
- Jacob set that in place. When Joseph saw his father lay his right hand upon Ephraim's head it displeased him, and he tried to move it to Manasseh's head; but Jacob refused and said, his younger brother shall be greater than he; and he set Ephraim before Manasseh.

**[Genesis 47:23-26](/bible/genesis/47#v23)**  taught in [Genesis 44-47](/study/genesis/44-47)

- Joseph gave the people seed and made it a law over Egypt: give the fifth part unto Pharaoh, and four parts are your own for seed of the field, for your food, and for your households and little ones.
- Joseph teaches how to save money: pay the fifth part, and lay up for the years of trouble. That wisdom is how Egypt got through the famine, and it is the same perseverance to practice now in times of tribulation.

## Exodus

**[Exodus 2:1](/bible/exodus/2#v1)**  taught in [Exodus 2-5](/study/exodus/2-5)

- A man of the house of Levi took to wife a daughter of Levi. These are Amram and Jochebed, the father and mother of Moses, Aaron, and Miriam.
- Precept **[Exodus 6:20](/bible/exodus/6#v20)**: Amram took him Jochebed to wife, and she bare him Aaron and Moses; the scripture names the parents of Moses.

**[Exodus 2:16-20](/bible/exodus/2#v16)**  taught in [Exodus 2-5](/study/exodus/2-5)

- The priest of Midian had seven daughters, and the shepherds drove them from the troughs; but Moses stood up and helped them, and watered their flock. Moses was a man of war, not a weak man.
- Reuel the priest of Midian is the same man called Jethro, the father in law of Moses.

**[Exodus 3:1-6](/bible/exodus/3#v1)**  taught in [Exodus 2-5](/study/exodus/2-5)

- Moses kept the flock of Jethro his father in law and led it to the backside of the desert, to Horeb the mountain of God; and the angel of the LORD appeared unto him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush, and the bush was not consumed.
- The voice out of the bush said, Draw not nigh hither: put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground; I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.
- The precepts identify that angel. Stephen recounts that the angel which appeared in the bush sent Moses to be a ruler and a deliverer; Joshua met the captain of the host of the LORD, who gave him the same command, Loose thy shoe from off thy foot; and the captain of their salvation, made perfect through sufferings, is Jesus Christ. Precept upon precept, the angel in the burning bush was Christ.
- The same angel went before Israel out of Egypt: Behold, I send an Angel before thee; beware of him, and obey his voice, provoke him not, for my name is in him.
- God is a Spirit, and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth; yet He has a celestial, angelic body, not flesh of this earth. The Ancient of days has a garment and the hair of his head like the pure wool; that is how man could be made in His image.
- Christ was not reincarnated when he came in the flesh. He took not on him the nature of angels, but the seed of Abraham, made like unto his brethren, born a man so that he could die for the nation of Israel, for an angel cannot die.
- Precept **[Hebrews 2:16-17](/bible/hebrews/2#v16)**: He took not on him the nature of angels; but he took on him the seed of Abraham, and in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren.

**[Exodus 4:6-8](/bible/exodus/4#v6)**  taught in [Exodus 2-5](/study/exodus/2-5)

- This was a miracle precisely because Moses was a very dark-skinned man; the daughters of the priest of Midian had taken him for an Egyptian. The hand came out white as snow and went back to his natural dark skin; had he been white already there would have been no sign in it.

**[Exodus 4:13-17](/bible/exodus/4#v13)**  taught in [Exodus 2-5](/study/exodus/2-5)

- Moses pressed, Send, I pray thee, by the hand of him whom thou wilt send; and the anger of the LORD was kindled against Moses. Is not Aaron the Levite thy brother? I know that he can speak well; he spoke the Hebrew of the people fluently.
- Aaron shall be thy spokesman unto the people: he shall be to thee instead of a mouth, and thou shalt be to him instead of God. Moses stood as God in this instance, speaking through Aaron his prophet, who interpreted to the people.

**[Exodus 4:27-31](/bible/exodus/4#v27)**  taught in [Exodus 2-5](/study/exodus/2-5)

- The LORD sent Aaron into the wilderness to meet Moses, and he met him in the mount of God and kissed him; Aaron believed right away. Moses told Aaron all the words of the LORD and all the signs.
- Aaron spake all the words which the LORD had spoken unto Moses and did the signs in the sight of the people, and the people believed: and when they heard that the LORD had visited the children of Israel, and that he had looked upon their affliction, they bowed their heads and worshipped. After a long time in which the Lord had not been dealing with them, He finally heard their prayers.

**[Exodus 5:1-2](/bible/exodus/5#v1)**  taught in [Exodus 2-5](/study/exodus/2-5)

- Moses and Aaron went in and told Pharaoh, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto me in the wilderness. That feast was the Passover.

**[Exodus 5:4-5](/bible/exodus/5#v4)**  taught in [Exodus 2-5](/study/exodus/2-5)

- The king of Egypt said, Wherefore do ye, Moses and Aaron, let the people from their works? get you unto your burdens. That is the standing complaint of the oppressor: the prophets are pulling the people from their labor and getting their hopes up that their God will deliver them.

**[Exodus 5:6-9](/bible/exodus/5#v6)**  taught in [Exodus 2-5](/study/exodus/2-5)

- The work of the Lord is the true calling over any occupation held in Babylon: consider the Apostle and High Priest of our profession, Christ Jesus; fight the good fight of faith, lay hold on eternal life, whereunto thou art also called, and hast professed a good profession before many witnesses.
- Precept **[Hebrews 3:1](/bible/hebrews/3#v1)**: Consider the Apostle and High Priest of our profession, Christ Jesus; the work of the Lord is the profession of the called.

**[Exodus 5:15-19](/bible/exodus/5#v15)**  taught in [Exodus 2-5](/study/exodus/2-5)

- The officers of the children of Israel came and cried unto Pharaoh, saying, Wherefore dealest thou thus with thy servants? There is no straw given unto thy servants, and they say to us, Make brick: and, behold, thy servants are beaten; but the fault is in thine own people.
- Pharaoh answered, Ye are idle, ye are idle: therefore ye say, Let us go and do sacrifice to the LORD. The charge of idleness is the standing answer to any who would go serve God, whether it is the three days' journey or keeping the Sabbath and the new moons now.
- The officers did see that they were in evil case. That is when they realized no appeal to Pharaoh would deliver them; deliverance was not coming from Egypt.
- Precept **[John 11:47-48](/bible/john/11#v47)**: The chief priests and Pharisees said, If we let him thus alone, the Romans shall come and take away both our place and nation; men set over the people fear losing their place more than they love the deliverer.

**[Exodus 5:20-21](/bible/exodus/5#v20)**  taught in [Exodus 2-5](/study/exodus/2-5)

- They met Moses and Aaron and said, The LORD look upon you, and judge; because ye have made our savour to be abhorred in the eyes of Pharaoh, and in the eyes of his servants, to put a sword in their hand to slay us.

**[Exodus 6:9-12](/bible/exodus/6#v9)**  taught in [Exodus 6-9](/study/exodus/6-9)

- Moses answered, Behold, the children of Israel have not hearkened unto me; how then shall Pharaoh hear me, who am of uncircumcised lips? This is why Aaron was given him for a mouthpiece in the next chapter.

**[Exodus 6:16-20](/bible/exodus/6#v16)**  taught in [Exodus 6-9](/study/exodus/6-9)

- Chapter 6 is dealing with the genealogy: the families of Reuben, Simeon, and Levi, and out of Levi came Moses and Aaron. Levi begat Kohath, Kohath begat Amram, and Amram took Jochebed to wife, and she bare him Aaron and Moses.

**[Exodus 6:26-27](/bible/exodus/6#v26)**  taught in [Exodus 6-9](/study/exodus/6-9)

- These are that Aaron and Moses to whom the Lord said, Bring out the children of Israel from the land of Egypt; these are they which spake to Pharaoh king of Egypt.

**[Exodus 7:1-2](/bible/exodus/7#v1)**  taught in [Exodus 6-9](/study/exodus/6-9)

- The Lord made Moses a god to Pharaoh, and Aaron his brother was his prophet. Moses was as God and Aaron was the mouthpiece, because Moses kept saying he was of uncircumcised lips.

**[Exodus 7:8-12](/bible/exodus/7#v8)**  taught in [Exodus 6-9](/study/exodus/6-9)

- It was Aaron who cast down the rod before Pharaoh, and it became a serpent. A lot of people to this day say it was Moses with the rod, because they do not read the Bible; Moses was telling Aaron what to do, and it was Aaron's rod that turned into the serpent.
- The magicians of Egypt did in like manner with their enchantments, and their rods became serpents; but Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods.

**[Exodus 7:19-22](/bible/exodus/7#v19)**  taught in [Exodus 6-9](/study/exodus/6-9)

- All the waters of Egypt were turned to blood, in the streams, the rivers, the ponds, the pools, and in vessels of wood and vessels of stone; the fish died and the river stank.
- With this first plague the Most High was mocking Sobek, the Egyptian idol god of the rivers, showing that he could not defend his own waters.
- The magicians did so with their enchantments, but they could only copy the plague; they could not turn the water back clear. Seven days were fulfilled after the Lord had smitten the river.
- Many Egyptologists claim this never happened, because to admit it happened is to admit that the idols they claim to be gods and goddesses were powerless before our God, and that those idols do not exist at all.

**[Exodus 8:5-8](/bible/exodus/8#v5)**  taught in [Exodus 6-9](/study/exodus/6-9)

- Aaron stretched out his hand over the waters, and the frogs came up and covered the land of Egypt. With this second plague the Most High was mocking Heqet, the Egyptian idol god of the frogs.
- So Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron and said, Entreat the Lord that he may take away the frogs. Why did Pharaoh not call on Heqet, the god of frogs? He had to go to the Lord.

**[Exodus 8:16-19](/bible/exodus/8#v16)**  taught in [Exodus 6-9](/study/exodus/6-9)

- All the dust of the land became lice in man and in beast throughout all the land of Egypt. Imagine that: the dust of the whole land of Egypt turned to lice.
- The magicians did so with their enchantments to bring forth lice, but this time they could not; and the magicians said unto Pharaoh, This is the finger of God. Yet Pharaoh's heart was hardened.
- With the third and fourth plagues, the lice and the swarms of flies, the Most High was mocking Beelzebub, the idol lord of flies and uncleanness.

**[Exodus 8:25-32](/bible/exodus/8#v25)**  taught in [Exodus 6-9](/study/exodus/6-9)

- Pharaoh said, Go sacrifice to your God in the land; but Moses answered, It is not meet, for we should sacrifice the abomination of the Egyptians before their eyes, and will they not stone us? Israel's sacrifices were the very animals Egypt held sacred, so Israel must go three days' journey into the wilderness.
- Moses warned Pharaoh, Let not Pharaoh deal deceitfully any more. The Lord removed the swarms so that there remained not one; and Pharaoh hardened his heart at this time also, and would not let the people go.

**[Exodus 9:8-12](/bible/exodus/9#v8)**  taught in [Exodus 6-9](/study/exodus/6-9)

- Moses sprinkled ashes of the furnace toward heaven, and it became a boil breaking forth with blains upon man and upon beast throughout all the land of Egypt.
- The magicians could not stand before Moses because of the boils, for the boils were upon the magicians themselves and upon all the Egyptians. With this sixth plague the Most High was mocking Sekhmet, the Egyptian idol goddess of health and beauty.

**[Exodus 9:27-30](/bible/exodus/9#v27)**  taught in [Exodus 6-9](/study/exodus/6-9)

- Pharaoh said, I have sinned this time: the Lord is righteous, and I and my people are wicked. This time? So all the other times, none of that was sin? Only now, under the thunder and hail, does he see it.
- Moses answered that the thunder and hail would cease, that thou mayest know how that the earth is the Lord's; but as for thee and thy servants, I know that ye will not yet fear the Lord God.

**[Exodus 10:3-6](/bible/exodus/10#v3)**  taught in [Exodus 10-13](/study/exodus/10-13)

- Moses went in to Pharaoh with a staff and an ultimatum: how long wilt thou refuse to humble thyself? Let my people go, or tomorrow, in twenty four hours, the locusts come into thy coast.
- The locusts would cover the face of the earth so no one could see the ground, eat the residue that escaped the hail, and fill the houses of Pharaoh, of his servants, and of all the Egyptians, such as neither their fathers nor their fathers' fathers had seen.

**[Exodus 10:7-11](/bible/exodus/10#v7)**  taught in [Exodus 10-13](/study/exodus/10-13)

- Pharaoh offered to let only the men go, and Moses and Aaron were driven out from his presence.

**[Exodus 10:16-17](/bible/exodus/10#v16)**  taught in [Exodus 10-13](/study/exodus/10-13)

- When the judgment got real, Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron in haste and confessed, I have sinned against the Lord your God and against you. Everything had been eaten by the locusts, and now he begged that this death only be taken from him.

**[Exodus 11:8-10](/bible/exodus/11#v8)**  taught in [Exodus 10-13](/study/exodus/10-13)

- All Pharaoh's servants would come down and bow to Moses, saying, Get thee out, and all the people that follow thee; and Moses went out from Pharaoh in a great anger.
- Pharaoh would not hearken, that the wonders of the Most High might be multiplied in the land of Egypt. He is in control of the leaders of every country, presidents, governors, senators, congressmen, and mayors alike; whoever gets in the seat, he twists and turns them about like the river.

**[Exodus 12:1-2](/bible/exodus/12#v1)**  taught in [Exodus 10-13](/study/exodus/10-13)

- This month shall be unto you the beginning of months, the first month of the year. The month is Abib, the month in which Israel came out of Egypt.
- Abib in the Bible dictionary is the pre-exilic name for the first month of the year; after the exile the name was changed to Nisan, and it falls between March and April. So the new year begins in the springtime, around March and April, not on January the first in the dead of winter; that is another lie taught in Babylon.

**[Exodus 12:24-28](/bible/exodus/12#v24)**  taught in [Exodus 10-13](/study/exodus/10-13)

- The feast days commemorate the Most High delivering his people and bringing judgment on their enemies; that is the same pattern the feast of dedication commemorates. The people bowed the head and worshipped, and the children of Israel went and did as the Lord had commanded Moses and Aaron.

**[Exodus 12:30-33](/bible/exodus/12#v30)**  taught in [Exodus 10-13](/study/exodus/10-13)

- The last time Pharaoh saw Moses he threatened to kill him if he ever saw his face again; now he called for Moses and Aaron by night, saying, Rise up, get you forth from among my people, go, serve the Lord as ye have said, take your flocks and your herds, and bless me also. Touch not mine anointed, and do my prophets no harm; the man who thought he could threaten the servant of the Most High had a complete change of tone.

**[Exodus 12:43-51](/bible/exodus/12#v43)**  taught in [Exodus 10-13](/study/exodus/10-13)

- Thus did all the children of Israel as the Lord commanded Moses and Aaron, and the selfsame day the Lord brought them out of the land of Egypt by their armies.

**[Exodus 14:10-12](/bible/exodus/14#v10)**  taught in [Exodus 14-17](/study/exodus/14-17)

- At the first sight of the Egyptians marching, Israel lost all faith. The Lord had done miracle after miracle before their eyes and brought them out with an high hand, yet at the first sign of danger they said He brought them out to die. Our people have always been a rebellious people of little faith: a very froward generation, children in whom is no faith.
- Let us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians: that is the same word our people speak today. Why are you stirring up trouble, why tell us to come out of the ways of Babylon; let us alone to serve the slave master, because serving him feels easier than having faith in the Most High.
- The same spirit was in Israel in the days of Christ: if we let him thus alone, all men will believe on him, and the Romans shall come. The fear of the oppressor outweighed belief in the miracles.
- In captivity the people wax old in mind and will not depart: woe to the bloody city, the prey departed not. Israel was the prey in Egypt and did not want to leave, the same as now.
- Precept **[John 11:47-48](/bible/john/11#v47)**: If we let him thus alone the Romans shall come; the same spirit feared the oppressor more than the Most High.

**[Exodus 15:20-21](/bible/exodus/15#v20)**  taught in [Exodus 14-17](/study/exodus/14-17)

- Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a timbrel, and all the women went out after her with timbrels and with dances, answering the song: sing ye to the Lord, for he hath triumphed gloriously.

**[Exodus 16:1-3](/bible/exodus/16#v1)**  taught in [Exodus 14-17](/study/exodus/14-17)

- The whole congregation murmured against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness: would to God we had died by the hand of the Lord in Egypt, when we sat by the flesh pots. The Lord commanded Moses to bring the people out, and when things got tough they made it his fault.

**[Exodus 16:6-8](/bible/exodus/16#v6)**  taught in [Exodus 14-17](/study/exodus/14-17)

- Your murmurings are not against us, but against the Lord. Moses and Aaron were nobody of themselves; complaining against the leadership the Most High sets is murmuring against the Most High Himself.

**[Exodus 16:31-36](/bible/exodus/16#v31)**  taught in [Exodus 14-17](/study/exodus/14-17)

- Aaron laid up an omer of manna before the Testimony, to be kept for generations, that they may see the bread wherewith the Lord fed Israel in the wilderness. The manna that spoiled overnight on other days was preserved for generations; that is the power of the Most High.

**[Exodus 17:8-13](/bible/exodus/17#v8)**  taught in [Exodus 14-17](/study/exodus/14-17)

- When Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed; when he let down his hand, Amalek prevailed. Aaron and Hur stayed up his hands until the going down of the sun. Moses was about eighty years old holding a rod above his head all day; the fathers were mighty men, stronger than we are today.

**[Exodus 18:1-6](/bible/exodus/18#v1)**  taught in [Exodus 18-21](/study/exodus/18-21)

- Jethro the priest of Midian, Moses' father in law, heard all that God had done for Moses and for Israel, and came to Moses in the wilderness with Zipporah and her two sons, Gershom and Eliezer.
- Jethro is the same man called Reuel in Exodus 2: the priest of Midian whose seven daughters Moses helped at the well, and who gave Moses Zipporah his daughter. Reuel and Jethro are one person.
- Precept **[Exodus 2:16-22](/bible/exodus/2#v16)**: The priest of Midian gave Moses Zipporah his daughter, and she bare Gershom; Reuel is Jethro, and the daughters took Moses for an Egyptian.

**[Exodus 19:1-2](/bible/exodus/19#v1)**  taught in [Exodus 18-21](/study/exodus/18-21)

- In the third month after going forth out of Egypt, Israel departed from Rephidim, came into the wilderness of Sinai, and camped before the mount. The journeys are recorded in Numbers 33: Israel departed from Rameses on the fifteenth day of the first month, on the morrow after the Passover, and later pitched in the wilderness of Sinai.
- Israel went forth out of Egypt with their armies. The tribes are armies, for the Lord is a man of war; Israel has always been at war and is at war today, a spiritual war.
- Moses wrote their goings out by the commandment of the Lord. The Lord gave the word, and great was the company of those that published it; so the claim that another people wrote the scriptures falls, for Moses wrote what God commanded him to write.
- Precept **[Numbers 33:1-4](/bible/numbers/33#v1)**: These are the journeys of Israel by their armies, and Moses wrote their goings out by the commandment of the Lord, departing from Rameses on the morrow after the Passover.

**[Exodus 19:5-8](/bible/exodus/19#v5)**  taught in [Exodus 18-21](/study/exodus/18-21)

- If ye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant, then ye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me above all people, a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation.

**[Exodus 19:12-13](/bible/exodus/19#v12)**  taught in [Exodus 18-21](/study/exodus/18-21)

- The Most High is serious about that charge. When the oxen shook the cart, Uzzah put forth his hand to the ark of God and took hold of it, and the anger of the Lord was kindled and he smote him there, for the stranger that came nigh the priests' office was to be put to death. Every family of the priesthood had its own responsibility in the tabernacle service, and none could transgress against another's charge; what the world later called unions came from that order.
- Precept **[Numbers 3:10](/bible/numbers/3#v10)**: Aaron and his sons waited on the priest's office, and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death.

**[Exodus 20:25-26](/bible/exodus/20#v25)**  taught in [Exodus 18-21](/study/exodus/18-21)

- This scripture helps explain pants, precept upon precept. The priests were given linen breeches to cover their nakedness from the loins even unto the thighs when they came near the altar, and Israel was called in this same reading to be a kingdom of priests, so do not be simple and say that law was only for Aaron and his sons.
- Precept **[Exodus 28:42-43](/bible/exodus/28#v42)**: Linen breeches to cover their nakedness, from the loins even unto the thighs, upon Aaron and his sons at the altar, a statute for ever.
- Precept **[Exodus 19:6](/bible/exodus/19#v6)**: Ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests; the priests' standard belongs to the whole nation.

**[Exodus 22:28](/bible/exodus/22#v28)**  taught in [Exodus 22-25](/study/exodus/22-25)

- [Acts 23](/bible/acts/23) is the example: when Paul spoke against the high priest, they that stood by said, Revilest thou God's high priest? And Paul answered, I wist not, brethren, that he was the high priest: for it is written, Thou shalt not speak evil of the ruler of thy people, quoting this very verse.
- Precept **[Acts 23:1-5](/bible/acts/23#v1)**: Paul, rebuked for reviling God's high priest, cited this law: it is written, Thou shalt not speak evil of the ruler of thy people.

**[Exodus 22:29-30](/bible/exodus/22#v29)**  taught in [Exodus 22-25](/study/exodus/22-25)

- Thou shalt not delay to offer the first of thy ripe fruits and of thy liquors, and the firstborn of thy sons shalt thou give unto me. The firstborn sons that opened the matrix were consecrated unto the Lord, and the father redeemed them back from the priest with shekels.
- Likewise with the oxen and the sheep: seven days the firstling stayed with his dam, his mother, and on the eighth day it was given. The Lord did not want it too young, taken as soon as it popped out; it had to be its appointed time with the mother, then brought to the priest.

**[Exodus 23:14-17](/bible/exodus/23#v14)**  taught in [Exodus 22-25](/study/exodus/22-25)

- None shall appear before me empty: you could not come empty-handed. You brought the tenth of your oil, your wine, whatever was required, for the priests to replenish the storehouses.

**[Exodus 24:1-3](/bible/exodus/24#v1)**  taught in [Exodus 22-25](/study/exodus/22-25)

- Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel were called up to worship afar off, and Moses alone came near the Lord.

**[Exodus 24:9-11](/bible/exodus/24#v9)**  taught in [Exodus 22-25](/study/exodus/22-25)

## Joshua

**[Joshua 1:1-2](/bible/joshua/1#v1)**  taught in [Joshua 1-4](/study/joshua/1-4)

- Joshua is called Moses' minister because when Moses was the leader, Joshua was the one always cleaving to him. He was not close to Moses in age; he was one of the young men that cleaved unto his elder, and if Moses was a good leader, beloved among the children of Israel, who better to rise up in his stead than the man who has his spirit?
- That is the spirit the young men coming up in this truth should aspire to: cleave to your leadership. It is not being around the longest that qualifies a man to rise up in another's stead; it is being the diligent servant who learned the ins and outs of how his leader was successful, and even observed his leader's mistakes so he knows what not to do in that position.
- Why was Joshua the one anointed? When the Lord told Moses he would see the land but not enter it, Moses did not argue or justify himself. His humble response was to ask the Lord to set a man over the congregation, that the congregation of the Lord be not as sheep which have no shepherd. Moses brought no candidates; he let the Lord pick, and the Lord picked the young man who had cleaved to him: promotion comes from the Lord, and he selects the leaders over his people.
- Moses said set a man over the congregation, not a woman, because women were never ordained to lead the children of Israel; that does not fit the divine order of the Most High. Miriam was a prophetess and one of the chief women, and she led the women; women have a real role in the nation, but leading the body is not it. When Miriam got puffed up and spoke against Moses, she was judged.
- Precept **[Numbers 27:18-20](/bible/numbers/27#v18)**: The Lord said, Take thee Joshua the son of Nun, a man in whom is the spirit; Moses laid his hand on him, set him before Eleazar and the congregation, and put some of his honour upon him so the people would give Joshua the same respect and be obedient.

**[Joshua 1:8](/bible/joshua/1#v8)**  taught in [Joshua 1-4](/study/joshua/1-4)

- This verse is the recipe for success, the formula for success, the true prosperity doctrine, and it has three steps: the law in your mouth, meditation day and night, and observing to do all that is written therein.
- This book of the law shall not depart out of thy mouth: when you open your mouth, what comes out is supposed to be scriptural; your conversation has to be modeled after what this book says. When people leave this truth the Lord removes his words out of their mouth, and their whole conversation changes: nothing is left but slander, falsehood, strange doctrines, and flattering speech.
- Thou shalt meditate therein day and night: to meditate is to think deeply and carefully, to contemplate, consider, ponder, and muse. Many read their four chapters a day, listen to videos, and quote their precept lists, but never meditate on what they read. You cannot just slap the butter on the biscuit; you have to let it sink into every layer.
- Solomon shows what a companion meditation makes of wisdom: when he came into his house he reposed himself with her, for her conversation has no bitterness and living with her has no sorrow, but mirth and joy. Get in the habit of chilling with wisdom when you get home. When you ponder the whole benefit package that comes with wisdom, you understand that to be allied unto wisdom is immortality; the wiser you get, the more spiritually invincible you become.
- Make meditation a daily custom, a holy habit, as our forefathers did: somewhere alone and peaceful where the mind can be clear, because you cannot meditate in the middle of activity and noise. When you meditate continually on the commandments, the Lord establishes your heart, and the evil thoughts you battle begin to flush out of your mind.
- That thou mayest observe to do according to all that is written therein: the end goal of talking about the commandments and meditating on the commandments is to do the commandments. Attending every class, hearing every breakdown, and keeping every precept list is all in vain if you never do thus saith the Lord; not doing the word of God is why we went into slavery and sit at the bottom of society.
- For then thou shalt make thy way prosperous, and then thou shalt have good success: only after the mouth, the meditation, and the doing does prosperity follow. Thus saith God, the second you transgress the commandments of the Lord you cannot prosper, so any pastor teaching prosperity while saying the law is done away with is teaching a lie. Real prosperity never came from sowing a seed into a basket; it comes from obeying the laws of the Most High, and that applies to us as a whole nation of people.
- Precept **[Deuteronomy 17:18-19](/bible/deuteronomy/17#v18)**: Every king of Israel had to write himself a copy of the law and read therein all the days of his life, not just on a sabbath, that he may learn to fear the Lord and keep the words of this law to do them; you are what you eat, and what you put within comes out in your conversation and your actions.
- Precept **[2 Chronicles 24:20](/bible/2-chronicles/24#v20)**: The spirit of God came upon Zechariah to ask, Why transgress ye the commandments of the Lord, that ye cannot prosper? The moment you transgress, the prosperity is over.
