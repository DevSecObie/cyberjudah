## Introduction

Deacon Yashua teaches this Sabbath morning class. I was meditating on the movement that we have had recently. People leave all the time; people leave jobs; people quit life. So whenever I see movement, it does not faze me. But especially when certain people who appear like they are grounded and rooted leave, and you hear the reasons that they state for leaving, it starts making you think. I started to examine how it is that so many of us get to the point where we go off in our understanding. Today's class will also explain why some of us do not even grow.

Every day, whether we realize it or not, there are two processes happening within you at the same time. From the day you are born, you are on a path to death: you peak, and then everything starts to break down. There is a daily decay that happens, not just physically but in this walk spiritually. The flesh does not stand still, the cares of this world are always pressing, temptations always rise, and sin is always deceptive. If you leave that alone, the natural man does not drift toward God; he drifts away from him. At the same time, if you are a repenting Israelite, there is a process of daily renewal in the spirit. A lot of us think we repented and we are done, but all these things constantly pressing you mean you have to counteract them.

We understand the principles of nourishment: you eat every day, because if you do not eat, your body weakens. Likewise the scripture tells you that you need to feed yourself God's word daily to nourish your spirit. Many of us come to the Sabbath, treat it like church, get the classes, and then go a whole week starving the spirit. Then it is no wonder you get to a point where you fade, where you doubt, where you see things happen in the body and feel conflicted rather than seeing clearly. You would not eat once a week and think you can make it to the next week on that one meal, so why do you do it with the scripture? This is going to be a scripture heavy class.

## Scriptures Opened

**[Deuteronomy 8:1-3](/bible/deuteronomy/8#v1)**  *[18:01]*

> <sup>[1](/bible/deuteronomy/8#v1)</sup> All the commandments which I command thee this day shall ye observe to do, that ye may live, and multiply, and go in and possess the land which the Lord sware unto your fathers.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/deuteronomy/8#v2)</sup> And thou shalt remember all the way which the Lord thy God led thee these forty years in the wilderness, to humble thee, and to prove thee, to know what was in thine heart, whether thou wouldest keep his commandments, or no.
>
> <sup>[3](/bible/deuteronomy/8#v3)</sup> And he humbled thee, and suffered thee to hunger, and fed thee with manna, which thou knewest not, neither did thy fathers know; that he might make thee know that man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the Lord doth man live.

- The Most High always intended it this way from the beginning. Thou shalt remember all the way which the Lord thy God led thee these forty years in the wilderness. What happened physically to our forefathers in the wilderness was there as a witness for us, an example. It was not just something historical: there are so many details in the things that happened, and why they happened, that are examples for us to apply today.
- The wilderness was to humble thee, and to prove thee, to know what was in thine heart, whether thou wouldest keep his commandments, or no. Much like we must do every day in this walk.
- He humbled thee, and suffered thee to hunger, and fed thee with manna, which thou knewest not, that he might make thee know that man doth not live by bread only. But was not manna a form of bread? So what is he talking about? There is a spiritual element here. The manna represents something that was supposed to nourish us later: not the feeding of literal bread into your mouth every day, but to teach us that our nourishment is dependent on God.
- Every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the Lord provided instructions to survive the wilderness. That is why the whole adult generation had to die: they did not heed those words. Every word was designed to show how to survive the wilderness and to show our dependency on God. Where was our dependency prior to that? Egypt. That is why they kept complaining: in Egypt we had this, in Egypt we had that. This is not our rest.
- What is being set up with the example of the manna is what we later understand was a foreshadowing of Christ. Christ comes in the volume of the book; Christ is everything, even in the little details of the examples.

**[Matthew 4:4](/bible/matthew/4#v4)**  *[22:25]*

> <sup>[4](/bible/matthew/4#v4)</sup> But he answered and said, It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.

- When you read Deuteronomy 8:3, this is what Christ was quoting to Satan in his temptation in the wilderness. Satan was tempting him because he was weakened and hungered from his fast. There is a lot going on here: it was not just to eat, but to not have God as his supply. Satan was going to be the thing that gave him ease in that moment, and Christ knew that was not it. He was setting an example for us to lean on the Most High in all these things.
- What he is really telling you, and you read it much later, is that the flesh profiteth nothing; the words are what is real life. All the prophets understood this. If you read and study, they always spoke about consuming the word, eating the word: it tasted like this, it was in my belly like that, it strengthened me. God wants us to acknowledge dependence on him, and not just in general, but daily.

- The whole congregation murmured against Moses and Aaron: would to God we had died in Egypt, when we sat by the flesh pots and did eat bread to the full. They wanted to go back to Egypt for dependency on Egypt, not realizing how much greater a thing God had in store for his people.
- I will rain bread from heaven for you; and the people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day. There was a portion for each day, and it was different for everyone: based on appetite, family size, your needs, because everybody needs a different level of nourishment. Some of us need more than others when it comes to the nourishment of the word of God.
- That I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no. Were they not keeping the Sabbath? What he is really talking about is whether they would stay on him daily, seeking him as the source and the supply.
- At even ye shall eat flesh, and in the morning ye shall be filled with bread: quails in the evening, manna in the morning. And ye shall know that I am the Lord your God.
- They said one to another, It is manna: for they wist not what it was. The word literally means, what is it? And Moses said, This is the bread which the Lord hath given you to eat. What they did not know yet in that moment, and what we need to understand, is that he was talking about Christ. The bread we were given to eat, the spiritual food from heaven, was talking about Christ.
- Gather of it every man according to his eating: he that gathered much had nothing over, and he that gathered little had no lack. According to what was needed for nourishment and appetite.
- Let no man leave of it till the morning. You were not supposed to store this. Some of them left of it until the morning, and it bred worms, and stank. If the bread represents the word of God, what it is really telling you is that yesterday's studies, yesterday's class, yesterday's nourishment for your spirit is not enough for the following day. Not that it has no value; it is not enough. You need to eat every day, and you need to be in the word every day.
- The perfect scenario is you read your four chapters a day, you watch a class, you have it all set up. But life is not always thrown at us that way. You can listen to something; you can read a couple of verses at a bare minimum, and not just random verses. The things that are most heavy unto thee, seek them out in the scripture and meditate on them. Sometimes too many verses is not good; maybe what you lack is to focus on one or two verses that get you right on what you are dealing with.
- The Bible talks extensively about bitterness and grudging, yet we still fall into it. That is how you know that somebody who says they are leaving because they are offended by somebody, and claims they will still keep God's laws, is not being honest. They left because they held a grudge and grew bitter against somebody, and then left. You have to be careful when feelings start coming up about people that are personal, and not about the sin they are doing.
- I will be candid: I am not in it every day, and I feel it when I am not. It does not manifest like hunger or thirst, but it is your mind that starts to stray; it is your spirit that starts to look at things differently. The ones that are most seductive and subtle are the things that deal with emotions, what you feel about others, or the weariness you get in the mission.
- Christ was saying in Matthew 4:4 that your spirit needs daily nourishment to survive the wilderness. And make no mistake, this is a wilderness. The Bible says we are pilgrims and strangers here; this is not our rest. This is a proving ground where we have grace, much like our forefathers had forty years of grace, to get it right.

**[Ecclesiasticus 33:27](/bible/sirach/33#v27)**  *[35:50]*

> <sup>[27](/bible/sirach/33#v27)</sup> Send him to labour, that he be not idle; for idleness teacheth much evil.

- Idleness teacheth much evil. A lot of times we look at this verse and talk about kids, or responsibilities in the body. But when you look at it individually, the idleness is that you are not feeding yourself with the scriptures every day; you are not doing something spiritual every day. The pull of the flesh will draw you in a direction where evil starts to creep in.
- You cannot just have an awareness of these things and not nourish your spirit every day, knowing that your flesh is fighting every minute, every moment, to maintain and revive the old man. That is why you see the pattern with people who have been here for years. An unspotted life is gray hairs unto him: it does not matter how long you have been here; what has been your reputation while you have been among us?

**[Matthew 6:11](/bible/matthew/6#v11)**  *[37:16]*

> <sup>[11](/bible/matthew/6#v11)</sup> Give us this day our daily bread.

- This is the Lord's prayer, and I wonder if anybody ever stopped to ask: why, in all of this, is he asking for food? Does anything else in the Lord's prayer pertain to food, or does the whole prayer pertain to the kingdom of heaven? It is not talking about the food that you eat. It is talking about the spiritual: God's word. Give us this day our daily bread is a reminder, in one of the foundational prayers, of the spiritual nourishment we should have every day.
- When he says that, understand it is connected to the history we read in Deuteronomy 8 and Exodus 16. It would seem so out of course in the whole prayer to just ask for daily sustenance; it is all a reminder of the manna.

> <sup>[1](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v1)</sup> Moreover, brethren, I would not that ye should be ignorant, how that all our fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through the sea;

> <sup>[2](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v2)</sup> And were all baptized unto Moses in the cloud and in the sea;

> <sup>[3](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v3)</sup> And did all eat the same spiritual meat;

> <sup>[5](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v5)</sup> But with many of them God was not well pleased: for they were overthrown in the wilderness.

> <sup>[6](/bible/1-corinthians/10#v6)</sup> Now these things were our examples, to the intent we should not lust after evil things, as they also lusted.

- Everybody tells you the Corinthians were Gentiles. If these were Gentiles, how is it that all our fathers were under the cloud and all passed through the sea in the wilderness? Reading is fundamental. Sometimes it is as simple as that.
- They were all baptized unto Moses in the cloud and in the sea. The baptism is the receiving of the laws and the doing of the laws, in the faith of Christ. Moses did not dip the six hundred thousand men and all the rest into rivers; where was the water? The Lord had to provide water from a rock.
- They did all eat the same spiritual meat, and did all drink the same spiritual drink: for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them, and that Rock was Christ. It is letting you know that the manna represents Christ. Everything that happened to them in the wilderness was the foreshadowing, the setup, to let them know of the dependency that we would need on Christ.
- With many of them God was not well pleased, for they were overthrown in the wilderness. Was there some enemy that overthrew them? No: they were overthrown by their own lusts, their own desires of the carnal man that they did not subdue.
- These things were our examples, to the intent we should not lust after evil things, as they also lusted. And how do you avoid lusting after those things? By feeding yourself with the word of God.
- To summarize: Israel needed manna every day to survive the wilderness, and Christ compared that to needing the word of God. I am not saying simply read your Bible. The point is that spiritual life requires consistent, continual nourishment, just as physical life requires continual food. Those of you who think you are ten toes down: when Jacob's trouble magnifies itself, you are going to survive that with this diet? You need to be training all the time for that day. Be the warrior in the garden instead of a gardener in a war. There is a big difference between trials and tribulation; the scripture says we have not yet resisted unto blood.

**[Psalms 1:1-2](/bible/psalms/1#v1)**  *[50:43]*

> <sup>[1](/bible/psalms/1#v1)</sup> Blessed is the man that walketh not in the counsel of the ungodly, nor standeth in the way of sinners, nor sitteth in the seat of the scornful.
>
> <sup>[2](/bible/psalms/1#v2)</sup> But his delight is in the law of the Lord; and in his law doth he meditate day and night.

- Meditation is not something you do once in a while, and not only when you are dealing with something. Meditate means to think upon it, muse upon it, keep it with you. Do not just read it and check it off.
- In verse 1 he walketh not in the counsel of the ungodly, he standeth not in the way of sinners, he sitteth not in the seat of the scornful. But verse 2 is how he accomplishes that: his delight is in the law of the Lord, and in his law doth he meditate day and night. Because of the way he is nourished in his spirit, he does not even want to sit among the ungodly.
- Consider why murmurers feel comfortable coming to you. Quote read in class: "Don't tell me what they say about me. Tell me why they feel comfortable telling you." You have to question that and examine it. Why is somebody so comfortable coming to you with something you know in your spirit is not right to be spoken? If your delight is in the law of God, you will be instant in season with that correction the minute you hear evil.

**[Psalms 119:97, 148](/bible/psalms/119#v97)**  *[55:35]*

- Oh how love I thy law! it is my meditation all the day. When Galatians 5 says walk in the spirit and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh, this is what he is talking about: your mind stayed on the word all the day.

- Mine eyes prevent the night watches, that I might meditate in thy word. In other words, I stay up all night to consume your word. As a king, David had all his responsibilities during the day; he could not wait to get into his private chambers and get into the word, and sometimes he did not even sleep at night.
- Some of us were like that in the beginning, when you first came in. And then, because you stopped nourishing yourself every day, you do not have that yearning anymore. And when it comes to the Bible, it is not the same food every day: you have prophecy, laws, examples, history, exhortation, adventure. It is a buffet; it is a cornucopia.

If there was ever a more proper similitude for what it is like to be in this truth and then go back to the things you put behind you, the things Romans said you were ashamed of, that is it. It becomes alluring for reasons you cannot understand from this side.

**[Galatians 5:17](/bible/galatians/5#v17)**  *[1:06:33]*

> <sup>[17](/bible/galatians/5#v17)</sup> For the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh: and these are contrary the one to the other: so that ye cannot do the things that ye would.

- The Bible does not present men as spiritually neutral. You have to be cold or hot; if you are lukewarm, he will spue you out. The flesh is always pulling and the spirit is always fighting.
- The flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh: and these are contrary the one to the other. There is always a pull against what you want to do. The spirit pulls you from the fleshly things, and the fleshly things pull you from the spiritual. We read that, we know it, and we do nothing about it, or not enough. This is what Paul is really talking about in Romans 7.

**[Matthew 26:41](/bible/matthew/26#v41)**  *[1:07:34]*

> <sup>[41](/bible/matthew/26#v41)</sup> Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation: the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak.

- Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation. He is giving you other words for Galatians 5:17: the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak. And we must embrace that. You know when you are hungry physically; some of you are just so accustomed to having food in your mouth. How come those same instincts do not move over to the scriptures? You will go from level one to level five in the best ways possible if you just apply what the Lord intended from the beginning.
- The wilderness was an opportunity not just to hear the word, but to apply it and consume it. There is a didactic portion of learning, and then there is application. Definition read in class: didactic refers to the theoretical instruction of concepts through lectures, readings, or structured lessons; application is the hands on execution of those theories, testing knowledge through practical experience. Like an electrician or a plumber: you go through the books, but on the job you are an apprentice, then a journeyman. The same principles apply in this walk. Every time you go through the repetition, it reinforces, and the Most High establishes your mind, so that when the problem comes up, you are instant in season and out of season.

**[Romans 8:5-6](/bible/romans/8#v5)**  *[1:10:45]*

> <sup>[5](/bible/romans/8#v5)</sup> For they that are after the flesh do mind the things of the flesh; but they that are after the Spirit the things of the Spirit.
>

- To be carnally minded is death; but to be spiritually minded is life and peace. Notice it is not just life, it is peace. You know why some of you still have so much drama in your lives? Because you are not applying this. You have no peace because you are trying to seek peace in things that are not God. The Bible talks about God's perfect peace, the peace of God which passeth all understanding: it will overcome anything you are dealing with. If it does not seem to work for you, you are still not applying what you need to apply.

He did that to us as a people in different captivities, and it can happen to you as an individual, as we read in Romans 1:28.
- Remember Psalms 106: they soon forgat his works. If they saw the miracles and went off, what makes you think you will sustain without daily sustenance in this word?

**[Wisdom of Solomon 16:20-26](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/16#v20)**  *[2:07:57]*

> <sup>[20](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/16#v20)</sup> Instead whereof thou feddest thine own people with angels’ food, and didst send them from heaven bread prepared without their labour, able to content every man’s delight, and agreeing to every taste.
>
> <sup>[21](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/16#v21)</sup> For thy sustenance declared thy sweetness unto thy children, and serving to the appetite of the eater, tempered itself to every man’s liking.
>
> <sup>[26](/bible/wisdom-of-solomon/16#v26)</sup> That thy children, O Lord, whom thou lovest, might know, that it is not the growing of fruits that nourisheth man: but that it is thy word, which preserveth them that put their trust in thee.

- Thou feddest thine own people with angels' food, and didst send them from heaven bread prepared without their labour, able to content every man's delight, and agreeing to every taste. The manna tasted like whatever it needed to taste like for you; it tempered itself to every man's liking.
- Likewise with the word: you have to feed yourself with what you need, and you cannot do that if you are not in there looking every day. Some of you are averse to reading the verses that deal with what you are dealing with; you study everything else except what is eating at you, because you do not want to fix it.
- That thy children, O Lord, whom thou lovest, might know that it is not the growing of fruits that nourisheth man: but that it is thy word, which preserveth them that put their trust in thee. It was never about feeding their stomachs. It was about teaching what truly preserves life, lining us up to accept and receive Christ.

**[John 6:31-35](/bible/john/6#v31)**  *[2:10:25]*

> <sup>[31](/bible/john/6#v31)</sup> Our fathers did eat manna in the desert; as it is written, He gave them bread from heaven to eat.
>
> <sup>[32](/bible/john/6#v32)</sup> Then Jesus said unto them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Moses gave you not that bread from heaven; but my Father giveth you the true bread from heaven.
>
> <sup>[33](/bible/john/6#v33)</sup> For the bread of God is he which cometh down from heaven, and giveth life unto the world.
>
> <sup>[34](/bible/john/6#v34)</sup> Then said they unto him, Lord, evermore give us this bread.
>
> <sup>[35](/bible/john/6#v35)</sup> And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.

- Our fathers did eat manna in the desert. Christ opens them up: Moses gave you not that bread from heaven; but my Father giveth you the true bread from heaven. For the bread of God is he which cometh down from heaven, and giveth life unto the world. He is talking about himself.
- I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst. What happened with the manna was always symbolic of Christ. Everything that happened from beginning to end, Christ is in it, and it was all for us to know and see that. So much for Christians who say the New Testament is greater than the Old: the Old established everything else. Here a little, there a little; that is how you unlock the scriptures.

**[John 14:6](/bible/john/14#v6)**  *[2:11:58]*

> <sup>[6](/bible/john/14#v6)</sup> Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.

- I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.

  Precepts:
  - **[1 John 2:6](/bible/1-john/2#v6)**
> <sup>[6](/bible/1-john/2#v6)</sup> He that saith he abideth in him ought himself also so to walk, even as he walked.

    The way: he that saith he abideth in him ought himself also so to walk, even as he walked.
  - **[Psalms 119:142](/bible/psalms/119#v142)**
> <sup>[142](/bible/psalms/119#v142)</sup> Thy righteousness is an everlasting righteousness, and thy law is the truth.

    The truth: thy law is the truth. He is the embodiment of it; he fulfilled and will fulfil all prophecies. Lo, I come: in the volume of the book it is written of me.
  - **[John 4:14](/bible/john/4#v14)**
> <sup>[14](/bible/john/4#v14)</sup> But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life.

    The life: whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; it shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life. That sustenance again: in 1 Corinthians 10 they all drank of that same spiritual drink.
  - **[1 John 5:10-12](/bible/1-john/5#v10)**
> <sup>[10](/bible/1-john/5#v10)</sup> He that believeth on the Son of God hath the witness in himself: he that believeth not God hath made him a liar; because he believeth not the record that God gave of his Son.
>
> <sup>[11](/bible/1-john/5#v11)</sup> And this is the record, that God hath given to us eternal life, and this life is in his Son.
>
> <sup>[12](/bible/1-john/5#v12)</sup> He that hath the Son hath life; and he that hath not the Son of God hath not life.

    The record that God gave of his Son is the whole Bible, starting in the Old Testament. At the time these letters were written, much of the New Testament was not there yet; the record was the Old Testament. The manna, the cloud, the rock, the water that came out of it so they could live: it physically happened so that we could have that today. He that hath the Son hath life: that is the bread, the sustenance from heaven.
  - **[John 10:9](/bible/john/10#v9)**
> <sup>[9](/bible/john/10#v9)</sup> I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.

    I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture. You want to get to the Most High? You enter in through Christ.
  - **[1 Timothy 2:5](/bible/1-timothy/2#v5)**
> <sup>[5](/bible/1-timothy/2#v5)</sup> For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;

    There is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus.

**[John 6:48-51, 57](/bible/john/6#v48)**  *[2:16:30]*

> <sup>[48](/bible/john/6#v48)</sup> I am that bread of life.
>
> <sup>[49](/bible/john/6#v49)</sup> Your fathers did eat manna in the wilderness, and are dead.
>
> <sup>[50](/bible/john/6#v50)</sup> This is the bread which cometh down from heaven, that a man may eat thereof, and not die.
>
> <sup>[51](/bible/john/6#v51)</sup> I am the living bread which came down from heaven: if any man eat of this bread, he shall live for ever: and the bread that I will give is my flesh, which I will give for the life of the world.
>
> <sup>[57](/bible/john/6#v57)</sup> As the living Father hath sent me, and I live by the Father: so he that eateth me, even he shall live by me.

- I am that bread of life. How many times does he have to say it? Your fathers did eat manna in the wilderness, and are dead. He is talking about what it represented: Christ is that manna.
- The bread that I will give is my flesh, which I will give for the life of the world. That goes into the flesh being his veil: he sacrificed that form, his blood was spilled above the law of sacrifice, so that we could have this grace period to repent.
- He that eateth me, even he shall live by me. When he says eat of him, what do you think that means? Consume his word every day. The Pharisees got offended when he said it; shame on them, they knew the Old Testament. That is why Paul went in the way he went in on Hebrews: you need milk again, because you forgot that all of that foretold of Christ. We feed upon Christ through his words.

    The bread which we break, is it not the communion of the body of Christ? For we being many are one bread, and one body: for we are all partakers of that one bread. We are all partakers of that manna.

> <sup>[25](/bible/1-corinthians/11#v25)</sup> After the same manner also he took the cup, when he had supped, saying, This cup is the new testament in my blood: this do ye, as oft as ye drink it, in remembrance of me.

You break bread every time we come together, but are you eating the bread that is abundant for us? The breaking of bread is also symbolic of that manna and the commitment we have to consume God's word every day.

The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise. If we really considered our ways, our thoughts, our behaviors, we would understand why we need to seek him daily, continually, to forgive us of our sins. You do not believe it if you are not feeding yourself constantly.

## In Closing

Remember: the body is dying. You are born and you are on a path to death. The flesh fights, the mind forgets, and the soul is weighed down by this veil, this tabernacle. So the inward man has to be renewed; you have to keep that man alive by the word daily. The manna showed us it, the prophets lived it and spoke about it, David meditated on it heavily, Paul explained it thoroughly, and Christ revealed himself to be that manna, the true bread from heaven. That is spirit, and that is life. I pray you get some understanding on that, and that we move to apply, as always.
