## Introduction

Sabbath class from Captain Yadain with IUIC Birmingham, Alabama, fresh off the trip to Birmingham, UK. The class is titled This Woman's Work. A couple weeks ago the class dealt with marriage issues from the other side of the room, the things the woman has to fix about herself, and the men were all happy. Now it gets switched up. With the Marriage on the Roll event about to take place in LA, this class gives the other perspective.

The title comes from the song This Woman's Work, recorded in 1988 by Kate Bush and featured in the movie She's Having a Baby; most of us know it from Maxwell and from Love & Basketball. Most people never heard the words. When you read the lyrics, it is a husband whose wife is in a life-threatening childbirth, and he is dealing with fear, helplessness, guilt, and regret: thinking about the things he should have done with his wife and how he took her for granted.

Disclaimer from the start: this class is not for the wicked sisters, the ones that do not clean, do not cook, are disrespectful, and are not taking care of their wifely duties. This class is about the righteous wives, the virtuous wives that do what they are supposed to do.

Book read in class: Trauma Letters: Healing the Child Within, by Captain Galayah, number one on Amazon right now. From the introduction: trauma is not just what happened to you; trauma is what happened inside of you when what happened to you went unresolved. Clinically, trauma is an emotional and physiological response to an event or series of events that overwhelm an individual's ability to cope. It is not measured by the severity of the event alone but by the internal impact it leaves behind. Trauma reshapes neural pathways, alters attachment patterns, influences decision making, and distorts identity formation. Developmental trauma in childhood is particularly powerful because it occurs during the years when the brain is still forming its internal working models of safety, love, authority, and belonging: a father's absence, a father's neglect, a parent's betrayal, a moment of abandonment.

But we come home mad from the whole day, emotional capacity at zero, sometimes negative ten: do not bother me, leave me alone. Then the phone rings and a brother says come through, and suddenly you are in the shower and on your way. You just spent 30 minutes telling your wife to leave you alone.
- Some of you come home to a clean house and never say the house looks good, it smells good in here. That goes into that unthankful part.
- Having a form of godliness, but denying the power thereof. That is the double mindedness: like the Pharisees, cleaning the outside of the cup, whited sepulchres. You sit down and teach brothers for hours, giving breakdowns from everywhere in the book, and your wife does not know basic scriptures, cannot counsel a depressed sister without asking you what to tell her. Why? Because you do not teach her anything. She asks a question, you get mad and tell her to go watch a class.

**[Ecclesiasticus 7:26](/bible/sirach/7#v26)**  *[1:15:57]*

> <sup>[26](/bible/sirach/7#v26)</sup> Hast thou a wife after thy mind? forsake her not: but give not thyself over to a light woman.

- Hast thou a wife after thy mind? Forsake her not. Your wife is supposed to be after your mind, a representative of you; you two are supposed to be one flesh. If you are a brother that is good in the scriptures, good in history, a great counselor, your wife should be a great counselor.
- But give not thyself over to a light woman. You should not want your wife not understanding the Bible. Is she going to break down the book of Daniel or 2 Esdras? No, but she should have a good basic understanding of scriptures, able to counsel a sister that calls and says I am depressed.

**[Philippians 2:3](/bible/philippians/2#v3)**  *[1:18:55]*

> <sup>[3](/bible/philippians/2#v3)</sup> Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves.

- Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves. A lot of you run your household on strife and vainglory. You cannot run your household on ego.
- Some of you do not teach your wives because you do not want her to know something you do not know, do not want her to study, because one day she may ask a question you cannot answer and your pride and ego take over. There was a brother whose wife could not do anything, could not leave the house without him, did not know how to drive; he was not pouring into her at all. If she is supposed to be that tower against death, how is she going to guide you with the scriptures when you are going through something if you never built the tower up? Instead of counseling with us about how you do not build your wife up, you come and say, my wife does not know anything. Were you studying with her? No.

**[Ecclesiasticus 26:5-6](/bible/sirach/26#v5)**  *[1:23:44]*

> <sup>[5](/bible/sirach/26#v5)</sup> There be three things that mine heart feareth; and for the fourth I was sore afraid: the slander of a city, the gathering together of an unruly multitude, and a false accusation: all these are worse than death.
>
> <sup>[6](/bible/sirach/26#v6)</sup> But a grief of heart and sorrow is a woman that is jealous over another woman, and a scourge of the tongue which communicateth with all.

- Some of us still live life in the past. We get on the sisters about not forgiving and not forgetting, but this side of the room is the exact same way. We hold on to old arguments: here we go again, you are about to bring that up. We hold on to issues from past relationships, being cheated on, an ex-wife. Instead of a bag lady, you are a bag brother, carrying all of it, and you take it out on your wife. You do not trust her, not for something she did, but because of something somebody else did to you.
- The comparison game works both ways. The worst thing a woman can do is compare her husband to a man from her past or to her father. But a woman does not want to be compared to another woman either. There be three things that mine heart feareth, and for the fourth I was sore afraid: the slander of a city, the gathering together of an unruly multitude, and a false accusation; all these are worse than death. But a grief of heart and sorrow is a woman that is jealous over another woman. The wisdom here is that this fourth thing is worse than the things that are worse than death. And a scourge of the tongue which communicateth with all: you will not have a silent and loving woman if she is jealous over another woman.

**[Philippians 3:13](/bible/philippians/3#v13)**  *[1:26:53]*

> <sup>[13](/bible/philippians/3#v13)</sup> Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before,

- Brothers, you have to follow the scripture too: forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before. Let the past go: the issues with your father not being around, how your father treated your mom, how your mom treated you, the ex-girlfriend, the ex-marriage. If you do not let it go and move forward with what you have, you are going to be in a worse situation.

**[Ecclesiastes 7:10](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v10)**  *[1:28:10]*

> <sup>[10](/bible/ecclesiastes/7#v10)</sup> Say not thou, What is the cause that the former days were better than these? for thou dost not enquire wisely concerning this.

- Say not thou, What is the cause that the former days were better than these? for thou dost not enquire wisely concerning this. Do not sit back thinking about how everything was good with the ex: the bathwater was ready, the food was always ready. Well, you should have stayed with her. But she did not believe in God and did not want to keep the commandments, so you left and found a woman that believes she is an Israelite, that keeps the commandments and has the faith of Jesus Christ. And now you are comparing her to a wicked woman.
- It is the children of Israel in the wilderness: 40 years marching around because they kept looking back at Egypt. We were slaves, but the food was good. The manna even tasted how they wanted it to taste, and they still were not happy; they wanted meat, and when the Most High gave them so much quail they could not take it, they complained again. They kept looking back at their worst condition while the Most High was trying to free them. That is us with the exes in the past. That was not a good situation; you were unequally yoked with a nonbeliever. Now you have a believer, so build her up, teach her, raise her in the scriptures. If you keep going back to how stuff was before, how are you encouraging her to be godly?
- Video played in class, a preacher making the point: we treat strangers better than we treat those that we love. We will be rude, disrespectful, mean, cutting, thoughtless, and hateful to those that love us, because we can get away with it. We will give a stranger a compliment, but when was the last time you told your spouse how great they are, how well they are doing, how good they look?
- With brothers we do it: bro, I see you, I can tell you have been studying. A brother cuts his hair and lines his beard up and it is a whole celebration in the school. But your wife sits in the salon chair for eight hours, comes home, and asks, can you tell I cut it? And you say, I don't know, yeah, it looks good. We compliment men on their hair more than we compliment our wives on theirs.

**[1 Corinthians 7:29](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v29)**  *[1:35:59]*

> <sup>[29](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v29)</sup> But this I say, brethren, the time is short: it remaineth, that both they that have wives be as though they had none;

- This is not a simp-out class. Do not go home, stop going to camp, stop traveling, and rub feet all day. The time is short: it remaineth that both they that have wives be as though they had none. You have to be about the Lord's business. Brothers have had a baby and been in Africa the next week. The work has to be done no matter what.

**[1 Corinthians 7:33](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v33)**  *[1:37:01]*

> <sup>[33](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v33)</sup> But he that is married careth for the things that are of the world, how he may please his wife.

- But he that is married careth for the things that are of the world, how he may please his wife. When you are not doing the work, you have to take care of your responsibilities at home, your prized possession, your pillar of rest. If you do not build her up, she is going to be a pillar of stress.
- Why does it matter? Because she is teaching your kids. Do you want an unhappy, mean, angry, bitter, emotional woman bringing up your kids while you are not around? Your son is going to pick up those traits, and your daughter too. Take care of that possession at home or you will destroy it.

**[Hebrews 10:24](/bible/hebrews/10#v24)**  *[1:41:14]*

> <sup>[24](/bible/hebrews/10#v24)</sup> And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works:

- Let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works. In the congregation we do that: a brother in sadness, a brother that suffered loss, we provoke him to love and good works. Then we go home and provoke our wives to nothing but to be angry at us.
- Think about what we tolerate from strangers. Brothers on the street get berated and mocked by disrespectful people and restrain themselves through the word of God. You get shoulder-bumped by Esau in public and keep walking, because we are trying to fix our people, not destroy them. But at home, let your wife forget to do something and you are ready for war, ready to cuss out your wife. You treat a stranger better than the person you are one flesh with. Something is not right about that.

**[Hebrews 3:13](/bible/hebrews/3#v13)**  *[1:43:41]*

> <sup>[13](/bible/hebrews/3#v13)</sup> But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.

- Exhort one another daily, while it is called To day. How do you exhort your wife daily? Exhorting is encouraging one to do what is right. Some of you wake up, leave for nine or ten hours, come back, shower, go to Care 101, and the whole relationship with your wife is, hey, what's up though. Five words a day, while you are on Telegram getting on brothers about how we have to communicate.
- From the index cards the women fill out in different congregations, leadership hears things that do not make sense: a husband that does not even want his wife to say good morning to him. There was a brother who demanded his wife read chapters every day, come up with ten questions a day for him, while she was homeschooling their five or six kids and he was never there. You do not even do that yourself. That is a militant mindset about our household, and it comes from fear, misplaced anger, and thinking we have to rule our houses with ego.
- If you do not teach your wife, you are not teaching your daughters, and you are not teaching your sons. You get a breakdown, an epiphany, something off the chain, and you do not tell your wife. Then she misreads a scripture and gets embarrassed, or she quotes Bishop, a captain, an officer, a mother in the truth, because that is the only biblical words she hears. When she references things, she references the people she hears teaching the Bible. She should hear you teaching the Bible. Why not?

**[Malachi 1:6](/bible/malachi/1#v6)**  *[1:52:17]*

> <sup>[6](/bible/malachi/1#v6)</sup> A son honoureth his father, and a servant his master: if then I be a father, where is mine honour? and if I be a master, where is my fear? saith the Lord of hosts unto you, O priests, that despise my name. And ye say, Wherein have we despised thy name?

- A son honoureth his father, and a servant his master. A lot of times men only give honor where they think it matters: to the boss in the world because there are consequences, to the men in the truth, double honor to the elders. In your household you think, it does not matter, she is not going to do anything, I can do whatever I want here.
- That is familiarity. You respect strangers because there are consequences and you care how other people see you. At home you feel like there are no consequences. A good woman does not want to embarrass her husband, so you know you can get away with it. And the question and answer read in class said it: we are often nicer to strangers than family due to impression management, assumed unconditional love, and accumulated history. You look at your wife and think, no matter what, you are bound to me until I die, so I can treat you how I want. That is not scriptural.
- Videos played in class: a husband kept singing a love song while his wife struck him, and a wife described the abuse she was suffering at home; the teaching point is that some of you have good, righteous women and treat them in reverse, and stories like these are real in this truth too.

**[1 Peter 3:7](/bible/1-peter/3#v7)**  *[1:53:02]*

> <sup>[7](/bible/1-peter/3#v7)</sup> Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered.

- Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel. If something is weak, you have to build it up.

**[Proverbs 12:18](/bible/proverbs/12#v18)**  *[1:54:24]*

> <sup>[18](/bible/proverbs/12#v18)</sup> There is that speaketh like the piercings of a sword: but the tongue of the wise is health.

- There is that speaketh like the piercings of a sword: but the tongue of the wise is health. A lot of us are mad about something and go home and speak to our wives like the piercing of a sword.

**[Ecclesiasticus 4:30](/bible/sirach/4#v30)**  *[1:54:48]*

> <sup>[30](/bible/sirach/4#v30)</sup> Be not as a lion in thy house, nor frantick among thy servants.

- Be not as a lion in thy house, nor frantick among thy servants. Especially if you are a weakling in the streets: out camping, sin walks right by, and you are afraid to go into the scriptures and call it out. Then you go in the house and roar.

**[Proverbs 6:13](/bible/proverbs/6#v13)**  *[1:55:50]*

> <sup>[13](/bible/proverbs/6#v13)</sup> He winketh with his eyes, he speaketh with his feet, he teacheth with his fingers;

- He winketh with his eyes, he speaketh with his feet, he teacheth with his fingers. Some of you go home and speak to your wives with your feet and teach with your fingers, meaning you put hands and feet on your wife. You are abusive to your wives, while out in the streets you take every disrespect and say, next teacher.

**[1 Timothy 5:8](/bible/1-timothy/5#v8)**  *[1:56:54]*

> <sup>[8](/bible/1-timothy/5#v8)</sup> But if any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an infidel.

- Problems come in our households because we keep score: I provide, I pay all the bills, I take care of this and that. But if any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an infidel. You are supposed to do that. That is your reasonable service, a commandment of God. Congratulations for doing your job. You cannot throw in your wife's face that you do what you are supposed to do.

**[Ephesians 5:28-29](/bible/ephesians/5#v28)**  *[1:57:38]*

> <sup>[28](/bible/ephesians/5#v28)</sup> So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies. He that loveth his wife loveth himself.
>
> <sup>[29](/bible/ephesians/5#v29)</sup> For no man ever yet hated his own flesh; but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church:

- So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies. He that loveth his wife loveth himself. For no man ever yet hated his own flesh; but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church. How you deal with the church should be the same way you deal with your wife and how you deal with yourself.

**[Colossians 3:19](/bible/colossians/3#v19)**  *[1:58:30]*

> <sup>[19](/bible/colossians/3#v19)</sup> Husbands, love your wives, and be not bitter against them.

- Husbands, love your wives, and be not bitter against them. Bitterness is nothing but unresolved issues with unmet expectations. And our expectations are not going to be fully met until we get into the kingdom. Until then we are laboring to enter into that rest. This is not our rest; like 1 Corinthians 7:28 says, you shall have trouble in the flesh. There will be issues, but do not be bitter toward your wife.

**[Proverbs 16:32](/bible/proverbs/16#v32)**  *[1:59:42]*

> <sup>[32](/bible/proverbs/16#v32)</sup> He that is slow to anger is better than the mighty; and he that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh a city.

- He that is slow to anger is better than the mighty; and he that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh a city. In the household you have to be slow to anger and deal with your wife as the weaker vessel. You figure out how to control your spirit at camp when a man twice your size is standing in front of you; control it at home too.
- Now, if you have a wife that is unruly, stand up for yourself. But if you have a sister that does what she is supposed to do for the most part, there has to be a balance. Your wife cannot always see the worst part of you.

**[1 Corinthians 7:3-4](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v3)**  *[2:00:29]*

> <sup>[3](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v3)</sup> Let the husband render unto the wife due benevolence: and likewise also the wife unto the husband.
>
> <sup>[4](/bible/1-corinthians/7#v4)</sup> The wife hath not power of her own body, but the husband: and likewise also the husband hath not power of his own body, but the wife.

- Sisters, how can you help? Let the husband render unto the wife due benevolence: and likewise also the wife unto the husband. The wife hath not power of her own body. It is strange to hear of men that do not want to render due benevolence to their own wives; that one is hard to understand.

**[Ephesians 5:33](/bible/ephesians/5#v33)**  *[2:01:49]*

> <sup>[33](/bible/ephesians/5#v33)</sup> Nevertheless let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband.

- Let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband. Respect and tone matter when we are addressing issues. Sisters, speak to your husband the right way; Proverbs 15:1, a soft answer turneth away wrath. Your timing and how you approach things matters as a woman.

**[Proverbs 14:1](/bible/proverbs/14#v1)**  *[2:02:22]*

> <sup>[1](/bible/proverbs/14#v1)</sup> Every wise woman buildeth her house: but the foolish plucketh it down with her hands.

- Every wise woman buildeth her house: but the foolish plucketh it down with her hands. You pluck it down by complaining, yelling, arguing, and being disrespectful.

**[Ecclesiasticus 25:1](/bible/sirach/25#v1)**  *[2:03:06]*

> <sup>[1](/bible/sirach/25#v1)</sup> In three things I was beautified, and stood up beautiful both before God and men: the unity of brethren, the love of neighbours, a man and a wife that agree together.

- In three things I was beautified, and stood up beautiful both before God and men: the unity of brethren, the love of neighbours, a man and a wife that agree together. A man and a wife that agree together is beautiful before God.

**[Ephesians 5:21](/bible/ephesians/5#v21)**  *[2:03:37]*

> <sup>[21](/bible/ephesians/5#v21)</sup> Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God.

- How do they agree together? Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God. You both agree that you are going to follow the Bible, the scriptures, the word of God.

**[Matthew 7:12](/bible/matthew/7#v12)**  *[2:04:16]*

> <sup>[12](/bible/matthew/7#v12)</sup> Therefore all things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them: for this is the law and the prophets.

- A captain once gave this counsel: you do not have a daughter, so you counsel one way, but those of us with daughters deal with this differently, because you do not want your daughter going through it. Therefore all things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them.
- Men, ask yourself the question: the way you are treating your wife, is that the way you want your daughter's husband to treat her? If the answer is no, then you have to fix it. Fix yourself: deal with your past, drop the pride, do not deal with misplaced anger, and do not dominate with control.

**[1 Corinthians 16:13-14](/bible/1-corinthians/16#v13)**  *[2:05:03]*

> <sup>[13](/bible/1-corinthians/16#v13)</sup> Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong.
>
> <sup>[14](/bible/1-corinthians/16#v14)</sup> Let all your things be done with charity.

- Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong. Let all your things be done with charity. Quit you like men means conduct yourself like a man: keeping the commandments, with maturity, courage, discipline, and responsibility. That is how you have to deal as a man, and that is how you have to deal with your wife in your household.

## In Closing

I pray this message did not go out void. If you are having a situation in your household, this can fix it. Brothers, we have to fix ourselves. Sisters, if you are a good wife, continue to be a good wife. Shalom, Most High and Christ bless. Happy Sabbath, happy new moon.
